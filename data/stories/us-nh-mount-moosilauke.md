Mount Moosilauke is a 4,802-foot peak in Grafton County, New Hampshire.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists its lookout as a hotel site that is now gone.[^2]

The Tip-Top House on the summit was built in 1860, according to a caption in a Dartmouth College library exhibit.[^1] A second caption dates the merger of the two summit houses to 1902.[^1] Moosilauke's summit was one of the early White Mountain lookout sites, and it already had a hotel or viewing platform and telephone service.[^3] The Dartmouth collection holds a 1910 print that was prepared for a report weighing what fire lookout stations were worth.[^1] A hiking history lists the summit among ground-level lookout stations.[^4]

Dartmouth's account attributes the 1942 loss of the Tip Top House to a suspected lightning strike.[^1] A Wikipedia article on the Moosilauke Ravine Lodge describes a Summit Camp that Dartmouth operated on the peak from 1920 to 1942, which was lost to a lightning-caused fire.[^5] The two accounts do not say whether they describe the same building.[^1][^5]

Today the only trace of the Tip Top House on the summit is its foundation stones.[^1] Paths run across the mountain from many directions and reach a wide, open summit above the tree line.[^1] Dartmouth received the summit as a gift in 1920, and the college holds just under 4,600 acres of the mountain.[^1]
