Cerro Alto lookout stood on the summit of Cerro Alto Peak, about nine miles from Morro Bay in San Luis Obispo County.[^1][^2] The Forest Service gives the peak's elevation as 2,620 feet, and Wikipedia says 2,624 feet.[^2][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as an enclosed tower, now gone.[^4] A record for the Rocky Butte lookout says that its cab had first replaced a Civilian Conservation Corps-era lookout on Cerro Alto.[^5]

In 1953 Mrs. C. C. Yates worked the station by herself from July 1 to November 8, spotting fires as far south as below Grover City. She and her husband had worked the lookout together before.[^1]

A newspaper item of August 18, 1939 reported that the Santa Margarita fire had burned more than 2,000 acres and was moving toward the station and the Hale ranch.[^1]

The Forest Service kept a fire tower on the summit until 1973.[^2][^3] Once the Los Padres National Forest had closed the station, the state fire agency, CDF, acquired the cab, had crews take it apart, transport it, and reassemble it on top of the Rocky Butte tower.[^5]

The Rocky Butte record says the lookout structure came down after 1999, clearing room for a communications facility.[^5] It also says the site is not open to the public, and that some items from it are on display at a Cal Fire training center in Baywood. The record does not say where the cab is now.[^5]

At Cerro Alto itself, a Forest Service campground sits at the base of the peak, with 22 units, and 15 of its sites can be booked through recreation.gov.[^2] Wikipedia describes a few routes to the summit, the quickest a 1.95-mile class-one route.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit today.[^2][^3]
