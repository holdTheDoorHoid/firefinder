Virgin Creek Butte in Trinity County is a ground site, not a tower, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks it gone.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page for the site, which calls it Virgin Creek Buttes, places it in the Trinity National Forest.[^2]

An item dated October 25, 1940 reports on the Virgin Buttes lookout man.[^2] A Trinity forest report, printed in the California Ranger and quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says he has a new way of dealing with the snakes on his hill.[^2] He saw five rattlers in his first week on duty.[^2] The item says that after he received a violin by mail, he had seen only one rattler since.[^2]

The Forest Service's New River trail guide describes a route of about 2.5 miles up the ridge from the junction of the Virgin Creek and Slide Creek trails.[^3] It calls the end of the route the site of an old Forest Service fire lookout and says the view across the New River drainage is unobstructed.[^3] The guide lists the route among the New River Backcountry trails and does not say what, if anything, still stands at the site.[^3]
