Funk Mountain sits about three miles north of Conconully, in the Okanogan National Forest.[^1][^2] A tree platform went up on its summit in 1914.[^1][^2] A ground cab followed in 1935, and the cab that stands today, an L-4 with a catwalk, replaced it in 1943 on a 40-foot treated timber tower.[^1][^2]

A woman named Mabel Gavin appears in the records as one of the mountain's lookouts.[^2] A 1957 newspaper item said that Mrs. Harry A. Gavin of Omak had put in eleven summers, mostly on Funk Mountain, and had served 1,171 days in the job.[^3] A 1960 award citation named Mabel M. Gavin and thirteen seasons on the mountain.[^3] The two items appear to describe the same woman, and a 1952 listing also names Mabel Gavin.[^2][^3] In 1966 a paper reported the lookout was manned, and advised visitors to walk the last half mile because the road was rough.[^3]

The lookout was returned to service in 2000 after years of sporadic use, and it was staffed full time that year.[^1][^2][^4] It has been used for emergency detection in summer.[^2]

In August 2021 the Muckamuck fire burned all the way around the summit.[^4] One account says the lookout survived but that the 1914 crow's nest did not.[^4] Another says the tower narrowly escaped the fire and makes no mention of the crow's nest.[^5]

A 2017 visitor said the public may still go up, though many catwalk planks and several stair steps were gone. A sign at the top of the stairs warns people to keep out.[^4] When the summit road is gated, the walk to the top is 1.9 miles.[^4] The most detailed account in the sources is from 2017, so check current conditions with the Okanogan National Forest before planning a trip.[^4]
