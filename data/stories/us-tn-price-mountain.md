The Tennessee lookout list places a tower at Price Mountain in Sequatchie County, with an elevation of 2,244 feet (684 m), and marks it removed.[^1] The same page says its author gave unofficial names to towers that the USGS maps and the state forestry list did not name, so a name on that list is not always an official one.[^1]

Across Tennessee, the Civilian Conservation Corps is reported to have been responsible for building 98 lookout houses and towers in the 1930s.[^2] According to the state forestry division, its first fire tower was a wood-frame Cagle tower of about 1933, atop Wolfpen Mountain in Sequatchie County; it was later demolished and replaced with a steel-frame tower named for that mountain.[^2]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the Price Mountain site today. Tom Dunigan's page says most former tower sites are now privately owned, and it advises against climbing the towers.[^1] Please ask the landowner before going onto any former tower site.

[^2]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers, 1933 to 1975 (Multiple Property Documentation Form), National Park Service, https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/64501237.pdf (accessed 2026-10-21).
