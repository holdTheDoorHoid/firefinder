Anderson Peak, in Monterey County's Los Padres National Forest, got its first lookout in 1942.[^1][^2] A small one-room cabin went up that year for the Aircraft Warning Service, which watched for enemy aircraft.[^1][^2] Harry and Lillian Ross staffed it from mid-1942.[^1]

The Forest Service built the post, supplied it and staffed it, and the Army paid the Forest Service back for everything it spent.[^1] Operators had to pass aircraft sightings, and any fires or smoke they saw, on to Forest Service dispatch. The peak therefore did two jobs: watching for enemy planes and detecting fires.[^1]

In 1943 a standard post with a ground cabin and an observation cupola replaced the first building.[^1][^2]

The cabin did not stay on the peak. Someone bought it and moved it to Marble Peak, where it became a private summer getaway.[^2] One source dates the move to the late 1940s and the other to the 1950s.[^1][^2] The cabin was later consumed in the Gordo-Rat-Creek Fire of 1984.[^1][^2] The register still lists the site as removed by that wildfire, but the cabin had left the peak decades earlier.[^1]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains on the peak today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1]
