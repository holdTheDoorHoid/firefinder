A fire lookout went up on Sassafras Mountain in 1932.[^1][^2] The lookout site describes a steel tower, 35 feet tall, with a stairway inside and a cab enclosed in glass.[^1] It says the South Carolina Forestry Commission bought the tower, while North Carolina covered the transport costs and the materials for its foundations and erection, and that the county where it stood helped pay for it.[^1] The Transylvania County Library describes it as one of five lookouts the county once had, and does not mention South Carolina, so the ownership arrangement is not settled by these sources.[^2]

The library says the Sassafras tower, like the one on Pilot Mountain, has been removed.[^2]

Wikipedia reports that an observation tower went up on the summit of Sassafras Mountain in 2019.[^3] The mountain sits on the South Carolina–North Carolina line, closest to Rosman, a North Carolina town.[^3] From the parking lot, the summit is about 300 feet away on an easy walk; the path is fairly steep, but it has no stairs.[^3] Sources do not say whether the 2019 tower stands where the 1932 tower did.[^1][^2][^3]
