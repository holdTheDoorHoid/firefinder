Davis Point is a lookout site in the Lolo National Forest, which firelookout.com places 11 miles south of Missoula.[^1] A 1944 newspaper item gives a different distance, describing the site as 20 miles south of Missoula by road.[^2] Firelookout.com also describes an earlier tower in the 1930s, set on a low spur 1.75 miles northeast of Baldy Mountain.[^1]

In November 1944 the Forest Service chose Davis Point for a new model lookout.[^2] A January 1946 item describes its design: slanted glass windows, a flat roof, and a 40-foot tower of creosote-treated wood carrying a 14-by-14-foot cab with insulated walls and roof.[^2] The plans were one of four designed by Forest Service headquarters in Washington, D.C., and regional engineers in Missoula made some design changes.[^2] The item said the lookout was being completed for use the next fire season.[^2]

Firelookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page both give 1957 as the year a new 41-foot treated timber tower went up at the site.[^1][^2]

In 1966 the tower and cab were moved to Blue Mountain, southwest of Missoula.[^2][^3][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower had been used in filming Red Skies of Montana while at Davis Point, and that the movie company paid for the slanted glass windows that reduce glare.[^4]

No source read describes what, if anything, remains at Davis Point today, or whether the site is open to the public.[^1][^2]

The tower now stands at Blue Mountain, six miles southwest of Missoula.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register, which registered it on June 5, 1996, calls it the last working lookout on the Missoula Ranger District.[^4] Firelookout.com reports heavy visitation, which it ties to the site's nearness to Missoula, and says an interpretive setting has been proposed there.[^3]
