The Clearwater Lookout was a 40-foot wooden tower with a live-in cab, built in 1961 for the state Department of Natural Resources (DNR) on a high ridge in Jefferson County.[^1][^2][^3] The DNR's own carpentry crew did the work.[^3] The ridge sat between the lower Clearwater River and Kalaloch, just three miles from the beach.[^2][^3]

Janice McDowell was the first keeper.[^3] A Daily Chronicle report from July 1961 said she would move there from a lookout on the Quinault Indian Reservation.[^2][^3] Mildred Drazkowski held the post again in 1962, Beth Barlow Velie in 1963, and Margaret Flora from 1964 to 1966.[^3] Willhite thinks Flora may have stayed longer.[^3]

In July 1967 a cable whipped through a pile of cedar slash at a logging camp on the North Fork of the Raft River, and the friction set the dry wood alight.[^2] The Clearwater station, ten miles to the north, spotted the smoke.[^2] By Tuesday morning the fire had spread to about 6,000 acres, which made it the worst in state protected timber since a 14,000-acre blaze in Clark and Skamania counties in 1952.[^2] The Oregonian reported these details, as quoted by Forest Lookouts.[^2]

The lookout was destroyed in 1969.[^1][^3] The register lists it as removed that year.[^1] It added the site to the register on February 11, 2020, on the nomination of Tammy McLeod.[^1]

The land belongs to a private timber company, and anyone hiking to the site needs a permit.[^3] Willhite writes that Rayonier sold the land in 2016 to Bavarian Timber LLC, which American Forest Management manages. Permits remain on sale, but they are costly.[^3] His photo captions describe two footings and an old structure at the summit. He also says the timber was last harvested around 2008.[^3] Anyone interested should get the permit first.
