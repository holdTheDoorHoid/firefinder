The Quaker Hill tower stood in Portsmouth, Rhode Island.[^1] A 1934 newspaper report, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, says a Civilian Conservation Corps crew began building it in October of that year, on land that belonged to Arthur A. Sherman and under the State Bureau of Forestry.[^1] The report describes a 60-foot steel stairway and cabin-type forest fire tower.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 65-foot steel tower, first operated in 1937, at an elevation of 270 feet (82 m).[^2] The two height figures disagree.[^1][^2]

A 1950 notice announced the tower's official opening, with visitors admitted for the season from Easter Sunday, and named Manuel Medeiros as the official fire tower observer for the area.[^1] The same notice says the tower operated under the State Division of Forests and Parks, within the State Department of Agriculture.[^1] A 1951 notice says Medeiros had been observer since the tower's reopening over a year earlier, and names Robert Teachman to fill the vacancy.[^1]

In April 1963 the Portsmouth tower watchers spotted a fire on Prudence Island that burned about 75 acres of blackberry bushes and bull briars.[^1]

The register says the tower was removed in 1963 and re-erected on High School Hill in Tiverton.[^2] A May 1963 notice says the governor had signed authority to condemn an acre off Brayton Road in Tiverton for relocating the Portsmouth fire tower.[^3] The Tiverton register says the tower there was built in 1964 and demolished in approximately 2005.[^4]

The Forest Lookouts Association lists the Quaker Hill tower as gone.[^5] No source I read describes the Portsmouth site today, so I cannot say what remains there or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^5]
