Meadow Peak has carried a fire lookout in one form or another since 1928, when the Forest Service put up a simple cupola cabin on the summit, part of the Kootenai National Forest's early fire-detection network.[^1] A taller 15-foot L-4 tower replaced it in 1936, and the structure standing today, a 10-foot tower with a 225-square-foot L-4 cab and catwalk, went up in 1957, built by contractor Leonard Sheffield with help from three Forest Service employees for a reported $6,299.[^1][^2] It went into service the following year, in 1958, with Mr. and Mrs. Dave Griffith as its first staff.[^2]

The lookout was staffed into the 1990s before fire detection shifted away from fixed towers, and for a time afterward the Montana Department of Natural Resources and Conservation kept it in service in cooperation with the Forest Service.[^1][^2] By the time it was surveyed for the National Historic Lookout Register, it had already gone unstaffed for roughly a decade, though weathering and some vandalism were noted as less severe than feared.[^2] It was added to the register on May 12, 2000, as US 344.[^2]

Meadow Peak was never destroyed or abandoned outright; it has simply outlived its working life as a fire-spotting post and found a second one as a rental. The Forest Service now rents the cabin through Recreation.gov, typically from mid-June through late September.[^1] Getting there is not casual: the route follows mountain roads with stretches of loose, sharp rock, and the agency recommends a high-clearance 4WD vehicle with off-road tires.[^1] Inside, the cabin sleeps four on two twin beds and comes with a propane cooktop, table and chairs, though guests must haul in their own water, bedding, and propane, since there is no running water or electricity.[^1] Cell service is reportedly available at the site, a rare amenity for a backcountry lookout.[^1]
