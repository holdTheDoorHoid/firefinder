The Nooksack lookout stood in Whatcom County, Washington, and the Washington Division of Forestry and DNR administered it.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb notes that the site was sometimes called Bowman Mountain Lookout, after the mountain.[^3]

The Register dates the tower to 1949 and describes it as an 80-foot pole tower with an L-6 cab.[^1] WillhiteWeb says a separate sleeping quarters stood below it.[^3] The guide names Lorna Anderson, a local and UW student, as the lookout in 1955 and 1956.[^3] A state report quoted on the Forest Lookouts site says a lookout was completed on Nooksack during the 1956 to 1958 biennium.[^4]

In 1957 the tower was trimmed to 50 feet and fitted with a 9-by-9-foot cab.[^1][^2][^3]

The Register says the tower and its ground cabin were removed about 1968.[^1] The Fire Lookout site and WillhiteWeb say they were destroyed around that time.[^2][^3] The wording differs, but the three sources that give a year agree on about 1968.

WillhiteWeb's photo captions include a tower footing and one footing tossed down the hillside. The footing captions carry no dates.[^3] A private timber company owns the summit, and DNR owns several parcels near it.[^3] From Acme, Mosquito Lake Road runs about 4 miles east to a gate, which is posted with permit and security patrol signs.[^3] The guide says you can get a permit.[^3] Please do not pass that gate without permission. The guide gives the hike as 6.4 miles one way, with about 2,600 feet of gain.[^3] Elevations differ by source: the Register and the Fire Lookout site say 3,350 feet, while WillhiteWeb gives 3,356 feet on USGS and 3,361 feet on lidar.[^1][^2][^3]
