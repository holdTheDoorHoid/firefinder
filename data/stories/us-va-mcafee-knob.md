The Virginia Forest Fire Lookout Association lists McAfee Knob in Roanoke County under its "Unconfirmed Sites" heading, with the type and status both marked unknown.[^1] The sources we found describe a hiking overlook rather than a lookout.[^2]

The knob stands on Catawba Mountain, about 15 miles northwest of central Roanoke. Its elevation is about 3,197 feet, and the view looks down about 1,600 feet to the Catawba Valley.[^2] The knob takes its name from James McAfee, a Scottish-Irish immigrant who made his home in the Catawba Valley in the late 1730s.[^2]

Hikers were reaching the knob around the turn of the century, and parking was already a problem then.[^3] In the mid-1900s, much of the mountain was used for farming.[^2] One fire-related detail in the sources is an old fire road near a high-voltage power-line clearing on the way to the summit.[^2]

Today the knob is a stop on the Appalachian Trail, and the Appalachian Trail Conservancy manages the McAfee Knob hiking trail.[^2] The hike from the VA-311 parking area is about 4.7 miles.[^2] Wikipedia calls the summit a geological feature and describes no lookout structure on it.[^2]
