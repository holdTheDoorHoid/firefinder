The Buffalo Lookout Tower was one of the South Carolina Forestry Commission's fire towers, in Kershaw County.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page quotes the commission's annual report for July 1946 to June 1947, which counts it as one of the two lookout towers put up that year.[^1]

The state's tower network began in 1930 with a tower near Camden.[^2] When a smoke was spotted, two towers each recorded its direction, and the fire lay where the two readings crossed on a map.[^2]

The commission has explained that the towers' detection role shrank as rural populations grew and aircraft came into use.[^3] During the 1980s, the system came to serve mainly as a communication network, and its towers were taken out of service over time.[^3] The commission's own history dates the closing of its fire tower system to October 1, 1993.[^2] A Post and Courier history dates the system's retirement to 1994, so the two accounts differ.[^4] The Forest Lookouts site lists Buffalo as removed, with no date.[^1] OpenStreetMap labels the tower "Buffalo Lookout Tower (Demolished)."[^5]

Both listings describe the tower as gone.[^1][^5] The sources I read do not describe what, if anything, remains at the site today, and none says whether the land is open to visitors.
