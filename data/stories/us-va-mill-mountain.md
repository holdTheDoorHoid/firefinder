The Mill Mountain Tower Site is a former U.S. Forest Service fire tower site in the George Washington National Forest, in Bath County, Virginia.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives its elevation as 3,047 feet (929 meters) and lists it as US 1708, VA 15. James Ramsey nominated it, and it was added on January 15, 2018.[^1]

The register says the original tower here was made of wood and was later replaced with steel. The Forest Service tower stood until the early 1950s, and the register dates its removal to about 1952.[^1] The site still has concrete footers, according to the register.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list shows the site as gone.[^2] It gives the county as Bath / Rockbridge, where the register says Bath County.[^1][^2]

The name Mill Mountain also appears on a second register record.[^3][^4] The register's White Rock Tower Site, US 1706, VA 13, sits in the Rich Hole Wilderness and the register describes it as lying atop Mill Mountain.[^3] Its coordinates lie well to the south and west of this site's.[^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts Virginia list also cross-references "Mill Mountain" to White Rock.[^4]

The Forest Service describes the White Rock site on the Mill Mountain ridge.[^5] A hiking trail, Forest Trail 466, climbs to it.[^5] The Forest Service says the tower is gone, but a concrete foundation, an abandoned building and a storage tank remain there.[^5]

These sources do not settle whether the White Rock ridge and the register's Mill Mountain site are the same place. No source read says whether the public may reach the register's Mill Mountain site, so check with the Forest Service before going looking for it.[^1][^5]
