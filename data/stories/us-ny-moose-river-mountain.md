In 1919 the Conservation Commission bought a steel tower and set it up on Moose River Mountain, a 2,208-foot peak in the Town of Webb, Herkimer County.[^2][^3] It replaced a wooden lookout that had run for eight years in Lyonsdale, Lewis County, about eleven miles away.[^2] The commission moved it for the view, and its report said the new hill offered a far better one.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on the height: Wikipedia, the NYSFFLA entry and Starr's list all give 60 feet for the Aermotor LS40 tower, while Forest Lookouts gives 47.[^2][^3][^4][^1] A cabin for the observer's living quarters was built in 1922.[^1] A 1963 obituary, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says one observer served there for 18 years before retiring in 1961.[^1]

The sources also differ on when staffing ended.[^3][^2][^1] The NYSFFLA entry and Wikipedia say the lookout closed at the end of the 1970 season, when aerial detection took over.[^3][^2] Forest Lookouts says it was staffed into the mid-1970s.[^1]

The tower came down in 1977.[^2][^3] It was judged excess to fire detection needs and classed as a non-conforming structure in the newly established Ha-de-ron-dah Wilderness Area.[^2][^3]

Today the site lies inside the Ha-de-ron-dah Wilderness Area.[^3] CNY Hiking lists an old trail to the site of the former fire tower.[^5] A 2013 hiking blog describes the walk from a parking area off Route 28, where the first stretch runs through hunting-club property before a gate marks the wilderness.[^6] No source I read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit.
