Wolfpen Mountain in Sequatchie County is the site the Register calls the first fire tower in Tennessee, and it says the site is also known as Cagle Tower.[^1] Tennessee Landforms gives the same account, naming Cagle as the state's first fire tower.[^2]

A National Register study of the Division of Forestry's towers reports the Division's own account: its first fire tower was a wood-frame structure of about 1933 that stood atop Wolfpen Mountain and is no longer standing.[^3] The study says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in the early 1930s and that it stood 50 feet tall.[^3] The Register gives its height as 60 feet, with an 8-by-8-foot cab and a catwalk.[^1]

The study says the first tower was later torn down and succeeded by an Aeromotor LS-40, and that the date of that change is unknown.[^3] The Register dates that replacement to the 1930s and calls the 80-foot Aermotor still in place lightly maintained.[^1] A 2006 visit report calls the current tower a 100-foot Standard Aermotor, so the sources do not agree on its height.[^1]

The Register lists the tower as registered on January 3, 2015, with Jerry Lutes as nominator.[^1] Tennessee Landforms lists the tower as standing.[^2]

A 2006 visit report says the cab was locked, that a tower sign allowed visitors to climb at their own risk, and that the tower was staffed sometimes.[^1] It also notes a paved road to the tower.[^1] I found no sources naming who staffed the tower or which fires it watched. That report is from 2006, and I found no newer access rule, so check with the Division of Forestry before going, and do not climb the cab without permission.
