Bald Hill, also called Mount Ochepetuck, is in the town of Union in Tolland County, Connecticut.[^1] In 1910 a plan was being arranged for a trial lookout on the hill, to serve as a hub for telephone messages about fires among five towns.[^1] The Rev. Harvey M. Lawson and Mrs. Lawson put up a summer house on the mountain in 1906.[^1] Once a telephone was in place, Mrs. Lawson started spotting fires as a volunteer.[^1]

By about 1912 the state had set up an official fire station on the hill and made Mrs. Lawson its first observer.[^1] In 1914 a fire tower went up on her bungalow at the summit.[^1] A 1932 state report called Mt. Ochepetuck the state's second fire observation.[^1]

The lookout was shut down just before the Second World War.[^1] A fire in December 1942 destroyed the tower and the bungalow, while Coast Artillery soldiers were using the tower as a lookout.[^1]

In 1956 the state announced a new fire tower near the Bell Telephone Co. relay tower, to be overseen by the State Park and Forest Commission.[^1] By October 1957 the state had built a steel tower on the mountain, and an observer was on duty for the fall season. The tower sat southeast of the telephone relay station.[^1]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register has an entry, Bald Hill Tower Site, for a 60-foot (18.3 m) steel tower that it says went into service in 1913.[^2] The entry was registered on September 9, 2008.[^2] The register's 1913 date is one year earlier than the 1914 bungalow tower in the Forest Lookouts history, so the two sources give different dates.[^1][^2]

The most recent tower in the Forest Lookouts history is the state's 1957 steel tower.[^1] The register lists the site as a former lookout. No source I read says whether a tower still stands there, or whether the ground is open to the public.[^1][^2] A Connecticut Siting Council facility list for Union describes an existing Northeast Utilities tower site off Bald Hill Road, about 2,000 feet north of Route 190, but I found nothing that ties it to the lookout.[^3]
