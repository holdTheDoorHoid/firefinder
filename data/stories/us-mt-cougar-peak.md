Cougar Peak's first lookout went up in 1930, an L-4 cab with a gable roof on the Lolo National Forest overlooking the Clark Fork River Valley.[^1][^2] That structure was replaced in 1952 by the hip-roof L-4 ground house with a surrounding catwalk that still stands today — the only one of its standard 1936 design left on the Lolo.[^1] The National Register of Historic Places lists Clyde Fickes, a Forest Service architect associated with many of the agency's standard lookout designs, in connection with the structure, with a period of significance running from 1952 to 1967.[^3]

The lookout was staffed regularly into the mid-1980s.[^1][^2] One of its more distinctive features isn't architectural but personal: rock carvings left on site by the lookouts who worked there over the decades, the earliest dated 1921, predating the current building by decades.[^1]

In 1995, a Passport in Time volunteer project reroofed the cabin and replaced and repaired its windows and door, along with other repairs, helping keep the structure sound after staffing ended.[^1][^2] Cougar Peak Lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2009 and is separately listed on the National Register of Historic Places.[^1][^3]

The lookout is now part of the Forest Service's cabin rental program, bookable through Recreation.gov, and sits near the Cabinet Mountains Wilderness with views over the Clark Fork Valley.[^1][^4] It sleeps up to four.[^4]
