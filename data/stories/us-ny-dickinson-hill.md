Dickinson Hill Fire Tower stands in Grafton Lakes State Park in Rensselaer County, New York.[^3][^1] The 60-foot steel tower was built in 1924.[^3] The register says the State of New York built it,[^1] and Wikipedia says the Aermotor Corporation made it as a prefabricated structure.[^2]

During World War II the tower was used by the Aircraft Warning Service, and in the 1970s the U.S. Navy staged communication experiments there.[^1]

Wikipedia and the New York Almanack both say the fire watch ended in 1972.[^2][^3] The register, however, says the tower was last used for fire detection in the 1980s, so the sources disagree on that date.[^1] An undated note on the register page says the State Police currently own the tower, which conflicts with the 2012 handover to state parks described below.[^1]

The tower was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2011.[^2][^3] In 2012 the tower was handed over to state parks. The Friends of Grafton Lakes State Park restored it, and it reopened to visitors that year.[^3]

By late October 2025, State Parks had finished a project that repaired all 80 steps and the cab and platform decking, and repainted the tower.[^3][^4]

Today the tower is a hiking destination.[^3] The walk is about 1.5 miles from the North Long Pond Road parking area.[^3] Check with Grafton Lakes State Park before you go, since hours and conditions can change.
