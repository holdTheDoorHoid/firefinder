Miranda Pine Mountain lies in Santa Barbara County, within Los Padres National Forest.[^1] Its register entry says it was built as an Aircraft Warning Service post around 1943, with a ground cabin and cupola.[^1] The Aircraft Warning Service was a civilian arm of the Army's Ground Observer Corps, formed during World War II to spot enemy aircraft entering US airspace.[^2] On the West Coast, many Forest Service fire spotter towers were turned into Aircraft Warning Service posts, but this one was built as a post from the start.[^2][^1]

The register gives the elevation as 3,999 feet (1,219 meters), but a hiking guide lists the mountain at 4,061 feet.[^1][^3] The register also says the removal date of the post is unknown.[^1] The service itself was deactivated on May 29, 1944.[^2]

The register does not describe what is at the site today, and we found no source saying whether visitors can reach it.[^1]
