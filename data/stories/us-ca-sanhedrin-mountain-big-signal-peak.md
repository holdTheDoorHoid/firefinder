The Sanhedrin Mountain lookout, also called Big Signal Peak, sits in the Sanhedrin Wilderness of the Mendocino National Forest.[^1][^3] The register says the summit has held two earlier structures: the first was built before 1923, and the second went up in 1933.[^1]

The building there now dates to 1961. The register suggests a microwave relay project was the likely reason it went up, and says it opened in 1962.[^1] The register's build field lists 1923, 1933 and 1961 together, so the 1923 date in our record does not match the structure standing today.[^1]

Newspaper items quoted on the Forest Lookouts site show the summit staffed in the 1920s and 1940s. A 1925 item names Harry Goldert as lookout at San Hedrin.[^2] A 1927 item names Chess Riffe as chief of the fire ranger lookout there.[^2] A 1942 item says Harry Presley had been at the Big Signal lookout since June 16.[^2]

The 1987 fire is where the accounts split. A Ukiah Daily Journal item of September 2 says the flames forced the lookout's evacuation.[^2] A September 3 item says the flames destroyed the tower.[^2] A September 13 item says the lookout was spared, contrary to earlier reports, and had been manned again.[^2] The sources disagree, and the items quoted here do not settle which report was right.

A 1992 note says budget cuts meant the lookout would not be staffed that fire season.[^2] The register listed the lookout on October 22, 2019, with Brad Eells as its nominator.[^1]

Today the picture is thinner. A November 2019 trip report describes a decommissioned lookout on the summit, with microwave relay gear and other antennae mounted on it.[^4] The Forest Service says there are no developed trails in the Sanhedrin, and that the only public access point is the lookout at the end of Forest Road 20N04.[^3] The trip writer says the last three miles, up a spur road, are too rough for ordinary cars.[^4] None of the sources we read says whether the cab is open to visitors. Climbing a decommissioned tower that carries relay equipment is not something we can recommend, so check with the Mendocino National Forest before going up.
