Mineral Peak Lookout stands on the peak of the same name in the Lolo National Forest, beside the Rattlesnake Wilderness north of Missoula, Montana.[^1][^3]

The summit had a lookout before this one.[^1] Firelookout.com says a crow's nest gave way to a pole tower with an L-4 cab in 1935.[^2] According to the nomination, the Lolo National Forest put up the current 1936-pattern L-4 house in 1957 in place of that earlier cab.[^3] The kit came from a Portland company, and the crew used the old cab as quarters while they worked.[^3]

Before the road was built, the site could be reached only by pack trail.[^3] A Forest Service crew then bulldozed a thirteen-mile road from Gold Creek to the top.[^4] The house is 14 feet square and sits on a 53-foot timber tower.[^3]

The lookout was a primary post from 1957 through 1967.[^4] In 1967 the forest counted it among twenty-six lookouts to be manned that season.[^3] A 1968 inspection found that the average number of first-discovery fires seen from the peak between 1958 and 1967 was only 1.4.[^3] After that inspection, the Forest Service reclassified the site as an emergency lookout, and by 1974 it was staffed only in extreme fire danger.[^4]

Between about 1980 and 2005 the house stood empty and got little upkeep.[^3] Restoration began in 2005 after an inspection found the tower stable.[^3] In 2006 the windows and door went to the Region 1 Historic Preservation Team shop, and the windows were back in place in July and August 2008.[^3] In 2012 a regional preservation team and volunteers put on a new cedar shingle roof.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register registered it on March 1, 1997.[^1] The National Register of Historic Places listed it on January 29, 2018.[^3]

An early bid to rent the house through the forest's cabin program was discouraged, the nomination says, because the access was hard and the tower seemed risky.[^3] When the nomination was written, hikers and backpackers were visiting it, and some of them stayed in the house.[^3] The nomination is dated November 2017, so the present-day picture may have changed since.[^3]

The sources do not agree on every detail. The NHLR gives the elevation as 7,326 feet, the nomination says 7,447 feet, and firelookout.com says 6,704 feet.[^1][^3][^2] Firelookout.com also places the lookout in Flathead County, while the NHLR and the nomination say Missoula County.[^2][^1][^3] The NHLR dates the kit that topped the tower to 1936, where firelookout.com gives 1935 for the cab.[^1][^2]
