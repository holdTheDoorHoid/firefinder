Trestle Peak is a former lookout in Bonner County, Idaho, within the Kaniksu National Forest.[^1][^2] The sources give its elevation as 6,032 feet on one page and 6,320 feet on the other.[^1][^2]

The sources do not agree on when the lookout was built.[^1][^2][^3] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page quotes a museum source in Bonner County, which says the lookout was built between 1910 and 1914 and that it is on the 1914 maps.[^2] A 1914 item quoted by the Forest Lookouts page reports telephone communications to the lookout that April.[^3]

Two other listings date a cabin with a roof platform to 1921.[^1][^3]

In August 1915 a lookout on Trestle Creek reported fires on Lightning Creek to the local forestry office.[^3] Tom D. Burleigh, then 22, staffed the lookout in 1917, and a museum account says he called in several blazes on his first day in July.[^2] A 1921 entry names Mr. Stidwell as staff, citing a regional book on Northwest lookouts.[^2]

The cabin has since been removed.[^1] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page says the authors are unsure when it came down.[^2]

The site is a hike-in on a trail that the same page calls fairly popular.[^2] The authors visited in 2019 and again in 2026, and found two rock dugouts at the site.[^2] Among the debris are wire, nails, glass, metal and tin.[^2] A fire has changed the landscape since the authors' first hike, yet they still find the views over Lake Pend Oreille and the Cabinets spectacular.[^2]
