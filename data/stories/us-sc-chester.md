Chester was a South Carolina Forestry Commission fire tower in Chester County.[^1] The state's 1939-40 report, as transcribed by Forest Lookouts, says the Civilian Conservation Corps handled all of that year's tower construction, including the telephone lines, at no cost to the state, and that a new tower was built at the Chester site.[^1] The report says the Corps finished the tower-unit buildings there, and lists what each unit held: a four-room dwelling, a barn, a chicken house, a smokehouse and a latrine.[^1] The commission's history says most tower sites covered 10 acres and had, besides the tower, a year-round home for the operator.[^3]

The 1957-58 report says the old wooden tower at Chester was replaced by a new steel structure.[^1] The commission's history adds that all of its wooden towers were eventually replaced with steel.[^3] The commission ended its fire tower network on October 1, 1993.[^3] The Forest Lookouts site now lists the Chester tower as removed.[^1]

A blog post about mapping South Carolina's towers located the Chester Lookout Tower from a place-name database, and its author did not know whether the tower still stood.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, and none says whether the land is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
