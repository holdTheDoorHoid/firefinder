In December 1912 the annual report of Assistant Supervisor Robert E. Clark described the fire lookout stations in the Leadville National Forest. It named a third station, classed as tertiary, on Midland Hill.[^1]

In June 1914 the Carbonate Chronicle of Leadville reported that a new trail east of Buena Vista would be completed, and that Midland Hill would become a fire lookout station once the trail reached it.[^1] The paper gave the view as the reason, describing a wide outlook over the Arkansas drainage, stretching from Granite to Salida.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Midland Hill among its unknown or undocumented sites. It says a site will be moved onto its lookout lists if evidence shows it was regularly used as a lookout.[^2]

Today the hill is a hiking destination. The Hiking Project guide describes a five-mile out-and-back whose directions begin with parking at Buena Vista River Park, and says the route gives hikers quick access to the hill’s views.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page lists the agency as the San Isabel National Forest.[^1] No source describes a lookout structure, or any remains of one, on the summit today.
