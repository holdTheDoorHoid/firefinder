Black Rock Mountain rises on the eastern edge of the Rocky Mountains, in the Ghost River area west of Calgary.[^5][^1] The lookout sits on its summit, on Crown land about 40 km north of Exshaw.[^5][^4]

Sources agree that it dates to about 1928.[^2][^3][^4][^5] They disagree about who built it. The register says the Canadian Forest Service constructed it, and the Calgary Guardian names Harry Fisher of the Canadian Forest Service as the man who surveyed and built it.[^1][^3] A 2020 Mountain Legacy Project article credits Fisher with the Alberta Forest Service instead.[^2]

It is a one-storey cabin, 3.7 by 3.7 m (12 by 12 ft), with a pyramid roof and wood shingles, open on all sides for views.[^5] Steel cables tied it to the mountain against the wind.[^3] Provincial rangers staffed it.[^1] Pack horses carried supplies from the Aura Ranger Station, later the Ghost Ranger Station, and a resupply trip took at least three days.[^3]

Sources differ on how the lookout reported smoke. The Calgary Guardian says it had telephone lines to the ranger station and no standard radio.[^3] The 2011 heritage statement says fires were reported by radio.[^5]

A section of the trail collapsed in 1950, and it was not repaired.[^5] The lookout closed at the end of that season, and Mockingbird Hill lookout to the northeast replaced it.[^2] The register says it was abandoned in the 1950s when part of the trail fell away, but the heritage statement and the Rocky Mountain Outlook both give 1950.[^1][^5][^4]

The register says the Friends of the Eastern Slope re-shingled the cabin in 1995, and that a crew of Junior Forest Rangers rebuilt the trail.[^1] The heritage statement dates re-shingling of the roof and walls to 1996.[^5] In July 2016 the Municipal District of Bighorn council unanimously approved applying for provincial historic resource designation for it. The Rocky Mountain Outlook said a decision could take 12 to 16 months.[^4]

Mountain Legacy Project photographs from 2020 show the lookout still standing on the summit.[^2] A Calgary Guardian article, undated in the copy we read, says the structure is still on the exposed summit, and that its only contents are signatures carved into the wood.[^3] An Alberta Historic Places article shows a 2009 photograph of it captioned as abandoned.[^6]

The Calgary Guardian describes a difficult hike in remote wilderness, about 18 km round trip with 900 m of climbing from the backcountry campground.[^3] The lookout is in Don Getty Wildland Provincial Park, and the most direct approach is the rough TransAlta Road off the Forestry Trunk Road.[^3]
