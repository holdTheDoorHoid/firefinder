Lafayette County's fire tower was known locally as the Thacker Mountain fire tower.[^1] It was originally used to locate forest fires and to house emergency response equipment.[^1] Before newer methods took over, watchmen took turns scanning the county horizon for smoke, a 2023 Oxford Eagle column by John Cofield recalls.[^2]

In May 2022 the county received only one bid for the removal work, just under $18,000. The board rejected it and re-advertised the job.[^1] By August the paper reported that the structure had posed a safety concern for years, and the board voted to go with Heavy Construction's $14,809 bid to take it down.[^3] District two supervisor Larry Gillespie said he regretted losing the tower, but that its liability risk had become too great.[^3]

In 2022 the county's emergency director said another tower had been built beside the old one to carry the county's transmitters, and that the old tower was no longer required for emergency response.[^1] A 2023 column by Cofield says the tower had decayed so badly that plans to take it down followed, and the writer mourns its loss.[^2] No source found confirms that the demolition was finished, and no source describes what remains at the site or whether it is open to visitors.
