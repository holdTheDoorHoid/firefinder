The Shuckstack Fire Tower stands on Twentymile Ridge in Great Smoky Mountains National Park, on the North Carolina side.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register places it in Swain County, North Carolina.[^2]

The Public Works Administration built the tower in 1934, along with a ground cabin at its base.[^3] The Register says the PWA and the National Park Service constructed it that year.[^2] The steel structure is roughly 60 feet tall, topped by a metal cab measuring 7 by 7 feet.[^3]

The National Park Service staffed the lookout until the late 1960s, when it was taken out of service.[^3] The tower was used to spot wildfires, and later served as a radio repeater tower.[^5] We found no record of particular fires it spotted.

In 1987, following the annual assessment of backcountry structures, the ground cabin was removed, leaving only its chimney and cistern.[^3] A partial rehabilitation in 2014 replaced the stairs and patched holes in the roof.[^3] A Smoky Mountain News feature, undated in our copy, quoted Peter Barr, director of the North Carolina chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association.[^5] It described rusting bolts, rotting floor planks and severe vandalism, and said the park had not yet decided on rehabilitating the tower.[^5] About $4,000 was held in an account kept by Barr's firetower group, earmarked for restoration and repair.[^5]

Wikipedia says the retired tower is still reachable by the public.[^4] Reaching the summit by the Appalachian Trail and a short spur trail covers about 3.5 miles from the north-side Fontana Dam lot.[^4] The Smokies Guide measures 3.4 miles from the road.[^1] The hike is rated medium to strenuous, and Wikipedia says the summit rises about 2,300 feet above Fontana Lake.[^1][^4] The Register calls it a popular hiker destination.[^2] We found no closure notice and no repair record newer than 2014, so check with the National Park Service before you go. If the stairs or any part of the tower look unsafe, or a sign says closed, stay below and do not climb.
