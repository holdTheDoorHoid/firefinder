The peak above Custer State Park's Black Hills had a fire watcher long before it carried the name Mount Coolidge. Newspapers from the 1920s describe a "Lookout Mountain" or "Sheep Mountain" station, first an old steel tower and then, starting in 1927-28, a 60-foot log tower built by the state park with logs cut on site.[^1] Jack Sheehan worked the station through much of that decade, famously facing down a wandering pet bear from partway up his ladder in 1926.[^1] The peak was officially renamed Mount Coolidge by the U.S. Geographic Board in February 1934, after President Coolidge's visits to the area; locals had called it that informally before the government made it official.[^1]

The log tower was still standing in 1939, when a Rapid City Journal story described it as built in 1928 to replace the earlier steel structure.[^1] Sometime after that, the Civilian Conservation Corps replaced it with the three-story stone lookout that stands today; sources disagree on exactly when, with the National Historic Lookout Register giving 1939 and the Forest Fire Lookout Association giving 1940-41; Custer State Park Resort describes it as one of the CCC's last projects in the Black Hills before the program ended.[^2][^3][^4][^5]

Known lookout staff over the following decades include Clarence Mann (1939-41), Lamont McKinney (1941-54), Gayle Dryden (1958-61), Gayle Duncan (1963), Fred Herbert (1966), and Jerry and Lynn Loomer (1983-90).[^3] The tower is still staffed today as a fire lookout and dispatch point for South Dakota Game, Fish and Parks, making it one of the lookouts in this region that never went fully dark.[^4][^2]

Mount Coolidge sits at roughly 6,023 feet and is one of the few lookouts you can drive to: a gravel spur off SD Highway 87, about 3 miles south of US 16A, climbs 1.7 narrow, blind-curved miles to the summit, with sharp drop-offs that make slow driving and watching for oncoming traffic essential.[^5][^6] It's open to visitors during daylight hours in summer, typically 9am to 5pm, with wide views reaching the Badlands on a clear day.[^6] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in June 2017.[^4]
