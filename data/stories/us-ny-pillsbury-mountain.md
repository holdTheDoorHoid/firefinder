Pillsbury Mountain sits at Arietta in Hamilton County, and it feels remote.[^2][^4]

The first station on the summit was a wooden tower with a log cabin, set up in 1918 by the landowner, the Champlain Reality Company.[^1] The NHLR says the company ran it as a secondary station during dry weather.[^1] The state's payroll roster is less clear about those early years. Its first Pillsbury entry is an observer from May 1920, and the roster itself warns that it is incomplete.[^3]

Operation passed to the state in 1924, the year a steel Aermotor LS-40 tower went up.[^1][^2] The NHLR gives the tower's height as 60 feet, while Wikipedia gives 69 feet, so the sources do not agree on that either.[^1][^2] The observer's cabin and shed were added in 1950.[^2]

Observers staffed the tower into the early 1980s, and the roster's last entry is from 1981.[^3] Bill Starr, who compiled the roster and now serves as the NHLR's state registrar, was himself an observer here in the late 1970s.[^1][^3] The state officially decommissioned the tower in 1983. That same year, forest rangers installed a solar-powered two-way radio repeater on it, and the repeater is still there.[^3][^1]

Upkeep has been uneven. The NHLR notes minor restoration work in 2005, and the same year the register listed the tower.[^1] A November 2016 visit report found the tower needing paint, stair and landing work, and safety netting repairs, and the observer's cabin open to the weather through a hole in the roof and door.[^1] The National Register listed the station in 2010.[^2]

Today the cab is closed to the public, but the tower stairs lead to a top landing with wide views.[^4][^1] The access roads are owned by Lyme Timber, and the public uses them under a conservation easement, so mind the private land and give way to logging trucks when they are active.[^4] In winter the roads close and the approach becomes a long walk.[^1] Given the repair notes from 2016, check current conditions before you climb.
