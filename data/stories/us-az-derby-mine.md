The Prescott National Forest set up a full-time, or "constant," lookout at the Derby Mine in 1911.[^1] The forest had two full-time lookouts, and the other was at Mt. Union.[^1]

The mine sits about seven miles west of Prescott, in Yavapai County's Thumb Butte district, and was also known as the Madizelle.[^2] A newspaper history places the mine's development and production years between 1905 and 1937. It adds that excess subsurface water hindered the mine's operation and made it unprofitable.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page files the lookout under the mine's name, so I have taken it to stand at the mine.[^1]

Around 1960 the land was recorded as Highland Pines, a real estate development with more than 57 homes. The mine's main shaft, over 350 feet deep, supplied water to the subdivision.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list gives the type as unknown and the status as abandoned, not gone.[^3]

The mine site now lies inside the Prescott National Forest.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout there, and none says whether the site is open to visitors.
