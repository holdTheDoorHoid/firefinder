Green Mountain's lookout stands on the summit in Effingham, in Carroll County, and it has been moved, shortened and given new cabs over the years.[^1][^2] In June 1922 the state bought 15 acres on the top of the mountain for a station to be run jointly with Maine.[^2] That year a 47-foot steel tower was moved to the site from Cedar Mountain in Maine.[^1][^2] The Forestry Commission said the new station overlooked a broad area in both states.[^2]

The register says the tower was cut down to 39 feet in 1977 and topped with a wooden cab in the Maine style, and it says no other structure of this kind stands in the state.[^1] A Coast and Geodetic Survey note from 1958 gives the overall height as about 35 feet, which matches neither figure.[^2]

Heights and elevations do not agree across the sources. A 1966 Portsmouth Herald item, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, described litter on the two-mile trail up the mountain and gave the mountain as 1,907 feet high.[^2] A 2025 hiking guide puts the summit at 1,884 feet.[^4] The register's elevation for the lookout, 855 feet, sits far below both.[^1]

Staffing was limited. A Forest Fire Lookout Association report from 2015 says the state staffed its towers only during high fire danger weather.[^3] That year the state hired contractors to rehabilitate the tower, with new concrete footings, a new cab, stair treads, railings and paint, at a cost of about $215,000 from state capital funds.[^2][^3]

The register lists the lookout as in part-time service.[^1] A 2025 hiking guide calls it no longer active and says its cab is locked.[^4] Hikers reach the summit on foot from High Watch Road, a 2.8-mile out-and-back that the guide rates as moderate.[^4] From the platform below the cab, the guide says, the views reach toward the White Mountains and the Ossipee range.[^4]
