From 1915 to 1926 the Cispus Mountain site, in Gifford Pinchot National Forest, Lewis County, Washington, held only a firefinder stand on its peak.[^1] The register describes the cab that came later as an R-5 or 4A variation, a 14-by-14-foot wooden building with a hip roof.[^1]

Sources disagree on the year the cab went up. The register and SummitPost say 1926, firelookout.com says 1925 for a live-in cabin, and a Forest Lookouts page says a structure was erected in 1928.[^1][^2][^3][^5]

Most sources say fire destroyed the cab in 1956, after it had fallen out of use.[^2][^3][^5] The register tells a different story. Citing an archaeological report, it says the lookout was dismantled, that some of its lumber went into a small cabin north of the peak, and that part of the lumber left behind was burned.[^1] Finds from 1982 below the peak included window frames, cabinets and sections of hip roof.[^1]

The Forest Service's Trail #127 leads to the former lookout site on Cispus Point, a brief branch from Klickitat Trail #7 above Jackpot Lake.[^4] Hikers, horse riders and cyclists may use the trail, which is closed to motorized travel.[^4] SummitPost describes a brief, undemanding walk with vistas of Mount Adams, Mount Rainier, Mount Saint Helens and Mount Hood.[^5]

Forest Road 20, reached from Highway 12, leads to the trailhead, and a 2016 SummitPost account says the road was rough and washed out in places.[^5]

No source we read describes what stands on the peak now, beyond the 1982 finds below it.[^1]
