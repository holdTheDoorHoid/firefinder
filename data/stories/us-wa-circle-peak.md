Circle Peak Lookout sat on a summit in Snohomish County, Washington, inside the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1] firelookout.com places it about 13 miles east of Darrington.[^2] The summit was rocky enough that a pile of rock had to be blasted away before a building could go up, according to WillhiteWeb.[^4]

The site began as a camp. Sources agree that a camp was set up in 1929.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register says a 12-by-12-foot frame cab replaced it in 1930,[^1] and Forest Lookouts gives the same size and year.[^3] firelookout.com instead calls the 1930 cab a gable-roofed L-4 cab, so the sources differ on the design.[^2] WillhiteWeb ties the blasting to the 1930 build,[^4] but Forest Lookouts quotes a June 1932 item, from Six Twenty-Six, about the rock being shot away.[^3] The dates do not line up, and the sources do not explain why.

WillhiteWeb names Andy Holland as the lookout in 1931 and calls him the first person to occupy the new lookout.[^4] Panorama photographs were taken from the cab roof in September 1935.[^3][^4] Harry Gladsjo was working in the lookout in 1961, according to a July 1961 Arlington Times item quoted by Forest Lookouts.[^3][^4]

Sources agree the lookout was gone by 1967.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register says it was removed that year,[^1] while firelookout.com and Forest Lookouts say it was destroyed, and WillhiteWeb says it burned.[^2][^3][^4]

No source describes what remains on the summit today. Forest Lookouts quotes a 1950 Coast and Geodetic Survey description of a wooden survey building about 12 feet tall at the station.[^3] That description predates the 1967 loss, and it does not say whether the building was the lookout.

According to WillhiteWeb, most of the final road to the trailhead was closed for many years before it reopened in 2020, and conditions could change from year to year.[^4] The hike is about 2 miles each way with roughly 2,000 feet of climb.[^4] The final traverse to the high point includes an exposed stretch over cliffs.[^4] We found no closure notice for the trail, but no source confirms the site's public status today.
