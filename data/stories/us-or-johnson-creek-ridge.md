In 1939 the Umatilla Indian Agency planned a lookout tower for the Johnson Creek area, inside the Umatilla Indian Reservation.[^1][^2] The first design was a 40-foot wooden tower, mostly built from nearby hewn material.[^2] Two men who visited the site that July judged that plan undesirable.[^2] After an August review, the plan switched to steel.[^2] The sources give three heights for the steel tower: 80 feet in the August recommendation, 82 feet 6 inches in the October 1939 contract, and 85 feet in a 1940 cost analysis.[^2] The register also gives 85 feet.[^1]

The steel tower was ordered from the International-Stacey Corporation at a cost of $1,399, plus $476.33 in transport charges.[^2] It went up in May and June 1940, built by the Civilian Conservation Corps' Indian Division, with a 7-by-7 steel cab.[^2][^5] The Forest Lookouts page calls it the only lookout built on the reservation.[^2]

An October 1940 letter says some parts had not been machined properly, and that an old-style fire finder, borrowed from the local Forest Service office, was used during fire season.[^2]

The tower came down in 1958.[^1][^4] The register says its lower half was reused for a new tower on Tiptop Mountain in 1959.[^1][^4] The Forest Lookouts Tip Top page describes the move differently: the district dismantled the steel tower and hauled it to La Grande, and a 1957 plan had proposed using about 50 feet of it.[^3]

The site is within the Umatilla Indian Reservation, and the former-site register listed it in November 2019.[^1] A 2025 summary of the Johnson Lands restoration area describes the 1938 to 1942 work there, including a lookout tower.[^5] No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the site now, and I found nothing saying whether visitors may enter the land.
