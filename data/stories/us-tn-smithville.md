Smithville's fire tower stands in DeKalb County, Tennessee, on a site that the Tennessee Landforms list puts at 1,145 feet.[^1] The same list numbers the tower 4-25 and marks it standing.[^1] Those numbers are district and tower numbers from a 1976 state list.[^1]

The sources do not say who built the tower, when it went up, how tall it is, or what it looked like in use. The National Register property document on the state's Division of Forestry fire towers says the division built 208 of them, between about 1930 and 1970.[^2] That document lists Smithville among the towers still standing as of March 26, 2014.[^2] No newer dated record appears in the sources.

The Landforms page says its lookout history is still being gathered.[^1] The sources do not say whether the tower can be climbed or who owns the site. The same document cautions that climbing surviving towers is dangerous, and that those towers are frequently in poor shape.[^2] Check with the Tennessee Division of Forestry or the landowner before going, and do not climb it.

[^1]: Tennessee Lookout Towers, Tennessee Landforms, https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers, 1933 to 1975 (multiple property document), NPGallery (National Park Service), https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/64501237.pdf (accessed 2026-10-09).
