Cinder Prairie is a former lookout site in Douglas County, Oregon, on the Umpqua National Forest, about 27 miles south of Oakridge.[^1][^3] A survey alidade was established at the site around 1927.[^2] By 1929 a shelter stood about 150 feet east of the lookout point. It was a pole frame covered with shakes, roughly 16 by 12 feet, with a dirt floor, and cooking was done over a campfire outside.[^2] The lookout house came in 1933, when the Civilian Conservation Corps built an L-4 cab there.[^1][^2][^3]

Local newspapers recorded a few of the people who worked it. In July 1931 the News-Review of Roseburg reported that Dean Paulson was in charge of the lookout.[^2] In 1953 a Reed College student, Bob Housley, was hired by the Forest Service for the summer to work the lookout.[^2] In August 1958 another item said Joe Sand was stationed there.[^2]

The cab's story ends in 1966. The register says it was removed that year, but firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^3] The two sources agree on the year and disagree on how the cab left the site.

I found no source that describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout today.[^1] A 1953 Forest Service survey description places a survey disk set in a drill hole in a small boulder, 10 feet northeast of the northeast corner of the lookout house and stamped "CINDER PRAIRIE L.O. 1938."[^2] Whether that disk is still there is not described. The same text puts the spot three hours by horse pack from Toketee Falls, by way of the Cinder Prairie Trail.[^2] That description dates from 1953, so check the route with the Umpqua National Forest before you go.
