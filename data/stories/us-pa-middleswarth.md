The Middleswarth Tower went up in 1923 on the highest point of Jack's Mountain in Snyder County.[^1][^2] A newspaper report dated April 26, 1923 describes a steel observation tower 60 feet tall, sitting 2,127 feet above sea level, and says it took a record sixteen hours to raise.[^2] The register of former lookouts gives the same year and calls it a steel Blaw-Knox tower.[^1]

Men from the Harrisburg office of the Forestry Department were on the crew of seven, alongside local rangers and towermen.[^2] The newspaper named Rosswell E. Erb of Troxelville as towerman, who would be on duty from 6:30 in the morning. It also said a telephone line to Troxelville was almost complete, so that fires could be reported quickly.[^2]

The register lists the tower as removed before 2005.[^1] A Wikipedia article on the Snyder-Middleswarth Natural Area says a fire tower built just west of the park was abandoned, with only its foundations left by 1992.[^3] That article does not name the tower.[^3] The 1923 report places the Middleswarth tower just south of the park, so the two may be the same, but no source we read confirms it.[^2][^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
