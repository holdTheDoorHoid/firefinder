Round Top Mountain Lookout stands inside Dinosaur National Monument, in Moffat County, Colorado, at about 8,575 feet.[^1][^2]

The current lookout is a two-story metal structure, about 13 feet square, with attached living quarters.[^1][^2] It replaced the older tower and cabin on the same site in 2002.[^1][^2][^3]

The register's build-year field says 1964, but its own description and the Park Service history both date the current structure to 2002.[^1][^3] The first lookout on the mountain was an 8-by-10-foot plywood cabin, put up around 1951 and added to in the following years.[^1][^3] In the mid-1950s a wooden tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab was added. The register's text calls it an 8-foot tower, but its photo caption gives 16 feet tall, which matches the Park Service account.[^1][^3]

According to the Park Service, staff have worked the lookout each season since the early 1950s.[^3] Named keepers include John and Jean Polhamus in 1953, Steve and Verona Rasmussen in 1969, and Phil Schultz, who had worked the lookout for 21 seasons by 2010.[^3] The sources read do not describe any notable fires the lookout spotted.

The register lists the lookout as registered on January 23, 2010, on a nomination by Sondra Kellogg.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association put the tower forward that January, and the Park Service reported the honor.[^3]

An undated Park Service overlook page describes the lookout as staffed in summer, when thunderstorms bring in reports of smokes from lightning.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, with a two-story cab.[^5] None of the pages read say whether visitors may climb it or drive up to it. Anyone planning a visit should check with the monument first.
