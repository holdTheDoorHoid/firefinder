The Matchwood lookout stood in Gogebic County, Michigan, inside the Ottawa National Forest.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page records a 110-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, erected in 1936.[^1] A 1977 newspaper report, reprinted on that page, says the structure had stood over the Marenisco Township landscape since 1936.[^1]

The report blamed newer ways of spotting fires, such as airplanes, for the tower's end, and it said the tower would be dismantled.[^1] The Forest Service hoped to keep the cab and about 20 feet of the steel frame, which were to go to the Sylvania Visitors Center as an exhibit on early fire detection. The remaining steel was to be offered for sale.[^1]

The article describes plans in the future tense, so it does not show that the work was finished or where the cab ended up.[^1] No source we read describes what remains at the site today or whether the spot is open to visitors.[^1]

[^1]: Matchwood, Forest Lookouts (reprints a May 19, 1977 article from The Wakefield News), https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/matchwood.html (accessed 2026-10-10).
