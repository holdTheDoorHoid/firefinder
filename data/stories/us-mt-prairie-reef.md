Prairie Reef Lookout sits about 30 miles west-northwest of Augusta, on the highest point of a bare-topped mountain in the Lewis and Clark National Forest.[^2][^3] The site was first developed with a camp lookout in the 1920s.[^1] An L-4 cab went up in 1931,[^1][^2] and a newspaper item dated 9 July 1931 says the Forest Service was building a station there, with materials carried in by pack horse.[^3]

The present cab is an R-6 flat cab, built in 1968.[^1][^2] The register and firelookout.com both give the 1931 and 1968 dates, but they describe two different cabs on the site, so the dates do not contradict each other.[^1][^2]

Staffing has been part of its story. A 1940 newspaper item mentions a lookout at the station, and in August 1984 a University of Montana student served there as a volunteer lookout.[^3] The register says the present cab is staffed every summer.[^1] A KTVH feature says the Helena-Lewis and Clark National Forest staffs five lookouts, Prairie Reef among them, though the copy I read carries no date.[^4]

I found no source that says who built the 1968 cab or what it cost. None of these sources reports a fire or a move at the site. They describe it as a working lookout rather than an abandoned one.[^1][^4]

None of the sources I read describes a road or trail to the site, or says whether the public may visit. Check with the Forest Service before planning a trip.
