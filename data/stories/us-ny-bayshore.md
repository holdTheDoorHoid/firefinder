In June 1945, the Long Island State Park Commission announced that the Conservation Department would improve fire protection on Long Island, including a new fire tower near Bay Shore.[^2]

The register dates the tower to 1948 and describes an Aermotor LS-40 on concrete footings, with a steel frame and wooden steps and landing platforms.[^1] The Long Island page calls it an 80-foot tower built by the Conservation Department.[^3] It stood on a sand pit on Department of Transportation property, on Fifth Avenue near Brentwood Road, in the community of Brentwood.[^3]

The Long Island page says it was the first and only structure at Bay Shore, and that it operated from 1948 until 1959.[^3] The page gives the reasons the Department cut back its forest fire work: a growing population and industry on Long Island, many well-equipped volunteer fire departments, and fewer woodlands.[^3] In October 1959 the state dropped its lookout services, and the title passed to Suffolk County.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed in 1960 by the state's Bureau of Forest Fire Control.[^1] The Long Island page also lists it as removed.[^3]

The register says the land is now privately held.[^1] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, including the footings.[^1] Because the land is private, ask the owner before going onto it.
