Pump Station Fire Tower stands in Tiadaghton State Forest in Lycoming County, Pennsylvania, at roughly 655 meters elevation.[^1][^2] The tower now on the site is not the first: a wood-and-tree lookout went up here in 1914, and in 1919 the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry replaced it with the 60-foot (18-meter) steel tower that still stands today, built by the Aermotor Windmill Company at a cost of $939.[^1][^2] The observer's cabin atop the tower was rebuilt in 1935.[^1]

For years the tower would have been staffed through fire seasons by state lookout observers watching for smoke, though no source here records who held the post or when staffing ended. By the time Jack Kelley climbed up to inspect it for the National Historic Lookout Register in April 2017, the tower had gone unstaffed for at least a decade.[^1] Kelley described it as "intact but decayed": broken glass scattered across a shaky cab floor, a map table reduced to a few boards, and faint inscriptions from the 1920s still visible on the cab walls under fallen debris.[^1] Tree growth has closed in around the cab, blocking most of the views the tower was built for.[^1]

The site carries no sign identifying it and nothing marks the way in, so a visitor unfamiliar with the location would likely walk past it.[^1] A former observer's cabin near the tower is part of a leasing program and may be occupied; it is private, not open forest land, and should be treated as such.[^1] The tower itself was added to the National Historic Lookout Register, nominated by Henry Isenberg, in July 2016.[^1] As of the most recent inspection on file it remains standing but deteriorating.[^1]
