Bloomer Hill is in Butte County.[^1] Newspapers put it 14 miles northeast of Oroville in 1924 and 20 miles from Oroville in 1925.[^3] A July 1924 report planned a 70-foot station on the crest, but the 1925 reports describe a 60-foot steel tower, built under a co-operative agreement between Butte County, the state forester and the U.S. Forest Service.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it an Aermotor Company product, believed erected in 1925, and says the agencies cooperated to finance and build it.[^1] A July 1925 county contract covered the cost of erecting a $1,300 lookout.[^3] State Forester M.B. Pratt gave the dedication speech at the station in June 1925.[^3] Sources disagree on the summit height: a June 1925 paper gave 3,040 feet, while the NHLR gives 3,005 feet.[^1][^3]

Lookout staff appear in the local papers. George Cunha was the forest service lookout in 1929, and Roy Fox was back on duty in 1930.[^3] Cunha was on the hill again in 1932 and 1933.[^3] Telephone lines reached the station in stages: a link to the Great Western Power Company at Las Plumas by 1926, and a 22-mile line to Oroville in 1931.[^3] In 1939 CCC crews were building a line from the Brush Creek ranger station up to the lookout.[^3] The newspaper transcripts focus on upkeep, phone lines and small incidents, such as a thief who made an improvised bed in the tower in 1932.[^3]

The cab was modified in 1959, and in 1973 the CDF replaced the CCC-era residence.[^1] The NHLR notes that the agency's Aermotor cabs all received catwalks, new windows and access doors.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list still shows the tower as standing.[^2] The NHLR registered it on October 15, 2019, and lists the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection as its administrator.[^1]

No current access or rental notice turned up in these sources, so check with the administering agency before any visit, and never climb a tower that is closed or private.
