Mudlick Mountain sits inside Sam A. Baker State Park in Wayne County, Missouri.[^2] The park was set up in 1927, and a Civilian Conservation Corps crew built much of its facilities in the 1930s.[^1] By July 1935 a newspaper item already described a steel tower on one of the park's high points as a post from which rangers could keep an eye on the park and report fires.[^2] We found no readable source that gives the year the tower was built. A 1947-48 state manual names the Missouri Conservation Commission as the towersite's owner.[^2]

Sources do not agree on the height. Two state annual reports give 85 feet, and a 1938 newspaper item describes a ranger's 85-foot vantage point in the tower.[^2] A 1935 article gives 80 feet, and a 1963 federal survey description puts the overall height at about 100 feet.[^2] That survey also describes a steel tower on four legs, with a closed observation room at the top.[^2] We have used 85 feet because both state reports give it.

In January 1958 a newspaper item reported that a towerman at Mudlick spotted a blaze that consumed eight acres on a Wayne County farm, and that containing it took two hours.[^2] That is the only staffing mention we found, and we found no other notable fire tied to the tower.[^2]

The sources do not say the tower was taken down. The last dated mention we found is a fiscal 1972-73 state report that says the road at the tower was reshaped.[^2] Its condition today is not confirmed.

Today, the Missouri Department of Conservation lists two hiking loops into the natural area: a 1.5-mile Shut-Ins Trail loop and a 12-mile Mudlick Mountain loop.[^1] We found no statement that the tower itself is open to climbers, so check with park staff before going up.[^1]
