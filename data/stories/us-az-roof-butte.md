Roof Butte is the highest peak in the Chuska Mountains, which run along the Arizona and New Mexico border.[^2] Its lookout sits on the Navajo Nation in Apache County, Arizona.[^1][^4]

The register's description calls the lookout one of the newest lookouts on the reservation and gives 1960 in parentheses. That figure is not labeled as a build date, so treat it as probable rather than confirmed.[^1] The register lists the site at 9,633 feet and records its registration on July 20, 2002, as US 440, AZ 36.[^1] Wikipedia gives the butte's elevation as 9,787 feet, so the two figures probably refer to different spots.[^2]

According to the register, the cab is a 7-by-7-foot wooden structure centered on a 14-by-14-foot metal platform, which sits on a 12-foot tower.[^1] The register does not name the material of the tower itself.[^1] It also says the lookout is already surrounded by electronic facilities.[^1]

Wikipedia says a staffed lookout tower stands on the peak, but it gives no date and names no staff.[^2] The sources read record no fire history for the lookout.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^3]

Roof Butte is on tribal land.[^1][^4] The Navajo Nation's park rules close all areas to non-Navajo visitors unless the visitor holds a valid camping, hiking, or backcountry permit from Navajo Parks and Recreation or another duly delegated tribal authority.[^5] The sources read do not say who issues a permit for the lookout itself, so anyone interested should ask the Navajo Nation before going. Do not climb the tower without permission.
