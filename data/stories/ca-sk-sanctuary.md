Sanctuary Fire Tower was one of six lookouts that Prince Albert National Park used to watch for smoke.[^2] The Waskesiu Heritage Museum dates the first five of those towers to 1938. On the museum's list, Sanctuary falls within that first group of five, though the museum gives no separate date for it.[^2] The Firetowers of Saskatchewan list gives Sanctuary's position and includes it among the park's towers.[^1]

Across the park's network, every tower had a single-room cabin at its foot and a pit privy. Power and running water were absent, and a wood stove handled heat and cooking.[^2] The cupola sat on top of the tower. The museum's general description puts the cupola on a tower about 24 m (80 ft) tall, and it gives no height for Sanctuary itself.[^2]

Tower people across the network could be stationed at one tower for up to two weeks, with shifts of eight to ten hours. Every day, a tower person reported to dispatch by two-way radio.[^2]

The park took its towers out of service in 2001 and switched to helicopter smoke patrols.[^2] The cupola now at the Waskesiu Heritage Museum came from the top of a tower the museum calls the Sanctuary Lake tower, just north of Grey Owl's cabin.[^2]

No source reached describes the Sanctuary site today, so it is not known whether the tower or any footings remain, or whether the spot is open to visitors.[^1][^2] The cupola is kept at the museum, where visitors can ask the guide to tour it.[^2] The museum is open daily in July and August, 10 am to 5:30 pm.[^2]
