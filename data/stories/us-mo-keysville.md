Keysville's fire tower stands on an 80-acre towersite about five miles south of Steelville, in Crawford County.[^1][^2] The state bought the land in 1946 and 1947, and the Missouri Department of Conservation's plan says it was bought to help detect and suppress wildfires.[^1][^3]

A newspaper item dated June 28, 1947 listed Keysville among the new towers: a steel tower 85 feet (26 m) tall in Crawford County.[^1] A 1956 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description describes it as a steel structure standing on four legs, with an eight-foot-square house on top.[^1] The same description says the Missouri Conservation Commission owns the tower and gives its height as about 85 feet (26 m).[^1]

The plan says the site was chosen because it sits south of Steelville and north of the Indian Trail tower.[^3] The plan gives the towersite an elevation of 1,140 feet and notes that everything around the site can be seen from the top of the tower.[^3]

The tower remains on the property.[^2] The department says it is used now and then when fire danger runs high.[^2]

The department lists the 80 acres as open to hunting, and a service road gives partial access to the site.[^2] To get there, drive south from Steelville on Route AA for five miles, then turn east onto Tower Road.[^2] The plan calls for regular maintenance so the tower stays safe for lookout staff, and it aims to discourage vandals from damaging the structure and its observation deck.[^3] None of these sources says the public may climb the tower, so this story does not suggest it.
