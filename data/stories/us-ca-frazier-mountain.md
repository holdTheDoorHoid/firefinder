Frazier Mountain Lookout stands on Frazier Mountain in the Los Padres National Forest, in Ventura County, California, at about 8,000 feet.[^1]

The register gives 1934 as the build year, but its own description says the current structure went up in 1943.[^1] A 2017 post by the Ridge Route Communities Museum says an earlier building burned in 1952, and the present one was brought in from Santa Barbara that year.[^2] A 2021 trip report says the wooden tower was built in 1936 and moved to the mountain in 1952.[^3] A 2008 photo report says the structure may date from 1934, though USGS data shows a building there in 1926.[^4] The sources do not agree on the date.

The register says a lookout was on the summit before 1920.[^1] In December 1920 a newspaper described Mrs. Mary Smith, reportedly the only woman lookout in the Forest Service, as living in a station on the summit of Frazier Mountain.[^5]

In February 1932, while searchers looked for a lost airliner, a Fresno Bee item described the station as having food, places to sleep and stoves for rescue parties.[^5] Later that year the lookout began getting its newspapers by air.[^1] The register says a Varney Airlines pilot dropped a San Francisco paper on the way south and a Los Angeles paper on the way back north.[^1] A Forest Service bulletin from August 1932, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said the drops began July 7.[^5]

Laine Davis and his wife were at the station during the 1933 season, and he was assigned there again in 1934.[^5] In August 1956 lookout Lee Hartley reported a fire at the peak, and crews kept the loss to 50 acres.[^5] Tom Fusek came to the tower in 1985, and by 1990 he was spending five days a week in its 64-square-foot room.[^6]

A September 1990 Los Angeles Times article said the tower cost up to $10,000 a year to run and might close after that fire season.[^6] Two later pages say lookout use ended in the 1990s.[^2][^3]

Descriptions I found call the tower abandoned.[^4][^7] A 2008 photo report says it had been left unused for some years and was in poor condition.[^4] Wikipedia says the summit also holds radio towers and an abandoned lookout.[^7] The road is a steep dirt track from the Chuchupate Ranger Station, and a 2011 blog says it is usually open from April to November.[^2][^8] Given that condition, I would not advise climbing the tower.[^4]
