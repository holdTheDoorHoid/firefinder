Hirz Mountain, on the Shasta-Trinity National Forest in Shasta County, California, takes its name from a gold miner who lived along Hirz Creek in the 1860s and was among the earliest white settlers in the McCloud River country.[^1] The mountain's view into the headwaters of the McCloud River and most of its tributaries made it a natural spot for fire detection, and a ground-level lookout structure stood there before the better-known buildings that followed.[^1]

In 1937, the Forest Service and the Civilian Conservation Corps rebuilt the lookout cabin, adding a garage and an outhouse; it was one of the last lookouts the CCC built in California.[^1][^2] The cabin itself is a 14-by-14-foot BC-3 live-in design.[^2] In 1949, crews raised that cabin onto a 20-foot steel K-brace tower with a catwalk, the structure that still stands today.[^1][^2] Sources are consistent on both dates, describing 1937 as the cabin's construction and 1949 as when it was lifted onto the steel tower.

How long Hirz Mountain was staffed as an active fire lookout isn't documented in the sources reviewed here.

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on May 10, 2005.[^2] That same year, it was restored as a Forest Service Centennial project, done in partnership with the Forest Retirees Association and the National Forest Foundation, and it was afterward placed on the agency's cabin rental system.[^2]

Today Hirz Mountain Lookout is bookable through Recreation.gov as a rustic overnight rental. It has two twin beds for two guests, a picnic table, and a composting toilet in a separate building, but no cooking facilities, linens, bedding, water, or electricity, so visitors need to bring their own water and gear.[^1]
