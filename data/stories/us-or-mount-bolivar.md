Mount Bolivar is the highest point in Coos County, and a fire lookout stood on its summit from 1914 until 1958.[^4][^1]

The first lookout was a shake cabin, 12 feet square, completed by November 1914.[^1][^3] The summit is solid rock, and each corner was anchored to it with telephone wire.[^3] A guard carried loads up the steep slope every time he went to the lookout.[^3]

In June 1914 District Ranger R.I. Helm planned to train Guard Price on the map and sighting device.[^3] That same month a small smoke appeared while a fire finder was being installed there, and it died out on its own.[^3] By August 1914 the lookout had reported 22 fires, eight inside the forest and fourteen outside.[^3]

A 1,600-gallon water tank went up for the lookout in 1915, but the next spring a snow slide carried it about 300 feet down the hillside.[^3] In 1928 a D-6 style house replaced the shake cabin.[^1][^3] Most of its materials came from Grants Pass and were carried about 20 miles on horseback.[^3]

In 1934 Andrew Flynn, who had spent six months as a clerk at CCC district headquarters, was given a Forest Service lookout post on Bolivar near Powers.[^3] By 1941 the station was staffed for 120 days, reporting through the Powers Ranger Station.[^3] In 1942 the summit served briefly as an Aircraft Warning Service post, active from March 10 to July 16, with the Roseburg Filter Center listed.[^3]

In 1955 the Forest Service was getting ready to receive bids for a new lookout building, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites register says a 10-foot L-4 tower went up that year.[^3][^1][^2] The Oregon lookouts history dates the new house to 1957.[^3] All three sources date its removal to 1958.[^1][^2][^3]

Today the Forest Service's Mt. Bolivar Trail #1259 runs 1.4 miles one way to the summit, which the agency says was once home to a fire lookout.[^4] A plaque on the summit, a gift from Venezuela, dates from a 1984 ceremony.[^4] No source we found describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout building.[^4] Only hikers may use the path, which runs through the Wild Rogue Wilderness, and the drive to the trailhead goes through Powers.[^4]
