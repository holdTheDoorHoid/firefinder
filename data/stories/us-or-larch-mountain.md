Larch Mountain, in Multnomah County, had a summit lookout from the mid-1910s until 1976.[^1][^2][^3]

The first sat on two fir trees, a platform and cab that the register dates to 1914. Firelookout.com and the 1915 newspapers give 1915.[^1][^3][^2] A June 1915 paper said the tower was finished except for its top platform, and an October item called it recently erected.[^2] An April 1915 plan called for a tower of huge timbers cut on site, with a view in every direction.[^2] By January 1924 the two fir trunks had weakened by decay, and visitors had been kept off the tower the summer before.[^2]

In November 1923 materials for an 85-foot steel tower were stored on the mountain for erection the next spring.[^2] The register gives 1923 for a 90-foot steel tower with a metal cab and a ground house. Firelookout.com gives 1924, and the Forest Lookouts chronology lists a 90-foot Aermotor tower with a steel cab, also in 1924.[^1][^3][^2] A 1947 federal survey description says the tower stood about 100 feet.[^2]

In the 1940s a timber tower with an L-4 cab and a public observation deck below it went up. Sources give its height as 84, 90 or 100 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

A 1920 newspaper noted that no one was kept on the summit in winter.[^2] In 1925 Mrs. W.M. Arrington was appointed state fire warden, the first woman so appointed in the district, and she was based at the summit cabin.[^2] Wilbur Harrison was the lookout in 1934 and 1935 and was still there in 1937. The name is spelled Wilbert in the 1934 and 1937 items.[^2]

From October 1942 to October 1943 Larch was an Aircraft Warning Service post, station Tare 2-9. When the post closed, the Forest Service kept the site for fire detection.[^2]

The structure was removed in 1976.[^1][^2][^3]

Today the summit is a public day-use area.[^4][^5] Larch Mountain Road, run by Multnomah County, closes seasonally from November to May or June.[^5] The picnic grounds have about 10 tables. The paved Sherrard Point Trail (#443) is 0.2 miles long, with 100 steps leading from the picnic area up to a viewpoint.[^4][^5] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit today.
