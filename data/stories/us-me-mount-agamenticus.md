Mount Agamenticus, also known as Aggie, is in the Town of York in York County, Maine.[^1] The state's forest agency put up the first lookout tower on the summit in 1918, a 24-foot steel tower erected with help from the U.S. Forest Service.[^2]

The 1920 season ran from late April to the end of September, and the watchman reported thirty-six fires that year.[^2] The Forest Service paid the watchman's salary under a Weeks Law agreement.[^2] In 1963 Edward Cahoon of North Berwick was named to man the tower.[^2]

In the fall of 1934 the old tower was dismantled, extended by 15 feet to 40 feet, and moved to Bear Mountain in Hartford, Oxford County. A new 47-foot steel tower with stairways went up on the summit in its place.[^2] The register agrees that a 47-foot tower replaced the first one in 1934, and says that tower was itself replaced in 1981 by a 65-foot tower, with a catwalk added in 1985.[^1]

On July 23, 1950, a blaze believed to have been started by blueberry pickers spread through dry brush and drew help from outlying towns.[^2] In April 1974 the tower spotted a woods fire along a power line in the High Pine section.[^2]

The register's build-year field reads 1985, though its own description puts the first tower in 1918.[^1] A Maine Historical Society photo inventory, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says a new cab was airlifted to the tower in 1981.[^2] The tower was listed in the register on August 22, 1994, which described it as active and staffed by the Town of York Fire Department.[^1]

The cab came off in 2019 because it was in very poor shape.[^3] A restoration project that began in 2014 has had its ups and downs.[^3] In December 2025 the steel was removed, because the footings and steel had deteriorated to the point that the tower was no longer safe.[^3] A steering committee has been formed to explore whether another tower should be built, what it would do, or some other course of action.[^3]

We found no source that says what, if anything, remains on the summit today or whether the site is open to visitors. Donations and grants raised for the project are still held in a fund for any future preservation or restoration work.[^3]
