A state fire tower once stood on Vulcan Hill, a timbered rise about half a mile north of the village of Vulcan in Dickinson County, Michigan.[^1]

On 25 May 1939 the Ironwood Daily Globe reported that the state conservation department was erecting a new tower on the hill to give more fire protection to that part of the district. The paper said it would be 125 feet (38 m) high, of the straight-ladder type, and in use about two weeks later.[^1] A Michigan fire-tower history site gives the height as 149 feet (45 m) and calls the builder unknown, so the two accounts disagree on height.[^2]

A national survey station on the hill, described in 1954, sits at the site of the lookout tower, so the tower stood there that year.[^1] A 1984 survey note calls the tower abandoned and says the site then belonged to the Brair Mountain ski resort.[^1]

The sources we read say nothing about what remains on the hill today.[^1] The 1984 note names a ski resort as owner, so check who owns the hill now before going onto it.[^1]
