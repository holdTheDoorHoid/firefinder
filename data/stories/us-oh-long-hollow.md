The Long Hollow lookout stood on High Knob, at the head of Long Hollow in Lawrence County, Ohio, inside the Wayne National Forest.[^1][^2] By April 1942 it was already a working tower.[^2] A local newspaper reported that the Forest Service had received an enclosed cab for it, and that Lester Massie was the lookout posted there.[^2]

The article described Long Hollow and Telegraph Hill as secondary towers, staffed only when fire hazard was high.[^2] Their open platforms gave the lookouts no shelter from the weather.[^2] The new cab was meant to give the radio and other equipment better protection, and to keep the general public off the towers.[^2] Civilian Conservation Corps men from Camp Vesuvius were to build it, and Forest Service suppression crews were to help when they were not fighting fires.[^2]

In a 2017 Ohio Cooperative Living article, Don Karas of the Ohio Department of Natural Resources described the wider program. Karas said the CCC built 11 steel-frame fire towers in southeast Ohio within an 18-month span in 1933 and 1934. The article adds that the corps put up eight more between 1934 and its 1942 disbandment.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Ohio list now marks the Long Hollow tower as gone.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register entered the site on September 3, 2014, as US 1565, OH 11, and gives its elevation as 1,000 feet (305 meters).[^1]

No source I read describes what remains on the hilltop today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^4]
