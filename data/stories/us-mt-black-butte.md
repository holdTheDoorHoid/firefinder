Black Butte sits in the Kootenai National Forest, about three miles southwest of Eureka, Montana.[^2] The first tower here was a 35-foot pole tower of the L-4 design, built in 1939. Before that, the spot was used for patrols in the 1920s.[^1][^2]

A man named Stanley Hanson was working the lookout for the Forest Service by 1948, according to a newspaper item quoted on the Montana Forest Lookouts site.[^3] In 1955 a spotter reported a fire on Dodge Creek and labelled it a smoker fire.[^3] The blaze stayed under a hundredth of an acre, and a tanker truck put it out.[^3] In 1956 a "gap filler" radar station was finished at the lookout.[^3] In 1957 a lookout was still manning the site, and the newspaper said it was the sole staffed lookout near Rexford.[^3]

The register says an 8-by-8-foot cab was set on top of the radar installation in 1973, and it had no living quarters.[^1][^2] The current tower, 45 feet tall, all-steel and live-in, went up in 1983.[^1][^2] The register adds that someone staffs it every summer.[^1]

The sources differ on two details. The register places the lookout in Flathead County, while the other three list Lincoln County.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register gives its elevation as 6,841 feet, but firelookout.com gives 4,064 feet.[^1][^2]

A locked gate stops public motor vehicles at the foot of the service road.[^5] A visitor in July 2022 walked up from the gate.[^5] That visitor saw a sign listing hours of 9 AM to 6 PM with no opening on Wednesdays or Thursdays, and found the tower unmanned that day, so climbing ended at the last flight of steps beneath the balcony.[^5] That sign detail is from one visit, so check with the Kootenai National Forest before you go, and do not climb the tower when it is closed or unstaffed.
