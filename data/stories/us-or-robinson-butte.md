Robinson Butte rises to 5,864 feet in Jackson County, Oregon, on land now part of the Rogue River-Siskiyou National Forest.[^1][^3] A lookout was stationed there by 1913, according to a fire-plan history quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, though the Register and firelookout.com date the start of the site to a camp in 1917.[^4][^1][^2]

The first tower was a 20-foot pole structure with an L-4 cab, built in 1933.[^1][^2] The Roaming Civic credits those early buildings to a Civilian Conservation Corps crew from Moon Prairie.[^5] A 1932 item, though, reports a lookout house under construction, so the early dates are not fully settled.[^4] In 1974 the pole tower was replaced by a 53-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 flat cab, trucked in from Blue Rock Lookout.[^1][^2][^4]

Observers were posted on the butte from the 1930s into the 1940s.[^4] A 1933 item says a couple stayed in the lookout all summer.[^4] A 1935 item reports that lightning stunned a lookout fireman's wife while she was on a forest service telephone and her husband was off at a nearby fire.[^4] For several years before 2016, the Oregon Department of Forestry's southwest Oregon district staffed the tower.[^4] None of the sources read names a particular fire the lookout helped catch.

In 2016 the tower failed an inspection.[^4][^5] Staff left it, a condemned notice was hung across the stairs, and the first set of steps was removed.[^4][^5] The Roaming Civic and Cheryl Hill's directory both list it as abandoned but still standing.[^5][^3] firelookout.com says the replacement tower is available for emergencies. That page carries no date and does not mention the 2016 inspection.[^2]

Treat Robinson Butte as closed. The Roaming Civic lists the tower as condemned as of 2016, and its 2022 visit report describes missing steps and an ominous note.[^5] The Register names the Forest Service as administrator, so that office is the place to check the site's current status.[^1]
