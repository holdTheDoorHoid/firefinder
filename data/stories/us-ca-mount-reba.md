The Mount Reba lookout was a trailer, not a tower. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes it as a mobile R1M lookout that was towed to the site as one finished unit, and it dates the lookout to 1967.[^1] The site is in Alpine County, in the Stanislaus National Forest, at 8,849 feet (2,697 meters).[^1] The register's own description gives the elevation as 8,420 feet and places the lookout on the Calaveras Ranger District, so the two figures do not match.[^1]

The register says the lookout was active until the early 1970s, and adds that little else is known.[^1] A photograph taken in 1969, held in the Stanislaus National Forest archive, shows the portable trailer at the site.[^1] We found no source that names who staffed it or lists any fire it watched.

The register gives its removal only as "Mid-1970's," so the trailer was probably taken away in the mid-1970s.[^1] The only other source we found is a survey record reproduced on the Forest Lookouts site. Caltrans described that survey station in 1972. It places the station on the top of a long ridge, near a portable Forest Service lookout known as Mt. Reba Lookout, about 0.7 mile east of the Mount Reba ski area.[^2]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit it. Anyone planning a trip should check with the Stanislaus National Forest first.
