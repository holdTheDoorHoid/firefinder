A Georgia tower list calls this lookout Saggus and gives its elevation as 605, with no unit stated.[^1] It was Taliaferro County's first fire tower, dedicated in early December 1957.[^2][^3] The county had come under Georgia Forestry Commission fire protection on July 1, a 1957 newspaper item says, and that item describes the tower as giving a view over the whole county.[^2]

The two accounts place the site differently. The newspaper put it four miles northeast of Crawfordville (it spells the town Crawfordsville), while the Commission's December magazine placed it a few miles north of town.[^2][^3]

The festivities opened with parades through Greensboro, Union Point and Crawfordville, and with bands from the high schools of Greene and Taliaferro counties.[^3] Guyton DeLoach, the Commission's director, spoke to 500 people about forestry, and Sara Ann Nelms, a Crawford County queen, christened the tower with a bottle of turpentine.[^3] A barbecue came next.[^3]

At the time the county was protected by the Greene County forestry unit, with ranger H.E. Moore of Greensboro.[^2] The December magazine reports that a fire suppression unit was now stationed in Taliaferro County itself.[^3]

No source I found describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
