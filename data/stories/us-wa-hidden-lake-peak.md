The U.S. Forest Service put up the Hidden Lake Peak lookout in 1931, a gable-roofed L-4 cab set on the summit rock, built by Larsen Bros. Construction as part of a wave of Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie lookouts raised that era.[^1][^2] It did its job as a fire lookout for about three decades, staffed into the 1950s, before the Forest Service pulled staffing from it and many other North Cascades lookouts in 1958.[^1][^3]

The building might easily have been left to rot after that, as happened to plenty of its neighbors. Instead, in 1961 a doctor, Fred Darvill, and the Skagit Alpine Club stepped in and got a special use permit from the Forest Service to maintain it.[^1] That volunteer stewardship is the reason the cab is still standing today rather than another cleared site like nearby Anderson Butte, whose 1930s lookout was torn down in 1964.[^4]

The lookout was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1987 and to the National Historic Lookout Register in 1996.[^2][^1] It sits on a popular hiking route in the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, right at the edge of North Cascades National Park, and today functions as an emergency hiking shelter rather than a staffed fire station.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register's page on the tower lists it as available for rental, though other sources describe it simply as an open shelter for hikers, so it's worth checking current Forest Service guidance before planning on more than drop-in emergency use.[^1][^3] It is not staffed or watched; hikers who reach it should treat it as the aging, volunteer-maintained structure it is.
