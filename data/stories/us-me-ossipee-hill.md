Ossipee Hill rises in Waterboro, in York County.[^1] A plan for a lookout there stalled in 1916 and 1917, because the owner of the summit wanted too high a price for the site.[^2] A steel tower 24 feet tall went up in the fall of 1918.[^3][^2]

In 1920 the lookout opened in late April, and a watchman stayed on duty until the end of September. Twenty-six fires were reported that year.[^2]

In October 1947 a wildfire overran the mountain. The historical marker says the flames destroyed the tower and drove the watchman out.[^1][^3] A 1947 newspaper item says the fire spotter had to leave his post as the blaze closed in.[^2] The sources also name different men: the marker says Mike Ferguson, and the newspaper says Donald Ferguson.[^3][^2]

The tower was rebuilt in wood in 1948. It is 30 feet tall, with a 13-by-13-foot cab.[^1][^3]

After state funding ended in 1991, volunteers from the area manned the tower.[^3]

The register dates the listing to November 25, 1996. The marker says the tower was accepted at the 1997 summer conference of the Forest Fire Lookout Association, so the two dates differ.[^1][^3]

The tower was closed for the 2016 fire season because there were too few volunteers.[^4]

In 2019 the Forest Fire Lookout Association awarded a $500 grant for materials to repair braces and cross members in the wooden frame.[^5] In late May 2019 the fire chief said vandals had hit the tower several times in recent months, and firefighters spent a morning repairing damage to the steel lock on the door.[^6]

In October 2024 volunteers replaced some of the lower leg ties, using lumber donated by Moulton Lumber in Cornish.[^7] The tower steward, Ken Kennedy, opened and closed the tower that year.[^7]

Alfred CCC camp work included a foot trail of about 0.4 miles from the parking area to the tower, according to a 1941 newspaper summary of the camp's work.[^2]
