The Powellville Fire Tower stood in Wicomico County, Maryland, and went up in 1943, during the Second World War.[^1] The United States Navy paid for it.[^1] The steel tower was meant to spot forest fires and also to serve as a warning post for aircraft and watercraft.[^1] At 140 feet, the National Historic Lookout Register calls it the tallest tower in Maryland, and it lists the Maryland Forest Service as its administering agency.[^1]

Maryland's own history says that as the state became more populated and systems such as 911 appeared, fire towers were no longer needed in many formerly rural areas, and many towers were taken down in the 1950s and 1960s.[^3]

The register added the tower on August 23, 2017.[^1] At that time, Maryland's Department of Natural Resources planned to remove it.[^1] A Forest Fire Lookout Association list of Maryland towers now marks the Powellville tower as gone.[^2]

None of the sources checked describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can visit it.[^1][^2]
