The Daggett lookout stood on Daggett Hill, just east of Simmons Settlement in Mackinac County, Michigan, inside the Hiawatha National Forest.[^1] An October 1938 newspaper report described it as a newer design, 120 feet (36.6 m) tall, with a stairway protected by handrails.[^1] Ranger DeGraff said that on a clear day the view from the top took in St Martins Bay, the Les Cheneaux Islands and the Pine and Carp river valleys.[^1]

A towerman at the Daggett tower near East Lake died suddenly in June 1939.[^1]

A federal survey description from 1965 calls the tower a steel structure on four legs, with an observation room at the top, on U.S. government land.[^1]

In September 1966 a newspaper reported that aerial fire detection had replaced three Mackinac County towers: Moran, Maple Hill and Daggett.[^1] The same report said current plans kept the Moran and Maple Hill towers for emergency detection.[^1] The Daggett tower was being dismantled, and the report said it would be set up again in Missouri for fire detection.[^1] No other source found confirms the Missouri site.

A map database lists a point called Daggett Lookout Tower in Marquette Township, but its entry gives no dates or history.[^2] No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
