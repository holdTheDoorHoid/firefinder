Cliff Ridge sits east of Chewelah in Stevens County, Washington, at 3,785 feet.[^1][^3]

In 1937 the lookout went up under the Resettlement Administration.[^4] Its tower was 45 feet high, with an L-4 cab on top.[^4] That July the Colville Examiner quoted a Mr. Whiting saying the lookout covered about 70 percent of the district, and that a lookout with 50 percent coverage counted as very good.[^3]

The Colville Examiner reported in May 1939 that lookouts had been installed on Cliff Ridge and Huckleberry Mountain as part of the Kaniksu ranger program.[^3] Willhite says a phone line was installed that year.[^4] Willhite also says that in 1938 timberland the Resettlement Administration had managed passed to the Kaniksu National Forest, which had only just been formed.[^4] In 1940 the Kaniksu Forest Service assigned Ray Bailey to the lookout.[^3][^4]

The sources disagree on how the lookout ended. Willhite says it was destroyed in the 1950s.[^4] The Register says it was removed in that decade,[^1] and firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^2]

Today Willhite's photos show footings below the summit, at the old lookout site, and a directional benchmark is set in one of them.[^4] One footing is almost under the dirt.[^4] No source we read describes a standing structure at the site.

Willhite's directions run north on Sand Canyon Road from Chewelah, then onto Major Road into the National Forest, and about a mile past a gated road to the start of the trail.[^4] The hike is 1.3 miles each way with about 600 feet of gain.[^4] The old summit road has become an overgrown trail, and Willhite warns that the route will be lost without more trail work.[^4]
