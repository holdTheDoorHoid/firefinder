Obermayer (Shagnasti) Mountain stands at 5,367 feet in the Kootenai National Forest, about 11 miles north-northwest of Yaak in Lincoln County.[^1] The lookout website dates the site to 1921, when a crow's nest with an 8-by-8-foot log cabin is recorded there.[^1]

The site is named in honor of a lookout whose name the sources spell differently. The website gives it as Antone Obermayer and says he was killed in the 1931 Keno Mountain fire.[^1] A federal lessons-learned page calls him Anton Obermayer, a 22-year-old lookout who was killed while fleeing the Deer Creek Fire, and dates his death to August 27, 1931.[^3] The same page says a falling tree struck him while he hiked to safety. A search party was sent out the following morning, and his body was found under the tree.[^3]

A 1931 newspaper report, quoted on a page about Yaak Mountain, says a falling tree killed him as he fled his station. It adds that forest officials had ordered him to abandon the station as the fire approached.[^4] The report is dated August 27 and says the death happened "last night," so it and the federal page disagree about which night it was.[^3][^4]

The website says the lookout was abandoned in the 1940s.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as abandoned, with the type crow's nest.[^2] The website also says the log cabin was "still there," but that remark is undated.[^1] No source we read describes what remains at the site today or whether it is open to the public.
