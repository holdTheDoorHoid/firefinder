The Liberty Hill tower stands in San Jacinto County and is listed under the Sam Houston National Forest on the Forest Lookouts pages.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list also places it in San Jacinto County, but labels it a USFS tower.[^4]

A historical marker erected by the US Forest Service says the tower was built to guard a forest that the Civilian Conservation Corps and the Forest Service had planted. It also says the tower was in use from 1955 to 1969.[^3] The sources read do not give a construction date, a height or the tower's design, so those details remain unknown.

The marker says the forest then switched to airplanes to spot wildfires.[^3] It also calls the tower the last remaining fire tower on Texas's national forests and grasslands. That is the marker's own claim, and the sources read do not check it.[^3] No notable fires or lookout keepers appear in the sources read.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list shows the tower as standing.[^4] The same list shows a separate Liberty Hill site in Polk County, called Liberty Hill (State), as gone, so the two should not be confused.[^4]

The sources read do not say whether the public may visit the grounds or climb the tower. Check with the Sam Houston National Forest before planning a trip, and keep off any tower that is closed or posted off limits.
