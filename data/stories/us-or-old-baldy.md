In 1924 the Forest Service put up a cupola-style lookout house on Old Baldy, a 6,340-foot peak in Klamath County, about 22 miles east-northeast of Ashland.[^1][^2][^3] The register lists Klamath FPA as the administering office.[^1] Floyd Murray and others cut the materials for the house at a Medford warehouse.[^3] It sat on an enclosed tower about ten feet tall and replaced an older lookout tree on Brush Mountain.[^3]

The names in the papers differ from one item to the next.[^3] A June 1924 newspaper item said Stan Peterson was stationed there again that summer.[^3] A September 1924 item called the lookout Dan Pederson, and an October 1925 item said Dan Peterson had held Brush Mountain or Old Baldy for thirteen consecutive years.[^3] In 1934 a Medford paper reported that men from a South Fork of the Rogue CCC camp were being sent to Old Baldy to work as lookouts.[^3]

Accounts also differ on how the lookout ended.[^1][^2][^3] The register says the cupola cabin was removed in 1961, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] A lookout history page says the lookout burned in 1961, after it was no longer needed.[^3]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
