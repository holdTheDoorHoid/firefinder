The lookout stood on Cowan Peak in Pacific County, Washington.[^1] Firetower.org lists the site as Cowan Point, at 1,940 feet (591 meters).[^2] It is about five miles north-northeast of Naselle.[^3]

A 20-foot wooden L-4 tower went up in 1947.[^1] Firetower.org says that first tower burned by accident.[^2] In June 1952 the tower was named Cowan Peak, and a newspaper reported it would be run cooperatively by the Washington Forest Fire Association, the State Division of Forestry and the Weyerhaeuser Timber Company.[^4]

In January 1953 a three-day storm hit the area. Willhite says winds of 112 miles per hour demolished the Washington Forest Fire Association lookout on the peak.[^1] A Longview newspaper reported the same in February.[^4] The sources disagree about how the first tower was lost: Firetower.org says it burned, while Willhite and the 1953 newspaper point to the storm.[^2][^1][^4]

A second 20-foot tower with an L-4 cab went up later in 1953.[^1][^2] Willhite says it was named in honor of Major Charles S. Cowan, then manager of the Washington Forest Fire Association.[^1]

In July 1959 William F. Glover, a 21-year-old lookout, died at his station. A newspaper reported he was overcome by heating gas during his first week of work for the state.[^4]

The second tower was abandoned in 1967.[^2] Firetower.org says it has since been removed.[^2] FireLookout.com and Willhite say it was probably destroyed soon after.[^3][^1]

Willhite describes the route as a DNR road that any vehicle can use, ending at a parking area near the site. From there an old road climbs into the timber, and the site is reached on foot. He lists the distance as a quarter mile, with 100 feet of gain.[^1] No source describes a standing structure today. The photo captions list three footings at the site, but his page does not date the visit.[^1]
