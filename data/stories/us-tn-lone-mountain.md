The Tennessee Landforms table lists a Lone Mountain tower in Morgan County at 1,394 feet.[^1] Its row gives no district and tower number, and its status column reads only "sitting," a word the page does not define.[^1]

The Tennessee Division of Forestry (TDF) built 208 fire lookout towers, with construction running from about 1930 to about 1970.[^2] None of the sources gives a build date, design, or height for the Lone Mountain tower.[^1][^2]

Lone Mountain is also the name of a state forest. According to the forest's own page, it was part of Morgan State Forest as the Lone Mountain Section until 1970, when it became a separate state forest.[^3] The page does not say whether this tower stands on that forest.[^3]

Whether the tower still stands is unclear. The 2014 National Register document lists the Morgan County towers that were still standing on March 26, 2014: Burrville, Catoosa, Jones Knob, Pilot Mountain, and Windswept. Lone Mountain is not on that list.[^2] The sources do not explain the word "sitting," so they cannot settle the question.[^1][^2] The Tennessee index of the National Historic Lookout Register does not name the tower either.[^4]

On climbing, the forest's page says fire towers on state forests are not open to the public. Photography is allowed, but climbing is not.[^3] No source ties this tower to the forest, so that rule may not apply here. Do not climb it, and ask the Tennessee Division of Forestry before going to see it.
