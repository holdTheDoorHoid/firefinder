The steel tower now called the Canopy Tower began on the Rainier lookout site in Washington, on the Fort Lewis Military Reservation.[^1] The first tower there was a 100-foot timber tower that the U.S. Army built in 1942.[^1] That timber tower was replaced by a steel tower.[^1] That steel tower is the one that stands today at IslandWood, a learning center on Bainbridge Island.[^2][^4]

Sources give different dates for the steel tower.[^1][^2][^3] Sahale's page says it was erected at Fort Lewis in 1966.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com say a 112-foot steel tower replaced the timber tower in 1968.[^1][^3]

The steel tower was sold and taken down around the turn of the century.[^1][^3] Sahale says the tower was retired in 1998 and sold as scrap, then sat in storage for years.[^2]

IslandWood had the dismantled steel rebuilt, with Sahale LLC doing the work.[^2] Sahale inventoried the old members and built a 3D model from the old plans.[^2] It also added an ADA-compliant helical stairwell through the middle of the structure, and the work was finished in February 2009.[^2] The register says the tower was dedicated under its new name, Canopy Tower, in May 2009.[^1]

Today the tower stands in a wooded valley at the IslandWood Environmental Learning Center.[^2] Sahale gives its height as 118 feet, though the same page also gives 125 feet in another place.[^2] The Washington lookout list marks it as standing.[^4] Sahale says thousands of children use it each year, and the sources do not describe visiting rules.[^2]
