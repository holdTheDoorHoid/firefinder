Martis Peak Lookout stands on a high point north of Lake Tahoe, in Placer County, and the U.S. Forest Service is listed as its administrator.[^1] The Register says the Forest Service built it in 1914 and that it is the only surviving example of a transition design.[^1] Its cab is 12 by 14 feet, a size that needed a short section of gable roof, and the Register notes that a 14-by-14-foot cab with a pyramid roof became the Region 5 standard in 1917.[^1]

The build date is not fully settled. A December 13, 1912 item, reprinted on a Forest Lookouts page, describes a telephone line to Martis lookout and says an iron lookout phone was installed there.[^2] That is two years before the Register's date, so either a lookout stood on the peak earlier or the Register is off. The sources read do not say which.

Newspaper items from 1932 through 1936 place Frank Maher at the Martis Peak lookout station.[^2] One 1932 item says he had been posted there for 15 years, and a 1934 item called it his seventeenth year on the mountain.[^2] The two counts are close but not the same, so the start date is approximate. In July 1927 the lookout spotted smoke that led rangers to a sheepherder who had burned three stumps to scare off coyotes, and a justice of the peace fined the herder $50.[^2]

According to the Register, the site was shut down in the late 1970s and heavily damaged by vandals by the mid-1990s.[^1] Volunteers, the California Department of Forestry and the Tahoe National Forest then restored it.[^1] The sources give no dates for that work.

The Register says staff are back five days a week, and volunteers cover weekends.[^1] A Lake Tahoe history post lists it with the two other lookouts in the basin and says all three are still standing.[^3] The post describes touring them.[^3] Neither source says whether this cab is open to the public, so check with the Tahoe National Forest before planning a visit.
