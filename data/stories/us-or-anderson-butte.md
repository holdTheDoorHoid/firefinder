Anderson Butte lies in the Rogue River National Forest, about nine miles west of Ashland, in Jackson County, Oregon.[^1][^2] The register gives its elevation as 5,195 feet, but a Coast and Geodetic Survey description quoted on the Forest Lookouts page gives 6,193 feet.[^1][^3]

Sources disagree on the year the tower went up. Medford newspapers describe a house on a 30-foot tower: work began on July 31, 1933, and the lookout was completed on September 12, 1933.[^3] Before that, a secondary lookout had been set up on the butte each summer for two years.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both date the L-4 tower to 1935.[^1][^2]

The earliest lookout named in the papers is John Cantrall, who had been employed there as lookout man for some time by October 1931.[^3] Bob Lindsay and his family moved into the lookout in June 1935, and Hugh Shurtleff held the post in 1936 and again in 1938.[^3] The lookout was staffed for 95 days in 1941.[^3] In 1948 control of the lookout passed from the Rogue River National Forest to the Southwest Oregon FPA.[^3] In 1953 the state forestry district placed a lookout on the butte, and in 1961 Mrs. Ida Mae Stone was placed there.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts page records the lookout as removed in 1965.[^3] The register says the tower was removed that year, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2]

In April 2015 a visitor reported that the tower's foundation blocks and a stair pad were all that remained.[^3] The road's entrance is blocked by dirt berms that full-size vehicles cannot get past. The site is about a mile's walk from there. The sources do not say whether visitors may go up the road today.[^3]
