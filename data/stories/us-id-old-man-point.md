Old Man Point was a lookout on the Clearwater National Forest in Idaho.[^2] In 1931 Ranger Roy Lewis first put the point into service, with a tent camp and Bill Dennison as lookout.[^3] That same year, carpenters Earnest Hartman and Harry Chenoweth, helped by Dennison, built a log tower and assembled a pre-cut L-4 cab on it.[^3]

Sources do not agree on the tower's height. Three of them give 10 feet.[^1][^2][^3] Louis Hartig's 1981 history of the Lochsa Ranger District gives 20 feet,[^3] and a 2016 Clearwater National Forest list does too.[^4]

The tower burned in the Pete King Fire in 1934 and was not replaced.[^1][^3][^4] Hartig, as quoted on Forest Lookouts, says the tower burned on August 17, a day or so after the lookout had been pulled to Lochsa Station to fight fires.[^3] A University of Idaho archive record dated August 18, 1934, is a first-person account of the Pete King Fire. It describes the CCC's fight to defend the camp and the Lochsa Ranger Station.[^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the site "Hike-In Burned."[^2] We found no source describing what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.
