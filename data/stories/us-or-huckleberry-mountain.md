Huckleberry Mountain Lookout sits at 5,451 feet in the Willamette National Forest, in Lane County, Oregon.[^1] (The Forest Service's own recreation page gives a different figure, 5,545 feet, for the same site; this story follows the National Historic Lookout Register's number.[^3]) From the lookout, views run from Mount Hood in the north to Mount Scott and Mount Thielsen in the south, with the Sisters and Diamond Peak also visible nearby.[^3] It's an L-4 ground house with a catwalk, but sources disagree on exactly when it went up: the National Historic Lookout Register gives 1934, firelookout.com says 1939, and the Forest Service's own recreation page for the site says 1938 with a hip roof.[^1][^2][^3] All three agree it's an L-4-style structure from the late 1930s; the exact year isn't settled.

The lookout was restored by volunteers and the Forest Service. NHLR dates that restoration to 1990-1991, while firelookout.com gives 1994 and adds that the work made the building handicap-accessible.[^1][^2] The Forest Service's own page describes "excellent condition as a result of a 3-year historic rehabilitation," without giving exact dates, which is broadly consistent with either account.[^3]

Huckleberry Mountain was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 1991, registered as US 26, nominated by Doug Newman.[^1] Unlike many historic lookouts that have been decommissioned, this one is still an active fire lookout, staffed seasonally by the Middle Fork Ranger District.[^3] That means public access is limited while a lookout is on duty.

The site is reached by an easy, half-mile trail, and there's no fee to visit.[^3] The area around the tower is open and grassy, with a rocky promontory to the south once used by the Kalapuya people, and a fragment of the original approach trail running north through old timber and huckleberry brush.[^3] Because it's a working lookout rather than a vacant historic structure, it is not available as an overnight rental; visitors should expect to view it from outside during staffed periods, and should check current Forest Service conditions before visiting, as it has been affected by nearby fire closures in the past.[^3]
