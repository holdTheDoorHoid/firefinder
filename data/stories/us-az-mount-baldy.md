Mount Baldy, known to the White Mountain Apache as Dził Łigai Sí'án, stands on the east side of the Fort Apache Indian Reservation, in Apache County.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as a ground site that is now gone.[^3]

The earliest staffing we found is from 1914.[^4] A St. Johns paper, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, said snow had kept the Supervisor from placing a lookout on the mountain, but that a lookout man was now on the point.[^4] A 1918 item credited to the St. Johns Observer says the Forestry Department hired a Miss Crosby to staff the station on the summit of Mt. Baldy that season. The item names no range, so tying it to this mountain relies on its St. Johns origin. The same item put the altitude at nearly 12,000 feet, while the Peakbagging photo page gives 11,403 feet for the lookout site.[^4][^2]

A survey datasheet dates the building's abandonment to 1958.[^2] A photo taken on June 1, 2004 shows only rubble where the lookout stood.[^2] A survey benchmark also remains.[^2]

The sources disagree about who manages the land. The Forest Lookouts page files the site under the Coronado National Forest.[^4] The Peakbagging page places the lookout on the Fort Apache Indian Reservation,[^2] and the Forest Service says the line between the reservation and the national forest runs part way up the eastern slope.[^1]

The Forest Service says the White Mountain Apache Tribe has designated the area where the sacred mountain stands as a tribal wilderness, and considers various activities that would damage the area off-limits.[^1] We found no source that describes the lookout site after 2004, or explains how a visitor could get permission to go there. Anyone planning a trip should ask the tribe first.
