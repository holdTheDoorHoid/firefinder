Cee Cee Ah (CCA) Peak sits in Pend Oreille County, Washington, about 11 miles north-northeast of Usk, in the Kaniksu and Colville National Forests.[^1][^2]

The first tower, a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, went up in 1933.[^1][^2][^4] A second tower with an L-6 cab followed in 1936, a quarter mile north.[^2][^4] The Forest Lookouts listing also describes two towers about a quarter mile apart.[^3]

The Forest Service pack train brought supplies to the lookout in early June 1935.[^4][^3] Firelookout records lookout personnel for 1952.[^1]

The L-4 was removed in 1955, according to the register and Willhite.[^2][^4] Firelookout says it was destroyed that year.[^1] The L-6 went on display at the Pend Oreille Fair in 1936, before it was set atop the north tower.[^4] In 1984 it went back to the fairgrounds as a museum piece.[^4] Willhite gives no date for what became of the north tower.[^4]

Willhite found the lower site down the south ridge, where a road ended.[^4] He photographed footings there, including a fourth footing that is a bit hidden, and a fire ring.[^4] He found no evidence at the summit, though the area appears flattened, and he thinks the summit is the likely spot for the second tower.[^4]

The road has a gate that stays in place all year.[^4] As of 2023, Willhite says, riders could still cover about 80 percent of the way up by bike, and the hike is 4.4 miles one way with 1,900 feet of gain.[^4] The sources do not say whether the public may pass the gate, so check before you go.[^4]
