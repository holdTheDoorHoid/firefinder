The Paddy Knob tower site sits at 4,479 feet, or 1,365 meters, along the West Virginia and Virginia border, inside the Monongahela National Forest.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places it in Bath County, Virginia, but the Forest Lookouts page lists it in Highland County.[^1][^2]

The tower went up in 1934, built by Civilian Conservation Corps labor.[^1][^2] It was a steel tower with a cab measuring 14 feet by 14 feet.[^1][^2] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as its administrator.[^1]

The final year of staffing was 1967.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts marks it as removed but gives no date for the removal.[^2]

A 1941 federal survey description, quoted on Forest Lookouts, describes a steel structure of roughly 50 feet, topped by a glass-enclosed cab.[^2] The register's figure is 40 feet, so the two sources disagree on the height.[^2][^1]

The register holds a 1935 photograph credited to the National Archives.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, is left on the site now, or whether visitors can reach it. The site lies within a national forest, and the sources do not describe any access rules for it.[^1]
