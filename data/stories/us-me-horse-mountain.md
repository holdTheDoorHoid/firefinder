Horse Mountain lies inside Baxter State Park, and its trail starts about half a mile from the Matagamon Gate.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association registered the former tower site in 2008 and marks the tower as gone.[^2][^3]

By 1919 the Maine Forest Service had a tower house and a cabin on the mountain.[^4] That year a report said an alidade and range finder had been installed there to help locate fires.[^4] A 1920 inventory lists a 12-foot steel tower.[^4]

In June 1919 a lookout and a patrolman built a bedroom onto the cabin. The same report says the watchman gave the tower house a double coat of paint that year.[^4]

In July 1989 a six-man volunteer crew from Wheelabrator Environmental Systems was flown in by a Maine Forest Service helicopter. The crew fitted new windows, a roof and a floor, and painted the steel tower.[^4] The park authority had marked the tower for restoration, with a plan to bring it back into use through its volunteer program.[^4]

The Baxter State Park Authority arranged for the tower's removal in 1999.[^4] The regional register says the tower was pulled down and burned that same year.[^2] The sources agree on the year but differ on how the tower left the summit.[^2][^4]

No tower stands on the summit now.[^5] A hiker who climbed it in September 2024 saw only footings of the old tower at the peak,[^5] and another guide mentions the concrete feet of the former tower.[^1] The only view is from the East Spur Overlook, reached by a 0.4-mile side trail, not from the summit.[^1][^5]
