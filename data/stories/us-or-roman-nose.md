Roman Nose sits on the divide between Lane and Douglas counties, about 18 miles east-southeast of Florence, at roughly 2,856 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

The earliest recorded activity on the mountain is a rag camp in 1917. That same year a fire patrol association moved to establish a lookout station there.[^1][^3] An October 1921 report says the house on the summit was finished.[^3] The register and firelookout.com put the first cupola cabin in 1922.[^1][^2]

Early lookouts named in the papers include L. B. Chruden in 1921, Eugene Callahan in 1925 and Phillip Johnson in 1928. A 1933 item names Veris Dahlin as the lookout, and a September 1941 item says Robert Morrow had been stationed there.[^3]

Work on a replacement started in 1957, and the new house was completed in 1958.[^3] The register calls the 1958 building a concrete base two stories high, topped with an Amort cab, while firelookout.com gives the base as 10 feet.[^1][^2] The cab came off in 2001, and firelookout.com says the Oregon Chapter FFLA salvaged it that year.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com also says the lookout was last staffed in the 1970s.[^2]

The register says the concrete tower is now used as a radio building.[^1] In 2008 the idle site was put back to work hosting a Forest Watch camera detection system.[^3] No source here says whether the site is open to visitors today or what a visitor could see.
