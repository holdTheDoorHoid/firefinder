The Klamath Forest Protective Association put a lookout on Hogback Mountain in 1947, by most accounts.[^2][^3] Forest Lookouts places it about six miles east of Klamath Falls, while Rex's Fire Lookout Page says three miles.[^2][^3] An improvement project included about 1,100 feet of new road to make the station reachable by car.[^2] The sources do not agree on the first tower. The register describes a 41-foot tower built in 1941, the 1947 accounts describe a 40-foot tower, and Rex's Fire Lookout Page gives 36 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

Jack Dale was the new tower's first lookout.[^2] The station had its share of trouble. In 1963 a state radio maintenance man went up to the tower and found a fire guard who had been taken ill. The guard survived.[^2] Vandals did damage in 1963 and again in 1974, when the damage was put at $1,400 to $1,500.[^2] The station logged five fires in 1972.[^2] Rex's Fire Lookout Page says the tower had summer staffing into the 2010s.[^3]

The old lookout was condemned in 1987 and burned to the ground, according to the Forest Lookouts history.[^1][^2] The next year the Oregon Department of Forestry moved a 30-foot steel tower from Sycan Butte near Silver Lake to the site and placed a new cab on top.[^1][^2][^3]

The register says the lookout is in active service and gives its registration date as March 31, 2003.[^1] A directory of Oregon's standing lookouts, dated July 2026, lists Hogback as staffed and accessible to the public.[^4] The directory's access column records whether the site can be legally reached, not whether you can get into the lookout itself.[^4] Rex's Fire Lookout Page places the site on private land and says access is limited.[^3] The sources disagree, so check with the Oregon Department of Forestry before you plan a visit, and do not climb the tower without permission.
