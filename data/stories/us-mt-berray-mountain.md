Berray Mountain is named for brothers Cap and Jim Berray, who reached Noxon in 1887 and later claimed land on the Bull River.[^2] The lookout on the summit sits in Sanders County, Montana, on the Kootenai National Forest, about ten miles north-northwest of Noxon.[^1][^2]

The first structure, dating to 1931, was a crow's nest tower with a log cabin where lookouts lived.[^1][^3] Early on it also had a 20-foot observation platform, and in 1940 a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced that setup.[^2][^3] The current R-6 flat cab on a 30-foot timber tower went up in 1965.[^1][^4]

Staffing came and went over the decades.[^2][^4] The Forest Service says the lookout stayed staffed for fire detection until 1973.[^4] A separate history describes it as staffed through the 1930s and 1940s, only sporadically in the 1950s, and regularly again in the 1960s and early 1970s.[^2] It has seen little use since the 1970s.[^1]

The site joined the National Historic Lookout Register on August 20, 1996.[^1] A Kootenai National Forest news release from August 2015, as quoted on a Montana lookout history page, called the lookout still intact, with its radio repeater still working.[^3]

The Forest Service now says restoration work is underway, aimed at preserving the tower.[^4] The route up is Trail #967 from the Berray Mountain trailhead.[^4] The Forest Service rates it Class 3, bars motor vehicles, and asks hikers to check with the Cabinet Ranger Station for current conditions.[^4] Hikers who reach the peak can see the Cabinet Mountains Wilderness and Scotchman Peaks.[^4] Nothing in these sources says the tower is open to climbing.[^4] With restoration under way, the safer course is to keep off it.
