Holston High Knob is a fire lookout on the southern high knob of Holston Ridge, in Carter County, Tennessee, near the Virginia state line.[^1] The register places it in the Cherokee National Forest, administered by the U.S. Forest Service, and gives its elevation as 4,117 feet.[^1] Wikipedia and a hiking guide put the summit at 4,136 feet, a small difference the sources do not explain.[^3][^4]

According to the register, the tower was first located in Mississippi.[^1] A hiking guide repeats that origin, and no other record turned up in our search, so the story rests mainly on the register.[^4] The register gives the steel tower a height of 100 feet and a cab measuring 12 by 12 feet, with a catwalk.[^1]

In 1943 the tower was moved to Tennessee to take the place of a wooden 60-foot tower that burned by accident.[^1] A hiking guide gives the same year for the move.[^4] The register also says the wooden tower had been built nine years before, which would put it around 1934, though no source gives that year directly.[^1]

The register says staffing ended in 1991, and the tower is now used for emergencies.[^1] A 2004 visit report described it as a regional landmark and said its condition ruled out staffing.[^1]

The biggest change came to the cab. A visit report dated June 8, 2004 found the walls and roof gone and the interior missing, with only the plywood floor left, open to the weather.[^1] Vandals had torn out part of the base fence, including the gate at the foot of the stairs.[^1] They had carried large stones up the tower and dropped them onto a privy roof, destroying it, and had smashed the glass cover of the electric meter at the base.[^1] The wooden stair steps and the catwalk floor had recently been replaced with steel.[^1] Three large dish antennas remained on the tower, along with two empty antenna mounts.[^1] The register does not say when the damage happened.[^1]

Wikipedia's Holston Mountain article places a dismantled Cherokee National Forest fire tower on Holston High Knob and says it is now a communications tower.[^3] The register describes the steel tower in detail, and a Tennessee lookout survey lists Holston High Knob as standing.[^1][^2] We found no later report to settle the question.

As of the 2004 visit, the lookout was unstaffed, and the register found no NHLR sign on the site or on the road up to it.[^1] A hiking guide says the tower cannot be climbed but still offers good views from the summit.[^4] The 2004 report also said there would be interest in volunteer help with upkeep and staffing, if a good program were set up.[^1] Because the register's only visit report is from June 2004, the tower's current condition is unknown.[^1]
