Beacon Pole Hill lies in Cumberland, Rhode Island, in Providence County.[^1][^2] The history we found for the hill is mostly about a colonial beacon, not a fire lookout, so the two should be kept apart.[^3]

The former-lookout register describes a wooden fire tower on the hill that began operating in 1920 and was taken down in 1935.[^1] The listing was registered on September 8, 2008, on the nomination of Henry Isenberg.[^1]

The hill's better-known past is a Revolutionary War beacon that consisted of a 70-foot wooden pole.[^3] In 1897 the Woonsocket chapter of the Daughters of the American Revolution held a ceremony at the hill and had a replica of the original pole built.[^3] The chapter had also set bronze tablets at the site, but vandals removed them.[^3]

According to Land Trust members, the hill holds remnants of one of the colonial beacons.[^2] A 2023 Valley Breeze report describes the Cumberland Land Trust acquiring an 84-acre property off Tower Hill Road, with Beacon Pole Hill at its edge rather than on it.[^2] The Land Trust's longer-term aim, hiking trails with room to park four or five cars, was described in that report as still some way off.[^2]

We found no source that describes any remains of the 1920 tower, and none that says whether the hilltop is open to visitors.
