Nulhedus Mountain sits in Somerset County, near the north line of Township 4, Range 17.[^1] In 1914 the state set up a lookout station on the mountain and put up a 60-foot steel tower.[^1] The forest commissioner's report for that year says the station's telephone was in the house on top of the tower.[^1]

The station had a fire watchman's camp, and a photo from about 1920 shows a Maine Forest Service employee standing beside it.[^2] A 1920 inventory still lists a 60-foot steel tower.[^1] We did not find the names of the watchmen who worked the mountain, or any notable fire on it, in the sources we read.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Nulhedus tower as standing, with an asterisk.[^3] The asterisk is used when a tower keeps its framework and supports after losing the cab.[^4] So the tower still stands, but the cab is gone.[^3][^4]

We found no current information about who owns the land or whether visitors are allowed, and no rental for the site. Until someone confirms the rules, do not climb the tower or walk onto the site.
