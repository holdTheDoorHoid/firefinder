Lander Mountain, also called Landers, is a summit in the Coast Range about 11 miles west of Roseburg.[^2][^1] After the 1913 fire season a 14 x 20 log cabin went up on top for $125, and a state report of 1914 said the view covered the county's north, south and east and that a lookout would be posted there regularly.[^1]

The register dates a 40-foot wooden live-in tower, which replaced the cabin, to 1933.[^3] An August 1935 item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page calls the tower 50 feet and says it was complete and ready for its cabin, which conflicts with the register's date and height.[^1]

Several people named Teller staffed the lookout through the later 1930s. Nancy Teller was there in 1935, and by 1937 she was in her third year with the Douglas fire patrol. In 1936 Henry Teller and his daughter were to be stationed there during the fire season, and in 1939 H.S. Teller and his wife were serving as lookouts.[^1]

A 1939 newspaper said the newly built Callahan road made the lookout reachable, and called it a popular spot for visitors.[^1] In 1949 Ida Beeman spent the summer there, and another lookout was transferred to the site that year.[^1]

Newspaper reports dated August 21, 1951 said the tower and the structure beneath it had burned in the path of the Hubbard Creek fire.[^1] The county's wildfire protection plan counts 15,574 acres burned by the Hubbard Creek Fire, along with one lookout tower and 19 homes, but it does not name Landers.[^4] A News-Review report of August 22 said the burning tower fell and set a fire below the ridge, and that the lookout attendant got out.[^1]

In 1951 the state allocated $4000 from a restoration fund to rebuild the lookout, but a June 1952 report said it would not be reconstructed.[^1]

The register lists the site as gone.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether visitors can reach it.
