The Mount Pleasant tower was a steel Aermotor tower on Mount Pleasant, near the Joe Zerbey Airport in Foster Township, Schuylkill County.[^2][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was built in 1921 and removed in the mid-1990s, and it added the site to the register on September 16, 2008.[^2] A 2015 newspaper gives 1920 as the build year instead.[^4]

Women were among its early staff.[^1] In April 1923 the Bedford Gazette reported that Emma Lord of Mount Pleasant was the first woman in the state appointed a fire warden, and that she would be placed in charge of the tower.[^1] Later that year the Altoona Tribune reported that two women would serve as tower observers, with Miss Lord stationed at Mount Pleasant.[^1] In October 1926 Mrs. Vernie Ringenary was in charge at the tower.[^1]

Sources disagree about its height. The 1923 Altoona Tribune item described a 100-foot steel tower, while the 2013 and 2015 reports say 85 feet.[^1][^3][^4] The later reports describe the tower as it stood near the airport, so this story uses 85 feet (25.9 m).[^3][^4]

In 1977 the county commissioners approved an agreement that let the county put an antenna on the tower.[^1]

The register says the tower was removed in the mid-1990s.[^2] A 2015 report says DCNR dismantled it in the late 1990s, and that it was then moved to the DCNR Weiser District office in Cressona, where it was used to train firefighters.[^4] In August 2013 its top section and cab were set up at the Owl Creek Recreation Area in Tamaqua.[^1][^3]

A 2013 newspaper report said the tower now stands two stories high, and that volunteers from the Weiser Forest Fire Wardens Association rescued it.[^3] In August 2015 the 100th anniversary of the Pennsylvania Forest Fire Wardens was celebrated at Owl Creek, with tours of the tower, which volunteers of South Ward Fire Company had recently restored.[^4]

The 2013 plan was for the tower to be open during hayrides and other special events at Owl Creek.[^3] We found no newer source on its hours or condition, or on whether visitors may go up into the cab. Check before you visit, and do not climb the tower if it is closed.
