The Careen Lake tower was built in 1964, when the Department of Natural Resources recorded one new tower at Careen Lake in its annual report for 1964-65.[^1]

According to a local history of the Big River area, the Department of Natural Resources spaced its northern Saskatchewan towers about 80 km (50 miles) apart so that tower men could stay in radio contact.[^2] Each tower was equipped with a fire finder, a two-way radio and binoculars.[^2] The towers were made of wood or steel and ranged from 11 m (35 ft) to 30 m (100 ft) tall.[^2]

A fire-tower listing on websiteservice.ca gives the site at 56 degrees, 54.68 minutes north and 108 degrees, 9.38 minutes west.[^3]

No source we found describes the Careen Lake site today. It is not known whether a tower, footings or other remains stand there, or whether the site is open to visitors.
