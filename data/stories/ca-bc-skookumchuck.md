Skookumchuck Lookout is in the East Kootenay region of British Columbia, at 1,921 m (6,301 ft) above sea level.[^1] The register dates it to 1948. It describes a tall brick base, with stairs that run inside the structure and then step outside for the final climb to the cab.[^1]

A 2025 travel blog describes the base as cinderblock and puts the lookout at 1,918 m (6,293 ft).[^2] The two descriptions differ, so we have used the register's figures.[^1][^2]

The blog says the lookout was constructed and staffed in the mid-20th century, when the B.C. forest service relied heavily on mountaintop towers.[^2] It also says that by the 1970s and 1980s, aerial surveillance and satellite imaging were replacing manual observation, and that Skookumchuck was one of many lookouts taken out of service and left exposed to the weather.[^2] The register lists the British Columbia Forest Service as the administering agency and gives a registration date of January 2, 2026.[^1]

The same blog's July 2025 trip account says the author reached the base by road and was glad the lookout was still there.[^2] A forest service road, the Skook FSR, leads toward it.[^3] Another trip report says a two-wheel-drive vehicle got as far as the 12 marker, and the author was unsure about road conditions higher up.[^3] A 2026 list of B.C. lookouts includes Skookumchuck among sites that are either confirmed to have a standing structure or likely to have an unmaintained one.[^4] The same list says access to most of them is difficult, usually requiring a high-clearance 4WD or an off-road vehicle.[^4]

We found no source that says whether the lookout is open to the public or whether it can be climbed. Check with the British Columbia Forest Service before you go, and do not climb any structure that is closed or private.[^1]
