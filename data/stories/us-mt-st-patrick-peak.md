St. Patrick Peak rises in Mineral County, Montana, about 15 miles south of Superior, on the Lolo National Forest.[^1] Firelookout.com gives its elevation as 7,124 feet.[^1] The page dates a log cabin on the site to 1919, and a D-6 cupola cabin to 1923.[^1] A February 1924 Daily Missoulian item says a man had spent the previous summer on the Lolo National Forest building a lookout post on the peak.[^3]

Newspaper extracts on a Montana lookout history site also point to an earlier station.[^3] A July 8, 1915 Mineral Independent item places E.L. Alwayrd at the St. Patrick lookout.[^3] That is before the 1919 log cabin on firelookout.com, so the dates do not line up neatly.[^1][^3]

Staffing shows up in a few summers after that. A September 1936 Missoulian item says William Sutphin served as a lookout on St. Patrick's knob that summer. It calls him the last lookout the Forest Service brought in to that district.[^3] A September 1940 Mineral Independent item says Lauress Fike served as a Forest Service lookout on the peak that summer.[^3]

Firelookout.com says the cabin was destroyed in 1965, and its page gives the site's span as 1923 to 1965.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a ground-type lookout that is gone.[^2]

No source here describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit the peak.[^1][^2]
