Bigelow Mountain, in Somerset County, Maine, had a state watch station by 1905.[^1] A September 1905 newspaper item calls the summit the third observatory on the mountain.[^1] That December, Forest Leaves described three new lookout telephone stations, one of them on Bigelow Mountain.[^1] An August 1907 Forest Leaves item says the station had a view of 200,000 acres of timber and farmland and reported 11 fires in 1905.[^1]

A 2011 newspaper account puts the first tower at about 1905.[^2] A 1917 report on forest protection describes a steel tower 38 feet high on Mount Bigelow.[^1] A 1920 inventory lists a 16-foot steel tower, and the two heights disagree.[^1]

A December 1925 article about the wreck of the Mount Abram tower noted that the Bigelow tower had been wrecked a few years earlier, and that it was the only earlier tower to have blown over.[^1]

Duluth Wing, who retired in 1984 after 38 years as a district forest ranger, said he was the watchman in the tower in 1947.[^2] He said the tower's steel legs were sound, but the cabin on top was deteriorating.[^2] In July 1949 the Portland Press Herald reported that Aubrey Bishop, 40, a fire warden at the lookout, had died of a heart attack.[^1]

Around 1960, Wing said, a 12-by-12-foot stone foundation was built to carry a livable cabin on top.[^2] In May 1962 the Lewiston Daily Sun reported that nine tons of material for a new fire tower had been airlifted to the summit.[^1]

The tower had stood empty since about 1972.[^2] The 2011 account links the vandalism to heavy Appalachian Trail traffic.[^2] Wing said the decision was to close the tower and hand fire watching to aircraft.[^2]

In 2011 the watchtower was owned by the Maine Bureau of Parks and Land, and the state burned it on site on April 15.[^2][^3] The chapter's report cited liability, continued vandalism and sensitive alpine vegetation among its reasons.[^3] It said the removal followed an extensive review with no opposition at public hearings.[^3] The cabin about a third of a mile below the summit was then used by Appalachian Trail hikers.[^2]

The Association's Maine list marks the site as gone.[^4] No source I read describes the summit since the 2011 burning, so what remains there is unknown.
