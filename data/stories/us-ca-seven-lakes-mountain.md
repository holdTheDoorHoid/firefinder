Seven Lakes Mountain is in Lassen County, California, and the Plumas National Forest is its managing agency.[^1][^3] A Reno Evening Gazette item from July 1956, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page, called the new Seven Lakes Lookout the latest addition to the Plumas forest's lookout system.[^1] It was half a mile from the California-Nevada boundary. Its name comes from seven tiny lakes, visible to the east across the Nevada line.[^1]

Raymond Scott of Susanville was manning the lookout in 1956.[^1]

By 1971 the lookout's future was in doubt. An April 1971 Portola Reporter item warned that budget cuts might end fire lookout service on Seven Lakes near Hallelujah Junction for at least the coming season. It described the risk as a strong possibility, though only a preliminary review.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page carries the label Removed beneath that item.[^1] It gives no date and no source for a removal. Both Forest Fire Lookout Association lists, by contrast, describe a ground-level lookout marked standing, with no register number.[^2][^3] We found no record confirming when, or whether, the lookout came down.

We found no public access information. Anyone curious should check with the Plumas National Forest first, and should not climb any structure they find there.
