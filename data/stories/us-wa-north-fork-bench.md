North Fork Bench is a small ridge in Snohomish County, in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, about 15 miles southeast of Darrington.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb places it just above Bedal Campground and the former North Fork Guard Station.[^3]

The lookout was an L-4 cab, and the Register dates it to 1936.[^1] WillhiteWeb reports the same build year.[^3] The panoramic photographs taken from the lookout are dated August 8, 1935, a year before that build date, and the sources do not explain the difference.[^1][^3][^4] Forest Lookouts credits the panoramas to George Clisby.[^4]

The cab was gone by 1955, according to the Register, Firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb.[^1][^2][^3]

WillhiteWeb's author searched the whole ridge in 2015 and found no signs of the former lookout.[^3] The exact site is not confirmed. A 1930s sketch map shows the lookout below the railroad grade, and the author believes that is where it stood. The page also points to a flat, disturbed spot near the road switchback at about 1,420 feet as a very possible location.[^3]

The site is close to the road, a good gravel surface with potholes, and the climb involves little elevation gain.[^3] Near the high point where an old road crosses the ridge, the page describes a large amount of old dumping debris.[^3] No source we read describes current access or closures, so check with the Forest Service before you go.
