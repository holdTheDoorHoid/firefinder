Middle Fork Peak Lookout sits on a 9,127-foot summit about 40 miles southwest of Salmon, in Lemhi County, Idaho.[^1][^2] The register lists the site on the Salmon National Forest, in the Salmon-Cobalt Ranger District, while a Forest Service page now uses the name Salmon-Challis National Forest for the unit.[^1][^4]

The first lookout on the summit was a cabin built in 1929.[^1][^2][^3] The sources disagree on its type: the register calls it a DuBois cabin, while Idaho Fire Lookouts lists an R-4 log cabin.[^1][^3] In 1962 the present lookout, a flat R-6 cab on a 10-foot concrete base, replaced it.[^1][^2][^3]

A 1982 account by Bing Young, quoted in a 2018 climbing guide, says the peak appears as a lookout in the 1919 to 1923 fire plans and describes a newer building that has replaced an early-1920s structure.[^5] That earlier date does not match the 1929 date given elsewhere, and these sources do not settle which is right.[^1][^2][^3][^5]

Staffing is disputed. The Forest Service says the Middle Fork lookout is still staffed each summer, along with two other lookouts.[^4] That page was last updated on May 22, 2025, so it predates the 2026 season and cannot settle the current status.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the tower as not staffed for 2025 and 2026.[^3] None of these sources names a notable fire at the lookout.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

The register added the lookout on September 13, 2012, as US 949, ID 101.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts describes it as drive-up.[^3] The 2018 climbing guide says the road stops just below the summit, about 14 miles past a junction, and that a 4WD vehicle with exceptionally good tires is needed.[^5] Check current road and staffing conditions with the Forest Service before you go.
