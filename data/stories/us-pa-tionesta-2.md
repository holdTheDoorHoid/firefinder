The register places the Tionesta fire tower in Forest County, Pennsylvania.[^1] It describes an 80-foot Aermotor LS-40 tower built in 1928, and it names the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry as the administering agency.[^1]

A 1937 newspaper item, quoted by the Eastern US lookout index, reported that the Tionesta tower crew held its fourth yearly picnic at the tower, west of Tionesta.[^2]

In April 1947 the Titusville airport reported a fire under a state contract to watch for smoke that pilots saw. The Tionesta towerman had also seen the same smoke and was getting ready to send a crew. The airport's call made that trip unnecessary. A farmer clearing an old field had started the blaze, which was already under control.[^2]

In June 1968 a bear kept four Department of Forests and Waters employees on edge for two hours near the Tionesta fire tower, which was being painted just off the Neiltown-Tionesta road. The News-Herald reported the episode.[^2]

The register says the tower has guarded the county's northwestern corner since 1928, and its entry is dated December 6, 2017.[^1] That 2017 entry is the newest source I read. No source I read says whether the tower can be climbed, how to reach it, or whether the public may visit.
