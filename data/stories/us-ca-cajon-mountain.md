Cajon Mountain was a U.S. Forest Service lookout on the east side of Cajon Pass, in the San Bernardino National Forest.[^1] The register says the structure was built in 1935 as a California Region 5 plan BC-301 cabin on a 30-foot K-brace steel tower, and it puts the site at 5,319 feet (1,621 m).[^1]

In July 1934 a San Bernardino County Sun item said Civilian Conservation Corps crews would build a spur to the summit, where a new lookout station was to go.[^2] The register gives 1935 as the year the lookout was built, so the 1934 item describes the plan rather than the finished station.[^1]

In November 1945 a San Mateo Times article reported that young women were replacing fire wardens in the lookout towers of the San Bernardino mountains. It named Margaret V. Johnson as the watch-keeper at this lookout.[^2] In the 1950s the lookout also helped with aircraft identification under the Skywatch program, according to a note on the Forest Lookouts page.[^2]

The register says the cabin came off some time after 1990, and the tower some time after 1997.[^1] A Peakbagging.com page says the cab was removed in 1997 and the 30-foot tower was taken down about 2004. It adds that plans to rebuild were abandoned.[^3]

A 2018 photo on that page shows the area where the lookout stood. The page says the only benchmark left is a survey mark placed in 1949 at a corner of the tower.[^3]

A hiking page for the peak describes an old fire lookout tower, missing its cabin and platform.[^4] A reader comment on the same page, dated October 2, 2012, says the towers are gone.[^4] We could not settle this from the sources.

The hiking page says to park at the Cajon Lookout road fork, reached after about 6.3 miles on the route it describes. From there the walk is about 3 miles round trip, partly on road and partly cross-country. It notes that the gate is sometimes open, and that there is no way to confirm this in advance.[^4] We found no official access notice, so check with the San Bernardino National Forest before you go.
