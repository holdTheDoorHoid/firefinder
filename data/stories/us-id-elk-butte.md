The Clearwater-Potlatch Timber Protective Association operates Elk Butte today.[^1] Its own history says two associations formed separately in 1905 and merged in 1966.[^5] The register listed the lookout in June 1997.[^1]

The lookout site dates to 1912, when it was first used as a camp lookout.[^2][^3] A 1914 annual report of the Potlatch association called Elk Butte its principal lookout.[^4] A platform tower and log cabin went up in 1918.[^2][^3] Hunters burned that cabin, and it was replaced in 1926.[^2][^3] A 30-foot wooden tower with a live-in cab followed in 1933.[^2][^3]

The steel tower that stands now is 80 feet tall.[^1][^2] The register and the lookout-history sites describe it as army or WWII surplus, erected in 1948, with the 1933 live-in cab set at the 30-foot level.[^1][^2][^3] The register gives a metal cab of 7 by 7 feet, but a 1958 fire warden's report describes a 14-by-14 cabin built into the tower midway up.[^1][^4]

The dates do not all match. A 1949 entry in the association's annual report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says a steel tower bought from War Assets in 1946 went up that year.[^4] A 1958 fire warden's report says the tower was converted and meant for the U.S. Air Forces, which differs from the army-surplus account given elsewhere.[^4]

The site was staffed into the 1990s, and firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts both say it is now on emergency status.[^2][^3] The register says the association still runs just two active lookouts, this one among them, and notes that the other "lookout within a lookout" tower is Elk Neck in Maryland.[^1]

I found no source that says whether visitors may climb it. It is an active fire lookout on emergency status, so ask the association before planning a visit.
