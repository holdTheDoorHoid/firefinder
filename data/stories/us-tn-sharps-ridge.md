The fire tower on Sharp's Ridge stands at the highest point of a seven-mile ridge north of downtown Knoxville, inside a city park.[^1] Tennessee Landforms lists it in Knox County, numbered 2-4 on a 1976 state district list, with a site at 1,355 feet, and marks it as standing.[^2] We could not find when it was built, who designed it, or who put it up. Wikipedia puts the ridge's highest ground point, where it places the abandoned tower, at 1,391 feet, so the two sources give different elevations.[^1][^2]

The ridge's modern story starts with a war memorial. By 1943 the city was working to acquire the land for a war-memorial park.[^3] Fundraisers planned a memorial on the ridge's highest peak, including an observation tower with a stairway inside.[^3] A road to the summit was completed in October 1944, largely for the memorial plan.[^3] The history does not say whether the planned observation tower was ever built, or whether it is the same structure as the fire tower.[^3] Weston Fulton, an inventor who helped lead the fundraising, died in 1946 before the project was complete.[^3] The park was dedicated on July 4, 1953, to honor soldiers of all wars, and a large stone marker still stands there.[^3]

We found no record of who staffed the tower or when it was manned. Wildfires also visited the ridge often: a 1935 report said they came "several times a year."[^3]

Wikipedia calls the tower on the summit abandoned.[^1] Tennessee Landforms still lists it as standing.[^2] We found no source that says it was taken down, so both can be true. The ridge also hosts an antenna farm used by most of Knoxville's broadcasters.[^1] In 1953 WROL went on the air, and the first East Tennessee television signals came from towers on the ridge.[^3] Those broadcast towers are a separate story. In 2023 a contractor began dismantling a 701-foot WBIR backup tower next to the J.B. Owen Overlook, which closed for parts of June, July and August.[^4]

The park is reached from Broadway, near Ludlow Street, about two miles north of downtown.[^3] A 2023 news report said the trails would stay open during the tower work.[^4] We found no source that says the old fire tower is open to visitors or to climbing. Tennessee Landforms advises people not to climb these towers, because steps are often removed or rotten.[^2] Please stay off the structure, and check with the City of Knoxville before going up.
