The Big Butte lookout stood on Big Southern Butte, a volcanic cone on the Snake River Plain near Atomic City, Idaho.[^1][^2] In May 1962 a newspaper reported that a modern lookout was planned for the butte.[^3] A June 1962 report said it would not be ready that season, so temporary arrangements were being made for the summer.[^3] The reason given was fire danger: heavy grass and shrubs from a snowy winter would become flash fuels in the dry summer.[^3]

A concrete structure with an observation cab was finished in 1963.[^3] Rex's page describes it as a two-story cab with slanted windows, built that year.[^4]

The lookout was last staffed in 2004.[^4][^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists a single known staffer for the site, identified as the last man to staff it.[^5]

To protect the lookout, air tankers laid down retardant in August 1996, according to a Times-News item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page. The page does not name the fire.[^3]

The lookout building and its related structures were taken down in 2013.[^3] Rex's page says the site was gone by the summer of that year.[^4]

The register lists the site as administered by the Department of the Interior's Bureau of Land Management.[^1] A climbing guide updated in November 2021 says the lookout was last manned in 2004 and came down at some later point. It also says the BLM keeps up the access road, likely because of electronic equipment at the summit.[^2] The sources we read do not describe what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit.[^2][^3][^4]

The BLM closes the road in winter, and the guide says many people hike it from the gate.[^2] A significant part of the land north of the butte lies within the Idaho National Laboratory, and that area is closed to the public.[^2]
