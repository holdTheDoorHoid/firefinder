Mount Greylock is a peak just under 10,000 feet in Custer County, Idaho.[^1][^4] The lookout on it is a ground-mounted L-4 cabin, and it sits in the Salmon-Challis National Forest.[^1][^2] The sources do not agree on when it was built.[^1][^2][^3][^5] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts say 1934,[^2][^3] while the register and a local history page say 1935.[^1][^5] The register also notes a Forest design catwalk on the cab.[^1]

A climbing guide says the cab was in use as a fire lookout between 1934 and 1942.[^4] The register says the cab was sometimes put to use for CCC-era training,[^1] and Idaho Fire Lookouts says it was occasionally used that way too.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts also says it has no written staffing record for the tower yet.[^3] I did not find a notable fire named in any of the pages I read.

The sources disagree on when the lookout was abandoned.[^1][^2][^3][^5] The register says 1939 or 1940,[^1] and the local history page gives around 1939 to 1940 as well.[^5] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts both say 1942.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com adds that the cab has visibly decayed in the years since.[^2]

The register, which listed the lookout in 2020, says it still stands more than 80 years after its last use.[^1] A 2024 climbing guide is less upbeat. It says the cab can no longer be lived in, and it shows photos of its ruins.[^4]

Idaho Fire Lookouts marks the site as hike-in.[^3] The climbing guide describes a route of roughly 2.2 miles from a ridge-top parking spot to the summit.[^4] None of the sources I read says what visitors may do at the site. Because the cab is no longer habitable, do not go inside the ruins, and check with the Salmon-Challis National Forest about access before you plan a trip.
