Hash Rock sits in the Ochoco National Forest, about 17 miles northeast of Prineville in Crook County, Oregon.[^2] The site was staffed by 1920.[^3] In July of that year the Crook County Journal reported that Wayne Anderson of Prineville had become primary lookout man, to work Hash Rock and Tamarack Point in turn.[^3]

A crow's nest was set up on the site in 1932.[^1][^2] The registry says a 25-foot wooden tower replaced it in 1935.[^1] firelookout.com calls that tower an L-5 design.[^2]

In 1921 Norman Harrison split duty between Hash Rock and Tamarack Point, and in 1927 Sam Warg was listed as lookout for both.[^3] In June 1928 lightning hit three trees on Hash Rock, including the one that held the phone.[^3] In August 1932 the Central Oregonian, as quoted on a lookout history page, reported that the Hash Rock lookout had sighted a fire on Grizzly Mountain.[^3] The Forest Service sent 40 men to the fire in under an hour.[^3]

The tower was no longer there by 1968, a date both the registry and firelookout.com give.[^1][^2] The site was added to the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register on June 23, 2011.[^1]

No source we read describes what remains at the site now. The registry has a 2005 photo of the site, but its caption says nothing more about it.[^1] No source we read says whether the public can reach the site.
