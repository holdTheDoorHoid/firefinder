Bald Mountain sits on the line between Nelson and Augusta counties in Virginia's Blue Ridge, and a steel lookout once stood on its summit.[^1][^2]

In 1932 a newspaper reported that two new lookout towers would be built, one of them on this peak. The planned tower was fifty feet of steel, topped by a seven-by-seven observatory, with living quarters at its foot.[^2] The Nelson listing says the Virginia Department of Forestry built a steel lookout here in the late 1930s, though its built field gives the early 1960s.[^1] A 1957 Coast and Geodetic Survey description of a station on Bald Mountain puts that station at the top of a lookout tower's roof.[^2] The sources disagree on when the tower went up.[^1][^2]

The listing recalls a lookout known as Mr. Seaman, radio call sign KID-363, who worked the tower in the early 1960s. His son remembers coming to the site with him, where he was shown how the Osborne Fire Finder worked.[^1] A cabin at the foot of the tower gave lookouts a place to stay.[^1]

The Nelson listing says the tower was removed in the 1970s.[^1] A second registry entry for the same peak, filed as Augusta County site US 1725, describes a 60-foot steel tower with a residence cabin below it.[^3][^4] The two records also name different administrators: the Virginia Department of Forestry in the Nelson record and the U.S. Forest Service in the Augusta one.[^1][^3]

The 2018 listing says only concrete footers remained.[^1] None of the sources used here describes the summit since then, so it is unclear what remains there or whether the public can reach the site.[^1]
