Casey Peak sits in Jefferson County, Montana, and the Forest Lookouts page places it in the Helena National Forest.[^1] Hikers reach the summit on foot from the Casey Meadows Trailhead, about 16 miles by road from Helena, according to a hiking blog.[^2] A trail listing also points visitors to the Helena National Forest visitor map.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts page reprints a June 1937 newspaper item about a new lookout station on High peak, at the head of McClellan Creek.[^1] The item describes a planned 20-foot log tower with a glass-walled cab, with the station expected to be in place around early July that year.[^1] The article does not name Casey Peak. The page places it under that name.[^1]

The sources disagree about when the lookout was built.[^4][^2] Firelookout.com describes an L-4 cab on a stone base from the 1930s and lists the site as ruins.[^4] The firelookout.org list also marks it as ruins.[^5] The hiking blog says the stone lower floor was part of a fire lookout that saw only brief service in the early 1900s, with a wooden upper floor above it.[^2]

A July 2023 hiking post describes a stone structure on the summit.[^2] The post says the wooden upper level had come down several years before it was written, so the stone base appears to be what remains.[^2] We found no source that describes the site in more detail.
