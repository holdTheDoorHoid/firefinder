Eddy Gulch Lookout sits on Klamath Peak in Siskiyou County, in the Klamath National Forest.[^2] The first station there was a cabin of rough-hewn logs, built in the summer of 1912.[^2][^3] A 1911 newspaper item already mentions a guard stationed at the lookout, so the earliest date is not fully settled.[^2]

According to the register, Hallie Morse Daggett was the first woman the Forest Service hired as a fire observer.[^1] She was hired for the 1913 season and stayed on the job for about fourteen years.[^2][^3] The register dates the new lookout building, with its catwalk, to 1926.[^1] The forest's own chronology has Daggett moving into it in June 1925, so the two sources differ by a year.[^1][^2] The register says she retired the year after that building went up, and a 1928 entry says she would not return that year.[^1][^2]

The metal tower on the peak dates from 1958, according to the register.[^1] A forest history entry from that year lists a new Eddy Gulch lookout tower.[^2] Soon after the tower went up, Nancy Culbertson took up the post, and the register credits her with thirty seasons there.[^1] A 2010 northern California chapter report noted new camera fire detection at the lookout and said it still had staff during fire season.[^4]

The register records a 1991 dedication of an interpretive trail honoring the two women.[^1] A different cabin, the one where Daggett lived in her later years, was moved to Etna City Park and renovated.[^3] A 2016 blog post makes clear that it is not her lookout cabin.[^3]

The most recent staffing report I found is from 2010, and I found no current access rules, so check with the Klamath National Forest before planning a visit.
