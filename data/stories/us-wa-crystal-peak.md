Crystal Peak sits inside Mount Rainier National Park in Pierce County, Washington, about 13 miles east-northeast of the mountain.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com files the site as Crystal Lake, and Willhite also uses the name Crystal Point.[^2][^4]

The register gives a 1930s build date.[^1] Willhite dates the cabin to 1934, one of four lookouts built that year under a single construction contract with Tolmie Peak, Mount Fremont and Gobblers Knob.[^4] He writes that a Seattle company built all four with hip roofs and two stories, and that the lower room served as storage.[^4]

The register and firelookout.com say the cabin was abandoned in the 1950s, and the Washington lookout list marks it as gone.[^1][^2][^3] The National Park Service history puts its removal later, in the 1960s or 1970s, after aerial surveillance replaced fire lookouts in the 1950s.[^5] Willhite says it lasted until the late 1960s or early 1970s.[^4] A correspondent's 2018 recollection, which Willhite posts, says heavy snow in the winter of 1971 and 1972 damaged the lookout so badly that it was torn down around 1972 or 1973. The same account says a park road crew went up to the site with dynamite in 1974 and put charges in the foundation.[^4]

Willhite reports that only concrete footings are left, and that they have shifted in recent years.[^4] A single phone insulator survives on a small tree beside the trail, just after the crossing of Crystal Creek.[^4]

The site lies inside the park.[^1] Willhite gives the hike as 4 miles with 3,500 feet of elevation gain.[^4] The Forest Lookouts site lists the lookout as removed.[^6]
