Belknap Mountain Lookout stands near the summit of Mount Belknap, in Belknap County, New Hampshire, inside the state forest that shares its name. The Register lists the state's Department of Resources and Economic Development as administrator.[^1][^3]

The New Hampshire Forestry Commission first used the mountain as a fire watch station in 1912.[^3] The steel tower and its 10-by-10-foot cab went up in 1914 and 1915.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1915 as the build year and describes a 34-foot steel tower with the New Hampshire wooden cab.[^1] Trailspotting, a hiking guide, says a tower has stood on the mountain since 1913, so the start date is not settled.[^4]

Between 1925 and 1928 the tower was raised 24 feet, and its ladder was replaced with stairs because of the growing number of visitors.[^3] The state bought the 556-acre farm from the owner's estate in 1927, which created the state forest.[^3] In 1940 the cab was replaced, and curbing went in around the well.[^3]

A Portsmouth Herald item from April 1942, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, said the tower would start operations that week.[^5] In 1979 the tower was raised another 10 feet with a new cab, and the old cab level became an open observation deck.[^1][^3] The Register says the lookout is staffed during fire season, though only sometimes.[^1] The sources read name no particular fires, although the Register says the lookout reports many of them.[^1]

The most recent work came in 2023, when the tower was repaired, repainted and given a new cab.[^4] A Forest Fire Lookout Association chapter report says wet weather delayed that work, that materials were airlifted to the site, and that the Belknap work cost $712,000 before contingencies. It said final costs were not yet available and that the tower was expected to be operating in 2024.[^2]

Today the Register lists the lookout as in active service, and a 2016 visit report calls the tower and its nearby watchman's cabin in very good condition.[^1] Hikers reach the tower by trail from the Gunstock recreation area.[^1] Trailspotting says the gate to the Upper Carriage Road trailhead opens only from late May through November and only during the day, so drivers should check the hours first.[^4] The sources do not say whether visitors may go inside the cab, so plan on the trails and the views from outside.
