Blue Anchor Station Fire Tower stands in Winslow Township, Camden County, part of the New Jersey Forest Fire Service's statewide lookout network.[^1] A steel tower was already up and running at the site by the time the state's Department of Conservation and Development filed its 1920–21 annual report, which noted that a "steel tower has been erected and opened at Blue Anchor, Camden County, making a total of nine active lookouts now in operation."[^2] That first tower was an Aermotor, per the National Historic Lookout Register, which says it was taken down in 1932 and moved north to become the "Culver tower" in Stokes State Forest.[^1] That claim is hard to fully square with the register's own page for the Stokes lookout (Culvers Lookout, US 44/NJ 1), which instead dates its present 47-foot Aermotor tower to 1933 and makes no mention of Blue Anchor, so whether it is really the same structure, and whether the move happened in 1932 or 1933, is not settled.[^1][^5] In Blue Anchor's place, the Civilian Conservation Corps built the 86-foot steel tower that stands today, made by the McKlintock-Marshall company with a 7-by-7-foot cab on top.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 566, New Jersey's NJ 19, nominated by Bob Spear and added to the register on March 15, 2004.[^1]

For decades the tower was watched by a single observer working rotating shifts. A Courier-Post reporter who visited in July 1940 found Hugh Keenan already twenty years into the job, equipped with a two-way radio, a telephone, maps, instruments for wind and humidity, and a sextant for pinpointing a fire's exact location; on spotting smoke he would radio the nearest district warden.[^2] The tower covers forest sections in the Forest Fire Service's Division C, one of 21 towers that still watch over the state.[^3]

That tradition continues. As of 2024, fire observer Mark Turner was climbing the tower's narrow zig-zag staircase for eight-hour shifts alone, scanning the surrounding Pine Barrens for smoke he says can be spotted from seven or eight miles off. Forest Fire Service Chief Bill Donnelly called the tower "critical in the response to wildfires," and the agency credits its network of towers with spotting more than half of New Jersey's wildfires.[^4]

Blue Anchor Station is still standing and in active service, one of the dwindling number of New Jersey's fire towers that remains staffed rather than retired. It sits on state land and is worked by Forest Fire Service personnel; nothing found here says whether the public can climb it, so visitors should assume it is not open without permission and check with the Forest Fire Service before approaching.
