Pilot Peak Lookout is in the Payette National Forest, in Valley County, Idaho, about 10 miles west-northwest of the Big Creek Ranger Station.[^2] The first structure at the site was a 12-by-12-foot log cabin built directly on the ground in 1919.[^1][^2] The lookout standing today is an L-4 cab, built in 1954 on a 10-foot concrete base.[^1][^2] Keeping Watch gives 1919 as the build year, which matches the first cabin rather than the 1954 cab.[^4][^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower is usually staffed during the fire season.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts describes it as staffed every year and lists known staff for the mid-1970s, along with Caroline Huntley for 2010 and 2011.[^3] Keeping Watch, a University of Idaho collection of lookout interviews, pairs its Pilot Peak entry with an interview with Jim and Caroline Huntley, who it says staffed lookouts in the early and mid-2000s.[^4] The two sources date the Huntleys' work to different years, so the record is not settled on when they worked here.[^3][^4]

In July 1985 the 3,000-acre Savage Creek fire spread across Elk Creek, and a newspaper item quoted on a lookout list page says the Pilot Peak lookout was ordered evacuated that Monday.[^5]

The National Historic Lookout Register added Pilot Peak on October 3, 2020.[^1] No source consulted reports the tower being moved, closed, or rented out since then.

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Pilot Peak as drive-up and staffed.[^3] None of the sources spells out visitor rules, so treat it as a working Forest Service lookout and check with the Payette National Forest before planning a visit.
