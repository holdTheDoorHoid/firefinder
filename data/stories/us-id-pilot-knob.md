Pilot Knob stands at 7,135 feet on the Nez Perce-Clearwater National Forest, well above the South Fork of the Clearwater River, in the country between Clearwater and Elk City.[^1][^2][^3]

The site began as a camp in 1914.[^1][^5] A July 1914 newspaper report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says Tom Crossley had set up a lookout station there, with forest guard George E. Watkins on duty.[^4] A 10-by-10-foot frame cabin went up in 1922.[^4][^5] In 1939 an L-4 cab was built on a 10-foot timber tower, and that is the structure that stands there today.[^4][^5]

The Register calls the cab a classic 1932 model and dates its construction to 1939.[^2] The 1932 date seems to refer to the cab's design rather than when it went up, so the two dates do not conflict.[^2]

Lightning was a real hazard at the site. In August 1916 two men stationed at the lookout, George Watkins and Levi Colgrove, were struck by lightning and left unconscious, and a telephone near their tent was destroyed.[^4]

Staffing has continued for decades.[^3] In October 2024 the Forest Service reported that Gary Dutcher had just finished his 40th consecutive season at the lookout.[^3] The same release says he does not mind visitors and is friendly when they come.[^3]

The tower is still standing and still in service on the forest.[^2] The Register notes it is one of the few with a Bosworth fire finder, and Fire Lookout says it is staffed every summer.[^2][^5] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as drive-up and staffed.[^1] It is an active staffed post on the Salmon River Ranger District, so ask the district before you plan a visit.[^3] We found no source describing a closure, a move or a fire at the site.
