Standing Indian Mountain is the highest point in both Macon and Clay counties.[^1] Its first lookout was a log cabin, and a ladder gave access to the roof.[^1] A lookout house went up on the summit in 1929.[^1]

In August 1936 the Cherokee Scout reported that funds would pay for a new bridle trail up from Deep Gap, and for a new stone observation tower at the summit.[^2] The site history says that what happened to the tower plan is unclear.[^1]

The lookout was abandoned in 1951.[^1] The tower on nearby Albert Mountain took its place.[^1] The Albert Mountain register entry says that tower replaced an earlier wooden tower on Standing Indian.[^5] The lookout was removed in the 1960s.[^1]

Nothing stands on the summit now.[^1] Some evidence remains, such as anchor points set into large rocks that held the guy wires.[^1] One account says the lookout went over a drop-off about ten feet from the summit.[^1]

Today the mountain is in the Southern Nantahala Wilderness, and the U.S. Forest Service is listed as the agency for the site.[^3][^4] The Appalachian Trail runs along the mountain, and the easiest route to the top is a hike.[^4]
