Fence Meadow Lookout sits on a high point above the Kings River Canyon, at the south end of the Sierra National Forest in Fresno County.[^1][^4]

The lookout dates to 1934, when a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab with a catwalk went up on a 20-foot H-braced steel tower.[^1][^2] Peakbagging says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the structure that year.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register listed it on April 21, 1999.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree on how rare this cab style is. The register puts the count at 20 surviving L-4 cabs in California, while fire-lookouts.org says 18.[^1][^2]

By 2008 the cab needed repairs. A $500 Forest Fire Lookout Association grant went to the Buck Rock Foundation to help the Forest Service with the rehabilitation, and the foundation matched it with another $500.[^2] That money was being used to restore the windows, window frames, siding, and shutters.[^2] Painting of the cab followed in 2009. In 2010 a new shutter system and plumbing and electrical work were done to make the tower safer, and most of the labor came from Buck Rock Foundation volunteers.[^2]

Photos taken on August 11, 2008 show a lookout standing behind an Osborne fire finder at the site.[^3]

The register says the lookout is still in active service, one of two on the Kings River Ranger District.[^1] Fire-lookouts.org, whose page was last updated in 2013, reports staffing by paid and volunteer lookouts.[^2]

Fence Meadow is in a remote area south of the Dinkey Creek camp area.[^3] The sources reviewed give no visiting rules, so check with the Kings River Ranger District before planning a trip.[^1]
