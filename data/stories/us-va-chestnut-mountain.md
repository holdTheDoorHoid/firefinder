The Chestnut Mountain lookout stood on Chestnut Ridge in Tazewell County, Virginia, overlooking Burke's Garden.[^1][^2] An October 1929 Harrisonburg Daily News item, quoted by a Forest Lookouts page, reported a new 48-foot steel tower on the line between Bland and Tazewell counties.[^2] A later history of the site also dates the tower to 1929.[^3]

In 1934 the U.S. Geological Survey placed some benchmarks, the main one set beneath the middle of the tower.[^3] When the USGS came back in 1958, it noted the tower had been taken down, though its concrete footings remained.[^3] A 1961 note said the tower had been relocated and supplied fresh figures for the station benchmark. The history reads that as a new tower having been put up.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the removal to circa 1980s, and the history says the second tower came down during that decade.[^1][^3]

The register says the former lookout cabin was restored in 1994 and is today the Chestnut Knob shelter on the Appalachian Trail.[^1] The history, though, places the shelter in a fire warden's house at the tower's base.[^3] The Appalachian Trail Conservancy describes the shelter as an aged stone cabin once used by a fire warden, in a clearing at the summit.[^4] A hiker's post mentions old fire tower foundations on the summit, explored along with the shelter.[^5]

The Appalachian Trail passes over the top of the site.[^3] The Conservancy's directions begin at the Walker Gap parking area and head south to the open summit, 1.3 miles in.[^4] On clear days, the register says, the view reaches Big Walker Mountain and Mount Rogers.[^1]
