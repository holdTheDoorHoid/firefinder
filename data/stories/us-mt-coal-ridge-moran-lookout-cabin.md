Coal Ridge, in the North Fork country of the Flathead National Forest, has a lookout history that starts with a cabin. The first structure on the ridge, at its northwest point, went up in 1928.[^1] The register also calls it the Moran Patrol Cabin.[^1] A North Fork group says it was the original lookout there, though the current map calls it a cabin.[^2]

Two towers followed. A 24-foot pole platform tower went up in the 1930s about 1.5 miles southeast of the cabin, and it is now ruins.[^3] In 1935 a 20-foot pole L-4 tower was built about a mile southeast of the original cabin.[^1] Montana Lookouts gives the 1935 tower a height of 24 feet, so the sources disagree on its height.[^4]

Outside the cabin, a metal pole held the Osborne Fire Finder, the tool for locating fire starts.[^2] The register says the site was used for emergencies in the 1960s.[^1] We found no source that names who staffed the lookout or any fire it watched.

The 1935 tower is now in ruins, and part of those ruins still stands.[^1] The cabin is still there.[^1] Forest Service crews have also added new siding to the cabin, and a North Fork post links that work to the Northwest Montana Lookout Association's 2014 projects page.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists the cabin as US 1498, MT 76, registered on January 9, 2021.[^1]

Today the cabin stands on land that the Forest Service administers.[^1] Forest Service Trail 2 starts at the Moran Basin Road trailhead and climbs to the ridgetop, where it meets Trail 14.[^5] A North Fork Trails hike report gives the round trip as about 6.8 miles and says the old Coal Ridge lookout's remains lie a little way east along the ridge.[^5] The same report mentions slides on Moran Basin Road that had been cleared only days earlier, so check road conditions first.[^5]

The firelookout.org listing marks the site "Tower Gone," which fits the ruins but not the standing cabin.[^6] We found no source describing a rental, and no source describing the cabin's inside or its view today.
