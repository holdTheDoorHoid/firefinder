Lolo Springs stood on the Lolo National Forest in Missoula County, Montana, about nine miles north-northeast of Lolo Pass, at an elevation of 5,320 feet.[^1]

Firelookout.com dates a log cupola cabin, a Region-1 design, to the 1920s, and a 30-foot pole platform to 1922.[^1] A June 1922 newspaper item about a Forest Service inspection tour mentions a lookout station recently built near Lolo Hot Springs, which may be the same place.[^2] About 1934, a 30-foot pole L-4 tower was put up with the cabin inside it.[^1]

The station was in use in 1928. A September 1928 Missoula newspaper notice refers to a Forest Service lookout at the Lolo Springs station.[^2] Another 1928 item relays a letter from a Forest Service worker at Lolo Hot Springs, who reported going weeks at a time without seeing anyone.[^2]

A photograph dated October 1957 in the Montana History Portal shows a metal sign at the base of a lookout, damaged by close-range shotgun blasts.[^3] The photo's title calls the lookout Lolo Peak, but a library note from the University of Montana says it is likely the Lolo Springs lookout.[^3]

Firelookout.com says the lookout was destroyed in 1963.[^1] The library note gives the same year.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's index lists the site as gone.[^4]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit it.
