This summit in South Dakota's Black Hills is the highest point in the state and the highest ground east of the Rocky Mountains, and fire watchers have been using it since 1911, when the first setup was just an alidade for sighting smoke.[^1][^2] A 12-by-12-foot log cabin went up just below the summit in 1919, then a wooden cab was built right on top the following year and enlarged to 16 by 16 feet in 1921.[^2] None of that early wood survives. The stone tower that still stands today is a Civilian Conservation Corps project, designed by an architect specifically for the site, built over roughly two to three years and finished around 1938 to 1940 depending on the source.[^1][^2] Howard Culver is recorded as one of its lookouts in 1939.[^2]

The tower kept watch for fire until it was staffed for the last time in 1967.[^1] It still stands, cared for today by the Custer Ranger District of the Black Hills National Forest, and now sits inside a designated wilderness area.[^1]

The mountain's name has its own recent history. Formerly known as Harney Peak, it was renamed Black Elk Peak by the U.S. Board on Geographic Names in 2016.[^1]

Despite the remote, wilderness setting, the peak is heavily visited: about 40,000 people make the hike up each year.[^1] The old lookout tower is not rented out or staffed, but the summit remains heavily visited, reached by trail through the Black Hills National Forest.[^1]
