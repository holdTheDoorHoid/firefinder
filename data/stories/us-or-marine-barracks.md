The Marine Corps ran a treatment and recuperation center at Klamath Falls, Oregon, for Marines and Navy personnel with mosquito-borne tropical diseases.[^1] Marine Corps Major General A. A. Vandergrift had watched some 4,000 of his troops decline from tropical illness in the South Pacific, and he went to a Congressional committee seeking $1.5 million for a barracks for treatment in Klamath Falls.[^1] The Navy Bureau of Medicine judged the town ideal, citing its high elevation and decent weather.[^1] Marines started arriving in April 1944, and the final ones were discharged in March 1946.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page, which quotes 1944 and 1945 Herald and News items, says a fire lookout was built near the barracks by August 1945.[^3] A caption to two photographs from that month calls it "the new fire lookout built near the Marine barracks," and says men from the installation manned it. It says the tower overlooked the Klamath basin "from a height of about 5500 feet" (about 1,676 m), with three men on duty around the clock.[^3] A telephone line connected the tower to the barracks' fire house.[^3] In August 1944 the barracks announced that government-supplied saddle horses would patrol firebreaks and hilltops and, in an emergency, dispatch firefighting parties to grass and forest fires.[^3]

The page dates the barracks' closure to 1946 and says the lookout was then no longer used.[^3] A 2016 radio history says the Oregon Institute of Technology owns the property and that the barracks are gone.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page marks the tower as removed.[^3]

A Marine Barracks Memorial Park was created on Old Fort Road in 1994.[^2] In 2016 a granite headstone at the end of that road marked the park.[^1] In 2019 the park moved a short distance and was renamed the North Ridge Marine Corps Memorial.[^2] Its panels cover the barracks' history, and its information boards explain a Superfund cleanup.[^2] The Herald and News reports that asbestos contamination was found on land now part of the North Ridge Estates housing subdivision, and that the Environmental Protection Agency declared it a Superfund cleanup site, with hazardous wastes removed between 2016 and 2018.[^2] No source we found describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout or where it stood.
