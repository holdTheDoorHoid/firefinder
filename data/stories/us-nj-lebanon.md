The Lebanon Station fire tower stands in Brendan Byrne State Forest in Burlington County, New Jersey.[^2][^3] The register dates the tower to 1950 and describes an 80-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] A January 2007 visit report in the register notes that it had been freshly painted in red and white.[^1]

The tower had a predecessor. Wikipedia's list of state towers says the service erected a simple wooden tripod called the Four Mile Tower in 1910, and that the Lebanon Station replaced it.[^2] The register says the Lebanon tower succeeded the Four Mile lookout, which had overlooked the fire-prone pinelands.[^1]

According to the register, the New Jersey Forest Fire Service owns, staffs and maintains the tower, which falls within its Division B.[^1] It was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on March 3, 2004.[^1] At that time the register noted the tower was 54 years old and one of the state's oldest.[^1]

Trees later crowded the view. According to the register, in recent years a pine plantation from the Civilian Conservation Corps era grew to about 80 feet and restricted the view. A timely timber harvest opened the vista again and provided timber for various state projects. The register does not say when the harvest happened.[^1]

The Forest Fire Service's Division B page says all of its towers are still in active service, although the same list records the Lakewood tower as decommissioned in July 2024. It also lists the Lebanon tower in Brendan Byrne State Forest.[^3] Nothing read for this story describes public visits to the cab, so the tower should be treated as a working state post rather than a place to climb.[^1][^3]
