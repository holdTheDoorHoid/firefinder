Pilot Knob is a summit in Clay County, Tennessee, which a 1999 county high-point trip report puts at 1,400 feet or higher.[^1] The same report describes a fire tower at the top.[^1]

Tennessee Landforms lists the tower at 1,398 feet, numbered 4-9, and marks it standing.[^2] On that site, the numbers are state forest tower numbers, district first and then tower, taken from a 1976 list.[^2]

We found no source that says when the tower was built or who designed it. The National Register form that covers the Tennessee Division of Forestry's fire towers, from 1933 to 1975, lists a Pilot Knob in Clay County among the towers still standing on March 26, 2014.[^3] That is the latest confirmation of the tower's condition that we found. The same list also has a different Pilot Knob in Cumberland County, so check the county before you rely on any report.[^3]

The 1999 report tells walkers to park at a gate on Pilot Knob Road and walk about a mile up to the tower.[^1] It does not say whether climbing is allowed, and we found no current access rules. Treat the gate as a sign that access may be limited.

A business directory entry calls the site the Tennessee State Forestry Dale Hollow Fire Tower and places it on Pilot Knob at Celina.[^4] The entry is undated and gives no history, so we treat that name as unconfirmed.

A May 10, 2023 post on the Abandoned Online site has a title about a Pilot Knob tower that ends in the word "Abandoned." The page names no county and says nothing else about the tower, so we cannot tell whether it means this one.[^5] Until someone confirms the tower's condition on the ground, treat the 2014 listing as the most recent record, not a guarantee. Do not climb it unless the landowner or the state says it is open.
