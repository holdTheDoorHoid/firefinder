Pinnacle Mountain lies near the McDowell and Rutherford county line in North Carolina.[^1] In November 1931 a cabin was being built on top of it to help locate forest fires, and the state's conservation department was helping put it up.[^1] The state's 1930-to-1932 report describes a 10 x 12 cabin used by the fire-season lookout man on duty.[^1]

By 1932 a wooden structure stood on the summit.[^1] In May a newspaper reported that a steel tower would replace it, at a cost of between $700 and $900.[^1] In November the paper said a 47-foot steel tower costing $850 would soon be erected, with the state paying half and Rutherford County and a lumber company paying the rest. It was meant to serve the state's fire-protection work in the South Mountain area.[^1]

The state's report for 1932 to 1934 says a 45-foot 9-inch steel tower, paid for with ECW funds, took the place of the wooden one.[^1] The newspaper planned 47 feet, while the report gives 45 feet 9 inches for the tower that went up, so the report's figure is used here.[^1]

No source I could read describes the summit today. It is not known from these sources whether the steel tower or the cabin still stands, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1]
