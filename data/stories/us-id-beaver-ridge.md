Beaver Ridge Lookout stands on the Clearwater National Forest in Idaho County, at about 7,370 feet, within the Powell Ranger District.[^1]

The site's history starts with a camp in 1919.[^2][^3] In 1922 the Forest Service built a log cabin with a cupola there, and the National Historic Lookout Register counts it as the first lookout at the site.[^1] The current tower, an R-6 flat cab with a catwalk on a 12-foot base, went up in 1963 and replaced that cabin.[^1][^2] The sources agree on 1963 for the current cab but not on its base material.[^1][^2][^4] The register and Idaho Fire Lookouts describe concrete, while another history page describes cinder block.[^1][^3][^4]

In August 1919, a wireless telephone at Beaver Ridge was used to alert the forest ranger stationed at Lolo Hot Springs to a blaze.[^4] A Nevada newspaper said at the time that it was reportedly the first such report in history.[^4]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists known staff for 1977, 1979 to 1981, 1990 to 1998 and 2022 to 2024, and says the lookout is manned in each fire season.[^3]

The register added the lookout on April 24, 2009.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts describes it as drive-up and staffed.[^3] No rental listing turned up in our search, and the pages we read do not say whether visitors may go up into the cab. Anyone planning a visit should check current Forest Service guidance for the Powell Ranger District first.
