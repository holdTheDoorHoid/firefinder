Knapp Mound, in Jackson County, Wisconsin, has carried a lookout tower since at least 1932.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says that early tower was 72 feet tall and a Baker Improved Strengthened type.[^1] The tower standing there now is a 68-foot Wisconsin standard model with a cage-protected ladder.[^1] The register does not say when it was built.[^1]

The state operated 119 fire towers in the early 1930s, and most of the 72 it was retiring were built in that decade.[^2][^3]

Years of vandalism led to the cab being replaced in 2008.[^1] The tower was staffed on and off during spring fire season until 2015.[^1]

After assessing the towers, the Department of Natural Resources found them unsafe, and replacing them was judged too costly.[^2] Sources disagree on when the network ended. The register and WJFW point to 2015, while the Journal Sentinel reported in December 2015 that the towers would leave service in 2016.[^1][^4][^2] Some have been dismantled, but many still stand as memorials to that work.[^4]

The register lists the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources as administrator, though that may be out of date after the statewide decommissioning.[^1] The Wisconsin lookout list marks Knapp Mound as standing.[^5] I found no public access information for this tower, so this story does not suggest a visit.[^1]
