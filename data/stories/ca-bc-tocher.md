Tocher Lookout stands on Tocher Ridge, in Yoho National Park, British Columbia.[^1] Arvid Backman's 1941 report named the ridge as a secondary site for a lookout.[^2] The site came up again in 1960.[^2] Another five years passed before steps were taken to build a lookout there and to shut the Mt. King station.[^2]

A travel blog dates the lookout to the mid-1960s.[^3] A heritage evaluation, copied onto a scouts' web page with no named author, says its design drew on drawings of a lookout in Terra Nova National Park, and that it has obliquely sloping windows.[^2]

The evaluation says the lookout was in use for seven years, and was an active lookout before 1972.[^2] The blog says it operated for only a few years before it was abandoned.[^3] The evaluation also says the lookout missed the large Amiskwi fire of 1971.[^2]

Much of its original metal cladding is gone, the evaluation says, but the interior still holds furniture and equipment from its days as a lookout.[^2] In July 2000, a group of Scouts took on a project to help restore it after a Parks Canada volunteer coordinator offered them the challenge.[^4]

Today a blog describes the lookout as in excellent condition, with relics from its lookout days still inside.[^3] Parks Canada lists the hike as 17.9 km one way from the Natural Bridge parking area, ending with a steep 4.8 km climb that gains 915 m (3,002 ft).[^1] Parks Canada advises that only very strong hikers attempt it, and says camping is not allowed.[^1]
