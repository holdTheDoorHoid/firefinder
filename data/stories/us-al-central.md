The Register names the U.S. Forest Service as the administrator of the Central Lookout Tower, which stands in Bankhead National Forest in Lawrence County, Alabama.[^1]

Newspapers from 1929 followed its construction.[^2] In May the Birmingham News reported that a new 100-foot steel observation tower would be built near the middle of the forest on Cheatham Road.[^2] By October the paper said it was nearing completion and would be called the Central lookout, to tell it apart from the forest's other towers.[^2] The Register says it was built in 1930 or earlier, and a 1930 map shows a Central Tower east of Cheatham Road.[^1]

The Register describes a 100-foot tower that is not an Aermotor. Its writer believes the International Derrick Company made it but could not confirm that.[^1]

The 1929 report names Silas Black as the tower's keeper and says he had moved his family into a government-erected home.[^2] In June 1930 the Decatur Daily reported that Mollie Downing, who was well past seventy, had climbed to the top, the oldest woman yet to do so.[^2] In April 1940 the same paper said Frank Armor would be chief observer at the tower for the Third Army's aircraft-warning service during May maneuvers, with help from two Civilian Conservation Corps men and a Moulton resident.[^2]

The Register records a site visit on November 26, 2022, and the tower was registered on August 1, 2023. It does not say whether the public may climb the tower.[^1]

A visitor kiosk for the North Alabama birding trail sits near the tower, and the trail guide says to reach it by driving south on AL Highway 33 about 11.4 miles from Moulton.[^3] The guide does not say whether visitors may climb the tower, so ask forest staff before going up.[^3]
