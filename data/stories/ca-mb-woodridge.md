The Woodridge fire tower stood in Woodridge Provincial Park, in the Rural Municipality of Piney, Manitoba.[^1] The Carillon reports that it was first built around 1960.[^1] A door covered the ladder that ran to the top.[^1]

Gary Hora, president of the Woodridge ATV Sandhogs, explained that the tower and the land beneath it were first under Manitoba Conservation, and later moved to Manitoba Parks after the province's fire towers became obsolete.[^2]

Towers were often staffed by local people, since a watcher nearby could respond faster than someone who had to travel to the tower.[^1] Bruce Therrien, a Sandilands resident, worked in the tower for several summers in the 1980s and early 2000s.[^1] Before satellite imaging, he relied on a compass and a pair of binoculars to find fires.[^1] From the tower he could see about 32 km (20 miles) in every direction on a clear day, and he sometimes spent up to 12 hours a day there when fire risk was high.[^1] When a fire was spotted, the watcher took measurements of its location and passed them to other towers, which allowed the blaze to be pinpointed by triangulation.[^1] Manitoba's towers first detected fires in 1913, and roughly 66 were built across the province, according to head researcher Gordon Goldsborough of the Manitoba Historical Society.[^1]

Councillor Ken Prociw told SteinbachOnline that the tower had not been used for wildfire detection in many years.[^2] The Carillon says it has not operated since 2010.[^1]

The province removed the tower on 12 November 2025, and the Manitoba Historical Society records it as demolished that day.[^1][^3] A provincial spokesperson said it was taken down over concerns about people climbing, and that earlier efforts to restrict access had not worked.[^1] Prociw and Hora both said residents got no public notice before it came down.[^2] Both thought safety and liability probably played a part, though neither knew the exact reason.[^2]

Locals had fought to keep it as a landmark, and it appeared on the Woodridge Community Hall mural.[^2][^1]

The site was part of Woodridge Provincial Park, and Prociw says it is still used for snowmobile staging, picnics and geocaching.[^1] The Sandhogs have held permits for the site since 2015 and use it to load and unload ATVs for trail riding.[^2] Manitoba Parks owns the parcel, which Hora says is 0.45 hectares (1.1 acres), the smallest park in Manitoba.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the tower's base today. Therrien hopes for a monument to mark where the tower stood.[^1]
