Laurel Hill, also called Laurel Mountain, is a summit in Lane County, Oregon.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com puts it about 11 miles south of Cottage Grove.[^3] A 1945 Forest Log item quoted on the Forest Lookouts site puts it about 20 miles south.[^2] The Eastern Lane FPA administered the site.[^1][^3]

Sources differ on what the first lookout was. One account says a camp was built in 1930, with a cab set atop a 45-foot tree in 1942.[^1][^3] Another describes a hip-roofed cab on a seven-foot tower, erected in 1942.[^2]

The cabin burned to the ground in March 1945, according to a Forest Log item quoted on the Forest Lookouts site. The item said plans called for a new 50-foot tower with a 14-by-14 cabin on top, subject to securing the materials.[^2] A 1945 Eastern Lane District annual report, also quoted there, describes a live-in lookout house on a 50-foot tower that cost $1,900.[^2] Firetower.org and firelookout.com both describe a 41-foot treated timber tower built in 1945, placed one mile west of the earlier site on firetower.org and half a mile west of the summit on firelookout.com.[^1][^3] The sources do not agree on that tower's height or location, and we could not settle it.

A 25-foot tower followed in 1954. Firetower.org calls it an L-4 flat tower, and firelookout.com describes it with a live-in cab on the summit.[^1][^3]

An article dated January 28, 1946, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, profiles Mary Bea Hall, an 18-year-old University of Oregon sophomore posted to the remote summit.[^2] The article says she had done that work for the last two summers and credits her with reporting every fire across a 256-square-mile expanse of Douglas fir that could be seen from the summit.[^2]

Firetower.org and firelookout.com both say the lookouts were all removed by 1979.[^1][^3] Neither source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit now, or whether visitors may go there. Check with the land managers before you plan a trip.

The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register lists the site as US 877, OR 244. It was registered on March 25, 2011, on the nomination of Ray Kresek.[^1]
