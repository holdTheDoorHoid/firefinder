The DeSoto Falls Tower stood on Lookout Mountain in DeKalb County, Alabama, on ground that is now part of DeSoto State Park.[^1][^3] The park is about eight miles northeast of Fort Payne, and it was dedicated as DeSoto State Park in 1939.[^3]

The tower was a 100-foot Aermotor MC-39 steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Alabama Forestry Commission relocated it and reconstructed it at this site in 1952.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register describes it the same way.[^2] The register's "Built" field also gives 1952, but neither register says when the tower was first put up or where it stood before it was moved.[^1][^2]

The description places the tower near DeSoto Falls, which Wikipedia calls the state's highest waterfall at 104 feet.[^1][^3] The register says the Alabama Division of State Parks managed the site and that the Alabama Forestry Commission administered it.[^1]

We found no source that names anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched. For context, a manuscript history of Alabama forestry, submitted in March 1961, says 157 lookout towers made up the state's forest-fire detection system.[^4]

The National Historic Lookout Register accepted the tower on January 7, 2009, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register added the site on October 6, 2017.[^1][^2]

Both registers date the tower's removal to around 2010, so there is no tower to climb at the site.[^1][^2]

DeSoto State Park is a public recreation area with 25 miles of hiking trails, and the falls lie within it.[^3] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the tower site, so visitors should ask park staff before looking for it.
