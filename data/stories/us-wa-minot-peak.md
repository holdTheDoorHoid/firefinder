Minot Peak, in Grays Harbor County, got its first lookout structure in 1929.[^1][^2][^3] Sources disagree on what it was. Firetower.org calls it a cabin.[^1] Two other histories say a tower was erected that year, citing the 22nd annual report of the Washington Forest Fire Association.[^3][^4] In 1935 a 50-foot pole tower with an observation room went up.[^1][^4]

George M. Landon was the lookout in 1930.[^4] That August a fire, believed to have been started by berry pickers or campers, climbed the mountain and forced him off his post.[^3] In July 1933 the lookout, which a newspaper says the state division of forestry maintained, spotted smoke from a burn at the open-air theater site in Sylvia Lake Park. The burn led to a charge against Chapin Collins, president of the Montesano Chamber of Commerce, who pleaded guilty.[^3][^4]

Ben Harner staffed the tower for several years in the early 1950s, followed by Martha Depoe of Shelton.[^4]

During World War II the site was used for aircraft warning. A May 1944 entry calls it Aircraft Warning Service Station "How 1-6." The service used state-owned facilities and added a 12-by-20-foot garage. When it ended, the state kept the structures for fire detection.[^3]

In late November 1945 a storm with gusts of 72 miles per hour brought the tower down after an anchor gave way.[^4] It was re-erected and put back in use in 1946.[^4]

In 1959 a standard 40-foot timber tower, with a cabin the lookout could live in, replaced the earlier one.[^1][^4] Both histories say that last lookout was destroyed about 1971.[^1][^4]

Today the summit, according to WillhiteWeb's peakbagging page, is "packed full of communication towers."[^4] We found no source describing any lookout remains there.

The guide lists a Weyerhaeuser permit area, under an Aberdeen general access permit.[^4] It gives about five miles from the Boy Scout camp on the P-100 line, or about one mile with a motorized access permit. Even with the permit, the last stretch is a gated road you walk.[^4] The one-mile approach depends on a motorized access permit, so check its terms before you go.[^4]
