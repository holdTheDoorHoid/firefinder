Medicine Point Lookout sits in the Bitterroot Mountains about 15 miles south of Darby, Montana, on land that's now part of the Bitterroot National Forest. Accounts of exactly when it went up differ: the National Historic Lookout Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps put up the tower in 1930, then added a pyramid-roof L-4 cab in 1933, resting it on an 8-foot wooden support.[^1] Firelookout.com instead gives 1940 as the build year for a similar L-4 cab on a 10-foot pole tower.[^2] The National Register of Historic Places nomination lists 1939 as the structure's period of significance, which sits closer to the earlier date.[^3] Whichever year is right, the cab itself is small: a 14-foot square with a 30-inch-wide walkway wrapping around it.[^4]

A number of lookouts staffed Medicine Point over the following decades. The earliest name on record is Mr. and Mrs. Raymond Barnett in 1946, followed over the years by Jack and his wife Schlager, Mr. and Mrs. Dale Dye, Luther Britton (in both 1957 and 1959), Walter Martin, Ralph Robinson III, Jim Fazio, Bill Friermuth, Kelsey Milner, James Maclean, Del Reynolds, Jim Kautz, and finally Rene Eustace in 1976, the last recorded staffing year.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register notes it was used only sporadically through the 1980s before going quiet.[^1][^2]

The tower was restored in 1999 and furnished to reflect its 1940s working days, down to a period stove, furniture, and dishes.[^1][^4] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register that October and later listed on the National Register of Historic Places.[^1][^3]

Medicine Point is public land administered by the Forest Service and open to rental through Recreation.gov. It's not a drive-up site: reaching it takes a roughly 3.5-mile hike with about 2,000 feet of elevation gain, and snowmobiles and other off-road vehicles aren't allowed on the trail, though horses are.[^4] The cab sleeps up to four, has a wood stove, and comes with a bow saw and ax for cutting firewood, but no running water — renters need to bring their own.[^4]
