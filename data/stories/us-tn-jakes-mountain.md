Jakes Mountain is a knob on the Cumberland Plateau in Sequatchie County, with an elevation of about 2,204 feet.[^1][^2] The earliest account we found is a newspaper report dated January 3, 1926, which the Forest Lookouts website quotes from the Kingsport Times.[^1] It describes a lookout tower of about seventy feet that was still to be built on the summit.[^1] A lookout man would watch for fires in the spring and fall and report them to the headquarters of the Rocky River Coal and Lumber Company at Campaign.[^1] The plan also called for about thirteen miles of telephone line, so that fire crews could be alerted quickly.[^1]

The report is written in the future tense, so it describes a plan rather than a finished tower. It also places the mountain in Van Buren County, while Tennessee Landforms and the National Register form both put it in Sequatchie County.[^1][^2][^3] The plan dates from 1926, before many of the Division of Forestry's towers went up in the 1930s, so it is unclear whether the company tower was ever built.[^1][^3] The National Register form lists Jakes Mountain among its Sequatchie County entries, with no further detail.[^3]

We found no build date, staffing record or named fire for Jakes Mountain. Tennessee Landforms lists the tower as standing.[^2] The same page notes that many towers have been taken down and that most sites are privately owned.[^2] It also warns against climbing, since the steps are frequently missing or decayed.[^2] We found no public visiting arrangement, so please do not climb it. Ask the landowner before going near the site.
