The Farris lookout stood in Walker County, about 14 miles southwest of Huntsville, reached by a dirt road.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list calls it Farris (West Sandy).[^2]

A 1935 Coast and Geodetic Survey description ties the survey mark to the tower's own site, so the tower was already standing by then.[^1] The mark sat about 64 feet north of the tower's center, and it was also about 64 feet from the southernmost pole of a telephone line that ran to the tower.[^1]

For context, a Texas column on East Texas fire lookout towers says spotting fires from trees was seldom effective, and that the Forest Service then began building metal lookout towers at strategic locations in the forests.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Farris tower as gone.[^2]

In 1937 the Texas Highway Department recovered the survey mark and its two reference disks, and found them in good condition.[^1] The datasheet records no later recovery.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, stands on the site today, or says whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2]
