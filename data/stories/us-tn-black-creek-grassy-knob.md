The Black Creek Tower rises above Robbins, Tennessee, on a ridge locals call Grassy Knob, between the Clear Fork and New Rivers.[^1][^3] The State of Tennessee built it in 1951, an eighty-foot, four-sided steel truss tower bolted together from galvanized steel members stamped with the mark of the W.S. Co. steel manufacturer.[^2][^3] A seven-by-seven-foot cab with bands of nine-light steel sash windows tops the structure, giving a full 360-degree view meant for spotting smoke across the surrounding hills; twelve steel staircases with wood plank treads climb to it.[^2] Around the base the state also put up an operator's cabin, a crew house, and several utility buildings, all raised at roughly the same time to support the people who staffed the tower.[^3]

Rangers used Black Creek for fire detection from 1951 until 1967.[^3] Tennessee lists it alongside Smith Knob Tower in Oneida as one of only two towers in Scott County that the state's Division of Forestry still operates when conditions call for it, and the National Historic Lookout Register's entry likewise describes it as "still serviceable when the need to use it arises."[^1][^3] It was formally entered on the National Historic Lookout Register in 2014, and it is also listed on the National Register of Historic Places, recognized for its engineering and its role in the state's fire-conservation history between 1951 and 1967.[^1][^4][^3]

Today the tower still stands on Grassy Knob, with the old operator's cabin now used as a storage shed and the crew house and other outbuildings still in place nearby.[^3] It is not set up as a public rental, and sources describe it as a state forestry asset rather than a visitor attraction, so anyone interested should check current access rules with the Tennessee Division of Forestry before heading out.
