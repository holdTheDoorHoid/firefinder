Osier Ridge has had a lookout watching over the Clearwater National Forest since 1921, when the Forest Service put up a log cabin and a 30-foot pole platform tower at the site.[^1][^2] That first structure lasted until 1938, when it was replaced by a taller pole tower, about 40 to 55 feet depending on the source, topped with an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] The tower standing today went up in 1961: a flat-roofed, 15-foot-square R-6 cab ringed by a catwalk, mounted on a timber tower given variously as 40 or 41 feet tall.[^1][^2][^3]

Unlike many of its peers, Osier Ridge was never retired. It has stayed in active use, staffed through the fire season to this day.[^1][^3] The known staffing record stretches back decades: Mark E. Lawrence worked a partial season there in 1931, and a married couple ran the lookout together in the summer of 1958 after applying out of the Kelly Creek Ranger Station, continuing into 1959.[^2] More recent volunteers have kept the tradition going, including staff who have described the thunderstorms and sunsets visible from the catwalk.[^2]

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2009.[^4] It remains a drive-up site on the Clearwater National Forest, reachable by road, and is staffed seasonally rather than available as a public rental — a visitor is as likely to find it occupied by a Forest Service volunteer as empty.
