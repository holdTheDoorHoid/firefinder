Mount Ashland is a former lookout site in Jackson County, Oregon, on the Rogue River National Forest, about 8 miles south of the city of Ashland.[^1][^2] The Forest Service administered the site, and the register gives its elevation as 7,533 feet, or 2,296 meters.[^1]

In 1922 a hip-roofed cabin was built at ground level on the site.[^1][^2]

Work on the last four miles of a Forest Service telephone line to the station began in 1934, done by a group of CCC crews.[^3] In 1935 a newspaper reported that Bert Peachy of Ashland had been sent up as an emergency lookout.[^3] The chronology says Ali J. Sandoz was sent to Mt. Ashland in 1938 to replace CCC men on duty there, and that Al J. Sandoz took up lookout duties in 1939.[^3] In 1940 the same chronology quotes a report that the mountain, ordinarily an emergency lookout, would not be manned for the time being.[^3]

Use of the lookout ended in 1942, according to Forest Lookouts.[^3] Firelookout.com also gives 1942 as the year the cabin was abandoned.[^2] The register and Forest Lookouts both say the structure was removed in 1959.[^1][^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, and none says whether the site is open to visitors. Wikipedia notes that a ski area operates on the mountain, but the sources we read do not say whether the old lookout site lies within it.[^4]
