The McDougal-Thorne Fire Tower, near Little and Big McDougal Lakes in Ontario, is eight-sided and still has its cab.[^1] The Firelookout.ca contributor who described it was not sure of the exact spot, but said the tower almost certainly stands on one side of the two lakes.[^1]

Most of Ontario's fire towers were built between the 1920s and the 1950s, during the peak years of forest fire protection.[^2] Responsibility for them passed to the province's Department of Lands and Forests, which became the Ministry of Natural Resources in 1972.[^2] From the 1960s on, aerial spotting and firefighting crews took over, and the towers saw less use.[^2] A fan-run history site says that, as of 2013, the Ontario ministry had decided to remove many towers still standing. It does not name the McDougal-Thorne tower.[^2]

The Firelookout.ca entry does not say who owns the tower or whether it is open to visitors, so do not climb it.[^1]
