Ironside Mountain Lookout stood in the Shasta-Trinity National Forest in Trinity County, California, at 5,169 feet.[^1] A 1961 geodetic survey description places a 14-by-14-foot building, 20 feet high, on the summit.[^3] We could not tell from that description whether the 20 feet covers only the cab or the whole tower, and we did not find a build year in the sources we read.

The earliest staffing we found is from 1911. A newspaper item reported that a fire guard had returned from his stay at the Ironsides Lookout Station.[^3] Later items name Wm. Yates in charge in 1915, Frank Hoffman stationed there in 1921, and Jas. E. Olsen on the lookout in 1926.[^3] A 1931 item says a telephone line had been finished to the lookout.[^3] In 1934 a district ranger and a colleague went up to "relocate the lookout to be stationed there," and the item does not explain what that meant.[^3]

In 1956 a Forest Service lookout was posted on the mountain, and Mrs. Gertrude Church of Southern California was named as the lookout.[^3] The same item says a jeep road was now open to the lookout and that a helicopter landing area had been built at the site.[^3]

A visit report on the Register, dated June 23, 2019, answers yes to "Good condition?"[^1] The same report says that Greg was staffing the lookout when the writers arrived.[^1]

The lookout was evacuated and wrapped against a wildfire threat in July 2008. In August 2015 it was wrapped in fire-resistant material as the River Complex fire advanced.[^3]

The Monument Fire burned the lookout on August 13, 2021.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says the tower was destroyed by the Monument Fire in August 2021, and the Register lists it as removed in 2021.[^2][^1] Local efforts asked for the tower to be rebuilt, and the 2021 chapter report says no word has come from the Shasta-Trinity archaeologist on what the future holds.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, is left at the site today, or whether the site is open to visitors. The Register's visit notes describe the approach as a narrow gravel road off Highway 299, but those notes were written about a standing tower.[^1] Anyone thinking of a visit should ask the Shasta-Trinity National Forest first.
