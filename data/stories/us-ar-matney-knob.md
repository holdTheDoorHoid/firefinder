Matney Knob is a hill about two miles west-southwest of Norfork, in Baxter County.[^1] In November 1933 the National Ozark Forest contracted to buy 1,750 acres that took in the knob. The Baxter Bulletin reported that the Forest Service would build a road to the crest and a lookout tower there, to be used for fire prevention.[^2] The same paper said the Civilian Conservation Corps company camped at Push Mountain would do the work.[^2] A September 1935 item said the tower had been completed a few months earlier, which puts its completion around 1935.[^2]

A 1953 U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey note gives the tower a steel frame on four legs, about 100 feet (30.5 m) tall, finished in aluminum paint.[^1]

Keepers show up in the local paper. A man named Mayford Huffines was called the tower keeper in December 1934, and a July 1935 item said he had kept the tower most of the past year.[^2] An October 1938 item said he had kept the Matney Knob tower for two years, was moving to the Devil's Knob tower, and that it was not known who would succeed him.[^2] The transcriptions spell his name several ways, including Huggines.[^2] In May 1946 Mr. Vickers and his family moved to what the paper called the Matney Tower.[^2]

A trail guide for the Ozark Highlands Trail describes the hill as having a cell and radio tower on it.[^3] None of the sources I read says whether the old lookout cab is still standing on the summit, so I cannot say what remains.[^3]

The Ozark Highlands Trail stretch from point 41 to the Matney Knob trailhead (point 42) is for hikers only, with no ATV or horse use.[^4] I found no source that closes the knob, but do not climb any tower you find there.[^4]
