Antrim Point was a cleared top near the Bitterroot River, about eight miles north of Stevensville in the Bitterroot National Forest.[^1][^2] A Forest Service survey station was set there in 1937, and the Forest Lookouts page dates the lookout site to the same year.[^2] The firelookout.com listing gives 1939 instead, when an L-4 cab was relocated there from Dutch Hill.[^1]

The survey brass tablet sat in a concrete post under the center of the lookout tower, and the U.S. Geological Survey recovered it in 1957.[^2] An eight-foot R-6 flat cab replaced the L-4 in 1968.[^1]

The named lookouts include Mr. and Mrs. Merrill Edmunds in 1943, Mr. and Mrs. Joe Ayres in 1946, and Ann Casselman from 1969 to 1971.[^1][^2]

The Forest Lookouts page says the lookout duties moved to Lookout Mountain in 1973.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register and firelookout.com date the cab's move to 1974, five years after it was built at Antrim Point.[^3][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list still marks Antrim Point as gone.[^5]

The cab now stands on Lookout Mountain, about 12 miles south of the West Fork ranger station, and the lookout there gets regular staff in fire season.[^1][^3][^4]

We found no source that describes what remains at the original point today, or whether the 1937 survey tablet survives.
