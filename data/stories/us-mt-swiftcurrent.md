Swiftcurrent Lookout sits above Swiftcurrent Lake in Glacier National Park, and it looks different from most of its neighbors. Rather than the usual catwalked timber cab, a 14-foot-square lookout box rests on a stone foundation roughly 10 feet tall, roofed in flagstone set in mortar, with chalet-style detailing borrowed from the nearby Many Glacier Hotel.[^1][^2] It went up in 1936, one of the newer additions to the park's chain of fire-watch posts, and its design was adapted from the Forest Service's standard plans.[^2][^3] Of all the lookouts in Glacier, it alone lacks a catwalk around the cab.[^1]

The lookout was staffed for decades as part of the park's fire-detection network, but that service lapsed for over ten years: no one held the post after 1990 until a refurbishment allowed staffing to begin again in 2001.[^3] Swiftcurrent was added to the National Register of Historic Places in December 1986 and registered on the National Historic Lookout Register in September 1991.[^1][^2]

One source gives the build year as 1935 rather than 1936; the National Historic Lookout Register, the National Register nomination, and the NRHP listing itself agree on 1936, so that is the better-supported date.[^1][^2]

Today the lookout is still standing and is a popular hiking destination, reachable by trail in the Many Glacier area of the park.[^1] As a working National Park Service fire lookout, it is not open to the public for entry or overnight rental; visitors can admire it from outside while it continues, when staffed, to watch for wildfire smoke across the Many Glacier valley.[^1][^3]
