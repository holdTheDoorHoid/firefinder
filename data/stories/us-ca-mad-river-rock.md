Mad River Rock is a rock outcrop on Mad River Ridge in Trinity County, inside Six Rivers National Forest, about 4,300 feet above sea level.[^1][^2]

In July 1934 a local paper reported that the Forest Service was building a new road to the rock, along with a new lookout, and that the Hetten Peak lookout was to be dropped.[^1] A 1935 Oakland Tribune feature, quoted on the same site, describes the work.[^1] The nearest highway was eight miles away.[^1] A crew of CCC youths and a bulldozer pushed a road through in ten days, though the superintendent had been given eight days.[^1] A high line swung lumber and other materials to the top, and 230 steps led up the rock.[^1]

The Kemnow site marks Mad River Rock as removed, with no date given.[^1] A trip report says a lookout once stood on the summit but was taken down long ago.[^2] The writer found traces of an old trail on the north side, the remains of wooden stairs, a few remains of the old tower, and old wood scattered about.[^2]

The trip report suggests Mad River Rock may be the most technically demanding P1K (a peak with more than 1,000 feet of prominence) in the state.[^2] This story therefore offers no route to the top. No source says whether the public may go to the summit.
