In April 1921, the Burlington Free Press reported that D. D. Holmes of Morristown had been hired for the summer as Mount Mansfield's fire lookout.[^1] He was also to act as custodian of Taft Lodge, roughly half a mile from the summit.[^1]

Taft Lodge was a shelter for hikers, and the Green Mountain Club's history of it does not mention a lookout.[^3] The Green Mountain Club says Clarence Cowles supervised its construction in 1920, and that the lodge was popular with hikers from the start.[^3] Caretakers were not based there again until 1969.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Mount Mansfield site with an unknown type of structure and an abandoned status.[^2]

Mount Mansfield lies within Mount Mansfield State Forest, and the Long Trail runs along its main ridge.[^4] A steep toll road climbs from Stowe to The Nose, where several regional radio and TV stations keep transmitter towers.[^4]

No source read describes what, if anything, remains of a fire lookout on the summit.[^1][^2][^4]
