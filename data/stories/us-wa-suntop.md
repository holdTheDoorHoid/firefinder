Sources don't fully agree on when Suntop Lookout went up. The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1932, Wikipedia says 1932 or 1933, the National Register of Historic Places nomination lists 1933, and firelookout.com says 1934.[^1][^2][^3] Whatever the exact year, it follows the Forest Service's standard "L-4 Lookout House" plan: a fourteen-foot-square, single-story frame cab at ground level, topped with a pyramid roof, with big windows on each face and sunshade shutters that swing out on hinges.[^2] It stands in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, about fifteen miles northeast of Mount Rainier, on a high alpine meadow with a view across to the Winthrop Glacier.[^1][^2]

Of the L-4 ground houses once built across the old Snoqualmie National Forest, only two are still standing, and Suntop is one of them.[^1][^2] During World War II it served as a lookout post in the U.S. Army's Aircraft Warning System, watching the sky rather than just the woods.[^1][^2] The road up to it, Forest Road 7315, wasn't finished until 1956; before that, reaching the lookout took real effort.[^1]

The lookout was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on July 14, 1987, and added to the National Historic Lookout Register on September 15, 1994.[^2] It has never been abandoned outright. It's staffed on and off by volunteers, including members of the Snoqualmie Fire Lookout Association, though staffing isn't constant; it was closed through the 2020-21 seasons because of COVID.[^1][^3] As of 2025, the interior has been fitted out to reflect 1960s-era fire equipment, with period pieces like a crank phone and an old helmet, and the site has gained a new approach trail, a deck, and a ramp that lets it be reached by visitors with disabilities.[^1]

Today the final mile is gated, and it typically stays that way except when someone is staffing the lookout or crews are up there working, so visitors cover that stretch on foot.[^1] The lookout is open to the public in summer months, but it is not a rental; visitors can look around and, when it's staffed, talk with the volunteers on duty.[^1][^2]
