A July 1931 newspaper said work on an 80-foot fire tower near Town Hill in Allegany County was due to start within about two weeks.[^1] By October the Midland Journal called it the fourth tower erected that summer, on the crest of Town Hill at 1,760 feet above sea level.[^1] The tower was meant to protect newly bought State Forest land and the Billmeyer State Game Refuge, and the paper said it was fitted with telephone connections and fire-finding instruments.[^1]

The state took the site by deed on 12 November 1931.[^3] The 2007 state report lists it under the Forest Service, on Green Ridge State Forest land.[^3] That report gives the base elevation as 1,685 feet, not 1,760, and we could not reconcile the two figures.[^3]

A state report from April 2007 found the tower structurally sound, with its cab, stairs and equipment buildings in good repair and its cabin windows recently repaired.[^3] The report covers towers as of December 2006, so conditions may have changed since then.[^3] The same report recommends keeping the site as a communications site.[^3] By then the site held county antennas on the forestry tower and state antennas on a newer tower, all inside a fenced compound.[^3] Its deed says the land reverts to the grantors, their heirs or assigns, if the state stops using it for forestry purposes.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^2] Its legend marks towers whose cab is gone with an asterisk, and the Town Hill entry has none.[^2]

The site fronts on Mountain Road, north of U.S. Route 40.[^3] Because it is a fenced communications compound, please do not climb the tower or go inside the fence.
