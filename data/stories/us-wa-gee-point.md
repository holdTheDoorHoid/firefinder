Gee Point is a former lookout site on the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest in Skagit County, Washington, at about 4,974 feet (1,516 meters).[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers the land.[^1]

The lookout was an L-5 cab built in 1934.[^1][^2][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register says an L-5 is a 10-by-10-foot cab, part of a family of hip-roofed cabs that also includes the larger L-4.[^5]

During the Second World War the site was an Aircraft Warning Service station, called Yoke 4-5.[^3][^4] The station used existing forest facilities, and the Forest Service kept the site for fire detection afterward.[^3][^4]

The station was activated on April 6, 1942, and deactivated on October 16, 1943.[^3] The same page also gives October 16, 1944 for the inactivation, and Willhite's page gives 10/16/43, so the sources disagree on the end year.[^3][^4]

The cab was abandoned in 1964.[^1][^2][^4] The register says the lookout is gone, and the Washington lookouts index marks the site as removed.[^1][^3]

Willhite reports the remains of a cabin on the route to the summit. He thinks a cabin below the summit was likely built for the wartime station.[^4]

The route starts at a large parking area at the end of a forest road and gains about 700 feet.[^4] Willhite's page asks visitors to call ahead, because forest thinning was being planned.[^4] Check with the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest before you go.
