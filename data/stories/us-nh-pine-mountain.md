Pine Mountain's lookout began in 1910, when a station was set up on the peak in Gorham.[^1][^3] That same year ten new towers were built, and seven of them, Pine among them, were paid for by the New Hampshire Timberland Owners Association.[^3] A new and improved watch tower went up in the 1915–1916 biennium.[^1] By 1922 the station had been temporarily shut down, except as an emergency post.[^1]

The Great New England Hurricane destroyed the lookout on September 21, 1938.[^1] NH Family Hikes reports that many towers lost in the 1938 hurricane were swapped for sturdier new ones.[^3] An August 1939 newspaper item listed a tower for Pine, at 2,410 feet in Gorham, as under construction or planned.[^1] NH Family Hikes gives the summit's elevation as 2,404 feet, so the two figures differ slightly.[^4]

Today the summit belongs to the Horton Center, a privately operated camp and worship site.[^4] NH Family Hikes reports that the official summit still has four cement footings where a tower once stood.[^4] firelookout.org lists the tower as gone.[^2] The hike is about 3.5 miles out and back with a loop, starting from a lot on Pinkham B Road. That road is seasonal, so check its gate status on TrailsNH before going.[^4]
