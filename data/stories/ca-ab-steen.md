Steen is a steel fire lookout in the far north-west of Alberta.[^1] The register places it at 59.63 N, 117.79 W, and gives 2015 as the year the present structure was built.[^1] The register gives its height as 30 m (98 ft), on ground 705 m (2,313 ft) above sea level.[^1]

Alberta says its fire lookouts are located where visibility is favourable to detect and report wildfires.[^2] Each site is staffed by a lookout observer who watches for fire all day, generally from April to October.[^3] Alberta's history of its lookouts says the towers stood on high boreal hills, raising the observer above anything that blocked the view.[^4] Seasonal hires who staffed the lookouts in fire season also kept up trails and phone lines and recorded the weather.[^4] The register lists a weather station at this site.[^1]

The register lists the tower as active.[^1]

The register codes the access mode as fly, which suggests the site is reached by air rather than by road.[^1] Alberta calls its lookout sites isolated, and says the staff who work them need to plan for a wide range of possibilities.[^3]

No source describes the site today. We cannot say what stands on the ground now or whether the public may go up.
