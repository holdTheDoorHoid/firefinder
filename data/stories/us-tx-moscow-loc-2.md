A Texas lookout list credits the Moscow tower in Polk County to the Texas Forest Service.[^2] A Coast and Geodetic Survey description written in 1949 calls the station Moscow Lookout Tower 17.[^1] It says the station sat on the highest point in the vicinity, about five miles west of the village of Moscow.[^1] The surveyors sighted on the peak of the roof.[^1]

The tower stood on four steel legs and carried a house at the top.[^1] The house had a pyramid-shaped roof, and the overall height was 110 feet, which converts to about 33.5 meters.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Moscow (Loc 2) site as gone, and it lists the other Polk County site, Moscow (Loc 1), as gone too.[^3] The 1949 description gives no coordinates, so it is not certain which of the two it describes.[^1]

No source describes the site now, so it is not known what, if anything, stands on the ground or whether the land is open to visitors.
