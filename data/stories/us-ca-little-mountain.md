San Bernardino County built its Little Mountain lookout in 1926, on high ground north of the city.[^1][^2] County Forester Roy M. Tuttle meant it to watch the San Bernardino mountains, and his patrols covered only land outside the national forest reserve.[^2] The Muscupiabe Land & Water Company deeded the site to the county in April 1926.[^2] The first station was a 7-by-7-foot wood cab on a 10-foot wood tower.[^1] A telephone line of more than a mile was built to connect the lookout with the city and the county forester's office.[^2] In August 1927 a newspaper reported that a heliograph would be installed there to flash signals to the federal lookout on Keller Peak.[^2]

In 1928 the county added a residence cabin under the tower.[^1][^2] Until then, the keeper, M.R. Jackson, had lived in a tent house.[^2] In 1938 a new 20-foot steel tower with an L4 cab replaced the original structure.[^1] By 1939 the state forestry department ran the station, with Milton R. Jackson in charge.[^2]

In September 1940 a brush fire, apparently started by hunters, swept to the summit and threatened the station.[^2] State Ranger Russell Z. Smith directed the crews that saved it.[^2] In September 1948 an army demolition squad, meaning to blow up munitions harmlessly, started a fire when bits of incendiaries landed in dry brush on the mountain.[^2] State Forester P.S. Black, who was in his lookout station, called for help, and a cleared patch of ground saved the station.[^2]

The register gives no date for the lookout's removal, and says only concrete footers are left.[^1] It also describes the site as state land, which does not match the 1926 county deed.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes the site after that. None says whether the public may visit, so anyone curious should first check who owns the land.
