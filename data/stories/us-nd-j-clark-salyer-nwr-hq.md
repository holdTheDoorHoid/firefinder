The headquarters of the J. Clark Salyer National Wildlife Refuge in McHenry County, North Dakota, once had a fire lookout tower.[^3][^4] The Fish and Wildlife Service says the refuge was first named the Lower Souris Refuge, and it was renamed for John Clark Salyer in 1967.[^5] A historic photograph of Dam 326, hosted by the Fish and Wildlife Service, has a caption that places the camera in the headquarters tower.[^4]

According to the register, the tower now in Rugby is a steel structure 110 feet tall, originally one of four at the refuge.[^1]

Its best-recorded chapter is its move. West Lookouts lists the headquarters tower as moved to Rugby in 1992.[^2] Local fundraising came before the move to the Prairie Village Museum that year, and the register prices the move at $12,000.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association pairs the Rugby tower with the headquarters site and lists the headquarters tower as removed.[^3] West Lookouts spells the refuge name "Sayler," while the register and the Fish and Wildlife Service use "Salyer."[^2][^1][^5]

No source we checked describes what remains at the headquarters site today.

The structure that stands now is at the Prairie Village Museum in Rugby.[^1][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing under the register number US 1414, ND 2, and the register's entry is dated March 25, 2020.[^3][^1] We found no source saying whether the tower can be climbed, so ask the museum before planning a visit.
