Snow Camp Mountain has carried a lookout in one form or another for well over a century. Accounts differ on exactly when it started: the National Historic Lookout Register says the first lookout went up in 1917, while firelookout.com traces it back further, to a tent camp in 1910 followed by a 14-by-14-foot cabin in 1924.[^1][^3] Whichever date marks the true beginning, the current building is the third structure to stand on the summit.[^2] During World War II the site did double duty, watching for fires and also serving as an Aircraft Warning System station scanning the coast for enemy aircraft.[^2]

The lookout that most visitors think of as "Snow Camp" was an R-6 flat-top cab built in 1958, which went on to be listed on the National Historic Lookout Register in 1991 and later worked as a Forest Service recreation rental.[^1][^3] That building burned in August 2002, destroyed by the Biscuit Fire, a massive blaze that eventually scorched more than half a million acres across southern Oregon and northern California.[^2][^3] The Forest Service rebuilt a replacement cabin on the same spot just two years later, in 2004, and that is the structure standing today.[^2][^3]

Snow Camp sits on the Rogue River-Siskiyou National Forest at an elevation of roughly 4,200 feet, on a summit shaped as much by fire as by its lookout history: the surrounding landscape still bears the marks of both the 2002 Biscuit Fire and the 2017 Chetco Bar Fire.[^2] On a clear day the view stretches to the Pacific, taking in Kellogg Beach, Point St. George, and Castle Rock near Crescent City, California.[^2]

Today the 15-by-15-foot cabin is rented out through Recreation.gov, run by the Gold Beach Ranger District.[^2] Guests can drive within sight of the lookout, but the final 200 yards to the summit are closed to public vehicles and have to be hiked in on foot, which can be a strenuous climb with gear.[^2] There's no water on site, so renters need to pack in their own, and entry is by a combination lock whose code comes with the booking confirmation.[^2]
