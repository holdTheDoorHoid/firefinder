The Porcupine lookout stood on a 2,252-foot summit in Thurston County, Washington.[^1] The register lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as administrator and calls the site Porcupine Mountain. Willhite and the Forest Lookouts site call the same summit Porcupine Ridge.[^1][^3][^4]

The Forest Lookouts site records a lookout constructed on the site in 1937, citing a Division of Forestry annual report.[^3] The tower was 100 feet tall and made of treated timber, with an 8-by-8-foot cab and an accompanying ground house.[^1]

Through the Second World War, the site ran year-round as an Aircraft Warning Service post.[^4] It was activated on March 9, 1942 under the Olympia Filter Center.[^3]

A 1952 newspaper item says one person held a job at the lookout station.[^3] A 1964 item describes a jeep ride up Porcupine Ridge to visit people at the lookout.[^3]

Sources differ on how the tower ended. The register says the structures were removed about 1968, while the firelookout.com page and Willhite say they were destroyed about 1968.[^1][^2][^4] The register added the site on June 20, 2012.[^1]

Today the summit is pretty much flat, with brush in places that makes the footings hard to find. Willhite saw debris dating from later years but could not link any of it to the tower.[^4] Aerial photos from 2015 still show a round patch of differently colored vegetation on the summit.[^4] Timber was cut from the forest around the summit about 2000.[^4]

The summit sits inside Weyerhaeuser's Vail Tree Farm, where a permit is required.[^4] A motorized permit takes you to a point just short of the site, and you bushwhack the rest of the way.[^4] Do not go without the landowner's permit.
