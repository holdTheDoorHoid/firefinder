The Sideling Hill fire tower stood in Buchanan State Forest, in Fulton County.[^1] In August 1922 a state forestry official inspected the planned spot for a fire observation tower on the hill.[^1] The next year the state's Department of Forests and Waters put up a sixty-foot steel tower there, to spot and report forest fires.[^1] A 1930 notice estimated the tower's coverage on a clear day at roughly 125,000 acres of forest land in Fulton and Bedford counties.[^1]

A 1924 notice placed the tower on Ball Hill, which could be reached by a road running south from the top of Sideling Hill, about a mile from the highway.[^1] It said many visitors had come to the tower since April, up to forty on a single day, and it praised the wide view over several counties.[^1]

In 1930 a notice also said the tower would get a beacon light, installed and kept up by the federal lighthouse service, to guide flyers on the mail line from Washington to Cleveland.[^1]

In July 1971 the Public Opinion newspaper noted that Anna May Mellott of Needmore, who had worked the Tuscarora tower, had been promoted to Sideling Hill that year.[^1]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains on Sideling Hill today, or whether the spot is open to visitors.
