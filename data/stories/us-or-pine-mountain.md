Pine Mountain sits east of Bend and south of U.S. Route 20.[^5] Money was raised in 1913 for a telephone line linking the Pine Mountain lookout to Bend,[^3] and in 1915 a Forest Service crew hauled lumber to Pine Mountain to build a lookout.[^3] A D-6 cupola cabin went up in 1921.[^1][^2] A 1931 newspaper report planned a new 50-foot tower, with the present one moved up the hill.[^3] In 1932 a 50-foot wooden tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab was added alongside the cabin.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree about what came next. The register says a 41-foot treated timber L-4 tower replaced both in 1951, while the Oregon lookout history describes a 28-foot tower with a 14-by-14 L-4 cab, completed in 1952.[^1][^3]

B.A. Johnson is named as the government lookout in 1918.[^3] Ernest Putnam was the lookout in October 1929, when lightning struck the lookout house. One paper said he was in the tower; another said he was resting on his bed.[^3] In July 1932 Putnam first spotted a fire in the Big Springs burn of 1930, just west of Bend.[^3]

The lookout was taken down for salvage in 1968.[^3] A different site history gives the tower's span as 1921 to 1951.[^2]

The mountain now has a University of Oregon observatory, eight miles south of Highway 20 from Millican.[^4] The observatory hosts public evenings on Friday and Saturday during 7 to 10 weekends between May and September.[^4] A Forest Service campground sits across from the observatory.[^4] Hikers can walk the ridge from the observatory to the true summit.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout today.
