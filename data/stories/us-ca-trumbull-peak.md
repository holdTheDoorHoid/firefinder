Trumbull Peak Lookout stands on Trumbull Peak in Mariposa County, California, at 1,448 meters, in the Stanislaus National Forest.[^1] The register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it from 1934 to 1935, and that the view looks up Yosemite Valley.[^1] The tower is 41 feet of steel with a metal cab, and a 19-by-30-foot quarters building sits partly on stilts because the slope is steep.[^1] The register listed the site in June 1998.[^1]

By 2012 the Forest Fire Lookout Association's California Sierra-Nevada chapter listed the lookout as abandoned.[^2] In 2011 the Motor Fire threatened it, but it was protected and saved.[^2] The same report says a trail was improved in 2012, that the plan to turn the site into a rental was on hold, and that the tower was heavily vandalized.[^2]

The Central California chapter of the FFLA commented in 2008 on a Forest Service project to restore the historic cabin at the base of the lookout as a recreation rental. The chapter asked for steps to keep the lookout standing where it is.[^4] The Forest Service signed the decision on March 24, 2009, and its project page lists the work as completed.[^3]

The sources do not agree on the restoration. The Forest Service page says the work was completed, the 2012 report says the rental plan was on hold, and the register still describes the site as awaiting restoration.[^3][^2][^1] The 2009 project was for the cabin, but the sources do not say whether the 2012 rental plan is the same work.

The FFLA California list still shows the lookout as standing.[^5] I found no current access or rental notice in the sources I checked, so today's condition and public access are unclear.
