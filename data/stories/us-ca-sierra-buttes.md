Sierra Buttes Lookout stands on a summit in Sierra County, in the Tahoe National Forest.[^1] Fire detection began on the site in 1908.[^1] By 1915 the Forest Service had announced a standard lookout house there, with a telephone line to Sierra City and a trail to the top.[^2]

The register describes a 1916 cabin, a 1933 cab, and the current metal cab, which it dates to 1963.[^1] The other sources do not line up neatly with that date. A Forest Service bid notice from August 1963 was still inviting bids to build the lookout.[^2] A 1968 item says a contractor erected the present lookout.[^2] A plaque at the foot of the stairs credits five Tahoe National Forest employees with attaching the stairs and platforms to the peak in summer 1964.[^3] The 1963 date is the register's, and the building history is less settled than that date suggests.

Lookouts worked the summit in season. Miss Rena Tomola was set to serve as summer lookout in 1920.[^2] By 1939 Sam Kasper had been on the peak for five years.[^2] A 1992 item says the season ran from early July through September, with Kenneth Ingle working a ten-day stretch followed by four days off.[^2]

Fire came close more than once. In August 1926 a newspaper reported that the lookout station was in danger from a forest fire near Sierra City.[^2] In 1954 a fire destroyed a storehouse for the lookout tower.[^2]

The lookout still stands, and a 2024 newspaper visitor account describes fire-detection gear placed at the station over the years.[^3] The register counts 176 stairs to the top.[^1] The Forest Service describes the trail from a parking area off Forest Road 93-2 as about 2.3 miles and calls it challenging, with no water at the trailhead.[^4] The sources read do not confirm current staffing or visiting rules, so check with the Tahoe National Forest before you go.
