Holsclaw Hill, inside what is now the Jefferson Memorial Forest in southwest Louisville, got a fire lookout in 1947.[^1][^2][^3] In April 1947 the county leased land on the hill near the Bullitt County line for a tower, and the county forester, Paul A. Yost, put the planned height at 90 feet (27 m).[^1] Back in December 1946 he had told a Lions Club meeting that a 100-foot (30.5 m) tower would go up the next spring, and that one person on watch could oversee 10,000 acres.[^1]

A 2009 culture-history report for the forest says the tower went up in 1947, one of the first jobs of Marlon D. Rennirt, who had just been hired as fire warden.[^2] It stood 100 feet tall, was bought from the Aeromotor Company of Chicago, and included phone and two-way radio equipment.[^2] The sources disagree on how much land it watched: the 1946 talk gave 10,000 acres, and the report says nearly 35,000.[^1][^2]

In late October 1947 the county forester was cleared to hire a lookout for the tower, and a newspaper item said it would be finished the next day.[^1] The report adds that a grandmother, Merle Ingram, often manned the tower.[^2]

Visitors could go up the tower, and those who wanted to climb it were enrolled in the Jefferson County Squirrel Club.[^2] A January 1948 newspaper item about the club said membership hinged on one thing, climbing the tower, which the item put at 200 feet.[^1] That figure does not match the 90-foot and 100-foot accounts.[^1][^2]

In April 1950 a notice said the Louisville Astronomical Society would hold a star party at the tower, and the public was invited.[^1]

According to the report, the tower was taken down in 2004.[^2] The forest now has a different structure, a 52-foot (16 m) Alpine climbing tower put up in 1992 in the Horine Reservation.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the hill today. Hiking and horse trails total nearly 60 miles (97 km) across the forest.[^3] Since the report says the tower came down, there is no lookout to climb.[^2]
