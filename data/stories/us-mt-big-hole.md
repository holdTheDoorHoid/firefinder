## Big Hole Lookout

Big Hole Lookout stands at 6,922 feet in the Lolo National Forest, about 13 miles from Thompson Falls in Sanders County, Montana.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service built it in 1930 for what was then the Cabinet National Forest, since folded into the Lolo.[^3][^4] Rather than the standard L-4 cab used on most Forest Service lookouts, the builders raised a "Grange Hall" type structure: a 14-by-14-foot box of four courses of heavy logs set on a short stone foundation. The style predates the L-4 and was difficult enough to build that it fell out of use; Big Hole is one of only two such lookouts still intact on the Lolo National Forest.[^3]

The lookout was staffed seasonally through 1972, watching for smoke over the surrounding peaks and valleys.[^1][^4] After fire detection shifted away from fixed lookouts, it was abandoned, and in 1984 it was found eligible for the National Register of Historic Places as part of a study of the forest's surviving lookouts, the "Lookouts on the Lolo" project — eligibility, not a formal listing.[^3]

The building then sat empty for decades. The Forest Service and volunteers worked on it through a series of Passport in Time projects, finishing a restoration in 2017.[^4] Gary Weber nominated the lookout to the National Historic Lookout Register, which added it on April 24, 2009.[^1]

In 2021, its first year open to the public, Big Hole Lookout became available to rent through Recreation.gov, running from June to early October.[^3] It's reached by a three-mile trail open to hikers, horses and mountain bikes, after a steep, winding 60-to-90-minute drive from Plains to the trailhead.[^3] Inside, the small cabin sleeps up to four on a twin bed and two cots, with a table, two chairs and a wood stove; a vault toilet stands about 200 feet away.[^3] Sources disagree slightly on the water: the rental listing describes a non-potable spring three-quarters of a mile downhill, while the Forest Service's own page for the lookout describes a spring a half-mile away that is drinkable once filtered.[^3][^4] Either way, renters should plan to carry their own water in.

Today the lookout is standing and open to the public by reservation, with no vehicle access and no utilities — a quiet perch over the Cabinet-area peaks that once depended on someone watching from exactly this spot.[^1][^3]
