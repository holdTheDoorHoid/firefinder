High Knob Lookout is a stone tower in the George Washington National Forest, on the state line between Rockingham County, Virginia, and Pendleton County, West Virginia.[^1] It is Virginia's only stone lookout, and it also commemorates the veterans and Civilian Conservation Corps workers who built it.[^1]

World War I veterans started construction in 1939 under the supervision of John Moffett.[^1] Men from the Civilian Conservation Corps camp at North River completed the tower in 1940.[^1] The builders used native rock from the mountaintop.[^2] The tower carries a wooden 14-by-14-foot cab with a catwalk.[^1]

By the 1940 fire season, fire tower watchman Gerald Fawley had stopped working from a rickety platform in a tree. He moved into the stone tower and worked there for three more seasons before transferring to the post on Cow Knob.[^2]

In August 1972 a Forest Service memo proposed declaring the forest's fire towers surplus and removing them, except for two. High Knob was among the towers included.[^2] A district ranger asked to keep High Knob, and the local VFW and Rockingham Historical Society expressed interest in keeping it too.[^2] The register notes that only three fire towers remain on the George Washington National Forest, where there were once 24.[^1] The tower was restored, with the register giving 2003 and a trail guide giving 2001 to 2003.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register listed it on January 27, 1994.[^1]

Today, a 2.5-mile out-and-back trail reaches the tower. It takes about 1.5 to 2.5 hours and has about 643 feet of elevation change, and the trailhead is about 30 minutes west of Harrisonburg by car.[^3] From the top, the tower gives 360-degree views, and plaques on the catwalk name the peaks in sight.[^3] The inside is a different matter. A May 2018 visit report noted that a few windows and the door were covered with wood, and the visitor wished the interior could be opened.[^1] That report does not establish current access, so check with the national forest before going inside.[^1]
