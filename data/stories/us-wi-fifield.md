The Fifield Fire Lookout Tower stands about five miles east of the community of Fifield in Price County, Wisconsin.[^1] The Wisconsin Conservation Commission, now the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources, constructed it in 1932.[^2] Land for the site came to the Forest Service in 1931 as part of a larger tract, and the state built the tower under a special use permit.[^1]

The galvanized steel ladder tower stands about 100 feet tall, and a protective cage surrounds its exterior ladder.[^1] Its top holds a seven-foot-square steel cab with a wood floor.[^1]

The Forest Service began operating the tower in 1935.[^1] The nomination also says a Civilian Conservation Corps camp ran it between at least 1935 and 1941.[^1] Wikipedia says the tower stayed in use until 1973, and the Forest Lookouts site gives 1973 as its last staffed season.[^3][^4] By contrast, the National Register form ends the period of significance in 1957 and names no final staffed year.[^1]

The nomination says the tower enabled the fire detection and suppression system before aircraft detection arrived.[^1]

Around 1973 the Forest Service removed roughly the bottom 20 feet of ladder to meet safety standards.[^1] The National Register form describes the cab's window glass as gone and its floor as deteriorated, though most of the tower remained intact.[^1] The dwelling and garage no longer stand.[^1] The Lookout Register says the Forest Service restored the tower, but it gives no date for that work.[^2]

The tower was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2007 and to the Lookout Register on January 2, 2008.[^3][^2]

Today the tower is a historic site with interpretive signs.[^5] A trail from the Wintergreen parking lot leads to the tower, but the public has no access to climb it.[^5] A National Forest parking pass is required.[^5] None of the sources read mention renting the tower.[^5]
