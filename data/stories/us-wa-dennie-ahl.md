The Dennie Ahl lookout stood in Mason County, Washington, in the Olympic National Forest.[^1][^2]

The Aircraft Warning Service activated the site on June 23, 1942, and a tower was built there in 1943.[^3] The register and firelookout.com describe a 30-foot treated timber L-4 tower.[^1][^2] Willhite Web describes a 40-foot timber tower with an L-4 cab on Gibbons Ridge, so the height is disputed.[^4]

The Aircraft Warning Service first called the site Dry Creek and then Gibbons.[^4] Simpson Logging gave the finished tower the name Dennie Ahl, after John "Dennie" Ahl, a Forest Service log scaler.[^4] Sources disagree on how he died. Willhite Web says he had a fatal heart attack in 1944 after his crew corralled a lightning fire.[^4] The Forest Lookouts page says he died of heart failure while rowing across Lake Cushman to service a fire crew.[^3]

The Shelton District reported in May 1956 that the lookout was manned, with extra district staff on standby.[^3]

The tower was taken down in 1971. The register says it was removed, while firelookout.com and Willhite Web say it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^4]

Willhite Web says little remains on the summit besides squared concrete slabs and footings.[^4] The footings were shoved over the edge into the timber to the west, and a trail down the east ridge leads to the old toilet.[^4] A 1972 USGS party found that the tops had snapped off the survey monuments and the disks were gone.[^4]

Willhite Web rates the access road as gravel and dirt and advises a higher-clearance vehicle. Its route runs from Highway 101 along Forest Service roads FR 23, FR 2340 and FR 200.[^4] We found no source saying whether the summit is open to visitors today.
