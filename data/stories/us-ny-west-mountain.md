Forest Lookouts lists the earliest West Mountain entry as July 1909, when a lookout on a 12-foot tower was set up at a cost of $1,064.44.[^1] Wikipedia describes an earlier 14-foot wooden signal tower, built for the U.S. Geological Survey in 1899, as the first structure on the mountain. The pages do not say whether that is the same tower.[^4] Wikipedia names William Wing Sanderson as the forest fire observer there in 1909.[^4]

In July 1911, two forest observers were appointed, one of them for West Mountain. That observer was engaged only during the fire season, at $60 a month.[^1]

Forest Lookouts dates a steel tower on the mountain to 1920.[^1] Wikipedia says the Conservation Commission built a 47-foot Aermotor LS-40 tower that year to replace the original, and Starr lists the same 47-foot tower.[^4][^2] The observer's living quarters got a new cabin in 1922.[^1] Wikipedia ties the end of fire lookout operations, after the 1970 season, to aerial detection.[^4]

The FFLA list now marks the West Mountain tower as gone.[^3] The register says the West and Kempshall Mountain towers came down in the 1980s and went into storage.[^5] Wikipedia says the tower was later removed.[^4]

The register says the museum bought the tower parts in the 1990s and built one tower to display permanently.[^5] Starr says parts of both towers went into one tower re-erected at the Essex County Historical Society building in Elizabethtown.[^2] The register calls the host the Adirondack History Museum, while Wikipedia calls it the Essex County Historical Museum.[^5][^4] Getting to the top means going inside the museum first.[^5] Nothing in the sources we read describes what remains on the mountain today.
