The Texas Forest Service numbered the Hallsville tower 53. It stood in Harrison County on a timbered hill, which a National Geodetic Survey datasheet places about four and a half miles east-northeast of Hallsville.[^1][^3]

A 1936 entry records a 100-foot steel tower erected for $932.00.[^1] The steel structure took the place of a hickory tree a patrolman had used.[^1]

In March 1944 vandals broke into the tower and its lock.[^1] According to a report from The Marshall News Messenger, the only thing taken was a pencil.[^1] The district forester warned that anyone caught going into a tower would face prosecution.[^1]

A March 1950 report, reproduced from the Corsicana Daily Sun, said a young woman died after a fall from the tower.[^1] She was believed to have fallen from about halfway up.[^1]

The 1950 report places the tower eight miles east of Hallsville, while the survey datasheet puts the hill about four and a half miles east-northeast of town. The two accounts disagree, and we found no source that settles it.[^1][^3]

The Forest Lookouts page dates the tower's last regular use to 1973.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas index lists the tower as gone.[^2]

A survey marker set on the same hill in 1950 was recorded as not found when a 2001 recovery visited it.[^3] No source we read describes what remains of the tower itself or whether the hilltop is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
