The first lookout on this peak went up in 1917, when it was still called San Dimas Peak.[^1][^3] The San Dimas Fruit Exchange paid for it, and the tower stood between San Dimas and Dalton canyons to detect fires that could damage the watersheds above the town.[^3]

In September 1919 a fire in San Gabriel Canyon overran the tower.[^3] A newspaper item from that month, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says the fire "has driven the forest rangers from the San Dimas fire lookout."[^2] Angeles Adventures says the fire destroyed the structure a few days later.[^3] A new tower followed in 1923, a short open timber tower on a DuBois 4A cab.[^3]

The sources disagree about the tower that stood on the peak after that.[^1][^3][^4] The register says the Angeles National Forest built a cabin on a 30-foot steel K-brace tower there in 1939.[^1] A historical marker says the tower stood on the peak from 1934 to 1992.[^4] Angeles Adventures says it was renovated or replaced sometime in the 1930s.[^3]

In June 1940 the peak was dedicated to the memory of W.A. Johnstone, a conservationist who died in 1937, and the lookout took the name Johnstone Peak.[^2][^1]

Angeles Adventures names A.M. Trout as an observer in the 1930s, and Frank Mathys, Everett L. Osborn and Fred J. Haner in the 1940s.[^3] In June 1946 a swarm of bees had settled between the shutter and the door. The two men had to retreat and call for help by radio before Osborn moved in for the season.[^2] In August 1924 an observer spotted the fire that became the 1924 San Gabriel Fire.[^3]

The last lookout observer was Sandy DeLapp in the 1950s. A photo caption dates the closure to 1955, and the history says smog pollution led to the tower being decommissioned.[^3] The tower then stood abandoned on the peak until the early 1990s, when it was moved to the Los Angeles County Fairplex in Pomona.[^1][^3] The register says the cabin was dismantled and rebuilt there in 1992.[^1]

At the Fairplex the lookout is an interpretive display. Volunteers operate it every year during the Los Angeles County Fair, and the marker says it is open during that fair.[^1][^4] The tower there is shorter, with a smaller cab, and is painted green.[^3]

The original summit lies within the San Dimas Experimental Forest, which Angeles Adventures says is closed to the general public except by special written permit.[^3] A radio-site listing for Johnstone Peak describes a 100-foot tower on Forest Service land.[^5] The sources I read do not say whether any part of the lookout survives on the summit. Under that closure, going up requires a special written permit.[^3]
