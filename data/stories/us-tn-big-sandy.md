Big Sandy was a fire tower in Benton County, Tennessee. The Tennessee Landforms table gives its elevation as 642 feet.[^1] That is about 196 m. The table gives it the state forest number 8-2. Its notes read that as district 8, tower 2, from a 1976 state list.[^1] The table's sources credit the state's Division of Forestry with 208 tower sites around 1976.[^1]

A National Register document about the state's towers says that, taken together, they gave physical shape to the Division of Forestry's fire detection network from 1933 to 1975.[^2] By the mid-1970s, they had lost their central role in state fire control, as faster and more precise ways of detecting fires took their place.[^2]

The table marks Big Sandy as removed.[^1] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.

The table's author says he gave unofficial names to towers that neither the USGS maps nor the state forestry office's list named, so some names in the table are his own.[^1]

[^1]: Tennessee Lookout Towers, Tennessee Landforms (Tom Dunigan), https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers multiple property document, National Park Service, https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/64501237.pdf (accessed 2026-10-21).
