Tumbledown Mountain, in Somerset County, got a wood lookout tower in 1910.[^1][^2][^4] The Maine Memory record says the steel tower that replaced it was built in 1914 at 3,542 feet.[^1] That steel tower was 24 feet tall and carried an 8-by-8 cab.[^1][^2] The 1914 Forest Commissioner's report also records a new telephone line to the top of the mountain.[^2]

A fire watchman's camp sat about a mile from the tower.[^3] The watchman who wrote about it in 1915 praised its beds, cook stove and nearby spring.[^3] A later photo of an interior camp, dated about 1920, has a note on its back that calls a radio a comfort in the evenings and a way to get news from across the U.S.[^3] The tower was painted in 1915, and a 1920 inventory still listed it as a 24-foot steel tower.[^2]

Today the Forest Fire Lookout Association marks Tumbledown as standing, with an asterisk.[^4] Its note explains that the mark is used when the structure is still there but the cab is no longer on it.[^4] A 2021 trip report agrees that the cab is gone and the ladder remains.[^5] The report describes a road on the north slope that gets within about a mile of the summit, with a bushwhack from its end to the top.[^5] The writer says no other standing tower in the Western Maine Mountains lacks a trail.[^5]

I found no current statement on the tower's condition, its owner, or whether it is open to visitors. The 2021 report is the newest account of its condition that I could read, so check before planning a visit.[^5]
