Yellow Mountain Lookout sits on Yellow Mountain in the Nantahala National Forest, in Macon County, North Carolina, at about 5,124 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 42, NC 1 and dates the registration to June 1, 1992.[^1]

Trail-guide writers give 1934 as the year it was built, and one of them credits the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^2][^3] The lookout is a wooden cab about 14 feet square with a steep pyramid roof, set on an 8-foot stone base.[^1] The Register says this kind of cabin was once widespread in the southern Appalachians and is now close to gone.[^1]

One trail guide says it had staff on duty until 1969.[^2] The sources I read do not name the people who watched from it or any fires it spotted.

The Register says the cabin sat empty for about 20 years before volunteers and district staff restored it.[^1] It does not give the restoration date. A trail guide dated January 2021 calls the restoration recent, but notes the cabin stands right at the forest's border, with private development closing in.[^2]

Today the lookout is reached on foot.[^2] One guide puts the start at Cody Gap, off Buck Creek Road near Highlands, while another names Cole Gap, so check a current map before setting out.[^2][^3] The sources do not say whether the cabin is open to climbing, so ask the Highlands Ranger District before going up it.
