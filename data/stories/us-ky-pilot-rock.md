Pilot Rock is a sandstone summit that sits right on the line between Christian and Todd counties, about 13 miles (21 km) northeast of Hopkinsville. Its top is 966 feet (295 m) above sea level.[^2]

In October 1952 the Kentucky New Era reported that state and government money was to pay for a wooden watchtower on the rock, then owned by Clarence Boyd. The planned tower was 20 feet (6 m) tall, one of four intended to cover Christian County.[^1]

A 2012 hiking blog says the United States Department of Agriculture built a fire tower on the summit in the 1960s, and that remnants of its foundation could still be seen.[^2] The sources do not say whether the 1952 plan became that tower.[^1][^2] A December 2000 trip report describes the ruins of the old fire tower on the summit.[^3]

The blog, the most recent source I found, describes a short walk to the top and a parking area for visitors. It says the USDA still owns the rock and that the summit is apparently open to the public, though it notes a rumor of private ownership.[^2] A 2013 update on the same post adds, second-hand, that a fence was put up to block access and that the site is now reportedly closed.[^2] I could not confirm that closure, and no source I found describes what remains at the summit after 2012.
