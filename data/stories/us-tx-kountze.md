During the 1920s the Texas Forest Service ran a lookout post from the cupola of the Hardin County courthouse in Kountze.[^1] A 1926 state report gives the cupola an elevation of about eighty feet, and says the lookout man there did not stay in it all the time and also fought fires when no forest patrolman was free.[^1] A 1927 newspaper item puts the cupola about eighty-five feet from the ground and credits its lookoutman with more than fifty fire discoveries in one fall season.[^2]

By 1943 the lookout worked from a glassed-in tower about 100 feet high.[^2] A newspaper item from that year names Mrs. N.D. Bartlett as the lookout stationed there.[^2] She spotted fires, located them and notified patrolmen and firefighters.[^2] The distances disagree: the 1943 item puts the tower five miles southwest of Kountze, while a 1952 survey places the station about 4.0 miles west-southwest.[^2]

The Coast and Geodetic Survey described the station in 1952.[^2] It was a steel frame on four legs, carrying a seven-foot square observation cage with a pyramidal roof, and it stood 100 feet overall on state-owned property in a small pine grove.[^2]

A 1984 recovery note found the station destroyed and said the lookout tower no longer existed.[^2] It put the tower's demolition in 1980, citing Texas Forest Service staff in Kountze.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list also marks the tower as gone.[^3]
