The Homestake lookout stands on private land in Crook County, Wyoming, about 14 miles southeast of Sundance.[^1] Its story begins with a tree. In 1922 the Homestake Mining Company of Lead, South Dakota, used a tall pine as a lookout and called it Moskee Lookout, after a logging camp nearby.[^2] In 1936 a steel platform replaced the pine, which had been used for firewood, and the Register gives 1936 as the build year.[^2] Firelookout.com describes the current structure as a 30-foot steel tower with an open platform.[^1]

The sources read do not describe who staffed the platform or name any fire it watched. According to the Register, the platform is still used now and then to spot forest fires.[^2]

The platform still stands, according to the Register.[^2] Ownership is less clear. Westlookouts says the land is now owned by the Moskee Land Corporation.[^3] The Register names Barrick Gold Corporation as the administrator, while firelookout.com names Homestake Mining Company and the Moskee Land Corporation.[^2][^1]

The height needs care. Westlookouts mentions a 120-foot figure and attaches it to the former Stumpy Point, North Carolina tower.[^3] The lower half of that tower went up on the same ranch in 2012, and Westlookouts does not tie the 120 feet to this platform.[^3]

The Register says the site is on private land and is not open to the public.[^2] Westlookouts adds that access requires permission.[^3] Anyone interested should ask the landowner first, and nobody should climb the platform without permission.
