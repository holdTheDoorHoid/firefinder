The Grand Lake Stream fire tower stood on a hardwood knoll in Washington County, Maine.[^1][^2] Built in 1934, it was an enclosed wooden tower on Downeast Lakes Land Trust property.[^3] The chapter calls it the last of its kind left in New England, and the land trust says the Maine Forest Service managed it to watch over the Downeast Lakes region.[^3][^1]

The land trust's trail nearby is the Tower Hill Trail, while the chapter's statewide list calls the hill Indian Hill.[^4][^2]

In its 2025 annual report, the chapter says structural architects and contractors from Maine Cabin Masters examined the tower and found it beyond saving.[^3] The land trust then said the tower had become a danger and must come down, and it told the town at a March meeting.[^3] The chapter's report, with data as of the end of 2025, said the removal planned for late fall had not yet happened.[^3]

The tower is now gone.[^4] The land trust scheduled a volunteer cleanup for July 10, 2026, to turn the site into a public picnic spot.[^5] Suggestions included a display sign with historical pictures, and the land trust said it would work with the historical society and the town to decide whether a replica should be funded.[^3]

The Tower Hill Trail still runs near the former site, with parking at the West Grand Lake Dam lot or along the extension of Tower Road.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower's base today.
