Mount Carrigain is a 4,700-foot peak in Grafton County, New Hampshire, and it still has a fire tower on its summit.[^1][^3] A tower on Carrigain was among ten built in 1910, as part of a system of fire towers across the White Mountains.[^1] Three partners, the NH Timberland Owners Association, the state of New Hampshire and the U.S. Forest Service, cooperated on that system.[^1] Of the ten new towers that year, seven, Carrigain among them, were paid for by the Association.[^1]

White Mountain History lists Carrigain among the three federal lookouts that remain, and it calls the Carrigain structure greatly modified.[^2] The same page says none of the three is in active service, and that their main purposes are teaching and recreation.[^2]

The cab is gone.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Carrigain as standing but marks it with an asterisk. That mark means the tower keeps its frame and support while the cab is no longer in place.[^3]

Forum posts add a few details, though they are recollections rather than official records. One hiker remembers a Thanksgiving trip in 1967, when the group slept in the cabin on top of the old tower.[^4] Another forum post says a Forest Service steel tower existed there from 1940 to 1981, and that the current platform went up soon after it came down.[^4] A separate post also thinks the platform has been there for several decades.[^4] Treat the 1940 and 1981 dates as unconfirmed until a state or federal record turns up.

White Mountain History says the lookouts have trail access.[^2] We could not confirm whether the platform can be climbed today, so please check conditions with the Forest Service before you go up.
