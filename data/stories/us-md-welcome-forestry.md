The Welcome Forestry Tower stands on Firetower Road in Charles County, Maryland, near the community of Welcome.[^1][^2]

Civilian Conservation Corps crews from the Doncaster Camp erected it in 1934, according to the register.[^1] The state's inventory form says the tower is believed to have been built by the Corps, and it counts it as one of two surviving towers in Southern Maryland that are believed to date from that program.[^2] The same year, Charles Timothy Ford of nearby Rosemary Lawn gave the state about three-quarters of an acre, on the condition that it be used for forestry or revert to him.[^2]

The tower is a riveted steel lattice structure, which the register puts at 120 feet high.[^1][^2] Its two main observation platforms and about six smaller ones gave a wide view of much of Charles County.[^2] It was part of a grid of towers across Southern Maryland.[^2] Information from the towers went to a control center in Brandywine, in Prince Georges County, where spotters could pinpoint a fire's approximate location and send runners into the field.[^2] The form says at least six towers once stood in the region.[^2]

The form does not say when the fire watch ended. It notes that the site was most recently used to prepare for any Y2K complications in 2000.[^2] The register says State Police and Natural Resources antennas are mounted on the structure.[^1] The form, last updated in 2003, says the Maryland State Police maintained the site, describes the tower as recently painted silver, and mentions a barbed wire fence around the site.[^2]

Today it is listed as standing, and the register recorded it on September 3, 2014 as US 1031, MD 6.[^1][^3] The sources read do not say whether the public may go in, so please do not climb it. Check with the Maryland Forest Service before you plan a visit.[^2][^1]
