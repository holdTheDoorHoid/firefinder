The tower at Watapi Lake was put up to spot fires on a bombing range. The Saskatchewan Department of Natural Resources annual report for 1952-53, as quoted on Canada Lookouts, says winter hauling brought the full set of materials for a tower to Wapati Lake, to detect fires on a bombing range being set up for the Royal Canadian Air Force [^1]. That range is the Cold Lake (or Primrose Lake) Air Weapons Range, which straddles the Saskatchewan-Alberta border [^2]. The federal government signed agreements with Saskatchewan and Alberta in 1953 to lease the range [^2]. A report entry for 1953-54 says a tower was erected at Watapi Lake to help detect fires on the Primrose Lake Air Weapons Range [^1].

The Saskatchewan fire-tower listing gives the site's position as 54 degrees 18.5333 minutes north, 109 degrees 39.0667 minutes west [^3].

A later entry, from the 1957-58 report, says a tower on the Air Weapons Range was taken down to be put up at a new site. It does not name that site [^1]. A separate Canada Lookouts page, filed under the name Watipi, quotes a 1963 entry from the 1963-64 annual report saying the cupola was replaced on the "Wapiti Tower" [^4]. That page does not say whether this was the Watapi Lake tower, so the two pages may describe different structures.

No source I could read describes what, if anything, stands at Watapi Lake today, or whether the public can visit the site.
