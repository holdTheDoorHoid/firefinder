Duckett Top stands in Madison County, North Carolina, northwest of Asheville.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1936 for the US Forest Service, and the Register describes it as one of the few two-story lookouts left in North Carolina.[^1] A wooden cab with a catwalk sits on a rock foundation.[^1] In 1966 the tower was transferred to the state forest service.[^1]

The Register, which dates from 1998, says the state forest service still staffed the tower.[^1] A 2024 Forest Fire Lookout Association report says staffing stopped around 2000 and the tower is no longer in use.[^2] Both statements may have been accurate when they were written, but the newer report is the better guide. We found no notable fires recorded for the tower in the sources we read.

The state forest service replaced the roof in 2008.[^3] In 2009 the first emergency stabilization grant from the Association, $150, went to new lookout steward Wes Greene, who paid for the rest of the paint and supplies.[^3] Greene repainted the whole tower over several weekends, some nights camped inside.[^4] The chapter's 2009 report also mentions a rededication at the lookout.[^4]

In February 2024 the Association awarded a $500 restoration grant to Bo Dossett, a Madison County ranger with the state forest service.[^2] The planned repairs included plywood over the missing windows, replacement of rotting siding, and metal flashing under the exterior siding.[^2] The grant was meant to help prevent further water damage.[^2] The report says that work had not yet begun.[^2] The 2025 report says the grant was returned because the work was postponed, with other funding expected when the project resumes.[^5]

The Register lists the North Carolina Division of Forest Resources as the administrator.[^1] We did not find a current access, visitor or rental notice, and the sources do not say whether the public may climb the tower. Treat it as closed to climbing until the state confirms otherwise, and check before you plan a visit.
