The Medicine Bow Peak lookout began as a small wooden ground cabin, built on the site about 1909.[^1] A later, larger building with a cupola took its place.[^1] The summit is listed at 12,009 feet (3,660 meters), though a 1920 notice gives 12,005 feet.[^1][^2]

In July 1909 a contract was awarded for a telephone line between Centennial and the peak.[^2] Fred J. Miller was recommended for appointment as the lookout in 1910, and by 1912 a newspaper described him as the ranger who kept the lonely post through the summer and fall.[^2]

In 1921 Lorraine Lindsley of Centennial took the post, which one paper called the highest lookout held by a woman in the United States.[^2] She resigned that October to return to the University of Wyoming.[^2] A March 1922 notice said women would no longer be hired as lookouts in that region, citing isolation and supply problems.[^2] Roy Last was back at the station in 1932, and Arthur Strouts was the lookout in 1936.[^2]

In July 1913 lightning started a fire on the lookout point, and by the time it was put out it had covered about an acre.[^2] In late September 1921 a smoke the lookout reported near Foxpark became a fire that for a time threatened the settlement.[^2]

A 1919 item reported a new observatory on the peak, with glass on all sides.[^2] In July 1922 the Forest Service sought bids for a new combined fire observatory and living quarters on the summit.[^2] The dates for the summit buildings do not line up across these sources. The firelookout.com page lists the cabin as "Cupola cabin. Gone."[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wyoming list marks the site as ground gone.[^4] A bare caption on the Forest Lookouts page reads "Burned by the Forest Service." It does not say which structure that was or when.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register places the site in the Medicine Bow National Forest, administered by the U.S. Forest Service.[^1] A 2022 hiking blog found the old lookout in the saddle below the summit, not on top.[^5] The writer called it "much more than a cairn," and noted that a sign on the route points the way out to it.[^5] Forest Lookouts shows 2019 photos of remains of the telephone line system on the peak.[^2] The blog gives Sugarloaf Campground as the regular start point for the climb.[^5] We found no closure notice for the site, but no source we read gives current visitor rules either.
