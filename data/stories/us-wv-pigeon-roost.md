Pigeon Roost went up around 1943 with Army money, while the Army trained troops in the West Virginia Maneuver Area.[^1][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates it to about 1943 and says it was meant to protect forest land that troops were using for training.[^1] Forest history says Pigeon Roost and Niges Cliff were built with military funds tied to that maneuver area, and that both were built entirely of wood timbers.[^3]

Eastern US Lookouts places the tower on the Potomac Ranger District near Bismarck, in Grant County.[^1][^2] It also says Army funding paid for three temporary towers, while the forest history names two towers built with military funds.[^2][^3]

The register and Eastern US Lookouts both give 1948 for the transfer to the State of West Virginia.[^1][^2] The forest history gives 1944 and 1948 for the two wartime towers without saying which is which.[^3] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service and the State of West Virginia as administrators.[^1]

The two lists give slightly different coordinates for the site, so the exact spot is not settled.[^1][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's West Virginia list marks the tower as gone.[^4]

A 2011 USGS topographic map, reproduced on Pastmaps, labels a Pigeon Roost Lookout Tower near Pigeonroost.[^5] A map label does not show whether a structure still stands. No source describes what remains at the site now, or whether visitors can reach it.
