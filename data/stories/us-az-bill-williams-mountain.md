Bill Williams Mountain has had a lookout since at least 1910.[^2] The first was an old spruce tree topped with a small platform, used as a patrol lookout in 1910.[^2] A 30-foot wooden tower followed in 1924.[^1][^2] The present Aermotor MC-39 tower dates to 1937.[^1][^2] It has a 7-by-7-foot metal cab with an unusual raised platform above the roof, and an airway beacon was mounted on top.[^1][^2] A 1939 newspaper item ranked the beacon among the country's highest.[^2]

The people who worked it left traces in the local papers. Charles Cattermole arrived in Williams from Prescott in May 1917 to start as lookout.[^2] In October 1938 Floyd Barker, standing in the tower, heard and then watched two bull elk fight in the clearing behind his cabin.[^2] A road to the summit was pushed through in 1954, built mainly for fire control, and it was not open to the public.[^2][^3]

The register recorded the lookout on May 1, 2001, and describes the tower as in active service.[^1] The Fire Lookout Association lists it as a standing tower.[^4] The sources also disagree about the elevation. The register gives the summit at 9,166 feet, while 1935 and 1939 newspaper items give 9,264 and 9,242 feet.[^1][^2]

Today the summit is a radio site.[^3][^5] In January 2014 the Forest Service signed a decision to raise an existing 71-foot lattice telecommunications tower on the summit to 100 feet.[^5] An undated Arizona Highways article says the trail starts at the Williams Ranger District station and that the lookout is open to visitors during the fire season.[^3] I could not find a current staffing or opening notice for this October, so the season may already be over. Check with the Williams Ranger District before you go, and do not climb the tower unless it is open to visitors.
