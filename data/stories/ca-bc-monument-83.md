Monument 83 is a log fire lookout on the Canada–United States border, on the eastern edge of E.C. Manning Park in southwestern British Columbia.[^1] The provincial register places it in a clearing in the subalpine zone.[^1]

The site dates to the 1920s, when it was a camp with a tree platform.[^2] A log cabin cupola went up there in 1930, and for a time it was hailed as the northernmost lookout in the United States, until surveys placed it on Canadian soil.[^2]

The register praises the cabin's crossed-log walls as a good example of that style of building, and notes a small observation room with glass walls, sitting atop the main roof.[^1] The province recognized the site on December 31, 1941.[^1]

The register says the decommissioned lookout shows the public the Forest Service's historic mandate to limit the spread of fires.[^1] The association says both lookouts were last used in the 1970s.[^2]

On the US side, a 9.1 m (30 ft) L-4 tower stands.[^2] The association's page gives 1952 for it in one place and 1953 in another.[^2]

The association's undated page says both structures remain intact.[^2] The site is reached by a hiking trail.[^3] The trailhead lies about 3.5 km east of Gibson Pass Road, off Highway 3, and the round trip is about 30 km.[^3] A hiking guide warns of considerable damage to the trail from wildfires and weather.[^3] It also notes that a border re-survey placed one of the two buildings in Canada.[^3] Backcountry fees apply to overnight stays.[^3]
