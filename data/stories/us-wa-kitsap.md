The Kitsap Lookout is a steel tower about 60 feet tall, on a forested summit three miles west of Bremerton in Kitsap County, Washington.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com gives the site's elevation as 1,360 feet, while Wikipedia gives the summit's elevation as 1,378 feet.[^1][^3]

The sources do not agree on who built it or exactly when. Firelookout.com gives 1940, while TrailChick, WillhiteWeb and Wikipedia say the 1940s or the early 1940s.[^1][^2][^3][^4] WillhiteWeb says it was likely built by the U.S. Navy. It describes two planks near the top that it says were used to line up 16-inch naval guns at the Puget Sound Navy Yard.[^4] TrailChick reports that some people say it was used only for naval purposes, while other research points to a fire lookout role in the 1940s.[^2] Wikipedia calls it a naval observation tower, but says its main use was as a typical fire lookout.[^3]

A friend's recollection, quoted on WillhiteWeb, says Charles R. Siegel Jr. worked as a fire lookout here about 1943.[^2][^4] TrailChick also places him at Kitsap in 1943.[^2] Wikipedia says the tower was tended until at least 1944.[^3] We found no notable fires connected with the tower in the sources we read.

Firelookout.com says the tower was abandoned in the 1950s.[^1] For a time it was presumed destroyed.[^3] TrailChick says it was found standing again in the early 2000s,[^2] and Wikipedia says it still stands.[^3] The Firelookout.org statewide list marks it as a standing tower.[^5]

Access is the main limit today. Wikipedia says the mountain lies almost entirely inside the City of Bremerton watershed and is not open to the general public. It also calls visiting highly discouraged, because the land is non-public government land.[^3] TrailChick says no road leads directly to the summit, and that the tower cannot be seen until you are nearly on top of it.[^2] We do not describe a route and do not recommend visiting or climbing the tower. We found no rental listing in the sources we read.
