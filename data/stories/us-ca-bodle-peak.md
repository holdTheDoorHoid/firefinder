In 1930 the Los Angeles County Forestry Department relocated its fire lookout from Blue Ridge in the San Gabriel Mountains to Bodle Peak, in the western Santa Monica Mountains.[^1][^2] The county forester said the move was meant to protect a risky mountain area with many summer homes and costly property.[^2] The register describes a 60-foot steel tower with an 8x8 observation cabin, taken down at Blue Ridge and stored at Bodle Peak until it was put back up in 1932.[^1] Angeles Adventures gives the tower's height as 50 feet, so the two sources disagree on height.[^1][^2]

The lookout was in service for only about four years.[^2] It was then taken down again and set up on Triunfo Peak, a few miles west.[^2] The Triunfo Peak register dates that reassembly to 1934, while the Bodle register says it came sometime after 1934.[^3][^1] The Triunfo register also describes an earlier tower on that summit, built in May 1930 and destroyed in the November 1930 Potrero fire, so its dates should be read with care.[^3] The Bodle register notes that the area burned in the 2018 Woolsey Fire.[^1]

According to the Triunfo register, the summit's tower ran every fire season until the county retired it in the late 1960s.[^3] A concrete base of unknown purpose is pictured at that summit today.[^3]

Today the Bodle summit is level ground with small foundation ruins and some rocky crags.[^2] The register adds that a satellite image shows the tower's piers still in place, plus what may be the foundation of a cabin.[^1] The register places the site on Bodle Peak Motorway, about 1.1 miles north of Mulholland Drive and east of Highway 23.[^1] None of these sources says whether visitors may go up to the summit.[^1][^2]
