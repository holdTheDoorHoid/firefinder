Kiona Peak sits about 12 miles east of Morton in Lewis County, Washington.[^2] The Department of Natural Resources administers the site.[^1]

The state's Division of Forestry set up its first lookout here in 1917.[^2][^3] Willhite says the state board of forest commissioners agreed in April 1917 to work with the federal government, drawing on reports of success at lookouts in Idaho and Oregon.[^3] Because the peak was so sharp, part of its top had to be blasted away to make room for a 10 by 12 station.[^3] B.P. Moore and his son Henry of Randle built it.[^3] The first design was a D-6 cupola, and an L-4 cab replaced it in 1935.[^1][^3]

A Weeks Law patrolman served as watchman in 1917.[^3] In 1918 the lookout was V.W. Bigger, a newspaper man from Texas.[^3] P.K. Herrington was the lookout in 1926 and 1927.[^3] In July 1926 he spotted a fire southeast of the Glenoma schoolhouse that burned over hundreds of acres.[^3] In 1930 Elcana DeRossett had a bad fall over a cliff, but he went back to work.[^3]

In 1919 the Washington Forest Fire Association said haze, smoke or fog often obscured the view from its lookouts, and that Kiona had been of little use.[^3] In 1922 the lookout was abandoned for the season.[^3]

From March 13, 1942 to April 1, 1944 the site was used by the AWS, and a sleeping quarters and woodshed were added.[^3] Firelookout.com's heading reads "R.I.P. 1917-1935," but its own text puts the end in 1963.[^2] In 1963 the site was abandoned when a new tower went up on Watch Mountain.[^1][^2][^3]

Willhite reports that much of the cabling still hangs on both sides of the cliff, and that a number of old boards and pieces lie below.[^3]

Willhite calls the approach long and hard. He says the most direct routes have many gates and no-trespassing signs. The route he recommends runs over logging roads for more than 10 miles one way, and he says he did not take it himself.[^3] We found no source that describes a public route to the summit, so check access before going.
