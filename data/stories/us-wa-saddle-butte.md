Saddle Butte rises in Asotin County, Washington, on the Umatilla National Forest.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association register and FireLookout.com give its elevation as 5,840 feet (1,780 m), while the header of the Willhite page says 5,900 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

Mt. Misery was linked to the butte by a telephone line in 1931.[^3] The lookout followed in 1932: a 20-foot round native timber tower with an L-4 observation cabin.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Dick Shafer held the lookout post that year, bringing in lumber by pack horse and helping with construction.[^3]

A 1965 newspaper item, quoted on a Washington lookouts site, described a contract to build 1.6 miles of road for "the construction of the Saddle Butte Lookout."[^4] The item does not say whether a new tower went up, and the other sources keep 1932 as the tower's date.[^1][^2][^3]

The tower has long since departed, according to the register.[^1] FireLookout.com leaves the end date as a question mark.[^2]

According to the Willhite page, visitors can see the footing and the outhouse.[^3] The page notes that a rock outline around the tent was still there in 2016.[^3]

The Willhite directions start in Pomeroy and head south 15 miles to the Umatilla National Forest.[^3] The page calls the 1.7-mile Saddle Butte Road rocky in places, recommends high clearance, and says to park at the summit.[^3]
