Middleboro's fire lookout started indoors. The town's first watch post was a room in the observatory of the Middleboro Town Hall.[^1]

By 1915 local officials wanted something better. A Boston newspaper reported that spring that plans were underway for a large steel tower on Barden Hill, described as the highest ground in that part of town and among the highest in Plymouth County, with the state covering part of the cost and the local Pierce trust covering the rest.[^2] The register for the lookout gives a slightly earlier date, saying the Town Hall post was replaced by a 48-foot tower on Barden Hill in 1913.[^1] The two sources don't agree on exactly when the Barden Hill tower went up, but it was clearly built in the mid-1910s. By January 1918 the state forester's annual report noted that the town had just paid to replace the tower's ladder with a proper staircase, and that the ladder itself had only been in use "for some two or three years" — consistent with a tower that was new, or close to it, a few years earlier.[^3]

That first steel tower on Barden Hill stood for about half a century. In 1966 it was replaced by the tower that still stands today: a 75-foot steel tower carrying a 10-by-10-foot cab, built under the Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control, with District 2 listed as a cooperating agency.[^1] The site sits at roughly 167 feet of elevation in Middleboro, Plymouth County.[^1]

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in December 2004, nominated by Henry Isenberg, and carries the register numbers US 616 and MA 27.[^1]

Beyond those bones, the record is thin. No source found here says who staffed the tower, when fire-watching there ended, or what condition the tower is in today, and nothing indicates whether the site is open to the public or on private land. Anyone planning a visit should treat its access and climbing status as unknown rather than assume either way.
