Sims Peak in Shasta County got a new Forest Service lookout station in 1931.[^1] A newspaper item from July that year reported that the station had been completed.[^1] A historical marker credits the crew of the Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Sims with building three fire lookouts, Sims among them, and says the camp lasted six years.[^2] The two accounts do not clearly agree, and we found no source that settles whether the camp's crew built the station or rebuilt an older one.[^1][^2]

The station had keepers through the 1940s and into the 1960s.[^1] A 1944 item notes that Rhae Herron was in her second year on the job.[^1] A 1945 item says Martha Glidden was doing the lookout work in her husband's place while he served in the armed forces.[^1] A 1947 item names Mildred Jackson as the Sims lookout.[^1] In 1962 Lenore Broze, a retired school teacher from Oakland, was in her 16th year, and the same item says Sims and Granite Peak were staffed only during extreme fire-hazard weather.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the Sims tower as gone.[^3] The Peakbagging table of California lookout sites lists Sims as a lookout site. Its key defines such a site as one whose building has been taken down, with possibly nothing left to see.[^4] Neither source gives a date for the removal or a reason for it.[^3][^4]

Sims Flat, a Forest Service campground off Sims Road near Castella, is the historic location of a 1930s CCC camp.[^5][^2] According to a marker there, the camp's rock-lined pathway now survives as little more than faint traces.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the peak where the lookout stood, or whether that ground is open to visitors.
