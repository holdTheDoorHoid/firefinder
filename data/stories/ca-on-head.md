The Head Fire Tower is an Ontario lookout listed in the Open Canada Fire Lookout Index, which puts it at 46.22528 N, 77.87944 W.[^1] A Department of Lands and Forests Tower Detection System Map from 1963 shows the tower.[^1]

A fan-run history site says most Ontario towers were built in the decades from the 1920s to the 1950s, a peak era for forest fire protection.[^2] The same site says the towers fell under the Department of Lands and Forests, which in 1972 became the Ministry of Natural Resources.[^2] Through the 1960s, aerial spotting and firefighting crews increasingly handled the work, and the towers saw steadily less use.[^2]

The tower's name matches the first word of Head, Clara and Maria, a municipality in Renfrew County in eastern Ontario.[^3] No source I found ties the tower to that township, so the match is only a clue.

Today, no source I found describes what remains at the Head site, or whether the public can visit it.
