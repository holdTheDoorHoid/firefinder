Bolton Mountain, in Chittenden County, got a steel tower on its summit in 1921 and 1922. Ralph Dunsmoor of Burlington did most of the work, and C.D. Ordway donated the tower.[^1] In September 1921 the Burlington Free Press reported that Dunsmoor's tower, then being built, was 30 feet high.[^1] A January 1922 report says the cost was shared by the state club, the Burlington section of the Green Mountain Club, the state forestry department and several private donors.[^1]

In July 1922 the Morrisville Messenger reported that Ralph Dunsmoor and friends had hauled the materials for a steel observation tower, 40 feet tall, to the summit. The paper added that such a tower would lift visitors above the treetops for a broad outlook.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts entry ends with the single word "Removed," and it gives no date.[^1] Firelookout.org lists the Bolton Mountain tower as gone.[^2]

No source I read describes the summit today, including whether anything from the 1922 tower remains or whether the public may go up there. A wooden tower stands at Bolton Valley Resort, which one guide calls the Bolton Fire Tower.[^3][^4] Visitors can climb it by the Vista Lookout Tower Trail.[^3] The Vermont Daily Chronicle says just one known wooden tower survives in the state, at Bolton Valley.[^4] The sources do not say whether that tower stands on the same summit as the 1922 steel tower, so do not assume it does.
