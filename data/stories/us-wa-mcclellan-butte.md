McClellan Butte is a popular hike along the I-90 corridor near North Bend, and it once carried a fire lookout.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a cabin there from the 1930s that no longer stands, and says the site was registered on June 18, 2012.[^1] FireLookout.com gives the same decade and calls the cabin gone.[^2]

Sources say little about the building itself.[^1][^2][^3] WillhiteWeb reports, citing Ray Kresek, that a cabin was used there in the 1930s.[^3] The same page judges a firefinder more likely on the summit, since the top is too cramped for a cabin.[^3] The register and FireLookout.com both describe a cabin, which conflicts with WillhiteWeb's firefinder view.[^1][^2][^3]

As of a 2013 Backpacker trip report, the trail officially ended about 100 feet below the exposed summit, on a small plateau.[^4] The report tentatively suggests the old cabin may have stood there.[^4] Near the top, hikers pass decaying pipes and cables, which the report says are the leftovers of radio beacons that once guided planes through the pass.[^4] WillhiteWeb also places some cabin remains in meadows beneath the summit rock.[^3]

The Forest Service lists the McClellan Butte trailhead as open and says it provides access to Trail 1015.[^5] Recreation passes cover day-use fees there.[^5] WillhiteWeb warns that the final rocky summit has serious exposure.[^3]
