The Temagami Fire Tower stands on Caribou Mountain, about 122 m (400 ft) above the town of Temagami in the Nipissing District of northeastern Ontario.[^1][^3] The first tower on the summit went up in 1910, a few metres east of the present site. It was a square-timber structure 13.7 m (45 ft) high.[^1][^2]

The tower was a detection post. Watchmen climbed it every day in fire season to scan for smoke.[^2] Forest Rangers staffed nine towers in the area. They used field glasses, a detailed map and an alidade and azimuth to fix the position of a fire.[^1] Towers passed their bearings to dispatch crews by telegraph, telephone or radio, and two or three bearings were enough to place a fire on a map.[^2]

In the late 1930s the wooden tower was demolished and replaced by a 25.9 m (85 ft) light steel tower.[^1] Because of rust, that steel tower was demolished in 1961 and sent over the edge of the cliff.[^1] A 30.5 m (100 ft) steel tower replaced it that year, built by seven Forest Rangers.[^1]

The tower standing today is that 1961 structure, a steel tower about 30.5 m (100 ft) tall.[^2][^3][^4]

Two pages say the tower was last used to spot fires 25 years before they were written.[^1][^3] The municipality dates the restoration to the mid-1990s and the re-dedication to 1998.[^2] Two pages give the reopening as 9 August 1998, when the tower was dedicated to the Ministry of Natural Resources' Forest Rangers.[^1][^3]

Today the tower is a place to visit. The Municipality of Temagami describes visitors climbing its steps, and warns that they can be icy in early spring or slippery when wet.[^2] A cupola at the top gives a 360-degree view that one visitor page puts at more than 40 km.[^3] Information on the Forest Rangers is kept in an interpretive centre close to the parking area.[^1] One visitor page gives the admission to the Caribou Mountain visitor area as $2. That page is undated, so check current fees before you go.[^3]
