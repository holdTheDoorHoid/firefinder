Eastwood is an unincorporated community in southwestern Carter County, inside the Mark Twain National Forest, about seven miles south of Van Buren.[^2] Its fire tower was planned in 1935 and completed in 1936.[^1]

In 1935 a crew of Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) men ran a telephone line toward a tower that the local paper said would be erected "down near the cemetery."[^1] A 1936 report placed the site about a mile east of town, on a high hill along the Bark Camp road, where the view was wide: it "commands a view of a large scope of country in all directions."[^1] The 1935 and 1936 items disagree on the site, and the 1936 location is used here.[^1]

Work on the foundations began in May 1936. By June, solid rock near the bottom of the holes had slowed the work, and the paper said blasting with dynamite was ruled out because it could shatter the rock below.[^1]

By August 1936 the Forest Service lookout was finished. The page's 1936 note gives it as a 100-foot steel tower.[^1]

In September 1936 Odis White held a half-time post as tower tender, sharing the duty on alternate days with another man, while a residence was being built at the site for whoever would be placed in charge of the lookout.[^1] Joe Donley replaced Youle Iller at the tower in December 1936.[^1] In 1938 the paper called Otis White the U.S. towerman at Eastwood.[^1] The paper spells the White first name three different ways: Odis, Otis and Oades.[^1]

In March 1946 the paper reported that Oades White and his family had moved from Eastwood to Birch Tree. It called him keeper of the Eastwood tower "for many moons" and said the paper assumed he had been moved to comparable work in Shannon County.[^1]

No source describes what remains at the Eastwood tower site today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1]
