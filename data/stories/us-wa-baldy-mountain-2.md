Baldy Mountain lies about 14 miles west of Chelan, in Chelan County, Washington.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both date a lookout here to 1931 and describe it as an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] The register says the cab "is now gone" and gives no year for its removal.[^1] The firelookout.com entry is as brief: "1931: L-4 cab. Gone."[^2]

The sources also differ on which forest the site belongs to. The register places it in the Wenatchee National Forest, while the Forest Lookouts page names the Chelan National Forest.[^1][^5]

Steve Willhite, who visited the summit, writes that a 1931 L-4 cab is reported for Baldy, but that he found no evidence of one.[^3] He describes a sheltered flat between the two highest rock knobs, with old cut stumps, fire rings and cans that suggest camping, and he thinks a camp may have been on the summit instead.[^3] He reports finding no building materials on the summit, such as anchors or concrete.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Washington list files the site as a ground site with the status Gone.[^4]

Willhite's route runs up the Entiat Valley Road past Ardenvoir, then along forest roads FR 5380 and FR 8410, and he advises high clearance.[^3] He gives an elevation gain of 1,000 feet and says a short scramble reaches the rocky top.[^3] No source describes a standing structure on the summit, and none says whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^3]
