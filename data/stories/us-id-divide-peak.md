Divide Peak, also called Seventy-Two, is a lookout site on the Montana-Idaho state line, in Shoshone County, Idaho.[^1][^2] It lies about 14 miles southeast of Clark Fork.[^1] Forest Lookouts lists the same tower on the Montana side, under Sanders County, as 72 Peak.[^3]

The lookout was reported complete on October 21, 1931, in the Sanders County Independent-Ledger.[^3][^2] It stood on a 30-foot tower, with a porch all the way around it and heavy insulation against lightning.[^2] The paper said a car could reach it on the Jacks Gulch road, south of Heron.[^2]

The tower was gone by 1950, but the sources give different accounts of how. Idaho Fire Lookouts says it was destroyed that year.[^2] Firelookout.com gives 1950 as the year it was gone, and Forest Lookouts lists it as removed, with no date.[^1][^3]

The sources also name different national forests. Firelookout.com lists the Coeur d'Alene and Kootenai National Forests, and Forest Lookouts names the Kootenai.[^1][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts first visited the wrong spot, then made a return trip and found the correct site.[^2] The page labels the site drive-up, but it is dated July 4, 2022, and no source says whether the road is open today.[^2] A 1971 photo caption on the page places a rock cairn at the site, and no source says whether any of it is still there.[^2] None of the pages we read describes what is on the summit today.
