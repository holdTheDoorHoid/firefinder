Big Butte is on the Umatilla National Forest, near Anatone in Asotin County.[^1][^3] It sits at about 5,000 feet.[^1][^2][^5]

The sources disagree about when the first tower went up. The Register says the site was first set up in 1938, with an L-6 cab on a 73-foot pole tower.[^1] Firelookout.com also gives 1938 for that pole tower and its cabin.[^2] Forest Lookouts quotes a Umatilla National Forest inventory listing an 80-foot native timber tower with a 7-by-7 cab erected in 1929, and TrailChick gives the same year.[^3][^5]

In 1950 an 82-foot treated timber tower with an L-4 cab and catwalk replaced the earlier tower.[^1][^2] During the winter of 2010 and 2011, a tree fell across a guy wire and damaged one of the tower legs.[^2][^5]

TrailChick says the cab was lowered to the ground in 2013 and the tower was taken apart.[^5] In 2014, two Forest Fire Lookout Association members and a crew of other volunteers and Forest Service staff spent three days stabilizing and restoring the cab.[^5] An association report from 2015 says final touches on the cab were completed.[^4] The lookout was once considered for a rental program.[^5] The longer-term plan, to rebuild the tower and replace the restored cab, never came to fruition.[^5]

A 2018 visit found the cab still on the ground.[^5] Washington Trails Association says the tower has been deconstructed.[^6] At the top, the tower cap lies on the ground, beside stacked lumber, leg hardware and some outbuildings.[^6] The route runs up Forest Road 170 from its junction with Forest Road 4304, about half a mile to a gate and a tenth of a mile more to the top.[^6] On the way up, the track climbs into a clearing with views to the south and west.[^6] No tower is left to climb, so check with the Pomeroy Ranger District for current conditions before you go.[^6][^1]
