Dayton Peak is in Mason County, Washington.[^1][^2] Sources give its distance from Shelton as three, six or eight miles.[^2][^3]

The first lookout began as an Aircraft Warning Service building, moved up from the nearby Lost Lake Hills in 1944.[^2][^3] Willhite Web describes that building as a 14-by-32-foot crew station.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register instead dates the first lookout to 1946 and gives it as 20 by 40 feet.[^3][^1] Sources also disagree on the observation room added on top. Willhite Web says a 10-by-10 room went up in the summer of 1945.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page quotes a forestry annual report saying a 10-by-10 tower was added in 1946.[^3][^4]

Gerald "Jerry" Needham was the lookout, and a 1963 passage still names him and Stephie, saying, with some doubt, that they had been there since 1944.[^3] Needham's own account says the family moved in with four children in May 1945.[^3] Curtis Snow was the official lookout in 1965, with David Wood on relief, though the same page's timeline calls him Kurt Snow.[^3] Nancy Huber was the lookout in 1966.[^3] In 1948 the station got public power, which the page calls a first for a Division of Forestry lookout.[^3]

In 1964 the cabin was replaced with a 40-foot timber tower with a live-in DNR cab.[^1][^2][^3] Willhite Web and the Forest Lookouts page put the new tower a quarter mile southeast of the first site, but firelookout.com says half a mile.[^3][^4][^2] The tower was removed in 1969.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both describe that removal as abrupt.[^1][^2]

No source describes a standing structure at the site now. Willhite Web's visit found concrete chunks on the highest summit, but could not say whether they came from the lookout tower or the radio tower that once stood there.[^3] Willhite Web says everyone agrees the first lookout stood on the northwest peak.[^3] Willhite Web's photos include a USGS benchmark with the disk missing.[^3]

Willhite Web says a permit is now needed on most roads to the top, though an old trail offers another route up.[^3] Any vehicle can reach the gate where the trail starts, and the walk is three miles one way.[^3] We found no source saying whether the summit is open to visitors today.
