Lookout Peak is a hilltop just west of Ukiah, in Mendocino County.[^1] The California Department of Forestry ran a fire lookout station there.[^1]

In March 1930 the chief of the division of forestry said a lookout station would operate next summer on the hill west of Clay Street.[^1] Pine Ridge had been considered first, but a survey found this hill suitable and much more accessible to telephone service.[^1] A fire trail was under construction from the foot of Clay Street, and a telephone line was to be installed with the station.[^1]

In June 1930 the Ukiah Dispatch Democrat reported that a twenty-foot "lookout tower" had gone up that week on the tallest of the hills just west of Ukiah.[^1] On June 18 the Ukiah Republican Press gave 20 feet for the building and tower together, so the papers do not agree on whether the 20 feet is the tower alone.[^1]

The papers name one observer, firewarden M. Hair.[^1] A July 19 report said he would go on duty on July 21, and a July 30 report said the station had opened on a Monday with him on duty.[^1]

The station was to keep 24-hour lookout service for the rest of that summer.[^1]

In August 1930 the lookout reported smoke from Pine Mountain, near the Sonoma and Mendocino county line.[^1] A forest ranger had it under control after several hours, by which time seven acres had burned over.[^1] In September 1930 the lookout spotted smoke from a house fire on Low Gap Road, and rangers kept it from reaching nearby timber.[^1]

The fire lookout index lists the tower as gone.[^2]

A mountain-bike news article says the Ukiah City Council approved a local trail group, the Ukiah Valley Trail Group, to build 22 miles of trails in the Mendocino Range.[^3] The first item on its list is a four-mile loop on a 700-acre strip that climbs about 2,000 feet.[^3] We found no source that describes what remains of the 1930 station, or whether the upper slopes are open to visitors today.[^3]
