Cahto Peak rises to about 4,198 feet in the Mendocino National Forest, one of the higher points in Mendocino County, and on a clear day the lookout built there is visible from as far as 35 miles off.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service pushed a road to the summit, and the lookout was dedicated on July 10, 1934.[^1] It is an enclosed tower cab, the kind of design built to let a lookout live and work in the same small room through fire season.[^2]

For decades someone watched from the cab each summer, scanning the surrounding ridgelines for smoke. That ended in 1990, when budget cuts took the staffing away, and the tower has stood unmanned since.[^1]

The structure is still standing today, administered by the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection (CAL FIRE).[^1] A volunteer survey in March 2019 found it holding up reasonably well but showing its age: the railings were weathered, a shutter had come partially loose, and the decking around the cab had deteriorated and broken through in a couple of places.[^1] Getting there means driving a BLM road that is gated roughly half a mile to a mile short of the lookout; the one roadside sign gives mileage to the peak without mentioning the fire lookout at all.[^1]

Cahto Peak carries two separate listings on paper: it is entered on the National Historic Lookout Register (US 1125, CA 110), registered March 20, 2016, and it also appears, as a standing enclosed tower, on the Forest Fire Lookout Association's register, which cross-references the same NHLR number.[^1][^2] Whether it is open to casual visitors is not documented here; treat the gated BLM road as what the record describes — a locked gate on forest land, not a marked trailhead.
