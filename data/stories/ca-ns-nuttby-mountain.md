Nuttby Mountain, in Nova Scotia, carried a fire lookout tower from 1937 to 2013.[^1] The peak sits west of Route 311, about 20 km north of Truro.[^1]

Steven McRae, a Nuttby resident who has studied the tower's history, says the steel arrived in Truro by train in 1937.[^2] His grandfather and two of the grandfather's brothers hauled it up the mountain with a horse-drawn wagon.[^2] Milton, one of those brothers, and a neighbour drilled anchor holes into the rock after the tower was raised.[^2] It was raised upright and fitted with an enclosed wooden observation deck, and it stood about 20 m (66 ft) in the air.[^2]

The Department of Lands and Forest oversaw the tower in its early years, and the Department of Natural Resources took over later.[^2] A fire ranger was posted in the tower from May until September, watching in every direction for smoke or fire.[^2] McRae compiled a partial list of the men who worked the deck; it has eight names, including Jim Higgins and Jack Ferguson.[^2] The job was once described as "days of boredom and moments of terror," yet it was considered very important.[^2]

In 1954, Hurricane Edna blew the top off the tower.[^3] McRae, who spent a lot of his childhood up there, remembers views over parts of Truro and, on a clear day, as far as P.E.I.[^2] McRae says his research placed the peak first among mainland Nova Scotia summits for elevation above sea level, and third in the province overall.[^2]

The tower was removed in November 2013.[^3][^2] A Maritimes scanning website's list of the department's towers, last updated in September 2009, included Nuttby Mtn.[^4]

Today telecommunication towers top the summit, and a gated road leads to it.[^1] A 45-megawatt wind farm was built on the mountain in 2010.[^1] No source describes what remains of the tower's footings or the anchor holes today, and none says whether the gate is open to the public.
