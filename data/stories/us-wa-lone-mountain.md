Lone Mountain rises on the Quinault Indian Reservation, about five miles west of the lower end of Lake Quinault and about a mile south of the Olympic Highway.[^4][^1] In 1937 a steel lookout tower was going up there, and R.E. Voorhies hauled the materials for it.[^4][^1] Pack horses were the only way to get the building materials up the steep trail, and a newspaper of the day said the job took 12 days.[^4][^1]

Panorama photos were taken there on July 22, 1937.[^3] A 1954 USGS visit, as reported on WillhiteWeb, describes a steel tower on four legs, 90 feet high.[^4] Most sources give its height as 90 feet, but the 1937 article said 80.[^1][^2][^4] Firelookout.com gives the 1940s as the decade it was built, which does not fit the 1937 construction.[^2][^4]

The tower sat abandoned for many years before it came down.[^1][^2] Photo captions on WillhiteWeb show it standing in 1996 and 2003.[^4] Two sources date its removal to 2011 and two to 2012.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Three of them say it was sold for scrap.[^2][^3][^4]

Photos of the site from 2022 show footings.[^4] A caption also mentions a tree on the summit, with anchors running through its middle.[^4]

Lone Mountain is on Quinault tribal land.[^4] Heed any signs that are posted, and ask about access permits, which WillhiteWeb says can be obtained in some years.[^4] The hike to the site is 1.3 miles one way, with about 700 feet of climbing.[^4]
