Hanging Rock, in Coos County, Oregon, got a 40-foot lookout tower in 1939.[^1][^3] In August 1939 the lookout was still housed in a tent.[^2] That month the Myrtle Point Herald reported that a new 40-foot tower and lookout station were to be built, with CCC boys under the ranger's supervision doing the work.[^2] The Coos Bay Times said in October that the new lookout was nearly finished.[^2] The tower carried an L-4 cab.[^1][^4] A newspaper report also said the lookout offered excellent coverage of the Rogue River country and the upper reaches of the Coquille's South Fork.[^2]

The lookout was staffed for 94 days in 1941, and its reports went to the Powers Ranger Station over a West Coast Telephone line.[^2] A 1940 guard placement plan said the site had good views of the Agness and Galice districts, where fires caused by people were most likely to start.[^2]

The register says the lookout was abandoned during the 1950s and no longer stands.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list both call the tower gone.[^3][^5] Weebly's history says the tower was removed, but gives no date.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.
