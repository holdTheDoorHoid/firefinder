The 150-foot solar tower on Mount Wilson was built for solar research, and fire watching came later.[^1][^2] Wikipedia says the tower was first completed in 1910, but unsatisfactory optics caused a two-year delay before a suitable lens was installed, and it lists completion in 1912.[^2] The 150 feet is its focal length; the whole structure is 176 feet tall.[^2] Its tower-in-a-tower design kept the optics isolated from wind.[^2]

The register says the tower was used for fire detection in 1919 and the 1920s.[^1] Angeles Adventures, which draws on Mark V. Thornton's 1986 book on fire lookouts, dates the Forest Service's occupation of the tower to 1924, a few years after the register's 1919 date.[^4] It says a lookout-fireman used the tower as a supplementary detection point, and that it was possibly used until the 1940s.[^4] Its 1919 newspaper quote describes a student observer detachment stationed on Mount Wilson, not the tower itself.[^4]

A 1924 Los Angeles Times item, quoted on Angeles Adventures, said the observatory tower had been given over to forestry officials for that season. It also named Lewis H. Humason of the observatory staff as a volunteer lookout.[^4] On July 15, 1924, the register reproduces a report that a lookout at the observatory spotted a forest fire on the west side of San Gabriel Canyon and telephoned the alarm.[^1]

Later, UCLA took over the solar tower from the Carnegie Observatories in 1985, once they had withdrawn their funding for the observatory.[^2] The register records the lookout's registration on November 3, 2019.[^1]

Today the observatory says its grounds are open to visitors, and its site lists weekend public tours along with private, engineering and self-guided options.[^3] A PBS SoCal guide says people on the weekend public tour usually enter the 150-foot tower.[^5] I found no source that opens the tower to visitors outside a scheduled tour, so check the observatory's site for current tour dates before going.
