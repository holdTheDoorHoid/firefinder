Numa Ridge got its start as a simple fire camp in 1922, when the spot was still called Indian Ridge.[^3] A frame cabin went up at the site, around 7,100 feet, the following year.[^3] The two-story timber lookout standing there today replaced that early cabin in 1934, built to a standard plan the Forest Service had developed for staffing a chain of overlapping lookouts across Glacier National Park.[^1][^2] Only nine miles separate it from the Canadian border, making it the northernmost lookout in the park; the cab measures 14 feet square and sits under a hip roof.[^1][^2]

The lookout has a long staffing record. Scotty Beaton reportedly held the post from 1922 to 1948.[^3] Decades later, in the summer of 1975, novelist and essayist Edward Abbey worked a season there as the fire lookout, an experience he wrote about afterward.[^2] Numa Ridge was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1986 as part of the Glacier National Park Multiple Resource Area, and it was separately registered on the National Historic Lookout Register in 1991.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree slightly on the exact build year: the park's own Register nomination describes the structure as built in 1933, while the National Historic Lookout Register and other trail guides give 1934.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout is still standing, per the most recent structure-status listings.[^4] Current-day staffing levels for Numa Ridge aren't confirmed by the sources checked here, though it remains an NPS-administered fire-watch structure rather than a rental. The site is reachable by a public trail from Lake Bowman, making it a popular destination for hikers even though the cab itself is off-limits to visitors.[^1]
