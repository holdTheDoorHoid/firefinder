The Forest Service had a lookout on Gearhart Mountain by 1930. The Lake County Tribune reported in July of that year that it had been open since July 1, with Lester Collins in charge.[^1] The same page quotes a 1926 newspaper report of a Klamath Forest Protective Association lookout on the summit that reported the Lakeview fire.[^1] It is not clear from these sources whether that earlier post stood on the same spot as the 1930 lookout.

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says an L-5 cab was built in 1935, about two miles southeast of the summit.[^2] Firelookout.com gives the same 1935 date.[^3] The register says the cab was replaced in the 1950s with a 10-foot treated timber R-6 tower, and Firelookout.com lists the same tower.[^2][^3]

In September 1937, two enrollees from the Camp Bly Civilian Conservation Corps camp were stationed at the lookout.[^1] A note on the same page describes it as an emergency post, used only when the weather was very hot.[^1]

A National Geodetic Survey station description quoted on the same page says that, because no azimuth mark had been set, the roof apex of the lookout cabin was used instead.[^1] It describes a 10-by-12-foot building about 15 feet high, 4 and a half miles southeast of the survey station.[^1]

The register says the tower is now gone.[^2] Firelookout.com agrees.[^3]

A 2020 hiking blog describes an abandoned fire lookout about a third of a mile up an old road from the Palisade Rocks trailhead.[^4] The author says it is closed to visitors for safety reasons and describes it as unmaintained and deteriorating.[^4] It is not clear whether this is the structure the register lists as gone.

The blog places the Palisade Rocks trail in the Gearhart Mountain Wilderness, in the Fremont-Winema National Forest, and reaches the old lookout from the same trailhead.[^4] The register lists the lookout site in the Fremont National Forest.[^2] The trailhead is at the end of National Forest road 012.[^4] In July 2020 the blog found the road a little washed out and rough, though a pickup could reach the trailhead, and it reports no facilities there.[^4] Because the lookout is closed for safety reasons, do not climb it.[^4] No source we read describes what remains of the 1935 cab site itself today.
