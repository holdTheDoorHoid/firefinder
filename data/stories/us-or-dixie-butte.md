Dixie Butte overlooks Prairie City from the eastern end of the John Day Valley.[^1] The lookout sits in the Malheur National Forest, in Grant County, Oregon.[^2] A cabin with a rooftop platform went up on the summit around 1911.[^3] That same year a lookout there was connected by telephone to the central office.[^4]

A new lookout building was finished in August 1921, and one newspaper called it the best equipped on the reserve.[^4] The L-4 ground house that replaced the cupola cabin of the early 1920s dates to 1935.[^2] A windstorm damaged that structure in the fall of 1967, and the present cabin followed in 1968.[^1][^2]

The cab is staffed each summer, according to one lookout history site.[^5] Cheryl Hill visited in July 2024 and spoke with the lookout on duty.[^3]

In August 1916 the lookout reported four forest fires in two days, and crews were sent out from Sumpter to fight them.[^4] A second 1916 report says an observer spotted smoke 45 miles away and phoned the fire's location to the John Day office.[^4]

The register says the present cabin remains in active service.[^2]

Forest Road 2610 is rough and rocky, and one guide recommends high-clearance vehicles because of washouts and gullies.[^1][^3] In 2024 Cheryl Hill drove about four miles of it and walked the rest.[^3] The Malheur National Forest's rental list names one lookout, Fall Mountain, and Dixie Butte is not on it.[^6]

Sources disagree on the elevation. The register gives 7,511 feet, while two other pages give 7,592 feet.[^2][^5][^3]

Before a visit, check with the Prairie City Ranger District, since the cab is in active use.[^2]
