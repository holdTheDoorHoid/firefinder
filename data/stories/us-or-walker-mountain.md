Walker Mountain Lookout stands on a peak in the Deschutes National Forest, in Klamath County, about 11 miles south of Crescent.[^1][^2][^4] The summit was used as a patrol point as early as 1907, and by 1913 a crow's nest had been set in a tree.[^1][^3]

The stone cabin for living quarters was begun in 1915, and the Register says it was closed in before that season ended.[^1] Sources disagree on the year. cherylhill.net agrees with the Register's 1915.[^3] firelookout.com dates the cabin to 1917 and calls it the oldest administrative building on the forest, and a photo caption in the Register also reads 1917.[^1][^2]

A pole tower about 25 feet tall went up in 1919.[^1][^3] A road to the summit followed in 1932, and the steel tower is 35 feet tall with a hip-roofed L-4 cab that measures 14 feet on each side.[^1][^2][^3] The Register is not consistent on the tower's date. It says construction began in the fall of 1932 and was not finished until 1933, yet it also says the tower was constructed in 1932.[^1]

Staffing ended at different times depending on the source. A 1991 report, as summarized by cherylhill.net, says the lookout had stopped full-time staffing and was manned only during high fire danger.[^3] The Roaming Civic says the tower has been out of use since the 1940s.[^4] The 2024 post says the tower is not staffed in any capacity.[^3]

The summit has since become a communication tower site.[^3] The 2024 post says the Forest Service approved a 140-foot tower and a 600-square-foot building for the Bonneville Power Administration in 2009.[^3]

The lookout tower is abandoned, and its bottom stairs have been removed, which the 2022 trip report says was meant to stop people climbing it.[^4] The Register records a 2005 restoration of the stone cabin.[^1] The 2024 visitor found the old cabin showing its age and wrote that nothing seemed to have been done since a 1998 volunteer project.[^3]

The Register listed the lookout on January 19, 1996.[^1] Forest roads lead to the summit.[^4] A 2024 post puts the summit 3.7 miles up Road 9402, and says that road had recently been graveled.[^3] The 2022 trip report says NF-9402 had been recently regraded, and that the summit is a little over 3.5 miles from the junction with NF-94 and NF-9402.[^4] The garage, privy and stone cabin on the summit can be visited, the 2022 trip report says.[^4] Check road and access conditions with the Crescent Ranger District before going.[^1][^4]
