The Timpson lookout tower stood about six miles southeast of the town of Timpson, in Shelby County, and the Texas Forest Service ran it.[^1] A 1958 Coast and Geodetic Survey description puts the tower on four steel legs, about 100 feet overall, with a glass-enclosed cab colored gray.[^1]

The tower was in use by August 1946, when a Timpson Weekly Times report named Forest Patrolman E. E. Covington at the Timpson forest fire lookout tower.[^1] He reported a July fire that burned more than eight acres. The same report put Shelby County's 1946 total at 23 fires and 1,191 acres burned.[^1]

In October 1964 the Texas Forest Service advertised for a crewman-equipment operator who had to live within 12 miles of the tower, at a salary of $100 a month.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas index marks the Timpson tower as gone.[^2] No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors may go there.
