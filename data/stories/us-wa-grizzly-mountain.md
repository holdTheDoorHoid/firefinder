The Grizzly Mountain lookout stands on a peak in Ferry County, Washington, about 19 miles southeast of Republic.[^1] Its summit is at 6,381 feet.[^2] The land is part of the Colville Indian Reservation.[^1][^2]

The tower went up in 1941.[^1][^2][^3] It is a steel tower about 30 feet tall, with a cab measuring 7 by 7 feet.[^1][^3] We found no source that explains why it was built, and none that names the people who staffed it or the fires it watched.

Some sources suggest the tower was moved here. All three sites we read say the evidence suggests it may have come from Moses Mountain, where an identical tower was removed in 1938. None of them describes a documented move.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout was abandoned in 1952.[^1][^2][^3] The ground cabin where people once lived is gone, and so is the roof.[^1][^3] The tower still stands, and it has taken on a new job, holding several antennas.[^1][^4]

In the summer of 2021 the Summit Trail fire, started by lightning on July 12, burned nearly 50,000 acres west of Inchelium.[^2] TrailChick says the fire threatened the lookout and could make it harder to reach.[^2] We did not find a report on what the fire did to the tower itself.

Visiting is mostly a question of the road. WillhiteWeb describes the last mile as extremely steep and says high-clearance four-wheel drive is needed there. It also notes that logging could keep the road open, and that conditions change from year to year.[^3] TrailChick drove to within about two miles of the summit in 2019, then walked after a large downed tree blocked the road.[^2] The sources we read do not state any rule for entering reservation land, so check with the land managers before you go. We do not recommend climbing the tower, which WillhiteWeb calls nerve-wracking because of missing boards and side rails.[^3]
