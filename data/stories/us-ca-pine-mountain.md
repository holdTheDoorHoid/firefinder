Pine Mountain Lookout went up in 1933 on the Mendocino National Forest, high above the Eel River country near Upper Lake.[^1] It's a BC-201 lookout-fireman cabin, a style built with windows on only three sides, giving the lookout a 180-degree field of view rather than the full circle most towers get.[^1][^2] Recreation.gov notes that this partial view is actually what makes the site historically notable among California lookouts.[^2]

The tower did regular fire-watch duty into the early 1940s, and newspaper notices from the area put names to the job: in the summer of 1943, Mr. and Mrs. B.M. Bucknell took over lookout duty from Ralph Johnson, who had been hurt fighting fire.[^3] A year later, Earl Mersinger was injured near Pine Mountain while fighting a fire, and the post was filled by Mrs. Carl Capell, with her niece June Marie Bauer helping out.[^3] Recreation.gov states fire detection work ran until 1942, with the gap suggesting staffing continued informally, or under a different arrangement, into the Bucknell and Mersinger years that followed.[^2]

With World War II came a second job: Pine Mountain was pressed into service as part of California's aircraft warning network, with people watching the skies as well as the ridgelines for smoke.[^1][^2]

The lookout eventually fell out of regular fire-detection use, and the Forest Service has since restored it.[^1] It is now part of the agency's rental program, open to the public roughly from May through October, when the dirt road in is passable by car.[^1][^4] Renters get a simple cabin with bunks, cots, and no running water or power, reached through a combination-locked gate, with the code sent ahead of arrival.[^4] Group size is capped at six, and no overnight camping is allowed anywhere else on the site.[^4]
