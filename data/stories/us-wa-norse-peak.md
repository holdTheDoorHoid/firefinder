Norse Peak rises about 16 miles east-northeast of Mount Rainier, at 6,860 feet, in Pierce County.[^1][^2] Its lookout was an L-4 cab, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates it to 1931.[^1] WillhiteWeb gives the same year,[^3] while the Forest Service says only that the lookout went up in the 1930s.[^4] Panoramic photographs from 1929 already show a telephone line at the site, three years before the cab was built.[^3]

In 1944 several newspapers across the country ran a story about two young women, Dora Hunt and Maxine Ilipkoe, who had been assigned to the lookout.[^3][^5] Before they could start work, the two had to train in map reading, weather reporting, fire-finding gear and basic fire-fighting.[^3] The newspaper story, dated February 25, 1944, describes a summer at the lookout, but the sources we read do not say which year that summer was.[^5] WillhiteWeb also reports that on July 11 a blizzard with ice hit the lookout.[^3]

The register and the Forest Service both say the cab was removed in 1956.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] WillhiteWeb is less certain and says it was either removed or destroyed.[^3]

Today, WillhiteWeb reports that few signs of the lookout are left at the site, and it has summit photographs from a 2026 visit.[^3] We found no source that describes the footings or any other remains in detail.

The cab is gone, so there is no tower to climb.[^1] The summit is reached on foot. The Norse Peak View Trail is a spur off Norse Peak Trail 1191 that leads to the summit and the former lookout site, about four miles from the trailhead to the spur junction and then half a mile more to the top.[^4] The spur enters Norse Peak Wilderness about 0.3 miles along, and wilderness rules apply inside it.[^4] Drivers from Enumclaw take State Highway 410 and Crystal Mountain Road, then walk 500 feet up Forest Service road 7190410 to the Halfcamp trailhead.[^4]
