Hawk Mountain sits in the Mount Hood National Forest, in Marion County, about 15 miles northwest of Mount Jefferson.[^2] Built in 1919, the site had a timber tower on three legs, about 60 feet tall, and a frame cabin that served as living quarters.[^1][^2][^3] The tower had no enclosed cab, only a platform on top for viewing.[^1]

Both the registry and firelookout.com date the site's abandonment to the 1950s.[^1][^2] A 1963 inspection report, quoted on a lookout history page, describes the tower as having no cab and as one that gets used when fire conditions are extreme.[^3] It rates the living quarters as in good condition, though it also notes peeling paint and windows that need putty.[^3] The 1950s date and the 1963 report do not sit easily together, and the sources do not settle it.

The tower came down in 1967, and the cabin stayed behind.[^1][^3][^4] After it fell into disrepair, a group of volunteers took on the cabin's restoration in 2002.[^4][^5] The site was added to the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register on January 2, 2026.[^1]

The cabin still stands.[^1] A field guide says visitors can stay in the restored cabin, first come, first serve, though some of its notes date from 2016.[^5] The same guide carries a notice dated August 2026 that the area is closed because of the Austin Fire, and it points to the Forest Service closure order.[^5] Anyone planning a visit should check that order first. Beyond the cabin, no source we read describes what remains of the tower.
