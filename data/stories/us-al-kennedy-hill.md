The Alabama Forestry Commission built the Kennedy Hill Fire Tower in 1941.[^1] The register says Barbour County's Board of Revenue asked the commission in 1940 for better fire tower coverage, and the commission responded by building a 100-foot steel tower, an Aermotor MC-39 with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] A 1819 News profile calls the MC-39 the most widespread steel lookout model across Alabama's forests, with the LS-40 next.[^2] By 1944, the register notes, the county had four fire towers in service, completing the planned system.[^1]

The register's visit report of May 1, 2019, by Andrew Zerbe, marks the tower as not staffed.[^1] Its comment calls it a "Usual vandalized tower."[^1] A power line corridor runs past the tower.[^1] We found no record of who staffed the tower or of any fire it watched.

The register says the state sold the tower at a surplus auction, roughly in early 2020, and that it now has a private owner.[^1] We could not confirm the sale from any other source we read.

The register says the tower cannot be reached by Kennedy Tower Road, where a gate stands in front of the last house on the road.[^1] Please do not climb it without the owner's permission.
