Goat Haunt is an isolated site in Glacier National Park, which the NPS presents as a place for marking its ties with Canada.[^1]

The National Park Service panoramic lookout project table lists a station called Goat Haunt Mt. On August 12, 1935, L. Moe took panoramic photographs from that station on infrared and panchromatic film.[^2] The Montana lookout index files the entry as "GOAT HAUNT ELO" and notes that panorama photos were taken on August 12, 1935.[^3]

Today, the Goat Haunt Overlook trail leads to the site of a former fire lookout.[^4] The NPS describes the walk as brief and steep, and says it offers a view over the Waterton Valley.[^4] The sources do not say whether that former lookout is the same station as the 1935 photo site.[^2][^4]

Goat Haunt's present infrastructure dates from the 1960s, when it was built under the Mission 66 program.[^1]
