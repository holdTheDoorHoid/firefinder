The Laurier fire tower was one of the lookouts of the Parry Sound Forest Fire District.[^1] Ontario's former Department of Lands and Forests founded that district in 1922 to protect the forests by detecting fires early from fire towers.[^1] Its headquarters was in the town of Parry Sound.[^1] The district's list of lookouts includes Laurier, alongside Boulter, Lount, Strong and others.[^1] The Parry Sound fire tower, which was built on the same site as the modern lookout at 17 George Street, is also on the district's list of 21 lookouts.[^1]

Firelookout.ca places Laurier on Chemical Road in the unorganized North East Parry Sound area, at 421 m (1,381 ft) elevation.[^2] A 1963 Tower Detection System map from the Department of Lands and Forests marks a 24 m (80 ft) steel tower here.[^2]

In 1969 only four towers in the district were still actively staffed: Ardbeg, Go Home, Stormy Lake and Boulter. Laurier was not one of them.[^1] The article says all four were phased out shortly after aerial firefighting came into use in the 1970s.[^1]

The Huntsville topographic map (sheet 31E) does not show the tower, the register notes.[^2] The register does not describe what remains at the Laurier site or whether it is open to the public.[^2]
