The Angeles National Forest built a fire lookout on Sierra Pelona Ridge in Los Angeles County.[^1] The register of former lookout sites says it was constructed between 1934 and 1936 and gives 1934 as its build year.[^1] The cab was a California Region 5 BC-301 design on a steel K-brace tower.[^1] A guide to the site says the lookout first appeared on a 1932 topo map and joined the Newhall District that year, so the start date is not settled.[^3]

The height is also disputed. The register gives 30 feet, and the guide's details block says 30 feet, though its text says 30 to 40 feet. A 1964 newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts site describes a 40-foot tower.[^1][^3][^2]

The guide names several people who staffed the lookout, including Ross Lewis in 1938, Ethel R. Babcock in 1953 and Cal Bedient in 1976.[^3] The register says it was still active in at least 1976.[^1]

Wind was a constant concern. The register says winds gusting to 100 mph were recorded there often.[^1] In 1956 a windstorm forced the operator to leave, and a newspaper item said the tower was reportedly coming apart in the gale.[^1][^2] In August 1964 a lookout spotted a brush fire in Bee Canyon, and about 175 firefighters worked it for nearly two hours.[^2]

The register does not say when the tower came down, and lists its removal as unknown.[^1] The guide notes that newspaper articles still place a lookout there in the 1980s, but it is unclear why and when the tower was removed.[^3]

According to the guide, only a handful of concrete slabs are left at the site, and there is no tower to climb.[^3] One of many off-road routes leads directly to the spot.[^3] Anyone going should check current rules with the Angeles National Forest first.
