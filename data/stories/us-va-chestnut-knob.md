Chestnut Knob, the highest point in Henry County, had a steel fire tower by 1939 [^2]. A Civilian Conservation Corps company built a steel tower there that stood 52 feet tall, with an observation room eight feet square on top [^2]. Crews ran nearly three miles of telephone line to connect it to the main line [^2]. A November 1939 report said the tower was staffed during fire season [^2].

A 1961 Martinsville Bulletin article described the tower as 53 feet high, with a glass-enclosed watch room and a view of Martinsville to the north [^2].

The former lookout register gives a different build story [^1]. It dates a 100-foot steel tower to 1964 [^1], which does not match the 53-foot tower the 1961 article describes [^2]. None of the sources says whether the taller tower replaced the earlier one.

The register says the tower has since been replaced by communication towers [^1]. It lists the administrator as a private owner [^1]. We found no source that says whether visitors may reach the summit, so ask the owner before visiting.

[^1]: Chestnut Knob Tower Site, Former Fire Lookout Sites, http://firetower.org/lookouts/us/va/chestnut-knob-tower-site/ (accessed 2026-10-10).
[^2]: Chestnut Knob, Forest Lookouts, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/chestnut-knob.html (accessed 2026-10-10).
