The Bowen Hill tower stood in Coventry, in Kent County, at about 600 feet (183 meters).[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes it as a 65-foot steel tower first operated in 1926.[^1]

An earlier date appears in a 1923 report from the state's Commissioner of Forestry. It says three new forest fire observation stations had been set up that year, bringing the total to five, and it lists Bowen Hill among the five.[^2] The report says the towers at those five stations ranged from 40 to 68 feet tall, were built of custom steel, and had plate-glass windows and an alidade for sighting fires.[^2] The register gives 1926 as the first year of operation, three years after the report, and no source we read explains the gap.[^1][^2]

Later reports mention work at the site.[^2] In 1950 the state reported a new anemometer mast and fuel-moisture scale shelter, to be used for an open-type fire-danger station at the Bowen Hill tower.[^2] A 1951 report said all of the state's lookout towers had been wired for electric power, in anticipation of swapping their battery radios for AC units.[^2]

The register says the tower was taken out of service in 1973, then moved to the Adult Correction Institution in Cranston, where it now serves as a guard tower.[^1]

The register lists the Coventry site as a former lookout, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association marks Bowen Hill as gone.[^1][^3] Its list of standing Rhode Island towers does not include Bowen Hill.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Coventry site or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2] The guard tower stands at the Adult Correction Institution, and no source suggests the public can see it there.[^1]
