In February 1938 the Pulaski Southwest Times reported that a forestry lookout tower for the east point of Peak Knob had arrived and was being taken to the mountain.[^1] The tower was 35 feet tall, with a glass enclosure about seven feet square at the top, and it had come from Chicago.[^1] It was the only one planned for Pulaski County, and it was hoped the tower would be in operation by March 15.[^1]

A March 1939 notice said an observer was to be stationed in the tower from 8 a.m. until sundown on dry-season days.[^1] By March 1946 a telephone line had been finished to the tower, and a forest warden named David Warden was on duty there from March 1 until mid-May.[^1] In March 1964 the tower was announced as manned for the first time that season, with Bobby Long to be on duty until the last part of May, depending on the weather.[^1]

The tower also drew trouble. In November 1944 three juveniles were arraigned for breaking into and damaging the tower.[^1] In April 1949 the lookout, David T. Warden, was cut off from telephone service for a time and estimated that 500 to 600 acres had burned.[^1]

In October 1953 the county forest warden reported that six windows had been shot out and a landing platform torn away, and that a lock on a cistern had been broken off.[^1] The property was then posted. Warden King said those wanting to see the tower must first get permission. Groups must have an adult leader.[^1]

The sources we read stop describing the tower's use in 1964. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, but we found no later staffing record, no recent description and no photo.[^1][^2]

The 1953 posting is the only access rule we found, and it is more than seventy years old. We found no current access information, so please ask the Virginia Department of Forestry before going near the tower.
