Glacier Ridge is a former lookout on the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest in Snohomish County, about 3 miles northwest of Glacier Peak.[^1][^2]

Panorama photos dated August 12, 1935 were taken from 26 feet up a structure that seems to be an unfinished tower built of timber.[^3][^4]

The finished lookout, a 40-foot tower with an L-4 cab, was built in 1936.[^1][^2][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register describes an L-4 as a 14-by-14-foot frame cab that could be mounted on a tall tower.[^5]

In 1942, Art Ryals was sent to the lookout to relieve Jim Tucker, according to an account on Willhite's site.[^4] The same account places a switchback at Mile 13 on the trail up to the lookout.[^4]

The lookout was abandoned in 1957.[^1][^2][^4] The register says it is now gone, and the Washington lookouts index marks it removed.[^1][^3]

Willhite's page lists the site as not yet visited, and it does not describe remains or access today.[^4]
