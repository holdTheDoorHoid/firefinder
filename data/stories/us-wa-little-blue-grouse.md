Little Blue Grouse Mountain rises to 4,230 feet (1,289 m) in Pend Oreille County, in the Colville National Forest, about 15 miles west of Newport.[^1][^2] The register says a pole platform tower and a cabin went up on the site in 1923, with a patrol point on Little Roundtop.[^1][^3]

Newspaper items hint at an earlier start.[^4] In July 1922, a Spokesman-Review item, as quoted on one lookout-history site, reported that Fire Warden H. L. Baker had set up a new lookout station on the mountain, west of Sacheen Lake.[^4] A September 1922 item named George Jones as the lookout here.[^4] WillhiteWeb reads a 1926 Spokane Daily Chronicle item, which put Richard Smith on "Blue Grouse Mountain" near Sacheen Lake, as referring to this peak.[^3]

The names get tangled. WillhiteWeb says the nearby and much higher Boyer Mountain was once called Blue Grouse Mountain locally, and a 1930 notice placed a lookout on Boyer under that name.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in the 1930s, according to the register and firelookout.com.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb guesses that a structure going up on the taller Boyer Mountain was the reason.[^3]

Today, reaching the peak takes planning.[^3] A gate on Buck Creek Road is open from May 14 to November 1, and visitors need a permit. WillhiteWeb says the land belongs to Inland Empire Paper Company.[^3] From the gate it is about 3.5 miles with roughly 1,800 feet of climbing, and no easy bushwhack route has been found.[^3] The author searched the spots most likely to have held the lookout and turned up no relics, but the summit is big enough that small traces could slip past.[^3] When the weather is good, there is a partial view.[^3] The sources describe no standing remains here.
