Bolan Mountain has carried a fire lookout since 1917, when the Forest Service built a cupola cabin on a log crib base, in what was then the Siskiyou National Forest.[^1] That first lookout stood for 36 years before being replaced in 1953 by an L-4-style cab, fourteen feet square under a hip roof, set on a fifteen-foot wood tower.[^1] At some later point the legs were taken down and the cab was lowered onto a low stone base, and the catwalk was widened with a deck added to one side.[^1]

The lookout earned a place on the National Historic Lookout Register, registered March 31, 2003, with the Illinois Valley Ranger District as cooperator, and is kept on "emergency status."[^1]

Then, in September 2020, the Slater Fire burned through the area and destroyed the structure.[^2][^3] A longtime lookout-tracking site, firelookout.com, still marks it "R.I.P. 1953-2020," reflecting its status in the years before it was rebuilt.[^2]

In 2024 the Siskiyou Mountain Club partnered with the Forest Service to rebuild the lookout after the fire.[^3] It again sits atop the mountain, wrapped in glass on every side, with views reaching the Red Buttes and Siskiyou Wilderness, Preston Peak and Bolan Lake, and on clear days, Mount Shasta to the southeast.[^3]

Today the one-room, fourteen-by-fourteen-foot cabin is rented through Recreation.gov during the snow-free season, for stays of up to three consecutive nights and up to four guests.[^3] Thirty to forty steep, uneven steps lead up to the cabin, which has a table, two chairs, and a single bed that folds out into two twins, but no running water, so guests pack their own in; a vault toilet stands nearby.[^3] Bolan Lake, a short hike below, is stocked with trout and open for swimming and non-motorized boating.[^3]

The result is a lookout that is as much a product of volunteer restoration as of the Forest Service crews who first built it: burned once, rebuilt twice, and once again open to anyone willing to carry in water for a night above the Siskiyous.
