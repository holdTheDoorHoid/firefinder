The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Big House Mountain only as an unconfirmed site, with its status given as unknown.[^1]

Big House Mountain is the western of two peaks that make up House Mountain, five miles west of Lexington in Rockbridge County, Virginia.[^2] Its eastern neighbor, Little House Mountain, stands a little lower.[^2] The summit of Big House is 3,645 feet (1,111 m) high.[^2]

The saddle between the two peaks was once a pasture and apple orchard. Several homesteads also stood on the upper mountain and in the saddle.[^2]

In the late 1980s, Rockbridge Conservation raised $150,000 toward buying the top of the mountain. The Virginia Outdoors Foundation received that money and, with another $175,000, bought the land and placed it under permanent protection as the House Mountain Reserve, which covers 968 acres.[^2]

A trail to the summit of Big House starts a short distance from the camping shelter in the saddle. The two-mile trail to the saddle begins where the state road ends.[^2]

No source I read describes a tower, footings or other remains on the summit today.
