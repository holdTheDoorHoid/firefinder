McFadden Peak rises in the Sierra Ancha, in the Pleasant Valley Ranger District of the Tonto National Forest, a summit named for William McFadden.[^1][^3] The first lookout here went up in 1929: a 20-foot steel tower topped by a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] Through the 1930s the site grew into a small outpost, with a cabin, a stock barn, and a shed added.[^1]

Those early buildings came down in 1964, when the Forest Service replaced them with the structure that stands today: a 10-foot concrete blockhouse supporting a 14-by-14-foot CL-100 series flat-top metal cab with a catwalk around it.[^1][^2]

The peak also carries an older trace of government surveying: a benchmark placed by the Forest Service in 1926, before the first tower even existed, and a second benchmark set in 1938.[^2] McFadden Peak Lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 1996, listed as US 194 and Arizona's fourth entry.[^1]

The lookout has continued to be staffed each fire season, watching for smoke across the Sierra Ancha.[^1][^3] It is reached by the unpaved McFadden Peak Road, Forest Road 561.[^3] The tower remains an active U.S. Forest Service facility on federal land rather than a public rental, and visitors should treat it as a working lookout, not a place to climb uninvited.
