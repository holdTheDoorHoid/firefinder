The Kellogg Mountain tower stood on a wooded ridge in Bradford County, Pennsylvania, about two miles south-southwest of Monroeton.[^1][^2]

The earliest item we found, from October 1933, reports that surveyors were doing preliminary work on the mountain for a fire tower.[^2] A November 1933 item said the steel frame had already arrived and that a stone cabin for a caretaker would go up nearby.[^2] The register dates the tower to 1934 and calls it a steel I-S design.[^1] By December 1934 a newspaper reported it was finished, built by Civilian Conservation Corps workers from the LaQuin camp.[^2]

Most newspapers of the period put the tower at 80 feet, though a December 1934 item said it rose 82 feet above the mountain.[^2] A 1969 federal survey description of the station gives 85 feet, and the sources do not explain the difference.[^2]

A towerman was named in spring 1935, when Harry W. Wilcox of Monroeton was appointed to the new tower.[^2] A 1939 press item said a year-round observer was stationed there.[^2] By 1940 Edward Hoffman of Towanda was its keeper.[^2] In 1941 the state Department of Forests and Waters installed a radio system for the Susquehanna River basin, meant to serve flood warning as well as forest fire protection, and a 100-watt relay station went up on the Kellogg tower.[^2] In 1946, when the county had only this one fire tower, James D. Rundell was in charge. In 1952 Robert Brennan of Monroeton was on duty there daily, from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed in 1987.[^1]

A hiking guide to State Game Lands 36 describes the site. It is dated November 2023 and was updated in January 2024, so it may not show conditions in 2026.[^3] The guide says the old concrete pillars where the tower stood are still there, and that it saw no old survey marker.[^3] Beside the parking lot is an abandoned building the guide calls a fire tower house.[^3] The 1933 item's stone caretaker's cabin may be the same building, though no source says so. The guide says the road up the mountain is closed outside hunting season, and that parking is available only while the entrance gates are open.[^3]
