The Deadman Mountain lookout, which West Lookouts also lists as Greyfall, stands on the summit of its mountain in Lincoln County, Wyoming, at 10,361 feet.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register says its ground cabin is identical to the one at Cabin Creek Peak, and lists it as another of the unusual four-window-panel designs.[^1]

The register and a firelookout.com page both give 1941 as the build year.[^1][^3] A July 1941 newspaper item, reprinted on the West Lookouts site, reports that a crew led by Mike Buol was putting up a fire lookout on Dead Man Mountain, which the item places on Gray's river.[^2] An August 1940 item on the same page reports work on an emergency telephone line from Alpine to what it calls the Dead Man mountain point, fire lookout.[^2] Read together, the two items suggest a lookout site was in use or planned before the cabin went up, but the sources do not say what stood there earlier.[^2]

I found no source on who staffed the lookout or whether it had notable fires.

The register, which listed the lookout on May 3, 2022, calls it severely deteriorated, though it still stands.[^1] A Komoot hiking listing says it has since been abandoned, though it adds that visitors can enjoy views from inside on a clear day.[^4]

Sources disagree on the forest. The register and the West Lookouts page name Bridger National Forest, while firelookout.com names Bridger-Teton National Forest.[^1][^2][^3] I found no current access notice or rental listing. The register's description of severe deterioration is a reason for caution about going inside.[^1]
