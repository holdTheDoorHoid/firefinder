The Jesmond Lookout stood on Jesmond Mountain in British Columbia's Cariboo region, in the area the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register calls Thompson-Nicola.[^1][^2] The register dates the tower to 1971 and says it was originally built by the British Columbia Wildfire Service.[^1] A 2026 newspaper report describes it as built in the early 1970s.[^3] The register gives its elevation as 1,959 m (6,426 ft), and the newspaper gives 1,980 m.[^1][^3]

The tower sat on a very exposed ridge.[^3] The newspaper says winds of up to 120 km/hr were recorded at the site, so cables were needed to hold the tower down.[^3] A visitor's account says the building is tied to the bedrock with metal cables.[^2]

From 1972 to 1999 observers were stationed there during fire season, for three weeks at a time from early May to late September.[^2] Each observer lived in the tower and had the job of spotting and reporting the first signs of a fire.[^2] A visitor's account says that historically about 12 fires a year were detected from the lookout.[^2]

Recreation Sites and Trails BC took the site over from the BC Wildfire Service.[^3] About ten years ago, the Clinton and District Outdoor Sportsman Association (CADOSA) entered a partnership agreement with Recreation Sites and Trails BC to look after the lookout.[^3]

The register dates the loss to the Fiftynine Creek Fire on 17 July 2026.[^1] The Free Press says the tower was destroyed in the Pear Lake wildfire, and the Four Wheel Drive Association of BC ties its loss to the Big Bar Complex wildfires.[^3][^4] Both reports say the loss was confirmed on 27 July.[^3][^4] According to the Free Press, the base is all that remains.[^3]

A statement from Recreation Sites and Trails BC, quoted in an August 2026 post, says the site stays shut for now, because a wildfire is still active, an evacuation order is in place and there are safety concerns.[^4] The district recreation officer, Curtis Ofstie, has no timeline for taking a closer look at the site, but says options will be explored, including a rebuild if funding is available.[^4] CADOSA is building a list of people who want to help with a rebuild.[^3] A rough 4WD road leads to the site.[^2] While it stays closed, the site is not open to visitors.[^4]
