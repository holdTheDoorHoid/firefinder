Firelookout.ca says the Dominion Forestry Service built a fire lookout on Joss Mountain in 1921.[^1] Shuswap Passion says the Dominion Government built it, along with two others, in the early 1920s to shield Railway Belt timber.[^2] Firelookout.ca adds that its square plan and stone foundation matched several nearby Dominion lookouts from the 1920s.[^1]

A service cabin was built in the pass below, and a telephone line linked the lookout to the valley.[^2] A packhorse trail was built in 1921 to serve the lookout.[^2]

The federal government had run the forest lands of the Railway Belt until 1930, when the province took them over.[^1] Sources differ on when the lookout was abandoned. Firelookout.ca says after 1931, while Shuswap Passion gives 1930.[^1][^2] In 2005 Columbia Helicopters restored the lookout, fitting it with a fresh roof and windows alongside the BC Wildfire Management Branch.[^2]

Today Shuswap Trails calls the summit building the rebuilt Dominion Forestry Lookout, and Shuswap Passion calls it in excellent condition.[^3][^2]

The trail is 7.5 km one-way, and Shuswap Trails puts the access point about 47 km east of Sicamous.[^3] The drive uses steep forestry roads, where 4WD is recommended.[^3] Shuswap Trails calls the hike a serious alpine backcountry trip in grizzly country. It says recreational access is monitored and seasonal closures apply.[^3] Firelookout.ca gives a different figure, about 9 km from Joss Pass to the lookout, so the two lengths are not directly comparable.[^1]
