Lee Butte sits on the Mormon Lake Ranger District of the Coconino National Forest, and the tower was built in 1933.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 46-foot International Derrick tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and says only three towers of that design are in Arizona.[^1] A 1989 Forest Service report on the region's lookouts gives the tower as 45 feet 9 inches high, with a 7-foot-square steel cab.[^2] The two heights differ by three inches, and this history cannot say which is right.[^1][^2] An August 1932 letter, quoted on a West Lookouts page, had proposed buying a 40-foot tower for Lee Butte, which matches neither figure.[^5]

The report files Lee Butte under the International Derrick type and says the company supplied steel towers and 7-foot-square steel cabs for lookouts.[^2] The register describes the tower as in good condition but inactive, and says it is listed on the National Register, along with its spacious ground house.[^1] The National Park Service record for the Lee Butte Lookout Tower and Cabin gives a publication date of January 28, 1988.[^3]

The register entry is dated April 1, 2001, and Dave Lorenz, Director of the AZ/NM FFLA, nominated the site.[^1] Our sources include no current staffing report, rental listing or public visiting guidance for Lee Butte. The Forest Fire Lookout Association still lists the tower as standing.[^4] Because the register describes it as inactive, and no source I found says the public may climb it, the sensible way to see it is from the ground unless the Forest Service says otherwise.[^1]
