The Chancellor lookout stood on state-owned land near Shady Grove Church in Spotsylvania County, Virginia.[^3] The Virginia Forest Service and the Spotsylvania Forest Fire Protective Association cooperated to put the tower up in February 1928.[^3][^1] It was an 80-foot Aermotor tower.[^1][^2] The register says it was the county's second tower to carry the Chancellor name.[^1] An item from July 1928, quoted on Forest Lookouts, went further and called it the first steel tower of that height in the state.[^3]

The register says the public could visit the tower during the 1920s and 1930s.[^1] A November 1929 notice quoted on Forest Lookouts said visitors were welcome on each remaining Sunday that month, unless it rained.[^3]

The register says staffing continued until the late 1960s.[^1] By April 1957 a radio had been installed at the tower, tying it to the Forest Service channel at Charlottesville, according to a Free Lance-Star item quoted on Forest Lookouts.[^3]

Both registers date its removal to around 2019.[^1][^2] The land is now privately held. The register says it was formerly administered by the Virginia Department of Forestry.[^1]

The register describes the land as wooded and says it was first planned for small neighborhoods, but the funding never arrived.[^1] It says the words "Prevent Forest Fires" were inscribed in the concrete at the base of the stairs, and that the tower seems to be the only one so marked.[^1] Its photos include an aerial image of the site, taken by drone in August 2019.[^1] No source we read says whether that concrete or any other part of the tower remains on the site, or whether the public may go there. The owner is private, so anyone interested should ask before going onto the land.[^1]
