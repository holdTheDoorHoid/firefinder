The Medford Station fire tower stands at Piper's Corner in Burlington County, New Jersey.[^1][^5] Plans were set in 1940, but construction waited until after World War II, and the tower went up in 1948.[^1] A May 1948 newspaper item, reprinted on a lookout history site, said the Forest Fire Service was putting up a 100-foot steel tower by Piper's Corner, in the Medford Lakes area.[^2] Corlen Engineering Co. of Collingswood was building it for about $4,500.[^2]

The new tower was meant to supplement a 60-foot tower at Retreat, in Southampton Township.[^2] It was also meant to shield owners of property around Medford Lakes from pine-country fires.[^2]

The register describes a 100-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, gives its elevation as 141 feet, and records it as in active service.[^1] It was registered on March 15, 2004.[^1] Wikipedia's list gives the same height of 100 feet (30 m) and elevation of 141 feet.[^4]

A WHYY report updated June 9, 2026 says the tower has been in use since 1948 and is one of 21 towers statewide.[^3] The article describes an observer who climbs 150 metal stairs to a 7-by-7-foot office at the top each morning and spends eight hours a day watching for smoke.[^3] WHYY places the tower above Wharton State Forest, while the register and the Division B page name Piper's Corner. The sources do not conflict outright, but the wording differs.[^3][^1][^5]

The Division B page lists the tower at Piper's Corner, with an elevation of 141 feet.[^5] No source read for this story describes public visits. The tower is a staffed state post, so it should be treated as one and not as a place to climb.[^3][^5]
