In February 1931 district ranger H.I. Snider recommended a fire lookout on Mount McClellan, a few miles south of Virginia City, Nevada.[^1] If it were set up, the station would be maintained with Forest Service funds.[^1]

The Forest Service built the station in 1932.[^1][^2] It paid $800 toward the cost, and about $400 more came from the Clarke-McNary act.[^1][^2] Washoe County built a road to the site that summer.[^1] A 1932 newspaper item said the station would be manned the following year.[^1] A 1951 Coast and Geodetic Survey description called it a one-story building about 14 feet square and 12 feet high.[^1]

George Merkle was the lookout in August 1933.[^1] He spotted a fire on the ridge above Jack's Valley after a thunderstorm, and a crew of 16 men from a CCC camp put it out.[^1] Iola Ferguson worked the lookout from 1944, spending every season on it until 1951 except one.[^1] A Reno paper that year said she was the only woman lookout in the state.[^1]

The Bureau of Land Management took over the lookout in 1963.[^2] In July 1970 a Nevada newspaper reported that smog had forced it to close.[^1] The 2012 California-Nevada chapter report says it was last staffed in 1970 and destroyed in 1971.[^2]

The firelookout.org Nevada list marks the site as gone.[^3] A climbing guide to the plateau says its top is full of cell towers and does not mention a lookout.[^4] We found no source describing what, if anything, remains at the site or whether the public can reach it, so check before visiting.
