Modoc Point is a former lookout site in Klamath County, Oregon, about nine miles south of Chiloquin, on the Winema National Forest.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree about when the tower went up. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the tower and cabin to 1933.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page quotes Forest Service and Indian Service reports that point to a later build.[^4] A 1935 project note said the improvements would be on tribal lands, and that year an 80-foot steel tower was bought from the Aermotor Company of Chicago.[^4] A survey then found the site needed 120 feet, so a 40-foot extension was needed.[^3][^4] A 12-by-16 living quarters cabin was completed in November 1936, and by 1937 the tower was complete except for its guy wires and lightning-protection wires.[^3][^4]

In 1940, two blazes started by careless smokers broke out on the Klamath Reservation on Sunday, July 28.[^3][^4] One, about a mile northwest of the lookout, was controlled by grazing permit holders and Indian Service officers before it got under way.[^3][^4]

A 1959 bid notice from the Klamath Tribes described the 120-foot steel tower and its cabin.[^3] In 1968 the lookout buildings were sold to a buyer in Lakeview for $35, and the contract required removal by December 30, 1969.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both show the tower and cabin had disappeared by 1969.[^1][^2]

A 2022 visit recorded on WillhiteWeb found footings at the lookout site, an outhouse, and a phone insulator along the last stretch of road to the top.[^3] The page says the access road offers views of Upper Klamath Lake, and it lists access as drive-up or close.[^3] A 1961 Metsker map note on that page says the lookout site was not at the Modoc Point marked on maps.[^3] None of the sources we read says who controls the land today.
