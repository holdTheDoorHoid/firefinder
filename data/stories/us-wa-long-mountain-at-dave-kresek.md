The Kresek Fire Tower began its working life on Spades Mountain in Kootenai County, Idaho, where a steel tower was erected in 1941.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register describes the tower as it stands today as a 50-foot Aermotor with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, while the Forest Fire Lookout Association's record of Spades Mountain gives the 1941 steel tower a height of 54 feet.[^1][^2]

That steel tower was later moved to Long Mountain, a station of the Idaho Department of Lands near Sandpoint.[^1][^2][^3] The move is dated 1963 by the Forest Fire Lookout Association and by Idaho Fire Lookouts, and 1964 by the Register and by the Forest Lookouts page.[^1][^2][^3][^4] A 1959 newspaper item quoted by the Forest Lookouts page reports that a new forestry station on Long Mountain, south of Sandpoint, was nearly built.[^3]

The most recent staffing date in these sources is 1967, when Joyce Sprinkle of Tucson began work as the fire lookout on Long Mountain.[^3][^4]

In 1998 the Idaho Department of Lands offered the tower for sale, and the buyer had to remove it from the site by September 30, 1998.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts says the tower was sold that year to Dave Kresek and moved to his private property.[^4] The Register says Dave Kresek, Ray Kresek's son, later relocated it to a 30-acre site, and records its listing on November 20, 1999.[^1]

Today the tower stands east of Spokane on private land.[^1][^4] According to the Register, visits can be booked in advance through the Fire Lookout Museum.[^1] No source describes public access, so arrange any visit through the museum rather than going to the site on your own.

The sources disagree on the tower's height and cab. The 1964 Long Mountain listing gives 46 feet for an Aermotor MC-40 steel tower, a 1998 sale notice describes 54 feet with a 12-by-12 wood cabin, and the Register gives 50 feet with a 7-by-7 metal cab.[^3][^1]
