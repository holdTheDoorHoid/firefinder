The Rockies is a summit in Lewis County, Washington, at 4,322 feet.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page also calls it Rockies (Little) and places it in the Snoqualmie National Forest. The register places it in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1][^3]

A lookout was working here by 1922. A Chehalis newspaper item from that July, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, names Wallace Mead as the lookout at the Rockies.[^3] A guide says the site had a telephone line.[^4] In 1923 the Rainier National Forest said it had recommended $1,000 for a lookout house. The sources do not say whether that money built the cabin later recorded here.[^3]

The site's D-6 cupola cabin dates from 1929.[^1][^2][^4]

In October 1931 a Forest Service employee named Senter died after a shooting accident near Morton.[^3][^4] The sources disagree on his first name. An Oregonian item calls him Herman Senter, a forest ranger, while a Centralia Daily Chronicle report gives Thurman Senter, age 29.[^3] The guide calls him the lookout, but the Centralia report places him at work on cabins roughly four miles from the station, with two colleagues.[^3][^4]

In February 1941 a searching plane spotted the wreckage of a McChord Field twin-engine bomber, reported missing, in timber near the lookout.[^3] The wreckage was spread across roughly two acres of woods.[^3][^4] The register says the crash nearly demolished the cabin.[^1] From 1942 to 1943 the site served the Aircraft Warning Service.[^3][^4]

The register records the cabin's removal in 1956. The firelookout.com page and the guide say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^4] None of the pages we read says how it ended.

Today, the guide says the mountain sits in a tangle of ownership and logging roads.[^4] Its route starts on FR 74, a gravel road the guide says is rough in places, and the guide describes its easiest route for a vehicle with 4WD and high clearance.[^4] The guide says much of the old summit trail survives, and that plenty of debris is still lying about.[^4] We found no source that describes footings or a foundation, and none says whether the site is open to visitors. Check with the land managers before you go.
