The Jackie Jones Lookout is a steel fire tower in Harriman State Park, in Rockland County, New York.[^1] The Palisades Interstate Park Commission put it up in 1928 and ran it until 1931. After that, Forest Fire Control operated it until the tower closed in the 1970s.[^1] The register also says it is the final steel fire tower in the Bear Mountain-Harriman area, and that its cab has windows of an unusual design.[^1]

A 1932 newspaper item, quoted on a fire-lookout history site, describes the observer at the tower working nine hours a day from April until November, and able to spot a fire anywhere in the county from the tower.[^2] The sources read do not name any particular fire it caught.

By January 2018 the tower was closed, and its bottom set of stairs had been taken off.[^1] The register's visit notes say talks with the park commission about repairs had been going on since the summer of 2016.[^1] In April and June 2018, 50 members of Team Rubicon replaced the stairs and platforms, the cab floor and windows, and repainted the tower.[^3] A trail guide credits the work to volunteers from the forest fire association's New York chapter and Team Rubicon.[^4] A visitor in August 2018 found the tower completely renovated, but was not sure who had done the work.[^1]

The trouble came back. In April 2021 the chapter's newsletter reported many cab windows missing and a great deal of graffiti.[^5] By November 2021, Hike the Hudson Valley reported that a fence now surrounded the tower, that it was shut, and that the closure might be for good.[^4] The chapter's 2023 annual report lists it among the towers closed while engineering inspections are pending.[^6]

The most recent source found, the 2023 report, lists the tower as closed.[^6] I found no source giving a reopening date, so treat the tower as closed. Please do not try to climb a fenced tower, and check with Harriman State Park before you plan a trip to see it.
