Round Mountain's lookout tower did not start out there.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says a 35-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab was built on Saddle Mountain, on the Coconino National Forest, in 1934.[^1] In 1960 it was moved to Round Mountain on the Kaibab National Forest, where a side door took the place of the usual trap door.[^1] A Coconino National Forest photo caption says the base structure was allegedly moved sometime from 1955 to 1960, and it calls the Saddle Mountain style the MC-39 rather than the MC30 the register names.[^3] This story follows the register's dates, but the two sources do not agree.[^1][^3]

The register listed the tower on March 15, 2006, as US 664, AZ 59.[^1] A Kaibab National Forest photo album, with pictures from 2018, says this site is not staffed and is inactive.[^2] The same album says the forest keeps about eight active lookouts during fire season.[^2]

The Williams Ranger District planned to demolish two lookouts, Red Hill and Round Mountain, because the buildings were unsafe for use.[^4] Scoping began on December 17, 2019, and a decision was signed on March 5, 2020.[^4] The Forest Service lists the project as completed. Its page gives May 2020 only as an estimated implementation date, and does not record when the work was done.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list now marks the Round Mountain tower as gone.[^5]

No source we read describes what, if anything, is left on the summit today, or whether the road to it is open. An undated travel account says the map shows a short spur road from FR 138 to the lookout. The writer's truck, loaded with kayaks, could not manage that road, so the party walked in about half a mile, and the writer was able to climb up to the observing hut.[^6] Because that account is undated, it does not show the site as it is now.
