Long Pine Key is a remnant of second-growth pine inside Everglades National Park.[^2] The site lies in Miami-Dade County.[^1]

In July 1950 the park's superintendent, Dan Beard, reported that the site had been cleared for immediate erection of an 85-foot fire tower.[^1] The tower was to cover Long Pine Key, Paradise Key and the park's east section, and was meant to head off serious fires like the spring blaze that had badly damaged that section.[^1] A 60-foot tower was also planned, at the end of the Humble Oil well road, if funds allowed.[^1]

The 1950 item reports that the tower has been delivered and that erection bids were still to be opened. It does not confirm a finished tower.[^1]

The lookout page records no history after 1950.[^1] No source I found describes what remains at the site today.

[^2]: Fire History and Fire Records for Everglades National Park (report T-619), National Park Service, https://npshistory.com/publications/ever/sfnrc/t-619.pdf (accessed 2026-10-21).
