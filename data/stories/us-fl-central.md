The Central Fire Tower stands in the Ocala National Forest in Marion County, Florida.[^1] The forest is the oldest national forest east of the Mississippi River, dating to 1908.[^2] A tourism flyer says the tower was built of wood in 1930 by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^2] That wooden tower began to deteriorate and was replaced with a steel structure.[^2] The flyer dates the replacement to 1946, while another lookout site gives 1949 and cites no source for that date.[^2][^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists the current tower as an Aermotor MC-100 with an R-6 cab.[^1] The flyer describes an eighty-foot steel structure on the south side of State Route 40.[^2]

When the tower was staffed, a lookout watched for smoke and took bearings with an alidade, a simple sighting device. Where two towers' bearings crossed, the lookout could pinpoint the fire.[^2]

The flyer says the tower stopped operating as a lookout in 1969.[^2] It was staffed again in 1985 and 1998, when heavy smoke limited aerial detection on the Ocala.[^2] The flyer does not name a single fire from those years.[^2]

Today the register notes that the tower is surrounded by a perimeter security fence.[^1] The flyer says it is still standing, calls it deteriorating, says no money is available to restore it, and warns it could be torn down.[^2] It also mentions light upkeep since the tower was retired.[^2]

A small pull-off on State Route 40 lets travelers on the Byway see the tower from the road.[^2] With the tower fenced, the sources describe only a roadside view of it.[^1][^2]
