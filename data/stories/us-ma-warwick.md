The Warwick Fire Tower stands in the Mt. Grace State Forest in Franklin County, Massachusetts.[^1] The first tower on the site was a 40-foot iron windmill-type tower with a ladder and a 6-by-6-foot cab, built in 1911.[^1] A 1918 state forester's report says the windmill tower had been kept for seven years with no building to shelter the observer.[^2]

The windmill tower gave way around 1919 and 1920, and the sources do not agree on what replaced it.[^1][^2] The register says a 68-foot iron tower went up in 1920.[^1] A Greenfield Gazette and Courier item from August 1919, quoted by a lookout history site, gives the new structure as 71 feet high.[^2] A state forester's report covering 1919 describes a 70-foot steel tower instead.[^2]

The 1938 hurricane blew the old tower down.[^1][^2] The present tower, a 68-foot steel structure with a 10-by-10 wooden cab, went up in 1939.[^1]

A 2014 visit report says the footings of the original tower are visible about 25 feet north of the current one.[^1] A 2016 visit report says the writer could not locate those footings and saw only two badly weathered concrete blocks.[^1]

In May 1934 Donald Davidson, who staffed the Mt. Grace lookout, spotted a New Hampshire fire and phoned in the alarm.[^2] A 2016 visit report says the tower is manned when fire danger runs high.[^1] A Pioneer Valley guide says the tower remains in use as a state lookout, staffed by DCR in fire season, and calls the forest a DCR property.[^3]

The register listed the tower on December 15, 2004, as US 628, MA 39, with Henry Isenberg as nominator.[^1] A 2014 visit report says the stair treads and platforms had been redone two years before, and a 2016 report describes the structure as in sound condition and kept up well.[^1]

A Pioneer Valley guide says the tower is open to the public and draws most of the people who come to the forest.[^3] A 2018 hiker, however, reported that the cab at the top of the stairs was closed to visitors.[^4] Check with the state forest before climbing, and do not go past a locked cab. The trail from the road to the summit runs about a mile and a half and climbs roughly 1,000 feet.[^4]
