The Twadell Point fire tower stands on the summit of Twadell Mountain, about a mile and a half north of the hamlet of East Branch, in Delaware County.[^1][^3]

The first tower on the site was a 45-foot modified windmill.[^2][^3] The Forest, Fish and Game Commission bought it for $307.70 and put it up in May 1910.[^1][^2][^3]

Sources disagree on when the observer's cabin went up. Wikipedia says a cabin stood on the mountain by 1916.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page instead quotes a 1917 annual report, which says seven observer cabins went up on mountain stations, one of them at Twadell Point.[^1] It also quotes a 1924 report of a new cabin there.[^1]

In 1919 the windmill gave way to a 47-foot Aermotor LS40 steel tower.[^2][^3] Wikipedia's account implies that steel tower is the one still standing.[^2]

The earliest observer named on the Forest Lookouts page is Alfred Waterman of East Branch, appointed in April 1914.[^1] In July 1924 a newspaper item quoted on the page said he had registered 182 visitors that season and had reported 12 fires so far.[^1] In April 1953, after a meeting with Air Force defense officials, the fire observers agreed to watch the sky and send reports to the Albany Filter Center.[^1] Merton Williams of East Branch was then posted at Twadell Point.[^1]

Lookout work stopped after the 1972 season, and the tower was then sold to the owner of the land.[^2] It is still standing on private land, and visitors are not admitted.[^2][^3][^4]

The sources give different elevations: 2,296 feet on the NYSFFLA list and 2,293 feet on Wikipedia. We have not picked one.[^2][^3]

Please do not try to reach or climb this tower. It is on private land and closed to visitors.
