End Butte is a peak in Idaho County, in the Nez Perce National Forest, about 24 miles southwest of Powell Ranger Station. Its elevation is listed as 7,303 feet.[^1][^2]

The site began as an observation point.[^4] In 1922, Fish Lake District Ranger Al Kolmorgan pitched a tent camp in a small meadow roughly three-quarters of a mile from the summit, and smokechaser Jess Mosher camped at End Butte that year.[^2][^4] A platform tower and a log cabin also stood two miles southeast at Rhoda Point.[^2]

In 1935 a tent-quartered lookout was set up near the top, and a telephone line was brought in from Fish Lake Saddle.[^4] The Clearwater National Forest list gives 1935 as the build year for a 10-foot L-4 entry.[^3] Louis Hartig's 1981 history, quoted on the Idaho County list, credits Ranger Hans Roeffler with packing in materials for an L-4 cabin and its 10-foot log tower in 1939.[^4] Two lookout listings date the L-4 cab and tower to 1940 instead.[^1][^2]

Seasonal staffing lasted until 1952, when aerial detection made the lookout obsolete.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Minnie Willson as staff for 1947 and 1948.[^2]

Two listings describe the lookout as abandoned in the 1950s.[^1][^2] The burn year is disputed. The Clearwater list says it burned in 1954,[^3] while Hartig says it was condemned and burned in 1955 or 1956.[^4]

The state list marks the tower as gone.[^5] Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the summit as a hike-in.[^2] A firelookout.com listing says the log cabin at Rhoda Point is still there, but no source describes what remains on the End Butte summit today.[^1][^2][^4]
