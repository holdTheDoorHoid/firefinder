Pioneer Ridge sits in the Flathead National Forest, about 18 miles southeast of Hungry Horse in Flathead County, Montana.[^1] The firelookout.com page gives the summit an elevation of 6,479 feet.[^1]

The page gives two building dates.[^1] In 1923 a two-story cab measuring 14 by 14 feet went up, and a cupola was added to it later.[^1] The tower the page describes is a 20-foot pole structure with an L-4 cab, built in 1933 near the southeast end of the summit ridge.[^1]

The page lists lookout personnel for three seasons: Mr. and Mrs. Jack Brown in 1956, Mr. and Mrs. Marvin Zaroff in 1957, and Ronald D. Frost in 1958.[^1] A 2017 oral history offers a first-hand view.[^2] Ivan O'Neil, a Forest Service lookout at Pioneer Ridge, recalls lying about his age to land a summer job on a brush crew at the Coram Ranger Station, which led to a lookout post the following summer.[^2] He kept watch for fires and lightning strikes and logged them, and he also worked as a smokechaser on several fires.[^2] He also mentions nearby stations, including Battery, Canyon and Wildcat, and says some of them went unstaffed.[^2]

The page says the tower had been abandoned since the 1970s and fell down under heavy snow in 1998.[^1] Its heading marks the lookout as R.I.P. 1933 to 1998.[^1]

No source describes what remains on the ridge today, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
