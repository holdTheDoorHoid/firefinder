The Nassawango Forestry Tower stands in Worcester County, Maryland, on land that fronts Snow Hill Road (Route 12). The state's Forest Service still uses the site for a field office, a shop complex and a communications tower.[^1][^3]

Woodland owners helped pay for it.[^2] The principal woodland owners of Worcester and Wicomico counties formed the Nassawango Forestry Association, and the two counties and the state covered the rest of the cost.[^2] A newspaper in late October 1928 said the work would begin the following week.[^2] A state forestry report says the association put the tower up in the fall of 1928, but the Register and the state's tower report both give 1929.[^1][^2][^3]

The tower was 120 feet tall.[^2] A 1929 state report said no fire-detection tower in the country was higher, as far as the report's authors knew.[^2] According to the state's tower report, it was the country's tallest forestry lookout when it went up.[^3]

The 1928 plan called for two lookouts.[^2] Samuel Long was the watchman by October 1929, and the district forester said only one forest fire had been reported from the tower since it went up.[^2] In March 1930 Long spotted smoke twelve miles away.[^2] It came from a small rubbish fire behind a store at Parsonsburg.[^2] By 1940 Margie Smullin Dennis was in charge, and a local paper noted she was the Eastern Shore's only woman in that job.[^2]

The Register dates the cab's removal and the stair replacement to 1996, and says the Maryland Forest Service still keeps the tower as a communications site and for emergency fire detection.[^1] The state's report says the steps were redone around 1996 and that a wooden deck took the cab's place, so the two sources disagree about the cab.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing and marks it with an asterisk, which its legend uses for towers that keep their structure but have lost the cab.[^4] One of the three deeds makes the tower's presence a condition of the land: if the tower is removed, the property reverts.[^3]

The state's report judges the steel structure sound.[^3] The Register lists the tower as registered on June 17, 2007.[^1] No source says whether the public can visit or climb it. The site is a working state compound, so check with the Maryland Forest Service before going.[^3]
