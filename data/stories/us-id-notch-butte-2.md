Notch Butte rises a few miles south of Shoshone, in Lincoln County, Idaho.[^3][^1] Keeping Watch places the butte at 42.88492, -114.41818.[^6] The Bureau of Land Management owns the site.[^4]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the lookout was built in 1941 as a ground cabin to a Forest Service Region 1 plan.[^1] Another page describes it as a 14-by-14-foot (4.3 by 4.3 m) cabin.[^3] Two lookout pages also list a cabin here in the 1930s, and the sources do not say whether that earlier cabin was the same building.[^2][^4]

The earliest keeper named in the sources is Julius Imgard.[^4] A Times-News item from August 1944 describes him as the Notch Butte lookout.[^4] A keeper list on firelookout.com starts with Julius and Doris Imgard in 1945 and ends with Paul Roberts, listed for 1983 to 1985.[^3]

In August 1944 a range fire raged about 10 miles south of Shoshone.[^4] The paper placed it less than a mile from the lookout station, and said grazing land was the only property it threatened.[^4] Later that month a fire along the Union Pacific tracks east of Besslen burned over more than 100 acres.[^4] Julius Imgard, the Notch Butte look-out, was the first to detect it, and the paper said sparks from a locomotive were believed to have started it.[^4]

The station also made the news for heat.[^4] A July 1954 item reported a reading of 103 at the station, the highest on record, and said the heat was not from fire.[^4]

The register says the site stayed in use into the 2000s, then became a communications site.[^1] It was added to the register on September 27, 2020.[^1] Kamstra's firelookout.com page says it was used for emergencies and later became an enclosed radio building.[^3] A climbing guide puts the conversion somewhere in the early 2000s.[^5]

A climbing guide updated in January 2023 describes the way up.[^5] An unsigned road branches off US-93, and the guide says to drive about 0.2 miles up it and park.[^5] The guide says the summit is a brief walk or bike ride from the highway, and that from the parking spot you hike up the service road.[^5] It also says the antenna-covered butte is easy to spot, and that the building on top once served as a fire lookout.[^5]

No source I found describes the building's condition today.
