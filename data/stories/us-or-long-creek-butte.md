Long Creek Butte rises in Grant County, about 19 miles north-northwest of John Day, on the Malheur National Forest.[^1][^2] A 1936 Forest Service description of a survey station on its summit places the butte about five miles southeast of the Long Creek post office.[^3]

In 1931 a local paper reported that a forest road crew would soon build a road to the butte.[^3] In July 1932 the same paper reported that construction of a lookout station was about to begin.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register describes a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1933.[^1] Forest Lookouts gives 1933 as the year the tower was completed.[^3]

The register says the poles that formed the legs were later swapped for treated timber.[^1] In 1948 a new tower treated with creosote was set under the existing house, according to a Malheur Historical Record entry quoted on Forest Lookouts. That entry calls the site Long Creek Mountain, and the sources do not say whether it is the same lookout.[^3]

The register cannot say when the tower came down.[^1] Forest Lookouts lists it as removed but gives no date.[^3]

The 1936 survey description says the marker sat in a foundation block at the base of one tower leg, and that roads ran up to the top.[^3] No source we read says whether that block, the marker or any road survives today. We found no source describing who may visit the site now, so we cannot say what a visitor would find there.
