The Thayerville Fire Tower stands in Garrett County, Maryland, on the south end of Meadow Mountain, on land that is now Deep Creek Lake State Park.[^1][^2]

A lookout tower went up on this spot during the summer of 1921, and the register counts it among the first lookouts built in Maryland.[^1][^2] A state forestry report says a new 47-foot tower was finished in time for the fall fire season.[^2] In June 1929 it was moved about a quarter mile southeast, to a site with a much wider view.[^2] By December 1934 a newspaper report said the old tower had been dismantled and replaced by an 80-foot steel tower.[^2]

The register describes today's structure differently: a 100-foot steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, built in 1938.[^1] The 1934 newspaper report and the register's 1938 date do not agree, and none of the sources read explain the difference.[^1][^2]

Rebecca Harvey, a Garrett County resident, spent more than two decades as a lookout at the tower, beginning in 1972 and staying until 1993.[^4] The register says the tower was last used for fire detection that year.[^1] A plaque honoring forest wardens and fire towers went up on October 15, 2006, and the register lists the tower as registered on April 23, 2007.[^1] The Discovery Center's page gives 2009 as the year it was listed instead, so the sources disagree on that date.[^3]

The tower has since been restored and put back into operation as a visitor destination, through a partnership with the Maryland Forest Service, the state park and its volunteers.[^1][^3] The Discovery Center calls it the only fire tower in Maryland still in working order, and says it is only occasionally open for climbing in summer.[^3] The register says it is open on a weekly basis during the summer, and advises calling Deep Creek Lake State Park for details.[^1] When it is staffed, the summer shifts fall on Thursday afternoons, handled by park staff or volunteers.[^1] The register places the tower just off Meadow Mountain Trail.[^1]

Because opening depends on the park, check with Deep Creek Lake State Park before planning a visit, and do not climb the tower when it is closed.
