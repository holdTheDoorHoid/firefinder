Alberta's wildfire lookout record gives 2004 as the year the Huckleberry lookout was constructed.[^1] The record describes a steel tower, 30 m (98 ft) tall, on a site 1,432 m (4,698 ft) above sea level.[^1] OpenStreetMap maps the structure as a lattice observation tower.[^2]

Alberta notes that its sites are remote, and that staff need to be ready for many kinds of situations, such as meeting a curious bear.[^3] Each site has a lookout observer posted to it, usually from April through October, to watch for fire.[^3]

The Alberta record marks Huckleberry as active and codes its access mode as drive.[^1]

No source we read describes the site today beyond that. None says whether the public may enter the grounds or climb the tower. Treat it as a working station: keep clear of it and do not climb it.
