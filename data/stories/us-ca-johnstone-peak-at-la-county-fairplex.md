A lookout first went up on this peak in 1917, when it was still called San Dimas Peak.[^1][^3] The San Dimas Fruit Exchange paid for it. It stood between San Dimas and Dalton Canyons to spot fires that could threaten the watersheds above San Dimas.[^3] A fire that broke out in San Gabriel Canyon in September 1919 overtook the tower a few days later and destroyed it.[^3] A newspaper report quoted on the Forest Lookouts page says that fire drove the rangers off the station.[^5] A new tower followed in 1923 on a 14-by-14-foot DuBois 4A cab.[^3] In June 1940 the peak was renamed Johnstone Peak, in memory of the conservationist W.A. Johnstone, who died in 1937.[^1][^5]

The sources disagree on when the cabin that was later moved went up. The National Historic Lookout Register says the Angeles National Forest built it in 1939 as a Plan BC-301 cabin on a 30-foot steel K-brace tower.[^1] The historical marker says the tower stood on the peak from 1934 to 1992.[^4] The Angeles Adventures history says the tower was renovated or replaced in the 1930s, without giving a single year.[^3]

The Angeles Adventures history names some of the observers: A.M. Trout in the 1930s, and Frank Mathys, Everett L. Osborn and Fred J. Haner in the 1940s.[^3] A photo caption shows Sandy DeLapp at the tower in 1953 and says it closed in 1955.[^3] The same history names DeLapp as the final observer and says smog pollution led to the tower being decommissioned.[^3] In August 1924 the San Dimas observer spotted the fire that became the 1924 San Gabriel Fire.[^3]

After sitting empty on the peak, the tower was moved to the Los Angeles County Fairplex in the early 1990s.[^3] The register says the cabin was taken apart and rebuilt at the Fairplex in 1992.[^1] The tower on display today is shorter, has a smaller cab, and is painted green.[^3]

Today the lookout is an interpretive display at the Fairplex in Pomona.[^1][^2] Volunteers from the Angeles National Forest Fire Lookout Association and the Southern California Mountains Foundation operate it each year during the Los Angeles County Fair, and the marker says it is open during that fair.[^1][^4] The register gives November 2, 2019 as its registration date.[^1] The original summit lies in the San Dimas Experimental Forest, which is technically closed to the general public except under special written permit, so do not go up there without one.[^3]
