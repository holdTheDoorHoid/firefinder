Jordan Peak rises to roughly 9,000 feet in the Sequoia National Forest in Tulare County, and the register says a lookout has stood there since at least 1923.[^1] Sources disagree on the elevation, giving 9,038 feet or 9,122 feet.[^1][^2] One lookout history site calls it possibly the oldest lookout site in the forest, and notes signs of an observation post there around 1914.[^2]

The structure that stood until 2020 went up in 1934 as a Region 1 cabin on a 20-foot steel tower.[^1][^2] Its L-4 style cab was modified over the years, and the tower was enclosed with metal siding in 1970.[^1][^2] The lookout went inactive in 1999 and 2000, returned to active service in 2001, and was registered on the national register on June 10 of that year.[^1] A lookout history page last updated in 2013 reported it staffed five days a week and open to the public.[^2]

The fire came in 2020.[^1] Jordan Peak was destroyed by fire during the SQF Complex Incident on September 14, 2020.[^1][^3] The site was added to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register on September 15, 2020.[^3]

A new tower now stands.[^4][^5] TETER Architects and Engineers describes a design-build project for new metal lookouts on Jordan Peak and Mule Peak, built to replace wooden towers lost to wildfire.[^4] The firm's post says the new towers honor "the original 1936 designs," which does not match the 1934 date in the older records.[^4][^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks Jordan Peak as "New 2026."[^5]

Access to the new tower is not confirmed in the sources reviewed. The register says the original was reachable only by trail.[^1]
