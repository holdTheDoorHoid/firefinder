The Georgia Trust says the Works Progress Administration built a 60-foot (18 m), three-story observation tower in 1935 on the grounds of A.H. Stephens State Park, in Crawfordville, Taliaferro County.[^1] The park's Wikipedia article credits both the Civilian Conservation Corps and the Works Progress Administration with the park's civil engineering in the 1930s.[^2]

The structure was used as a fire watch at one point and still contains a 3,500-gallon water tank.[^1] Long-running water intrusion and damage prompted a restoration.[^1] The work included a new roof and flashing, fixes and fresh paint for the windows, and a refresh of the interior.[^1] The project also added new wiring and an accessible ramp, intended to help tell the tower's story.[^1]

A 1957 newspaper report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, announced a new county fire tower for Taliaferro County.[^3] The dedication was set for Dec. 4 at a site four miles northeast of Crawfordsville, which is how the report spells the town.[^3] The report said the tower gives visibility over the whole county.[^3]

A Georgia tower list has an entry named "taliaferro?" with the note "loc?".[^4] Its coordinates are not the same as the park's listed point, so the list may mean the park tower, the 1957 county tower, or another structure.[^4][^2]

The restored tower is located on the grounds of A.H. Stephens State Park today.[^1] The pages read do not say whether visitors may climb it, so ask the park before a visit.
