The state began building a fire lookout on Chipman Hill, in Middlebury in Addison County, in 1932.[^1][^2] The state forestry report for that year says the hill had been chosen as the site of a new tower.[^1] Work started that August.[^1] A newspaper item from August 1932 said state workers were building it to the forestry department's plans, with Middlebury College supplying the logs and the Batell Park trustees donating part of the labor.[^1]

The plans called for a 55-foot tower with an observation lookout on top. It was to be built of logs and to harmonize with the wooded hilltop.[^1] A June 1933 notice mentioned a new tool house near the fire tower, replacing an older building that had been torn down.[^1]

Newspapers said that once the tower was finished, the state would provide a fire warden, and that the public could go up to enjoy the view.[^1]

Forest Lookouts marks the tower as removed but gives no date.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Vermont list shows the site as a tower that is gone.[^2]

Chipman Hill is now a town park, and the Town of Middlebury lists Chipman Hill Park with hours of 6:30am to 9pm.[^4] By mid-century the hill came to hold the town reservoir, along with TV and radio towers and, later, microwave towers.[^3] Driving to the top had mostly stopped by the 1970s.[^3] A 2018 town document describes a fenced tower site with an equipment building and an old cell tower. It is about cell and radio equipment and does not mention the 1932 lookout.[^5]

No source we read describes what, if anything, of the 1932 lookout is still on the summit. Keep to the public trails and stay out of the fenced area.
