Benchmark Mountain is the high point on West Cady Ridge, in Snohomish County.[^1][^5] The lookout stood in the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1]

The lookout was built over the summers of 1929 and 1930, according to WillhiteWeb.[^3] The other two pages give 1929 as the build year.[^1][^2] It was an 11-by-11-foot frame cabin with a 6-by-6-foot cupola on the second floor, and the cupola held an Osborne fire finder.[^1][^3] WillhiteWeb says the frame came from alpine hemlock gathered in the vicinity, and the siding and roofing were brought in by horse.[^3]

Norm McCausland was the first lookout here.[^3] His beat ran north toward the Whitechuck Glacier and south toward Evergreen Mountain, and he was also expected to check on miners and to make sure sheepherders kept their animals in the proper locations.[^3] Early on there was no telephone, so a lookout had to walk down to Cady Pass to call in a fire.[^3] Rex's page lists panorama photographs taken on August 24, 1934.[^4]

The register says the cabin was removed in 1965.[^1] Firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb both say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^3] The sources differ on the word, and none describes how it came down.[^2][^3]

A 2021 hike recorded by WillhiteWeb found part of one footing and an unknown foundation at the northern edge of the summit ridge.[^3] Photo captions there also note nails, glass and screws.[^3] SummitPost says there are still hints of the old lookout just north of the summit.[^5]

The main approach is the West Cady Ridge trail.[^5] SummitPost says the trail starts in the Wild Sky Wilderness and continues to the top.[^5] The sources disagree on length.[^3][^5] WillhiteWeb lists a hiking distance of 8 miles,[^3] while SummitPost says several books list 16 miles round trip, but the real figure is closer to 14.[^5]
