Yellow Peak Lookout stands in the high desert and scattered timber islands of the Sheldon National Wildlife Refuge in northwestern Nevada's Washoe County, at an elevation of about 6,512 feet.[^1] It went up in 1981: a small groundhouse, nine feet on a side, raised two feet off the ground with a wooden catwalk running around it.[^1] The Bureau of Land Management, which administers the lookout and is listed as the refuge's cooperator here, staffs it only during periods of high fire danger rather than year-round.[^1] It is one of just three fire lookouts left standing in Nevada, and one of two run by the BLM.[^1] The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on March 1, 1999, carrying the register numbers US 301 and NV 2.[^1]

Beyond that register listing, little else about its service history, specific staffing years, or public access has been documented. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Nevada list repeats the same register number but places the lookout about a mile away from the National Historic Lookout Register's coordinates, a discrepancy already flagged in this record rather than resolved here.[^2]
