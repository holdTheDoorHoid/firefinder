Squaw Butte Lookout sits on the south summit of Squaw Butte, about 10 miles north-northeast of Emmett, in Gem County, Idaho.[^2][^1] The Bureau of Land Management runs it.[^1]

Sources do not agree on when the first cab went up.[^1][^2][^4] The register says a lookout has stood there since 1933, with structures replaced in 1938, 1953 and finally 1981.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists an 8-by-8-foot cab in 1933 that lightning destroyed in 1938.[^4] The firelookout.com page instead dates the first 8-by-8-foot cab to 1940, and says lightning destroyed it before a two-story metal cab and trailer went up in 1953.[^2] The three sources that date the present two-story cab all give 1981, and two place it on the south summit.[^1][^2][^4] A National Geodetic Survey station description, dated 1959 and reprinted on the Idaho Lookouts site, gives a corrugated metal building 24 feet tall.[^3] That is older than the 1981 cab, so it describes an earlier structure.[^3]

The earliest named lookout is Albert Thompson in 1940.[^2] Orville and Helen Blessinger are listed together for 1962 to 1983, and Doreen Mullins for 2001 to 2014.[^2][^4] The register says the lookout is usually staffed during the fire season.[^1] None of the sources read for this story describes a specific fire at the lookout.[^1][^2][^4]

Nothing here says the lookout was abandoned, moved or rented.[^1][^2][^4] The register records the 1981 building as current and gives the site's September 2020 registration.[^1]

A climbing guide reports that the peak's name was changed from Squaw Butte in 2022. The lookout records still use the older name, and this story did not check a federal name record.[^5][^1]

A maintained road leads to the lookout at the south end of Peak 5894, and 2WD vehicles generally reach the saddle below it.[^5] Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the site as a drive-up, staffed lookout.[^4] No source describes visitor entry to the cab, which is an active fire lookout, so check with the Bureau of Land Management before going up.[^1][^4]
