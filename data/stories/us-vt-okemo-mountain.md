Okemo Mountain Lookout stands in the Okemo State Forest in Rutland County, Vermont.[^1][^5] The register says the Vermont Department of Forest, Parks and Recreation administers it.[^1]

The first tower here was made of wood.[^1] The register says the state acquired two acres at the summit of Ludlow Mountain in 1921, and that a tower built with help from the Okemo Mountain Club went up in 1924.[^1] A 1922 newspaper item, quoted on the Eastern U.S. Lookouts site, says a tower was then being erected on Ludlow Mountain.[^2] The sources use two names for the same summit. The register and a 1922 item say Ludlow Mountain, while the state forest page says Okemo Mountain.[^1][^2][^5]

The Civilian Conservation Corps began the present steel tower in 1934, and the register says it was completed around 1937.[^1] A 1934 item says a CCC company would camp on the mountain to build a steel tower.[^2] A September 1937 item says the new steel tower stood 60 feet and replaced a wooden one half that height, and that a three-room fire-warden cabin sat at its base.[^2] The register says that cabin was removed in 1947. It also says a watchman's cabin went up 100 feet from the tower in 1943, and that a stone chimney built in 1951 for the lookout cabin is all that remains of that cabin today.[^1] The register does not say whether the lookout cabin and the watchman's cabin were the same building.

Wardens staffed the tower into the autumn. A 1934 item says Lindsey S. Hall was stationed at the tower until November 1, and a 1963 item says a man intended to remain there into the first week of November.[^2] The sources read do not name any fire the tower spotted.

The register lists later repairs. In 2010 a contractor replaced 10 steps, added ground wires, and installed a chain link fence along the stairs and landings.[^1][^2] In 2012 Okemo Mountain Resort's generosity made a new steel plate roof possible.[^1] A 2025 maintenance report says work covering lightning protection, paint, cab railings and wire mesh was completed at Okemo, along with Burke and Bear Hill.[^4] The state's 2025 plan also included safety railings for this tower, with work extending into spring 2026.[^3]

Today the paved Okemo Mountain Road reaches the summit, and a 3-mile trail climbs from the base.[^5] The state describes the refurbished summit tower as offering spectacular 360-degree views.[^5] The state expected shorter closures while the railings went in, and it points visitors to Trail Finder for closure updates before they go.[^3]
