In the summer of 1937, Yellowstone set out to build a new primary lookout in its east-central section.[^2] A newspaper that August described it as the park's fourth primary lookout, with four glass sides.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps put up the 14-by-14 ground cabin that year, and a park carpenter finished it off.[^1]

The sources do not agree on its job. The register says it was used for either primary or secondary detection, varying by source.[^1] Staffing is recorded for 1937 to 1974, 1984 to 1988, and 1993.[^1] The sources I checked do not name a particular fire, but the register says the park has periodically used it to watch fires.[^1]

The cabin is still standing.[^1][^3] The register lists it as Building #724 and records its registration date as March 1, 2021.[^1] The register also says it is currently maintained as a fire lookout.[^1]

Visiting it means a long walk.[^4] One visitor's account describes 34 miles round trip from a trailhead, with permits required for overnight stays. The same account says it is a cabin rather than a tower, and that the staffer on duty expected six visitors or fewer in a summer.[^4]

The register gives the elevation as 9,663 feet, while the other two sources give 9,650 feet.[^1][^3][^4]
