Jubilee Lookout stands on Jubilee Mountain in the Columbia-Shuswap Regional District of British Columbia, and it is reached by the Jubilee Mountain Forest Service Road.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register (NHLR) lists the British Columbia Forest Service as the administrator.[^1] The two sources disagree on the elevation: the NHLR gives 1,678 m (5,505 ft), while firelookout.ca gives 1,671 m (5,482 ft).[^1][^2]

The first lookout here was a wooden timber tower, built in 1957.[^2] Lightning destroyed it around 1989 or 1990, and a steel tower went up shortly afterward and stayed in service into the 2000s.[^2] The NHLR calls the tower fairly modern and says its design is uncommon in British Columbia.[^1]

A fire spread over a large part of the mountain on July 31, 2023.[^3] The NHLR says the tower was nearly lost to fire that year, but survived.[^1]

The NHLR describes the structure and cab as in reasonable condition.[^1] The NHLR registered the lookout on January 2, 2026.[^1] A July 2026 Columbia Valley Pioneer report says the tower is now shut and locked.[^3] The old fire lookout tower still stands.[^3] A log bench near the lookout, which had been burned black, has since been restored and can be used again.[^3] An amateur radio report from late September 2025 found the access road in great shape.[^4]
