Thomas Mountain Lookout stood on the 6,841-foot summit of Thomas Mountain, in Riverside County, within the San Bernardino National Forest.[^1] The register says it was built in 1935 as a 60-foot Aermotor steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot observation cabin on top, and a BC-201 cabin for the operator. It lists the US Forest Service as the administering agency.[^1] Wikipedia also gives 1935 and says the tower was used as a plane-spotting post during World War II.[^2] Hundred Peaks describes a 60-foot tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab and dates it to 1938, so the sources do not agree on the build year.[^3]

Hundred Peaks says a ranger outpost and an AWS ground observer station stood nearby from 1936 to 1945.[^3]

Fire and vandalism damaged the site.[^2] According to the register, the cabin was torn down and burned, and the tower was sold for scrap in 1973 once vandals had badly damaged the structures.[^1] Wikipedia gives the same 1973 date for the scrapping.[^2]

A 2018 photo caption in the register describes foundations and stonework as visible.[^1] The register also lists the residence cabin foundation, stone walls and access road as still in place.[^1] Hundred Peaks, by contrast, reports that only the foundation slabs are left.[^3] The register names the San Jacinto Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] I found no source that says whether the summit is open to visitors now, so check with that district before you go.
