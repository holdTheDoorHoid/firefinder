Mount Toby rises just east of the Connecticut River, in the towns of Sunderland and Leverett, and its summit has carried a fire lookout for more than a century.[^1][^3]

The register traces the current tower back through two predecessors.[^2] The first was a 58-foot structure on land of the Amherst Agricultural College, now the University of Massachusetts. The 1938 hurricane destroyed it. A 68-foot steel tower built in 1939 took its place, and it later blew down.[^2] The present 68-foot steel tower, with a wooden cab, went into service in 1951.[^2] Trailspotting describes the first structure differently, as an open wooden platform.[^3]

The register's visit reports describe the tower as staffed only sometimes, and seasonally. One of them is dated April 8, 2016.[^2]

Wikipedia describes the fire tower as open to the public and says it gives 360-degree views.[^1] Trailspotting says the cabin is locked, but visitors can still climb the outside stairs, eight flights of metal ones, to a lookout spot above the trees.[^3] The Blue Trail follows the power lines that serve the tower.[^3] The land is Mount Toby State Forest, which also works as a demonstration forest run by the University of Massachusetts Amherst.[^3]

The register notes a large amount of broadcasting equipment under and on the tower, and it lists the Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control as administrator.[^2] Trailspotting, relaying the Fire Lookout Association, says the cabin was last replaced in 2002.[^3] A November 2024 Trailspotting post treats the tower as still standing.[^3]
