The Darrington Ranger Station lookout was a rooftop post, not a mountaintop tower.[^2] It was a fire finder mounted on the roof of the station's barn, about half a mile north of the town of Darrington in Snohomish County, Washington.[^1] The barn itself dates to 1916, when it was built as a livery barn.[^5][^6]

Two sources put the rooftop lookout platform in 1932.[^3][^5] An August 1933 account quoted by the Forest Lookouts site describes a sturdy platform built on top of the barn, with a fire finder installed and phone lines set up so that real dispatching conditions could be copied.[^7]

Sources differ on its main use. SummitPost also credits the site with local fire watching, in addition to training.[^3] Discover Darrington says its main job was preparing future lookouts to use the fire finder at Fire Lookout School.[^5] Firelookout.com says it was used from 1932 through the 1940s, paired with the Whitechuck Bench Lookout during the annual Fire School.[^1] TrailChick dates the training use to 1934.[^6]

Sources disagree on how long the platform lasted. SummitPost says the platform was abandoned during the 1940s and later covered over with a new roof, while the building itself has changed little.[^3] Discover Darrington says the date of its removal is unclear, somewhere in the 1940s.[^5] A Washington lookout list marks the site as abandoned, not gone.[^2]

The barn still stands on the north end of Darrington, at the Ranger Station.[^5] Whether the platform survives is unclear. TrailChick, which visited in July 2018, describes an old fire platform on the barn, while Willhite says the barn once had one.[^6][^4]

The barn can be seen from outside.[^6] TrailChick says you can drive along Forest Lane close to the barn, or park at the gravel lot for the Whitehorse Trail and walk a short distance.[^6] SummitPost says visiting the barn is acceptable, but going inside is generally not allowed.[^3]
