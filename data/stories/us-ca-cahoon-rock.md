Cahoon Rock was a fire lookout in Tulare County, in Sequoia-Kings Canyon National Park.[^1] The register gives its elevation as 9,288 feet (2,831 m).[^1] It says the lookout was built around 1935, with a cabin laid out to a U.S. Forest Service California Region 5 plan (BC-301) sitting on top of a 20-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts site reprints several old newspaper clippings about the station.[^2] A July 1936 Fresno Bee item named A.S. Walker as the lookout at the Cahoon Rock Station, and described an earthquake that shook dishes out of cupboards and rocked the tower.[^2]

In July 1945 park officials praised Rose Vaughn, the woman lookout at Cahoon Rock, for stopping a lightning fire on her own.[^2] A bolt hit the ground 150 yards from her station.[^2] She grabbed a shovel and fought the blaze, and by the time help came she had reported three other fires and had her own fire under control.[^2]

Vaughn set off for the remote station again in June 1946.[^2] A 1951 clipping said she had held the job for nine years.[^2] In August 1951 she fell ill on the mountain, and a pack train carried her off to a hospital in Exeter.[^2]

The sources do not agree on the station's elevation. The clippings give 9,000 feet in 1936, 10,500 feet in 1945 and 9,200 feet in 1951, while the register gives 9,288 feet.[^1][^2]

The register does not say when the lookout came down.[^1] It lists the removal date as unknown and says nothing remains at the site.[^1] The register names the National Park Service as the agency that administers the site.[^1] We found no other description of the site today, and no source we read says whether visitors can reach it.
