In April 1938 Virginia's conservation commission announced that a forest lookout tower would be built in Mathews County that spring.[^1] The announcement said the forest service, a division of the commission, would put up a 100-foot steel tower on Highway 14 about a mile and a half east of Foster.[^1] The site had been donated by George A. Phillips and his wife, and the tower was expected to cost about $1,800.[^1] We could not find a completion date in the sources we read.[^1]

A National Geodetic Survey datasheet for a survey mark at the tower quotes a 1942 Coast and Geodetic Survey description. The description calls it a four-legged steel frame about 100 feet high, topped by a glass-walled cab, and says the state forest service owned and ran it.[^2]

The two location descriptions do not match. The 1938 announcement puts the tower about a mile and a half east of Foster, while the 1942 description puts it about 4.1 miles northeast of the town of Mathews.[^1][^2] We could not tell whether they describe the same spot.[^1][^2] The datasheet's 1961 note places the survey mark between old and new State Highway 14.[^2]

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched.[^1][^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] The datasheet's last recovery note, from 2002, says the survey mark was recovered in good condition. That note describes the survey mark, not the tower, so it tells us little about the tower's present state.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page names the Virginia Department of Forestry as the agency.[^1] We found no public access information and no recent description of the tower. Please ask the Virginia Department of Forestry before going near it.
