The Oakridge Lookout sits on the Navajo Indian Reservation in Apache County, Arizona, at an elevation of 7,606 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says it was constructed in 1941 and gives its design as International Derrick, a type it shares with other towers on the reservation.[^1] The tower is 83 feet tall and carries a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1]

The Register accepted the lookout on July 20, 2002, as US 438, AZ 34, and lists the Bureau of Indian Affairs as a cooperator.[^1] It uses both spellings, Oakridge and Oak Ridge, on its own pages.[^1] The Register's description calls the tower "in active service," but the page does not say when that was written.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list gives the name Oak Ridge and shows the tower as standing, with the same register number.[^2]

The sources I read say nothing about who built the tower, who staffed it, or which fires it watched.[^1][^2] They also say nothing about whether the public can visit or climb it. Anyone thinking of a trip should ask the Navajo Nation or the Bureau of Indian Affairs first.[^1][^2]
