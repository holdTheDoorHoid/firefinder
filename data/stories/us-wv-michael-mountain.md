The Michael Mountain lookout was built in 1924 on the summit of Michael Mountain in Pocahontas County, West Virginia.[^1][^2] The state had bought the bulk of what became Seneca State Forest in early 1924, and protecting the forest came first.[^3]

A January 1925 newspaper described a 45-foot steel observatory on the summit, with a dwelling for the observer nearby. The fire-fighting plan relied on the observer, one ranger and local wardens, who would be summoned if necessary.[^2]

In the forest's first two years under state ownership, ten fires burned and only 88 acres were lost. Those figures cover the whole forest, not this lookout alone.[^3]

In 1935 the lookout duties moved to a tower on Thorny Mountain, also in the state forest.[^2][^4] Sources disagree about what happened to the tower itself. The register says the Michael tower was moved and still stands as the Thorny Mountain tower.[^1] The state forest page describes the Thorny tower as the Michael tower's successor.[^3] Forest Lookouts calls Thorny a new site.[^4] A January 1937 newspaper had said an unusual observer-quarters tower would go up on Michael Mountain, and a November 1937 report says that type was completed on Thorny Mountain.[^2][^4]

Staffing at Thorny ran into the 1980s. The state forest page says seasonal staffing lasted until the mid-1980s, while Forest Lookouts dates the final staffed year to 1988.[^3][^4]

The register lists the original summit tower as relocated, and no source I read describes what, if anything, is left on the summit now.[^1] The Thorny Mountain tower is still standing.[^1] State Parks describes the Seneca fire tower as 65 feet tall, and The Dyrt gives Thorny the same height.[^5][^6] State Parks took over the lookout in 2013 and reopened it in 2015.[^4][^6] It is now rented for overnight stays by reservation.[^6] The state forest page still lists the tower as ready for duty if needed, which does not fit the rental arrangement.[^3]
