Bolivar Lookout sits on Craggy Peak in the Klamath National Forest, in Siskiyou County.[^2] A lookout was already in use there by September 1921, when a local paper noted that F. J. Friday had a few days off from the Bolivar lookout.[^1] The earlier cab was a Region 5 BC-302 design, which the National Historic Lookout Register says was standing before 1939.[^2]

The sources disagree about who owned the ground. The Register dates the handover from Southern Pacific Railroad to the Forest Service to 1939.[^2] The Forest Lookouts site instead says the Shasta Forest accepted title to a 20-acre site in May 1936, after the lookout had been built on leased Southern Pacific land.[^1] I could not reconcile these dates, so both are reported here.

The first cab did not last. In September 1959 the roof blew off, and a temporary tent took its place.[^1][^2] According to the Register, an all-steel CL-100 cab on a 9-foot block tower took over from the old structure in 1960.[^2] Forest Service diaries quoted in a Klamath forest history describe hauling foundation forms up the peak in October 1960 and pouring the floor slab in November 1960.[^1]

The Klamath National Forest took over the lookout from the Shasta National Forest in 1951.[^1] A 2012 photo page says the lookout is usually manned in fire season and that a good road from Callahan reaches it.[^3] In 2021 a news report featured Karita Knisely, who worked the one-room tower from May to October, four days a week, watching for smoke.[^4]

The Register accepted the lookout in October 2019.[^2] The 2021 report shows it was still in use then, and I found no source saying it has been removed.[^4]

I did not find a public rental or visitor program for Bolivar. Because it is a working station in fire season, check with the Klamath National Forest before planning any trip up the peak.
