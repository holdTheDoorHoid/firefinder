A steel fire tower stands on Keeney Knob in Summers County, West Virginia.[^1][^4] The Register dates it to 1937, credits the Civilian Conservation Corps, or CCC, with building it, and gives its height as 88 feet.[^1] An August 1936 newspaper item said members of the CCC camp at Union were doing the work under State Forester O. O. Nutter.[^2]

The newspapers do not agree on every detail. A January 1937 item put the knob in Greenbrier County, about eight miles northwest of Alderson, while the Register puts it in Summers County.[^2][^1] The reported heights run from 75 feet in 1936 to 79 feet in June 1937, 85 feet in October 1937, and the Register's 88 feet.[^2][^1] The 1936 item also called the tower under construction, but later items describe erection starting in 1937 and finishing that October, so the earlier report looks premature.[^2]

The plan was for more than a lookout. The state conservation commission planned a 13-acre public park with the tower at its center, a cabin for the keeper was to top it, and a telephone connection was to let the tower call areas where fires were burning.[^3][^2]

O. H. Mann was named attendant in 1936.[^2] A September 1937 item called him the tower keeper.[^2] The plan was for an attendant on staff at all times, but the Hinton News author could not find when the tower stopped being used as a fire tower.[^3]

The structure has suffered over the years. Vandals damaged the wooden stairs to the lookout cabin, the cabin was set on fire more than once, and the stairs have fallen into disrepair over recent decades.[^3]

The Register recorded it on August 30, 2015, as US 1103, WV 14.[^1] A Forest Fire Lookout Association list also marks it standing.[^4] A 2024 Hinton News article reported the cabin floor gone and the cabin no longer accessible.[^3] We found no current access rules or staffing record for the tower. The Register names the West Virginia Division of Forestry as administrator, so that agency is the place to ask before any visit.[^1]
