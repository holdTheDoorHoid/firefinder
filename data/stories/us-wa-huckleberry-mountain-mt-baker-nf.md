The Huckleberry Mountain lookout stood in Skagit County, in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, about 14 miles east-northeast of Darrington.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb's page for this lookout, at 5,856 feet, calls it the Huckleberry Ridge site.[^4] It says an early drawing placed it on Huckleberry Mountain, which it considers probably a mapmaker's error.[^4] The cab was 10 by 10 feet and of the L-5 type, built in 1935.[^1][^3][^4] Panoramic photos were taken from its roof on September 7, 1935.[^3][^4]

Vandals burned the cab in 1962.[^1][^2][^4] Forest Lookouts says only that vandals burned it.[^3]

WillhiteWeb's trip photos are undated.[^4] They show a cleared spot where the lookout building once stood, plus stove parts and phone-line insulators along the trail.[^4] The same account gives a hiking distance of 7 miles, starting at a trailhead roughly 15 miles along the Suiattle River Road.[^4] The first two miles are often grown over, and near the end hikers tend to lose the route.[^4] Volunteers clear the trail about every five years.[^4]

We found no source that says whether the site or its trail is open to visitors today. Check with the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest before going.[^1][^4]
