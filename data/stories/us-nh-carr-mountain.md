In August 1939 the Portsmouth Herald reported that a Forest Service crew was building a fire observation tower on Carr Mountain, at 3,470 feet in the northeast part of Wentworth, New Hampshire. The paper said the tower would be finished within weeks, under a crew led by Everett Kimball of Lacinia.[^1]

NH Family Hikes gives a different span, saying the summit held a fire tower from 1929 to 1948.[^2] The newspaper date and the guide's start date do not match, so the start of the tower's life is uncertain.[^1][^2]

A 1940 Groton Times item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says that Malcomb MacKenzie, who had been ill with appendicitis, went back to work at the Carr Mountain tower that October.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^3] The hike guide, however, reports that four large cement footings from the tower still stand among the ledges on the summit.[^2]

The hike starts at a lot off Stinson Lake Road in Ellsworth, while the summit lies in Wentworth.[^2] Trees have blocked most of the view, and the guide says the mountain was dropped from the 52-With-a-View list in 2001 for that reason.[^2] There is no tower to climb.[^2][^3]
