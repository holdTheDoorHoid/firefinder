Barren Mountain, in Piscataquis County, Maine, is a 2,640-foot peak that the Appalachian Trail passes over.[^1][^2] A hiker's history site reports that a Coast and Geodetic Survey team placed benchmarks on the summit in 1941 and mentioned no structures there at that time. The same team also used, and likely built, a 15-foot pole tower for its survey lines.[^1]

A steel tower that had stood on Pleasant Pond Mountain was relocated to Barren Mountain in 1951.[^1] A survey team's 1955 description calls it a Maine Forest Service lookout, about 40 feet tall, with four steel legs and an eight-foot-square cab painted white.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page places the tower in the Maine Forestry District.[^3] A hiker's Appalachian Trail journal records an active lookout man on duty in 1965.[^1]

The FFLA's Maine list marks the tower "Standing*", and its key says the asterisk is for towers that keep their frame and support after the cab has gone.[^2] The hiker site likewise reports that the tower still stands without a cab.[^1] Neither source says when the cab came down. We found no statement that the frame is open to visitors, so this page does not recommend climbing it.[^1][^2]
