Patrick's Knob stands in Sanders County, Montana, on the Lolo National Forest, south of the town of Plains.[^1][^2] The sources disagree on how far south: firelookout.com says six miles, and a 2018 Sanders County Ledger article says about 15 miles.[^2][^3]

The Register says the knob takes its name from John W. Patrick, who came to the Plains valley in 1869 and later ran a ferry across the Clark Fork River.[^1]

The first lookout was a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1934.[^2][^3] The Register says that tower served until it was condemned.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed in 1968, after which a cupola trailer took its place.[^2] The Register describes a mobile lookout trailer used for a few years before the new tower was built.[^1][^2]

The present tower went up in 1976.[^1][^2] The Register describes an R-6 flattop with a catwalk, built on a 10-foot concrete base, and the Ledger calls it a 15-by-15-foot, two-story tower.[^1][^3]

The Register says staffing continues regularly through fire season.[^1] Firelookout.com says it is staffed in summer.[^2] In June 2018 the Forest Service and a Youth Conservation Corps crew opened the station ahead of a summer watch, and the Ledger noted the lookout's elevation as 6,837 feet.[^3][^2]

The Register registered the lookout on April 24, 2009, as US 792, MT 49, and it is still in use.[^1][^2] The sources do not say whether the public may climb the tower. The Register names the Plains/Thompson Falls Ranger District as a cooperator, so that office is the place to ask before any visit.[^1]
