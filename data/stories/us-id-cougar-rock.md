The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Cougar Rock as a ground lookout that is gone.[^1] It stood in the Boise National Forest in Valley County, Idaho, at about 7,563 feet.[^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts page says the site began in the 1930s with an alidade and a tent camp.[^4] A 16-by-16-foot cabin followed in 1934, according to firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts, which both call it a frame building.[^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts page is less certain: one entry calls the 1934 building a tent cabin, and another, dated to the mid-1930s, calls the cabin non-standard.[^4]

Forest Lookouts says the last fire season the lookout was used was 1942.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts has no documented staffing history for it.[^3]

All three lookout pages date its destruction to 1950.[^2][^3][^4] The climbing guide describes a lookout on a summit it calls Stolle Peak, which the current USGS map labels the Cougar Rock Benchmark, and dates that lookout to the same span, 1934 to 1950.[^5] Forest Lookouts quotes a 1953 USGS station description that puts the benchmark at an abandoned lookout about two miles southwest of Cougar Rock.[^4] The sources therefore do not clearly show that this lookout stood on the peak named Cougar Rock.[^4][^5]

Forest Lookouts says only a pile of debris remained in 1988.[^4] The climbing guide's author visited the summit that year and found little of the structure left.[^5] The same guide says the summit trail has gone unmaintained for years, and it reports fires in the area after 1988, though the author did not know whether any reached the summit.[^5] Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the site as hike-in.[^3] No source describes the site now, and none says whether the public may go there.
