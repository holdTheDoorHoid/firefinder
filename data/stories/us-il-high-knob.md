High Knob sits in Gallatin County, in the Shawnee National Forest.[^3] The tower went up in 1934.[^2] Alongside the 60-foot steel stair tower, the grounds held a dwelling, a garage, a latrine and a picnic area.[^2]

A Forest Service page says the lookout was once staffed by a Civilian Conservation Corps crew from Camp Cadiz, in the early years of the forest.[^1] The Forest Service also says Trigg is the only one of the forest's sixteen lookouts that remains.[^6]

In August 1972 a notice in the Southern Illinoisan sought bids to sell this tower and seven other steel lookout towers on the forest.[^3] The Central Illinois lookout list marks the tower as removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Illinois list shows it as "Tower Gone."[^3][^4] Illinois Country Living, which profiles a tower historian, reports that almost all Illinois towers were taken down or sold in the early 1970s.[^5]

The Forest Service now runs the site as a day-use recreation area, open all year from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m.[^1] Concrete footings from the old tower are still on the ground.[^1] A 2025 Forest Fire Lookout Association report describes a visit and says the footers were still in place.[^2]

Daytime parking is at the picnic area, the starting point for about five miles of trails below the knob.[^1] There are no restrooms and no drinking water.[^1] Overnight parking is allowed just outside the recreation area, by the stone pillar at the entrance.[^1]
