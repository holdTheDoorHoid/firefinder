Jackson Peak sits about 10 miles east of Lowman, in Boise County on the Boise National Forest.[^2][^1] The first lookout went up in 1927.[^1][^2] A 1927 Idaho Statesman report, as quoted on one Idaho lookout history site, describes a glass-walled station where a man and his wife lived and worked for the whole fire season.[^4]

The register says the first lookout was similar to a California Plan 4A design.[^1] A later history describes it as a two-story log structure.[^4] Photo evidence suggests it was raised onto a 10-foot log base in 1937.[^2]

The date of the current building is not settled. The register says the current two-story structure was built in 1979 and is still staffed each fire season.[^1] firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts describe a flat cab on a 10-foot concrete base, dated 1981.[^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts history page gives 1981 for a two-story flat roof house.[^4]

In July 1988, the Willis Gulch fire threatened the lookout and a nearby private subdivision.[^4]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists known staff from 1962, and its most recent entry is Roger Lockwood, dated 2025.[^3]

The register added the lookout on September 28, 2020.[^1]

The 1927 account describes a trail as the only road up or down.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts now describes the site as drive-up and staffed.[^3] Check with the Boise National Forest before you plan a visit.
