The Gauley tower stands on a hilltop in Calhoun County, Mississippi, at about 591 feet, and the National Historic Lookout Register describes it as an Aermotor lookout tower.[^1] The register gives no build date.[^1]

The most specific account we found comes from a student history of the county written in 1935 and 1936 and posted on a local history site.[^2] It describes a camp of Civilian Conservation Corps workers between Calhoun City and Derma, whose crews built two lookout towers for fire prevention: one near Wardwell and the other atop Gauley Mountain.[^2] The register's Gauley tower is most likely the second of these, given the shared name, but neither source says so outright.[^1][^2]

We found no record of who staffed the tower or when it last served as a lookout.[^1] The register notes that a house once stood next to the tower and has since been torn down, though its foundation remains.[^1] A quonset hut at the site has lost one of its sides but still stands.[^1] A tag at the site suggests the U.S. Forest Service managed it at some point.[^1]

The register's present-tense description suggests the tower still stands, but it gives no visit date, no access rules and nothing about climbing.[^1] The entry was registered on June 26, 2022, nominated by Jason Johns, and that is the most recent date we found.[^1] The register names the Mississippi Forestry Commission as administrator, so ask that agency before going up anything, and do not climb it without permission.[^1]

[^1]: Gauley Lookout Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/ms/gauley-lookout-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Calhoun City History, msgw.org, http://msgw.org/calhoun/resources/calhouncityhistory.html (accessed 2026-10-09).
