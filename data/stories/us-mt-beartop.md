Beartop Lookout sits on a peak in the Lewis and Clark National Forest, about 32 miles west of Choteau in Teton County, Montana.[^1] Newspapers of the 1930s spelled the name several ways, including Bear Top and Bare Top, so older records can be hard to match.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the site began as a camp in 1928, and it dates the current L-4 cab to 1933, when it cost $650.[^3] Local clippings suggest a slightly different timeline. A new lookout house was being built on the peak in late September 1932, and a November 1932 item put its cost at about $650, paid from an emergency relief fund.[^2] A 1931 item says one smoke chaser occupied the Bear Top lookout that summer, so a structure may have stood there before the new house.[^2]

Between 1935 and 1940, newspapers named several summer lookouts for the peak, including Hugh Munyan in 1939.[^2] The register calls the cab usually staffed and says it is the sole original L-4 cab in the Bob Marshall Wilderness Complex.[^3]

A 1966 notice names Dennis Seaton as the Beartop Mountain lookout.[^2] The cab got a new roof in June 2009.[^2] The register recorded it on April 12, 2022, as US 1542, MT 105.[^3]

The Forest Service's cabin page describes rentals booked through Recreation.gov, but it does not name Beartop, and I found no listing that says whether the cab can be visited.[^4] A hiking listing carries the name Beartop Trail #129 for a route in the Bob Marshall Wilderness.[^5] Because the register describes the cab as usually staffed, check with the Forest Service before you plan a trip.
