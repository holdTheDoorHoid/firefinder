Wrong Ridge is in Teton County, Montana, on the Lewis and Clark National Forest, about 35 miles west-northwest of Choteau.[^1] Its elevation is 8,090 feet.[^1]

Rex Kamstra's page lists a log cabin lookout here in 1928, a quarter mile south of the Continental Divide.[^1] A February 1931 Choteau Acantha notice lists $1,223.74 in National Forest improvements to the Wrong Ridge lookout house.[^2]

The reprinted newspaper notices show seasonal lookouts working the ridge through the 1930s. In 1931 two smoke chasers worked the forest that summer, and one of them occupied the Wrong Ridge lookout.[^2] In 1933 a lookout was stationed there most of the summer, and a telephone line was built to an emergency lookout on the ridge.[^2] Notices from 1934, 1935 and 1938 name other people assigned to Wrong Ridge.[^2]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1940, quoted on Forest Lookouts, places the lookout house on the highest point of a low wooded ridge, four miles north of Wrong Creek Guard Station.[^2] It puts the house three-quarters of a mile south of the Continental Divide.[^2] Kamstra's page gives a quarter mile, so the two sources disagree on that distance, and we could not settle which is right.[^1][^2]

Kamstra's page says the cabin was abandoned in the 1950s, but it gives no exact year.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list shows the site as a ground-level lookout that is gone.[^3]

We found no source that says what, if anything, remains on the ridge today, or whether visitors can reach it.
