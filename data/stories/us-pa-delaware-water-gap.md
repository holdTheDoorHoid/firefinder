Delaware Water Gap, in Monroe County, Pennsylvania, had a steel fire tower built in 1921.[^1] An item from August 1921, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, called it one of three more steel towers then being erected across the Delaware Forest District.[^2]

The register calls it a steel Aermotor tower.[^1] A 1934 newspaper item, quoted on the same Forest Lookouts page, described it as a 60-foot, all-steel tower of the Pennsylvania Department of Forests and Waters.[^2]

In October 1934 the towerman, Francis Kern, lived with his wife and daughter in the cabin at the foot of the tower.[^2] On October 18 that year the towerman, interrupted in his watch by a sound on the stairs, found the couple's 19-month-old daughter near the top. He then put a barrier at the foot of the stairs.[^2]

The register says the tower is no longer standing, and it gives no coordinates for the site.[^1]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the spot is open to visitors.
