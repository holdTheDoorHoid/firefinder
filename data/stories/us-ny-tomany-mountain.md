Tomany Mountain is a peak northwest of Arietta in Hamilton County, in the Adirondacks, and it once carried a fire lookout tower.[^1]

Wikipedia dates the first structure to 1912, when the Conservation Commission put up a wooden fire tower.[^1] A Daily Gazette column, however, describes the first lookout as a spiral staircase climbing a white pine tree.[^2]

In 1916 a 50-foot Aermotor LL25 tower took its place.[^1][^3] An observer's cabin built in 1950 stood beside the older model 1922 cabin.[^4] Bill Starr's history names Orville and Bertha Slade as the observers.[^4]

The lookout closed at the end of the 1970 fire season, and Wikipedia links that closure to fire detection by aircraft.[^1][^3] Starr's list says the station then stayed on a stand-by list until it was declared surplus property in 1976.[^3]

Starr's list records the tower coming down in August 1986, while the Daily Gazette gives 1987.[^3][^2] Wikipedia says the pieces were left scattered near the summit.[^1]

Today the summit is reached on foot from New York State Route 10.[^1] The trail is not officially maintained, though old state trail markers remain, and the trail follows telephone poles, many still standing.[^1][^5] At the top, Speculator Chamber describes the footers where the tower stood, near a benchmark, and the remnants of the observer's cabin.[^5] The Daily Gazette reports that many pieces of the tower lie scattered over the mountain's edge, alongside cabin remains and a trash dump.[^2]
