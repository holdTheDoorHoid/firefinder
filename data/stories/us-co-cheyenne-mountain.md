In September 1911, the Colorado Springs Gazette reported that the government's forestry department would keep a fire lookout station on Cheyenne Mountain that fall. It was to overlook the eastern slope of the Front Range.[^1]

The paper said it would be one of three such stations set up in the Rocky Mountains that fall. It also said the number of men to be employed had not been officially announced.[^1]

A Routt County newspaper repeated the plan on September 22, 1911. It described a station that would look out over the eastern slope of the Front Range, near Colorado Springs.[^1]

A Colorado lookout index places the mountain in El Paso County, at 38.73704, -104.88089.[^2] Wikipedia notes that no public main road reaches the mountain.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains of a lookout there today, so its condition is unknown, and so is whether the public can reach any surviving site.[^1][^2][^3]
