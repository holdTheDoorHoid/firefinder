The Doty Lookout stood on a ridge in Lewis County, Washington, at about 2,081 feet.[^1][^3][^4] It sat near the village of Doty, about 10 miles west of Chehalis; firelookout.com says 16 miles.[^2][^3][^4] The tower went up in 1942, built by the Washington Division of Forestry.[^4] It was a 28-foot pole tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and there was a house as well.[^1] A lookout history site lists the station as activated on August 24, 1942.[^3]

Two women staffed the lookout for summer 1954 and kept watch for fires and aircraft.[^3] A September 1960 newspaper item said the state Department of Natural Resources ran the station from its Chehalis headquarters during fire season.[^3]

Vandalism was a yearly problem.[^3] In September 1960 a break-in knocked boards off the tower and tore down a radio antenna pole.[^3] By June 1964 a state district administrator reported the tower "in a mess" after vandals smashed thirty windows and stole copper tubing and cable.[^3]

The sources do not agree on when the tower came down.[^1][^2][^4] A trip report says it was destroyed in 1964.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both give 1966 instead.[^1][^2]

Our sources describe no standing structure at the site today. A trip report places some remnants near the summit, at the edge of the woods.[^4] A recent timber harvest opened part of the view, but new trees are closing it off, and mature trees block what remains.[^4]

The site is on Department of Natural Resources land and is reached by about 5 miles of gravel road, starting at a gate.[^4] The trip report also says the roads have not had gates for many years, though it tells visitors to park at one.[^4] The trip report describes a hike of about 0.8 mile that gains about 300 feet.[^4] A landslide that took out the road just past the gate dates from 2015.[^4]
