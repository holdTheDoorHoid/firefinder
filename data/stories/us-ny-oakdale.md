In April 1922 a tower about 90 feet high stood on the grounds of the South Side Sportsmen's Club in Oakdale, a hamlet in the Town of Islip on Long Island's South Shore.[^1][^3]

The tower was meant for early warning.[^1] A Conservation Commission forestry official who endorsed the request said it would help fire wardens locate fires and deal with them in their early stages.[^1]

The club had offered to give the tower to the town.[^1] The local fire warden and fire ranger, named in the 1922 item as Wohlfarth and Dare, asked the Board of Supervisors to authorize moving it.[^1] Wohlfarth was to get bids for taking it to a vantage point on his own property near Central Islip.[^1]

Superintendent Howard said Islip was well ahead of most of the state's towns on forest fire prevention.[^1]

A statewide lookout list now marks the Oakdale tower as gone, with no date or detail given.[^2]

The club has since disbanded.[^4] Its grounds are now part of Connetquot River State Park Preserve.[^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains of the tower site today.
