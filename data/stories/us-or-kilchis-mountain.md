The Oregon Department of Forestry administered a lookout on Kilchis Mountain, which the records date to 1955: a 25-foot steel tower with a cab and a cabin on the site.[^1][^3] The register and firelookout.com give the cab as 7 feet square, while the Weebly record gives 7½ feet.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com places the site 11 miles northeast of Tillamook.[^2] The tower's steps were creosoted the following year.[^3]

The Cedar Butte area has burned repeatedly.[^4] A hiking guide names the 1918 Cedar Butte Fire, which scorched 40,000 acres, and says three large Tillamook Burn fires crossed the area in the 1930s and 1940s.[^4]

The records say the lookout was abandoned in 1965 and then removed, although the register's description says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3] A hiking guide says a lookout on Cedar Butte was dismantled in the early 1950s, earlier than the 1955 date in the records.[^4] A mapping site lists a "Kilchis Lookout" tower about 1.1 km from the register's coordinates and at nearly the same elevation.[^5] No source I found says whether that tower is the lookout the guide describes on Cedar Butte.[^4][^5]

A hiking guide describes the former site on Cedar Butte in the Tillamook State Forest. It says the cabin was demolished and that broken glass from it is still scattered across the site.[^4] Two benches there give a westward view down the South Fork Kilchis valley to the ocean.[^4] Tree growth since the dismantling has narrowed the views, the guide says.[^4] The guide calls the walk an easy hike, reached by driving Highway 6 and then a gravel road to a trailhead pullout.[^4] No source I found describes the site in more detail.[^4]
