Dock Butte is a peak in the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, in Skagit County, Washington, about nine miles south of Mount Baker.[^1][^2] Around 1935 an L-4 cab was built there, and panoramic photographs were taken on September 18 of that year.[^3][^4] The register credits those panoramas to the National Archives Pacific Northwest Region.[^1] In 1939 the U.S. Geological Survey placed some benchmarks and mentioned the lookout.[^3]

The sources agree on the year the cab ended, 1964, but not on how. FireLookout.com and the Willhite guide say it was destroyed.[^2][^3] The register says it was removed.[^1] The Forest Service trail page says the building was burned.[^5]

The Forest Service calls the place the site of an abandoned fire lookout, reached on Trail 604.1.[^5] From the Blue Lake trailhead the trail runs 0.2 miles to the Dock Butte junction, then 1.5 miles to the site. The last half mile is moderately steep and exposed.[^5] Parking is at the end of Forest Service Road 1230, and a recreation pass is required at the trailhead.[^5] An active alert on the trail page says Forest Service roads 12 and 1230 will be closed for repairs in August. The alert gives no year, so check before you drive in.[^5] Visitors should bring drinking water, since the site has none.[^5] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the cab on the summit today.[^5]
