The Tappan Fire Tower stands on Moores Ridge Road, about three miles southeast of Dennison in Tuscarawas County.[^1][^2] The Ohio Department of Natural Resources is listed as the agency that runs it, and the National Historic Lookout Register accepted the tower on September 3, 2014, as US 1029, OH 17.[^3][^1]

The Division of Forestry built the tower in 1950, and it went into operation in 1951.[^2] The 2018 Times-Reporter article gives its height as 80 feet, with 107 steps to the top, and says the tower had a telephone and a two-way radio so the watchman could stay in touch with other Division of Forestry staff.[^2]

Joe Eslick of Feed Springs helped build the tower in 1950 and became its watchman the next year.[^2] He worked there until he retired in 1967.[^2] A 1967 newspaper item quoted on the Eastern U.S. lookouts site calls him the only operator the tower had since its erection.[^3] The sources read name no notable fires that the tower watched.

The Division of Forestry stopped using its towers as fire lookouts in 1978, according to the 2018 article, and most of them were sold or removed.[^2] The article does not say when Tappan's own watch ended.[^2] The tower once served as a radio antenna for the Muskingum Watershed Conservancy District, but no longer does.[^2] In 2010 the Ohio chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association reported that Tappan was one of two state-owned towers still needing paint.[^4]

The 2018 article found the tower still standing, "abandoned and in a state of disrepair."[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list also marks it as standing.[^5] The sources read do not say whether the public may visit. The newest description calls the tower abandoned and in disrepair. Do not climb it. Check with the Ohio Department of Natural Resources before going near the site.
