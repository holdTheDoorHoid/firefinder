Bolero Lookout stood on Bolero Peak in the Santa Ana Mountains of Orange County, California.[^1][^2] Orange County fire officials built it in 1946, one of three lookouts raised in the mountains just after World War II.[^1][^2] The Los Angeles Times says state prison work crews built these rickety structures.[^2] The lookout was a two-story wooden structure.[^2]

Lois Smith, named in a 1989 article, was paid full time by the county as a lookout for several years in the 1960s.[^2] The fire season runs from June through October, and lookouts rotated on a 10-day-on, 5-day-off schedule.[^2]

A fire captain said that by the 1960s smog had made the lookouts all but useless, and new fire stations made the towers seem less important.[^2] The county took its lookouts down one by one, and Bolero was the last one standing.[^2][^3] The lookout had not been used for over a decade by 1989.[^3]

Recent Santa Ana winds had left the structure leaning, and fire officials judged it too hazardous to keep standing.[^2] On March 15, 1989, the Orange County Fire Department demolished it with a bulldozer in about 20 minutes.[^3][^2]

The Times describes Bolero Peak as 1,600 feet high, while the Former Fire Lookout Sites record lists the lookout site at 1,756 feet.[^2][^1] Forest Lookouts places the site in the Cleveland National Forest.[^3]

An Orange County Parks listing is titled After Work Cardio Hike: Creek to Bolero Lookout, which suggests a hike route runs to the site.[^4] No source we read says what, if anything, remains on the peak today, or whether visitors may reach it.
