Moscow Mountain rises about eight miles northeast of Moscow, in Latah County, Idaho, to 4,721 feet.[^1] A University of Idaho photo record places the old tower at 46.805553, -116.840425.[^4] Two summits carried towers. The first went up in 1934 on the east summit: a 40-foot round pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^1][^3] A 74-foot steel Aermotor followed on the west summit the next year.[^2] The site is listed under the St. Joe National Forest and the Idaho Department of Lands.[^1]

A 1951 Kendrick Gazette item mentions a couple named Stedman who had just come back from the Moscow Mountain lookout station.[^3] In May 1956 a University of Idaho forestry conference planned a television and microwave demonstration of fire detection from the lookout.[^3] That August, State Forester Roger Guernsey said the tower should be replaced.[^3]

The steel tower was moved to the east summit in 1959 to take the place of the old pole tower.[^1][^3] In 1981 a private owner removed the tower and took it to a summer cabin on the southern end of Lake Coeur d'Alene.[^1][^2][^3]

No source describes what remains on either summit today. Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the site "Drive-Up Removed," though its page does not explain the label.[^2] Whether the site is open to the public is not stated in these sources.
