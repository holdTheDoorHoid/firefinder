Timber Butte Cabin looks like a fire lookout, but it was never a working one.[^3] Volunteers built it in 2005 as a replica of the gabled L-4 lookout design, which was common in the region in the 1930s. It has a wooden catwalk.[^1][^2] A $10,000 grant from Lane County and other gifts from individuals and organizations helped fund the build.[^1]

A list of Oregon's standing lookouts leaves the cabin out. Its note says the cabin was built to rent to the public, and that it resembles a lookout but was never used as one.[^3] The same note adds that "Timber Butte" is not an official name on maps.[^3] Other sources still call it a replica lookout, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^2][^4]

It sits at 3,650 feet in the Fall Creek area of the Willamette National Forest, 19 miles from Lowell.[^1] Because the list says the site was never used as a lookout point, the cabin has no fire-watch history to record.[^3]

Today it is rented through Recreation.gov and open from mid-April through late October.[^1] The 14-by-14-foot cabin sleeps four on a double bed, with a propane heating stove, and a three-burner cooktop sits on the deck. The cabin supplies propane, dishes and cookware.[^1] There is no electricity and no drinking water, so guests must bring their own water.[^1] Entry is by combination lock, and guests request the combination from the Middle Fork Ranger District, which takes those calls on weekdays only.[^1] The cabin can be reached by car, though guests walk about 300 feet uphill from parking, and a high-clearance vehicle is recommended.[^2] The listing says the heating stove is propane gas only, and warns that burning firewood inside it will result in serious injury, explosion or worse.[^1]
