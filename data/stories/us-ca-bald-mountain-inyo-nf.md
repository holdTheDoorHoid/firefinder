Bald Mountain Lookout stands on the Inyo National Forest in Mono County, California, in the Mono Lake Ranger District.[^4][^2]

The original lookout went up in 1943 under the Civilian Public Service, a wartime agency that followed the CCC and was staffed by conscientious objectors.[^1] The present structure dates from 1963 and combines concrete block with a steel cab.[^2][^1]

In 1956 a fire broke out just north of the lookout, and Mrs. Gloria Lasky was the first to call it in from there. Forest Service staff and three deer hunters contained it, according to a Reno Evening Gazette item quoted on a California lookout history site.[^4] In 1969 a Mason Valley News item, also quoted there, described a shared arrangement linking the Inyo and Toiyabe national forests that brought the lookout into the Toiyabe fire detection network, with Mr. and Mrs. Smallwood staffing it.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association says the lookout stopped full-time operation sometime in the 1980s. In 2024 the FFLA teamed up with the Mono Lake Ranger District to reopen it with trained volunteers.[^2] According to the FFLA, no other standing and operating lookout remains on the Inyo National Forest.[^2]

The first full season, in 2025, ran from June 17 to October 12, which is 113 days in service. The FFLA lined up 46 visiting volunteers for it, and the report lists two short gaps, on July 4 to 6 and October 1 to 2.[^3] Visitors hosted that year totaled 1,943.[^3] In 2025, Eastern Sierra residents were also recruited and trained, with the aim of forming a local chapter that could fill shifts.[^2]

The 2025 report says the indoor toilet still does not work and the outside paint is flaking.[^3]

To drive there, take Logging Camp Road from Highway 395 at the top of Deadman Summit, then continue on Forest Service route 1S05 and watch for the signs.[^5] The Forest Service suggests a vehicle with high clearance and four-wheel drive.[^5] The road is open around the clock, and no fee is charged.[^5] The cab is a working lookout that volunteers staff, and I found no visitor rule for it in these sources, so check with the Mono Lake Ranger District before you go up.[^2]
