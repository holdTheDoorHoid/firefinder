Russel Mountain Lookout sits on a peak of about 7,500 feet in Baker County, roughly 15 miles north of the town of Halfway, inside the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest.[^1][^3] The register says the site has been used for fire detection since 1921, when a 50-foot tower topped by an open platform went up.[^1][^2]

The tower standing today dates to 1949.[^1][^5] In August of that year a local newspaper reported timbers hauled out to the site, where the Forest Service was erecting a lookout tower, and by September 1950 a prefabricated, creosoted 82-foot tower was nearing completion.[^2] An L-4 cab went on top in 1950.[^1][^2] The sources do not fully agree on the details.[^2][^3] A 1949 newspaper gave the height as 80 feet, and one Forest Lookouts entry dates an 82-foot tower to 1941.[^2]

A 1925 diary extract records lightning starting six fires while the lookout was at the tower.[^2] In 1954 a time-lapse movie camera was to be installed at the lookout to record how lightning storms built up.[^2] Newspaper items name a few people stationed there, including David Wirth in 1963 and Doug Payton in 1964.[^2]

In 1987 the Forest Service said the lookout had been dropped as a fire-spotting post, because aerial observation was a more cost-effective method and a lookout in Idaho covered much of the same ground. It asked the public for ideas on the tower's future.[^5] An undated listing says a forest fire threatened the tower in the 1990s and that it is now available for emergencies.[^3] A 2023 visitor found it no longer staffed and listed as abandoned, with signs telling people to stay off.[^5]

The register added the lookout on July 5, 2022.[^1] A directory of Oregon lookouts lists the tower as standing.[^4]

Access is the least clear part of the record. A 2020 account lists the site as drive-up and found the lookout tower closed.[^6] A 2023 visitor reached the tower on foot via the Sugarloaf Trail, covering 1.9 miles to the lookout access road and 0.2 miles more. That visitor noted the Forest Service seems to treat the spot as a trailhead.[^5] No source says the tower may be climbed, and the posted signs ask people to stay off it.[^5]
