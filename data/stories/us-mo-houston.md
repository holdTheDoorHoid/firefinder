The Houston fire tower went up in 1960 as a 120-foot steel tower [^1][^2]. It sits on a towersite six miles west of the town of Houston, in Texas County, reached by way of Highway 17 [^1][^2].

The 2015 management plan from the Missouri Department of Conservation lists a radio tower on the same site and says the Texas County towersites are kept mainly for wildfire detection [^2]. An older listing names the Missouri Conservation Commission instead [^1], and the 2015 plan says Department personnel will visually inspect the towers [^2].

I found no record of who staffed this tower or of any fire it spotted, so that part of its history is still open.

The 2015 plan still lists the tower on its site, and says the Texas County fire towers are likely eligible for the National Register of Historic Places [^2]. I found no newer report on the tower's condition.

I also could not find a current statement on whether the public can visit. The 2015 plan does not address public access at this site [^2], so check with the Department of Conservation before any trip, and do not climb the tower unless access is confirmed.
