Raven Roost is a high point on a long east-west ridge in the Naches Ranger District, and a fire lookout went up there in 1934.[^1][^4] The cab was a 14-by-14-foot L-4 built on the ground.[^4] WillhiteWeb credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it, along with a narrow one-way road up to the top.[^4] Panoramic photos from August 1934 are credited to the National Archives' Pacific Northwest Region, though the captions give both the 16th and the 18th as the day.[^1][^3][^4]

The FFLOS register and firelookout.com both say the cab was abandoned by 1956.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb tells a longer story. It names Dick Simmons as the last person to man the lookout, in 1962, and says the lookout's last several years were used only when fire danger ran high.[^4] The register's 1956 date may mark the end of routine use, while the 1962 date marks the last person on duty.[^1][^4]

WillhiteWeb dates the removal to about 1964, clearing the way for microwave radio facilities.[^4] The summit was lowered 34 feet to make the top flat enough for parking, and the road was improved as part of the lease deal for the tower sites.[^4] The FFLOS register puts the removal in the mid-1960s.[^1]

Today the top holds communications towers. WillhiteWeb describes a large parking area, along with railings that recall the summit's years as a tourist spot.[^4] None of the sources consulted says what, if anything, remains of the cab.

Access is by Forest Service roads.[^4][^6] WillhiteWeb calls the road fair dirt and gravel, lists the site as drive-up, and warns that snow drifts can cover the road until about July 1.[^4] The Forest Service's Raven Roost Trailhead sits at the end of spur road 866, reached by following Crow Creek Road 1902.[^6] The Washington Trails Association describes the Raven Roost Trail as starting at the high point at road's end.[^5] Hikers entering Norse Peak Wilderness need a free permit from the trailhead.[^6]
