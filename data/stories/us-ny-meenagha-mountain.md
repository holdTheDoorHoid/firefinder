Meenagha Mountain rises in the Adirondacks, northeast of the hamlet of Paul Smiths in Franklin County.[^1] Wikipedia spells it Meenahga, but the Forest Fire Lookout Association index and the Adirondack Architectural Heritage list both use Meenagha, so we keep that spelling.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1927, Conservation Department forest ranger Albert Tebeau erected a 73-foot Aermotor LS40 lookout for the Adirondack-Florida School.[^1] The school used it most. When fire danger ran high, the tower also served as a fire observation station, under an arrangement with the state Department of Environmental Conservation.[^1]

We found no record of any particular fire the tower watched.

Wikipedia says the tower still stands on private land, with no public access.[^1] The Adirondack Architectural Heritage list also files it under private land, a group where some allow public access by easement.[^3] We found nothing showing that this tower does.

CNY Hiking lists Meenagha among the five New York fire towers it describes as privately owned.[^4]

A visitor's 2019 post on the Adirondack Forum says a house has been put up on the summit.[^5] We could not confirm that from any other source, so treat it as unverified.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association index still lists the tower as standing.[^2] Because it is private and closed, do not try to climb it or reach it without the landowner's permission.
