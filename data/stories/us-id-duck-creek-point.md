Duck Creek Point Lookout stands at about 9,130 feet (2,783 meters) in Lemhi County, in the Yellowjacket Mountains, which are part of the Salmon River Mountains.[^1][^5]

The register describes a 10-by-10-foot cab similar to an L-5, built in 1935 as a ground house.[^1] A log base was added later: the register dates it to 1944, and the Idaho lookout list to 1943.[^1][^3] A Forest Lookouts page describes the building as a two-story log lookout with an L-5 cab.[^4]

The Idaho list dates the lookout's abandonment to the 1950s, and firelookout.com says it was last used in that decade.[^3][^2] A 1982 history by Bing Young, quoted on a climbing guide site, says the lookout fell out of use ahead of many of the Depression-era lookouts, and that the road was never finished to Duck Creek Point.[^5] The same guide, quoting that history, reports a 1975 forest inventory that recommended the building be burned.[^5]

The register notes a new roof in 2004 and says the lookout was no longer staffed by its 2010 visit.[^1] The register added it on July 22, 2010, after a July 9, 2010 visit that found the nearest paved road 60 miles away, a 1.7-mile hike, and a fairly rough road to the lookout that is best taken by ATV or dirt bike.[^1]

Today the last visit report is from 2010, so the present condition is unknown. The 1982 history and the 2010 visit also appear to disagree on whether a road reaches the lookout.[^5][^1] The sources also disagree on the forest name: the register says Salmon-Challis, while firelookout.com and the Idaho list say Salmon National Forest.[^1][^2][^3] Check the forest before you plan a trip, and do not assume the cab is open to climbing.
