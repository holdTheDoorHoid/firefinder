Hull Mountain rises to about 2,100 metres in the Mendocino National Forest, in Mendocino County.[^1] A lookout station was on the summit by 1940.[^2] A newspaper item from that July described a glass-enclosed lookout station at about 7,000 feet.[^2] The cabin the register records was built in 1974 and replaced an earlier R1 ground cabin.[^1] It was a single-story hexagonal building with flying-buttress supports for its overhang, designed by a Forest Service architect, Robert Sandusky.[^1]

In September 1940 a visitor described lookout Charles Henry, whose wind charger ran his radio and lit his house.[^2] That November, Ranger Floyd Long's annual report described a lightning fire on the north side of San Hedrin that crews could not find.[^2] The lookout could still see the fire.[^2] Once Suppression Foreman Foster built a smoke signal, the lookout picked it up, and radio talks with the lookout gave him the direction and distance to the fire.[^2]

The register says the 1974 cabin was destroyed in a fire on a date it does not give.[^1]

No source read describes what remains at the summit today, or whether a route to it is open.[^1][^2] The register lists a 2009 photo captioned "Lookout Site," but its text does not say what that photo shows.[^1]
