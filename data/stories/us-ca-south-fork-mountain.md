The South Fork Mountain lookout site is in Shasta County, in rugged country north of the Whiskeytown-Shasta-Trinity National Recreation Area, at about 3,450 feet (1,052 meters).[^1] The register gives 1982 as the year it was constructed.[^1]

The design came from the state forestry service. The register credits CDF engineer Mike Plesha with the tower design, Plan No. 1559-2A.[^1] Its legs are hollow steel columns first meant for atomic bomb test towers built above ground. When those tests stopped, the General Services Administration sold off the leftover material, and this was the third CDF tower built from it.[^1] The cab is a Plesha design, the 732-6A.[^1]

The 1982 date may not be the first word on the site. A 1977 item from The Intermountain News, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says a lookout on the south fork, north of Whiskeytown Lake, would be staffed starting Saturday.[^2] The sources we read do not say whether an earlier lookout stood on this peak, or whether the 1977 item meant another one.

The later record is thinner. In 1985 a CAL FIRE notice of exemption concerns renewing a permit that let Pacific Bell use an access road to the site.[^3] A 1994 notice describes replacing a 60-foot wooden, four-pole antenna tower there.[^4] A 2012 Northern California chapter report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists South Fork as no longer existing and as a repeater site.[^5] The association's California list still marks the tower Standing*. Its key defines that asterisk as a tower whose cab is no longer in place.[^6]

The register's own text does not agree with itself. It says a repeater antenna is attached to the cab, but also that the cabin and storage structure went at an unknown date, leaving only the tower.[^1] The list's cab reading adds to the conflict.[^6] We could not settle which is current.

Today the register says the public can reach the site, which lies 6.7 miles from paved road, though that access note dates from 1991.[^1] It also cautions that the radiation level is very high near the lookout catwalk.[^1] We advise against climbing the tower. Ask the Cal Fire Shasta-Trinity unit about the site before any visit.
