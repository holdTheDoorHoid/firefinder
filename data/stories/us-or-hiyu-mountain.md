Hiyu Mountain rises 1,500 feet directly above Bull Run Lake, in Clackamas County, Oregon.[^4][^1] In 1929 the Forest Service built a 20-foot wooden tower with an open platform on the summit.[^1][^2][^4] A roof was added later, and in 1933 an L-4 cab replaced the original structure.[^1][^2][^4] The new cab had enclosed living space for staff.[^4] When it was built, the site could only be reached by trail, and materials were brought in by packhorse.[^4]

A July 1936 Sunday Oregonian item reported that the lookout was to be manned.[^3] In October 1934, Oregon newspapers reported that a Forest Service employee had fallen about 20 feet from the tower and been taken to a hospital.[^3] A 1958 Hood River District historical report, quoted on Rex's page, says the lookout was reactivated in August.[^3]

The Forest Service started to wind down its lookout network in the early 1960s.[^4] Rex's page says this one was deactivated in 1964.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both say the tower was gone by 1967, and the WyEast Blog says the lookout structure was removed by then.[^1][^2][^4]

A 2016 WyEast Blog article says the summit road, whose main use has been logging, also gives access to radio antennas where the old lookout building once stood.[^4] The same article says the Forest Service has since officially closed the trail to the summit, as part of a campaign with the City of Portland Water Bureau to deny any public access to the Bull Run Reserve.[^4] This story does not recommend hiking there.[^4] No source we read is more recent than that 2016 article, so conditions may have changed.
