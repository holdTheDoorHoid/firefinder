Black Crater sits in the Deschutes National Forest, about ten miles west of Sisters, Oregon.[^2] The Bend Bulletin reported in March 1922 that a new lookout station was planned there for that season.[^3]

A standard lookout house was going up on Black Crater in September 1925, with J. F. Blanchard, the lookout, building it.[^3] Pack horses hauled the lumber about two and a half miles.[^3] The Register describes a frame cabin built that year.[^1] In the 1930s the frame cabin gave way to an L-4 cab, and the Register says that cab is now gone.[^1][^2]

Lookouts named in the newspapers include Lee Armstrong, who was relieved from the peak in September 1923; J. F. Blanchard; Edward Zane, named for 1928; and Dick Ayres, a University of Idaho forestry student, who staffed the lookout in 1930.[^3] The only report from the 1926 season that the Forest Lookouts page quotes was a false alarm.[^3] In 1932 the station was designated for precipitation recording and was to get a rain gauge.[^3]

The sources disagree slightly on the summit height. The Register gives 7,251 feet, while a 1936 Forest Service station description puts the lookout house at 7,260 feet on the summit.[^1][^3]

The Forest Service closed the station once lookouts had been set up on easier-to-reach buttes nearby. A 1953 Bend Bulletin item describes the old building as dismantled by then.[^3]

None of the sources we read describes what, if anything, is left on the summit today. The Forest Service lists a day-use trailhead for Black Crater Trail #4058. The trail enters the Three Sisters Wilderness a short distance past the trailhead, and day visitors need a wilderness permit.[^4] We found no agency statement on whether the old lookout site itself is open to visitors.
