Tumac Mountain is a cinder cone on a high plateau between White Pass and Bumping Lake, in Lewis County.[^1][^3] A camp was set up on the summit in 1930.[^1][^2][^3][^4] An L-4 cab went up in 1937.[^1][^2][^3] Forest Lookouts, citing Ray Kresek, gives 1938 for the cab instead.[^4]

The register, FireLookout.com and WillhiteWeb all say the cab was removed by 1968, though none gives an exact date.[^1][^2][^3]

Bob Baldwin staffed the lookout during the 1950 fire season.[^2][^4] In 1961 a Centralia College student named Joe Mills manned the lookout for the summer, according to a Daily Chronicle item from October 1961 that WillhiteWeb and Forest Lookouts both quote.[^3][^4] The item says the lookout received 179 visitors between July 15 and September 15.[^3][^4]

WillhiteWeb says the summit can be reached on foot. It describes a rough gravel access road and a route of about 3.75 miles.[^3] The route follows trail 44: 1.5 miles to the smaller Twin Lakes, then 2.25 miles to the summit.[^3] A small Forest Service campground sits at the trailhead.[^3] Severe washouts closed the Deep Creek Road from 2006 to 2013, according to WillhiteWeb.[^3]

WillhiteWeb calls the lookout the mountain's most notable feature, and its 2015 photo captions show lookout footings and an eyebolt on the summit.[^3]
