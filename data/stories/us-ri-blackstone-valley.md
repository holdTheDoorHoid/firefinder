The Blackstone Valley lookout stood on Durfee Hill, in Burrillville within Providence County, at about 805 feet (245 meters).[^1][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 65-foot steel tower there that was first operated in 1926.[^1] An earlier plan came from a January 1916 report by the state forestry commissioner, which proposed a lookout on Durfee Hill to watch over most of Providence County's woodland.[^3] The same report also mentions acquiring the Durfee Hill area as a state forest reserve.[^3]

The tower's time on the hill was short.[^1] The register says it ended operation at the end of the 1937 fire season.[^1] A 1937 state annual report, as quoted by a lookout history site, found that only four existing towers were worth keeping.[^2] It said a tower on Buck Hill in Burrillville would round out the coverage, and that the Blackstone Valley tower had been moved there.[^2]

The tower's later history is on Buck Hill.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 65-foot steel tower there, built in 1938 and administered by the state Department of Environmental Management.[^4] The sources do not explain how the 1937 move and the 1938 build year fit together.[^2][^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Buck Hill as a standing tower but does not list Durfee Hill.[^5] The register gives the Buck Hill cab as 8 by 8 feet and says the tower is the only one in Rhode Island with a catwalk.[^4] It also credits the tower with listening in on German radio broadcasts during the Second World War and relaying their movements to Allied forces landing at Normandy.[^4] We found no second source for that wartime account.[^4]

A 2017 visit report on the register records no public access to the tower.[^4] A 2016 visit report says the state stopped staffing its towers in the 1990s.[^4]

Today, the register lists Durfee Hill as a former lookout site. No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the hill.[^1][^5]
