Spout Springs Mountain, about 15 miles north-northwest of Elgin in Union County, was developed as a lookout in the 1920s.[^1][^2][^3] Early on, the site had a pole platform, 30 feet tall, and a tent camp.[^3] Sources disagree on when the 50-foot L-4 tower arrived. The register and firelookout.com both date it to 1935.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts quotes an August 1926 account in which a crew found a 50-foot tower already framed and had it raised in less than two hours.[^3]

A 1943 inspection, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, found the old tower too decayed to use safely.[^3] That same year, Mrs. Elsie Ralph, the lookout, packed out on a cow because her horses had gone and she had no car.[^3] Firelookout.com gives her name as Mrs. James Ralph and lists Bob Faust as a lookout in 1968.[^2]

In July 1950 a lightning strike started the season's first wildfire on the Walla Walla district.[^3] Lookout Dean McKenzie found the blaze about a quarter mile east of the lookout site and put it out.[^3]

Replacement was already being planned in 1946.[^3] A June item said a wooden tower at Spout Springs was to be replaced that summer.[^3] A February 1947 item described an 83-foot structure with a 14-by-14 lookout house.[^3] The history also quotes a 1944 note that the old tower was condemned and razed, with a caution that the date is questionable.[^3] A September 1947 item said the old tower had toppled the year before.[^3] The new 83-foot treated timber tower was completed in 1948.[^3][^1]

The lookout was staffed through 1990.[^1] That year a falling tree landed across the guy wires, and the tower was destroyed.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^5]

No source we found describes what remains at the summit, or whether the public may visit it. A Forest Service page titled Spout Springs describes a sno-park on Oregon 204, roughly three and a half miles east of Tollgate, with snowmobiling and parking.[^4] Permits are required from November 1 to April 15.[^4] The page does not describe the lookout.[^4]
