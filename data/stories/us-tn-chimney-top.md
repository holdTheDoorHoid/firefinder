The Tennessee State Division of Forestry finished a new fire lookout on a spur of Chimney Top Mountain in 1948.[^1] An August 1948 newspaper report, reprinted by the Forest Lookouts site, said the tower was built to help spot fires in parts of four counties and was given the state number ET-14.[^1] The register's page shows September 5, 1948 under the tower's name, but it does not say what that date marks.[^2]

The spur had no truck trail to the tower site, so the tower was taken apart and carried up in pieces.[^1] It went by truck as far as the trail allowed, then by team and sled for another mile, and the last three-quarters of a mile was done by hand.[^1] The newspaper called it a short wood tower, unlike other towers in this section of the state, and said the standard 60-foot steel tower was unnecessary here.[^1] It said the ground there sat at 3,018 feet and the tower rose 16 feet above it.[^1]

The article named Lookout Tillman Hensley as the person in charge.[^1] It said the towers were closed at the time and would reopen on October 15 for the fall fire season.[^1] The sources we read do not describe any later staffing, any fire the tower watched, or when it was last used.

The register added the tower on March 14, 2014, as US 994, TN 15, and gives the county as Hawkins.[^2] The 1948 article places the tower on the Greene and Hawkins county line, so the two sources disagree on the county.[^1][^2] The register lists the elevation as 2,960 feet, not the 3,018 feet in the article.[^1][^2] A Tennessee Landforms list of towers also marks the Chimneytop tower as standing.[^3]

We found no recent description of the tower's condition, public access or climbing status. Check with the Tennessee Division of Forestry before planning a visit.
