Pattymocus Butte is a low, rocky peak in Tehama County, in open cattle country about seven miles from the nearest paved road.[^1] It was already an active fire detection site by 1921, run cooperatively by the Forest Service and the state, and in 1950 the state took over running it alone.[^1] The station was first called Beegum Lookout.[^1]

In 1970 the buildings that stand there now replaced an earlier CCC lookout and its garage.[^1] The tower is the shortest steel K-brace tower in California, the register says, and its cab is an early version of a standard CDF design that the agency still uses in Region 2.[^1] The register also says neither structure has been changed since it was built, and it added the site to its list in October 2019.[^1]

In a 2016 photo note, the lookout was described as in good condition but not staffed, and CalFire was said to manage it even though it sits on National Forest land.[^2] No AC power lines reach the summit, and a storage building near the lookout appears to be used as a solar-powered radio repeater site.[^2] A 1952 photo of the outhouse at the lookout, taken by Vern F. Knedler, is in the public domain.[^3]

I found no public visiting information, so treat this as a Cal Fire site and do not climb the tower.
