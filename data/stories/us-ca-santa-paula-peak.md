Santa Paula Peak is in Ventura County.[^1] The lookout sat in the Santa Barbara National Forest, in what is now the southern part of the Los Padres.[^1][^2] In September 1929 the Lompoc Review reported that work was moving ahead on a new lookout station on Santa Paula Creek.[^1] The project was expected to cost about $6,000, and the station was meant to let foresters keep watch over the forest's southern section.[^1]

Craig Carey's 2011 post, citing a source named Brown, dates the lookout to 1930, though some sources say 1931.[^2] A reader's comment on that post describes a 4AR-type cab, 14 by 14 with a steeply pitched roof, the kind of design the Forest Service used in California until about 1932.[^2]

In September 1932 C.A. Kennison was the fire lookout.[^1] A newspaper report dated September 18, 1932 said the fire had forced him to leave the station.[^1] That fire was the Matilija Fire.[^2] Carey writes that flames surrounded the lookout, that a last-ditch backfire saved it, and that it only just escaped destruction.[^2]

How the lookout ended is not confirmed.[^2] Carey says early research suggests it was destroyed in the 1948 Wheeler Springs fire, and he still considers its fate unresolved.[^2] A 2019 trip report says the writer believes it burned in the Wheeler fire, but asks that the guess not be quoted.[^3]

The Fire Lookout index lists the tower as gone.[^4] A 2019 trip report describes a register box on the summit, along with some leftover metal from the old tower.[^3]

The route to the summit crosses private land.[^3] The main approach, through Timber Canyon in Fillmore, has locked gates, and the property owners share the codes with only a few people.[^2][^3] The East Fork trail is badly overgrown and has been damaged by washouts.[^3] Anyone wanting to go up needs permission first.[^3]
