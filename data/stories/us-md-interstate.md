The Interstate tower stands on the Maryland and Delaware line, in Caroline County, Maryland, and Sussex County, Delaware.[^1][^4] The 1931 state report describes it as lying along the Bridgeville-Denton highway, while the state's 2007 report places it at Route 404 where it meets the line.[^1][^4]

The two states built the tower in 1931 and owned and ran it together.[^1] A state report from that year says erection ran from July 29 to October 15, when the towers began operating.[^1] Each tower was steel with a wooden stairway up to a seven-foot cab, the upper half of it glass.[^1] The report gives the height as 100 feet to the cab floor and 110 feet overall.[^1]

In 1937 the two states agreed to reconstruct and heighten the tower, adding new piers and a 20-foot bottom extension.[^1] According to the 1938 state report, Maryland furnished the materials, and Delaware's labor came through the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] After the 1938 rebuild the tower stood 120 feet to the cab floor and 130 feet overall, and the cab gained insulation in its side walls.[^1]

A Denton Journal item of October 28, 1933 announced Emory Williamson as the new towerman.[^1] A 1939 account names him again, spelled Emery.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page names no other lookout for the tower.[^1]

Delaware's list says its five towers went to the State Police and were all taken down between 2002 and 2006.[^2] It lists the Interstate tower separately, as standing.[^2] The Maryland list also marks it standing.[^3] A Maryland Department of Natural Resources report from April 2007 describes the tower as present, 120 feet tall and in fair condition.[^4] The report also notes that some tower deeds, including this one, carry a clause returning the land to the original owners' heirs if forestry use ends.[^4] We found no later condition report, so the tower's state today is unverified.

The 2007 report says the tower fronts on Route 404.[^4] We found no visiting rules, so ask the state forest service before going near it.[^4]
