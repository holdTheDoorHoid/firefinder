The Hunter lookout stood in Carter County, Missouri, on a 40-acre towersite that the Missouri Conservation Commission acquired in fiscal 1947–48.[^1] By September 1948 the site had a towerman. A local paper reported that John D. (Turley) Webb, the Hunter towerman, was attending a week of fire-control school at Lake of the Ozarks.[^1]

Sources disagree on the tower's age. A 1974 award notice quoted by the Forest Lookouts history says Joe L. Bounds began as a permanent towerman on February 1, 1949, at the "newly constructed" Hunter lookout tower.[^1] The same history dates the erection of a 100-foot (30.5 m) steel tower to 1962.[^1]

In March 1951, the Chillicothe Constitution-Tribune reported that a district forester said some fires were still burning but all were under control, and that smoke was still being reported from the Hunter fire tower.[^1]

In February 2023 the tower was sold and moved off the site.[^1]

No source I could open describes what, if anything, remains on the ground, or whether the site is open to visitors.
