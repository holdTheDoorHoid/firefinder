Stanley Peak sits in Lincoln County, Montana, on the Kootenai National Forest, about 12 miles south-southwest of Troy.[^1] Firelookout.com gives its elevation as 6,600 feet.[^1] The Forest Service's list of local landmark names says the peak and Stanley Creek are named for Foster Stanley, who was reported as the first man to move his family to Lake City, a railroad town, in 1891.[^3] He took up a homestead on the north shore of Bull Lake in 1892.[^3]

Firelookout.com dates the Stanley Peak cabin to 1934.[^1] The cabin sat near the northeast end of a ridge two miles long.[^1]

Firelookout.com says the cabin was abandoned in the 1940s.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a ground-type lookout that is gone.[^2]

No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit it.[^1][^2]
