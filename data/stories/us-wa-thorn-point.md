The Thorn Point lookout stood in Columbia County, Washington, a few miles from Dayton.[^2][^4] Firelookout.com puts it four miles east of town, while a 1963 survey description puts its station five miles east-southeast.[^2][^4]

The first lookout here was an L-4 tower in the 1940s.[^1][^3] Sources disagree on when the replacement went up. The register and Firelookout.com date a 40-foot wooden tower built by the Washington Department of Natural Resources to about 1960. WillhiteWeb says around 1962, when a state biennial report called it a replacement.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

In 1963 a survey crew set a station on top of the tower and described it as a ten-foot square wood house on a four-legged wooden tower, about 75 feet overall.[^3][^4] That height does not match the 40-foot figure, and none of the pages we read explains the gap.[^1][^2]

In March 1970 a newspaper reported that the station had been broken into three times. The article listed damage to the structure and said the value of articles was estimated at $460. It placed the station near Dayton in the Umatilla National Forest and said it was maintained cooperatively with the Washington State Department of Natural Resources.[^3]

The tower was removed in 1996.[^1][^2][^3]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
