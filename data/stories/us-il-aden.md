An April 1, 1954 report from the Mount Vernon Register-News, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, puts the Aden fire tower about eight miles north of McLeansboro in Hamilton County. The Illinois Division of Forestry ran it.[^1] In September 1953 Florence Musgrave of Aden was appointed the tower's lookout. The 1954 report says she is the first woman the Division has ever had as a fire-tower lookout, and that she had been on duty at the tower daily through the fire season since then.[^1]

A National Geodetic Survey station record, described by the Coast and Geodetic Survey in 1961, says the tower stood on four steel legs under a metal cabin, about 110 feet high to the cabin top.[^1] The same record puts the station about 3.5 miles west of Aden. A 1980 sale notice gives the former tower site as one mile west of Aden on Route 242, so the two sources disagree on the distance.[^1]

In August 1975 the Southern Illinoisan carried a notice inviting bids to demolish the lookout.[^1] In 1980 the former tower site, about three acres, was offered for sale by sealed bid, with a minimum of $4,900.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site marks the tower as removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as gone.[^1][^2]

No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors. Bob Frakes, a historian of fire lookouts, says three Illinois towers still remain; the article does not list Aden among the towers still standing.[^3]
