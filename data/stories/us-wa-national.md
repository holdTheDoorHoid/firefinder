The National Lookout stood in north central Lewis County, at 3,604 feet.[^1][^3] It sat about 18 miles west-southwest of Mount Rainier.[^2] The register lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as its administrator.[^1]

The first tower was a 60-foot wooden structure with an 8-by-8-foot cab, built in 1936 by Civilian Conservation Corps crews.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

During World War II the site was an aircraft warning post.[^4] It was activated on August 14, 1942, and deactivated on July 1, 1943.[^3][^4] Willhite says it was manned around the clock during the war.[^4] Dave Anderson staffed the lookout in 1942.[^4] A 12-by-20-foot garage, paid for with Aircraft Warning Service funds, was kept for fire control after the war.[^4]

In 1965 a 40-foot wooden tower with a live-in R-6 cab replaced the 1936 lookout.[^1][^3][^4] A state report gives 1964 to 1966 for the replacement, so the sources differ slightly on the year.[^3] The old cab was exhibited at the Southwest Washington Fair in 1965.[^4] A newspaper of that year, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said it would go on display at Camp Six, a logging museum in Tacoma.[^3] Willhite could not say where the cab is today.[^4]

The register says the 1965 tower was removed in 1969.[^1] Firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^4]

Willhite sums it up bluntly: the town, the lookout and the view have all disappeared.[^4] Photos taken in 2016 and shared on the same page show footings beside the road, the remains of an outhouse, and bricks.[^4]

Willhite describes a route from Highway 706 east of Elbe. He expects the last road to be gated at its start, and from that gate the walk to the summit is about 4 miles.[^4] The hike is on state land, where visitors should not block gates.[^4]
