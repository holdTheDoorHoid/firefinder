Wild Horse Lookout is a ground cabin rather than a tall tower.[^1] It is an L-4 design on the Challis National Forest in Custer County, Idaho.[^1] Sources give its elevation as 9,556 feet or 9,546 feet, a gap of ten feet.[^1][^2][^3]

TopoZone says the Forest Service built the lookout in 1935 to help spot and manage forest fires.[^5] The Register, firelookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page agree on 1935.[^1][^2][^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 1936 instead.[^3]

Sources disagree on when the lookout stopped being manned.[^5][^1][^2][^4] TopoZone says it was manned until 1943.[^5] The Register, firelookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page date its abandonment to 1946.[^1][^2][^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts says it has no documented staffing history for the site.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in 1946.[^1][^3] In the 1970s the cabin was restored and reused as an outfitter's cabin.[^1][^3] The Register lists it as registered on November 27, 2016, and a June 2016 visit report on file there notes that the catwalk needed paint and repair.[^1] In an April 2022 post, Idaho Fire Lookouts described the cabin as being in fairly decent condition.[^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as a hike-in.[^3] TopoZone describes a road about 3.5 miles long that climbs 2,500 feet, has a 50-inch gate, and suits only high-clearance 4x4 vehicles, dirt bikes or hikers. The same site says the lookout is open to the public.[^5] The Register's visit report describes a long walk up an ATV or UTV road.[^1] The sources read mention no closure and no rental, though the cabin was once an outfitter's cabin.[^3][^5]
