Residents of Cazadero built and staffed a lookout on Red Oat Mountain in 1967.[^1] They judged that the county needed more detection, since it had just two state towers.[^1] In 1981 they moved the wooden tower to a better site on Pole Mountain.[^1][^3] As of 2013, the two-story tower had stood on the peak since 1981.[^2]

Staffing has depended on local money. A 2013 Press Democrat article reported that residents raised more than $40,000 a year to keep the peak staffed from June to the first October rains, with four part-time lookouts sharing the shifts.[^2] The register adds that the association funds the season and that CAL Fire helps with radios and daily support.[^1] A 2018 volunteer report says the tower was manned each year for 35 years, until 2018.[^4]

The land changed hands in 2014.[^5] The Elliott family chose to sell the peak, and the Sonoma Land Trust closed escrow on the 238-acre property.[^5][^3] Before the sale, the land trust had promised to let the lookout stay.[^2]

The old wooden tower did not last. A 2019 Press Democrat article said it had been taken out of service in 2017 after years of wear, and that a drive to raise an initial $100,000 for a steel replacement was under way.[^3] The 2018 report, however, says staffing ran until 2018, so the closing date is disputed.[^4] By July 2020 the land trust's newsletter said the tower was too dangerous to staff.[^10]

Work on the replacement began in April 2024, when the group posted that construction had started.[^7] By November 2024 it said the new building, made mainly of steel, was in its final stages.[^7][^8] The pages I could read do not say whether it is open to visitors yet, so check with the association before you plan around it.

Today the Cazadero Community Services District says the site is staffed, and the lookout group says its two 360-degree cameras cover about 25 miles.[^9][^6]

The Sonoma Land Trust owns the 238-acre Pole Mountain Preserve.[^5] The land trust gives the summit's height as 2,204 feet; the register gives 2,139 feet.[^5][^1] Hikers can walk the preserve, which opened to the public in September 2018. The route is a 15-mile roundtrip from the Jenner Headlands parking lot on Highway 1.[^5] The tower itself is different. The register says the site is private property, so general visits must be arranged through CAL Fire or the association.[^1] Please do not try to climb the cab. Ask first.
