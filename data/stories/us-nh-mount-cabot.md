Mount Cabot is a peak in Kilkenny, in Coos County, New Hampshire.[^1][^2] A fire tower stood on its summit ridge beginning in 1911, according to one hiking blog.[^3] The Eastern US Lookouts page gives other dates for the summit. It says a new and improved watch tower was built in the 1915-16 biennium, and it quotes a September 1924 state newsletter saying four new lookout towers were being erected, one of them on Mount Cabot.[^1] The sources do not say whether these were one tower rebuilt or a series of towers.

In 1939 the White Mountain National Forest took over three borderline towers from the state, one of them on Mount Cabot.[^1]

The tower did not last. Mountain Wandering says it was dynamited in 1965 as a training operation for U.S. Army Special Forces.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^2]

SummitPost describes old tower foundations in a small clearing at the southern end of the ridge.[^5] The Forest Service notes a former fire tower site nearby, at Mt Cabot Cabin on the Kilkenny Ridge Trail.[^4] Mountain Wandering says the trail meets the former horse route to the tower.[^3] The site is on White Mountain National Forest land.[^4]
