The Purcell Mountain lookout, also called Trails End, was a ground cab on a summit near Randle in Lewis County, Washington.[^1][^5] Sources describe it as a standard ground cabin built in 1933.[^3][^4]

The register gives the site an elevation of 5,442 feet, while a climbing guide gives the summit as 5,422 feet.[^1][^5] In July 1934 a photographer recorded only as J.D.R. took panoramic pictures from ten feet above the ground, on top of the cab.[^5][^6] The scans were made in 1997 from archived material, courtesy of the National Archives Pacific Northwest Region in Seattle.[^6]

The sources I read do not say who staffed the cab, for how long, or what fires it watched.

A 1946 survey description places a lookout house on the summit at that time, with a survey marker set eight feet southeast of the house's southeast corner.[^4] Sources agree the cab was abandoned in the 1950s.[^1][^3][^5] The register says it was removed in the 1960s, and a Washington lookouts listing says only "Removed."[^1][^4] Two other pages say it was destroyed in that decade.[^3][^5] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Washington list marks the site as gone.[^2]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today. The site lies within the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1] A climbing guide says the road is blocked near the west ridge at about 4,480 feet.[^5] The guide's northwest route is 1.5 miles one way, with about 1,000 feet of gain, and it notes that the summit trail is not maintained and offers little in the way of views.[^5] I found no closure notice, but check with the Gifford Pinchot National Forest before you go, since the road may not be passable.
