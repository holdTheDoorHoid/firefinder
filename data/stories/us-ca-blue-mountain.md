The Blue Mountain Fire Lookout sits on a sharp peak on a large, southwest-trending ridge north of the South Fork of the Mokelumne River, in Calaveras County.[^1] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator.[^1]

The register says the Forest Service leased one acre on the mountain in 1914 for a lookout and supply station, and that the site was already in use before 1908.[^1] The first permanent lookout went up in 1911, and its wood came from the first Calaveras Ranger Station.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps put up a second lookout in 1934.[^1]

The building standing today dates to 1966.[^1] The register says it replaced a CCC-era lookout, follows CDF plan 809RA (a variation of the 809R plans), and is California's sole single-story 809R tower.[^1] Cal Fire now operates it.[^1] An ALERTCalifornia post says the lookout replaced an original "1966 CCC era structure."[^2] That conflicts with the register's 1934 date for the CCC lookout.[^1][^2] I have followed the register.

Old newspaper items, quoted on a Forest Lookouts page, show the lookout at work.[^3] In a 1922 district newsletter, a camper who lit a small brush pile to test the lookout reported that six minutes later the lookout called up to say a small smoke was rising near his camp.[^3] In October 1927 a lookout named J. Turner saw smoke from two fires that a woman and five children had started after getting lost, and sent a guard who found them.[^3]

The register rates the building as excellent and says it has seen few changes since it went up.[^1] It also says the station is 16 miles from paved road and that access is restricted, though that note is marked 10/91.[^1] CAL FIRE's own lookout fact sheet lists Blue Mountain and says its lookouts may be staffed by volunteers or seasonal personnel, usually only during peak fire season. I found no staffing record for this one.[^4] The fact sheet also says the agency does not rent out its lookouts.[^4] ALERTCalifornia also runs two cameras at the site.[^2]
