The Dolly Sods (Plains) tower stood in Grant County, West Virginia, on the Monongahela National Forest, not far from where the Dolly Sods Picnic Area is today.[^1][^3] It was a 30-foot steel tower, finished in 1931 at a cost of $2,019.50.[^1][^2] The Forest was adding towers in those years to help catch wildfires early, and this was one of four built between 1928 and 1931.[^3]

Both the Plains register page and the Forest Lookouts page record 1943 as the year the tower was moved.[^1][^2] In that year the Army paid to have it taken down and moved several miles north to Bell Knob, where it helped spot fires on the Army's live-fire range, land that is now Dolly Sods Wilderness.[^3] One travel article says the move went south instead.[^6] The coordinates on the two registers put Bell Knob slightly north of the Plains spot, which agrees with the Forest Service account.[^1][^4][^3]

Bell Knob's own register page says the tower was first built along the Rohrbaugh Plains Trail.[^4]

Today the Plains spot is listed as gone.[^5] The Register's Plains page carries photographs of footings at the site, captioned September 2008.[^1] No source we read describes what is there now, or whether the ground is open to visitors.[^1]

The tower itself still stands at Bell Knob on the Allegheny Front.[^4][^5] Part of the wooden cab came down before October 2015, but the platform, steel legs and stairs are still there.[^4] A 2020 visit report found the stairs passable only for the first flight and the top still locked, so we do not encourage anyone to climb it.[^4]
