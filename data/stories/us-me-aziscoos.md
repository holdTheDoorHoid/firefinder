Aziscoos Mountain, in Lincoln Plantation in Oxford County, got a lookout in 1910, when the Maine Forestry District established one with a wood tower.[^1][^2]

A 1918 panoramic map by the Maine Forestry Service is centered on the Aziscoos fire tower, so the tower was standing by then.[^3]

A 1917 report describes a ten-foot wooden tower and a home camp, 12 by 14 feet inside, for the watchman about half a mile down the trail from the summit.[^2] The same report says a lookout cabin that had been carried as far as Errol, New Hampshire, two years earlier was being moved up to the summit, and the foreman had not yet reported whether the job was fully completed.[^2]

In 1919 the tower site was moved and a new steel tower went up.[^2]

The sources do not agree on its height. A 1919 district report gives about twenty-seven feet. A Daily Kennebec Journal item from the same year gives 36 feet and places the mountain in Grafton, while the 1920 inventory gives 24 feet.[^2]

A Maine Memory photo record of the watchman's camp says the watchman worked from the tower in daytime and slept in a cabin farther down the mountain.[^4] The record's title dates the photo to about 1929, but its creation date is given as about 1920.[^4]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the tower was removed in 2004.[^1] No other source we read gives a removal date.

The Maine list gives coordinates for the mountain and marks the tower as gone.[^5] We found no source that describes what remains on the summit now, or whether the site is open to visitors.
