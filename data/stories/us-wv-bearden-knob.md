A tower went up on Bearden Knob in Tucker County in 1943, on the Cheat Ranger District of the Monongahela National Forest.[^1][^2] It replaced an older lookout on nearby Canaan Mountain, a wood-framed cabin built atop a large rock in 1924.[^3] That cabin was abandoned in 1943 for the new tower, two miles away, and was dismantled in 1946.[^3]

A Monongahela fire-tower history says the U.S. Army was training soldiers in the West Virginia Maneuver Area at the time, and the Forest added towers across the northern half of its land in 1943 and 1944.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history site says Army money paid for three temporary towers around 1943, with Bearden Knob among them.[^4] The same Forest history describes the Bearden tower as built entirely of wood timbers, as were a few others.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page marks the tower as Removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout index lists it as Tower Gone.[^4][^5] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site in September 2008.[^2]

The same Forest history says most of its towers were torn down, with only concrete piers and steps left as evidence they stood, but it does not say what became of the Bearden tower.[^1] No source I read describes what remains on the knob today, or whether visitors can reach it.
