Edith Peak sits in the Lolo National Forest about 20 miles north-northwest of Missoula, and firelookout.com lists its county as Sanders/Missoula.[^1] Its lookout was a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, set about a mile south of the summit.[^1]

The build date is disputed.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com says 1935.[^1] A Daily Missoulian list of new Lolo structures from September 1928 includes Edith Peak.[^2] In April 1934 the Missoulian reported that materials for ten new towers had been ordered, with Edith Peak named as a Frenchtown-district site.[^2] A September 1935 item describes a man hurt by a splinter while cutting wood at the Edith Peak lookout, which suggests the lookout was in place by then.[^2]

A Forest Service photograph dated August 1953 shows flames from a fire near Ninemile and is catalogued as the Edith Peak fire.[^4] A National Register study places Edith Peak on the Lolo's summer 1967 list, a plan covering 26 lookouts.[^3]

Firelookout.com says the tower was destroyed in 1972.[^1] Neither source describes what remains at Edith Peak today, and neither says whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
