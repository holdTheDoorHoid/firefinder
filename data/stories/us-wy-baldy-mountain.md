Baldy Mountain was one of six lookouts whose construction began in the 1930s in the northern part of the Teton National Forest.[^1] Its permanent station followed in 1942.[^2] A Jackson's Hole Courier report from that August named Baldy among the new permanent stations, and said the buildings had first been put together at the Jackson Forest Service property before being taken apart and hauled to the site by truck.[^2]

In July 1954 the Teton Forest, citing two weeks of hot, dry weather, said it would man the Monument Ridge and Munger Mountain lookouts. In the same notice it listed Baldy Mountain among the lookouts to be manned within the next week.[^2]

A lookout inventory kept by the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Baldy Mountain as a ground lookout that is gone. It gives no year for when the structure came down.[^3]

The forest has since been combined with its neighbor. The Teton and Bridger national forests were administratively consolidated on July 1, 1973.[^1] The site, about 26 miles east-northeast of Jackson Hole at 8,547 feet, is now listed under the Bridger-Teton National Forest.[^4]

A National Geodetic Survey data sheet still carries the name "Baldy Mountain Lookout," with no description attached.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains there today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
