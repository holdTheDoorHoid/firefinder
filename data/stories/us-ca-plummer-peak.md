Plummer Peak Lookout dates to 1937.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a Region 5 BC-301 plan lookout on a 20-foot steel tower.[^1] A 1961 station description of the summit gives the tower as 32 feet high, so the sources disagree on its height.[^1][^2]

Bill Duncan is the operator the sources name.[^1][^2] In 1940 he kept a cow on the peak, for fresh milk.[^1] A June 28, 1940 Trinity Forest News item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, reports that Duncan claims the record for the first cow ever milked on top of Plummer Peak.[^2]

A November 7, 1941 item said Duncan had been on Plummer and nearby Dubakella Mountain for 32 years.[^2] That would reach back before the 1937 tower, and the sources do not say whether an earlier lookout stood on the summit.[^2]

The register lists the Shasta-Trinity National Forest as administering agency. It was registered on October 29, 2019.[^1]

On April 12, 2022 the Forest Service signed a decision approving an AlertWildfire camera at the Plummer Peak communication site south of Hayfork.[^3] The decision places the camera on an existing American Tower Corporation tower there.[^3]

I could not confirm from the sources I read whether the lookout still stands or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^3]
