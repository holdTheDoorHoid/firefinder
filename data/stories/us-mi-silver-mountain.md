Silver Mountain is a massive outcrop of basalt in the Ottawa National Forest, about 36 miles south of Houghton.[^1] A wooden fire tower once stood on its summit, but the sources do not agree on when it went up.[^1][^2] A hiking guide places it somewhere between the 1920s and the early 1970s.[^1] A 1971 notice in the Wakefield News says it was erected in 1940 and stood 40 feet high.[^2] The L'Anse Sentinel, though, reported in April 1930 that Ejnar Uddenberg was to take charge of the Silver Mountain fire tower, which points to a tower already in use by then.[^2] A Michigan fire tower history describes the summit structure differently: a live-in lookout, 14 by 14 feet and held down by guy wires. It also says the author is aware of only one fire lookout used in Michigan.[^3]

Ejnar Uddenberg was the towerman there, and local papers name him at the tower again in 1931 and 1934.[^2] In November 1963 a trail and stairway up the mountain to the lookout tower was under construction.[^2] In October 1969 vandals shot out four windows and broke into the tower, and a district ranger noted that every fall brings damage from hunters.[^2]

In September 1971 the Ottawa National Forest sent out bids for the tower's removal, at a time when aircraft were making fire towers nearly obsolete.[^2] The hiking guide now describes the tower as long gone.[^1]

Concrete pedestals that once carried the tower can still be seen near the summit.[^1] The summit sits inside the national forest, and the guide says visitors are welcome to climb it.[^1] The hike from the parking lot to the summit is 0.9 miles and rated intermediate difficulty.[^1]
