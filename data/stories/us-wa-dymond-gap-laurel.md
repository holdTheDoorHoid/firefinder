Dymond Gap, also called Laurel and sometimes spelled Diamond, was about seven miles south-southwest of Glenwood in Klickitat County.[^2][^4] The state forestry division put up a 100-foot treated timber tower there in 1937.[^3][^4] Its cab measured 8 by 8 feet, and a house went with it.[^1][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1938 as the build year, so the sources do not agree.[^1]

Newspaper items name some of the people who staffed it. Lloyd Caryl of Trout Lake was set to be the lookout in 1940, and Donna Kuhnhausen was on duty in May 1955.[^3][^4] In 1967 a newspaper item reported that a newlywed couple was now at home on the lookout in Glenwood.[^3]

The tower came down in 1978.[^1][^3][^4] The register says it was removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2] The house was moved to private property near Glenwood, where a local landowner has it.[^2][^4] Willhite reports that in 2021 the owner was preparing to move it to his son's property, also in the valley.[^4]

None of the four pages describes what, if anything, is left of the tower at the site today.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Willhite says most vehicles can handle the road if the driver goes slowly.[^4] The first half mile crosses private land, where the signs say no trespassing, which he reads as applying only off the roadway.[^4] The land beyond belongs to the Department of Natural Resources, and he was not certain whether a recreation easement covers the route.[^4]
