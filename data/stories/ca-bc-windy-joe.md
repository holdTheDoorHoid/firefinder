The lookout occupies a subalpine clearing on the summit of Windy Joe Mountain, inside E.C. Manning Provincial Park in southwestern British Columbia.[^2] Its elevation is 1,817 metres (5,961 feet).[^1]

A hiking blog dates the building to the 1950s.[^3] The register describes a single wood-frame lookout with glass-walled observation rooms on two levels. It says those rooms were essential to the building's original purpose.[^2] The register links it to the BC Forest Service's fire watch in the Cascade Range, part of a forest-management goal of limiting the spread of natural and man-made fires.[^2] Around the mid-20th century, trees were cut back so the observation rooms overlooked the surrounding mountain slopes.[^2]

The name refers to Joseph Hilton, one of the park's first rangers. He held the trapping rights for an area that includes the park, and he passed them along.[^2]

At one time the lookout was both a working lookout and a public interpretive site.[^2] A 2004 visit report records it as not staffed.[^1]

The register says it has been decommissioned and is now a welcome hut for hikers and skiers.[^2]

A 2004 visit report judged the tower fully accessible to visitors and in sound condition.[^1] The same report places the trailhead at the Manning Park horse ranch behind Manning Park Lodge, and gives the round trip as 14.6 km (9.1 miles), which it calls difficult.[^1] A hiking blog starts at the Pacific Crest Trail trailhead on Gibson Pass Road and puts the route at 15 km.[^3]
