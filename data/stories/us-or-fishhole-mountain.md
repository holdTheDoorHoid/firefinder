Fishhole Mountain rises to 7,069 feet in Lake County, on the Fremont National Forest, about 24 miles west-northwest of Lakeview.[^1][^2]

In September 1933 a side camp was building a road and telephone line to the summit.[^3] By October the crew reported that the road could reach to within a quarter mile of the top.[^3]

The lookout tower was completed on October 10, 1938, by Civilian Conservation Corps men from the camp at Bly.[^3] The cost was $2,546.14.[^3] The register says the CCC built it in 1939,[^1] and a 1959 inspection says the tower and house were constructed in 1940.[^3] We could not settle the year. The register calls the tower a 54-foot wooden L-4 tower.[^1] The 1959 inspection describes a 54-foot treated timber CT-2 tower with a 14-by-14 house.[^3] One site also lists an earlier 119-foot tower from 1934,[^2] which the Forest Lookouts page does not mention.[^3]

In 1937 the lookout was an emergency station, occupied only in extremely hot weather.[^3] Two CCC enrollees from the Bly camp were posted there.[^3] In August 1946 a lightning fire gutted 250 to 300 acres east of Bly, and the Fishhole lookout spotted it.[^3] In July 1947 David Marshall was moving to Fishhole, which had no telephone line, so he was to get one of the new frequency-modulated radios.[^3] Roy Laird took over from him that September.[^3]

A 1979 newspaper profile describes Francis E. "Fishhole Al" Alvord, a lookout on the Fremont since 1951 and at Fishhole since 1964.[^3] He lived in one-room quarters 55 feet off the ground. Lookout tenders brought water to the top, and his own pulley system hauled it up the 68 steps to the tower.[^3]

In 1973 a newspaper reported that Fishhole was tentatively scheduled to close beginning in 1974, but the 1979 profile shows a lookout still there.[^3] In August 1977 the Lost Fire burned the Fishhole Guard Station, and a Forest Service spokesman said a fire lookout post had to be abandoned.[^3] Firelookout.com says the 54-foot tower burned in that fire,[^2] but the 1979 profile, which describes a manned tower, does not support that.[^3]

A 1983 letter from the Bly District Ranger said the lookout reported more fires than any other on the forest, and asked for a structural survey.[^3] A report dated August 12 that year found the stairs and deck unsafe and recommended keeping people off the tower.[^3]

In 1984 a fire crew used a small Cat to pull the tower down, then burned what was left.[^3] The register gives 1988 for the removal.[^1] We could not reconcile the two dates. No source we read describes the summit today. A 1967 memo said two of the four footings were badly spalled but could be repaired.[^3] No later source we read says whether any footings remain.
