Carter Mountain sits in McCurtain County, in Oklahoma's southeastern corner.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register (NHLR) calls its tower the second lookout in the state, and it was registered on September 16, 1997.[^1]

The first tower here was made of wood.[^1][^2] The NHLR dates it to 1927,[^1] but the Forest Lookouts history is less sure and marks that year with a question mark.[^2] By 1929 the Oklahoma Forest Service maintained a 55-foot steel tower near Carter Mountain, and 2,100 people from 20 states and Canada registered there that year.[^2]

A watchman named Buddy Baker spent four years on the mountain.[^2] In June 1930 he spotted smoke about two miles from his tower, reported it at the forest office in Broken Bow, and ran to the scene, where he put out the flames while a sleeping three-year-old's bedding was only starting to smoke.[^2]

In 1936 a 100-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot steel cab went up.[^1][^2] The two accounts do not agree on the order of events. The NHLR says that tower replaced the original wooden one, but the 1930 newspaper report describes a 55-foot steel tower already in use in 1929.[^1][^2]

The register says the 1936 tower is still in service, and that Oklahoma Forestry Services administers it.[^1] A 2018 state press release mentioned that campers at a teen forestry camp visit the Carter Mountain Fire Tower.[^3] I found no published rules for visiting. Because it is still a working lookout,[^1] check with Oklahoma Forestry Services before you go, and do not climb it without permission.
