Pequawket is the lookout on the summit of Kearsarge North, a White Mountain peak about four miles northeast of North Conway in Carroll County.[^4][^1] Wikipedia gives the summit's elevation as 3,268 feet.[^4]

An inn once stood on the summit, but storms destroyed it twice. In 1909 the Appalachian Mountain Club let the state forestry commission use the ruined hotel as a fire lookout.[^4]

The register says the tower went into service in 1913 and was one of the earliest detection sites in the state.[^1] Wikipedia also dates the tower to 1913.[^4]

The register describes a 28-foot steel tower that first had a standard New Hampshire wooden cab. That cab was remodeled in 1953 and again in 1977, and an enlarged cab was built in 2000.[^1]

The peak was renamed Kearsarge North in 1957.[^4] The state and the U.S. Forest Service staffed the tower until 1960.[^4]

In late 1948 the Forest Service hired a helicopter to carry 27,000 pounds of building materials to the summit for a lookout tower, a newspaper reported at the time.[^2] On December 5, 1948, the helicopter carrying those materials reached the summit. As the pilot was about to land, its engine lost all power while the aircraft was about 20 feet up, and it crashed. The pilot jumped clear.[^5] None of these sources says whether that construction replaced the 1913 tower or added to it.[^1][^2][^4][^5]

In late August and early September 2012 the Saco Ranger District of the White Mountain National Forest did some extensive repairs on the tower.[^3]

The Forest Service now presents the summit as a tower observation site. Hikers can reach the summit by the 3.1-mile Kearsarge North Trail from a parking lot on Hurricane Mountain Road, or by the 5.1-mile Weeks Brook Trail from South Chatham Road.[^6] The tower is fully enclosed in glass windows, and the site has no restrooms and no drinking water.[^6] Wikipedia says the tower is open to visitors to the summit.[^4]

Whether anyone staffs the tower today is unclear. The 2025 chapter report lists "Kearsarge" among the towers staffed that year, but does not say which one it meant.[^7] Wikipedia notes that the peak is sometimes called Mount Kearsarge, a name officially assigned to a mountain in Merrimack County, so that line cannot confirm staffing here.[^4][^7]
