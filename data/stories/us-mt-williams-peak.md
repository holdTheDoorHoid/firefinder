The peak was developed in 1934 with a 30-foot pole tower and an L-4 cab.[^2] The lookout that stands there now is a 53-foot timber tower with an R-6 flat cab.[^2][^4] The cab was first an exhibit at the 1967 World Scout Jamboree in Idaho.[^1] It was erected at Little Joe near St. Regis in 1969, then moved to Williams Peak in 1977, where it replaced the 1934 tower.[^1]

The sources do not agree on the build year.[^1][^2] The register's detail page lists 1977 as the year built, even though its own description puts the erection at Little Joe in 1969.[^1] Firelookout.com says the tower was built at Little Joe in 1969 and moved to this site in 1977.[^2] A travel blog says the tower was hauled in from Idaho in 1977, without mentioning Little Joe.[^4]

Firelookout.com lists known lookout staff from 1953 through 1959, and says the tower has been unstaffed for years.[^2] The page does not say exactly when staffing ended.[^2]

The register recorded the lookout in March 2016.[^1] The land was once industrial timber land, and Montana Fish, Wildlife & Parks later acquired it.[^1] The tower still sits on state property.[^2]

Today the tower is closed to the public and fenced off.[^3][^4] It stands in Fish Creek State Park, which is open year round with hiking among its activities.[^1][^3] Hikers can still reach the summit of Williams Peak.[^4] One writer described a 3.4-mile round trip with over 1,200 feet of climbing, and rated it an easy hike.[^4] The writer found the views expansive, with logging roads visible all around.[^4] Please stay outside the fence and enjoy the view from the trail.
