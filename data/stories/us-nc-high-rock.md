The High Rock Tower went up on Healing Springs Mountain in Davidson County in 1954.[^1] The Wagner Construction Company of Salisbury built it, and blasting hard rock for the foundation made the job take much longer than expected.[^1] The steel tower stood 73 feet high and had a small glass-enclosed cab at the top.[^1] A newspaper report from October 1954 described it as the county's first tower for spotting forest fires.[^1]

The tower went into service before the work was finished, because fire conditions in the area were dangerous.[^1] Before the tower, spotters had worked for two years mainly from the top of the city's water tank, where a telephone had been installed.[^1] Inside the glass cab, one man was posted with binoculars and a two-way radio.[^1]

The index that carries the report lists the North Carolina Division of Forest Resources as the agency.[^1] The tower stood on property that belonged to the J.M. Daniel estate. The property was leased, and the lease also covered the stretch the road crosses and the land beneath a power line running to the tower.[^1]

No source I could read says what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1]

[^1]: North Carolina lookouts index, High Rock entry (quoting The Dispatch, October 12, 1954), Forest Lookouts, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/north-carolina.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
