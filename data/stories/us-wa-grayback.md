The lookout site on Grayback Mountain sits at 3,766 feet in Klickitat County.[^1][^2] An L-4 lookout cab went up there in 1945.[^1][^2][^3] The site is administered by the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR.[^1]

In 1950, Buck Yeley was the State Forestry Service lookout at Greyback Mountain.[^4] Dewey Hapgood took over the station in 1953.[^4] In 1956 a newspaper reported that Juanita Ward, a sophomore at Whitworth College, would spend her third summer as the lookout.[^4]

In the mid-1950s a somewhat flat area on the summit was used as an airstrip by air patrol pilot Bob Edling, and the cab was one of his lunch stops.[^1][^3]

A two-story DNR cab was built in 1972, and it was removed in 1996.[^1][^2][^3]

WillhiteWeb's notes disagree about whether a lookout stood on top in the 1970s and 1980s.[^3] One correspondent said there was none in 1973.[^3] A 1979 DNR survey made no mention of one, but a 1989 National Geodetic Survey crew did.[^3]

In an undated note, WillhiteWeb reports that a landowner behind a gate said all access has been restricted and new signs have been posted.[^3] WillhiteWeb gives the route as 5.5 miles from the lower gate and 4.3 miles from the upper gate.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.
