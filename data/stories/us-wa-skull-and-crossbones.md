The Skull and Crossbones lookout stood on a ridge at 6,500 feet, about 10 miles west-southwest of Loomis in Okanogan County, Washington.[^1][^2][^3] Both the registry and firelookout.com name the Okanogan National Forest, yet both also place the site in the Loomis State Forest.[^1][^2]

The lookout was a ground-mounted L-4 cab, not one set on a tower, built in the early 1930s.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb dates construction to 1932, when an August newspaper item reported that two men had finished building several lookout houses, including this one above Loomis.[^3] It went up here rather than on nearby Big Camp, which was higher, probably because of road access.[^3] The ridge was also reportedly the setting of a brief early-1900s clash between sheepherders and cattlemen.[^3]

Dick Loudon was the lookout in 1933. M.F. Gadberry held the post in 1934 and again in 1935.[^3][^4]

In 1953 the cab was sold and moved by helicopter to Horseshoe Basin.[^1][^2][^3] Emmit Smith then used the cab in connection with his grazing permit on National Forest land.[^5] The area was soon folded into the Pasayten Wilderness, and the permit continued in 1968.[^5] WillhiteWeb cannot say how long the cab stayed, whether it was demolished, or whether the Smith family moved it again.[^5]

At the original ridge, WillhiteWeb's undated visit photos show a wood and rock wall still standing, stove parts, and cans, one still holding tobacco.[^3] The Horseshoe Basin cab's fate is unknown, and WillhiteWeb marks it as needing a site visit.[^5]

Getting to the original site means roughly 20 miles of approach roads across the Loomis State Forest, and those roads keep changing and washing out.[^3] The road up the last stretch is abandoned and cannot be driven near the summit, so visitors walk the final part.[^3]
