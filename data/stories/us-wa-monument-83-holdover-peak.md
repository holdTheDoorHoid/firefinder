Monument 83, also called Holdover Peak, sits on the US-Canada border in the Okanogan National Forest and the Pasayten Wilderness.[^1][^2][^3] The name marks its place on the border, 83 miles from the zero point at Peace Arch Park in Surrey, British Columbia.[^1]

The site's first use came in the 1920s, when a camp with a tree platform stood here.[^1] In 1930 a log cupola cabin went up.[^1] For a while it was counted as the northernmost lookout in the US, until surveys placed it a few feet inside Canada.[^1][^2] It is now British Columbia's southernmost lookout.[^2]

In 1953 the US built a 30-foot treated timber L-4 tower on its side of the line.[^1][^2] That made the site the northernmost lookout in Washington and the United States again.[^1] TrailChick dates the tower to 1952, so the sources disagree on that year.[^3]

firelookout.com names two known staff members: Fred Vanderpool in 1929 and Holden White in 1952.[^2] The lookouts' last use came in the 1970s.[^1] The NHLR also records a stint as an Aircraft Warning Service site from July to September 1942.[^1] The sources read do not record a fire spotted from the site.[^1][^2][^3]

Both structures are described as still intact.[^1][^2] The site is a hike-in, reached by trail.[^2][^3] firelookout.com puts the trail at 10 miles from the Canadian side, or 30 miles from the US side across the Pasayten Wilderness.[^2] TrailChick calls it a good winter ski-touring spot if the trail is clear of fallen trees.[^3]

A carved wooden gravestone at the site bears an inscription naming Pasayten Pete, a pack mule said to have been well loved.[^1] The sources read do not give current trail conditions, so check with the Okanogan National Forest before planning a trip.[^1][^2][^3]
