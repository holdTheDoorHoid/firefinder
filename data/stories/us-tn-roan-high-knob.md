Roan High Knob sits on the Tennessee and North Carolina line.[^1] In 1933 the Civilian Conservation Corps erected a fire tower and a fire warden's cabin on Roan High Knob.[^1][^2] The tower did not prove very useful.[^2] WillhiteWeb says it was often above the clouds, which made it hard to use for spotting fires, and Wikipedia calls it ineffective and short-lived.[^1][^2]

The cabin lasted much longer.[^1][^3] A 1980 renovation turned the cabin into the Roan High Knob Shelter, used by Appalachian Trail hikers.[^1][^3] The Tennessee Eastman Hiking and Canoeing Club's wiki gives 1934 for the cabin and credits the Forest Service, so the sources differ by a year.[^1][^2][^3]

Tennessee's lookout register lists the tower at 6,285 feet, under the Cherokee National Forest, and marks it removed.[^6]

The shelter has been closed since 2024.[^4] The Appalachian Trail Conservancy closed it in September 2024 for structural concerns, and its September 2025 update says it remained closed with no timeline for reopening.[^4] Hikers are told to keep clear of the shelter area.[^4] A Forest Service project page lists a shelter repair project as completed, with a decision dated April 29, 2025, which does not match the trail update.[^4][^5]

The tower is gone, but WillhiteWeb's photos show lookout footings in the summit area.[^1][^6] The summit is reached by a blue-blazed side trail off the Appalachian Trail.[^1][^3] WillhiteWeb gives the detour as 0.2 mile and the wiki gives it as 0.1 mile.[^1][^3]
