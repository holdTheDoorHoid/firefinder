Baker Butte sits on the Mogollon Rim District of the Coconino National Forest.[^1] In January 1912 the Forest Service chose it as a primary lookout, with a man to be stationed there at all times and a telephone connection to the supervisor's office.[^2] A cabin was built for the lookout in 1915, and H.C. Johns reported for duty there as a lookout man in April of that year.[^2]

The register dates the first tower to 1921 and describes a six-by-six wooden cab atop a 20-foot metal tower, climbed by ladder.[^1] Forest Service letters complicate that picture. In late 1920 officials hoped to swap the old wooden tower for a steel one, and in August 1922 a tower was bought from a Wisconsin company, though the forest supervisor judged its material too light.[^2] The sources disagree on when the first tower went up, and this history cannot settle it.

In 1936 the Civilian Conservation Corps replaced the first tower with the present 12-by-12 live-in cab on a 30-foot Aermotor MC-24 tower.[^1] The register notes that the cab had no catwalk.[^1] A June 1937 exchange over the foundation included a memo saying the tower had not yet been completed, so the work seems to have run into 1937.[^2]

Communications and staffing both turn up in the records. A 1939 plan called for a 27-mile telephone line from the tower to the Long Valley ranger station, and a 1948 plan included radio sets for the Baker Butte station.[^2] A 1953 request for a heater noted the lookout was usually occupied from early spring until late fall or early winter.[^2] In November 1973 a lookout officer was snowed in and had to be rescued by rangers.[^2]

Today the tower is listed as standing.[^3] A steel catwalk was added in 2008, and the register recorded the lookout on March 15, 2006.[^2][^1] I found no current staffing report, rental listing or public visiting guidance for Baker Butte. The register says towers available for rent are marked on its listing pages, and this one carries no such mark.[^4][^1]
