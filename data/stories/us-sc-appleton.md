The Appleton tower first stood near the crossroads at Appleton, in Allendale County, South Carolina.[^1][^2] It is an 80-foot Aermotor LS-40 steel tower, with a cab measuring 7 by 7 feet.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1930 as the build year.[^1] The register's own description, though, places the original build in the late 1930s. The sources we read do not settle which is right.[^1]

Across the state, the towers were not normally staffed after dark.[^3] Starting in the 1960s, women filled most operator posts, and their husbands often worked as firefighters.[^3]

The Forestry Commission shut down its lookout network on October 1, 1993.[^3] According to the register, the state sold the tower in the late 1990s, and it was then relocated roughly three miles away and rebuilt.[^1] Forest Lookouts gives the same sequence as a private sale followed by a move to a private site.[^2] That page dates the sale only to 199??.[^2]

The register added the tower on February 24, 2013, and includes an aerial view of it from that year.[^1]

We could not confirm what the tower looks like today. The register shows it rebuilt at its new site, but no source we read gives its current condition.[^1] The tower is at a private location, and we found no public access.[^2] Ask the owner before going near it, and do not go onto the property without permission.
