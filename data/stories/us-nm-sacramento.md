The Sacramento Lookout stands on the Sacramento Ranger District of the Lincoln National Forest, in Otero County, New Mexico.[^1]

The tower that stood here before the current one was an Aermotor LX-24, which the register and the 2020 report date to 1925.[^1][^3] A May 1924 newspaper describes a new sixty-foot steel fire tower being rushed up at the Sacramento site, and the sources do not say whether it is the same tower.[^2]

The LX-24 burned in April 1967. During a wind-whipped fire in the Danley Canyon area, officials evacuated the lookout and said the structure was probably destroyed.[^1][^2] A Forest Service ranger said the fire was probably set by a person, and the FBI and Forest Service are investigating.[^2]

The replacement went up in 1968, a 67-foot steel tower with a 14-by-14-foot CL100-series cab.[^1][^3] It was staffed from 1968 until 2010, when it became a standby lookout.[^3]

The 2020 state report said the interior would be slightly modified so the lookout could join a cabin rental program, and that it would be nominated for the National Register as an addendum.[^3]

Today the state index lists the tower as standing.[^4] We did not find a current rental listing, so whether the cabin can be rented now is unknown. The sources do not say whether the public can climb the tower. Check with the Sacramento Ranger District before planning a stay or a visit.
