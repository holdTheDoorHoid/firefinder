The Tire Mountain lookout was a platform on a tree that had been topped, with a three-sided shelter at ground level, on a flat summit in the Willamette National Forest.[^4] The site lies about seven miles north-northwest of Oakridge, in Lane County, Oregon, at 4,330 feet, or 1,320 meters.[^1][^2]

The register and firelookout.com date the site's development to the 1920s, when a tree crow's nest went up there.[^1][^2] A December 1925 newspaper item, quoted on the Oregon Lookouts page, said the peak would likely be named a fire lookout point once a forest supervisor and a ranger had climbed it.[^3] In 1932 a shelter was added.[^1][^2] The register calls it an Adirondack shelter and says a cabin followed in 1936.[^1] The Oregon Lookouts page describes a 30-foot lookout tree set up that year for emergency observation.[^3]

The register dates the lookout's destruction to the 1950s.[^1] The sources disagree about what is left. The register says the crow's nest is still standing, and firelookout.com says it is still there.[^1][^2] The Oregon Hikers field guide says nothing of the old lookout remains, and that a rusting garbage can now sits at the summit.[^5] We could not settle this from the sources we read.

The Forest Service describes the lookout in the past tense.[^4] A side trail at the four-mile mark of the 6.1-mile Tire Mountain Trail leads to the top, and hikers, riders on horseback, mountain bikers and motorcyclists may all use the trail.[^4] The same page says the area was affected by the Tire Mountain Fire and asks visitors to check current conditions with the local ranger station before going.[^4]
