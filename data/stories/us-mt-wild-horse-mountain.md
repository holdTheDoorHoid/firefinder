The lookout stood on Wild Horse Mountain in Sanders County, Montana, about seven miles west of St. Ignatius.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site also calls it Wild Horse (Redmans Ridge).[^2]

That site lists the lookout under the National Bison Range and the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service.[^2] In February 1934 a Missoulian item, quoted there, described a crew of 25 men on the Bison Range who were building roads and putting up three 60-foot steel towers as lookouts.[^2] The towers were to go at High Point, Wild Horse Mesa and Thumb-Nob.[^2] The firelookout.com record dates its steel tower to the 1930s and gives a height of 72 feet.[^1]

The two heights do not match.[^1][^2] The 1934 item describes towers still going up, so it may not give the finished size.[^2] The sources do not settle which figure is right.[^1][^2]

The firelookout.com record marks the tower as gone, and so does the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list.[^1][^3] Neither source says when it came down or why.[^1][^3]

The sources do not describe what, if anything, remains at the site, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
