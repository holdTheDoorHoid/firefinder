Bear Valley Lookout sits in Valley County, Idaho, inside the River of No Return Wilderness on the Boise National Forest.[^1][^2] The site is about 8,291 feet up, and firelookout.com places it 19 miles southeast of Warm Lake.[^2][^3]

The tower was put up in 1936.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register describes a 46-foot steel tower carrying a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and the Idaho Fire Lookouts and Forest Lookouts pages match that picture.[^1][^3][^4] firelookout.com also gives the 1936 date and the 46-foot height.[^2] A 1959 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, puts the tower at about 70 feet with an eight-foot-square cab on top, which conflicts with the 46-foot figure.[^4] The sources do not reconcile those figures.

The Idaho Fire Lookouts page lists a relief staffer, Linda Bellmer Bass, from 1974 to 1982.[^3] firelookout.com takes a different view and says the tower has not been used since the 1970s.[^2] The sources do not say when active use ended.[^2][^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register registered the lookout on September 25, 2011, as US 929, and the nomination came from Luke Channer. The register says the lookout is maintained for emergency use.[^1]

Today's status is the least clear part of the record. The Idaho Fire Lookouts page labels the site "Drive-Up Emergency Use," but the register places it inside a wilderness area, and the sources do not explain how both things hold.[^3][^1] None of the sources says whether the lookout can be rented or visited right now.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Check with the Boise National Forest before planning any trip, and do not go inside the cab unless the Forest Service says it is open.
