The Sunset Peak lookout stood in the Angeles National Forest, in the San Gabriel Mountains.[^1] It is gone now.[^1] The register dates the tower to 1927 and says it replaced an earlier site on Lookout Mountain, across the saddle to the north.[^1] A 1927 newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said the Old Baldy station would be replaced by a new 20-foot steel tower on Sunset Peak.[^2] Angeles Adventures instead says the older Old Baldy tower was moved to the summit that year.[^3] A March 1915 newspaper item had described a 50-foot tower for the crest, which does not match the 20 feet the register gives for the tower built.[^2][^1]

The register thinks the cab was an L-4 or BC-301 type on that tower. Angeles Adventures describes a 14-by-14-foot cab instead.[^1][^3] Charles E. Horn and his wife were stationed there in 1928.[^3] In October that year lightning struck the cabin.[^2] The Horns judged there was no point in staying, and that Friday night a second strike set off a fire that destroyed the cabin.[^2] Angeles Adventures says the steel tower survived and the lookout was quickly rebuilt.[^3]

A July 1933 item quoted by Forest Lookouts said an automobile had taken over from the pack trains that had long carried supplies up, and that a winter motorway had made the trip more convenient.[^2] Angeles Adventures dates the first motor supply run to 1932, when Glendora Ridge Road had just been finished.[^3]

In December 1938 a fire threatened the lookout from both sides.[^2] Sherman Foster, the lookout on duty, stayed at his post. He got a message through to the foreman of a nearby WPA camp, who moved all the men out without trouble.[^2]

The lookout was deactivated in 1974 because smog blocked the view.[^2] The register says the cab and tower were reportedly taken apart and sent to the Ontario air tanker base in 1974 or 1975, and that their final disposition is unknown.[^1] Angeles Adventures gives the span as 1927 to 1975 and lists the structure as demolished.[^3]

According to the register, only the tower footings and a house foundation are left.[^1] The register also says a suspected cistern is covered by corrugated sheeting.[^1] The usual route starts at Cow Canyon Saddle, along Glendora Ridge Road, and HikingGuy lists the hike at 7.5 miles.[^4] The same guide advises checking with the Angeles National Forest about closures before setting out.[^4]
