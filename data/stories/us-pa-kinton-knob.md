The Kinton Knob fire tower was built on the knob at the north end of Wills Mountain, about three miles west of Bedford, Pennsylvania.[^1][^2]

In April 1967 a contract went to a Louisville firm to erect a tower that would stand 2,722 feet above sea level, the highest point in Bedford County east of the Allegheny Ridge.[^1] By late October the work was finished. The Altoona Mirror described a new 80-foot fire tower of all-steel construction, which cost about $26,000.[^1] OpenStreetMap maps the tower at 779 metres, about 2,556 feet, which does not match the papers' figures.[^3][^1]

Mrs. George Armstrong of Schellsburg was picked to staff the tower until mid-December, when snowfall brings the fire risk down.[^1] Until phone lines reached the site, she was to keep in two-way radio contact with other fire towers and district headquarters.[^1]

In 1967 access was a dirt road, freshly cut from Route 30.[^1] In 1968 the Department of Forests and Waters helped fund work on a 2.5-mile road from J.C. Smith Reservoir above Lakewood to the tower. A 1968 newspaper item said the road was not officially a public road.[^1]

In June 1982 storms bent a communications tower on the knob in half.[^1] The fire tower and an AT&T tower nearby seem to have come through unharmed.[^1]

We found no source that says whether the fire tower still stands or whether the public may visit it. Wikipedia's short article on the mountain says its summit has an array of communication towers, and that views from the top are limited.[^2] It does not mention the fire tower.[^2] Check with the managing agency before going near the site, and do not climb any tower that is closed to the public.
