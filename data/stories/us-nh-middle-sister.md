Middle Sister is one of the Three Sisters, a trio of sub-peaks just below Mount Chocorua in Albany, New Hampshire.[^1][^2] Its fire lookout was a groundhouse, a design in which the cabin sat on the ground instead of on top of a tower.[^2]

The sources do not agree on when it began. A Chocorua Lake account says architect Charles Howard Walker built a stone fire tower on the peak in 1921, to replace the fire stand on Chocorua's summit.[^3] The Eastern US Lookouts page says lookout duties moved from Chocorua to this site in 1927, and ScenicNH also dates its operation from 1927.[^4][^2]

The lookout stopped its work in 1948.[^4][^2][^3] The Chocorua Lake account says the stone structure was later used to house radio repeater equipment.[^3] ScenicNH says a Forest Service radio repeater sat inside the foundation, and it notes that the repeater was removed by 2019.[^2]

ScenicNH and the Chocorua Lake account both report that the stone foundation is intact, with some leftover pieces of the lookout on site.[^2][^3] SectionHiker also puts old fire-tower remains at the summit.[^1] ScenicNH places the site along the Middle Sister Trail, and the Eastern US Lookouts page puts it in the White Mountain National Forest.[^2][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as gone.[^5]
