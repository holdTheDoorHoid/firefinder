Chopmist Fire Tower stands on Chopmist Hill in Scituate, Rhode Island, at about 730 feet above sea level.[^1][^2] The sources do not agree on when the current tower went up.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says a steel tower replaced an earlier wooden one in 1946, and that the current tower is the third on the site.[^1] A state forestry report from 1926 describes a new sixty-foot steel tower that replaced an old wooden one that year.[^2] An earlier report from 1918 listed a forty-foot wooden tower on the hill, and a 1925 report found it in very bad shape and urged a steel replacement.[^2]

For years the hill had a tower observer. The state marked the retirement of Mrs. Louise Ide in 1955, after 25 years as the Chopmist Hill Tower Observer.[^2] By 1964 the trees around the tower had grown tall enough to make the wardens' view harder. A state official said cutting them back would cost too much, because the trees stood on private land.[^2] The sources we read do not name any particular fire the tower spotted.

The Register lists the tower as US 1016, RI 5, registered on September 2, 2014. It says the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management administers the site.[^1]

Visit reports on the Register from 2015 and 2016 describe a tower in fair to good shape. They mention soft stairs, paint that needed doing, and an old state radio antenna still on the cab. One 2016 visitor said Chopmist Hill was among the two best-condition towers in Rhode Island.[^1]

In 2022 a group of ham radio operators and former or current emergency personnel re-roofed the tower. The work was part of a wider effort to restore many of Rhode Island's remaining fire towers.[^3]

The newest visit report we found is dated December 15, 2016. It says the cab door was locked behind a perimeter fence, and that no sign directed visitors to the tower.[^1] We could not find a current access rule, so ask the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management before going near it, and do not climb it.[^1]
