The Shannock Hill Fire Tower stands in Richmond, Rhode Island, in Washington County, at an elevation of 290 feet (88 m).[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management as its administrator.[^1]

The build date is disputed. The register's Built field gives 1968, but its own description says the 80-foot tower was built in 1986.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association and the Westerly Sun both say 1968.[^2][^4]

An earlier tower stood there first. The register says a tower had been on the site since 1926.[^1] A 1954–55 state report, quoted on Forest Lookouts, says the Shannock cab had deteriorated and asks for money for new cabs, so a cab was already on the hill in the mid-1950s, more than a decade before 1968.[^3]

The tower was manned in the 1930s and 1940s. The 1936 state report, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, says it was manned as early as March 27.[^3] In April 1946 a woman lookout, Mrs. Hannah Whaley, spotted a blaze on Orient Point, Long Island, New York, about 40 miles away.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association says the tower had not been used for fire detection since the early 2000s, and that its cab carried radio gear for municipal and state emergency networks.[^2]

On July 6, 2022, three teens set off fireworks at the abandoned tower, and flames gutted the cab.[^2] The register gives July 7 for the fire.[^1] The Westerly Sun reports that the fire damaged the cabin but not the steel supports.[^4]

The site is still the main emergency radio site for neighboring police and fire departments.[^4] Since the fire, Richmond police have relied on a loaned state mobile tower, which the Westerly Sun says will not work as a permanent solution.[^4] In February 2025 the town said a new 160-foot steel tower would go on the 5.1-acre site where the old one stood, inside a fence.[^5]

The fate of the old tower is disputed. A Nov. 4 Richmond Town Council vote asked staff to draft a letter supporting restoration of the historic tower. The article gives no year, but its mention of a federal shutdown points to late 2025.[^4] A February 2025 community association article says the new tower will replace the original, while the Westerly Sun says the old tower's historical value will be kept and that the Historical Society first wanted it torn down.[^4][^5]

The Westerly Sun says the planned compound is meant to stop people from getting in by simply climbing a fence.[^4] A 2016 visit report on the register called the tower possibly the worst-condition one its visitor had seen, and said the fence around its base was basically for show.[^1] Do not climb the tower or the fence.
