Pickett Peak Lookout stands in Trinity County, California, at about 5,180 feet, according to the Register.[^1] The sources disagree on the forest. The Register puts it in the Shasta-Trinity National Forest, while Peakbagging places it on the boundary of the Six Rivers and Trinity forests and gives its elevation as 5,778 feet.[^1][^3]

The Register dates use of the peak as a lookout back to at least 1922.[^1] A district news item dated October 13, 1922, reported that the lookout noticed a fire that seemed to be spreading.[^2] A fireman sent to investigate found it nearing the Forest boundary and went to work on it.[^2] A winter storm collapsed a 60-foot steel tower here in 1933, and a newspaper of the time said it would probably be an almost total loss.[^1][^2]

The Register says the present cab is a 13-by-13 National Plan CL-100 on a 55-foot K-brace steel tower that went up in 1965.[^1] Peakbagging reports a footing dated 1972 and suggests the tower was built then, so the two dates conflict.[^3][^1]

The lookout was reopened with paid Forest Service staffing in 2013, after 20 years closed.[^4] That August, lightning fires were spotted and reported from it.[^4] The Register recorded it on October 30, 2019, as US 1369, CA 192.[^1]

An undated Peakbagging page says the lookout is in good condition but not staffed regularly.[^3] A 2023 hiking blog says it is no longer in use but still solid enough to visit.[^5] The blog reached it on foot from the Bailey Canyon Campground trailhead and found that road maps disagree: an older map shows pavement to the campground, while newer ones show high clearance.[^5] The Forest Service describes the upper trail as historic, built around the turn of the century to make the ridge top easier to reach for a fire lookout.[^6]

No official access rule for the cab turned up in the sources read. Anyone planning a visit should check with the Forest Service first and should not climb the tower.
