Black Ridge is in the Okanogan National Forest, about 17 miles west of Twisp, at an elevation of 7,552 feet.[^2][^1]

The lookout began as a camp.[^1][^4] In 1934 a camp with firefinders was built, with two stations about a quarter mile apart.[^1][^4] On September 20, 1934, panoramic photographs were taken at both stations.[^3][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register credits the photographs to the National Archives Pacific Northwest Region.[^1]

In 1935 an L-4 cab replaced the camp.[^1][^2][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register describes an L-4 as a 14-by-14-foot frame cab that could sit atop a tall firetower.[^5]

Willhite's page gives the build dates and little else.[^4] I found no account of who staffed the stations or what fires they spotted.

The cab's end is recorded two ways. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the L-4 was removed in 1952.[^1] Willhite reports that the cab was said to be removed that year.[^4] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed in 1952.[^2] The Weebly index lists the site as removed.[^3] None of these sources describes what, if anything, remains on the ground today, or how to reach the site.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
