Fingerboard Mountain sits in Orange County, inside Harriman State Park, north of Lake Tiorati.[^1][^2] In 1915 the park brought in a professional forester, and a wooden pole tower with a telephone line to the nearest patrol office was erected at the site.[^3]

A December 1927 newspaper item, quoted by the Eastern lookouts site, reported that one of three 60-foot steel towers was being built on the northeast summit.[^2] Wikipedia and WillhiteWeb both date the 60-foot tower to 1928.[^1][^3] Each describes a 60-foot Aermotor LS40 put up by the Palisades State Park Commission, one of three such towers that year.[^1][^3]

In the late 1960s, Ranger Tim Sullivan reported that the tower was manned regularly in the dry fall hunting season, so that fires left by careless hunters could be put out quickly.[^3]

Accounts differ on when the tower came down. Wikipedia says it stopped lookout work in the early 1970s and was then closed and removed.[^1] WillhiteWeb says it was closed through the 1970s and into the early 1980s and suffered vandalism.[^3] By the mid-1980s, it says, the tower had been taken apart, and its parts went to New Jersey's Department of Forestry to repair other Aermotor towers.[^3] The Eastern site lists the tower only as removed.[^2]

The tower is gone, and the FFLA's New York list marks it as "Tower Gone."[^4] The mountain is state park land, and the Appalachian Trail passes it.[^2][^3] WillhiteWeb describes a side road of about 0.2 mile from the trail, with a climb of about 100 feet.[^3] Its photos of the site include a caption reading "Glass and other stuff," but the text does not describe any footings or other remains, so we cannot say what is left today.[^3]
