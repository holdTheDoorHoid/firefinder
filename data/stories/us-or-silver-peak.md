Silver Peak is a ground-level lookout site in the Siskiyou National Forest, about 17 miles east of Gold Beach.[^1][^2][^5]

The sources disagree on the county. The register and firelookout.com place it in Josephine County.[^1][^2] A May 1940 newspaper item, quoted by a lookout history site, put the new lookout in Curry County's Agness district.[^3]

The register dates the L-4 cab to 1940.[^1] The history site says a one-mile spur trail reached the lookout that year, and that the L-4 lookout house was built in 1941.[^3]

In 1941 the lookout was manned for 100 days, with its reports going to the Agness Ranger Station.[^3]

In October 1962 the Columbus Day Storm damaged the lookout house, according to the history site.[^3]

The register says the cab was removed in 1968, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] The history site instead says it was destroyed in the Biscuit Fire in 2002.[^3] The fires that became the Biscuit Fire started on July 13, 2002, when lightning from storms over the Siskiyou Mountains ignited five separate fires.[^4] By early August they had grown together, and the Forest Service dubbed the combined blaze the Biscuit Fire.[^4] The sources do not agree on how the cab ended, and none explains the gap.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as ground gone.[^5] No source we read describes what remains there, or whether the public can reach it.
