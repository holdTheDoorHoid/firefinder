Brushy Bald is a mountain about 10 miles north-northeast of Gold Beach, in Curry County.[^1][^2]

In 1930 the State Forester was building a lookout house on the mountain. A newspaper item from that summer says material went up the slope on a "lizard" hauled by a mule.[^3] A 1934 item calls it a state forestry lookout station, while the register and firelookout.com name the Coos FPA as the agency.[^1][^2][^3]

In August 1933 a fire burned roughly 1,500 acres of brush, and backfires were needed to protect the lookout station.[^3] In June 1934 Mitchell Anderson and his wife moved from Pistol River to the station, where he was to work that year.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com date the 30-foot tower and cabin to 1937.[^1][^2] A September 1937 item says two men were out building the station.[^3] Kemnow's timeline lists the 30-foot tower as completed in 1938.[^3]

The station was activated on March 9, 1942, for the Roseburg Filter Center.[^3] A September 1942 report says a 20-by-22 portable building had been finished, and that it was sealed and double-walled.[^3] In May 1942 a newspaper item noted that Ruby Purdin was working with her husband at the station.[^3] In July 1952 the Coos Bay Times reported that Jean Sandine and Camille O'Toole were staffing the lookout near Gold Beach.[^3]

The 30-foot tower was destroyed in the Columbus Day storm on October 12, 1962.[^1][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the summit is open to visitors.
