Salamander Rock's steel lookout went up about 1935, near the summit of the rock on the northern edge of Frederick's city watershed.[^1] A March 1935 Frederick newspaper report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, said the state had approved a 100-foot, all-steel tower there and that a Civilian Conservation Corps crew would start work at once.[^2]

The state could not build until it owned the land.[^2] In 1935 the City of Frederick deeded about an acre near the rock to Maryland for a forest fire lookout.[^1][^2] An April 1935 report also said the state had first planned to take down the existing Hamburg tower and put up a new one there, but it settled on the Salamander site instead.[^2]

The finished tower stood 100 feet tall. Inside, a metal stair climbed to a small cab on top.[^1] When federal money was diverted to the war effort, the city took over weekend staffing of the tower to give the watershed some fire protection.[^1] In August 1949 a district forest supervisor said the tower had for a while been open as a precaution.[^2]

The Maryland Historical Trust found no sign that the tower had been altered to carry communications gear.[^1] In 1956 AT&T bought land to the north for a telecommunications tower, and in 1970 the tower was slated for removal.[^1] A Maryland Historical Trust eligibility review judged the site significant for its link to the government's fire-control effort, but not eligible for the National Register, because it lacks the physical features to show that history.[^1]

The steel tower is gone.[^1][^4] Four concrete footings remain, roughly 20 feet apart, hidden by undergrowth.[^1] East of the gravel road are the stone ruins of a one-story building that probably sheltered tower staff; it has no roof, windows or doors.[^1] The road runs north to a cell tower site.[^1] The Frederick News-Post says walkers who know the Salamander Trail have seen the ruins.[^3] A trail runs close by, but none of the sources read says whether the site is open to visitors.[^1]
