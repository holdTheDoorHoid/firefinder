Sleeping Beauty is a rock outcrop in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, in Skamania County, rising to 4,907 feet.[^1][^5]

Sources date the lookout to about 1930 or 1931. WillhiteWeb and Washington Forest Lookouts describe a gable-roofed lookout house from around 1930, while the Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1931.[^1][^3][^4]

Getting supplies up was the hard part. A January 1931 story in The Sunday Oregonian, quoted by WillhiteWeb, described packhorse loads that took 11 days to reach the base, then a 700-foot cable worked by a windlass to raise everything 350 feet up the rock.[^3] Ernie Childs, who worked as a packer for the Forest Service then, said the building came pre-cut from a Portland firm. Don Williams became the first lookout.[^3]

The lookout stood on a rock about 200 feet higher than the land around it.[^3] The trail climbs past handmade rock walls, some more than 10 feet high, and the Forest Service says the Civilian Conservation Corps did the rock work in the 1930s.[^3][^5]

The lookout was apparently still in use in 1966, and it came down a few years later.[^1][^3] The register and WillhiteWeb say it was removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed, so the sources differ on how it ended.[^1][^2][^3]

The Forest Service says the trail leads over bare rock to the old lookout site, and that eye bolts once used to anchor the lookout are still visible.[^5] WillhiteWeb's photo captions show footings and a foundation still in place.[^3]

The trail is open to hikers only and closed to motorized use and bicycles. The Forest Service lists it at 1.4 miles and very steep.[^5] WillhiteWeb gives 1.5 miles with 1,400 feet of climbing, and says any car can make the drive. Its directions run west about a mile from Trout Lake to Road 88, then north on that road for 7 miles.[^3]
