Pumice Butte, in Klamath County, once carried a 60-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2] The register dates the tower to 1933.[^2] A July 1931 Lake County Examiner item, quoted on a collection page, says the Forest Service maintains a lookout on the butte, two years before the register's 1933 date.[^2][^4] A 1932 entry on the same page records that lightning protection was installed on the tower.[^4] Firelookout.com names the Winema National Forest, while the collection page names the Fremont National Forest.[^3][^4]

In 1940 the station was manned for 90 days and sent its reports to the Walker Range Patrol at LaPine, with the telephone as its only link.[^4] A 1942 district memo put the height from ground to the cab floor at 61 feet 11 inches, a little above the register's 60-foot figure.[^2][^4]

The register and firelookout.com both say the tower was gone by 1965.[^2][^3] A 1956 note gave the station its air-marking number, F-2.[^4]

The register shows a 2007 photograph of the site, but its caption does not describe what remains.[^2] The sources consulted do not describe the summit today or say whether the public can reach it.
