The White Rock lookout stood on a rocky summit about 17 miles southeast of Roseburg, in Douglas County, Oregon.[^2][^3] Work on a new station building was under way in September 1917, when the Riddle Tribune reported that the structure would be walled with glass.[^3] In July 1917 a new three-mile trail from South Myrtle to the station had been completed.[^3]

The register dates the first building to 1919, a cabin with an open-air cupola, and says a D-6 cupola cabin replaced it in 1923.[^1] Forest Lookouts quotes an August 1923 report of two lookout houses under construction that summer, one of them at White Rock, cut out in Roseburg and shipped in knocked-down pieces.[^3] Forest Lookouts also lists a 14-by-16 log cabin built in 1926 for $10, and a ground house with a 7-by-7 cupola in 1928.[^3] Those entries do not match the register's account, so the building history is unclear.[^1][^3]

Forest Ranger U. F. Collins and Forest Inspector Robert Wallace were busy building the new station in 1917.[^3] In July 1930 Neil Tripp had been at the lookout station, but illness kept him at home for a few days.[^3] In July 1926 the lookout reported 25 to 30 deliberately set fires on the divide between North and South Myrtle creeks.[^3] In January 1931 reports said vandals had damaged the station, but the supervising warden found that lightning had struck it, breaking 15 of its 18 windows.[^3] In October 1943 the Army deactivated the White Rock AWS station.[^3]

The register says the lookout was removed in 1970.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] Forest Lookouts says it was discontinued and removed in 1967.[^3] The sources disagree on the year.

None of the three sources describes what remains at the summit today, or whether the public can reach it.
