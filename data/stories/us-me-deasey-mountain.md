Deasey Mountain's fire lookout is a small cab that sits directly on the ground rather than on a tower. Few lookouts of this type stand in Maine.[^1][^2]

The Maine Forest Service put it up in 1929, and it was used to spot fires until 1969.[^2] It sits on the summit of the mountain.[^3]

The cab is eight feet square and is secured by four heavy cables fastened to granite eyebolts.[^1] A watcher used an alidade, a sighting device fitted with hand-drawn maps of the area, along with binoculars and a crank telephone. The alidade let watchers locate fires, and a watcher was a key connection to fire managers.[^1]

Sources disagree on the elevation. The Register gives 1,876 feet, the Park Service's peak page gives 1,942 feet, and its lookout page gives 1,950 feet.[^2][^3][^1]

The Park Service brought the lookout back to its historic look in 2024, with students from the Stephen T. Mather Building Arts & Craftsmanship High School helping.[^1] Earlier, members of the Maine chapter of the International Appalachian Trail looked after the cab for 15 years, and in 2016 they installed a replica of the original alidade.[^1]

The Register names Elliotsville Plantation, Inc. as administrator. The Park Service now presents the lookout as part of Katahdin Woods and Waters National Monument.[^2][^3] The sources do not explain how the land changed hands.

Getting there means hiking. The shortest hike is five miles from Wassataquoik Gate and requires fording Wassataquoik Stream.[^3] Camping is barred at the summit and at the lookout, and the Park Service puts the round trip at 10 miles.[^3] The lookout page does not say whether visitors may go inside the cab.[^1]
