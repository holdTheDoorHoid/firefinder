Webb Mountain's first lookout went up in 1930, a 14-by-14-foot cabin built from a Columbia Falls "redi-cut" kit.[^1] A separate 35-foot tree platform stood about half a mile to the west, giving crews another vantage point over this stretch of the Kootenai National Forest.[^1] The original cabin didn't last: in 1959 the Forest Service replaced it with the structure still on the summit today, a 10-foot concrete base topped by a flat-roofed R-6 cab.[^1][^2]

For decades the lookout served its intended purpose, watching for smoke across the Kootenai country for more than 40 years.[^2] In 1994 a forest fire came close enough to threaten the building itself, though it survived.[^1]

Rather than let the tower sit empty, the Forest Service added it to the recreation rental program, and it's been available to overnight guests ever since.[^1][^2] The cabin sits at roughly 5,988 feet, ringed by a catwalk and lit on all sides by windows, with a wood stove, table, and sleeping space for up to five people; there's no running water or electricity, so renters pack in their own supplies.[^2] A car can make it most of the way up, but the final quarter mile of access road is steep and rocky enough that low-clearance vehicles should not attempt it.[^2] The lookout also sits directly on the Pacific Northwest National Scenic Trail, so renters may find through-hikers passing by.[^2] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on January 9, 2021.[^3]

Sources disagree slightly on how to date the structure: some treat 1930 as the lookout's founding year and others effectively date the current building to 1959, when the original cabin was replaced.[^1][^2]
