The Cheat View fire tower was a state project.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry dates the tower to about 1927 and places it near the lower end of the Cheat River canyon. It stood 65 feet tall.[^1][^2]

The ground was privately owned when the tower went up.[^1] An August 1927 newspaper item said the state fish and game commission had secured the ground under a 99-year lease, and it called the tower the state's second-largest fire observation tower.[^2] The same item said the tower would watch over 55 square miles of timberland, and that owners within its view would be offered fire protection at one cent an acre a year.[^2]

A May 1934 newspaper report quoted rangers at the tower as saying a man suspected of setting three fires had been tracked as far as Pennsylvania.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's state list marks the tower as gone.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry reports that the land is now in public hands, part of Snake Hill Wildlife Management Area.[^1] The management area lies in Monongalia and Preston Counties, along the Cheat canyon.[^4] A travel guide says the area's purchase protected the view known as Cheat View.[^5]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry and the Forest Lookouts page list Monongalia County, but the state list says Preston.[^1][^2][^3] No source I read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower on the site today.[^1]
