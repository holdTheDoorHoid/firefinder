Castle Rock sits about four miles southwest of McKenzie Bridge, although 1923 newspaper reports give five or six miles.[^2][^3] Sources disagree on when the first structure went up.[^1][^2][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association dates a tent camp to 1914, while firelookout.com gives a shelter in 1917 and Eugene Weekly gives one in 1918.[^1][^2][^4] A newspaper report shows a forest guard stationed there in August 1915.[^3]

The houses that followed are also dated differently.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association dates a hexagonal log cabin to 1921, then a D-6 cupola cabin to 1924, then an L-4 cab to 1938.[^1] Newspapers from 1923 describe a new house finished that August, built with glass on all four sides.[^3] Eugene Weekly describes a cabin with a cupola in 1924 and a larger cabin in 1938.[^4] A 1938 report says work had begun on a new station.[^3]

Newspapers name several watchers.[^3] Laurell Canning, a University of Oregon student, was posted there in 1918, and she spotted the Avenue Creek fire from the post that year.[^3] In 1921 Mrs. J. D. Baughman reported a large fire on Mount Hagan.[^3] Claude Robinson, a graduate of the university, served in 1924.[^3] In 1948 a newspaper noted that no man was needed for the post and that Grace Harbick would handle it alone.[^3] Eugene Weekly says Doug Newman staffed it each summer in the 1960s.[^4]

Vandals burned the lookout on October 31, 1974.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com lists it as gone from that year.[^2]

Eugene Weekly says the only remains of the old tower are anchor bolts, while a solar panel and antenna from the Beaverton firm Elevate Technology Group now stand on the summit.[^4] Oregon Hikers reports that a fire lookout once occupied the summit and is gone.[^5] Trees and other vegetation have grown up, so visitors must walk to different spots to see in each direction.[^5]

Visitors reach the summit on foot from a Forest Service trailhead at the end of a gravel road.[^4][^6] Oregon Hikers calls that road rough in a few places, though low-clearance cars can get through.[^5] The trail is about 1.2 to 1.25 miles long, depending on the guide.[^4][^5] The Forest Service lists the site as open, asks visitors to check current conditions with the local ranger station, and says its roads get no snow or ice maintenance.[^6]
