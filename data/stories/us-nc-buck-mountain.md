Buck Mountain's tower stands in the Uwharrie National Forest, in the community of Uwharrie in Montgomery County.[^1] The register puts the metal structure at 1936 and says the Civilian Conservation Corps probably built it.[^1] The cab, which measures 7 by 7 feet, has its base 94 feet up.[^1]

The tower was once staffed through the dry season.[^2] Retired ranger Jessie Sanders recalls sometimes logging 14-hour shifts, seven days a week, in the tower.[^2] A 2012 article says that, with limited resources, a ranger went out only in extreme conditions, and that the tower was mainly used to locate fires after a smoke report.[^2] Phones had replaced the tower's old radio system, but the Osborne Fire Finder, a device developed almost a century ago, was still in operation.[^2]

A Forest Service study, reported in 2012, found the tower eligible for the National Register of Historic Places.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register added it on March 24, 2020.[^1]

The tower was still standing in 2024.[^3] A 2012 article describes a chain-link fence around the base and a locked gate that keeps out casual visitors.[^2] The road up the mountain runs past that locked gate.[^3] A 2024 blog post reports that the top floor is padlocked.[^3] The 2012 article's author climbed the tower with a ranger's permission, and neither source says whether the public may climb the stairs.[^2][^3]
