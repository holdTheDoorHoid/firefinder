Mahogany Ridge was a fire lookout planned for the Modoc National Forest in Modoc County, California.[^1] Records quoted on the Forest Lookouts site show a February 1942 plan for a standard 14-by-14 cab on a 32-foot (9.8 m) tower, in the northeast quarter of section 28, township 39 north, range 15 east.[^1]

That same month the forest warned that building materials might be hard to get, and suggested that a rough building under a good roof would still be useful.[^1]

A November 1944 building-needs report listed the station's type as living quarters combined with an observation tower, and included a garage, a wind charger and a latrine. Its remarks add that the station had never been occupied, though detection work needed it once the site could be reached and materials were on hand.[^1]

A 1947 review of the fire season said the site had long been proposed as a detection point, yet no one had ever been posted there. The same review noted that most of the ground it could see was beyond the reach of any other lookout.[^1]

The sources do not say whether the tower was finished. No source describes the site today, so what stands there, and whether the site is open to visitors, is unknown.

[^1]: Mahogany Ridge, Forest Lookouts, western US (Ron Kemnow), https://californialookouts.weebly.com/mahogany-ridge.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
