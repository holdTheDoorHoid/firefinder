The Buck Rock lookout site is about 26 miles north of Medford, near Trail, in Jackson County, Oregon.[^2][^4][^1] A 10-foot wooden tower went up there in 1925, and both the register and firelookout.com give that year.[^1][^2]

The replacement cab is dated differently in different sources. The register says a 10-foot tower with an L-4 cab was built in 1959.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page quotes district reports instead: material was bought in 1960, and in 1961 new construction was finished and the old building was razed.[^3]

Staffing was seasonal.[^3] In 1939 a Medford newspaper reported that Walt Sherrid and his wife had moved into the station.[^3] Later summers brought Scottie Parrick in 1951 and 1952, Agnes Warrior in 1955, and Roscoe Smith in 1960.[^3]

In 1930 the lookout spotted a man-caused fire of about 2.5 acres along Trail Creek.[^3] In 1942 lookout Bill Thompson noticed a blaze on a farm 13 miles away, where a barn burned; a pumper saved the other buildings.[^3]

During World War II the site served as an Aircraft Warning Site.[^4] A 1943 Army request for activation costs described a 14-by-14 cupola ground house and called for a living quarters building and a woodshed.[^3] In August 1960 a bear tried to break into the station and killed the lookout's dog, but the building was not entered.[^3]

The register says the lookout was destroyed in 1967.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was abandoned that year and is gone, and the 2018 blog says every structure was taken down in 1967.[^2][^4] A 1964 survey description calls the tower abandoned, yet a 1965 district report says broken windows were replaced and a new map was installed at the lookout.[^3]

Today, a 2018 visitor found solar panels and a small comm tower on the site.[^4] Gravel and two-track roads reach within half a mile of Buck Rock, and the visitor reports that a 4x4 or ATV can reach the top.[^4]

We found no source that says whether the public may go up. A 1964 survey places a concrete slab directly beneath the tower's center, but we found no source saying whether any of it remains.[^3] Check with the managing agency before you go; the register lists the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^1]
