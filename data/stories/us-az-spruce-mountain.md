Spruce Mountain was a fire lookout well before its current tower went up.[^2] The summit was first used as a daily patrol lookout in 1911, and by June 1921 crews were cutting timbers for a new tower there.[^2] The tower standing today, the register says, was constructed in 1936 and carries a modified L-4 cab on a 30-foot Aermotor MC-24 tower.[^1] A hiking blog credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it.[^3] The sources do not say whether the 1936 tower replaced the one planned in 1921.

Newspapers name the lookouts on duty over the years. In July 1921 a small fire, believed to be the year's first lightning fire, was first reported from the station, where lookout F.M. Mulkins was on duty.[^2] In 1932 lookout Dwight Morris spent a night felling a lightning-struck pine to smother its fire.[^2] In 1938 a ranger placed a new lookout there, a forestry student from the University of California named A. Starker Leopold.[^2] A 1949 paper, citing a Forest Service report, said the fire lookout had been put back on the mountain, a wording that hints it had been off duty for a time.[^2]

The register added the tower on August 5, 2003, as US 531, AZ 53, nominated by Dave Lorenz.[^1] Its own figures disagree: the details list the elevation as 7,633 feet, while the description gives 7,693.[^1]

The register says the lookout "continues in active service."[^1] A hiking blog says a volunteer staffs the lookout in peak fire season, and that visitors are urged to climb about 55 feet to talk with that person.[^3] That height does not match the register's 30-foot tower, and none of the sources explains the gap.

Most visitors hike the Groom Creek Trail.[^3] The blog says a rough road also reaches the top, and that the hike takes about two hours to reach the fire road, then a quarter mile more.[^3] A Forest Service page notes that the Spruce Mountain picnic site has a gate that stays locked whenever the tower goes unstaffed, so check whether the volunteer is on duty before you set out.[^4]
