The lookout stood on the summit of Squaw Mountain.[^5] In 2007 the Board on Geographic Names renamed the peak Tumala Mountain.[^4] It lies in the Mount Hood National Forest, near Estacada.[^4]

The lookout began as a frame cabin built in 1916.[^1][^2] A 1918 newspaper described the station as a 12-by-12 building about ten feet high, part lumber and part glass, with telephones and a revolving lamp.[^3] Sources disagree about the 1930s replacement.[^1][^2] One describes a 12-by-12 cab on a pole platform tower, and another an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] In July 1932 newspapers reported a new house, 14 feet square and enclosed in glass. Another report that month said the station was being heavily built up as protection against lightning.[^3]

Staffing is recorded in the 1930s and again in the 1950s.[^3] In summer 1932 Kenneth Dart, a Hubbard schoolteacher, worked there as a lookout man.[^3] In 1935 the Statesman Journal reported that Elmer Klutke had been stationed on Squaw Mountain during fire season.[^3] A 1957 district report says the lookout was manned for 25 days that year, and that a catwalk was built and two new shutters were installed.[^3]

The Oregon Lookouts history lists the lookout as deactivated in 1964, then marked removed with no date given.[^3] Firetower.org and firelookout.com both date its removal to 1966.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list also marks it Gone.[^6]

No readable source I found describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.[^1][^2][^3] The Outbound, a hiking guide, calls the summit the site of a former fire lookout.[^5] It describes a 3.6-mile round trip from the Twin Springs Trailhead. Near the summit the trail meets an old service road that once led to the lookout.[^5] The guide says the lookout once stood atop the summit and does not describe anything standing there now.[^5] No agency page I checked says whether the route is open to visitors.
