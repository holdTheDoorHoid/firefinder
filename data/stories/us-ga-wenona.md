In January 1950 the Crisp County Forest Protection Unit had received a steel fire lookout tower, and its erection was about to begin.[^1] The Macon News printed the report, and a lookout history site quotes it.[^1]

Fires had done heavy damage across the county in an abnormally dry spell. The paper expected the tower, planned for a site two miles south of Wenona, to hold the county's fire losses down.[^1]

The tower had been ordered some months before, but a recent steel strike held up its delivery. The paper said it was among the earliest such towers to reach Georgia.[^1]

The local fire unit then had a ranger and two assistants. The paper said a towerman and an assistant patrolman would be added once the tower was up.[^1]

The Georgia tower list gives a site named wenona in Crisp County, at 451 feet (137 m) above sea level, tagged gfc-329. The list's notes say the gfc code marks towers from an older Georgia Forestry Commission list that recorded the sites of 203 towers.[^2] That entry may well be this tower: it has the name Wenona, the place the paper gave for the tower's site, and it is in Crisp County.

No source describes what remains at the site today, and none says whether it is public land or open to visitors.
