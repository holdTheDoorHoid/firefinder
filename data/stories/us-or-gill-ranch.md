The Gill brothers, Tom and Joe, ranched near Enterprise in Wallowa County, Oregon, next to the national forest.[^1][^2] In 1931 they told the district ranger they would help with fire detection if he would install a telephone at their place. The telephone went in that fall, and the boys used the cupola of their barn as a lookout.[^1][^2]

The brothers then put up a taller tower on a slightly higher spot, at their own expense.[^1] The sources do not agree on the year or the height. The register dates the tower to 1933 and gives 40 feet.[^2] Firelookout.com also gives 1933, but says 36 feet.[^3] A June 1936 Forest Service item puts the move to a 40-foot tower in the winter of 1934.[^1] A 1937 history by J.F. Irwin says the brothers built a 36-foot tower after the fall of 1933.[^1]

The brothers took turns on lookout duty during fire season, a 1937 Oregonian article reported.[^1] The June 1936 item praised both brothers as capable firefighters who would answer a fire call at any point in the season, even when their own work was urgent.[^1] By October 1937 the Forest Service also kept fire-finding equipment, a supply cache and a telephone at the tower.[^1]

The register and Firelookout.com both say the lookout is gone.[^2][^3] The site is on private land, and both list it as private.[^2][^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower today.
