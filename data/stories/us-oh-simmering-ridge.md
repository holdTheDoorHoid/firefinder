An October 1934 Chillicothe Gazette item already refers to the Simmering Ridge tower, which lay between South Webster and Oak Hill near the Jackson and Scioto county line.[^1] It said Russell M. Ramsey would staff the tower, with Wm. J. Simmering as his substitute.[^1]

In April 1936 a Portsmouth Times camp item from CCC Camp F-4 at Camp Webster said lookouts were kept on duty in the tower through fire season. Their job was to spot smoke and report it to camp, and a crew was then sent out at once.[^1]

In November 1936 a woods fire broke out between Hope Hollow and Hales Creek, near South Webster, late in the afternoon.[^1] Visibility was so poor that the Simmering Ridge lookout, staffed by CCC men from Camp Webster, could not sight the fire.[^1] 23 CCC enrollees from Camp Webster, joined by local residents, fought the blaze, which threatened several homes.[^1]

The Ohio fire tower list marks the tower as gone, and the Forest Lookouts page ends its entry with the single word "Removed," with no date or method.[^3][^1] The register places the site in Jackson County, Ohio, at 980 feet (299 m), while the Ohio list says Scioto County, so the sources disagree on the county.[^2][^3]

No source I read describes what, if anything, is left at the site today or whether visitors can reach it.
