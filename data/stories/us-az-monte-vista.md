Monte Vista Lookout sits in the southern Chiricahua Mountains, in the Coronado National Forest, at about 9,480 feet.[^1][^2] The site has had three towers over its life.[^2] A crow's nest tower went up in 1916, a 30-foot Aermotor tower replaced it in 1922, and the current 40-foot steel tower with a 14-by-14-foot CL-100 cab dates to 1966.[^1][^2]

A support cabin at the base of the lookout was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in 1933.[^3] The National Register listing, added January 28, 1988, is for that cabin, not the tower.[^3] Wikipedia also says a 1956 report that the cabin had been destroyed in a forest fire was wrong.[^3]

In 2011 the Horseshoe II fire burned across much of the Chiricahua Mountains and destroyed Barfoot Lookout, but it passed near Monte Vista without reaching the site.[^2] The register listed the tower on January 31, 2003,[^1] and a 2013 Green Valley Hiking Club summary called the lookout active and usually staffed in the high-danger part of fire season.[^2]

Getting there is on foot.[^2] The Green Valley Hiking Club describes trailheads in the Turkey Creek area, such as Morse Canyon Trail #43 or Pole Bridge Trail #264, plus two Rucker Canyon routes, one starting at the end of Forest Road 628.[^2] The sources say nothing about visitors entering the cab, and the register lists the station in active service, so treat it as a working lookout to see from the trail rather than a tower to climb.[^1][^2]
