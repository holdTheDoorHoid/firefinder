Napoleon Hill lies in Lemhi County, Idaho, in the Salmon National Forest, about 12 miles north-northwest of Salmon.[^1][^2]

Forest Lookouts says the first lookout here was built in 1932 by Murdock McNicoll.[^3] The building was prefabricated over the winter, and with help from a packer named Neal Poyner, the materials were hauled to the summit.[^3]

The other two pages describe a two-story log cabin from 1935, on a ridge two miles north of the summit.[^1][^2] The pages do not settle whether the 1932 lookout and the 1935 cabin were the same building.[^1][^3]

A 1936 Recorder Herald item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says Walter Waetzig left for his lookout on the mountain after a training camp for forest workers.[^3]

Both lookout pages record emergency status in 1950 and say the lookout was gone by 1966.[^1][^2] In 1951 the Forest Service proposed disposing of the telephone line that ran from the lookout to the Salmon River, with disposal to begin on or after August 16.[^3]

A climbing guide describes forest roads to the base of the hill's south ridge, then a bushwhack up to its summits.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout, or whether that site is open to visitors.
