Chenango Lake's fire tower stood about four miles east of Norwich, on the south side of State Route 23.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps erected it, using a 79-foot-6-inch International Derrick tower that the U.S. Forest Service had supplied to New York State.[^1] The footings went in during 1934 and the tower was finished in 1935.[^1] Its first season, in 1936, logged two fires and 49 visitors.[^1]

Observers staffed the tower from 1936 through 1943, and again in 1946.[^1] That July, the district forester told a newspaper that the tower would move to the town of Brookfield in Madison County once a foundation was ready there.[^2] The 1946 report only planned the move.[^2] The Searchable list dates it to 1948, when the tower was dismantled and moved to a site near Brookfield.[^1][^2]

At Brookfield the tower stood five miles southwest of the hamlet.[^1] It was closed at the end of the 1971 season, once aerial detection had taken over the job, and it was sold and removed in 1983.[^1]

The New York lookout directory lists the Chenango Lake tower as gone.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains at either site today, so there are no visiting details to pass on.
