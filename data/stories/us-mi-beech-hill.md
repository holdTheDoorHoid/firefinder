Beach Hill is in Luce County, Michigan.[^2] A 1934 newspaper item called it the highest land in the county.[^2] The Michigan Fire Towers directory now lists the lookout as the Beech Hill Fire Tower, and the sources spell the name two ways: the 1934 items say Beach Hill, and a 1939 item says Beech Hill.[^1][^2]

The tower was reported complete in June 1934.[^2] That report said most of the construction was carried out by CCC workers, and that the tower was ready for use.[^2] A July 1934 item gave the height as 100 feet, said the structure was steel, and mentioned a cabin at the top with stairs inside.[^2] Passable roads to the hill were built with CCC labor too.[^2] A page of 1934 newspaper items lists the tower under the Michigan Department of Conservation.[^2]

The same July 1934 item said no towerman would be assigned until the state conservation budget was approved.[^2] In the meantime, existing personnel would be assigned to cover the tower as needed.[^2] By May 1939 a newspaper identified Elmer Lent as the man responsible for the Beech Hill fire tower.[^2]

The sources disagree about the cabin. The July 1934 item describes one on top, while the directory marks its cabin field as "no".[^1][^2] A map-sales site describes a USGS topographic map of the area that portrays 1973 conditions and was published in 1977; it shows the Beech Hill Lookout Tower on the hill.[^3]

No source we read describes the site today, so we cannot say whether the tower still stands or whether the hill is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
