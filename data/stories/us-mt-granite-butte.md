## Granite Butte Lookout

Granite Butte Lookout stands on the Continental Divide in the Helena-Lewis and Clark National Forest, Lewis and Clark County, Montana, at an elevation the National Historic Lookout Register puts at 6,887 feet and Recreation.gov at 7,587 feet.[^1][^2] It's one of only four lookouts still remaining on the Helena side of the forest.[^1]

The first structure on the site was an L-4 cab on a 20-foot tower, which the National Historic Lookout Register and Recreation.gov both date to 1932.[^1][^2] Sources disagree on what came next: firelookout.com instead describes the original tower as a 1934 L-4 build, and says it was replaced in 1954 by a 20-foot treated-timber tower with an R-6 flat cab.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register and Recreation.gov instead say the present 15-by-15-foot R-6 flat cab, on a treated wood tower, was built in 1962.[^1][^2] Given that two of the three sources agree on 1932 and 1962, those are the dates used here, but the one-source discrepancy is worth noting for anyone checking the record further.

Firelookout.com states the lookout was staffed into the 1990s, though neither of the other two sources gives an end date for staffing.[^3] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on October 15, 2008.[^1]

Granite Butte is still standing today and open to the public as a rental, added to the Recreation Rental program in 2016 according to firelookout.com.[^3] The current cabin, recently renovated, sits on a grassy ridge directly on the Continental Divide National Scenic Trail, which passes right by the tower.[^2] It sleeps up to four on a twin bed and three cots, with a table, chairs, a woodstove and a propane cook stove, but no electricity or indoor plumbing; a vault toilet stands nearby.[^2] The access road is described as rough toward the end, with a high-clearance vehicle recommended, and the surrounding ridge offers both summer wildflower meadows and, in winter, cross-country skiing and access to groomed snowmobile trails.[^2]
