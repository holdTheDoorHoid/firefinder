The Bolinger tower went up in 1948 in the Bolinger area of Bossier Parish.[^1] A history column from the Bossier Parish Libraries History Center calls it the fourth and final tower in that group, after Plain Dealing, Bodcau and Gidden's Hill.[^1] The Forestry Commission placed its towers, telephone lines and fire crews to protect each district, and the towers in the fourth district were tied together by two privately run phone lines that ended at the district headquarters in Minden.[^1]

Survey records describe a four-legged steel tower about 100 feet high, with a glass-enclosed lookout cab on top.[^2][^3] It stood on the highest point of a timbered ridge about two miles west of Bolinger, on the north side of a graded east-west road.[^2] The federal survey first recorded its marker in 1950.[^3]

The other towers in the group ran all twelve months, but Bolinger's operated for only six months a year.[^1] Watchmen were on call around the clock except during heavy rain, and they typically lived in a cottage on or near the tower.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page marks the tower as removed.[^2] A 2002 recovery note on the survey marker says local information reported that the tower had been torn down, and a GPS search that year found no trace of the tower or its base.[^3] Wikipedia's list of Louisiana towers also says it was torn down, but its map history runs backward: it puts the tower on a 1984 map revision and says it disappears from a 1981 version.[^4]

The 2002 search is the most recent check on the ground that we found, and it turned up nothing.[^3] No source describes what remains at the site now, or whether the public can visit it.
