The Keystone Fire Tower began far from where visitors can climb it today. It went up in Jackson County, in southeastern Ohio, in 1942, built by the Aermotor Windmill Company of Chicago, which supplied the U.S. Forest Service with galvanized-steel fire towers.[^2] Aermotor sold three styles of climb — outside ladder, inside ladder, or inside staircase — and Keystone was built as the costliest kind, with a full internal staircase rather than a ladder.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register, which catalogued the tower in 2014 at its original site, measured it at 45 feet;[^1] a Metro Parks account written after the move instead gives 82 feet from the ground to the lookout cab's floor, a discrepancy the sources don't resolve.[^2]

By 2019 the tower stood abandoned on private land. A visitor who filed a report with the Register that year found the first flight of stairs removed and the site "heavily vandalized by locals," reachable only by asking the landowner's permission.[^1]

In 2023 the landowning Taylor family donated the structure to Columbus and Franklin County Metro Parks.[^2] The park system hired a contractor to dismantle it, truck it in three sections roughly 75 miles, re-galvanize the steel, and rebuild it at Scioto Grove Metro Park in Grove City — work that cost about $140,000.[^2] Crews added safety fencing, a rebuilt lookout cab, and new stair treads and windows.[^3][^2] The rebuilt tower opened to the public on August 15, 2023.[^3]

Today anyone can climb its 111 steps during daylight hours.[^2] From the top, the view takes in Scioto Grove's stretch of the Scioto River and, about seven miles off, the Columbus skyline.[^2] One account of the move called it a fitting coincidence: the tower arrived in central Ohio almost exactly 82 years after it first went up in Jackson County.[^2]
