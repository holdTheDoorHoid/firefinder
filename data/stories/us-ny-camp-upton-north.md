The Camp Upton North Tower Site is in Brookhaven, Suffolk County, New York, about three miles northeast of Yaphank.[^1] A Long Island fire tower list puts it about three and a half miles from Yaphank.[^2]

The New York State Conservation Department erected the steel lookout in 1932.[^1][^2] A January 1932 report said it stood on headquarters hill at Camp Upton, where the Army's cantonment offices had been during the war, and called it the fifth such tower in Suffolk County.[^3] A June 1931 item from the same source said the two towers then standing, at Selden and Flanders, had been found inadequate by a state ranger for the wooded country between them, where clouds often hid fires.[^3]

The register describes an Aermotor LS-40 steel tower with wood steps and landing platforms.[^1] The sources disagree on its height. The Long Island list calls it a 60-foot tower, while the 1932 report says it was 70 feet high.[^2][^3]

The 1932 report said one attendant would be on watch in the tower from March 15 to November 15.[^3]

The register says the tower was closed in 1953 and removed in 1955 by the state's Conservation Department.[^1] The Long Island list gives the same closing year, but says both towers were taken down in 1955 or 1956 by Brookhaven National Laboratory, then the owner of the land.[^2] The two accounts disagree about who took the tower down.[^1][^2] The list also says the north site still has concrete footers.[^2]

The land is now part of the Brookhaven National Laboratory campus.[^1] The laboratory's history says Camp Upton, the Army's camp, was active from 1917 to 1920 and again from 1940 to 1946, and that the laboratory was born in January 1947.[^4] We found no source that says whether the old tower site is open to visitors.
