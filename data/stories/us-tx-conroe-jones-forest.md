The Texas Forest Service ran lookout tower No. 7 in what a federal survey called Conroe State Forest No. 2, about 5.5 miles south-southwest of Conroe in Montgomery County.[^1][^2] Sources disagree on the forest's name: the Central US Lookouts page says J.G. Jones, while the state list says W.C. Jones.[^1][^2]

A 1942 survey station description calls it a steel lookout tower of standard design, topped by an enclosed lookout room, and puts its height at about 105 feet.[^1]

A 2010 column by Bob Bowman says towers like the one now at Lufkin were erected by the Civilian Conservation Corps in 1936.[^3] The same column says the Lufkin tower was relocated from near Conroe in 1976.[^3] It is now part of the Texas Forest Museum at Lufkin, where it stands alongside a logging train and other relics of the sawmill and logging-camp trade.[^3] No source ties tower No. 7 to the Lufkin tower by name, so that match is probable, not confirmed. The column also says the Lufkin tower has been altered to keep out would-be climbers.[^3]

The column gives the Lufkin tower's height as 100 feet, while the survey says about 105 feet, so the height is not settled.[^3][^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association still lists the original site as Tower Gone.[^4] The column is the only source for the Lufkin tower's present location.[^3] Current museum hours were not checked, so confirm them before a visit.
