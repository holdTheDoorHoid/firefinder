Big Hill Lookout stands in the heart of the Eldorado National Forest, in the Crystal Basin Recreation Area just south of Union Valley Reservoir.[^1][^3] The first lookout on the summit was a 14-by-14-foot cab on a 23-foot tower, dating to 1935.[^1] Staffing goes back at least to 1916, when a local newspaper reported that W.W. Martin and his family would run the lookout on Big Hill that season for the Forest Service.[^2]

That original lookout did not survive the Cleveland Fire of 1992, which destroyed the cab and tower.[^1][^3] A replacement cab with a catwalk, along with a groundhouse residence, was built in 1993.[^1] The timing gets a little tangled. A Mountain Democrat report from July 1994 says the new cab was hoisted onto the tower that month, which fits if the cab was built in 1993 and set in place in 1994.[^2] That reading is my inference from the two sources, since neither says it directly.

Big Hill entered the National Historic Lookout Register in September 2004.[^1] An undated Forest Service brochure says the lookout is maintained and staffed seven days a week during fire season.[^3] The register adds that the lookout is a center of fire control operations and that a seasonal helicopter air attack base shares the site.[^1]

The sources describe Big Hill as a working fire site and do not describe any public visiting arrangement, so treat access as restricted and check with the Eldorado National Forest before planning a visit.[^1][^3]
