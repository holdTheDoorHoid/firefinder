A 1913 state forestry report describes a watchman at a cabin and telephone on Bald Mountain in Townshend, and says no tower was needed.[^1] By June 1922 an inspector of fire lookout stations was making preliminary arrangements for a new lookout tower there, because the old one had outlived its usefulness.[^1] A July 1922 newspaper reported that work on the new observation tower was complete.[^1] The Townshend State Park history dates a fire tower there to 1924.[^2]

A steel tower came later.[^1] A 1933 newspaper said a steel tower would replace the old state lookout tower.[^1] A 1936 state report says members of the 119th Company CCC erected a steel fire tower on Bald Mountain, and a 1949 newspaper gives 1934 as the build year.[^1]

In 1924 the lookout was staffed only part of the time, for want of money.[^1] The 1921 season was busy: this station reported more forest fires than any other mountain lookout in the state.[^1] In October 1938 Nathan Dauchy was named to the lookout post.[^1]

A 1938 state report says the old wooden tower was razed.[^1] In August 1949 a newspaper reported that the 50-foot steel tower was coming down and would be re-erected on Mount Olga the following week.[^1] The state's 1949 to 1950 forestry report says the tower was taken down to be erected there.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the steel lookout now on Mount Olga, in Molly Stark State Park, was moved there from Bald Mountain in 1949.[^3] A hiking guide gives the move as 1955.[^4] The 1949 newspaper and state report say the tower was being taken down for the Olga site, which fits the register's date.[^1][^3]

The Townshend site has no tower today.[^5] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Vermont list marks the Bald Mountain tower as gone.[^5] No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit. The state park says the Bald Mountain Trail, a 1.7-mile route, leads to the summit, but an undated park alert says a flood washed out its footbridge and the trail is temporarily closed.[^2]
