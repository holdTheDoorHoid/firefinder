The Scott Lookout Tower stands in the Nebraska National Forest, in the Bessey Ranger District, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^2][^1] A first tower went up on the site in 1916.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register counts the tower built in 1944 as the third one there.[^1] The register recorded the tower on June 15, 1991.[^1]

Its most recent chapter is a fire story. The Bovee Fire swept through the forest in October 2022 and destroyed the Nebraska State 4-H Camp.[^3] The tower was damaged on October 2.[^4] A fire management officer who had climbed up to watch the blaze stayed until the last possible moment, then came down just before the flames could reach it.[^3]

Accounts of the damage differ.[^3][^1][^4] A regional news report says the fire destroyed the tower.[^3] The register says the fire wiped out the tower's cab.[^1] Wildfire Today, writing in mid-October 2022, reported visible damage to the wooden steps and the upper cabin, and said the steel framework still stood while a structural engineer was to assess it.[^4]

The Forest Service's page, last updated March 28, 2025, says the site is undergoing renovations. It adds that visitors may come to see the tower but not go inside it.[^2] The grounds are open year round, around the clock.[^2] Children under 18 need an adult with them, and there is no fee.[^2]

To reach it, drive east from Thedford on Highway 2 for about 17 miles, turn south onto State Spur 86B, and follow it about 3 miles to a loop that circles the tower.[^2] Parking lies on the tower's right side.[^2] A vault toilet is on site, but there is no potable water.[^2] A hiking trail three miles long links the tower with the Bessey Recreation Complex.[^2]
