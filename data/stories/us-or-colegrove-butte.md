Colegrove Butte is a high point in Curry County, close to the Oregon Coast Highway.[^1] An April 1933 field report by P.S. King says the writer wanted to talk with Demar Colegrove about putting a lookout on that high point.[^1]

By late April 1934 a lookout stood on the summit, on Mr. Colegrove's land.[^1] On April 26, 1934, Mr. Ostrander and Mr. McCarty went up and found that no tower had been built. The house, set right on top of the mountain, blocked about half the view to the north.[^1] By June 1934 the timber on the north side had been felled. A June 12 entry in the same field report called the house a good standard lookout house, and said that felling the north-side timber gave it good visibility for the entire country.[^1]

A June 1934 item in the Curry County Reporter, quoted on the same page, said a lookout cabin had been built only a quarter mile back from the road to Brookings. It said Stanley Colegrove was expected to be the first observer.[^1]

The page records activation on August 30, 1942, and names the Roseburg Filter Center.[^1] On September 11, 1942, W.N. Parke, identified as an AWS inspector, reported that a 20x22 portable building with double walls had been completed.[^1]

The page's last entry is a single word, "Removed," with no date.[^1] No source describes what remains on the summit today, or whether the butte is open to visitors.

[^1]: Colegrove Butte - Forest Lookouts (western US), Ron Kemnow, https://oregonlookouts.weebly.com/colegrove-butte.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
