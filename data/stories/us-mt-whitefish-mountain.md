The Whitefish Mountain lookout stood about three miles north of Whitefish, in Flathead County, Montana, on Flathead National Forest land.[^1][^2] Its first tower, built in 1938, was a 50-foot pole tower of the L-4 type.[^1]

The firelookout.com record names lookout couples in 1952, 1957, 1958 and 1965.[^1] In June 1952 vandals broke into the lookout, took the batteries out of the telephone and left the food box open, its contents scattered about.[^3] In July 1954 a roadside fire between Whitefish and the lookout was blamed on campers.[^3] Walter Stickney, the lookout, spotted the fire while he was out fetching water and put it out himself.[^3]

The first tower was destroyed in 1974.[^1] In 1978 a 35-foot steel tower, moved from the Ingalls lookout, was set up about a mile east on Stoltz Lumber Company land.[^1][^4]

Sources disagree about its height. The 2022 Whitefish Pilot article describes a 40-foot lookout that was manned for years and sat on the south face of the Whitefish Range, not far from the summit of Big Mountain.[^5] Firelookout.com gives 35 feet for the steel tower set up in 1978.[^1] SummitPost gives 35 feet for the Ingalls Mountain lookout, which it says was moved to that site in 1978.[^4] The Pilot says the site was Forest Service land that the F.H. Stoltze Land and Lumber Co. had originally given to the agency.[^5] After the tower was declared surplus, a private owner took it home instead of letting it be scrapped.[^5]

Firelookout.com says the tower was moved in 1993 to private property about three miles southeast of Whitefish.[^1]

The Pilot found the tower standing near the driveway of a rural Whitefish home, where trees in the yard, taller than the tower, block the views.[^5] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a relocated Whitefish Mountain tower, still standing, with its location marked private.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the original site.
