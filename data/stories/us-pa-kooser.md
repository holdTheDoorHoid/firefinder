The Kooser tower went up in 1934 on Laurel Hill, a mile south of the Bakersville–Mt. Pleasant pike where that road crosses the Westmoreland and Somerset county line.[^1] A notice from March 1935 said it stood 80 feet high on a spot 2,920 feet up, and that ECW labor had erected it.[^1] A Tribune-Review article ties it to Civilian Conservation Corps work from the Depression era and gives its height as 82 feet.[^2] That is two feet more than the 1935 notice, and the sources do not explain whether the ECW labor of 1935 and the Civilian Conservation Corps labor are the same work.[^1][^2]

The state planned to staff it for the first time that spring, and a 1935 notice named E. T. Beck as the towerman there.[^1] Beck was still at the tower in May 1936.[^1] That same year a notice said WPA sawmill lumber would go into towermen's cabins, and that a cabin was planned at the summit so the towerman could stay on site day and night during fire season.[^1] The sources do not say whether that cabin was ever built.

In April 1941 the observer in the tower spotted a fire near Bakersville, in Somerset Township. The fire got out of control near Normalville, in Fayette County, and destroyed a church and two dwellings. The observer sent two crews of 20 men each to the scene.[^1] In April 1945 a posting notice named Martin Krepps as the towerman, the last one the sources name.[^1]

The Fish and Boat Commission was deeded the tower in 1984 and used it as a mount for communications gear.[^2] When the commission agreed to hand it over to the state park system, DCNR ranger Mike Mumau, who oversees Kooser and Laurel Hill State Parks, looked into saving it.[^2] The steel frame was judged sound, but the wooden steps and platforms were rotting. A Friends of the Park group wrote a grant application, the state gave the group $4,000, volunteers raised matching money, and park staff gave their labor to restore the tower.[^2]

The article describes the tower as padlocked against vandals. Climbing is allowed only with park staff supervising, at events scheduled through the year.[^2] A DCNR tour in October 2021 was a short hike to the tower, with registration required and climbers at least 18 years old.[^3] The sources use two park names for the same place, Kooser State Park and Laurel Hill State Park.[^2][^3] The newest dated item in the sources is that 2021 tour, so check with the park before going, and do not climb the tower on your own.
