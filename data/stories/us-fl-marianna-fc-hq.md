The Florida Forest Service let construction contracts in 1950 for a 100-foot lookout tower at Marianna, in Jackson County.[^1] The same announcement described an office and two dwellings planned for the tower site.[^1] That office was to be the county ranger's base for the Jackson County fire control unit.[^1] The district forester, J. Edwin Moore, also said four lookout towers were planned for the county.[^1]

A newspaper report from March 1954 placed ranger W.B. Martin at the Marianna Fire Tower, one of five towers in the county.[^1] The same report counted 64 wildfires in the county during February, more than half of the 122 recorded from July 1, 1953 to March 1, 1954.[^1] It also described a patrol plane, flying daily when weather allowed, as a major lookout medium against forest fires.[^1]

No source we found describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.
