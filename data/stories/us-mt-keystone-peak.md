Keystone Peak rises to 5,843 feet in Mineral County, Montana.[^1] Firelookout.com puts it at 5,878 feet.[^2] The peak, Keystone Creek and the town of Keystone all take their name from the Keystone Mine, which Phillip O'Rourke and others discovered in 1887.[^1] That silver mine ran on and off until the 1930s.[^1]

A fire lookout went up on the peak in 1934, and firelookout.com calls it a 10' pole L-4 tower.[^2] The Montana History Portal says a lookout stood there from 1934 until 1980.[^1] Both firelookout.com and Montanalookouts list the Lolo National Forest as the agency.[^2][^3]

The sources do not agree on how the lookout ended.[^2][^3][^4] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed in 1980.[^2] Montanalookouts lists the tower as removed and gives no date.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks it as gone.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, is still standing on the summit, or whether visitors can reach it.[^2][^3]
