The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says a 24-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab went up on the site in 1935.[^1] Rex's page gives a 16-foot round timber tower with an L-4 cab for 1935,[^2] while firelookout.com dates a 20-foot pole tower to 1933.[^3] The sources disagree on both the year and the height.[^1][^2][^3] The site lies about seven miles northwest of Monument, in Grant County, within the Umatilla National Forest.[^3]

Lookouts were stationed here through the 1930s and 1940s.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com names Harold Gentry for 1934 and Harold Stevens for 1936.[^3] An October 1935 Heppner paper reported that a woman had come to the station for a week to join her husband there.[^2] In July 1947 a 17-year-old Heppner high school student working as a forest service lookout here was fatally hurt by a falling limb during a line squall, according to an Oregonian report quoted on Rex's page.[^2]

The Heppner Ranger District's annual report said in January 1956 that the lookout would be dismantled that season.[^2] The register says it was destroyed in 1956.[^1] Firelookout.com adds that a later ten-foot wooden platform tower, whose history it calls in question, burned in 2024.[^3]

The register lists the site as a former lookout administered by the U.S. Forest Service.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the road to it is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
