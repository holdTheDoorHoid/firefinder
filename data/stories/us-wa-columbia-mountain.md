The U.S. Forest Service built the Columbia Mountain lookout on the Colville National Forest in 1914.[^1] The forest's second supervisor, C.C. Reed, designed it.[^1] It was a hand-hewn log cabin about 12 by 12 feet, with a 15-foot platform above it for the lookout.[^2][^4] The site had a camp and stone shelter from 1912.[^2][^3]

In 1928 a 16-foot pole tower with a cab replaced the cabin as the lookout, and the cabin became a storage shed.[^1][^2] FireLookout.com dates that tower to the 1930s instead, and TrailChick credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with the later lookout.[^3][^4]

Newspaper items that WillhiteWeb reprints name Forest Guard Hugh McIntosh at the station in 1915, and writer Gladys Murray as the person set to run it in 1919.[^2] FireLookout.com lists Roy Snyder in 1923 and Ed Walden in 1929.[^3] TrailChick says two Slagle brothers served there during World War II.[^4]

In August 1914 a fire reported from the Columbia lookout was in an inaccessible, heavily wooded region, and crews had to cut a trail of about two miles to reach it.[^2] A clerk named Barrett thought lightning had started the fire.[^2]

The NHLR says the 1928 tower stayed in service until the mid-1950s and burned in the 1960s.[^1] WillhiteWeb says it lasted into the 1960s, and that the tower and cab are gone with debris remaining.[^2]

The Washington chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association began restoring the aging cabin in 1993, led by Ray Kresek.[^1] The cabin was registered on the National Historic Lookout Register on January 18, 1995, as US 103, WA 9.[^1] In the summers of 2009 and 2010, Passport In Time volunteers and Forest Service staff, led by archaeologist Stuart Chilvers, replaced rotted sections and the roof, reusing as much of the original as they could.[^1][^2][^5]

The log cabin still stands, but the pole tower and its cab are gone.[^1][^2] To get there, take Highway 20 to Sherman Pass, park at the large signed trailhead, and follow the Kettle Crest Trail north for a hike of about 2.5 miles.[^2] The sources read do not say whether visitors may go inside the cabin, so ask the Forest Service before you go.[^1]
