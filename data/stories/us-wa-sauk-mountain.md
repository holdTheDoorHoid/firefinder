The first lookout on Sauk Mountain, in Skagit County, was a cupola cabin built in 1928.[^1][^2][^4] The register and firelookout.com date its replacement, an R-6 flat cab, to 1958.[^1][^2] A Willhite page says a flat-top cab and tower went up in 1957,[^4] and a 1957 Arlington Times item, quoted on another lookout history site, describes a helicopter hauling material to the summit to build a new lookout.[^3]

The lookout served as an Aircraft Warning Service station, "Yoke 6-5," during World War II, starting in 1942.[^4] That station was deactivated in October 1943, and the Forest Service kept the site for fire detection.[^3][^4] A Willhite page says Philip Whalen manned the lookout in 1953.[^4]

Most sources say the Forest Service removed the lookout in 1986.[^4][^5][^6][^7] A 2013 trip report says vandalism was the reason.[^5] The register entry gives 1991 instead.[^1]

Today the summit is a hiking destination.[^6][^7] The Forest Service calls trail 613 heavily used and says the site once hosted a fire lookout, removed in the 1980s.[^7] The round trip is 4.2 miles with about 1,200 feet of gain.[^6] A June 2026 hiker found the road to the trailhead fine, though a little rough in spots.[^6] That hiker also reported a steel cross that remains near the old lookout site. The hiker found little information about the cross, and a few people the hiker asked online did not seem to remember when it appeared.[^6] Apart from the cross, the report does not describe what is left of the lookout.[^6]
