Silica Mountain sits about 22 miles southwest of Oakridge in Douglas County, Oregon.[^2][^1] An Oregon lookout timeline says a lookout was set up in a tree about 30 feet up in 1920.[^3] By September 1927 a newspaper describes a tall fir cut off at 60 feet, with a fire-finding perch on top and a ladder built up the side of the tree.[^3] A ranger shelter was going up at the base of the mountain, and the paper says a lookout went up top only by special order or when a lightning storm was expected or under way.[^3] The sources do not say whether the 30-foot tree and the 60-foot fir were the same structure.[^3]

In 1931 a 16-foot round fir tower with twelve legs and a 14-by-14-foot gable-roofed cab went up.[^3] The Register and firelookout.com both give 1931 as the build year and describe a 16-foot wooden live-in tower.[^1][^2]

A September 1927 Eugene newspaper says an emergency lookout had been stationed at Silica Mountain for the Umpqua National Forest.[^3] In 1933 a CCC crew from the Brice Creek camp went out to Silica to maintain trails and telephone lines.[^3] The Oregon Lookouts page says the site began within the Umpqua National Forest and was later run by the Douglas County Fire Patrol.[^3]

The Register says the tower was removed about 1965. firelookout.com says it was destroyed at about that time.[^1][^2] The Register registered the site in March 2011, on the nomination of Ray Kresek.[^1]

The Register lists the site as a former lookout, and no source we read describes what remains on the mountain today.[^1] We also found nothing on whether the public can reach it.
