The Anderson Camp lookout stood on Anderson Mountain in the Umpqua National Forest, in Douglas County, Oregon.[^1][^3][^4] The name is older than the tower.[^3] Anderson Camp is also a meadow camp, which a 2018 hiker says commemorates Frank Anderson, an early sheepherder.[^3]

A Roseburg newspaper notice from September 1933 said a 10-by-10 lookout was to be built before the snow flew.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page says the Civilian Conservation Corps built an L-5 cab on a 30-foot round timber tower in 1934.[^1] The 2018 hiking report gives 1933 as the build year and calls it a 30-foot pole tower with a live-in cab.[^3] We could not tell whether the 1933 plan and the 1934 cab were the same building.

A 1940 notice quoted on the Forest Lookouts page says Louis Gable had been stationed at Anderson Camp during the summer months.[^1] A fence enclosing 20 acres went up in 1935, at a cost of $30.29.[^1]

Sources disagree on when the lookout was given up. The Forest Lookouts page says it was abandoned about the middle of the 1950s, while the 2018 report and firelookout.com both give 1958.[^1][^3][^4] The 2018 report says the tower was later destroyed by burning, but it gives no date.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list marks the tower as gone.[^2] When the 2018 hiker visited, only the tower's footings and some of its wooden support legs were left, and trees had grown up and blocked the view.[^3]

The site is reached on foot.[^3] The hiker parked at the Forest Road 6515 trailhead, followed the Anderson Camp Trail, which the Forest Service says gets limited maintenance, and then went south along the Rogue-Umpqua Divide Trail to the old lookout site.[^3] The 2018 report describes no closure or permit requirement.[^3]
