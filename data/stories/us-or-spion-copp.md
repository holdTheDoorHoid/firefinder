Spion Copp is a butte about 15 miles south of John Day, in Grant County, on the Malheur National Forest.[^1][^2][^3] The register gives the site's elevation as 5,603 feet.[^1]

A lookout tower went up there in the 1930s. The register describes it as 15 feet tall.[^1][^2] A southwest panorama of the lookout, dated June 20, 1938, is credited to the National Archives Pacific Northwest Region.[^1][^3]

The register says the lookout was superseded by Dry Soda Lookout in 1941 and no longer stands.[^1] Firelookout.com says the same.[^2] Dry Soda, the lookout that replaced it, is still standing and in good condition, according to the National Historic Lookout Register, which says it is still staffed for fire detection each year.[^4]

In July 1972 the federal government put the 2.5 acres atop the butte up for sale through the General Services Administration.[^3] That September the land went to two snowmobile enthusiasts, who paid $1,301 for it and planned to build a cabin for overnight snowmobiling trips.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com both say the site is on private land with no public access.[^1][^2] We found no source that describes what, if anything, stands on the butte today, so visitors should stay off private land unless they have permission.
