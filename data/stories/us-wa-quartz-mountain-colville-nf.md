Quartz Mountain rises about six miles southeast of Republic, in Ferry County, Washington, on the Colville National Forest.[^1][^2] The register puts the site at 4,799 feet, or 1,463 meters.[^1]

The first tower went up in 1932 or 1933. A Republic newspaper item from September 1932 mentions a ranger raising a lookout tower there,[^3] and the register dates the build to 1933.[^1] Two later newspaper items disagree on the height: a July 1933 item calls the tower about 50 feet high, while a 1935 item says the station was 40 feet high.[^3]

The road to the site was completed in 1934, and a garage and storehouse went up at its end for use during fire season.[^3]

In 1955 a 53-foot L-4 tower was brought over from Churchill Mountain. In 1959 its L-4 cab was taken off, the tower was cut down to 30 feet, and a flat R-6 cab was set on top.[^1][^2]

Known lookout staff are listed from 1945 through 1959, including Alwilda Webb in 1945 and Tom Windsor in 1959.[^2] The Washington Lookouts page says the lookout was still used for fire detection in 1977, its last season.[^3] The register says it was abandoned in the 1970s and then suffered vandalism.[^1] The same page also lists the lookout as activated on May 9, 1942, and deactivated on July 1, 1943, under the Seattle Filter Center, without further explanation.[^3]

On October 7, 1997, the cabin and its 30-foot tower burned to the ground. The register says an act of arson destroyed the tower that October, and a Spokesman-Review item quoted on the Washington Lookouts page says Colville National Forest officials blamed arson.[^1][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the tower as gone.[^4] We found no source that describes what remains at the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
