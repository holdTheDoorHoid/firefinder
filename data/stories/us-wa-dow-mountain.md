Dow Mountain rises between Hood Canal and Lake Cushman in Mason County, Washington.[^4][^1] The register puts the site at 2,516 feet, while a trip report gives the summit as 2,521 feet.[^1][^4]

Before 1942, a platform 15 feet high stood on a snag on the mountain.[^4] In 1942 the state built a 12-by-24-foot house with a half second story.[^1][^4] The register calls it the first lookout on the site.[^1]

A trip report says the house was used that same year as an Aircraft Warning Service post, Charlie 3-3.[^4] A May 1944 report lists the same station and says the service added no other improvements.[^3]

The trip report lists a lookout for most summers from 1955 to 1967, and says 1967 may have been the lookout's last year.[^4]

In 1963 the state Department of Natural Resources raised a 40-foot timber tower with a live-in cab beside the old house.[^4][^1] The register says the tower replaced the house, and the trip report says the old house was torn down within a few years.[^1][^4]

The later lookout had disappeared by 1972.[^4] USGS surveyors reported that the house had been torn down and the summit leveled off as a place for helicopters to land.[^4] Survey benchmarks on the summit were destroyed too.[^4] Firelookout.com lists the 1963 tower as gone.[^2]

Today the summit is clear but ringed by trees.[^4] The trip report describes the mountain as a pleasant woodland outing, good for a rainy day.[^4]

The trip report describes a route of about 3 miles and 1,800 feet of gain, starting at the Lake Cushman Maintenance Office.[^4] Signs at the gate say driving the road beyond is limited to Lake Cushman Maintenance Company members.[^4] The trip report says nothing it found indicates that hiking past that gate is banned, though one neighbor has complained about the web page.[^4] The trip report recommends the south-side west road, which it says has the fewest private property issues, and notes that part of the upper route crosses State Lands.[^4]
