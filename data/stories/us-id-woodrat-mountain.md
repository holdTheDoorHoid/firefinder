Firelookout.com says a log cabin went up on Woodrat Mountain in 1925.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page quotes a 1981 history of the Lochsa Ranger District by Louis Hartig.[^3] In that history, Ranger Jack Parsell of the Middlefork District had a one-room log cabin built there as lookout quarters, before any road reached Woodrat.[^3] Rex's page also places a 40-foot tower with an observatory on the ridge at about the same time.[^3]

Firelookout.com puts a 60-foot platform tower in 1928, then a 40-foot L-4 in 1935 and a 41-foot treated-timber L-4 in 1950.[^1][^2][^4] Rex's page says the cabin and tower were staffed seasonally until 1946, when the Nezperce Forest replaced both with a 41-foot L-4 cabin on a treated-timber tower.[^3] The two accounts differ by four years on that last tower.[^1][^3] They also differ on the early towers: Rex's page has a 40-foot tower at about 1925, while Firelookout.com has a 60-foot platform tower in 1928.[^1][^3]

Rex's page says Woodrat passed to the Nezperce Forest when the Selway was divided in 1934-35, and joined the Lochsa District by boundary change in 1956.[^3] It was in use as late as 1966, according to the same page.[^3]

Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts both say the lookout was destroyed in 1972.[^1][^2] Rex's page lists it as removed, with no date.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks it gone.[^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts marks the site as drive-up and gives its elevation as 4,983 feet.[^2] Rex's page says the point is on state land, although the Forest Service built the improvements there and manned them.[^3] No source describes what remains at the site today.
