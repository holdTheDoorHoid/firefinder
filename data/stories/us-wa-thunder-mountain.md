Thunder Mountain, in the Salmo-Priest Wilderness of Pend Oreille County, once had two lookouts on its summit, about 100 yards apart.[^1][^2][^3]

The first was a cupola cabin built at the south end of the summit in 1926.[^1][^3] In 1934 a 50-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab went up at the north end.[^1][^3] The register says the tower replaced the cabin, but the same entry and Willhite both describe the two standing side by side until the 1950s.[^1][^3]

Willhite names the 1926 lookout as Mark Cook, who was struck by lightning in his cabin.[^3] His injuries were slight, limited to burns on his hands, feet and buttocks.[^3] Another strike destroyed the cabin a few years later.[^3]

Both structures were taken down or destroyed during the 1950s.[^1][^2][^3] The register and Willhite say they were removed, while FirelookOut says they were destroyed.[^1][^2][^3] A Washington lookout list marks the site as removed.[^4]

Willhite's undated photographs show footings at both summit sites and cable anchors, and the text places the footings of the former 50-foot tower at the north summit.[^3] One photo caption says the south site still has a view.[^3]

Reaching the site is a backcountry hike.[^1][^3] Willhite lists 14 miles round trip from the Thunder Creek trailhead, with 2,400 feet of gain.[^3] The Shedroof Divide crosses below the mountain, and an old, unmaintained trail leads to the summit.[^5] Willhite warns that the south-slope spur is abandoned, with no upkeep and no signs.[^3]
