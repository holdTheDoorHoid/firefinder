North Baldy sits in the Kaniksu and Colville National Forests in Pend Oreille County, Washington, at 6,173 feet.[^1][^2] A camp was developed a quarter mile south of the summit in 1909.[^1] A log cabin came in 1918, and a cupola cabin followed in 1928.[^1] Firelookout.com dates the cupola cabin to 1925, so the two sources disagree.[^2] A 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab came in 1938.[^1][^2]

A 1930 newspaper account, quoted on Rex's Fire Lookout Page, describes the lookout on North Baldy phoning the forest supervisor during a plane emergency nearby.[^3] Firelookout.com lists Susan Wright as a known lookout in 1967.[^2]

After the Second World War the summit became a radar site.[^1] The Air Force acquired 56 and a half acres in 1957 and built a steel radar tower there.[^1][^3] The Air Force declared the site excess in 1961, and the land went back to the Forest Service in 1962.[^3]

In 1964 a 41-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 flat-roof cab went up.[^1][^3] It was abandoned in the 1970s.[^1] It had no guy cables or lightning hardware, yet it stayed upright into the 1980s.[^1]

The tower was torn down in 1989.[^1] Firetower.org and WillhiteWeb say the top 30 feet of the 41-foot tower went to Big Meadow Lake, west of Ione, to become a wildlife observation tower.[^1][^4][^5] Rex's page says the tower was moved as a whole, which conflicts with those two accounts.[^3]

Today WillhiteWeb identifies the Big Meadow Lake viewing platform as the former North Baldy tower.[^5] That visit report is undated, and it says access was closed for vandalism during the visit.[^5] Vehicles can reach the campground from Colville or Ione.[^5] We found no source that describes the summit today.[^1]
