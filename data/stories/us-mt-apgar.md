Apgar Lookout sits a quarter mile below the summit of Apgar Mountain, on the southeast spur above Lake McDonald in Glacier National Park.[^3] The first structure on the site, a log cabin on an elevated platform, went up in 1927, but it burned in a forest fire only two weeks after it was finished.[^2][^3] The National Park Service rebuilt immediately, using the same design: a two-story wooden structure with an enclosed ten-foot tower and a 14-by-14-foot cab under a pyramidal roof.[^1][^2] Sources disagree on exactly when: the National Historic Lookout Register and the NRHP nomination both date the present building to 1930, while Wikipedia's infobox and firelookout.com give 1929.[^1][^2][^3]

Apgar is one of several near-identical lookouts the Park Service built in the 1920s and 1930s to form an overlapping fire-watch network across Glacier, using a standard U.S. Forest Service cab design adapted for park use.[^2] At roughly 5,130 feet, it is the lowest-elevation lookout in the park.[^1] No source consulted gives details on who staffed the tower or for how long.

The lookout was formally added to the National Register of Historic Places on December 16, 1986, as part of the Glacier National Park Multiple Resource Area, and was registered with the National Historic Lookout Register on September 15, 1991, as US 33.[^1][^2] Wikipedia reports that the structure is boarded up and no longer in use.[^2]

A trail leads to the lookout, and it remains standing on National Park Service land, but nothing in the sources consulted indicates that the public may enter or climb it. Visitors should treat it as a historic structure to view from outside rather than a place to stay overnight.
