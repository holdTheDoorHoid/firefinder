The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Toxaway Mountain tower in 1935.[^1][^2] It was a steel tower 45 feet 9 inches tall, with a one-room cabin, and the work cost $880.19.[^1] Company 3448 out of Camp Sledge in Brevard did the building.[^2] The library column carries a photograph captioned as the Toxaway Mountain fire tower in 1964.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page lists Jackson and Transylvania counties, and the North Carolina Division of Forest Resources, for the site.[^1][^3] A Transylvania County library column, reprinted on a North Carolina history blog in October 2016, says the Toxaway tower is one of only two western North Carolina lookouts still staffed.[^2][^4] The same column says towers were usually staffed from October through May, and that because the summits were isolated, watchmen also made their homes there.[^2]

According to the Forest Lookouts history, a state surplus-property auction began on March 22, 2018. The entry also says the site lease had ended and that the structure had to come down.[^1]

No source I found says whether the tower was taken down after that, or whether it still stands. A list of North Carolina lookout towers, marked as revised in June 2021, still includes Toxaway Mountain with its coordinates.[^3] No source describes the site today or says whether the public can reach it, so check before you go. Please do not climb a tower that is closed or private.
