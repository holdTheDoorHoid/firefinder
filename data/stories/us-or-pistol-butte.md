Pistol Butte stands about 19 miles southwest of Bend, in the Deschutes National Forest.[^2] A Bend Bulletin item from August 1932 described a 30-foot tower approved for the butte.[^3] The tower completed in 1933 was 20 feet tall, a round timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot L-4 hip-roof cab that cost $1,172.94.[^3]

Sources disagree on the build year. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and Forest Lookouts give 1933, while firelookout.com gives 1932.[^1][^2][^3]

Frederick Vogel, who had married that June, was slated to spend the summer on the butte as a Forest Service lookout.[^3] That August he used his fire finder on the full moon, and the Bend Bulletin reported that his estimate was within 65 miles of the moon's 2,162-mile diameter.[^3] In 1954 the tower hosted lookout training.[^3]

The tower went to emergency status in 1952.[^1][^2] The register says it was removed by 1969, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed by then.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the summit is open to visitors.
