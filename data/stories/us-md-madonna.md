The state put up a fire tower at Madonna, in the town of Madonna in Harford County, in 1939.[^1] The tower stood on a 1.67-acre parcel the state had acquired under a deed dated February 10, 1936.[^1]

The idea was older. In February 1932 State Forester F.W. Besley urged a fire tower for the northwestern part of the county, to guard valuable woodland at the sources of Deer Creek and the Gunpowder River.[^2] He asked that interested citizens, the county commissioners and the State pay for it, and a forest warden began a campaign in his section to raise the money.[^2]

The state's report says the forestry tower was removed years ago. Three communications towers once stood on the site, and they have since been replaced by one tower for state, federal and local use.[^1]

Today the site is a Forest Service regional work center, reached by Madonna Road north from Norrisville Road, and it also holds a multi-use communications complex.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association list also marks the tower as gone.[^3] No source describes any remains of the 1939 tower, and the report does not say whether the public may visit. Anyone planning a visit should check with the Forest Service first, since the report describes the site as a regional work center.[^1]
