Mountain Fire Lookout Tower stands in Oconto County, Wisconsin, about two and a half miles north of the community of Mountain, off Old Highway 32.[^1][^2] The tower's first life began in 1932, when the Aermotor company prefabricated it and the Wisconsin Conservation Commission erected it at another spot in the national forest, about two and a half miles away.[^3] A pier at that original site is dated October 22, 1932.[^3]

The old spot was hard to reach, accessible only by a foot trail.[^3] In 1935 Civilian Conservation Corps crews took the tower apart, and they are believed to have rebuilt it at its present site.[^3] The Forest Service bought that ground from the Holt Lumber Company in January 1935.[^3]

The Forest Service and Wikipedia both date the tower to 1935, the year it was moved, while the nomination dates the first erection to 1932.[^1][^2][^3]

The tower is an Aermotor LS-40 model, resting on four concrete piers.[^3] It is about 100 feet tall, with a seven-by-seven-foot cab at the top.[^3][^1]

A local camp is believed to have supplied the labor that moved the tower, and camp enrollees manned the lookout and fought fires nearby.[^3] One former enrollee recalled staffing it in 1940.[^3]

The tower kept serving through the 1960s, when aircraft spotting took over.[^3] The nomination gives April 25, 1970, as the date of the last fire called in from the tower.[^3]

A radio antenna went up in 1977, along with a relay shelter at the base.[^3] Both were removed in 1993 or 1994.[^3] The tower was rehabilitated as an interpretive site in 1993 and 1994, when decayed wooden parts were replaced and six interpretive panels went up at its base.[^3]

The forest says the tower was restored in 2015 and 2016 with local help and more than 500 hours of staff work.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on May 21, 1992.[^4] The National Register of Historic Places added it on August 19, 2008.[^2][^5] The register calls it the only remaining lookout on the forest, while the nomination counts it as one of two remaining towers out of the 19 that once served the forest.[^4][^3]

The forest calls it the first Wisconsin lookout on both registers.[^1] That is not quite right for the National Register, since the Fifield tower was added in 2007, a year before this one.[^1][^6]

Today the tower is open from May 1 to November 1, daily from 8 a.m. to sunset.[^1] It is the only one of the forest's 14 original towers that visitors may climb to the top.[^1]
