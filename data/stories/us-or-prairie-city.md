Prairie City's historic lookout is not a tower. It is an Osborne fire-finder on a stand, set inside an enclosure about a mile south of town.[^1][^2] The build date is only roughly known. The Register gives a date of around the 1930s, a directory of Oregon lookouts lists 1930, and a 2023 visitor's account places it in the early 1930s.[^1][^4][^5] A photo note on a Forest Lookouts page for the town also records panorama photographs taken by Osborne on September 11, 1935.[^3]

The sources read name no one who watched from the site, and none describes a particular fire the finder helped with. The 2023 account says the finder is now reserved for emergencies such as heavy lightning, and the directory lists the site as unmanned.[^5][^4]

The finder is still standing. The Register added it on July 5, 2022, under the numbers US 1637, OR 155.[^1] The wooden box that once held it was swapped for a sturdier metal one sometime between 2016 and 2018, and the Register records a new lockbox in the same window.[^5][^1]

Today the Malheur National Forest, through the Forest Service, administers the site.[^1] The finder itself stays locked in its box and is used only in emergencies.[^2][^5] The public can see the box from a small pull-out on the road that becomes CR-60 and Strawberry Road. The 2023 writer noted that the finder itself looks directly onto the Strawberry Mountain Wilderness.[^5] Anyone visiting should look from the road and leave the locked box alone. The sources read do not say whether the site has ever been closed or restored.
