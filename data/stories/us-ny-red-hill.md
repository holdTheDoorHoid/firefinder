## Red Hill Fire Tower

Red Hill rises to about 2,980 feet (908 m) on the southern edge of the Catskills, in the Sundown Wild Forest in Ulster County.[^1][^4] The state put up a fire lookout tower on its summit in 1920, one of the last towers built to close a gap in the Catskills' forest-fire detection network.[^2] It is a 60-foot steel Aermotor tower topped by a 7-by-7-foot steel cab, fitted with an unusual half-inch tube alidade for sighting smoke.[^1] An observer's cabin — a one-story, 14-by-24-foot frame building — followed in 1931.[^2]

State observers staffed Red Hill for seven decades, with short closures in 1971 and the early 1980s and a return to full-time duty in 1987 during a stretch of heavy fire activity.[^2] It closed for good in 1990, the last fire tower in the Catskills to go out of service.[^2] Afterward the tower sat idle, and because state policy barred keeping nonessential structures on Forest Preserve land, it was slated for demolition.[^2]

A restoration campaign changed that. Worried residents of nearby Denning and Claryville asked the Catskill Center for Conservation and Development for help, and starting in 1998 the Center and the state Department of Environmental Conservation rebuilt the tower and cabin, paying for it with state grants matched by local fundraising — bake sales and a quilt raffle among them.[^2] Volunteers from the New York–New Jersey Trail Conference built a new access trail down the mountain's slope as part of the same effort.[^2] Red Hill reopened to the public in 2000, the first of the Catskills' restored fire towers to do so.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register had already listed it in March 1995, nominated by FFLA representative Bob Spear;[^1] the tower followed onto the National Register of Historic Places on September 23, 2001, as part of the multiple-property listing for New York's state forest-preserve fire observation stations.[^3]

Today the Red Hill Fire Tower Committee and volunteer guides staff the cab on weekends between Memorial Day and Columbus Day.[^1] A 2017 visit report found the tower, cabin, picnic table, privy, trail, register box and kiosk all in good condition, though it noted nearby electronic equipment encroaching on the site.[^1] The hike in is about a mile, to what one hiking guide calls a "fairly remote" tower.[^4] The tower and its grounds are state-owned, administered by the DEC.[^1]
