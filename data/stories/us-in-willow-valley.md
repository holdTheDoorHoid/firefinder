Willow Valley Fire Tower stands in Martin State Forest in Martin County, in the forested hills of southern Indiana.[^1] In August 1931 the state conservation department announced that a 100-foot steel tower had been delivered at Shoals and would go up near Willow Hill, along U.S. 50.[^3] It was meant to protect about 150,000 acres in three counties.[^3] The state supplied the tower and an observer, while nearby residents agreed to organize, provide tools and join in fire fighting.[^3]

The sources give different dates for the build. The register says a Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Martin State Forest built it in 1932.[^1] A county survey quoted by a hobbyist site says the tower was completed in 1933.[^2] Taken together, the sources put the tower's arrival in 1931, its construction in 1932 and its completion in 1933.[^1][^2][^3] This story cannot say which of these is the right one.

In February 1958 a brush fire of about 100 acres near Indian Springs was spotted from the tower.[^3] Crane workers and 12 state firefighters worked to slow it across hilly ground.[^3] The register says the tower was removed from active service in 1976.[^1]

The register says it is still kept up for emergency use, and the public mostly visits it as a viewpoint.[^1] Its observation deck sits about two-thirds of the way up.[^1] The state says short spur trails from the forest trail lead to it.[^4] A hobbyist site describes it as standing, with condition below ideal, but calls the view great.[^2] The same site says the cab is climbable.[^2] This story does not encourage anyone to climb it.
