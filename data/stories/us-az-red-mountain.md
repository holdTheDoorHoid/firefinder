Red Mountain Lookout stands on Red Mountain in the Patagonia Mountains, southeast of the town of Patagonia in Santa Cruz County, Arizona.[^1][^2] It was built in 1973 and is the newest fire lookout on the Coronado National Forest.[^1][^2]

The cab is wooden and of the R-6 type, with a flat roof that overhangs the walls, and it has a catwalk with a wooden railing. A 10-foot concrete foundation supports it.[^1][^2] The R-6 design dates to 1953, when Region 6 of the Forest Service, which covers Washington and Oregon, first developed it.[^2] The cab measures 15 by 15 feet.[^2]

The register entry is dated January 31, 2003, as US 452 and AZ 45.[^1] The site now has communications equipment. The register notes several communications towers at the lookout, and the 2020 summary says the site also carries radio relay service for the Forest Service, Border Patrol and the county sheriff.[^1][^2] A memorial plaque to the Coleman family is on the site, but the summary has no further information about the family.[^2]

The register describes the lookout as in active service.[^1] A January 2020 summary calls it semi-active and says the Sierra Vista Ranger District administers it.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^3] I found no account of a notable fire at the site.[^1][^2][^3]

The sources disagree on elevation. The register gives 5,758 feet (1,755 m). The 2020 summary says the forest road ends at the lookout at 6,373 feet, and a 2010 hiking log gives the same figure for the summit.[^1][^2][^4]

A January 2020 summary says forest road FR-4649 leaves Harshaw Road south of Patagonia and runs to the lookout.[^2] A 2010 hike log lists a distance of six miles and a gain of 2,000 feet.[^4] I did not find a current statement on whether visitors may go up into the cab, and the sources do not mention a gate. Check with the Sierra Vista Ranger District before you go, since the road and the site may have changed since those reports were written.
