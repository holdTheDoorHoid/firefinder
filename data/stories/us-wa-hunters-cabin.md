Hunters Cabin was a fire lookout site in Lewis County, Washington, about fifteen miles east of Chehalis.[^2] The register lists the Washington Forest Fire Association as the administering agency.[^1] It gives the elevation as 2,681 feet (817 meters), while Willhite gives 2,695 feet, adding that early maps had 2,681.[^1][^4]

A trail led to a place called Hunters Cabin as early as 1916. The lookout site sits just over a mile from that cabin.[^4] The register and firelookout.com both date the first cabin on the site to 1940.[^1][^2] Willhite says the site's origin date is unknown, but the Kresek inventory also shows a cabin there in 1940.[^4] A 1925 newspaper item says a phone line was put in at Hunter's cabin.[^3] Willhite thinks the elevation in that item matches the lookout rather than the cabin.[^3][^4]

In June 1956 The Daily Chronicle reported that Elmer Balch would stand fire watch at Hunter's Cabin that summer.[^3] Willhite also names him as the lookout that year.[^4]

Aerial photos from 1952 show the lookout cabin.[^4] In 1954 a new cabin replaced the older one. The register and firelookout.com call it a U.S. Army cabin.[^1][^2][^4]

The lookout was given up in 1965.[^1][^2][^4] According to the register, it no longer stands.[^1]

According to Willhite, the final traces were wiped out in 2020 when a turbine went up for the Skookumchuck Wind Farm.[^4] His 2018 photos, taken just before the wind farm was built, show a pile of cabin boards under the duff and a bed frame.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site in April 2020.[^1]

Willhite places the site inside Weyerhaeuser's Vail Tree Farm and thinks a permit is probably needed.[^4] He also expects that the wind farm has added its own restrictions.[^4] We found no source that describes public access, so ask the landowner before going.
