Leek Spring Hill is a 7,629-foot peak in the Eldorado National Forest, in El Dorado County, California.[^1] The current cab dates to 1961.[^1] The register ties the cab to a CL-100 design from the 1959 revision of the National Plan, set on a 30-foot steel tower with K-braces.[^1]

The site was in use long before that.[^2] In 1915 Forest officers put up a tower and a lookout house, and local papers that year describe the tower as 50 or 60 feet tall, with a 14-foot-square house.[^2] The papers disagree on the tower height.[^2] Melvin Marshall worked the station in 1914 and returned for the 1915 season.[^2] Later papers name Eugene Compton on duty there in 1929, and Howard Cullers leaving for the station in 1936.[^2]

The papers also record fires. In August 1915 a fire near Peddler Hill was reported with its direction from the Leek Spring tower, and a forest fireman reached it in 23 minutes and held it to about two-fifths of an acre.[^2] In 1929 the lookout spotted a blaze along a pipeline below the El Dorado Power house, which papers thought careless smokers had started.[^2] The sources I read do not say what became of the 1915 tower or house.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] A road guide says the lookout is typically staffed during fire season.[^4] The register added the lookout on January 3, 2015, on a nomination by M. Sills.[^1] A visit note dated September 4, 2004 on the register says the lookout was manned and that visitors are welcome.[^1]

Access is the open question. The 2004 note gives directions from Forest Route 5 and says two-wheel drive can make the trip.[^1] The road guide describes an unpaved road of about 2.5 miles from the Mormon Emigrant Trail, suited only to four-wheel-drive vehicles, not plowed in winter, and usually open from mid-to-late June.[^4] Because the two accounts disagree, check current road and staffing conditions with the Eldorado National Forest before going.
