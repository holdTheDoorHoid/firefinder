The Rattlesnake Ridge lookout was a tower on a summit about three miles south-southwest of North Bend, in King County, Washington.[^2] The register lists the site at 3,517 feet, or 1,072 meters, under US 1296.[^1]

The register says the tower was built in the 1930s and is now gone.[^1][^2] It also lists the Washington Division of Forestry and DNR as the administering agency.[^1]

The Willhite lookouts page treats this summit as the East Peak of Rattlesnake Mountain.[^3] It says the tower is known from the Kresek lookout inventory, which lists a tower there in the 1930s.[^3] The page's author has found no map or article about the tower.[^3] USGS notes from 1937 and 1956 do not mention one, and the author reasons that, given the land ownership of the time, the tower would have stood on Weyerhaeuser land.[^3]

A 1953 map on the same page marks an Airway Beacon still in place on the East summit, at the same 3,517-foot elevation.[^3] Two photo captions on the page mention an Airway Beacon, one of them at the summit, and neither gives a date.[^3]

The page describes a trail that runs the length of the mountain, with trailheads at each end. It names the Rattlesnake Ledge trailhead as one of them and says that trailhead is near North Bend, reached from I-90.[^3] We found no source that describes a lookout standing on the summit today, and none says who manages the summit now.
