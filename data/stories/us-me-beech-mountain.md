Beech Mountain sits on the west side of Mount Desert Island, inside Acadia National Park.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the mountain has hosted a lookout since 1943, while the Park Service says the first wooden tower was in use from 1941.[^1][^2][^3]

Civilian Conservation Corps workers built that original wooden lookout, and it was a key link in fire-warning communications during the Fire of 1947.[^2] When it began to deteriorate, a steel tower was airlifted into place in 1960 under Operation Beech.[^2] The register describes the present prefab steel tower as built between 1960 and 1962, and the Park Service says the lookout was operational again in 1962.[^1][^2]

Staffing has changed over time. The Park Service says daytime staffing was cut back to times of high fire danger, and that the tower has not been manned since 1976.[^2][^3] The register, though, says park rangers still staff it during high fire danger.[^1] The two accounts do not agree, so check before you go.

Of the two lookouts the park officially used, only the Beech Mountain tower remains. Maine has more than 140 lookouts still standing, and this one is among them.[^2]

Today, visitors hike to the tower from the Beech Mountain Parking Lot.[^2] When park staff are present, often in summer, the platform and inside room are open to the public.[^2] Leashed dogs may join hikers on the trails, but they cannot climb the tower because of its open-grate steps.[^2] A 2009 Park Service notice said the tower is usually not open to the public, so plan on it being closed unless staff are there.[^2][^3]
