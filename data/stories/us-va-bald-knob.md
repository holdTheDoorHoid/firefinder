Bald Knob Lookout stands on Warm Springs Mountain in Bath County, Virginia, at 4,249 feet (1,295 m).[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1934 for the Virginia Department of Forestry, on a state-owned easement from the Hot Springs Company.[^1] It is a 35-foot steel Aermotor tower, 7 by 7 feet, bought on a cooperative basis: the state and the Bath County Protective Association each paid half.[^1] A 1935 Coast and Geodetic Survey recovery note, quoted on a Weebly lookout page, places the fire tower on the highest part of the knob.[^3]

The U.S. Forest Service later bought the property.[^1] The property now lies within The Nature Conservancy's Warm Springs Mountain Preserve, and the Register lists the Conservancy as administrator.[^1] The Weebly page instead names the Virginia Department of Forestry, so the two pages differ on who runs the tower.[^1][^3]

A Register visit report from May 10, 2018 rated the tower in good condition and found it not staffed.[^1] The same report says an NHLR sign is posted, and its comments tell visitors to follow the fire road up from a red gate off Airport Road.[^1]

The preserve is open during daylight hours, and its public trails are Ingalls Overlook, Sandy Gap and Bear Loop.[^4] The preserve page does not say whether the public may reach the tower itself, and no source describes a rental.[^1][^4] Ask The Nature Conservancy before going, and do not climb the tower unless its access has been confirmed.
