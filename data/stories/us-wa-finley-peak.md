Finley Peak is in Jefferson County, Washington, in what is now Olympic National Park.[^1][^4] The U.S. Forest Service built a log cabin lookout there in 1916.[^1][^2] It had a hip roof and sat in a saddle on a knife-edge ridge between the peak's highpoints.[^1][^4] WillhiteWeb considers it probably the earliest lookout structure in the Olympic National Park area.[^4]

In 1919 it was among the earliest lookouts to get a radio for reporting fires.[^1][^2] In July 1920 a Mason County Journal report said the forest supervisor had appointed Mavie Olsen, a pioneer school teacher, as lookout, and called her the first woman lookout appointed for the forest.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb spells the name Olson in its profile of her, and says the lookout was her summer home while she taught school in winter.[^4]

In 1921 newspapers reported a new wireless station at the lookout.[^3][^4] A 1932 Centralia newspaper report said Edwin Wilson was in charge of its radio observation station, which looked out over most of Jefferson County.[^3][^4]

The lookout was abandoned in 1930.[^1][^2][^4] The sources disagree on how the cabin ended. The register says it was removed in 1947, while firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^4]

No source we read describes a standing structure today. WillhiteWeb's photos have captions that mention stove parts and a built-up base at the site, and one is labeled as the lookout site in 2018.[^4]

Getting there is hard. WillhiteWeb says Finley Peak has no easy access, partly because maps label its summit in different places. The most often attempted route runs via Three Lakes, and then up an abandoned trail that reportedly disappears after half a mile.[^4] It warns that the southern route is only for people experienced with steep off-trail route finding.[^4] The site is in a national park.[^1] Check current park rules before you go.
