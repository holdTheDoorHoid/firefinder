Carrisa Lookout stands in the Sacramento Ranger District of the Lincoln National Forest, in Otero County, New Mexico.[^1][^3] The register describes an 80-foot Aermotor MC-39 tower with a 7-by-7-foot steel cab, constructed in 1935 by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1][^3] The Forest Service gives the tower's erection date as 1934, and puts the observer's cabin and shed in 1935.[^2]

The name is spelled more than one way. The register uses Carrisa, while the Forest Service and the 2020 report use Carrissa.[^1][^2][^3] The 2020 report says the National Register nomination spelled it "Carissa" by mistake.[^3]

Two 1958 newspaper items, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page under Carrissa, concern fire guards at a lookout the papers call the Carrizo or Carizozo fire lookout.[^4] An April item says a man was to serve as fire guard there that summer. An August item says a couple had served there that summer. The spelling varies between items, so the link to this tower is not certain. An October 1932 item on the same page mentions a new Forest Service road at a "Carisso tower." If that refers to this site, a tower stood here before the 1934 and 1935 dates.[^4]

The complex was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1988.[^2][^3] According to the 2020 report, work started in 2010 and finished in 2012.[^3] The Forest Service says the structures were restored in 2012.[^2][^3] The 2020 report says it was the Lincoln National Forest's first restoration effort aimed at a future cabin rental program.[^3] The register added it on February 12, 2012, with Mark H. Gutzman as nominator.[^1]

Today, the Forest Service page, last updated February 19, 2025, labels the site "Site Open." The road to the lookout is gated and closed, so the area is reached on foot.[^2] The page says the site "is being converted soon to a cabin rental site," but I found no source saying it has opened.[^2] The 2020 report also says the vault toilet was replaced with an accessible toilet.[^3]
