The Mara fire lookout stands on the summit of Morton Peak, also known as Mara Mountain, in British Columbia.[^4] The B.C. Forest Service built the original lookout in 1925. A new structure replaced it in 1950, and the Forest Service used that building until 1998.[^1][^2]

At one point the building was in poor condition, and its removal was being discussed.[^2] The Kingfisher Interpretive Centre stepped in, taking charge of the building and carrying out repairs.[^2]

In 2012 the Ministry of Forests began restoring the lookout to expand local recreation.[^1] The province said the initial restoration and upgrading was expected to be completed in mid-October.[^3] A local account says the B.C. government funded a complete restoration in 2012 and then assumed control of the site through Recreation Sites and Trails BC.[^2] The province called the 2012 work initial, while the local account describes that government funding as a complete restoration.[^3][^2]

Today the summit can be reached by a 9.4 km trail or by a rough road.[^5][^4] The trail partly follows the old pack trail. The maintained section ends at a 4x4 post at the entrance to Kingfisher Creek Provincial Park. Past that point, the route has flags but is harder to follow in places.[^5]

From Kingfisher, the road to the top covers 31.9 km (19.82 miles).[^4] It needs high clearance and a 4WD higher up, and it may be closed by snow until August.[^4] Mara Mountain reaches 2,249 m (7,378 ft) above sea level.[^4] None of the sources read says whether the building is open to visitors, so treat the summit as a hiking and driving destination, not a place to climb into the structure.[^4][^5]
