The St. Croix fire tower stands in St. Croix State Park in Pine County, Minnesota.[^1][^4] The National Register nomination for the park's CCC and WPA historic resources describes it as a standard 100-foot steel tower, braced with crossing members, and lists it among the historic district's features.[^2] One source calls it a 100-foot Aermotor tower.[^3]

Sources differ on the year it went up. The Forest Lookouts page and a Savage Pacer column say 1936,[^3][^5] while the state park and Wikipedia say 1937.[^4][^6] The nomination gives 1936 to 1937.[^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it, according to the park and Wikipedia.[^4][^6]

A 1948 newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page names a woman employed at the tower, and it mentions several bad fires that week.[^3] The park says watchmen spent hours at a time in the tower scanning for fires without breaks, until staffing ended in 1981 and aerial surveys became the norm.[^4]

Today the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^1] The park says visitors may climb it.[^4] A Savage Pacer column notes that the state's fire towers are usually open to the public but closed from November 1 through May 31, and it advises checking ahead before you go.[^5]
