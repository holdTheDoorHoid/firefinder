In December 1931, the forest fire protection branch of the Wisconsin conservation commission was building a telephone network and four watch towers in its 11th district. One tower was to stand at Quincy Bluff, southwest of Friendship, according to a newspaper notice from that month quoted on the Forest Lookouts page.[^1] A May 1932 item listed Quincy among the district's finished towers.[^1] The sources read do not give the tower's height, design or builder.

The sources read do not name the people who staffed it, and they describe no particular smokes or fires at the site.

A tornado on June 23, 2004 damaged trees in the Quincy Bluff area and the lookout tower, according to the Forest Lookouts log.[^1] A 2015 Star News article says the tower was destroyed in that tornado, and reports that DNR northern region supervisor Dan Schumacher cited it as an example.[^3] The two accounts do not agree on how much was lost.

The Star News gives the cost of the rebuilt tower as more than $300,000 and dates the rebuild to 2009.[^3] The Forest Lookouts log places the completed new tower in June 2009 and says it was finished enough in April to go back into service.[^1]

In December 2015 the DNR announced plans to close all of its remaining fire towers.[^3] The firelookout.org list of Wisconsin lookouts still marks the Quincy Bluff tower as standing, but that list carries no date.[^2] The sources do not say what the DNR decided for this tower, or whether anyone staffs it now.

The bluff lies within the Quincy Bluff and Wetlands State Natural Area, which the DNR took over from The Nature Conservancy.[^4] A visitor's blog says the site has dozens of ways in, and also notes that several private in-holdings are closed to the public.[^4] The sources do not say whether the tower itself can be approached. Ask the DNR before going, and do not climb it.
