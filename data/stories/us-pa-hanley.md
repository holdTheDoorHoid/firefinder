The Hanley tower stood in Elk County, Pennsylvania, on land belonging to the Hanley Oil Company.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes it as a steel Aermotor tower built in 1926.[^1] A survey description, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, calls it an 80-foot steel fire tower of the Pennsylvania State Department of Forests and Waters.[^2] It places the tower about 600 feet south of the Highland–Sackett public road, 1.7 miles southwest of the village of Highland.[^2] The steel cabin on top measured about 7 by 7 by 9 feet and had 4-foot glass windows on every side.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed around 1990.[^1] A 2004 station recovery note, also quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the tower had been abandoned and removed, and that the spot had become dense woods.[^2] Neither source gives an exact removal date.[^1][^2]

The Forest Lookouts page lists the Allegheny National Forest as the agency.[^2] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today or whether visitors can reach it.
