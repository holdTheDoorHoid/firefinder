The Stoddard Creek lookout is a two-story cab, 10 feet by 10, on the summit in Idaho County.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com says the cab is the only structure ever on that summit, and that it sits in the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness.[^2]

The register dates the cab to 1934 and says it was occupied from about 1941 through the mid-1970s.[^1] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts also give 1934.[^2][^3] A 1982 history, quoted in the climbing guide, places the build closer to 1945, under a forest supervisor who ordered 10-by-10 cabs to save money.[^4] The sources disagree, and the 1934 date is the one most of them share.[^1][^2][^3]

Firelookout.com says the cab was used for emergencies into the 1970s.[^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts says it has no documented staffing history for the lookout.[^3] The 1982 history says the lookout reported many fires over the years.[^4]

The same history says the lookout was recently abandoned in 1977.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts and firelookout.com both describe it as abandoned since the 1970s.[^3][^2] The history also says a Wilderness Planning team urged that the cab be destroyed, citing serious hazards inside.[^4] The register lists an August 2015 photo, and recorded the lookout on March 20, 2016.[^1] The sources do not say what state the cab is in now.[^1][^2][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the site as hike-in.[^3] The 1982 history describes access on foot or by horseback, at least two days along the Middle Fork-Stoddard Creek Trail from the Middle Fork's mouth to the Main Salmon River.[^4] The sources describe the inside as unsafe, and none of them describes public entry or a rental.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
