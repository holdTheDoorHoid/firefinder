The Register dates a bonfire on the highest peak in Pilot Hill to the Gold Rush era. It served as a signal fire for travelers and merchants carrying goods to the miners.[^1] The present building's date is disputed. The Register dates its as-built plans to 1958, says the building is thought to date from the Folsom Dam project, and says it succeeded a 1935 CCC lookout.[^1] Peakbagging.com instead gives 1985 for the tower, also tied to the Folsom Dam project.[^2] The Register's plan dates are the more specific, so this account leans toward 1958, but the sources disagree. The building is based on the revised 809R plan, with a few deviations. It has aluminum sheeting and angled cab windows.[^1]

CAL FIRE lists the El Dorado County lookout as Pilot Peak Lookout in its Amador-El Dorado Unit.[^3] Peakbagging.com says the tower fell into disrepair, lost its roof in a storm, and had its cameras removed by 2008.[^2] After CAL FIRE finished repairs, volunteers took over the watch when the tower reopened in June 2019, mostly working 6 to 12 hour shifts.[^2] A CBS Sacramento report says the Pilot Peak Smoke Seekers, a volunteer group, keep watch for 12 hours a day, every day of the week through fire season, and that its volunteers were first to notice a fire.[^4] The sources do not say outright that the Pilot Peak name refers to this building, but the descriptions match, so this account treats them as one tower.

The Register says visitors cannot easily reach the site. It sits roughly 1.5 miles from a paved road, behind a private subdivision and a security gate, according to a note marked 10/91.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^5] Anyone planning a visit should ask CAL FIRE first.
