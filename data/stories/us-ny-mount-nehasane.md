Mount Nehasane is a lookout site in Hamilton County.[^1] The name also belongs to Nehasane Park, a private preserve that the Webb family owned.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list shows a platform tower on Mount Nehasane and marks it gone.[^1]

Nehasane Park was private, so the Webb family, not the state, hired the observer on Mount Electra.[^2] The state shared the cost of paying that observer.[^3]

Do not confuse the Mount Nehasane platform with Mount Electra. Mount Electra, once called Rock Lake Mountain, had a 60-foot Aermotor LS40 tower that the Webb estate erected about 1920.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Mount Nehasane platform on its own line.[^1]

The two sources give different accounts of how the Electra tower ended. The NYSFFLA tower page says it was dismantled in 1989 and left at the site.[^3] Adirondack Life says the Electra tower was pushed over.[^4]

We found no source that describes the Mount Nehasane site today, or says whether the public can reach it. In a 1908 photograph, Adirondack Life describes the Nehasane Park backdrop as part of today's William C. Whitney Wilderness Area.[^4]
