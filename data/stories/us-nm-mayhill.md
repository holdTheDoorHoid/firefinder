The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Mayhill lookout in 1935, as part of its Mayhill camp, F-24-N.[^1][^2] The camp had put up its permanent buildings in 1934.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 62-foot Aeromotor LX-24 tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab and wooden steps.[^1] The Forest Service gives the tower as 48 feet tall, so the two sources disagree on its height.[^1][^2]

In August 1938 the Alamogordo News reported that remodeling of the Mayhill tower and cabin, along with two other towers, had been completed.[^5]

In 1967 the tower was moved to James Ridge, where it became the James Ridge Lookout.[^2][^3] The Forest Service says the 1967 move is the reference point for whether the tower can qualify for the National Register of Historic Places.[^3] The original site no longer has its tower, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as gone.[^7]

The James Ridge tower stands on the Sacramento Ranger District and is also on the National Historic Lookout Register as US 1110, NM 36.[^3][^4] The Forest Service lists the site as open.[^3] It says the lookout has been extensively remodeled since the move, sits on a 62-foot steel tower, and that no potable water is available there.[^3] At the original Mayhill site, a Flickr album titled Mayhill Lookout shows the former site and an antenna that now sits nearby.[^6] No other source we read describes what is at the old site now.
