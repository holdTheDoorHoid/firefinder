The Beaver Creek (West Point) Fire Tower went up in 1951 to guard the Beaver Creek and Yellow Creek forest areas, two miles south of West Point, Ohio.[^1] A newspaper report from late April 1951 said the 80-foot steel tower was nearly finished, and that it was one of ten the state's Division of Forestry was putting up that year.[^1]

That report also placed the tower on the county's highest point, the 1,447-foot Round Knob in Madison Township, and said its glassed-in cabin was expected to be finished early the following week.[^1]

The plan was for daylight-only staffing, by a lookout the Ohio Division of Forestry would name, and for the tower to run only in critical fire seasons: late March to May, and September and October.[^1] Floyd H. Beatty was to be the towerman for the fall 1951 season.[^1] An item dated October 31, 1951 said the tower had spotted a grass fire that Tuesday afternoon, and that Wellsville volunteers put it out.[^1] Margaret Ronshak started in spring 1967, and a March 6, 1968 item still named her as observer at the Madison Township fire tower.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register and the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register both recorded the tower on September 3, 2014.[^2][^3] The Register gives its location as Beaver Creek State Forest, administered by the Ohio Division of Forestry, and numbers it US 1022.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register lists a separate entry, US 1578, for the "West Point Tower Site," and says the tower "may have been moved to Beaver Creek State Park."[^3]

A statewide list from the Forest Fire Lookout Association still marks the tower as standing.[^4] The Forest Lookouts page for the site, however, ends with a single word, "Removed," and gives no date.[^1] The sources disagree, and none of them says when or why the tower may have been taken down or moved.

None of the sources read describes current access, rental, or the tower's condition, so those remain unknown. Before any visit, check with the Ohio Division of Forestry, and do not climb a tower that is closed or off limits.
