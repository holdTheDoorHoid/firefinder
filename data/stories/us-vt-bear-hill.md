Bear Hill sits inside Allis State Park, in the town of Brookfield, Orange County, Vermont.[^1][^2] The state forestry department finished a wooden lookout tower on the summit in November 1932.[^1] It stood 40 feet tall and had a glass-enclosed top.[^1] The land came to the state from a donor named Allis, and that gift gave the park its name.[^1]

In 1938 the state rebuilt the cab, repaired the telephone line and prepared the station so a lookout watchman could work there.[^1] That same year a lightning strike hurt the wooden cab, and Civilian Conservation Corps crews made repairs.[^2] A 1940 state report describes a hurricane that tipped the tower onto two legs, with guy cables keeping it from falling over.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page names no lookout who worked the tower and no particular fire it spotted.[^1]

The wooden tower did not last. A state report covering 1947 to 1948 says lightning struck it and the fire that followed destroyed it.[^1] The replacement had a steel base topped by a wooden cab.[^1] In 1955 the wooden tower was taken down for safety reasons, and in spring 1956 a steel tower brought from Gilson Mountain went up on the site.[^1]

Sources disagree about the steel tower. The National Register nomination for Allis State Park says a private organization built the present steel tower in 1966, replacing an original wooden tower built in 1933.[^2] That contradicts the state's 1956 date, and the 1933 date also differs from the 1932 completion date in the newspaper accounts.[^1][^2] We could not settle which is right.

The present tower stands about 48 feet tall, according to a 2025 news report.[^3] In 2025 a $450,000 grant was being used for fencing, metal grating on the stairs, an interior railing and lightning grounding. The project began on Burke Mountain and Bear Hill.[^3] A 2025 Vermont chapter report says maintenance was completed at Okemo, Burke and Bear Hill, and the work there included lightning protection, painting, railings inside the cab, and wire mesh on the stairs and landings.[^4] The tower is listed as standing.[^5] Allis State Park is one of the oldest state parks in Vermont.[^2] We found no current rules about climbing the tower, so ask the park before going up.
