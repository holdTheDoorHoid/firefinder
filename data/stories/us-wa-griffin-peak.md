Griffin Peak is a former lookout site in the Umatilla National Forest, about 20 miles east of Walla Walla, in Columbia County, Washington.[^1][^2]

Panoramic photographs taken on August 19, 1935 were shot from a tower about 20 feet above the ground.[^4] The same photographs also capture the road into the site and a gate.[^4] The sources do not say whether that tower was the same one built later.[^4]

In about 1938 a tower with an L-4 cab went up on the peak, according to the Kresek Inventory as cited by WillhiteWeb.[^4] A Washington lookouts listing gives the same circa-1938 date and calls it an L-4 tower.[^3]

Sources agree that the tower was eventually abandoned and no longer stands.[^1][^2][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register added the site on December 17, 2012.[^1]

We found no source describing what, if anything, remains at the site today, and none says whether the public can visit it.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as the administering agency.[^1]
