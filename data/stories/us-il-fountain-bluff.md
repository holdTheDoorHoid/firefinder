On August 27, 1934 the Murphysboro Daily Independent reported plans for a steel lookout and observation station on Big Hill, the local name for Fountain Bluff.[^1] A later listing calls the area a popular resort for picnics, with a Forest Service lookout station and a 60-foot tower where visitors were welcome.[^1]

By mid-November 1934 the station was operating.[^1] Forest Service workers from Camp Glenn had put out six fires since November 1, and most of the reports came from the lookout station.[^1]

In April 1936 a C.C.C. Camp Glenn worker posted at the tower became seriously ill while on duty.[^1] He was later back at his post.[^1] In the month before mid-April 1936, the paper counted 310 visitors to the tower.[^1]

In June 1949 the Southern Illinoisan and Daily Independent reported that vandals had damaged picnic tables and fireplaces near the fire tower on the summit.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts site marks the tower as removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as gone.[^1][^2] Wikipedia says a public fire tower had long stood on the summit and was reported dismantled as of 1985.[^3]

A National Geodetic Survey recovery note from 1975, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says the survey mark could not be found.[^1] The same note also records remains nearby: an old concrete foundation and some pieces of an old tower.[^1]

Wikipedia says the hill is part of the Shawnee National Forest, apart from a small private portion, and gives Happy Hollow Road off Illinois Route 3 as the access road.[^3] No source I found describes what remains on the summit today, or whether the 1975 foundation is still there.
