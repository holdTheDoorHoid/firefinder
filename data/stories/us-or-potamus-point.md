Potamus Point sits in Morrow County, Oregon, in the Umatilla National Forest, about 16 miles southwest of Ukiah.[^1][^2] The lookout was built in the 1930s as a 60-foot wooden L-4 tower.[^2][^1] A 1932 newspaper notice describes a planned three-mile telephone line to the emergency lookout station on Potamus, which suggests the station already existed by then.[^3] A bronze survey disk stamped with 1936 lies under the center of the tower, according to a later survey note.[^3]

Firelookout.com lists Max Buschke as the lookout in 1934 and Owen Leathers in 1936.[^1] Newspaper notices from August 1937 first report Jim Stevens back at the Potamus lookout, then report that he had not returned.[^3]

Every source that mentions the tower agrees it is gone. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it is now gone, and firelookout.com marks it gone too.[^2][^1] The Forest Lookouts history lists it as removed but gives no date.[^3] None of the sources says when the tower came down.

The Forest Service lists the Potamus Point Interpretive Site as open, with a scenic view, and says potable water is not available there.[^4] A tourism listing calls it a stop on the Blue Mountain Scenic Byway, with a canyon view down the North Fork John Day River drainage.[^5] Neither page mentions the lookout, so no source describes what, if anything, remains of the tower site today. The Forest Service page showed a Phase A public use restriction when we read it, so check current rules before you go.[^4]
