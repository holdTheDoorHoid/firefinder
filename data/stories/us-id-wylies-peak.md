Wylies Peak's lookout was a log cabin with a cupola, sitting on a massive granite boulder.[^4] Firelookout.com, Idaho Fire Lookouts and the Forest Service all give 1925 as the build year.[^1][^2][^4] A January 1977 Spokane Daily Chronicle item, quoted on Rex's Fire Lookout Page, gives an earlier window of 1923 or 1924, crediting Floyd Cossit and a crew who hauled building logs up the rock by cable.[^3] Rex's own timeline puts the cabin in 1923.[^3]

In 1957 the lookout passed from Bitterroot National Forest to Nez Perce National Forest oversight.[^3] Summer staffing ran until 1934, and the Forest Service says it was closed that year.[^3][^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts names Al Koenigs for 1927 and Al Williams for 1934.[^2]

The National Register of Historic Places listed the lookout on June 5, 1975, and delisted it on October 17, 1983, according to Wikipedia's Idaho County table.[^5] An NRHP index page instead gives 1983 as the year it was added.[^6] Firelookout.com says it stood abandoned for years.[^1] A 1977 article quoted on Rex's page said the Forest Service intended to restore the building.[^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts and the Forest Service both say lightning destroyed the cabin in 1983.[^2][^4] Rex's page instead describes a 1983 structural fire of uncertain origin, possibly lightning or vandalism.[^3]

Firelookout.com places the site in the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness, and Idaho Fire Lookouts marks it as hike-in.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as gone.[^7] No source describes what remains on the peak today.
