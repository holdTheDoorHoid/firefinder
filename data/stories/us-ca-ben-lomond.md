Ben Lomond is a peak in the Plumas National Forest, north of Belden, looking down on the North Fork of the Feather River.[^1][^2] Its lookout was a small square cab on the peak.[^2] A history sheet quotes a 1985 description of it as a small square building with windows on every side. The sheet gives it as about ten feet square under a peaked roof, and dates its construction to before 1915.[^2]

The sheet gives the elevation as 6,453 feet, while a benchmark table gives 6,457 feet, so the height is roughly 6,450 feet.[^2][^4]

Buffe Williams is recorded as manning the lookout in 1915.[^2] Newspaper notices from 1920 to 1924 place Tom Gray at the station each summer, and a 1924 notice says he had gone there for the five previous years as well.[^1] In June 1930, Ranger Robert Harvey Abby's diary mentions a lookout man who had just moved to Ben Lomond for the summer fire season.[^2]

A 1933 map shows the lookout, but a 1937 lookout study does not, so the history sheet judges that it was probably abandoned between 1933 and 1936.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as gone.[^3]

The same sheet says some debris from the lookout remains on the peak.[^2] No source describes the summit more fully, or says whether it is open to visitors.
