The Leon Fire Tower Site is a former lookout in Crenshaw County, Alabama, administered by the Alabama Forestry Commission.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added it on September 18, 2019, at the nomination of Andrew Zerbe, under the numbers US 1765 and AL 26.[^1] The register lists the site's elevation as 487 feet, or 148 meters.[^1]

Thomas Kaufmann, writing an opinion column for 1819 News, says that over 200 of these lookouts once dotted Alabama.[^2] He names aircraft as the most common explanation for their decline, since planes could see fires over a far wider expanse than a 100-foot tower could.[^2] He adds that neglect, and the liability of people climbing the towers, led to their dismantling.[^2] He also says the state now keeps around a third of its former lookouts.[^2]

Today, a water tank stands on the site of the former tower, and the register says no trace of the tower remains.[^1] No source we read says whether the grounds are open to visitors, and there is no tower left to climb.[^1]
