Grass Mountain rises to about 3,600 feet in Benton County, roughly 26 miles southeast of Newport, and a fire lookout stood on its summit from the mid-1930s until 1968.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on when the first tower went up. A Eugene newspaper said in October 1934 that the tower and its house were practically finished, but the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register and Wikipedia both date the 40-foot tower to 1935.[^3][^1][^4] Its cab measured 14 by 14 feet.[^1]

Several people staffed the station, and some of them were couples. In 1935 a Mrs. Norman Sapp, with her infant son, spent time there with her husband.[^3] A 1937 item named Lloyd Sassman as the stationed lookout, and later notices named the Cathcarts in 1939 and the Taylors in 1942.[^3] In 1937 the Siuslaw National Forest supervisor, R. S. Shelley, directed the lookouts, this one included.[^3]

In 1946 the newly organized Polk-Benton District of the State Board of Forestry agreed to maintain the station.[^3] A new tower was erected in 1950, and a 1951 record describes a 42-foot timber tower, while FFLOS gives 40 feet for the 1951 tower.[^3][^1] A windstorm in October 1962 caused the loss of the garage, and a ground cabin was built in 1963 with money from a state restoration fund.[^3] The lookout was removed in 1968, according to FFLOS and Wikipedia, which says it was dismantled. The Forest Lookouts page gives 1969 instead.[^1][^4][^3]

The tower itself is gone, but a Summit Post trip report says concrete footings remain in the area. The same report says trees block every view except the one to the south.[^5] The same report describes an older road that leads to a gate where the hike begins.[^5] We found no official source on current access or ownership, so anyone planning a visit should check with the land manager first.
