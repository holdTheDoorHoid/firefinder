The Rehoboth Tower Site #1 stood on Great Meadow Hill, at 264 feet (80 meters), in Rehoboth, Massachusetts.[^2] Rehoboth is in Bristol County.[^1]

Sources disagree about where the first tower came from. The register says a 28-foot steel tower stood on nearby Richmond Hill in 1912 and was moved to Great Meadow Hill in 1916.[^1] The former-site register says the tower was a 50-foot steel structure that came to the hill from Dighton and began operating in 1916, and that a 12-foot extension and spiral stairs were added after the move.[^2] We could not settle which account is right. Both agree the tower was on Great Meadow Hill from 1916.[^1][^2]

The state forest history confirms that the state owned a fire tower on the summit of Great Meadow Hill.[^3] When the Commonwealth bought about 140 acres and set up Rehoboth State Forest in 1934, it noted that an existing fire tower was nearby on the summit.[^3]

Until 1922 the tower stood on private land with the owner's consent; the land was then officially bought.[^2] A new 68-foot tower replaced the older one in 1931.[^2] The register also dates the 68-foot tower to 1931.[^1] In 1946 the tower received a new cab and two-way radios.[^2]

The U.S. Army required the tower's removal in 1955 on national security grounds.[^2] The register places the reconstructed tower at its present spot on Long Hill, and the former-site register says it was moved about three miles south.[^1][^2] The state forest history gives a different account. It says a Nike Battery PR-19 fire-control area built at the top of Great Meadow Hill in 1955 forced the tower to move to Reservoir Avenue in Rehoboth.[^3] We could not reconcile the two accounts.

The state forest history places the PR-19 fire-control area at the top of Great Meadow Hill.[^3] One Nike history lists the same PR-19 area at 13 Fire Tower Road and says the area is now a Massachusetts Army National Guard armory.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, of the tower remains on the summit, or whether visitors can reach the hilltop.

The tower itself is at its present location on Long Hill, according to the register, and the Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control administers it.[^1] We found no recent visit or condition report for it.
