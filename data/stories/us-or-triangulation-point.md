The Oregon Department of Forestry built a wooden live-in lookout on Triangulation Point in 1955, on a 3,294-foot peak about 15 miles northeast of Tillamook.[^1][^2] Sources disagree on its size.[^1][^3] The register calls it a 12-foot wooden live-in tower, while a 1955 annual report to the Oregon Department of Forestry, quoted on another site, describes a 14-by-14 cabin constructed on the ground.[^1][^3] That report gives the cost of the work, excluding furniture, as $3,138.63, and says the cabin was built to replace the lookout on nearby Cedar Butte.[^3][^4]

The peak sits inside the Tillamook Burn, and dead trees left by those fires still dot the surrounding forest.[^3][^4]

In June 1957 a local paper, the Vernonia Eagle, reported that Margaret Wells would start work at the lookout the coming Monday.[^3]

The register says the cabin was used only for emergencies from 1964, and a second site marks it gone in 1966.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page lists the site only as "Removed," with no date or account of how the cabin came down.[^3]

The summit is now a satellite relay site behind a gate with a radiofrequency warning sign.[^4] The track to it passes that gate, so the open alternative is South Triangulation Point, which can be reached by scrambling up from a road junction.[^4] The Oregon Hikers page notes that a fire lookout once stood on the summit, but none of the sources cited here describes what, if anything, remains of it.[^4]
