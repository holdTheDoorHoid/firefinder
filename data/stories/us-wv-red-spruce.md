The Red Spruce tower stood in Pocahontas County, West Virginia, on the Monongahela National Forest.[^1][^2] Civilian Conservation Corps labor put it up in 1940. It was a 40-foot steel tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab.[^1][^2][^3]

A Forest Service history counts it among the nine towers the CCC built on the forest between 1933 and 1945.[^3]

The lookout William McCloud was on duty on April 19, 1963, when a storm hit. The forest's history says he spent three hours in severe winds while an unrelenting electrical storm battered his cab.[^3] The last year the tower was staffed was 1965.[^1][^2]

The tower is now listed as gone, though no source we read gives the year it came down.[^5][^2] A Forest Service history says most of the forest's towers were demolished, with only concrete piers and steps left behind.[^3] The register added this site on September 16, 2008, on the nomination of Henry Isenberg.[^1]

A Monongahela trail guide describes a fire tower site on Red Spruce Knob. We take it to be this same site, but no source says so outright.[^4] The guide describes a 2.5-mile round-trip hike that begins just off the Highland Scenic Highway. It says the tower's concrete remains sit in the middle of the loop, next to some old metal gear.[^4]
