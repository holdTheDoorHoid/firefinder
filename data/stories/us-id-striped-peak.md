Striped Peak lies in Shoshone County, Idaho, about four miles southwest of Wallace.[^3] It lies along the crest of Saint Joe Mountain, straight south of Osborn.[^5]

The lookout was in the works by 1919.[^2] The Wallace Miner reported that E. C. Pulaski, the local Forest Service representative, said a station would soon be established on the peak.[^2] The register names the Forest Service's Ed Pulaski as the man behind the announcement.[^1] The register dates the structure to 1919 or 1920, and describes it as a 12-by-12 D-6 cupola.[^1] Firelookout.com instead says it was built in 1924, and the register shows a photo captioned circa 1924.[^1][^3] The sources do not agree on when it went up.

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists two people who staffed it. Everett H. Bryan is given for 1920, citing a North Idaho Press item dated 1968, and G. Richardson for 1928, citing a Wallace Press-Times item from that year.[^4] The 1968 date on that first citation is nearly fifty years after 1920, so the 1920 date is unverified. In July 1929 the Spokesman-Review reported that a forest fire along Marble Creek had nearly burned out, as the Striped Peak lookout had reported to the ranger station.[^2]

The lookout was destroyed by a wildfire in 1936 and never rebuilt.[^1][^2][^4][^5] Firelookout.com says it was accidentally burned that year.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts reports that a book, Up the Swiftwater, places a lightning strike and burning in the 1960s.[^4] Four other sources give 1936, so we follow them and treat the book's account as unconfirmed.

The register says some remains are still on the site.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts, in a post dated June 25, 2025, says the D-6 foundation is fairly easy to see and that many remnants are in a pit where the building burned. It also found a stacked rock foundation that may have been a second building, and a memorial whose honoree it has not identified.[^4] A climbing guide published in March 2021 describes hiking routes to the summit, from Elsie Lake or from a road out of Osborn, and says the summit view makes the climb worthwhile.[^5] Idaho Fire Lookouts labels the site hike-in.[^4] We found no closure notice, but the sources do not describe current conditions, so check with the Forest Service before you go.
