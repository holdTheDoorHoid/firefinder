Number 4 Mountain is in Piscataquis County, and the vistas just past the old tower site look out over Moosehead Lake.[^1][^2][^6]

Forest Lookouts dates the site's start as a lookout to 1913.[^1] A Lewiston Daily Sun item from May 1935 describes a 48-foot tower there.[^1] The sources we read do not say when the present structure went up.

Fire wardens once staffed the tower, and the trail today roughly follows their old route up the mountain.[^6] We found no notable fire on the mountain in the sources we read.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing with an asterisk, which means the structure is still there and the cab is gone.[^2][^3] A hiking guide says the remains of an old fire tower are at the summit.[^4] The same guide says visitors can climb a ladder for the views, but the Bureau of Parks and Lands' 2014 plan update for the Moosehead area calls the tower closed.[^4][^5] Where the sources disagree, we follow the plan and advise against climbing.

The 2014 plan describes the trail as reached from a trailhead off Meadow Brook Road that was still to be developed.[^5] A trail site's directions describe that road as private land the public can still reach, with a temporary parking area beside it.[^7] The trail runs about 1.7 miles one way.[^6] The same site cautions that access to the trail is not assured in any season.[^7] Please leave the tower alone and check conditions before you go.
