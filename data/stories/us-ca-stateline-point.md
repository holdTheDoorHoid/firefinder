The Stateline Point lookout stood on a hill above Kings Beach, at the north end of Lake Tahoe on the California-Nevada border.[^3] The register calls it a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 ground cabin, and notes a further photo of a 20-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1] The register gives its build date only as the 1930s.[^1] A compilation of California lookouts dates a lookout cabin on the site to 1936.[^2]

Newspaper items quoted in that compilation name some of the people who staffed the post.[^2] In August 1937 Lookout Fireman Johnny Nelson, then on Stateline Point, took in a fawn that a tourist had accidentally hit on the highway.[^2] The fawn, later named Duke, spent the winter at the Galena Creek ERA camp, where the workers decided it should be the camp's chief mascot.[^2] In July 1940, J. E. Cox, the Stateline lookout, helped put out a forest fire near Kings Beach.[^2] It was thought to have been caused by a carelessly tossed cigarette.[^2] In 1964 Harold (Doc) Thornton received a $150 cash award for superior performance as a Forest Service fire lookout, and the same compilation says he had served at the North Shore tower for the last two summers.[^2]

The lookout was removed in 2002.[^1][^2][^4] A 2008 Marin Independent Journal walk said the station had stood for 70 years until 2002, and that cell phones and aircraft had made it unnecessary.[^3]

Today the site is a short day-use hike with a south-facing view over the lake.[^4][^5] The summit has restrooms, picnic tables and free telescopes, and nine new interpretive panels went up in 2025.[^4] A 2008 visitor found a rock platform in the old building's footprint.[^3] The route is an old paved forest road, about half a mile one way, that climbs from an iron pipe gate reached from Reservoir Drive and Lakeshore Avenue. The trailhead is in a residential area.[^4] Visitors should park clear of the gate and the driveways.[^4] There is no fee, and camping and overnight stays are not allowed.[^4]

Sources disagree on the climb. The 2008 walk gives about 100 feet of gain, while the 2026 guide lists 335 to 400 feet.[^3][^4]
