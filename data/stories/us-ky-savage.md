Savage was a steel fire lookout in Clinton County, planned in 1954.[^1] In July 1954 Bruce Sloan, president of the Sloan Coal Company, gave a tenth-acre site at the heads of Duvall Valley and Gap Creek to the Division of Forestry.[^1]

The Clinton County News reported a plan for an 80-foot (24.4 m) steel tower, which it said would be the highest in the Cumberland River Fires Control Unit.[^1] A bid notice from September 1954 described 60-foot (18.3 m) towers and placed this one at the heads of Duvall Valley and "Gay Creek." The two notices disagree on the height.[^1]

The paper's February 1970 account dates construction to the winter of 1954, with first use the following spring.[^1] William H. Peercy assisted with building the tower and was its first lookout. He later became a dispatcher and fire crew leader.[^1] The same account credits Henry Foster and Bruce Sloan with donating the site and the access road.[^1]

A June 1956 item credited Earl T. Denney, Savage's regular lookout, with finding most of the fires. Five were reported while he was on duty, and one more after his shift had ended.[^1]

No source I could reach describes the site today, so what remains there, and whether it is open to visitors, is not known.

[^1]: Savage - FOREST LOOKOUTS, quoting Clinton County News items of 1954, 1956 and 1970, Forest Lookouts, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/savage.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
