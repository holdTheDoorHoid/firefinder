In August 1956 the Trinity National Forest announced a lookout on Bowerman Ridge, east of old Minersville, to keep watch over the Trinity Project area while it was under construction.[^1] The announcement described a wooden structure 40 feet high, meant to be temporary. The Bureau of Reclamation was footing the $12,500 bill, and a fire suppression crew from the Minersville guard station was building it.[^1]

The lookout was completed in early spring 1957, with Forest Service crews doing the work.[^1] In May that year J.B. Hill and his wife Eva were manning it. A newspaper item from that May said the lookout gave a bird's-eye view of the Trinity Project dam, and that any fire it caught early could be reported to the suppression crews.[^1]

The dam it overlooked, Trinity Dam on the Trinity River, was begun in 1957 and finished in the early 1960s under the federal Central Valley Project.[^4]

The Fire Lookout Association's California list now marks Bowerman Ridge as gone.[^2]

A Forest Service mountain-bike route guide from the Weaverville Ranger Station of Shasta-Trinity National Forest runs up Bowerman Ridge Road and then down to an old lookout site, from which the guide says you get an excellent view of the southwest Trinity Alps and of Trinity Lake below.[^3] The 1956 announcement used the older name, Trinity National Forest. No source describes what, if anything, remains on the site today, and none says whether the spot itself is open to visitors beyond the forest roads that lead to it.
