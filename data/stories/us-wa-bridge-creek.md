Bridge Creek is a former lookout site on Okanogan National Forest land, about 10 miles west of Winthrop in Okanogan County, Washington.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register gives the build date as the 1930s, not a single year, and describes a camp with a platform tower on the site.[^1] Willhite's guide describes the same camp and says he has no pictures or maps of it.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in the 1940s.[^1][^2] None of the sources we read names a person who staffed it.[^1][^2][^3]

HistoryLink does not tie either of the fires it describes to this lookout.[^4] It does describe the Little Bridge Creek area around 1939 and 1940.[^4] In 1939 a forest fire there was fought entirely on the ground, at a cost of $44,000.[^4] In August 1940, two Forest Service fire guards made Washington's first smokejumper jump onto a forest wildfire, at Little Bridge Creek.[^4]

Willhite's route starts on the Twisp River Road and turns onto Little Bridge Creek Road.[^3] It runs almost 3 miles of gravel to Forest Road 100, then continues to a pass with parking, a rustic camp and two gates.[^3] The hike to the summit is 1.3 miles with 550 feet of climb.[^3] Willhite suggests a bald knob about three minutes south of the high point for the best views.[^3] He notes a sign asking visitors to shut the gate behind them.[^3]

The sources describe no remains at the site, so we cannot say what, if anything, stands there today.[^1][^2][^3]
