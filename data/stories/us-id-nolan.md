The Nolan lookout, also called Nolan Point, stood in Idaho County about 38 miles west-northwest of Salmon, at roughly 8,215 feet on the Salmon National Forest.[^1][^2]

In September 1940, a Recorder Herald item quoted on Ron Kemnow's Forest Lookouts page said a timber tower was going up on the point, on high ground west of the Middle Fork's mouth.[^2] Two pages date the tower and its log cabin to 1941 and describe a 60-foot pole tower.[^1][^2] The 1940 report describes work still underway, so the sources do not say exactly when the tower was finished.[^2]

The same Forest Lookouts page says the lookout was burned in the fall of 1967, as ordered.[^2] Rex Kamstra's firelookout.com page and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Idaho list both mark the site as gone.[^1][^3]

The Idaho Fire Lookouts site tags the spot as hike-in, so reaching it means walking in.[^4] None of the pages I read describes what, if anything, remains there now, or whether the site is open to visitors.
