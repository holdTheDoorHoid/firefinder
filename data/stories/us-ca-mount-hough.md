Mount Hough stands at 7,234 feet in Plumas County, and the Plumas National Forest administers its lookout.[^1] A 1915 newspaper placed the summit station 12 miles north of Quincy.[^2]

The date of the first station is disputed.[^1][^2] A June 1910 item in the Plumas National Bulletin said a lookout station had been established on the mountain.[^2] The Register dates the first structure to 1911, and a July 1911 report said the Forest Service had erected a sheltered station that year.[^1][^2]

The 1911 station gave way in 1915 to a larger building, which a newspaper described as two stories tall.[^2] The Register says the replacement was probably a short tower on a standard plan, so the sources disagree.[^1] A BC-301 cabin replaced it in 1934, and a building put up in 1986 now houses some microwave relays.[^1][^2]

Staffing was seasonal for a long stretch.[^2] In November 1930 the lookout took down his interior equipment and left the station until spring, and a 1931 item said the lookout's register showed 917 visitors that summer.[^2] Later entries name Walter Shockley in 1950 and Wanda Hanson in 1965.[^2] The Register adds that Wayne Pease has been an observer at the lookout for 30 of his 50 years.[^1]

Fires reported from the station include a brush fire south of Quincy in 1929, a 1950 lightning fire that the regular lookout was fighting while his wife phoned from the station, and a September 1973 fire near Spanish Creek that burned three acres.[^2]

The Register says the lookout is manned on a regular basis to spot fires.[^1] I could not find a dated staffing record, an access rule, or a rental listing in the pages I read, so current visiting arrangements remain unconfirmed.[^1][^2] Because it is a working Forest Service station, check with the Plumas National Forest before planning a trip.[^1]
