The Summit Guard Station lookout was a ground-level lookout house with a wood stove, not a tower, on a ridge in Skamania County's Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^2][^1]

The register records a ground cab built there in the 1930s, and firelookout.com gives the same decade.[^1][^3] The register gives only the decade, not a single year.[^1]

The Osborne panoramic survey team photographed the site on July 9, 1937.[^2][^4] Those photos show the guard station buildings farther down the ridge.[^2] Willhite says a photo that Skamania County records label Lookout Mountain was actually taken at the guard station.[^2]

The register says the lookout is gone, and the Washington Lookouts page lists it as removed.[^1][^4]

Willhite's photo captions mention a remaining anchor at the site, and he notes old wood in the brush.[^2] A survey benchmark named SUMMIT, shown on USGS maps, is at the lookout location.[^2]

Willhite lists the access as rough roads.[^2] The lookout location sits just above the Bluff Mountain Trailhead, and from the parking area north of the site it takes several minutes to walk through brush to reach it.[^2]
