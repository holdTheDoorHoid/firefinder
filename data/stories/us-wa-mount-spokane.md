Mount Spokane has had a fire lookout on its summit since the 1930s, long before the tower that now sits on Quartz Mountain.[^1][^5] In 1932 the federal Forest Service offered a lookout building, on condition that someone be put in charge during fire season.[^5] Firelookout.com names the upper-floor dormer of the stone Vista House, a Civilian Conservation Corps project from 1934, as the first lookout.[^1]

Later towers followed. An 85-foot wood tower went up in 1948 and collapsed in its first winter under heavy snow.[^3][^1] A 45-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced it in 1950.[^1][^3] The Washington Department of Natural Resources swapped that for a 40-foot timber tower in 1963, and built the 40-foot tower that stood on the summit from 1979.[^1][^2]

Firelookout.com lists named lookouts from 1945 to 1968, among them Adelia Blake, who is listed for 1945 to 1955.[^1] The 1979 tower was used for fire spotting until it was permanently decommissioned in 1994.[^2] TrailChick says that over its 60 years of service the Mount Spokane lookout held the record for reported wildfires, ahead of any of the other 657 fire lookouts in Washington.[^3] In 1937 a forest service lookout on the mountain phoned in the location of four fires during a lightning storm.[^5]

Facing ongoing repair costs, the DNR was ready to demolish the tower until park staff and an advisory committee asked for it to go to the park instead.[^2] In June 2001 crews scrapped the tower.[^2][^3] The 9,000-pound cab was lifted by crane and carried by truck to a ranger's residence, where it waited while permits were processed.[^2][^3] In late August 2004 a parks crew made the old road passable for heavy trucks, poured four concrete pillars, and set the cab on a new 10-foot tower on Quartz Mountain.[^2] Firelookout.com says part of the old tower was rebuilt there as well, a detail the NHLR does not mention.[^1]

The Quartz cab has been a rental since 2005, reservable from June through September for about $100 a night, according to the register.[^2] Washington State Parks describes it as a summer-only wooden lookout, 14 by 14 feet, that sleeps four and requires a two-night minimum stay.[^4] Reservations open up to nine months ahead.[^4] The only way in is on foot or by bike, along a 2.7-mile trail from parking at Selkirk Lodge.[^4]

On the summit itself, firelookout.org lists the 1979 tower as gone and the Vista House as still standing.[^6] We found no source describing what, if anything, remains of the 1979 tower on the summit today.
