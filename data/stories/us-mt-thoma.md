Thoma Lookout sits on a high point of about 7,100 feet in the Flathead National Forest, a few miles shy of the Canadian border.[^1][^2] The white, wooden-shingled cab is an early gable-roofed L-4 design, built in 1930.[^1][^2]

A 2014 Flathead Beacon feature counts it as one of just four remaining examples of its design, and says it holds a place on the National Register of Historic Places.[^3] The lookout's entry in the National Historic Lookout Register is dated September 28, 2010.[^1]

Its use lapsed for a long stretch. The 2014 article says summer 2010 was the first season staffed since 1973. Before the Forest Service decided in 2009 to staff it again, the article says, the cab had only received periodic maintenance.[^3] Firelookout.com says the cab saw emergency use in the 1970s.[^4]

Chuck Manning, who now chairs the Northwest Montana Lookout Association, manned Thoma during the summer of 1963.[^2] Leif Haugen transferred to Thoma in 2010 and helped refurbish the tower; the 2014 article says he had to meet historic standards in building the cabinetry, a bed and the fire-finder's tower.[^3]

Restoration is dated two ways. Firelookout.com dates the start of the work to 2010, while NHLR describes the lookout as just restored at registration.[^4][^1]

In 2026 Haugen, described as Northwest Montana's longest-serving fire lookout, was still at his post at Thoma.[^2] The cab is staffed in summer.[^4] None of the sources read gives public visiting rules, so anyone planning a trip should check with the Flathead National Forest first.
