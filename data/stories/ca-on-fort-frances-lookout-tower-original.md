The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says this tower was built in the 1930s near Atikokan, Ontario, and served as a forest fire lookout and radar tower.[^1] A Fort Frances Times article, drawing on Atikokan museum archives, tells a different story. It says the RCAF started a gap-filler radar project in 1959, and that the tower appears to have been built but never went into service.[^2] The Times places the tower at Pal Lake, where the Atikokan museum listed a joint radar and forestry tower.[^2] Its Pal Lake coordinates are far from the register's point for the original site, which the register puts in Kenora District.[^1][^2]

The province acquired the tower in 1964, and the Department of Lands and Forests used it as a forest fire lookout for at least a few years. The staffed fire lookout program was eventually dropped.[^2] By 1972, the Times says, virtually no one was using it.[^2]

In 1972, James Wrench Towse, Fort Frances' Industrial Development Commissioner, arranged for the tower to be shifted to Point Park as a tourist draw.[^3] The Times says it was taken down and hauled to town.[^2] The tower is 33 m (100 ft) tall and weighs about 90 tons.[^2][^3] It was put back together in sections, lifted into place with cranes, and it has a red-and-white cupola.[^2] Leo Bernier, the minister of natural resources, opened it on 1 July 1972, and more than 1,000 people climbed it that summer during Fun in the Sun.[^2]

In 2009 the tower was moved again, from Point Park to the Sorting Gap Marina on the Rainy River.[^3][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the Municipality of Fort Frances as administrator.[^4] The Times says access is only available during the summer months, and that it was stopped because of COVID-19.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the original site today.
