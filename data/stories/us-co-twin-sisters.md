The Forest Service built a lookout on the Twin Sisters peak in 1914, the year before Rocky Mountain National Park was established.[^1][^4] A fire guard from Longmont was assigned to take charge of the new station, and a trail up the mountain was finished by September.[^2]

An October 1920 newspaper account describes a 7-by-7 white wood lookout house with windows on every side, about a hundred feet from a small stone house.[^2][^3] From 1914 through 1969 the stone house sheltered crews who manned the lookout.[^3]

In 1917 a fire observer named Heaton was in charge.[^2] In August 1922 rangers brought under control a fire on the Windy Gulch Trail that had been spotted from the lookout, before it did much harm.[^2] That October A.H. Heaton closed the station for the winter after painting the building white and putting on new storm windows.[^2] In 1925 the National Park Service took over the station, which it said had been of much value in spotting fires.[^1][^2]

The lookout was replaced more than once, and the sources do not agree on the dates.[^1][^2][^3][^4] A new $16,000 lookout was reported finished in June 1950, and a March 1951 newspaper report said the station had fallen, apparently in high winds.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says high winds destroyed the 1950 tower the next year, that a steel tower replaced it in 1953, and that it was taken down in 1976.[^1] The National Register nomination for the stone building says a replacement built in 1949 stayed active until 1969, when it was abandoned and removed.[^3] Wikipedia gives 1977 for its dismantling.[^4]

The stone house is still there.[^4] In 1973 the building was converted to a fire cache and radio repeater station,[^3] and Wikipedia says it is now used as a radio repeater station.[^4] It was added to the National Register of Historic Places on December 24, 1992.[^4] The lookout tower itself is listed as removed.[^5] No source reviewed describes visitor access to the stone building.
