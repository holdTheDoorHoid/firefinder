Summit Prairie is a former lookout site in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, in Skamania County, Washington.[^1] The Register gives its elevation as 5,230 feet (1,594 meters).[^1][^2]

The first structure was a log and shake cabin with a roof platform, built in 1924 about half a mile northwest of the summit. Some called it the Dark Cabin.[^1][^3] In 1933 an L-4 cab was added on the summit itself.[^1][^3] Willhite says the lookout took its name from a small prairie on the ridgetop, which most maps label Summit Prairie, rather than from Quartz Creek Ridge.[^3]

A 1937 newspaper item, quoted on Willhite's page, says Amber Niblock had charge of a lookout station there.[^3] The page lists pairs of lookouts for 1954 through 1956.[^3] A 1963 item says a volunteer offered to serve as lookout at Summit Prairie.[^3] The site was staffed until 1963. After that, trail crews used it, and both structures were destroyed in 1967.[^1][^2]

None of the sources we read gives a cause for the 1967 loss.

A photo caption on Willhite's page reads "Footings in 2019" and adds that one of them has been moved for ease of motorcycle passing.[^3] No other remains are described in the sources we read.

Willhite describes the route as 1.5 miles and about 700 feet of climbing, starting from the trailhead just before the end of paved FR 9085.[^3] The sources we read mention no closure, but none gives current access rules.
