Maine's fire towers were meant to catch forest fires early, protecting the lumber and papermaking businesses that relied on the state's trees.[^1] The former-lookout register lists the Lawler Hill site under Benedicta, in Aroostook County.[^2]

In its 1914 report, the state forest commissioner said a man had been kept on Lawler Hill for lookout duty, and that the state already had the material for a steel tower to go up there in the spring of 1915.[^3] A volunteer-compiled list gives 1915 as the year a 48-foot steel tower was built, citing the same report.[^3] The report describes that tower as a plan, so the completion date rests on the list alone.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1978.[^2]

The association's Maine list marks the tower as gone.[^4] No source read describes what, if anything, remains on the hilltop today, or whether the public can reach it.
