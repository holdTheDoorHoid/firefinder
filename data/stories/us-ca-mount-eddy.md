Mount Eddy, a peak just west of Sisson in the Shasta-Trinity National Forest, got its first fire lookout in 1912.[^1][^2] The first house was a cupola, completed and staffed that year.[^1] A July 1912 newspaper said lumber was being carried up by mule, and a telephone line was planned to link the station with the forest headquarters in Sisson.[^2]

The register describes frequent severe storms, high winds and lightning strikes at the site, and says lightning strikes on the building twice injured its operators.[^1] A September 1912 paper said the federal lookout on the peak had spotted all eighteen fires that thunderstorms started that August.[^2] In August 1920 lightning struck the station, tore a hole in the station house and knocked the lookout, J. S. McClemmons, unconscious.[^2] The clippings spell his name several ways.[^2] That October a windstorm blew out the windows, and the keeper, F. A. Nixon, crawled under a small platform and lay there until morning.[^2] The Forest Service dug a shelter tunnel near the summit in 1922.[^2] In September 1923 another storm left the lookout house a "practically total wreck," and William J. Hale and his son had to spend the night in that tunnel.[^2] Hale had taken the post after two earlier occupants refused to return.[^2]

In 1931 Matt Merchant spotted a sawmill fire 54 miles away, a distance the paper called a record for northern California.[^2]

According to the register, a California Region 5 Plan 4A cabin took the first house's place at some point in the 1920s.[^1] It gives no removal date. It does say a foundation still exists at the summit.[^1]

Today the summit is reached on foot.[^4] The Forest Service describes a side trip from the Pacific Crest Trail: a climb to the uppermost Deadfall Lake, then a side trail up to the summit.[^4] A hiker's trip report describes the remains of an old lookout tower near the top.[^3] The sources do not say whether that is the same foundation the register mentions.
