Bear Mountain, also called Shasta Bear, is a Shasta County lookout whose register entry gives 1980 as its build year.[^1] The register says the steel tower of that year was erected around an older lookout from the 1930s.[^1] The original was a Civilian Conservation Corps era lookout, and after the older tower and cab were demolished, a new steel tower was finished with a second-story room and a cab.[^1]

The register calls the design a Plesha plan and says the steel columns were first meant for atomic bomb test towers.[^1] It also notes that this was the first tower the state forestry agency built using these materials.[^1] The cab is a 732-6A type.[^1]

A Cal Fire list from the 2008 fire season names the lookout among those that may be staffed that year.[^2] The same list says most lookouts are staffed only during peak fire season.[^2] No source shows who staffed it, or whether it was staffed after 2008.

The Bear Fire overran the site in 1990, and the lookout station was evacuated.[^1] The register records the lookout as US 1377 and CA 200, registered on October 31, 2019.[^1]

The register says the site is about five miles from a paved road and sits behind locked gates, so access is restricted.[^1] None of the sources read describes the cab's current condition. If you are curious about it, ask Cal Fire's Shasta-Trinity Unit before going near it, and respect the locked gates.
