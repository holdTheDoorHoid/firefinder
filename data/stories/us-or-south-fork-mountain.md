South Fork Mountain is in Clackamas County, Oregon, within the Mount Hood National Forest.[^1] The register says a camp, which it calls a rag-camp, started on the site in 1925. A 50-foot pole tower with an open platform was built there in 1930.[^1][^2]

A May 1932 item names Fred Brown as the veteran lookout on the mountain. I found no other record of who staffed it.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in 1960 and is gone, according to the FFLOS register and firelookout.com.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service administered the site.[^1]

Oregon Hikers describes the former lookout site as a viewpoint on the boundary of the Clackamas Wilderness, where the views run from Mount Rainier to the Three Sisters.[^4] Tree growth since the lookout came down means visitors have to move around a little to see the views.[^4] The road to the top is usually gated lower down, though it is opened during deer hunting season.[^4] That access note comes from a hiking guide, not an agency notice. No source I found describes what remains of the tower or cabin at the summit.
