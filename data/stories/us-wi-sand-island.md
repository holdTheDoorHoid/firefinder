In May 1932 the Post-Crescent counted the Sand Island tower among the towers already completed in district 11.[^1] It stood in the Town of Grant, in the southwest part of Portage County.[^1] A Stevens Point Journal account from October 1967 said it had gone up about 35 years earlier and stood 85 feet tall.[^1] From that height, Stevens Point could be seen more than 15 miles away, and on clear days Rib Mountain near Wausau could be picked out with binoculars.[^1]

By the time it came down, the tower had sat unused for the last five years.[^1] Budget limits were part of the reason, and the article said other towers gave fairly good coverage in the area.[^1]

In October 1967 the Division of Conservation was dismantling it, and the paper called it the county's one and only fire tower.[^1] One of the crew, Ralph Keck of Tomahawk, had helped put it up in the 1930s.[^1] The article said plans for the tower were uncertain; it might be put up again elsewhere in the state.[^1]

The statewide lookout list marks the site as gone.[^2] No source describes the site today, so what remains there and whether it is open to visitors are unknown.
