The Big Bend lookout was a live-in cab with a catwalk. The Civilian Conservation Corps put it up in 1941 on the Wythe Ranger District of the Jefferson National Forest.[^1] A 1958 Coast and Geodetic Survey description places its survey mark at the old tower site, about 3.5 miles east-southeast of the pass where U.S. Highways 21 and 52 cross Walker Mountain.[^2]

A Mr. Kime, who created the Big Walker Lookout attraction on State Route 52, bought the tower and took it down for its timber around 1953 to 1954.[^1] By the 1958 survey the tower was already gone; the mark sits 17 feet northwest of a concrete footing of the old tower.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks the tower as gone, so there is no tower left to climb.[^4]

The register reports that the footers can still be seen in the woods just north of the Big Bend picnic area, but that they have badly deteriorated. It gives no date for that description.[^1]

Visitors can reach the picnic area by a side trip on FSR 206. The register calls this road FS 206; it is the gravel road the CCC built as a firebreak.[^1][^3] Scenic Drives USA describes the picnic site as a good place to pause, with views of the ridge and valley terrain to the south.[^3]

The Appalachian Trail originally ran past the tower along the Walker Mountain ridge.[^1] It was shifted farther north in 1977 because the ridge had too little water for hikers.[^1]
