In July 1911 a newspaper reported that work was about to begin on a line to San Antone Mountain, "where a lookout station for forest fires will be established."[^1] The item describes a plan. It does not say when the station was finished.

A National Register nomination covering the region's forest lookouts describes the San Antone Lookout as a log cabin with a short tower built directly onto it.[^2] A July 1918 newspaper item, reprinted on a lookout history site, places a lookout house on top of a mountain in the Carson National Forest. It describes the house as about 12 by 12 feet, with one floor and walls "almost wholly of glass."[^1] The two descriptions do not match, and no source says whether they describe the same building.

On the night of June 18, 1918, lightning hit the cabin and punched a hole in its roof. The newspaper said the lookoutman, Mr. Ground, was in bed, and it supposed that his bedding had insulated him. He was not hurt.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the San Antone site as a gone ground lookout.[^3] No source here gives a date for its removal.

Today the mountain lies within the Carson National Forest, which the U.S. Forest Service administers.[^4] Its summit has radio and television towers. Forest Road 418 goes to the top, but a locked gate stands where it runs across private property, and Wikipedia says the road may not be open to motor vehicles for the general public.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout.
