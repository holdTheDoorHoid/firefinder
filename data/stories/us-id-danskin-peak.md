Danskin Peak Lookout is in Elmore County on the Boise National Forest, at about 2,044 meters.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates it to 1940 and describes a wood-frame cabin on a stone first story.[^1] A firelookout.com history calls it a two-story stone and shake cabin, also built in 1940.[^2] A separate Forest Lookouts page describes the cab as hip-roofed with shake siding over a stone and concrete first story, and says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it.[^3] An Idaho News 6 feature instead says it was built in 1941, so the sources do not fully agree on the start date.[^4]

According to the register, the lookout is usually staffed in fire season.[^1] An Idaho lookout list records known staff for 2021, 2022 and 2024.[^5] The Idaho News 6 feature follows a summer staffer who said he could see 110 miles on a good day. He keeps four weeks of supplies on hand because getting up to the tower is a pain, though he called this one of the easier towers to reach.[^4]

On August 11, 2013, the Elk Fire Complex became the country's largest wildfire, and the lookout was evacuated and coated in retardant to protect it.[^3] The register added Danskin Peak Lookout on September 27, 2020.[^1][^5]

Today the Idaho lookout list marks Danskin Peak as drive-up and staffed.[^5] The sources do not say whether the cab is open to visitors, so check with the Boise National Forest before you go up.
