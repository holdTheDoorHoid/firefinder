Gaudineer Knob sits on the Randolph and Pocahontas county line in the Monongahela National Forest, and a fire tower once stood on its high point.[^1][^2][^6] The Forest Service chose the peak as a memorial to Donald R. Gaudineer, a ranger who died in April 1936 while trying to rescue his children from a house fire.[^1][^3]

Civilian Conservation Corps crews built the tower in 1936 and 1937, working from Camp Hutton.[^2][^4] Its dedication came on July 5, 1937, in Gaudineer's memory.[^2][^3]

The sources disagree on how long the tower watched the forest.[^1][^2] Wikipedia says it surveyed the surrounding landscape from 1936 through the 1970s, which would start before the dedication.[^1] The registry says it stayed in service until the early 1970s.[^2]

e-WV says the tower was abandoned in the early 1970s.[^3] Only its concrete footings remain.[^2][^1]

A commemorative plaque at the start of the trail sits a short distance from the tower's foundation.[^5] The knob can be reached by drive-up access via FR 27A.[^1] The nearby Gaudineer Scenic Area was set aside in 1964 to protect a virgin red spruce stand, and an easy half-mile loop winds through its mossy second-growth spruce.[^3][^5]
