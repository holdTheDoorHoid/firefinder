Prince Albert National Park once had six fire towers, and Boundary was one of them.[^1] The Waskesiu Heritage Museum says each tower was named after its location, and that the first five towers were built in 1938, with Boundary built in 1940.[^1]

The museum describes the park's towers as a set, and it does not say which details applied to Boundary.[^1] A tower person could be stationed at one tower for up to two weeks, working 8 to 10 hours a day, and checked in with fire dispatch each day by two-way radio.[^1] Each tower had a one-room cabin at its base, with a pit privy and a wood stove, and no electricity or running water.[^1] The cupola began as an eight-sided plywood structure and was gradually replaced with fibreglass and wood during the 1960s.[^1] The museum says the cupola sat on a 24.4 m (80 ft) tower.[^1]

The Firetowers of Saskatchewan site gives Boundary's location as 53-57.0167 X 106-44.5000, and lists a Boundary Tower among the Prince Albert National Park towers.[^2]

The museum says the park's towers were retired in 2001, when helicopter smoke patrols replaced them.[^1] It adds that Saskatchewan still keeps fire towers in service.[^1] Parks Canada's current lookout page names two towers, Height of Land and Spruce River Highlands, and does not mention Boundary.[^3]

We found no source that describes what remains of the Boundary tower or its site today, or whether the public can visit it.
