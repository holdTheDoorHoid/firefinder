The Missouri Conservation Commission bought steel in 1938 for a 100-foot lookout tower at Eminence, in Shannon County.[^1] In April 1939 a newspaper reported that the Forestry Section was building it on Highway 19 near town, and that the ground around it was being planted in short leaf pine.[^1] A 1939 state report lists the 100-foot steel tower as erected.[^1] The commission also acquired 640 acres for the fire tower in 1940.[^1]

In September 1941 a Franklin County paper reported that 1,360 people had climbed the Eminence tower in the previous month.[^1] During the war years, a 1943 newspaper item said Mrs. Thomas J. Chilton, the wife of a forest assistant, had been filling in as towerman so her husband could fight fires.[^1] The next spring a 1944 newspaper item said she had climbed the tower several times a day from February to April.[^1] The two items disagree about her family.[^1] The 1943 item calls her the mother of two children, while the 1944 item says she had three small boys.[^1]

Later entries are mostly upkeep.[^1] A bedroom was added to the residence in fiscal 1956-57, and a barn and garage were reshingled in 1965-66.[^1] Garage doors went up in 1968-69, and in 1971-72 the residence received new flooring and a repaired roof.[^1] A new roof went on the residence again in 1977-78, which is the last dated entry on the page.[^1]

The page does not say whether the tower was later taken down or still stands.[^1] No other source I read describes the Eminence site today, so I cannot say whether any part of the tower or its footings remains, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1]

[^1]: Eminence - FOREST LOOKOUTS, Forest Lookouts (Central US Lookouts), https://centraluslookouts.weebly.com/eminence.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
