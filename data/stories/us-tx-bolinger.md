Bolinger was a steel fire lookout in Bossier Parish, Louisiana.[^1][^2]

By 1940, the Louisiana Forestry Commission had been created through an amendment to the state constitution.[^3] Early on, the commission positioned lookout towers, phone networks and firefighting crews around the state, so each district had coverage.[^3] Bossier Parish started with two towers, at Plain Dealing and Bodcau.[^3] A third went up on Gidden's Hill in 1946, and the fourth and last was built in the Bolinger area in 1948.[^3]

The Bolinger tower stood about 100 feet on four steel legs, topped by a glass-walled lookout cab.[^1] The Bossier towers ranged from 100 to 120 feet, depending on the elevation of each site.[^3] The Coast and Geodetic Survey described the station in 1950.[^1] The survey station was about two miles west of Bolinger, on the highest point of a timbered ridge.[^1]

The Bolinger tower ran for six months of the year, while the other Bossier towers operated all year.[^3] The parish history blog says watchmen were on call around the clock except in heavy rain, and usually lived in a cottage at or near the tower.[^3]

Wikipedia's list of Louisiana fire towers marks the Bolinger tower as torn down, with no date given.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page lists it as removed.[^1]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether it is public. Wikipedia's row for the tower says aerial imagery of the site could not be found.[^2]
