The Ludlow Fire Tower stands on Minechoag Mountain in Hampden County, Massachusetts, just east of Springfield.[^1] The land is part of the Ludlow State Forest.[^1]

The 50-acre forest was purchased in 1936, and that year the Civilian Conservation Corps started the tower.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page credits the Corps with putting up a 60-foot steel tower, with a 7-by-7 cab, in 1936.[^3] The register says construction was begun, and the other page says erected, so the two use different words, but both put the work in 1936. Neither gives a completion date.[^1][^3]

The original cab was metal and 7 by 7 feet, on a 60-foot steel tower.[^1] In 1967 a 10-by-10-foot aluminum cab took its place, and a further upgrade came in 1999.[^1]

The sources read name no early keepers and describe no specific fires. The register's April 2016 visit report says the tower is staffed sometimes, seasonally.[^1]

The tower is still standing. In 2015 the Massachusetts maintenance crew did repairs on 9 of the state's towers, and Ludlow received new landing platforms.[^2] The register describes the tower as in active service.[^1]

The most recent visit report the register holds is from April 2016. It rates the outside of the tower as in good condition, but notes a chain link fence with barbed wire along the top encloses it, and that a good deal of communications gear is mounted on the structure.[^1] The same report adds that it is missing from trail maps and sits fairly remote.[^1] The register calls the land both the Ludlow State Forest and the Ludlow State Park.[^1] No newer source on current access turned up. The tower is administered by the Mass. Bureau of Forest Fire Control.[^1] Check with that agency before any visit, and do not climb a fenced tower.
