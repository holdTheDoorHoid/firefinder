The Shickshinny tower stood on Knob Mountain near Shickshinny in Luzerne County.[^1][^2] The register describes an Aermotor steel tower put up in 1921 and taken down after 1957.[^1] A June 1921 notice said a state tower would probably go on the top of the mountain that summer.[^2] By September several hundred people had climbed up to see the new lookout, which had 84 steps and nine rest platforms.[^2] A November 1921 item placed it in the Wyoming State Forest.[^2]

In September 1922 local citizens called a work day for October 9 to build an automobile road half a mile up the mountain to the tower.[^2] The Emergency Relief Association earmarked $8,437 for an improved road in 1934, and a WPA project widened it in 1936.[^2]

Walter Kline was appointed towerman in March 1922, and Bruce Henrie became watchman in October 1923.[^2] In March 1930 the state appointed Mrs. Bruce Henry, the wife of a state fire inspector, as a watcher.[^2] The paper believed she was the first woman to hold such a post in the state.[^2] Frank Robbins was named lookout in 1935.[^2]

In October 1947 Oscar Featherman, in charge at the tower, reported a large fire on Jackson Hill and Red Rock Mountain. It took four hours and three crews to put out, and about 10 acres burned.[^2] In April 1955 John Falkowski was listed as the operator.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed after 1957.[^1] The latest dated entry on the history page is the 1955 listing.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on Knob Mountain now, or whether the public can reach the summit.
