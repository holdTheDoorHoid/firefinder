The Conservation Commission acquired an 80-acre towersite on Taum Sauk, the highest point in the state, in fiscal 1948-49.[^1] The steel tower was built in 1949, though a July 1949 newspaper said heavy rain and inadequate road access had held up construction.[^2][^1]

Reports disagree on its height.[^1][^2][^4] A July 1949 paper gave 85 feet while the tower was going up, and a November 1950 paper described a 60-foot steel tower with a ten by ten cab.[^1] The Register and a 2025 blog post both give 72 feet.[^2][^4]

By March 1950 the tower was up, but a suitable road to it had not yet been completed.[^1] That spring a newspaper reported that temporary towerman Sam Jones planned to ride three miles to and from the tower each day on his mare.[^1] The road was finished in fiscal 1951-52, and a towerman's residence was completed in 1952-53.[^1] A dedication was set for October 1952, sponsored by the Arcadia Valley Rotary Club.[^1]

Further work followed. A barn and chicken house went up in 1956-57, a water system followed in 1958-59, the cab got a new roof and siding in 1966-67, and a road was built at the site in 1975-76.[^1]

Missouri created Taum Sauk Mountain State Park in 1991.[^6] The Register places the tower within a state park and gives its registration date as April 28, 1997.[^2]

The tower still stands within the park.[^3][^4] A 2025 blogger climbed the 72-foot tower and called the view from the top impressive.[^4] The state's park page encourages hikers to reach the mountaintop, and a paved path leads to the highpoint.[^5][^3]
