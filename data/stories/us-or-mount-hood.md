The summit lookout on Mount Hood began in the summer of 1915, when the Forest Service set up a station on the peak.[^4] The plan, reported that July, was to keep a lookout on the peak to watch the Bull Run forest reserve through the whole year.[^3] The register says the first setup, in 1915, was a fire finder on the summit cairn and a wood-floored tent. Later that year a D-6 cupola cabin prototype, which the register links to Lige Coleman, replaced them.[^1]

Elijah "Lige" Coalman, also spelled Coleman in some sources, was named to run the station.[^3] In August 1918 the Oregonian reported that Coalman had resigned recently.[^3] By late September 1918, Mark Weygandt was the observer.[^3]

Both the register and firelookout.com date the cabin's abandonment to 1935 and its slide off the summit to 1941.[^1][^2] We did not find a source that describes the summit today or what, if anything, remains of the cabin.
