Hickory Ridge Lookout Tower stands on the edge of the Charles C. Deam Wilderness in Indiana's Hoosier National Forest.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps began building it in 1936, a 110-foot steel Aermotor tower topped by a small seven-by-seven-foot cab.[^1][^2] The climb up originally took 123 wooden steps; those have since been replaced with 133 steel ones.[^2] At the base there was once a cabin or guard station, a latrine, and a garage, though only the tower itself remains today.[^2]

For about 40 years, lookouts staffed the tower during periods of high fire danger, with a small crew of firefighters based at the bottom ready to respond when smoke was spotted.[^2] Some of the busiest stretches saw as many as four or five fire reports a day, and one of the largest fires on record in the area came within half a mile of the tower before crews stopped it.[^2] The tower itself was never burned, though it has taken at least one lightning strike over the years.[^2]

Staffing ended in the late 1970s, as aerial patrols and other fire-detection methods made a continuously manned tower unnecessary.[^2] Hickory Ridge had originally been one of eight fire towers on the Hoosier National Forest; it is now the only one still standing.[^1][^2] The structure underwent a restoration in 1989, and the following year, in October 1990, it was added to the National Historic Lookout Register, the first Indiana tower to earn that listing.[^1][^2]

The tower has drawn local media attention over the years, including a 2013 feature on Indiana Public Media.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register's own records do not note a public NHLR sign at the site or any current staffing or volunteer program, and nothing in the sources consulted says whether the structure is open for the public to climb today.[^1] Anyone visiting the Deam Wilderness area should check current Forest Service guidance before assuming access.
