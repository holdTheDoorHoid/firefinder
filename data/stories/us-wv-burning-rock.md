The Burning Rock tower stood near the Wyoming and Logan County line.[^1] A February 1961 clipping puts the site at 3,050 feet above sea level.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places it in Wyoming County and gives the elevation as 3,013 feet (918 metres).[^2] The two figures differ, and this record keeps the register's number.

An August 1942 clipping refers to the fire tower at Burning Rock.[^1] A clipping dated November 16, 1954 says the tower opened that day.[^1] The clippings do not agree on when a tower first stood there, and this record gives no build year.[^1]

A March 1950 notice lists Hufford Toler, of Simon, as reappointed for Burning Rock.[^1] Robert Blankenship was named as an observer there in October 1957 and again in October 1958.[^1] In October 1960 Willie Trent, of Pineville, was at the tower.[^1]

In February 1961 Wyoming County forester Avery McMillin said a new road now let trucks reach the tower, so attendants no longer had to climb the final stretch.[^1] A new block cabin had also been finished at the site.[^1]

The FireLookout.org list marks the tower as gone.[^3] Its register record dates from January 26, 2017, when Brian M. Powell nominated it.[^2] The register names the West Virginia Division of Forestry as administrator.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the site today, and none says whether the public can reach it. Anyone planning a visit should ask the Division of Forestry first.
