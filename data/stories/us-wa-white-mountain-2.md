White Mountain rises to 6,921 feet in Ferry County, Washington, on the Colville National Forest.[^1] It sits about 16 miles east-southeast of Republic.[^2] An April 1923 report in the Spokane Daily Chronicle called it among the highest points in the Kettle Range and said Forest Service rangers would build a lookout on its summit.[^3]

A trail and telephone line had been built to the top in the fall of 1922.[^4] The same report said rangers were framing lumber for the station. The lumber was to reach the summit by pack horse over roughly six miles of difficult trail.[^3] A September 1923 report said the cabin was completed by July 1 and used as a lookout that year.[^3][^4]

The sources disagree on the year: firetower.org gives 1919 and firelookout.com gives 1922, but the September 1923 report describes a finished cabin, so this story uses 1923.[^1][^2][^3]

Named lookouts include H.P. Hasler in 1923, Ralph Buckley, who took up the lookout in July 1928, S.B. Hayward in 1929, Mr. and Mrs. Chas. Bouk of Republic in 1944, and Homer Therleen in 1945.[^2][^3][^4]

The cupola cabin was removed in 1950.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year, but firetower.org and WillhiteWeb both say it was removed.[^1][^2][^4]

Little remains on the summit today. The Washington Trails Association says only cement steps and a few scattered nails are left of the old lookout.[^5] The 28-mile round trip on the Kettle Crest Trail includes a side trip to the old site that WTA puts at about a mile and rates as easy.[^5]
