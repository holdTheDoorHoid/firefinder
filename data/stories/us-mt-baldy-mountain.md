Baldy Mountain, in Lincoln County, Montana, has been used to spot fires since about 1910, when a 45-foot pole platform tower was developed on the site.[^3][^1][^2] A log cabin with an L-6 cupola replaced that platform in 1927.[^1][^3] The present tower, a treated-timber structure with an L-4 cab, went up in 1957.[^1][^2]

The NHLR and firelookout.com give the present tower a height of 30 feet, but Recreation.gov says the cabin sits atop a 26-foot tower. The sources do not explain the gap.[^1][^3][^2] The NHLR registered the lookout on January 9, 2021 as US 1512, MT 90.[^1]

Today the lookout is in the Recreation Rental program.[^1][^3] Recreation.gov lists it as a 144-square-foot cabin that holds four people, typically open from late June through late September.[^2] There is no electricity or water, so guests must bring their own.[^2] Whether the drive is practical depends on the weather, and a high-clearance vehicle is advised for the rough roads.[^2] Yaak Falls, a popular spot, lies a short drive from the lookout, reached from Yaak Highway 508.[^2]
