Norway Bluff sits in Piscataquis County, Maine, near Munsungan Lake, in the area a local paper calls T9 R9 WELS.[^1][^4] A 1914 Forest Commissioner’s report says a 24-foot steel lookout had gone up the summer before.[^1] The same report describes a watchman’s camp on the mountainside and a telephone line that let the lookout man reach Oxbow Plantation.[^1] A 1920 inventory still listed a 24-foot steel tower.[^1]

We did not find a named lookout or a notable fire tied to the tower in the sources we read.

The tower came down in 2012 as part of the state’s MSCommNet radio project, a statewide upgrade that affected many fire towers in Maine.[^2] Its removal from the mountain took place on May 7, 2012, and it reached the museum on June 13.[^2][^1]

In early October 2012 a man was doing carpentry repairs on the tower display at the museum grounds. The concrete pad was already in place, and the freshly painted steel frame was ready to be set on it.[^3] Volunteers and Ashland Boy Scouts logged well over 600 hours on the cab, the frame and the reassembly.[^4] A dedication ceremony was held at the museum on July 7, 2013.[^5]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing at the museum and the original site as gone.[^6] A 2013 article says the museum added a display of photos and keepsakes, donated by local families, about life at the area's fire towers.[^4] We did not find a source that says whether visitors can climb it, so check with the museum first.
