Scalplock Mountain Lookout stands in Glacier National Park, about two miles northeast of Essex in Flathead County, Montana.[^2] The register calls it the southernmost lookout in the park, overlooking Theodore Roosevelt Pass.[^1]

Glacier's superintendent, E.T. Scoyen, recommended the mountain as a lookout site in 1931.[^5] The site was chosen for its views into the Park Creek valley and the Middle Fork of the Flathead River.[^5] The lookout was built that year.[^1][^4][^5] It was an accepted fact that many fires began with careless people in passing cars and trains.[^5] The nomination says that being able to view the travel corridor along the park's southwest boundary became an administrative mandate.[^5]

The lookout is a two-story frame building with a cab about 14 by 14 feet on a 10-foot tower with a catwalk.[^1][^2][^5] Its plan came from Forest Service designs, and Forest and Park Service engineers often modified it.[^4][^5] It was one of several similar lookouts constructed in the park between the late 1920s and the early 1940s, and the nomination describes a planned network that spanned the park and adjoining national forest land.[^5]

Today the lookout is staffed in summer.[^2] The sources do not describe how staffing changed over the decades, though Wikipedia calls it one of a chain of staffed posts within the park.[^2][^4] Firelookout.com says it was rehabilitated in 1999.[^2] A 1984 nomination described the structure as being in good condition.[^4][^5]

The lookout was added to the National Register of Historic Places on February 14, 1986.[^4] The register lists it as US 39, MT 7, and it was registered on March 1, 1992.[^1]

The lookout is reached by trail that connects to the Boundary Trail near the Walton Ranger Station.[^3] No source reviewed says visitors may go inside the cab, so check with Glacier National Park before planning a visit.[^3][^2]

The sources also name the route below the lookout differently. The register points to the Burlington Northern railroad and U.S. Highway 2, while the nomination names the Roosevelt Highway and the Great Northern Railway.[^1][^5] Elevation is 6,771 feet on the register and 6,919 feet on firelookout.com.[^1][^2]
