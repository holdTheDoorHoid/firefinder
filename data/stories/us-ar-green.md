The Green lookout tower was in Stone County, Arkansas, on the Ozark National Forest.[^1] A June 12, 1931 item from the Baxter Bulletin, quoted on the Central US Lookouts page, said the Forest Service was just finishing a new fire tower on the old Frank Green place, between Fifty Six and Calico Rock.[^1] The item describes the site as one of the highest points in the section, and says that on days of good visibility, lookouts there and on Push Mountain could see every section of the forest's eastern district.[^1]

The page quotes a few notices that name the tower's attendants. A September 1932 item calls Francis Green an attendant at the lookout tower, and a March 1948 item says Doyle Lawrence held the job of tending it.[^1]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey station description from 1953 calls the tower a four-legged steel structure with an observers' cabin on top.[^1] The survey's 60.71-foot figure is the distance from the station to the center of the tower's base, not the tower's height.[^1]

Statewide, the Encyclopedia of Arkansas counts 122 known fire towers at the peak of the system.[^2] By 1994, forty-eight were still standing, and whether any were still in use is unknown.[^2]

No source describes the Green site today. We cannot say from these sources whether anything still stands there, or whether visitors can reach the spot.[^1]
