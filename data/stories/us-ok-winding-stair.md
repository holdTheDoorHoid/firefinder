In June 1934 a newspaper reported that a fire tower would be built on Winding Stair Mountain, at the head of Sycamore Creek, that fall.[^1] It was meant to guard new federal land added to the Kiamichi national forest, a division of the Ouachita National Forest.[^1]

By mid-November 1935 a report described a 40-foot tower as already built on the highest point of the mountain.[^1] The same item said a sky-line drive was being widened and a picnic ground cleared nearby. It also said a Civilian Conservation Corps company had been ordered disbanded and had abandoned its camp. The disbanding was unexpected, since much of the work was still unfinished.[^1]

An October 1936 notice gives a different picture of the height. It says a 28-foot wooden tower was then in use and that a 33-foot steel tower would replace it on the highest peak.[^2] The notice calls the site the Sycamore tower, placed over a federal triangulation station at 2,428 feet, and names two tower guards, Tom Doyle and Sebe Johnson.[^2] The sources do not show whether the steel tower was built, so the 1935 and 1936 heights remain in conflict.

In 1957 a short item said three men, Carl Kannady, Cecil Leachman and Harlan Hickman, worked the tower in turns.[^1] A butane gas system with a vented heater was being installed, and until then the tower had been heated with a small kerosene stove.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts site marks the tower as removed. It gives no date or method for the removal.[^1]

The Winding Stair Mountains are now part of a national recreation area in the Ouachita National Forest. A section of the Ouachita National Recreation Trail runs through it.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.
