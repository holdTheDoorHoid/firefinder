The Goetzville fire tower was in Chippewa County, in the eastern Upper Peninsula, and the Michigan Department of Conservation ran it.[^1][^2] Jim Pudelko, who lived as a boy a quarter mile from the tower, remembered it as 110 feet tall, with a ladder instead of stairs.[^2]

In April 1954 the Evening News reported that Ken Weston of Sterlingville was back on the tower.[^1] Pudelko was hired in 1965 as a Class-B towerman.[^2] He worked about a year as a towerman before military service.[^2] When he returned, fire towers were being phased out in favor of aerial patrols.[^2]

Brunner's 2016 blog post reprints a July 2016 Sault News article built around Pudelko's memories.[^2] It says the state put old towers up for public sale in the 1980s, and that the adjacent property owner bought the Goetzville tower.[^2] The account hedges those sale details.[^2]

The same post says a tower still stands at Goetzville, without saying whether it is the one the neighbor bought.[^2] We found no newer source on the site. If it is the tower the neighbor bought, it may stand on private land, and the post does not say whether the public can visit it.[^2]
