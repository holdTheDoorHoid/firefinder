Grass Mountain, a high point of the Sierra Pelona range, lies within the Angeles National Forest.[^3] A lookout went up there in 1934 as a cooperative project of the Forest Service and the Los Angeles County Forestry Department.[^1] It was a California Region revision of the L-4 cabin, set on a 10-foot open timber tower.[^1]

Records name four lookouts between 1938 and 1951: George Ealy (1938), Herbert Gage (1940), George W. Wilson (1941) and Herbert Walstead (1951).[^3]

The lookout's end came in a wildfire. The register's photo caption ties its destruction to the Munz Lake fire of August 27, 1951.[^1] A newspaper report from that day says the fire was thought to have been caused by a discarded cigarette, and that a U.S. Forestry Service lookout atop Grass Mountain went up in flames.[^2]

Reports differ on the fire's size. The August 27 report put the burn at more than 8,000 acres. A Signal report of August 30, quoted by Angeles Adventures, gave 9,000 acres for the fire that razed the tower.[^2][^3]

The lookout was not rebuilt.[^1]

Today the summit holds only a few concrete blocks, plus a remote weather station that the county fire department placed there.[^3]
