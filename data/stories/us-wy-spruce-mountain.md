The first lookout on Spruce Mountain, a 30-foot pole-and-L-4-cab tower, went up in the summer of 1938, built by Civilian Conservation Corps crews working in what is now the Medicine Bow National Forest.[^1][^2] Sources differ on exactly when it was replaced: Recreation.gov says the original stood for about thirty years before being swapped for a taller, sturdier tower, while a lookout-history site describes the present structure as a 41-foot treated-timber R-6 cab that replaced the original 1930s pole tower, without giving an exact year.[^2][^3] A lookout-history record lists a staffer named H. Arnold Maves at the site in 1950.[^3]

The tower did its job as a fire lookout into the 1990s.[^2] By then staffing of the backcountry lookout had ended, and in 1997 the Forest Service renovated the structure and opened it to the public for overnight rental.[^2] It sits at roughly 10,000 feet in the Medicine Bow National Forest, about 30 miles west-southwest of Laramie, Albany County, Wyoming.[^1][^3]

Spruce Mountain Lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in January 2015.[^1] Visit reports filed with the register in 2004 and 2008 describe a tower in generally sound condition — stairs, glass and shutters all holding up — though both visits noted it could use paint, and volunteers have pitched in on upkeep around the site.[^1]

Today the lookout remains a working rental rather than a museum piece. It's open from June through September, and for most of that season guests can drive right up to it, though lingering June snowdrifts can force visitors to pack gear in on foot for a stretch.[^2] The U.S. Forest Service's Laramie Ranger District manages bookings and can advise on current access conditions before a stay.[^2]
