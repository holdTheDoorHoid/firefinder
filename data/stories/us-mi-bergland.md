The Bergland Lookout Tower stands in Ontonagon County, Michigan, inside the Ottawa National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register believes the tower dates from the 1950s, the same period in which the town of White Pine and its company housing took shape.[^1]

The site has an earlier history. A windstorm near Bergland blew down a 100-foot steel fire watch tower. A report dated July 7, 1953 described that tower as over 20 years old and a total loss.[^2] State conservation department officials said a replacement tower would go up on the same spot, brought in from a secondary watch point.[^2] The report does not say when that move happened, and the sources read do not make clear whether the tower standing today is that replacement.

The report says no one was hurt because the night watchman was off shift when the storm arrived.[^2] The sources read name no later watchers and no fires for this site.

The Register registered the tower on January 8, 2021, after John Strasser nominated it.[^1] Its listed elevation is 1,763 feet (537 meters).[^1] The Register page does not say whether the tower still stands, and the sources do not say whether the public can visit or climb it. Ask the Ottawa National Forest before planning a trip, and do not climb the tower without permission.
