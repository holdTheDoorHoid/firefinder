Around 1932 a 30-foot pole tower went up at Martin, in the Wenatchee National Forest of Kittitas County, Washington.[^1][^2] The register gives the build date as the 1930s, and a photograph captioned as showing the tower under construction is dated 1932.[^1] Martin had grown up as a Northern Pacific stop at the east end of the Stampede Pass tunnel, which was built between 1886 and 1888.[^3]

The firelookout.com page lists one known lookout worker, Morris Jenkins, for 1933.[^2]

The register lists the lookout as gone.[^1] Pinning down the exact site needed two visits by the WillhiteWeb author, and on the second, in 2015, the footings turned up just north of the power lines.[^3]

Only a little of the town remains.[^3] Willhite puts the walk at under a mile to the tunnel and a quarter mile to the lookout site.[^3] Visitors often walk along the tracks for a look at the East Portal, although the railroad would prefer visitors not be there.[^3] No source describes a standing structure at the site today.[^1][^3] Willhite describes footings and shows a photo captioned "Decaying wood and bolts."[^3] None of the sources says whether the site is open to the public.[^1][^2][^3]
