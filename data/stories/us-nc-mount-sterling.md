Mount Sterling Tower stands at 5,842 feet, on one of the higher summits of Great Smoky Mountains National Park, in Haywood County, North Carolina.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1933: a 60-foot steel Aermotor tower topped with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] A cabin for the fire watchman once stood just north of the tower.[^1]

For years the tower was staffed on a fixed seasonal schedule, with lookouts working two-week tours from mid-February to mid-May and again from mid-October to mid-December. When they weren't on duty in the cab, they lived in that cabin.[^1] The summit has long drawn backcountry campers as well.[^1]

Like most fire lookouts, Mount Sterling's watch eventually ended. Wikipedia's entry for the mountain now calls the tower on its summit "abandoned."[^2] The National Park Service kept the structure standing, though, and put it to a new job as a radio repeater.[^1] The register entry describing it says the trap door was still open and the climb still worth it for the view, though neither source gives a date for when fire-watching there stopped.

The tower was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on March 19, 2007, nominated by Peter Barr, under the numbers US 689 and NC 10.[^1] It can be reached by the 2.7-mile Mount Sterling Trail from Mount Sterling Gap, or by longer routes: the 5.3-mile Mount Sterling Ridge Trail, or the Baxter Creek Trail from Big Creek Campground.[^2] No source found for this entry states that the tower is closed to hikers, but none confirms current public access either, so anyone planning to climb it should check current National Park Service guidance first.
