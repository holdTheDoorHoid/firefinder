Little Cowhorn Lookout sits on a mountaintop in Oregon's Willamette National Forest, in Lane County, about 20 miles north of Oakridge.[^1][^2][^3] The Forest Service describes the summit as open and rocky.[^3] Published elevations differ: the register gives 3,988 feet, while the Forest Service page gives 4,243.[^1][^3]

The register lists it as US 400, OR 47 and records its registration on December 1, 2001.[^1]

The site was first developed in 1923 with a cupola-style cabin.[^1] In 1960 that was replaced by a 14-by-14-foot flat-topped cab on a short wooden base, the building that stands there today.[^1][^5]

The new cab was in service only a few years. It was placed on standby in 1967, and the register says it was partly restored in the early 1990s.[^1] It is not currently staffed.[^3] A hiking field guide dates a restoration to 2004.[^4] According to the Forest Service, volunteers have maintained it for years, and it is in very good shape.[^3]

One lookout-history listing calls it abandoned for many years, which does not fit the later upkeep or the Forest Service's report of very good condition.[^2][^3]

Today the Forest Service lists the site as open 24 hours a day with no fee.[^3] The trail is about one mile and rated moderate, and the last stretch crosses a rock face that has a handrail, so go carefully.[^3] There is no water or restroom.[^3] A hiking field guide says the lookout is available first come, first served, and asks visitors to leave the place tidier than they found it.[^4]

The Forest Service notes that the area was affected by the Bedrock Fire, and that recent wildfires have raised the risk of landslides and debris flows.[^3] Check current notices before going.
