Deboullie Mountain stands on public reserve land in the Maine northwoods, and the Maine Forest Service owns the tower.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register listed it on November 24, 1995.[^1]

Work on a 12-foot steel tower began in 1919, and the tower was erected in 1920.[^2] Until then, the watchman had used a temporary lookout in the trees.[^2] A 48-foot steel tower with a cab followed in 1929 or 1930, and the register gives 1930 as its build year.[^2][^1]

The sources disagree on height. The register describes a 60-foot guyed tower, while the Forest Lookouts history and a local camp's website put it at 48 feet.[^1][^2][^5]

In 1937 lightning struck the lookout and destroyed it. A replacement was built straight away.[^2]

A 2020 Bangor Daily News column by the superintendent of the Allagash Wilderness Waterway says the last Maine Forest Service towers were abandoned after a 1991 budget crisis, and that Deboullie was among the last to close. The column gives no year for Deboullie's closing.[^3]

In 2016 the Maine Public Lands Division renovated the watchman's camp so it could double as a shelter and picnic spot, with helicopter support from the Forest Service.[^4]

In November 2019 an extreme windstorm knocked the historic cab off the tower, according to the camp's website.[^5] In August 2020 a helicopter company lifted new cabs onto the towers, and Deboullie's was one of three replaced that week.[^6]

The new cab was modeled on the old one.[^3] The column is headlined "Hikers can visit fire tower cabs on these 3 Maine mountains." The Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2020 Maine report describes all three towers as remote, with largely no roads to them.[^3][^6]
