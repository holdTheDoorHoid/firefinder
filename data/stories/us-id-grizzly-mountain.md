Grizzly Mountain stands at 5,950 feet in the Coeur d'Alene National Forest.[^2] The first lookout there was a 12-by-12-foot cabin of stone and frame, put up in 1914.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com calls it the first lookout established on the forest.[^1]

In 1928 an inspection report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, described plans for an L-4 type house to go up in 1929, and said a tower was also discussed.[^3] The 1914 cabin was replaced in 1929 by a cupola cabin, and the ridge top held four crow's nest platforms.[^1][^2][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists these staff members for 1930 to 1944: Roy Houghtaling in 1930, Carl Block in 1934, George Adams in 1935, Fred Bentley in 1937 and 1938, with Mrs. Mary Bentley in 1938, and Allen Rutherford in 1944.[^2] Firelookout.com also names Allen Rutherford for 1944.[^1]

The 1946 entry is a married couple, the Millers. The first name differs between the two sites: Firelookout.com gives Menno Miller, while Idaho Fire Lookouts gives Manny Miller, citing a Kellogg Evening News item of July 1, 1946.[^1][^2]

The lookout was abandoned in 1955.[^2][^3]

A September 2022 Idaho Fire Lookouts post found only rock foundations from the cabin and cupola, plus a little telephone wire.[^2] Most of the rocks have visitors' names carved on them, and the oldest the writers could make out dates to the mid-1960s.[^2] Firelookout.com says four crow's nest platforms still stand along the ridge, but the 2022 post says none remain, so the two sources disagree.[^1][^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as drive-up.[^2] No source describes whether the site is open to visitors today.[^1][^2][^3]
