The Gumboot Mountain lookout stood in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, in Skamania County, Washington, about 20 miles north-northeast of Washougal.[^1][^2]

The site sat on the Yacolt burn, an area of roughly 100,000 acres where a stand of timber was killed in 1902.[^3][^5] In July 1919 the Oregonian reported that about 7,000 pounds of parts for a lookout house were being carried by horse toward the Gumboot lookout site on that burn.[^4][^3] It was the first house ordered under a revised plan aimed at lighter buildings.[^3] It was assembled at a Portland mill and shipped in pieces.[^3]

The cabin was finished by October 1920.[^3][^4] John Huffman and John Meyers of the Forest Service built it, and a trail of three and a half miles was cut to the summit.[^4][^3] Three parties cooperated on the project: the Forest Service, the state of Washington and the Washington Forest Fire Association.[^3] One listing calls the 1920 building a cupola cabin.[^2]

The dates are disputed.[^1][^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1931 as the build year and describes an L-4 cab, and it does not mention the 1920 building.[^1] WillhiteWeb and the Forest Fire Lookout Association listing both describe the 1920 cabin and then an L-4 cab added in 1931.[^2][^3]

In August 1927 forest fires in the Columbia National Forest forced crews out of the Siouxon fire district.[^4] The Statesman Journal reported that the Gumboot station was believed likely to burn, and that the instruments had been taken out and the houses vacated.[^4]

The register and the Forest Fire Lookout Association both list the cabin as gone.[^1][^2]

WillhiteWeb describes a 2017 visit in which the final road was overgrown and the author stopped shortly before the summit.[^3] Photo captions from that visit include "Standard nails, glass and bolts," but the text does not explain what they show.[^3] The same page gives a hike of up to about a mile, and notes that in 2017 a forest road dead-ended in washouts near the bottom of the mountain.[^3] We found no source saying whether the site is open to the public today.[^3]
