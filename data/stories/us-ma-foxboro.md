High Rock, at the summit of what is now F. Gilbert Hills State Forest, was the spot the state picked for a fire watch tower as part of its High Rock Reserve park.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built a 60-foot Aermotor tower there in 1934, and phone, power and a road went in the same year.[^1][^2]

A quoted account on SEMRECC's history page said that from April to October of the following year, one observer would staff the glass-enclosed cab, spotting forest fires and telling the fire crews where each blaze was.[^1]

In 1951 AT&T bought the land under the tower, and the tower was moved to take the place of an older, inactive tower site in Dover.[^1][^2] SEMRECC says a lightning strike destroyed the moved tower in 1984, and it was later rebuilt.[^1] The Dover site now bears the name of the MacAllester Fire Tower, whose present 60-foot steel tower went up in 1953.[^5] The Forest Lookouts listing marks the High Rock tower as removed.[^3]

SEMRECC's building history covers the site and building where its headquarters stands today, but it does not say which site that is.[^1] An undated long-lines.com page about the High Rock AT&T site says its tower has a Nextel antenna system and calls the site inactive.[^4] No source we read describes what remains of the 1934 lookout cab or its footings.[^1][^2]
