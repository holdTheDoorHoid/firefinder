The Starlington Fire Tower Site is in Butler County, Alabama.[^1] The register lists its elevation at 359 feet, or 109 meters.[^1] The Alabama Forestry Commission administered the site.[^1]

Alabama once had a great many of these towers.[^2] In an opinion piece, Thomas Kaufmann writes that at one time over 200 dotted the state.[^2] Kaufmann identifies the most common explanation for their fall from use as aircraft spotting fires across far more ground than a 100-foot tower.[^2]

The register lists the site as registered on September 18, 2019, nominated by Andrew Zerbe. Its registry numbers are US 1764, AL 25.[^1]

The register says only the stands are left.[^1] By Kaufmann's estimate, probably fewer than 75 of the state's historic towers survive.[^2] The register says nothing about public access, so anyone interested should ask the Forestry Commission before going out to look.[^1]
