The Civilian Conservation Corps built the McCarthy Point lookout in 1936 on the rim of Mill Creek Canyon, in Tehama County, California, within the Lassen National Forest.[^1][^2] According to the Forest Service, the lookout was meant to watch for wildfires.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a single-story home with a bedroom enclosed in glass, used for spotting fires.[^1]

According to the Forest Service, the lookout took part in a statewide aircraft-monitoring network during World War II.[^2] The cabin was later rented out, and SFGate reported that guests paid $75 a night for a basic overnight stay.[^3]

The Park Fire of 2024 destroyed the lookout.[^1][^2] The register pages also list a removal year of 2004, which conflicts with those fire accounts, and we follow the 2024 fire accounts.[^1][^4]

The Forest Service says visitors can no longer reach the site or use it for recreation.[^2] The register says only the foundation survives, and SFGate says that foundation is stone.[^1][^3] We found no source that describes anything else left at the site.
