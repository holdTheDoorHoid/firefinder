Windy Knob is a 2,803-foot summit in Lewis County, Washington, inside the Weyerhaeuser Vail Tree Farm.[^1][^2][^4]

The sources do not agree on what stood on the summit. The register says a 140-foot spar tree with a platform went up in the 1940s and was abandoned in the 1950s.[^1] FireLookout.com gives the same decades.[^2] WillhiteWeb describes two such spar trees rather than one.[^4] The register also mentions a two-story lookout with a cab, used in the 1950s, but it says no record shows when that structure was built, abandoned or removed.[^1] WillhiteWeb has a 1952 picture of a ground cab, but no date for when that cab was placed.[^4]

The summit was occupied in 1952. A June 14, 1952 item in the Daily Chronicle of Centralia says Harry Williams was located on Windy Knob and would enjoy company if visitors let him know in advance.[^3] WillhiteWeb quotes an earlier item from the same paper, dated April 29, 1952, that places Harry Williams at the Windy Knob lookout.[^4]

Logging changed the summit later. WillhiteWeb says one side was cut around 1995 and the other around 2010, and the top served as a logging landing both times.[^4]

The register says the lookouts are gone, and it gives April 6, 2020 as the date the site was registered.[^1]

WillhiteWeb says the open top is now a place where trucks turn around.[^4] The 1952 aerial shows the structure near the middle of the summit, and WillhiteWeb says remnants are hard to find, though some may still lie in the heavy brush around the top.[^4] No source describes footings or a foundation on the summit today.

Access is by permit. WillhiteWeb says a permit is needed to visit legally: a motorized permit lets you drive to the top, and a non-motorized permit starts at a gate at the end of Pigeon Springs Road.[^4] The land is private timberland.[^2][^4]
