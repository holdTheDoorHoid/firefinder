The Ingraham Hill fire tower stood on the same hill as the radio and television transmitters of WNBF, south of Binghamton in Broome County, New York.[^1] In June 1950 the state put its plan on hold while officials checked whether a tower would interfere with those transmitters.[^1] In November 1950 the state approved a lease, and work was set to begin near the WNBF transmitter.[^1]

Bill Starr's list of New York DEC towers gives 1950 as the year the tower opened and was built, and lists it as an Aermotor LS-40, 80 feet tall.[^2] The 1950 press describes it differently. It gives a height of 91 feet overall, with the cab 80 feet up to the platform.[^1] The plans also called for a glass-enclosed cab and a three-room cabin at the foot for the observer.[^1] A July 1951 report still called the tower a work in progress, due for completion by July 15, so the exact start date is unclear.[^1][^2]

The tower was meant to do two jobs. It would be a spotter post for the civil defense programs of Broome County and Binghamton, and a fire lookout for the state.[^1] The plan called for the state to man it seven days a week from April 1 to November 30.[^1]

Starr's list says a company bought the tower in the early 1970s, took the walls off the cab, and fitted two-way radio antennas to its floor.[^2] In 2011 it still stood, but only as a skeleton of what it had been.[^2] In October 2016 the tower was taken down, and the chapter's annual report for that year calls it the one loss of the year.[^3] A November 2016 newsletter had reported that the 80-foot tower came down, and thanked Tom Vrooman for telling the chapter.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list marks the tower as gone.[^5]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. The 2011 list describes the site as posted private land and closed to the public.[^2] The 1950 press also named two different owners for the site: WNBF-TV, and Clark Associates, Inc.[^1] Stay off private land, and ask the landowner before going near the hill.
