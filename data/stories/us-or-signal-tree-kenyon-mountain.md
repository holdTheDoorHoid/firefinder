The Signal Tree station on Kenyon Mountain was in Coos County, Oregon, and the Coos Forest Protective Association administered it.[^1] A 1925 newspaper item called it a new station near Camas Valley.[^3]

Its first lookout is described two ways. The Register calls it a cupola cabin from the 1920s.[^1] A lookout-history page describes a large red fir with pipes driven up its trunk as a spiral ladder to a viewing perch.[^3] A 1924 newspaper item on that page has a lookout on Kenyon Mountain reporting a new fire near Camas Valley.[^3]

In 1935 a CCC crew from Camp Bradford finished a 100-foot tower, and the old tree and its platform were taken down.[^3] The Register also gives 1935 for a 100-foot timber tower fitted with an L-6 cab.[^1] Rainwater the pipes caught drained into the heart of the wood until the tree became dangerous and had to be removed.[^3] One timeline gives the 1935 tower as 110 feet, which the Register and firelookout.com do not.[^3][^1][^2]

The papers named some of the people who staffed it. In September 1930 Glade A. Sanford took the lookout post, replacing J. F. Burkhart, whose school had started on September 2nd.[^3] In 1931 a substitute was to be provided after the assigned lookout fell ill.[^3]

In 1959 a Coos district annual report says the 1935 tower was torn down and a 50-foot treated timber tower took its place.[^3] A May 1960 newspaper reports a 50-foot tower going up to replace one judged unsafe.[^3] The Register and firelookout.com date the present 53-foot tower to 1961, and it carried an Oregon Department of Forestry cab.[^1][^2] The sources disagree on the year and the height, so the exact date stays open.[^1][^2][^3]

A 1965 district report names Elk Mountain, about 3.5 miles southwest of Sitkum, as the successor to the Brewster Rock, Signal Tree and Blue Ridge lookouts.[^3] A 1966 entry records a cement-block building for radio-repeater equipment at the Signal Tree site.[^3] When the Register registered the tower in March 2003, it still described the tower as in active service.[^1]

Firelookout.com says a detection camera replaced the tower in the 2000s, and that the tower came down about 2011.[^2] The Oregon Lookouts timeline gives the removal date as May 26, 2011.[^3]

No source we read says what, if anything, still stands on the summit, or whether the public can reach it. The 1966 radio building and the detection camera are the last installations named, and no source says whether either survives.[^3][^2] An undated account of the CCC work says a road built by the crews made the lookout reachable by car, but that may not describe the road today.[^3]
