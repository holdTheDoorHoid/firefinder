Borestone Mountain stands in Piscataquis County, in Elliotsville Plantation.[^1][^4] Early Forestry District reports spelled it Boarstone, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's register keeps that older spelling.[^2][^3]

The Maine Forestry District put a station on the summit in 1913 or 1914.[^2][^4] A Maine lookout listing dates a groundhouse to 1913,[^4] while a 1914 report, quoted on a lookout-history site, describes a station and a small camp on top.[^2] The same report said no tower was needed, because the peak stands out abruptly.[^2] A 1917 report then called the structure a tower house, with its rods cemented into the ledge, yet still said the lookout needed no tower.[^2]

A new cabin for the lookout man was built in 1916.[^2] We found no named lookout and no fire tied to the station in the sources we read. A 1920 inventory listed the station as a wooden, ground-level cabin.[^2]

The ground house was taken down in 1950.[^2][^4] Both the Maine lookout listing and the register give the landowner's request as the reason.[^4][^3]

Today the mountain is part of the Borestone Mountain Audubon Sanctuary, run by Maine Audubon.[^1][^5] The sanctuary covers almost 1,600 acres and is open to the public year round, from dawn to dusk.[^5] The Wikipedia entry says the trail to the summit is open to hikers 365 days a year.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's coordinates for Boarstone sit within about 40 metres of Wikipedia's coordinates for the summit, so the register and the sanctuary very likely describe the same peak.[^6][^1] We found no source describing what, if anything, remains of the 1913 or 1914 station.
