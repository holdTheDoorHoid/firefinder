Devil Mountain is a peak in the San Juan National Forest, in Archuleta County, Colorado.[^1][^4] The Forest Service built a 30-foot steel tower there in 1955, with a 13-by-13-foot cab, to take over lookout duties from Chimney Rock.[^1][^2]

The two records give different sizes for the same tower. The Colorado record gives 30 feet and a 13-foot cab for the Devil Mountain tower, while the New Mexico record gives 40 feet and a 14-foot cab for the same structure at its new site. Neither explains the difference.[^1][^2]

The tower was later declared surplus. The Colorado register says it was sold to the Jicarilla Apache Nation, while the New Mexico register says the Bureau of Indian Affairs claimed it.[^1][^2] A Forest Lookouts list says it was sold and moved to a location in New Mexico.[^3] In 1996 a Chinook helicopter from the Nevada Army National Guard moved it in three trips.[^2] The New Mexico site was picked on Tecolote Mesa, to cover 131,000 acres of land the tribe had bought.[^2] The tower is now called Atole, and the New Mexico record says it is in active service with the Bureau of Indian Affairs.[^1][^2]

The footings that remain on Devil Mountain are used for a radio tower, according to the Colorado register.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the lookout as removed.[^4] None of the sources I read describes the site's condition today or says whether the public can reach it.[^1][^4]
