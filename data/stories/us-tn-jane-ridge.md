The Jane Ridge tower stood in Bledsoe County, on ground the Tennessee Landforms list puts at 2,100 feet, about 640 meters.[^1] The list's elevations come from USGS terrain data, so the figure describes the ground, not the tower.[^1]

Unlike several other Bledsoe towers on the same list, Jane Ridge has no state tower code.[^1] The list marks it as removed.[^1]

The list says most former tower locations are now private property.[^1] It does not say whether this one is.[^1] The Division's Bledsoe State Forest page says state forest fire towers are closed to the public, though visitors may photograph them.[^2] No source we read places Jane Ridge inside that forest, so that rule may not cover this site.

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Jane Ridge site today, or whether the public can reach it. The list advises against climbing the towers, because steps are often gone or rotten.[^1] Visitors should not assume that anything left at the site is safe to climb.

[^1]: Tennessee lookout towers, tnlandforms.us (Tom Dunigan), https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Bledsoe State Forest, Tennessee Division of Forestry, https://www.tn.gov/agriculture/forests/state-forests/bledsoe.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
