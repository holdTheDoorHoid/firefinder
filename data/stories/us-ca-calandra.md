Calandra Lookout stands on Williams Hill in Monterey County.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates it to 1942 and says its cab is a BC-301 design.[^1] A 2010 visit note on Peakbagging gives the build year as 1944 and describes a 30-foot L-601 tower with a 14-by-14 wood cab, so the two sources disagree on the date.[^5]

The tower carries the name of Joseph Calandra, an assistant state forest ranger with the California Department of Forestry, now Cal Fire.[^4] He was caught by fire on the Bixby Mountain Fire in Big Sur on 16 August 1939 and died of his burns.[^4] A bronze plaque on the site says the lookout honors him for saving a companion.[^1] A 1939 newspaper item quoted on the California Lookouts site places the blaze on the Little Sur River, so the sources name the place differently.[^2]

On 28 July 1945 a fire on Williams Hill trapped two men: Edward Gates, a dozer operator for the Los Padres National Forest, and Assistant State Forest Ranger Paul Nesgis.[^3] Gates left his machine to run on foot, but the fire caught him in thick chaparral. Nesgis was reported missing, and his body was not found until the following March.[^3] The Register says both men died in a fire visible from the station.[^1]

The latest condition report I found, on the Register page, is from a November 2019 visit. It calls the tower serviceable, says it needs paint, and says it would need a state building inspection and a radiation safety check.[^1] The aluminum skin has protected the support timbers, and the report says the cab's outlook is still usable despite the nearby towers.[^1] The same visit marks the site as unstaffed.[^1]

Reaching the summit is the hard part. One road to the top crosses private land, where Cal Fire has permission to enter. The other crosses a Bureau of Land Management recreation area and ends at a locked gate.[^1][^5] The Register notes no NHLR sign and no signed directions to the tower.[^1] Anyone planning a visit should ask Cal Fire or the BLM first.
