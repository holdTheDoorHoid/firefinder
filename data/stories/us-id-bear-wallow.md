Bear Wallow began as a two-story log cabin with a cupola, built in 1930.[^2][^4] The register dates a D-6 cupola at the site to the same year.[^1]

In 1956 the cabin was replaced by an L-4 cab on a tower of treated timber, 41 feet tall.[^2][^4][^5] The register gives the new tower's height as 30 feet.[^1] The Forest Service history says the new tower was up by 1956 and the log lookout that came before it was removed.[^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists one known name for the site, Gail Renshaw, in 1948.[^3] The Forest Service says the tower had about two decades of staffing.[^5]

Four sources give 1971 as the year the lookout was abandoned.[^1][^2][^3][^4] That does not sit well with the Forest Service's figure of about 20 years of staffing.[^5] The dates for its removal also differ. The register and Rex's give 1986.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts say it was gone by 1987.[^2][^3] The Forest Service history quotes a packer who remembered taking it down in 1984. It says the old tower was cleaned out and burned in 1981, 1984 or 1987.[^5]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the site today. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as hike-in and gone.[^3] The packer's account describes crews carrying out the windows, chairs and tools by mule, and reaching the road by Selway Falls the next morning.[^5] Whether the site is open to visitors is not stated in these sources. Check with the Nez Perce-Clearwater National Forests before planning a trip.[^6]
