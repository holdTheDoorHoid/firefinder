Also known as the Luminary Tower, the Worthington Knob Fire Tower is in Bledsoe County, Tennessee, above the Sequatchie Valley.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register gives its elevation as 2,554 feet (778 metres).[^1][^3] It dates construction to 1961 and 1962.[^1] The tower is about 50 feet tall, and the register considers it probably an Aermotor design.[^1] The sources read do not explain where the Luminary name comes from.[^1]

The Tennessee Division of Forestry administers the tower.[^1] A regional lookout site lists it in the Division's District 3, in Bledsoe County.[^2] No source read names the people who staffed the tower or records any fires it spotted.[^1][^2]

The Tennessee inventory numbers the tower 3-7, taken from a 1976 list of towers, and marks it as standing.[^3] The register gives its registry numbers as US 1299 and TN 34, and records its registration on March 17, 2019, with Victoria Oliver as nominator.[^1]

The register's photograph is dated April 2018 and credited to Victoria Oliver.[^1] A state page on Bledsoe State Forest says fire towers on state forests are not open to the public, and that visitors may photograph but not climb them.[^4] The sources read do not say whether this tower stands on state forest land, so check with the Division before planning a visit.[^1][^4]
