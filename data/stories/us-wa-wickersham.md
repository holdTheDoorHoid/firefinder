The Wickersham lookout stood in Skagit County, Washington.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was built in 1941 as an 83-foot wooden tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab and a cabin, and lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as administrator.[^1] The firelookout.com listing gives the same tower and cab.[^2]

Willhite doubts that account. He cites a Kresek lookout inventory that places an 83-foot tower called Wickersham on Anderson Mountain in 1941, but he says he has found no written or ground evidence of a lookout there, and he suspects the inventory was wrong.[^4]

A different record describes an Aircraft Warning Service station.[^3] The AWS was a civilian service within the Army's Ground Observer Corps during World War II.[^5] Forest Lookouts calls the site Aircraft Warning Service Station "Yoke 1-7." It was activated on September 2, 1942, under the Bellingham Filter Center.[^3][^4] AWS funds paid for a 14-by-32-foot cabin on private property.[^3][^4] After deactivation, the State of Washington kept the site for fire detection.[^3]

Weyerhaeuser company maps marked the cabin as a fire lookout.[^4] Willhite is unsure whether the cabin was used for fire at all, or for how long, and he guesses the trees quickly overtook the view.[^4]

The sources also disagree on where the site stood and how high it is. Forest Lookouts gives it as 36N-4E-2, which is section 2, while Willhite's list of AWS stations puts it in the northeast quarter of the southeast quarter of section 5.[^3][^4] The register gives the elevation as 3,365 feet (1,026 meters), but Willhite gives about 2,400 feet.[^1][^4]

The register and firelookout.com both say the tower and cabin are gone, and Forest Lookouts marks the site as removed.[^1][^2][^3] A 2023 update on Willhite's page says the new landowners found a map that places a fire lookout at this spot, though he adds that the area was recently thinned and trees now cover around 90 percent of the forest floor.[^4]

According to Willhite, the land is private. A dirt road from the small town of Wickersham leads to a gate with parking, and hikers have told him that signs there claim a permit is needed.[^4] We found no source that gives current access rules, so ask the landowners before going.
