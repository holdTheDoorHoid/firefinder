Brundage Lookout stands on the summit ridge of Brundage Mountain, about six miles north-northwest of McCall in Valley County, Idaho.[^1][^2][^5] The register gives its elevation as 7,569 feet, while Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 7,552.[^1][^3]

The site's history starts well before the current cab. A log cabin for living quarters, still there, dates to 1914, and a two-story log house of 18 by 18 feet followed in 1919.[^2][^4] The register and Idaho Fire Lookouts date the 20-by-20-foot log lookout that replaced it to 1929, and Idaho Fire Lookouts says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it. Forest Lookouts places it in the 1930s, also with Civilian Conservation Corps labor.[^1][^3][^4] The current two-story flat-top cab went up in 1982.[^1][^2][^3] Sources disagree on what it is made of: the register says concrete and wood on a rubble base, firelookout.com describes a wooden cabin on a concrete slab, and Forest Lookouts describes cinder block and wood frame.[^1][^2][^4]

A November 1925 newspaper item, which Forest Lookouts attributes to the Twin Falls Daily News, called the Brundage station typical of the lookouts the Forest Service maintained and described the view over valuable timber and grazing land.[^4]

Staff changed often. In 1936 the Idaho Statesman reported that Johnny Hargraves, who had for years been in charge, was seriously ill, and by July a couple from Ola were set to take charge for the summer.[^4] Firelookout.com lists Paul and Virginia Drahn in 1943.[^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts adds Mr. and Mrs. B.I. Hale from 1946 to 1954 and Mr. and Mrs. Roger Taylor from 1956 to 1962, a couple known for keeping a piano in the lookout.[^3] In 1929, 671 people registered at the lookout, according to Forest Lookouts, which also describes a narrow road to its top.[^4]

The pages read name no specific fire that the lookout spotted. A 1925 item describes the work in general terms: a smoke is seen, the range is taken, and fire crews are sent out.[^4]

The 1982 cab is the building there today.[^1] The register says the Southern Idaho Timber Protective Association staffs the lookout each fire season, and Idaho Fire Lookouts says the same.[^1][^3] The register says the timber association administers it, with the Forest Service and Idaho Department of Lands as cooperators, but the resort calls it an active Forest Service lookout, so these sources do not settle who runs it.[^1][^5]

The tower is not open to the public. The resort says a viewing platform is open, and that the lookout serves as someone's residence and active workplace.[^5] From the top of the Bluebird Express chairlift, the Lookout Trail runs south along the summit ridge, which the resort calls rocky but relatively flat.[^5] Hikers come in summer and skiers in winter.[^1] No rental turned up in the sources read.
