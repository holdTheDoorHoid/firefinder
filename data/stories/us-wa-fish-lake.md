Fish Lake Lookout stood in Kittitas County, Washington, on the Wenatchee National Forest, at about 3,700 feet, or 1,128 meters.[^1][^2] Both the register and firelookout.com describe a 40-foot pole L-4 tower built in 1934 near the valley floor.[^1][^2] The Willhite page gives the same design and calls the valley-floor site unique.[^4] Panorama photos dated August 16, 1934 are credited to the National Archives.[^1][^3] The Willhite page also repeats an unverified online claim that an earlier lookout in this area, built in an avalanche zone, lasted only a year.[^4]

The only lookout named in our sources is Dave McDonald, listed for 1933 on firelookout.com.[^2] That year falls before the 1934 build date on the same page, and the sources do not explain the gap.[^2]

The sources agree the tower was gone by 1948.[^1][^2][^4] The register says it was removed that year, while firelookout.com and the Willhite page say it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^4]

The Willhite page reports that forest now surrounds the old site.[^4] Its photo captions label footings and an anchor, and one shows a clearing in the trees that served as the lookout's parking area.[^4]

The Willhite page gives directions by way of Exit 80 off I-90, north through Roslyn and Ronald to Salmon la Sac, then turning right on FR 4330 toward Tucquala Lake.[^4] The road is gravel and good for all vehicles.[^4] The old lookout road, Road 6231, was abandoned at its junction near the lake, and the page says to walk it about 0.3 miles to the site.[^4] Another route starts from the Fish Lake Guard Station, just down the hillside, where a trail heads up the hill.[^4] The register's 3,700 feet and the 3,460 feet in the Willhite access notes do not match.[^1][^4]
