The Medicine Lodge Lookout was a fire tower in the Cypress Hills, which lie on the Alberta and Saskatchewan border.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says it was first erected in 1967 as a steel tower with a round crow's nest cab.[^1]

The register says the tower was dismantled in 1993.[^1] It was then reestablished as a 6.1 m (20 ft) tower in memory of Forest Officer Thomas N. Trott.[^1] Trott had hoped to turn the Medicine Lodge fire tower site into a forestry museum.[^2] He had a fatal heart attack on 10 September 1996, while the project was still underway.[^2]

His colleagues finished the project in July 1997 and named the museum the Tom Trott Forestry Museum, to honour him.[^2] The register entry is dated July 15, 1997.[^1]

Park staff at Cypress Hills now invite people to visit the Tom Trott Forestry Museum.[^2] We did not find a source that says whether the 6.1 m tower still stands or whether it can be climbed. Ask park staff before you go, and stay away from any tower that is closed.

[^2]: Tom Trott Forestry Museum, Veterans Affairs Canada, https://www.veterans.gc.ca/en/remembrance/memorials/canada/tom-trott-forestry-museum (accessed 2026-10-20).
