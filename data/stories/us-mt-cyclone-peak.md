Cyclone Peak Lookout sits in the Whitefish Range of the Flathead National Forest, about four miles south of Polebridge.[^4][^2] The tower standing there now is a 41-foot treated timber structure with an R-6 cab, built in 1967.[^1][^2] It replaced a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab that dated to 1935.[^1][^2] The record does not say why the older tower was replaced.

The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower is staffed in the summer.[^1] A 2014 Flathead Beacon guide says the same.[^3] The site firelookout.com lists known lookout staff beginning with a married couple in 1957 and a man in 1958.[^2] The sources I checked describe no fires, storm damage or rebuilds after 1967.

A hiking guide still calls it an active fire lookout.[^4] The NHLR registered it on January 9, 2021, as US 1499, MT 77.[^1]

The Flathead Beacon says the route begins at a gated dirt road and gains about 1,000 feet to the tower.[^3] The guides do not agree on the distance. The Flathead Beacon calls it a 2.5-mile jaunt, while Trekking Wild describes a 4.2-mile out-and-back hike.[^3][^4] One Trekking Wild visitor found the building locked on an earlier fall hike.[^4] On a later visit a volunteer doing maintenance on the windows invited the writer in for a short tour.[^4] That guide says access to the building depends on staffing and on whether the person on duty can welcome visitors.[^4]

No rental listing turned up in the sources. Check with the Flathead National Forest before the trip, since the cab may be locked.
