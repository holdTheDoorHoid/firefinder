The City of Salamanca put up a steel fire tower in the fall of 1938.[^1] The 2022 Forest Fire Lookout Association newsletter says the city erected it to watch about 1,100 acres it had bought and reforested around its water reservoir in Newton Run Hollow, just north of the city.[^1] The earlier newspaper account the article quotes credits the city's Water & Light Department.[^1] The article places the tower in the Town of Little Valley, and the tower now stands in Rock City State Forest in Cattaraugus County.[^1][^2]

The 2022 writer could not name the maker.[^1] The tower is about 40 feet tall to its floor, and the writer ruled out an Aermotor, an International Derrick, a Blaw-Knox and an International Stacey. The writer found no name plate, and the steel is so rusted that no stencil marks were visible.[^1]

According to the writer, a man from Elkdale staffed the tower before World War II, though the article that named him has been lost.[^1] During the war, Civilian Air Patrol crews logged aircraft movements from the tower.[^1] The writer believes it was no longer staffed when the war ended, as the city's forests had matured and the state's McCarty Hill tower came into use.[^1] The writer could find no other newspaper article about the tower.[^1]

The tower still stands, but its cab, if it ever had one, is missing. The lowest rung of the external ladder has come loose from its base.[^1] The writer climbed one step and then stopped, out of concern that the tower might collapse.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list shows it as a standing platform tower.[^3]

The tower sits on State Forest land.[^1] The writer biked into the State Forest from the Hungry Hollow Road, which was open to the summit ridge that day, and then went on foot looking for the tower.[^1] The roads to the parking area and to Little Rock City open on April 1, or once they dry out enough for a car.[^1] Anyone visiting should leave the tower alone, since the writer judged it at risk of collapse.[^1]
