The Clove Mountain fire tower stands on Clove Mountain in Dutchess County, New York, about seven miles south of the village of Millbrook.[^2][^4] The New York State Conservation Department erected a steel fire lookout tower on the summit in 1933, with an observer's cabin finished soon after.[^1]

Two sources agree on the basics. The tower is 60 feet tall and is an Aermotor LS-40 model.[^1][^2] It sits about three and a half miles east of James Baird State Park, on the Taconic State Parkway.[^2]

Planning came first. In 1930, early wildfire detection in Dutchess County was judged inadequate, and a tower for Clove Mountain was formally proposed along with one for Mount Beacon.[^1]

The first five observers at Clove served together from April 1933 until May 1960, and each was hired at a starting salary of $100 a month.[^1] A 1953 notice quoted by the Forest Lookouts site names Oscar Ferris as the fire observer on Clove Mountain that year.[^3] We found no record of a particular fire the tower spotted.

The tower stayed in service through the 1988 fire season and was officially decommissioned the following year.[^1] Both the tower and its cabin still stand, but they are not actively maintained and sit on private property.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the tower as standing but closed to visitors, and that entry carries no date.[^2][^4]

Please do not try to climb or reach the tower. It is on private land, and the list says it is closed to the public.[^1][^2]
