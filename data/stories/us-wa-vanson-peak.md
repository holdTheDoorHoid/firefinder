Vanson Peak sits near the north edge of the Mount St. Helens National Volcanic Monument, in Lewis County, Washington.[^1][^2] The summit held an L-4 cab, which the Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates to 1930.[^3] Firelookout.com gives the same year.[^4] Eric Willhite, whose trip report covers the site, says the cab was likely built that year.[^1][^2] His page also has photos from 1929, taken when the site was a camp.[^2]

From 1942 to 1944 the Aircraft Warning Service used the summit for spotting.[^2] The Seattle Filter Center activated that post on March 19, 1942, and deactivated it on April 1, 1944.[^1]

In May 1964 a newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said the Vanson Peak lookout and two others in the Spirit Lake ranger district would be closed that summer, except for possible emergency use.[^1]

Accounts of the end differ. The register says the cab was removed in the 1960s, while firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed sometime in that decade.[^2][^3][^4]

Today the summit is a small rock outcrop and clearing that looks out over Mount Rainier, Mount Adams and Riffe Lake.[^5] The Washington Trails Association describes it as a day hike from the Goat Mountain trail, inside the Monument.[^5] The easiest approach starts on Weyerhaeuser land and needs an access permit or key, according to Willhite.[^2] Willhite also reports that the blast zone stopped just short of the site.[^2]

Willhite's photos include one captioned "The front step to the lookout building." No source we read says more about what stands there now.[^2]
