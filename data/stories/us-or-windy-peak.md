Windy Peak is a summit near Greenleaf in Lane County, Oregon.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the tower was built in 1938 as a 50-foot live-in tower.[^1] The Oregon Lookouts page describes it as a 50-foot round pole timber tower, and says a road to the peak was completed in September 1937, with work on the tower starting that same week.[^2]

The Forest Service ran the lookout, with observers on duty.[^2] A 1941 item describes a five-man Forest Service crew doing maintenance, including lightning protection.[^2] In April 1943, two women went up to relieve the observers for a week.[^2]

The Oregon Lookouts page records the lookout as activated on March 16, 1942, with the Eugene Filter Center listed beside the date.[^2] A 1944 report says the Aircraft Warning Service used it as post William 9-1, and that the Forest Service kept the station for fire detection when that service ended.[^2]

A June 1949 item named Jack Francis of Michigan State University as the only new lookout, and said his wife and small son would be with him.[^2]

The lookout was destroyed in a 1958 windstorm.[^1][^2][^3]

The register lists the site as administered by the Bureau of Land Management.[^1] firelookout.com pairs the Siuslaw National Forest with the BLM.[^3] We found no source that describes what, if anything, is left on the summit now, or whether the public can reach it.

In a November 2024 news release, the BLM said it had closed Windy Peak Road to vehicles in Lane County after a landslide, and planned to keep it closed for two years.[^4] The release does not mention the old lookout, so it is unclear whether the closure affects the route to the summit.
