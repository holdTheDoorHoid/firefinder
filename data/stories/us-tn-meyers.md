Meyers is a lookout site in Bledsoe County, Tennessee, listed at an elevation of 2,110 feet (643 m).[^1] Forest Lookouts also lists a Meyers lookout in Bledsoe County.[^3]

The Tennessee Division of Forestry was responsible for building 208 fire lookout towers between about 1930 and 1970, according to a 2014 National Register of Historic Places study of its towers.[^2] That study's table of towers still standing on March 26, 2014 includes Meyers, in Bledsoe County.[^2]

Tennessee Landforms marks the site as historical.[^1] Its list covers both current towers and former sites.[^1] The two sources disagree on whether the tower still stands.[^1][^2] The landforms list was revised in January 2026, so it is the newer of the two.[^1]

Today, the same list also reports that many towers are gone and that most lookout sites in Tennessee are now on private land.[^1] We found no source that describes the current condition of the Meyers site, so we cannot say what, if anything, remains of the tower or whether visitors may go there. The list advises against climbing the towers and notes that steps have often been removed.[^1]
