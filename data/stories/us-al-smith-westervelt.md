The National Historic Lookout Register places the Smith-Westervelt Lookout in Tuscaloosa County, Alabama, and describes it as a 100-foot steel tower.[^1] The register says the tower was dedicated in July 1940 in honor of Herbert E. Westervelt, who founded Gulf States Paper, now the Westervelt Company.[^1] It also dates the steel tower to about 1940, as a replacement for a wooden predecessor that burned.[^1]

A Tuscaloosa News item from March 5, 1966, reproduced on a lookout-history site, tells a different story.[^2] The report dates the wooden Westervelt tower to 1940 and ties it to H.E. Westervelt.[^2] The same storm account says the wooden tower was lifted clean off its foundation, guide wires kept it from being blown away, and the Westervelt Tower was among the towers destroyed.[^2] The report also says a steel replacement for the Westervelt tower was already being planned.[^2]

The sources do not agree on what ended the first wooden tower. The register says it burned, while the 1966 report points to a tornado. They also disagree on which tower dates from 1940: the register puts the steel tower at about 1940, and the report puts the wooden tower there.[^1][^2]

The register lists its registration date as May 27, 2016, and names the Alabama Forestry Commission as administrator.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page has a Pickens County heading over its Westervelt entry, while the register places the tower in Tuscaloosa County.[^1][^2]

I found no source that confirms a tower stands at the site today, so its current condition is unverified. The sources also do not say whether it can be climbed or visited. Ask the Alabama Forestry Commission before going to the site.[^1][^2]

[^1]: Smith-Westervelt Lookout, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/al/smith-westervelt-lookout/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Westervelt, Forest Lookouts, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/westervelt.html (accessed 2026-10-09).
