Grasshopper Mountain sits in the Umpqua National Forest in Douglas County, Oregon, about 33 miles northeast of Tiller.[^3] A camp was set up there in 1912, and a cupola cabin was built in 1925.[^1][^5]

In 1933 the Civilian Conservation Corps built an L-4 cab on a round timber tower about ten feet tall.[^3][^5] A 1933 newspaper item said the new lookout would replace the old one.[^3] In 1958 the Forest Service replaced that cab with a prefabricated R-6 flat cab.[^1][^3] A helicopter carried segments of the prefabricated house to the top, and 3,800 pounds of building materials were delivered to the summit.[^3]

Victor Boyd left for Tiller in 1917 to be stationed at the lookout, and Euberto Newton of Tiller lived in a tent for about a month in 1958 while the old house was torn down.[^3] In July 1927 lookout-fireman Walter Lerwell reported the first fire at 4 p.m. and had it under control by nightfall, according to a Roseburg News-Review item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page.[^3]

The lookout came down in 1977.[^1][^3][^5] The registry, the Forest Lookouts page and a 2018 hike report all say it was removed, but firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3][^5]

The most recent account of what remains on the summit that we found is a 2018 hike report. It reports four concrete footings at the top, with a cedar tree grown up beside one of them.[^5] The Forest Service describes Trail #1580 as passing through meadows to the former lookout, which lies inside the Rogue-Umpqua Divide Wilderness.[^4] The trail is reached from Grasshopper Trail #1574 via the Acker Divide Trailhead, and wilderness restrictions apply.[^4] No water is available on the trail, and expect snow cover from mid-fall through mid-spring.[^4] The trail stops at 800-foot cliffs, where Cliff and Buckeye Lakes come into view.[^4] We found no later description of what remains on the summit.
