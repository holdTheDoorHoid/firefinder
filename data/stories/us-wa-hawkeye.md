Hawkeye Point is a 7,431-foot summit in the Goat Rocks Wilderness, about 11 miles southeast of Packwood in Lewis County.[^1][^2][^5]

A cupola cabin went up on the summit in 1927, the first lookout built in the Packwood District.[^5] It was used from 1927 to 1933.[^3] WillhiteWeb's page includes a 1928 journal kept by the honeymoon couple who staffed the lookout, and panoramic photos were taken from the cabin in 1929.[^3][^4]

Fog ended its run. WillhiteWeb says the lookout was abandoned in 1933 because fog too often enveloped the peak and impaired visibility, and a replacement went up down the ridge at Goat Ridge.[^3] TrailChick also reports that heavy fog forced the change.[^5]

The building stood for many years after it was abandoned. USGS survey parties mentioned it in the 1940s, and a 1956 newspaper reported that foresters had stopped using the station several years before, because its elevation was too great and low cloud often hid the forest from view.[^3][^4] The register says it was gone by 1966. WillhiteWeb thinks it likely burned that year, while TrailChick says it was destroyed sometime in the mid-1960s.[^1][^3][^5]

The summit is reached on foot. WillhiteWeb lists a hike of about five miles one way with 3,300 feet of gain.[^3] TrailChick's 2017 loop reached it by a steep boot path that climbs roughly 800 feet over three-quarters of a mile from the Lily Basin trail junction.[^5] TrailChick found the broad summit a nice picnic spot with a summit benchmark, and WillhiteWeb says the original USGS benchmark was stamped Hawkeye L.O. 1938.[^3][^5] None of the sources describes what, if anything, remains of the cabin itself.
