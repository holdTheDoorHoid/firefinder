Glastenbury Mountain sits in Bennington County, Vermont, and its steel fire tower dates to 1927. The Vermont Timberland Owners' Association ordered the tower in late 1926, with the cooperation of the state forest service. It was meant to cover a section of Bennington County that had little fire protection.[^1][^2]

The tower was dedicated on July 4, 1927. Five miles of telephone cable tied it into the network.[^2] Sources give different heights. A December 1926 newspaper gives 47 feet, a June 1970 article says 60 feet, and the register gives 50 feet.[^1][^2] The same 1926 newspaper said the observation room would be eight feet square, reached by 65 steps.[^1]

Repairs were made to the tower in 1932.[^1][^2] In 1938 the trail was improved, the tower and camp were painted, and a new fire finder map was installed.[^2][^1] The tower was staffed into the late 1940s.[^1][^2] An April 1947 notice sought a man for the post, and the register says the last year it was manned was 1949.[^1][^2] In December 1958 a local paper reported that air patrols now handled fire protection for the area.[^1]

A July 1975 article says the National Forest had obtained the site from the Glastenbury Timberlands Trust, along with an easement for the Appalachian Trail.[^1] A Youth Conservation Corps crew rebuilt the tower with materials flown in by helicopter.[^2] The register dates that work to 1976, while a July 1976 newspaper says it happened the summer before.[^2][^1] A Vermont chapter report says the tower was renovated between 2005 and 2006.[^3]

In 2022, after an inspection found them in poor condition, the first flight of stairs was removed.[^2] That same year a Vermont chapter report said it had received reports that the FAA had closed the tower for structural integrity reasons.[^3] A Forest Service alert gives a start date of April 22, 2025, so the two dates do not match.[^4] The register added the tower on December 31, 2023.[^2]

Today the tower is closed to public use.[^4] The Forest Service says the FAA has recently deemed it structurally unsafe, and it asks people not to climb it.[^4] A hiker's account from July 2026 found it still closed, with no views from the summit.[^5]

The trails around the mountain remain open, including the Glastenbury/West Ridge Loop.[^4] Before you plan a trip, call the Manchester Ranger Station, which the alert lists as its contact.[^4]
