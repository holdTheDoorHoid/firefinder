The Shelburne fire tower crowns the summit of Massaemet Mountain above Shelburne Falls, in Franklin County [^1][^3]. The register says the Massaemet Tower Committee began building it in 1909 and that it went into service in 1911 [^1]. A November 1911 newspaper item says the State Forestry Commission set up its station on the mountain in August of the year before, which is 1910 [^2]. The stone was quarried on the site [^1].

The register puts the tower at 63 feet and describes a 10-by-10-foot wooden cab that has been improved several times [^1]. A 1940 Massachusetts Geodetic Survey station description gives the stone tower as 60 feet high [^2].

Nelson Woodward was the observer for about 26 seasons, and he resigned in 1937, when Donald G. Wood took the post [^2]. Later observers included Kendal S. Woods, named in 1942, and Preston Ware, who reported that 505 visitors signed the log in the summer and fall of 1948 [^2]. In 1969 Harley Hunkler was the fire watcher in charge [^2]. Early on, the observer had sighted several forest fires from the summit [^2].

In 1916 the tower was the property of the village's citizens, a committee controlled it, and the state wanted to take it over [^2]. By 1935 the town had presented the structure to the state, on the stipulation that it stay available to hikers at all times [^2].

The register says the Bureau of Forest Fire Control operates the tower [^1]. The register's 2016 visit reports describe a seasonal observer on duty from late March to early September [^1]. A recent trail guide says the tower is still in active service [^3].

A 1.25-mile trail climbs to the stone tower from a kiosk where Route 2 meets Halligan Avenue [^4]. The wooden cab stays locked when no one is using it, but the public can still climb the 72 spiral steps to the top [^3].
