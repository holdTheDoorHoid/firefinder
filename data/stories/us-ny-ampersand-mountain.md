Ampersand Mountain, in Franklin County, got a state observation station in August 1911, but at first there was no tower.[^1] The first structure was a stone hut for the observer.[^1] A steel Aermotor LS40 tower went up in 1921, and the state's annual report recorded a new steel tower at the station that year.[^1][^4] An Essex County newspaper that spring said the frame would be set in concrete on the granite summit.[^1]

Sources disagree on the height, and most give 22 feet.[^1][^2][^3][^4] A 1942 survey description of the station calls the four-legged steel tower about 50 feet high.[^1]

Walter C. Rice was the observer on top for about eight years.[^1] A June 1922 newspaper reported that his health would no longer let him work at that altitude, so he gave up the post.[^1] Wikipedia says he manned the tower until 1923, so the two accounts do not quite match.[^4] In 1959 a bear attacked a state observer as he walked down from the tower. He was treated and went home.[^1]

In September 1921 a strong west wind raised the risk of a fire getting out of control and broke telephone wires to the towers on nearby peaks.[^1] Telephone service between Ampersand and Saint Regis Mountain was disrupted.[^1]

The state closed the tower at the end of the 1970 season, once aerial detection had taken over.[^1] Wikipedia says aircraft had become the more cost-effective way to spot fires.[^4] The structure was taken down in 1977 because it was no longer needed, and because it was a non-conforming use in the newly created High Peaks Wilderness Area.[^1] The sources differ on the month: one says August and the other says July.[^1][^4]

Today the summit is bare rock, and a memorial to Walter Channing Rice stands near the top.[^4][^6] On the trail about 1.6 miles up, the old observer's cabin site has the remains of a metal bedframe. Little else of the cabin survives.[^5] The sources I read do not describe whether any of the tower's concrete footings remain.

The summit is on state land, and the trail is open to hikers.[^5] The trail starts at a trailhead on NY 3, and it is short but steep.[^5] The hike is very popular, and the parking area often overflows.[^5] The summit stands at the northwestern corner of the High Peaks Wilderness, and state land stewards are weighing ways to move and enlarge the trailhead.[^5]
