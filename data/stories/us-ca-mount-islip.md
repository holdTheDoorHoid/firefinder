In September 1927 Los Angeles County's forestry department announced that a steel lookout would be erected on Mount Islip, within the Angeles National Forest.[^1] Planners expected mule pack trains to carry the steel ten miles from where the nearest road ended.[^1] The register says the finished tower, built in 1927, had an 8-by-8 observation cabin atop 22 feet of X-brace steel framing, and it notes a stone residence cabin close by.[^2]

The register and Angeles Adventures disagree about the agency. The register names the county fire department, while Angeles Adventures names the county forestry department.[^2][^3] Angeles Adventures also says that Islip, like other county lookouts of the 1920s, had a stone hut as living quarters for staff and their families.[^3]

Work had begun by September 23, 1927, and in June 1928 a newspaper called the new tower a big asset to the county's forestry department.[^1] A January 1929 report, looking back on the year, described putting up the tower and its cabin on the peak as an unusually hard job.[^1] The register gives the summit as 8,262 feet, while the 1929 report gives 8,240 feet.[^2][^1]

A federal forest lookout was reported at the tower in August 1938.[^1] A former lookout's wife later recalled paying five dollars a month in rent for their one-room lookout, and said groceries were brought up once a month.[^3]

The register says the tower was reportedly relocated to South Mount Hawkins during 1938.[^2] Angeles Adventures gives the same year and says the tower had stood about a decade.[^3] The register adds that a later structure stood until 2002.[^2]

Today, concrete footings from the tower are still on Islip, beside the stone cabin's ruins.[^3] Modern Hiker reports that, near the summit, an old stone cabin's remains and a fire lookout's foundations still stand.[^4] Modern Hiker calls the hike a 7-mile trail that begins at Islip Saddle, and the Forest Service lists a 0.85-mile Mt. Islip Trail that begins at Windy Gap.[^4][^5]
