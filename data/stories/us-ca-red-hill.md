Red Hill is a lookout site in the Plumas National Forest, in Plumas County, California.[^1][^3] In October 1931 the Indian Valley Record reported that the Forest Service would put a lookout station there to add fire protection over a large area of forest.[^1] By May 1934 the Feather River Bulletin was describing a new road to the Red Hill lookout, which it called a 60-foot steel tower.[^1]

A 1951 item from the same paper puts the lookout's quarters on top of a 50-foot tower.[^1] The two items disagree on the height, and we could not settle it. Our record uses the 60-foot figure, which is 18.3 metres.

The clippings name a run of seasonal lookouts. Bob Cotter was at the Red Hill lookout in July 1934.[^1] James Fair took the post in 1941 and was lookout again in 1942.[^1] Mariemily Lenartz handled the station in 1943.[^1] In 1945 Lt. Robert L. Pease, a recently discharged veteran, was installed there.[^1] In 1946 Rufe McFadden watched from Red Hill and guided a fire crew to a blaze by radio.[^1] Lillie Andrews was the lookout in 1951,[^1] and Jean Rippey was back as lookout on Red Hill in 1964.[^1]

In June 1956 a crew was getting the lookout ready for occupancy.[^1] The latest item on the Forest Lookouts page is from 1964, and it says nothing about what happened to the tower after that.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks the tower Standing*.[^3] Its statewide list gives the same mark.[^2] According to the association's legend, the asterisk marks a tower that keeps its structure but has lost its cab.[^3] Both lists therefore show a standing structure, but we could not confirm its current state from any other source we found. We found no public access information. Anyone curious should check with the Plumas National Forest first, and should not climb any structure they find there.
