Hunter Peak stands in Park County, Wyoming, on the Shoshone National Forest, about 30 miles northwest of Cody.[^2]

In late September 1912, supplies were being hauled from Cody to the peak, which lies two miles from Crandall Creek, for a Forestry Department lookout cabin.[^1] The plan called for an eight-foot-square cabin with windows on all sides at the summit, a telephone inside, and a second cabin below it, near water, for the ranger.[^1]

The station was finished by November 1913. A local paper described a 13-by-13-foot building with an 8-by-8-foot lookout tower on it, and said a telephone line now ran straight to the Cody office.[^1] A 1913 memorandum said a man would be kept on the lookout, and that it could report fires in the Park as well as in the forest.[^1]

The fire season ran August through October, when a man was to occupy the building.[^1] In July 1914 the Forest Service planned repairs to the Cody-Sunlight telephone line, which it said connected directly with the Hunter Peak station.[^1] An August 1914 item said Arthur Braten had left for Crandall Creek and would take the work at a "Lookout Station." The item does not name the peak, but it is probably this one, since the peak lies near Crandall Creek.[^1]

The two descriptions differ. The 1913 paper gives a 13-by-13-foot building, while the firelookout.com listing gives a 10-foot log base. Both describe an 8-by-8-foot lookout on top.[^1][^2]

The firelookout.com listing marks the lookout gone.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page ends its entry with the word "Removed."[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a ground lookout that is gone.[^3]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the peak now, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
