The Williamstown Tower Site was on Berlin Mountain in Berkshire County, Massachusetts.[^1] The register gives the site's elevation as 2,978 feet, or 908 meters.[^1] Wikipedia gives the mountain's height as 2,818 feet (859 m), so the two figures disagree.[^2]

The Commonwealth of Massachusetts built the 50-foot steel tower around 1915.[^1][^2] It was one of two towers built to replace the lookout on Mount Greylock, the other being at Savoy.[^1] The register calls the Greylock site semi-blind, and Wikipedia says it had poor visibility.[^1][^2]

According to the register, the tower went into service in 1915 and was manned until 1923.[^1] Massachusetts, New York and Vermont each paid part of the cost of running the tower, and the town of Williamstown contributed $300 toward it.[^1] Part of that money was meant to let the public reach the tower by ladder.[^1] Wikipedia says Vermont, New York and Massachusetts helped pay to run the tower for about three years.[^2]

In late 1923, Massachusetts moved the tower to Brodie Mountain.[^2][^3] Wikipedia says the move followed New York's 1922 addition of two fire observation stations in Rensselaer County.[^2] Only the footings are left on Berlin Mountain.[^1][^2]

The tower structure went to Brodie Mountain in Hancock.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page records a 50-foot tower built there in 1967 and a new tubular steel tower erected in 2009.[^3] We found no source that says whether any of the original structure survives in the 2009 tower.

Brodie's tower had been closed since before World War II.[^3] A 1952 newspaper quoted on the Forest Lookouts page said a previously abandoned tower on the mountain would return to service.[^3] By April 1954 it was still out of service, and a right-of-way dispute was holding up the state's plan to reopen it.[^3]

The register places the site in Berkshire County, but Wikipedia says the mountain's summit lies on the New York side of the state line.[^1][^2]

We found no source that says whether the public may visit the footings or the Brodie tower. Check access before going, and do not climb a tower that is closed or on private land.
