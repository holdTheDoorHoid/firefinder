The Western Maryland Railroad built a wooden observatory on the summit of Mount Quirauk in 1883, as part of its Pen-Mar resort.[^4] A windstorm demolished it in 1885, and a 70-foot steel tower called Tip-Top Tower opened in June 1886.[^4] A 1921 newspaper report said the tower had gone up 25 years earlier, so the two accounts disagree on the date.[^1] Newspapers of the time called Quirauk the highest peak of the Blue Ridge north of North Carolina.[^1]

In 1921 the resort allowed Maryland and Pennsylvania to turn the tower into a joint fire lookout.[^4] The Bell Telephone Company was to string wires to connect it, and the two states agreed that costs would be shared equally.[^1]

Most reports gave the height as 80 feet, though a January 1922 report said 85.[^1] The lookout was a glass room 10 feet square at the top, and a cabin for the watchman stood at the foot.[^1]

The 1921 plan called for a watchman on duty day and night, with two men dividing the shifts during the fire seasons.[^1] In 1923 the forest guard, L.S. Brown of Waynesboro, found 13 fires, and the largest Maryland fire that season burned over 700 acres.[^4]

In November 1927 Clay Willard, Pennsylvania's state forester, was shot at the tower. The Evening Star reported his account that he had been attacked with his own gun while repairing a tire.[^1] He was reported to be in critical condition at Waynesboro Hospital.[^1]

The tower later carried radio equipment. In 1948 a Hagerstown station replaced the flagstaff on top with the region's first FM radio tower, and in 1964 a federal communications tower more than 200 feet tall went up beside it.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing but without its cab.[^2] Wikipedia says it still stands, mostly hidden by trees, and that it is used for telecommunications at "Site C," an outpost of a Defense Department emergency relocation site.[^3]

A fenced-in area of the mountaintop is federal property and counts as a restricted area.[^3] WillhiteWeb says permission is needed to hike up to the summit.[^4] Please do not climb the tower, and ask for permission before going near the summit.
