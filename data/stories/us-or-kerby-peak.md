A fire lookout was on Kerby Peak by 1916, when a telephone line was built to the summit and a cooperative station was set up with the federal forest service.[^3] Newspapers reported a state-built lookout house there by 1928.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com date an elevated L-4 cab on the summit to 1934,[^1][^2] so the cab may have replaced an earlier house. The sources do not say whether it did.

Newspaper items name a few of the men who manned the station.[^3] William Barr was there in 1922 and came home when the fire season closed in 1923.[^3] Ben Carlyle was named lookout for the 1928 season.[^3] In July 1921 the lookout spotted a small fire near Love Station and reported it.[^3] In 1931 lightning badly damaged the lookout house.[^3] In July 1938 a state fire warden fell 14 feet from a cliff near Kerby, was taken to the station overnight, and was carried out on a stretcher the next morning.[^3]

Ben Bleecha manned the station in 1957.[^3] A February 1958 item says he had held the job for two seasons and died in a Portland hospital while being treated for tuberculosis.[^3] A May 1958 item said the old lookout would be rebuilt, or possibly moved to Roundtop.[^3] The lookout burned in 1959, because the building was feared to be infected with tuberculosis.[^3][^4] The register says it was removed that year, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2]

An undated SummitPost climbing page says the lookout burned down in 1959 and that a few remnants of the structure are on the summit.[^4] The page describes a route from Selma on Highway 199 that ends at a trailhead with parking for at least five vehicles, and says no Northwest Forest Pass is needed.[^4] It also says the mountain is not accessible in winter and spring.[^4] No source I found describes the remnants in more detail.[^4]
