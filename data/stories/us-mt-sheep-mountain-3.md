Sheep Mountain rises about 10 miles northeast of Missoula, in the Lolo National Forest.[^1] The lookout there was an L-4 cabin, and firelookout.com dates it to 1933.[^1] Newspaper items point to different dates.[^1][^2] In April 1930 the Daily Missoulian reported that a new lookout would be built on the peak that summer, behind Jumbo, and a 1925 item already mentions a forest lookout station on Sheep Mountain east of town.[^2] The sources do not agree on when the cabin went up.[^1][^2]

The Missoulian named people at the lookout in 1934.[^2] In May, Carl Rooney was sent to the Sheep Mountain lookout.[^2] In July, Ralph Hogan was the lookout on the mountain.[^2]

Firelookout.com says the cabin was abandoned in the 1940s and was gone by 1962.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the Missoula County peak as gone.[^3] A 1966 newspaper item, though, says J. Penkake was assigned to the lookout north of Missoula, which does not fit a 1962 date.[^2] The sources do not settle which account is right.[^1][^2]

The name is shared with another peak.[^4] A Sheep Mountain about 15 miles southwest of Superior, in Mineral County, had a lookout built in 1928.[^4] That is a different site, and this story does not cover it.

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on Sheep Mountain today, or whether the public can reach the peak.[^1][^3]
