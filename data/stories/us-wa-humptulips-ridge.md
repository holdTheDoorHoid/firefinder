Willhite and the Forest Lookouts page both describe a portable ground house with a wood frame, measuring 10 by 10 feet. Willhite places it on a summit in Grays Harbor County, Washington.[^3][^4] The sources do not agree on what stood there or when.[^1][^2]

The register says a cabin was built on the site in 1942, and that a trailer was set up there in 1964.[^1] Firelookout.com instead describes an L-5 cab, dated with a question mark to the 1950s, that was moved two miles southeast to the end of the ridge in 1958. That spot is commonly called the Humptulips Auxiliary Lookout.[^2] Willhite adds that the Kresek inventory points to use starting in 1942 and removal no later than 1970.[^4]

The Forest Lookouts page gives no date for the building and lists it as removed.[^3]

All the sources that give a date agree that the structures were gone by 1970.[^1][^2][^4]

No source describes a standing structure at the site today. Willhite describes the summit now as a level, wooded spot where trees hide every view.[^4] His photos include part of a stovepipe.[^4] The ground is a detached DNR tract sitting inside national forest land.[^4] The Forest Lookouts page files the site under Olympic National Forest.[^3] We found no source that says whether the public may go onto the DNR land, so check with DNR before visiting.
