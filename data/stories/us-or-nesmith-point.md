Nesmith Point is the highest point on the lip of the Columbia River Gorge [^1]. A tent cabin went up on the summit in 1929, and an alidade was established there the same year [^2][^3]. The register says an L-4 cab replaced the tent in the 1930s [^2][^4]. An October 1934 newspaper notice reported a standard 14 x 14 lookout station on a 12-foot tower being completed there, one that looked directly down on Cascade Locks [^3].

In September 1934, CCC crew members from the Latourelle Prairie camp were helping bring new buildings toward completion [^3]. A road joining the Larch Mountain and Oak Grove roads had recently been finished, with a branch running out to the station [^3]. In May 1937 a CCC crew was working on the Nesmith Point Trail at Warrendale [^3]. That August, a planned line change was to route Nesmith Point and other stations through Eagle Creek and Tanner Butte instead of Indian Mountain [^3].

Sources do not agree on when the cab came down [^2][^3]. An Oregon lookout history says it was removed around 1959 [^3]. The register says it was gone by 1963, and firelookout.com also gives 1963 [^2][^4]. Wikipedia says the lookout was destroyed but gives no date [^1].

No source describes the summit in detail today. A hike guide from Friends of the Columbia Gorge says that partway up the climb an old road leads to what it calls an old fire lookout and an abandoned outhouse [^5]. It does not say whether that is the 1930s cab or something else, so it stays unconfirmed [^5]. A 1938 Coast and Geodetic Survey description named the survey station as the finial of the lookout house, and a 1948 recovery note said it was in good condition [^3]. No source says whether that mark survives.

Legal access is by trail only, because the one road into the peak runs through the closed Bull Run Watershed [^1]. Trail #428 climbs from an Interstate 84 frontage road [^1]. Friends lists the out-and-back hike at 10.2 miles with about 3,700 feet of gain, rates it expert, and promises great Gorge views from the top [^5].
