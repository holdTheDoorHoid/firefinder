The Idaho Department of Lands is listed as the agency for the lookout on Mica Peak, about six miles south of Post Falls.[^2] A 16-by-16-foot frame cabin went up there in 1952.[^2][^3][^4] A writer on one site thinks the old photos show a gable L-4 design.[^3] A January 1953 newspaper report of the state forester's two-year account listed new electrified lookout houses on Cottonwood and Mica peaks.[^5]

Lookouts on record include Mr. and Mrs. William Dierks in 1957 and 1961, and Norman and Joyce Clark in 1967.[^1][^2] The cabin was abandoned as a lookout in 1983.[^1][^2][^5]

Today the summit is a radio site with many communication structures.[^3][^4] Two listings say the old cabin now houses communication equipment.[^1][^2] Peakbagging says the cabin is no longer there,[^4] while Willhite says it is unclear when the cabin was demolished.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as gone.[^6]

The road is the real obstacle. The usual approach crosses private land and then timber-company land, and the last stretch belongs to an LLC that posts no-access signs.[^3] The summit land is retained by the State of Idaho.[^3] Another Mica Peak, across the state line in Washington, also had a fire lookout.[^3] Treat the Idaho summit as off limits unless you have permission.
