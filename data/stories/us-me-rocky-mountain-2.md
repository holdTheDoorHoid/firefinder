Rocky Mountain sits in the Maine North Woods, in Aroostook County.[^1] Landowners built the first lookout there in 1907, and a 1914 report called that log station not high enough for the territory.[^1][^2]

In 1917 Chief Warden Hosea Buck reported a wooden tower 20 feet high on the south bluff.[^2] A 1919 report, though, describes a log tower built in 1917 on another bluff, so the sources disagree about what went up that year.[^2]

The Maine Forest Service's steel tower went up in 1921. The register gives it as 54 feet with an 8-by-8-foot wooden cab. A 1920 inventory and a 1921 newspaper item give 48 feet instead.[^1][^2]

The register lists the lookout as registered on November 24, 1995, with Robert Spear of the FFLA as nominator.[^1]

On October 30, 2017, a windstorm brought the cab down.[^2][^3]

The FFLA's Maine list still shows the steel tower as standing, with an asterisk that the list defines as a tower whose cab is no longer in place.[^4] Reaching it takes a permit for the private timber roads that run from the Dickey checkpoint, and the summit itself is a trail-only walk.[^1]
