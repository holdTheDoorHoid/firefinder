Joanna Bald Lookout sits on Joanna Bald, a peak of about 4,716 feet in Graham County, North Carolina, within the Nantahala National Forest.[^1] One hiking guide places the lookout in the Snowbird Mountains of far western North Carolina.[^2]

The Register's description says the tower was built in 1952, as a replacement for a wooden lookout house with a cupola from the early 1930s.[^1] The Register's date box says 1942, so the Register disagrees with its own description.[^1] Two hiking guides also give 1952.[^2][^3] This story uses 1952.

The tower is 31 feet tall, with a 14-by-14-foot live-in cab and a catwalk.[^1][^3]

The U.S. Forest Service staffed the lookout first, and the North Carolina Forest Service staffed it until 2002.[^1] One hiking guide says the cab was staffed into the early 2000s.[^2]

The Register says the tower is now maintained for emergency use.[^1] Vehicles cannot reach the top.[^3] A locked gate on Forest Service Road 423B sits about half a mile below the summit, and the hike starts there.[^2][^3] The drive up FR 423 is steep and rough, and one guide advises a vehicle with clearance, ideally four-wheel drive.[^2]

Usually the upper cab stays locked, and the view from the summit and the stairs is restricted.[^3] Please respect the locked cab and do not try to force entry.
