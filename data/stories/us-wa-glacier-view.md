Glacier View is a high point on a long ridge that runs parallel to the western boundary of Mount Rainier National Park.[^1][^6] A fire lookout went up there about 1934.[^2] Its cab was an L-4, a design the National Historic Lookout Register describes as a 14-by-14-foot frame cab that could sit atop a tall tower.[^2][^3] Robert Cooper took panoramic photos there on July 23, 1934.[^4]

A Washington lookouts listing shows the lookout activated on July 4, 1942, and deactivated on October 16, 1943, with the Seattle Filter Center.[^4] The cab was removed in 1960.[^2] The Seattle Times gives the run as 1934 to 1960.[^5]

Today the former lookout site is on the summit of Glacier View, in the northwest corner of the Glacier View Wilderness.[^1][^6] Congress designated that wilderness in 1984.[^1]

From State Route 706, you look for Forest Road 59 a few miles past Ashford.[^6] The Seattle Times says the dirt road is rough, especially early on, though a regular passenger car can make it.[^5] WillhiteWeb says the road is closed because of washouts.[^6] The two accounts disagree, so check current road conditions with the Forest Service before you go.[^5][^6]

WillhiteWeb says Glacier View has the best viewpoint on its ridge, and that you can reach its unblocked view without bushwhacking.[^6]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains of the cab or its footings at the site today.
