Crossroads Fire Tower stands on Arkansas Highway 133 North in Ashley County, near the town of Hamburg.[^3][^1] It is a steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab,[^1][^4] and the handout calls it the tallest remaining tower in Arkansas.[^2]

The tower went up in 1935.[^1][^2][^3] The Register credits the Arkansas Forestry Commission with constructing it,[^1] while the handout says the Aermotor Company built it and Civilian Conservation Corps crews erected it that year.[^2] Wikipedia's infobox gives 1936, but its text and the other sources say 1935.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

As first built, it stood 100 feet tall.[^3] Sometime between 1936 and 1943 a 20-foot section was added so the cab could see over the trees, which also changed the tower's taper.[^2] The Register dates that addition to about 1940.[^1] The tower now stands 120 feet tall.[^2][^4]

Operators kept watch from the cab for decades.[^2] The handout lists John D. Tucker from 1935 to 1943, and Glendine Rauls from 1975 to 1985.[^2] The tower was decommissioned in 1985,[^1][^4] and the handout says Crossroads was phased out in favor of aerial fire detection.[^2]

The City of Hamburg bought the property in 2000.[^2] Work on the cab began on May 12, 2008.[^1] The handout credits the city's historic preservation program, Plum Creek Timber, Home Depot and the Forest Fire Lookout Association with making the restoration possible.[^2] A grand opening for the finished restoration was held on November 12 and 13, 2009.[^4]

The Register lists the tower as registered on June 29, 2005.[^1] Wikipedia says it was added to the National Register of Historic Places on March 2, 2006.[^3]

Today the City of Hamburg owns the tower.[^1] None of the sources consulted says whether it is open for climbing or how a visit would be arranged, and none mentions a rental. Anyone interested should check with the city before visiting, and should not climb it without permission.
