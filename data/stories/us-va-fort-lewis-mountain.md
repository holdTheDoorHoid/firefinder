Fort Lewis Mountain stretches from Ironto in Montgomery County to Masons Cove in Roanoke County, Virginia.[^2] It took its name from Fort Lewis, an early 19th-century fort just outside Salem.[^2]

The Virginia Department of Forestry administered the lookout, and the register dates it to 1935.[^1] Local Boy Scout hikes often stopped at the tower. One of the last locals to volunteer there, Buddy Altice, remembers Scouts coming up to learn the fire finder and staying overnight to learn camping.[^1]

A 1991 state survey said the Department of Forestry owned fire towers in every county, that regular aerial surveillance was gradually replacing towers for fire protection, and that the department planned to demolish about half of its existing towers.[^3] That survey describes the agency as a whole and does not name this tower.

The register says the tower was taken down in the mid-1990s, with a removal range of 1994 to 1995.[^1] It also says a cabin at the foot of the tower still remains.[^1]

Wikipedia lists a fire road as the easiest route up the mountain.[^2] No source we checked describes the tower site today, so we cannot say what remains at the summit or whether visitors can reach it.
