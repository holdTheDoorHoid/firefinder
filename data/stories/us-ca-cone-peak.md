Cone Peak Lookout stands in Monterey County, on the Los Padres National Forest, at an elevation of 5,155 feet.[^1][^5] The first cabin went up in 1923.[^1][^2] The first cab was 13 by 13 feet, wooden, and built as a ground house on a rock foundation, with a hipped roof the register calls a "dunce cap" and a design it calls the "California Lookout."[^1] Mules hauled the pieces up the two-mile, steep trail.[^1]

During World War II, its coastal position made it a station for the Aircraft Warning Service.[^1][^2] In 1959 the hipped roof was replaced with a flat one that could take a small helicopter.[^1][^2] The wooden sides were later replaced with metal siding, which the register says has protected the structure well from fires and vandalism.[^1] The last lookout known to have staffed it served until 1990.[^1]

By 2018 the cab held radio repeater equipment, and the register says it could not quickly be used as a lookout again.[^1] In 2020 the Dolan Fire burned over the peak. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California-South report that year says the structure survived undamaged, but it calls the structure an "all-metal tower." That does not match the register's description of a ground cab that was wooden and was later sheathed in metal.[^3][^1]

A 2023 report from the same chapter says the access road to the trailhead suffered major washout damage in December 2021. It adds that "Repairs to the road have begun, but will be extensive."[^4]

The Forest Service listing, last updated August 31, 2026, shows the site as closed after the Timber and Plaskett Fires and says it can be reached only on foot.[^5] The Monterey Ranger District lists an emergency closure order with exceptions, so check it before planning any trip.[^5]
