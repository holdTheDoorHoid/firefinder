Clock was a short-lived fire observation station on Long Island.[^1] The Long Island fire towers page says the station operated for part of a single year.[^1] Starr's 2011 list gives 1920 as its only year of operation and says the site never had a steel fire tower.[^2]

The Long Island page places the station on the former Wheatley Estate of Edwin D. Morgan III, at the end of Clock Tower Road in Old Westbury. It describes that identification as probable rather than certain.[^1] The page also describes a room above the clock with windows on every side. It places the station on high ground roughly eleven miles west of the Dix Hills, between Harbor Hill and High Hill.[^1]

The page says its picture comes from the Old Long Island website.[^1] That site's post describes a clock tower that sat opposite the front door of the Wheatly estate, which McKim, Mead & White designed between 1890 and 1900.[^3] The post does not mention an observation station, so the link between that tower and Clock rests on the Long Island page.[^1]

Starr's list files Clock under Suffolk and points the Oakdale entry to it.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list places it in Nassau, as a clock tower that is gone.[^4]

The Long Island page and Starr's list both record the station as removed, and Starr adds that it was abandoned.[^1][^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, is left on the estate today, or whether the grounds are open to visitors.[^1][^2][^4]
