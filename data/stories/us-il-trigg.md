Trigg Tower stands on a high hill in the Shawnee National Forest in southern Illinois, reached by Trigg Tower Road from the Simpson area.[^6][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as the only survivor of sixteen towers built in the forest between 1934 and 1939.[^1]

The first tower on the hill was a wooden one that Civilian Conservation Corps enrollees from Camp Simpson put up in 1934.[^1] The register gives it a height of 40 feet, and the Forest Service gives 60 feet for the same wooden structure.[^1][^4] In 1938 enrollees from Camp Eddyville replaced it with a steel tower that the register says originally stood 100 feet tall.[^1][^4] A description of the station from about 1942 gives 40 feet, which does not fit that steel tower.[^6][^1]

The register credits the CCC with supplying the first tower men who watched for fires.[^1] By 1967 airplanes had taken over most fire spotting, and the register says the other fifteen towers were abandoned and demolished.[^1]

In 1973 the upper fifty feet of the steel frame came off for safety reasons, and an observation platform was built at the top.[^1][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, with its cab no longer in place.[^2]

The tower has closed twice in recent years. A Forest Fire Lookout Association Illinois report dated 4 January 2021 says Trigg closed the previous October for protective fencing repairs, work it expected to run into 2021.[^3]

In May 2026 the Forest Service announced that, weather permitting, it would close the tower and its parking area from May 11 for repairs.[^5] The planned work includes replacing the fencing around the top of the observation platform, replacing deck boards and stair treads, and sandblasting and repainting the tower.[^4][^5] The work was planned to take about a month, and no more than 90 days.[^4]

The Forest Service page I read still shows the site as temporarily closed, so check with the Forest Service before you go.[^4] When it reopens, visitors can park in a small lot at the base and go up to the observation platform, which offers views of the Shawnee Hills.[^4][^5] Stay out of any closed area marked with signs while the work goes on.[^5]
