The Georgia Forestry Commission put up the Miller County Fire Tower in 1956, on a forestry station in the town of Colquitt, in the southwest corner of the state.[^1] It is a 99-foot International Stacy steel tower with a metal cab about seven feet square.[^1][^2] The Register points to the catwalk as what sets it apart.[^1] A Forest Lookouts listing gives the same height and cab size but spells the maker's name "International-Stacey."[^2]

The tower was staffed until the late 1980s.[^1] The Register says it is now kept for emergency detection and radio work for the forestry station on the same site.[^1] The sources read do not name any fire the tower helped with, so its record of fires cannot be told here.[^1][^2]

The Register describes the tower in the present tense and calls it maintained, but its page gives no date for that.[^1] Nothing in the sources read says it has been moved or taken down.[^1][^2] The Register added it on March 15, 2006, under the numbers US 660 and GA 4.[^1]

The sources read do not describe public visits.[^1][^2] The tower stands on the grounds of a forestry station, so anyone curious should contact the Georgia Forestry Commission before going there, and should not climb it without permission.[^1]
