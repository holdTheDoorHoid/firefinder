Yamsay Mountain sits in Klamath County, Oregon, in the Winema National Forest.[^2] The Klamath Indian Agency's forestry department built a truck trail to the summit in 1928.[^1] In 1929 the Indian Service was building a steel lookout tower there.[^1] By August that year a steel Aermotor tower 80 feet tall, capped by a 7-by-7 steel cab, stood on the summit, and a 12-by-16 ground cabin served as living quarters.[^1] The register calls that living space a tent cabin, so the sources differ on that detail.[^2]

In 1955 a newspaper reported that Claudette Parazoo would be the lookout that summer for the Indian Service.[^1]

By 1961 heavy winter ice had buckled the steel tower.[^1] The Winema National Forest announced a $9,000 treated-timber tower that would stand 20 feet tall and have living quarters at the top.[^1] The register says the new tower replaced the steel one that year.[^2] Firelookout.com gives the same sequence in one line.[^3] A climbing guide that cites a book on Northwest lookouts says the steel tower was dismantled in 1961.[^5]

In 1968 the lookout reported smoke in an area where a fire had already been put out.[^1] The burning stump was not classed as a reportable fire, since it had not spread beyond the spot where it began.[^1]

In 1973 the forest said the lookout would not be manned for the whole fire season and would be used only at peak fire danger.[^1] The register says the timber tower was used for emergencies that year and is now gone.[^2]

A PeakBagging.com page, with a July 4, 2017 picture, says what remains is all that is left of the lookout. The page says the lookout came down some time after 1974.[^4] The climbing guide describes only concrete abutments and a great many nails on the summit.[^5]

I found no closure notice. The site lies in the Winema National Forest, and the climbing guide gives driving directions from U.S. 97 and Oregon 76 near Silver Lake.[^4][^5]
