Trout Mountain, in Piscataquis County beside Baxter State Park, once carried a Maine fire lookout on its top.[^1][^2] A Maine Forest Service panorama dated 1931 is centered on the Trout Mt. fire tower, and a Maine Memory caption says the lookout station moved to Trout Mountain that year.[^3][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the Black Cat Mountain tower was removed and relocated to Trout Mountain in 1931.[^5]

The old tower was closed in the 1970s, and the galvanizers' account says the closure came from safety concerns.[^6][^7] A 2021 Portland Press Herald article says the old, rusted tower had toppled more than four decades earlier and lay on its side in the spruce and fir.[^8] The Conservancy says the old tower is now ruined on the forest floor, and a hiking guide says the ruins can still be seen just beyond the newer tower.[^2][^9]

The Maine chapter of The Nature Conservancy hired David Vana of Davana LLC to build an 80-foot observation tower on the summit.[^10] It was erected in 2020.[^2] Vana used the middle sections of a 120-foot tower he had salvaged in Alabama, which was in use until 2017, and he added newly galvanized steel to finish the Maine installation.[^6] The tower has six flights of stairs, and the Piscataquis Observer describes a 2.7-mile hike to reach it.[^7] The Conservancy says a 2.7-mile trail rewards hikers with 360-degree views, and that climbers go up at their own risk.[^2]

The sources disagree on the summit's height: 1,409 feet in the Conservancy's account and 1,427 feet in the galvanizers'.[^2][^6] After the tower went up, people complained that it glinted in the sunlight and marred a well-known view of Katahdin.[^10]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the original tower as gone. It also lists a separate standing tower at nearly the same coordinates, labeled "Relocated from Alabama."[^1]
