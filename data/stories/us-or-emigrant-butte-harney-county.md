Emigrant Butte rises in the Ochoco National Forest about 24 miles northwest of Burns, at 5,390 feet.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^1]

A house with a cupola went up on the butte in 1921, according to the register and firelookout.com.[^1][^2] A November 1921 item from the Central Oregonian, quoted on Rex's page, says Ranger E.W. Donnelly built a lookout house there.[^3] A September 1922 item in the same paper describes a new box-type house, 12 by 12 feet, with a 7-by-7 cupola that held the fire finder.[^3] It does not say whether that house replaced the 1921 one.[^3]

Staffing is recorded for only a few seasons.[^3] Thomas Ewing of Brothers ran the station in the summer of 1922.[^3] In 1929, Al Frey was the lookout until he took a winter job.[^3] Lester Williams covered the rest of the season, and Jack Choate came over from Dry Mountain once Williams had departed on September 21.[^3]

The register says a 20-foot L-4 tower replaced the house in 1933, and firelookout.com dates a 20-foot L-4 tower to 1933.[^1][^2] Rex's page quotes an October 1933 Central Oregonian item saying W.H. Lohuis was building a tower and a house at the butte, among several NRA projects.[^3] That item describes both, so it does not clearly match the register's account of a replacement.[^1][^3]

In 1939 the Forest Service asked for approval to add one building at the lookout that combined a garage, a woodshed and storage space.[^3] Rex's page does not say whether it was finished.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com both say the tower was gone by the 1940s, but neither says how it came down.[^1][^2] Rex's page ends the entry with a bare "Removed" and no date.[^3]

We found no source that describes what remains on the butte now. The register has a photo captioned as the site in 2008, but its text does not describe what is standing there.[^1] Access is not stated in the sources we read. Check with the Ochoco National Forest before planning a visit.
