In July 1926 the state forester, Col. Page S. Bunker, announced an 80-foot steel forest-fire observation tower to be built near Hacoda, in eastern Covington County.[^1] The state planned it as the first of four such towers in heavily timbered parts of Alabama.[^1]

By August the site had been named as Panther Creek Hill, on the Florala-Kinston highway, and piers had been set.[^1] The July report had put the tower near Hacoda, and the two reports do not say whether they describe the same site; this account follows the later, more specific one.[^1] Public-spirited landowners donated 40 acres for the site to the state commission of forestry.[^1] The tower was also to connect to neighboring telephone lines, so an observer could more easily communicate with landowners about fires found on their holdings.[^1]

A September report, credited to the Andalusia Star-News, said construction had started.[^1] The plan called for forest rangers to keep a daily lookout through the fire season.[^1]

No source describes the Panther Creek Hill site today, so what remains there, and whether the land is open to visitors, is not known from these sources.[^1]

[^1]: Lawrence - Forest Lookouts, Forest Lookouts (reprints 1926 press reports), https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/lawrence.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
