A June 1957 Rome News-Tribune article, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page, tells most of the story of the Hermitage lookout in Floyd County.[^1] The 100-foot wooden tower had stood guard over the county's forestland since 1938 and served the Georgia Forestry Commission.[^1] From its top it looked out over Hermitage, a community the article describes as having once prospered.[^1] The Georgia list places the site at 34.33926 north and 85.04717 west.[^2]

Joseph C. Lowery manned the wooden tower for its 19 years.[^1] He started at 64 and climbed its 120 spiraling steps three times a day.[^1] The article puts his total climbs at an estimated 19,710 by the time he retired last year at 82, which places his retirement in 1956.[^1] The same article says he asked to stay on long enough to serve in the new tower, and that his wish was granted.[^1] It reports both points without reconciling them.[^1]

By June 1957 a permanent steel structure had replaced the wooden tower.[^1] For more than a year the old tower had been the only wooden lookout left in the state, and officials said that status, not instability, was the reason for replacing it.[^1] The commission expected age to claim its sturdy beams in time, and officials felt its role in state fire protection was too vital to risk.[^1] Pulling it from its foundation took two bulldozers almost half a day.[^1]

In 1957 the steel tower was manned by Charles L. Evans, who was 21.[^1]

The 100-foot height belongs to the wooden tower.[^1] The Forest Lookouts record for the site ends with the word "Removed," and no source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today or whether it is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
