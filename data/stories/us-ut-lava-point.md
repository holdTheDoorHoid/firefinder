Lava Point is found in Zion National Park's northwest corner.[^2] The first lookout there is hard to date. One source says it was possibly built in 1957.[^2]

The original building burned, and the sources disagree on when.[^1][^2] One says a lightning strike destroyed it in 1957.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the fire to about 1963.[^1] A cabin of local design, about 10 by 10 feet, took its place.[^1][^2] The register says it went up soon after the fire.[^1]

WillhiteWeb says Zion's boundaries were enlarged in 1959, which freed up land for a road to the lookout.[^2] A 1959 Salt Lake Tribune report, quoted on another lookout site, says the Senate had approved a bill that included land for that road.[^4]

The structure came down in 1999.[^1][^2] The Park Service later turned the area into a viewpoint.[^5]

Today the site is a well-used observation point that the National Park Service maintains.[^2] Park fire managers still use the site to spot fires after storms, and the register describes it as a favorite stop for visitors.[^1] Visitors can usually reach the overlook from May to September, weather permitting, and it sits off Kolob Terrace Road.[^3] The Park Service lists parking, a picnic table and a scenic viewpoint.[^3] The last 1.5 miles of road are unpaved and may be unusable in wet weather.[^2]

No source describes what remains of the cabin at the site now.
