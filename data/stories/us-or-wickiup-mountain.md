A tower went up on Wickiup Mountain in 1940, on the ridgetop about a mile and a half south of the summit, in Clatsop County, roughly 13 miles southeast of Astoria.[^1][^2] A cab of the L-4 type was added in 1951.[^1] In 1955 a pole tower replaced the tower and cab on the same ridge.[^1]

The two sources disagree on how tall that 1955 tower was. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 40-foot pole tower, while firelookout.com lists a 50-foot wooden tower of the state forestry agency.[^1][^2] The Oregon Department of Forestry administered the site.[^1]

A 1952 photo caption says Fire Warden Richard Spray and his crew gave the lookout a fresh coat of paint that year, and that the woman on the right of the photo was the lookout.[^1] From the 1960s on, the tower was used only in emergencies.[^1]

The tower is now gone.[^1][^2] Keith Argow, Ph.D., CF, writes that the logger who owned Camp 18 wanted the tower for a museum, and that a helicopter company had agreed to airlift it.[^1] Around that time, Argow adds, the tower was taken apart and burned.[^1]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the ridge today.
