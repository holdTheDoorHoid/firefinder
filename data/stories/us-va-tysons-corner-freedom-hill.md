The Virginia Forest Fire Lookout Association lists "Tysons Corner (Freedom Hill)" in Fairfax County under its "Unconfirmed Sites" heading, with the type and status both marked unknown.[^1] The sources we found do not tie a fire lookout to that name. They describe a Civil War earthwork and a military tower instead.[^2][^3][^4]

Freedom Hill sits in Fairfax County, between Vienna and Tysons.[^3] Late in the Civil War, a redoubt went up on the hill to guard Washington.[^3] The Park Authority dates the work to 1864 to 1865, while NovaHistory gives January 1865.[^2][^3] A company of the 5th Pennsylvania Heavy Artillery manned its battery.[^3]

The Park Authority says the redoubt is preserved in Freedom Hill Park.[^2]

In 1952 a microwave tower was built in Tysons Corner.[^4] Wikipedia describes it as part of a continuity-of-government plan, and says the U.S. Army administers it from Fort Belvoir.[^4] It was built to serve as the major relay between Washington and backup communication sites on the west side of the Blue Ridge Mountains.[^4] Its present uses are classified.[^4]

Today the sources describe a preserved earthwork in Freedom Hill Park and a tower whose present uses are classified. They describe no fire lookout at either place.[^2][^4]
