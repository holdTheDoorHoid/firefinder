Bailey Mountain was a lookout site inside the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness, about nine miles north-northeast of Moose Creek Ranger Station.[^4]

The first lookout was a log cabin with a cupola, built in 1927.[^1][^4] A 1955 Forest Service condition report, quoted by one history, thought it was probably built around 1926.[^3] That report found the logs on two walls deteriorating fast and recommended a tower of 20 to 30 feet topped by a cab.[^3]

The site was abandoned around 1936, then brought back as a main lookout in 1948.[^3] In 1957 the Nez Perce National Forest took over running the site from the Bitterroot.[^3] In 1959 a new R-6 cab, set on a 10-foot timber tower, went up beside the log cabin and took over from it.[^3][^4]

Most accounts give the new tower as 10 feet tall, though a Forest Service table lists 12 feet.[^1][^4][^5] A 1965 U.S. Geological Survey station description says the roof is flat and 20 feet high.[^3]

The staff lists name one observer, Luther Britton, in 1956.[^1][^2]

A Forest Service post says the old log cabin was removed once the new lookout was finished, while a Forest Service table dates its removal to the 1970s.[^4][^5] The R-6 cab was in use until 1989, when staffing ended and it was taken down.[^4][^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 1980 for the loss instead.[^2]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as hike-in and destroyed or gone.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today.
