The register puts the Bull Mountain site at 3,193 feet (973 meters) in Patrick County, Virginia.[^1] The site's first tower, made of wood, went up in 1943 for the Virginia Forest Service.[^1][^3]

A 60-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab took its place in 1949.[^1][^2] The site also gained a one-room residence cabin at ground level in 1950.[^1]

In 1989, when it was an emergency lookout, the site was dedicated to Governor Gerald L. Baliles. He grew up nearby and visited the tower often as a boy.[^1][^3]

The sources I read say nothing about who staffed the tower or which fires it watched.

The tower still stands, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^4] The register names the Virginia Department of Forestry as its administrator.[^1][^2]

Public access is the open question. The register says nothing about visiting, and I found no current guidance on whether people can reach or enter the tower. A 1964 survey note describes going up to the top through a pipe gate on the fire road, but it does not say whether that gate is open today.[^2] Ask the Virginia Department of Forestry before you go, and do not climb the tower without permission.
