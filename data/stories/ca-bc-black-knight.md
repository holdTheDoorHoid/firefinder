The Canada Lookouts index lists a Black Knight lookout at 1234 m (4,049 ft), with coordinates of 49.878 N, 119.308 W.[^1] It files the site under Kootenay-Boundary.[^1]

A history of Black Mountain, Kelowna, says that in the early 1890s five settler families came from eastern Washington and settled on land around what they called Black Knight Mountain.[^2] The same history says a forest service road reached the mountain's 1,286 m (4,219 ft) summit in 1953, and that a fire lookout station came a year later, in 1954.[^2]

The two sources do not line up neatly. The index puts its Black Knight site at 1234 m under Kootenay-Boundary, while the history places the mountain at Kelowna. Neither source says that the 1954 station and the index's Black Knight lookout are one and the same.[^1][^2]

In 2014 the regional district, Westbank First Nation and the province together created Black Mountain/Sntsk'il'nten Regional Park.[^2]

No source I could read describes what remains at the lookout site today, or whether visitors may go there.
