Victory's fire tower went up in 1925 in Venango County, on Mrs. Laura Taylor's farm.[^1][^2] A September 1925 newspaper item says the observation tower, a half mile from Springville and eight miles from Franklin, was nearly finished.[^2] The tower was galvanized steel and 65 feet high, and it stood in woodland.[^2] The register calls it a steel Aermotor tower built that year.[^1][^2]

On April 12, 1926, Byron Blair, who was manning the new tower, found the first forest fire of that season in Venango County.[^2] Pennsylvania Railroad crews had the blaze under control that day, after about twenty acres had burned.[^2]

The register gives 1959 as the year the tower was removed.[^1] A 1935 Coast and Geodetic Survey description places a reference mark directly beneath the state forest fire tower.[^2] The survey station, on Pecan Hill, lies about 37 feet from that mark and roughly eight road miles southwest of Franklin, along State Highway 8.[^2]

No source we read describes what remains on the hill today, and none says whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
