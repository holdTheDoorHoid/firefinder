In 1934 the Forest Service had poured foundations for new fire towers in the southern end of the Hiawatha National Forest.[^1] A newspaper item dated 1 September 1934, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, named the site near the Alger County airport, where the tower was to be known as the Wetmore tower and to stand one hundred feet high.[^1] The same item said no cabin would be built on it.[^1]

A survey note from 1955 describes the finished structure: a four-leg steel frame painted orange, with an enclosed observing room at the top and an overall height of 107 feet (32.6 m).[^1] It places the tower one mile south of Wetmore on Federal Forest Highway 13, and says the U.S. Forest Service owned and operated it.[^1] Earlier, the newspaper had put it near the Alger County airport, and the sources do not say whether the two sites are the same.[^1] The 1934 item said no cabin would be built, yet the 1955 note describes an enclosed room at the top. The sources do not say whether those two descriptions mean the same thing.[^1]

None of the sources we read describes the tower or its site after 1955.[^1] We cannot say whether it still stands or whether the public may climb it, so check with the Forest Service before going.[^1]

[^1]: Wetmore - FOREST LOOKOUTS, Forest Lookouts, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/wetmore.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
