Aeneas Mountain, in Okanogan County, Washington, was first established as a fire camp in 1914.[^1][^2] Some sources call the site the Lemanasky Lookout.[^6] According to the Register, the Forest Service built the first lookout here, a single-room log cabin, in 1920.[^1] TrailChick says the cabin stood until 1956.[^2]

In 1934 the Civilian Conservation Corps put an L-4 cab atop a 20-foot timber tower.[^2][^3] The Forest Service sold the tower to the Department of Natural Resources in 1954.[^2] According to the Register, the Washington Dept. of Natural Resources put up the present tower in 1980.[^1] The Register gives its height as 40 feet, while firelookout.com gives 42 feet.[^1][^4]

Bill Knosher was named to the station in 1922.[^3] Ray Kresek staffed the tower in 1955.[^2] TrailChick counts 104 summers of continuous staffing, from 1916 through 2020.[^2] The Register records 2020 as the final staffed year.[^1]

The state removed the lookout in late 2024.[^2][^4][^5] TrailChick says it came down in November, and firelookout.com says October.[^2][^4] The Washington chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association reported that the state chose to replace the lookout with a camera tower, and that it toppled the structure with a bulldozer.[^5]

The sources do not describe what, if anything, remains on the summit, so this story does not describe any remains or view. The Register lists the site under the Washington Dept. of Natural Resources - Northeast Division.[^1] TrailChick says many people have run into trouble over road access and private landowners.[^2] WillhiteWeb says the access road passes through several private properties, and DNR holds an easement.[^6] TrailChick describes a second route that stays entirely on public land. It follows a road for about 5.5 miles to the north side of the summit, then runs along ridge game trails.[^2]
