Firelookout.com lists the Nisqually Lookout as a U.S. Army tower in Pierce County, Washington.[^1] It places the tower four miles northwest of Roy, at 355 feet, and dates it to 1942.[^1] The Washington Lookouts index also gives the U.S. Army as owner and marks the tower removed.[^2]

WillhiteWeb is not sure it ever existed.[^4] Its author found no evidence of a lookout near the place firelookout.com gives, and no staff who knew of one.[^3] In late summer 2013, while working for the Army as a forestry technician and wildland firefighter, the author had access to these spots.[^4] The author would not be surprised if a lookout had stood on the 10th Field Artillery Bluff, which overlooks the Ninety-first Division Prairie, but could not confirm one.[^3] Of the six possible Fort Lewis lookout sites, the author found five and remained uncertain about this one.[^4]

Firelookout.com marks the tower abandoned.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as gone.[^5]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. The page places the possible site on Fort Lewis land, now known as Joint Base Lewis-McChord, so a visitor cannot assume public access. Ask the base for permission before going.[^4]
