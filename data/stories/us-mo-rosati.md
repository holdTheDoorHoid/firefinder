The Central US Lookouts guide lists a 60-foot (about 18.3 m) steel tower at the Rosati site under 1941, the earliest date it gives.[^1] The site is the Rosati towersite near St. James in Phelps County, and the guide names the Missouri Conservation Commission as its agency.[^1]

Staffing appears in the record in 1957. The guide quotes a Daily Standard item of October 26, 1957, reporting that forest towerman Eugene Farrow, Sr. was leaving Rosati for the Lennox tower with a promotion, and that Justein E. McMillen would take over at Rosati.[^1] Upkeep also appears in the department's annual reports: major repairs to the residence in fiscal 1956–57 and again in 1967–68, a bathroom and roof repaired in 1965–66, and overhead garage doors hung at the towersite in 1969–70.[^1]

A 1973 item from the Neosho Daily News, quoted by the guide, says the department once ran a successful chestnut planting at the towersite, and that its foresters gathered seed and shipped it to other parts of the nation.[^1]

In October 2019 the Phelps County Focus reported that only a few towers in the county were still standing, and that the Rosati tower site was listed as climbable. The article drew on fire tower enthusiast Bob Frakes, who was then finishing a book on Missouri's lookouts.[^2] That is the most recent account I could find of its condition, so check with the Missouri Department of Conservation before climbing anything there.

The department's site map of the Rosati Towersite gives its area as 92 acres, and its legend includes symbols for a fire tower and a gate.[^3] The map does not say whether the public may enter the site.
