James Ridge Lookout stands on James Ridge in the Lincoln National Forest, in Otero County, New Mexico.[^1] The New Mexico chapter's 2020 year-end report calls it the lookout for the area around the resort village of Cloudcroft.[^2]

The tower did not start on James Ridge.[^3] The Forest Service says the Mayhill Lookout was constructed in 1935 as part of a Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Mayhill, and that its cab sat on a 48-foot tower.[^3]

It was moved in 1967 and set up again on James Ridge.[^4][^5] The 2020 report says the tower was remodeled during the move, and that it gained a partial catwalk and metal stair treads.[^2]

The register and the Forest Service both describe the present tower as 62 feet high, with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^4] The Forest Service says the lookout has been remodeled extensively since it left Mayhill.[^4] The 48-foot Mayhill figure and the 62-foot James Ridge figure come from different pages, and the sources do not explain the gap.[^3][^4]

The register recorded the tower on August 30, 2015.[^1] The 2020 report says the 1967 move is why it missed the 1988 thematic listing of lookouts. A nomination adding it to that listing was under way in 2020.[^2] The Forest Service says its future eligibility will be based on the 1967 move.[^4]

The Forest Service lists the site as open.[^4] Its page says potable water is unavailable there and gives no restroom details.[^4] A volunteer mans the lookout each year, according to the 2020 report.[^2]

The 2020 report marks the lookouts it proposed for the cabin rental program with a C. James Ridge does not carry that mark.[^2]

Before you drive up, check current forest alerts. The Forest Service lists a temporary closure at Bluff Springs on the Sacramento Ranger District.[^4] None of the sources I read say whether visitors may climb the tower.
