Squally Jim is a lookout site on Walville Peak in the Willapa Hills of Pacific County, Washington, at 2,419 feet.[^1][^2] Newspapers from 1927 and 1929 called it the Walville lookout, and a 1929 item says Oral Weese of Pe Ell was in charge of it.[^2][^3]

Sources disagree about the first structure. A 1941 report of the Washington Forest Fire Association describes a semi-permanent building, 10 feet square and 16 feet high, on a hilltop reached by a new road.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the site was first used in 1942 with an 83-foot wooden tower and a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] The Willhite trip report calls that taller tower possible but does not confirm it.[^2]

In March 1942 the site was activated as an Air Warning Service station.[^3] The service added a 12-by-20-foot garage, and the state kept the site when the service ended.[^3] A 40-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot live-in cab went up in 1955.[^2][^3] The register dates a 40-foot live-in to 1954.[^1]

In September 1951 a fire broke out near the lookout station.[^3] A newspaper reported crews' estimate of about 1,000 acres.[^3] The trip report says a piece of burning paper blew out of the stove pipe and started a fire that burned 1,800 acres.[^2]

In January 1966 Mrs. Roy Thompson of Lebam told a group about her time as a watchman at the station, saying she had kept watch for five years.[^3]

The Columbus Day storm destroyed the lookout on October 12, 1962.[^1][^3] Sources differ on the replacement. The register says an identical tower went up the same year, while a state report quoted on Forest Lookouts gives 1962 to 64.[^1][^3] The trip report's account of the replacement timing is unclear.[^2] The state removed the tower in 1968.[^1][^3] The trip report and firelookout.com say the state destroyed it.[^2][^4]

The trip report does not say when the writer visited. It says roughly half the site was logged in 2015, which left open views to the north.[^2] The building site itself was not disturbed, though excavators came close to it, and the writer found one anchor or footing bolt.[^2] A new road reaches the summit.[^2] The trip report calls the land state land and notes that the entrance gate is often left open, most of all during hunting season.[^2] We found no official access rule, so check before you go.
