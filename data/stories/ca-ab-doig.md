The earliest public record I could find for the Doig lookout is Alberta's 2009 map of its lookout towers, which marks a site called DOIG[^1].

The community of Doig River lies in Clear Hills County, and Wikipedia says it was named after the Doig River[^2].

Alberta's history of its boreal lookouts explains that the towers stood on hilltops, lifting the observer above whatever blocked the view[^3]. From the 1920s, steel came in and gradually replaced wood as the material used to build towers[^3].

The province counts 127 wildfire lookout sites[^4]. Each site has an observer on duty, watching for fires through the day, usually from April to October[^4].

According to a 2016 Alberta Wildfire blog post[^5], lookout observers called in more than a quarter of the wildfires in the Peace River District in 2015.

Alberta's bear-safety guidance describes its wildfire lookout sites as isolated[^4]. Priority sites have permanent electric fencing around the residences, towers, outhouses and generator buildings[^4]. The fence posts carry high-visibility paint, a year-round warning to backcountry visitors[^4].

No public source I found describes what stands at the Doig site today, or whether the public may visit it.
