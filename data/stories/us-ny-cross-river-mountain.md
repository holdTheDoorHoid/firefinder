Westchester County's Park Commission chose the top of Stony Ridge, inside the Ward Pound Ridge Reservation, for a fire observation tower in 1926.[^1][^2] Later newspaper notices called the same summit Stone Hill and Stone Mountain.[^2] The steel was the standard type the State Conservation Commission used, and it had already reached the site when the plan was announced.[^2] The notices did not agree on the height, giving 60 feet in one and 69 feet in another.[^2] They also gave the summit about 860 feet up, while the register's text says the tower stood 960 feet above sea level.[^2][^1]

The 1926 plan called for an observer to be stationed from November 1 until the first real snowfall, with a telephone line to park headquarters.[^2] By 1929 the observer was watching all day from October.[^2] The county ran the lookout at first, and the register says the NYS Conservation Department took over in the 1930s.[^1] A 1932 notice said the state would keep operating the tower, and that nine fires had broken out in the region in 1931, all put out before serious damage.[^2] The register also reports that about 500 fires were reported from the tower between 1966 and 1968, based on a 1975 county report.[^1]

The DEC decommissioned the tower in 1971.[^1] The register gives two different removal years. One line says it was taken down in 1980, and the description says it came down without county approval around 1982.[^1] The exact year is unsettled.[^1]

Today the summit is being rebuilt.[^3] A 501(c)(3) called the Friends of the Fire Tower has been raising money for it through races and donations.[^3] A 2023 association newsletter reported that the county's Board of Supervisors had approved funding to return a tower to the summit.[^4] By mid-August 2026 the second section had been lifted onto the first, making a structure about 40 feet high on foundations anchored in November 2025.[^3] On September 6 the third section was still being assembled.[^3] The project says the tower will not open until more ground-level work, including a security fence, is finished.[^3]

The register says the original stood at the highest point of Cross River Mountain, with a 360-degree view and visibility of 50 to 100 miles, depending on the weather.[^1] No source read for this story describes what the rebuilt tower will show. OpenStreetMap marks the old site as a "Site of Former Fire Tower".[^5] The tower is not open yet.[^3] Visitors should not try to climb the site while work continues.
