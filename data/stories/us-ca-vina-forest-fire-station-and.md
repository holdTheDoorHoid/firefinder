Vina is a community in Tehama County, along Highway 99 between Chico and Red Bluff.[^1][^2][^3] The base sits on a small, grassy hill just east of the highway.[^1]

The original station and lookout, which the register dates to about 1954, were destroyed by lightning in 1961.[^1] The replacement went up that year and was virtually identical to the original.[^1] A 2018 Chico newspaper report gives 1955 as the date of the older base, so the two sources differ by a year.[^2]

The register describes a cupola that still held a firefinder, and says a barracks and mess hall were added later in the 1970s.[^1] It also notes that fire engines were no longer housed at the facility.[^1] The base has been operational as a helitack base since 1974, and the Chico paper says it was updated for helicopters that year.[^2][^3]

In 2018 Cal Fire tore down the old buildings and demolished the station to make way for a new helitack base.[^1][^2] Cal Fire expected the project to cost $15.26 million, and in March moved the helicopter to Red Bluff Airport so the work could go ahead.[^2] The rebuilt base includes a barracks for 22 people, and the 2018 plan also listed a rescue training tower, a radio tower and a weather station.[^2][^3]

The county now describes the base as running 24 hours a day, every day of the year, with a night-vision goggle program.[^3] In the summer of 2020 Vina was the first in California to take delivery of a new Black Hawk.[^3] The base responds in initial attack to wildland fires in Shasta, Tehama and Butte counties.[^3]

No source describes public access to the grounds today. The register's note that the site is open to visitors carries a 1991 date, so it describes the old station, not the base as it stands now.[^1] No source describes any part of the old station still standing.[^1]
