Crescent Lake South is a lookout tower in the Crescent Lake National Wildlife Refuge, in Garden County, Nebraska. The refuge was set aside in 1931 to protect its wetlands for migrating birds and other wildlife.[^5] The tower's register page quotes the Fish and Wildlife Service saying the refuge has two towers of this kind.[^1]

The register's account of the tower's construction rests on a January 31, 1937 newspaper item from the Nebraska State Journal, which it quotes.[^1] The item says that work at the Garden county refuge began under the WPA in 1936, with relief laborers improving the site, and that crews built a 100-foot steel lookout tower, plus a service building and a cellar for seed storage.[^1][^2] The same page also quotes the Fish and Wildlife Service as placing both refuge towers in the early 1940s, built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] The two dates do not agree, and the newspaper does not say which tower it means. The sources we reached do not settle the question.

Nothing we found names who staffed the south tower or records a fire it watched, so its working life is largely undocumented.

The Fish and Wildlife Service, as quoted on the register page, says the towers have stopped being used to spot fires and now serve wildlife.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the south tower as standing.[^3]

The register added the tower on August 9, 2026, as US 1845, NE 3, and gives the site's elevation as 3,974 feet (1,211 metres). It lists the Fish and Wildlife Service as administrator.[^1] The refuge says thousands of people visit each year for recreation, but its visitor page also warns that a recent wildfire and extreme dryness could leave refuge roads and trails impassable.[^4] No source we reached says whether the tower itself can be climbed, so check with the refuge before assuming it is open, and stay off any tower that is closed.
