In March 1955 the board of the Klamath Forest Protective Association allowed the Aircraft Warning Service, also called the Ground Observer Corps, to build a 10-by-10 observation tower on the association's grounds. The permission came with a condition: if the tower were ever abandoned, the association would own it.[^1]

An August 1955 Herald and News item reported the lookout nearly finished. It stood next to the association headquarters on Highway 66, near Klamath Falls, and was being built through volunteer work and contributions.[^1]

The structure was used for aircraft observation before radar became effective.[^2] On January 1, 1959, it was turned over to the association.[^1] A Klamath-Lake District Oregon Department of Forestry report for 1958 says the building could then serve as a training tower for new lookouts.[^1]

Both lookout records list the structure as gone or removed, and neither gives a date.[^1][^2] The two records also disagree on its form. The Forest Lookouts page calls it a tower, while the Klamath County list describes a two-story enclosed house.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the headquarters site today.
