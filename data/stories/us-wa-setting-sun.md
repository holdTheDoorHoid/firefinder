Setting Sun Mountain lies in Okanogan County, eight miles north-northwest of Mazama, within the Okanogan National Forest.[^2][^1] The register gives 1932 as the year the L-5 cab was built.[^1] WillhiteWeb reports that the cab went up in 1933, and it cites two Wenatchee Daily World articles.[^3] One said Roma Johnson had blazed a trail for an emergency lookout on the mountain.[^3] The other said Tracey Heath was nearing completion of a new Forest Service lookout station on the peak.[^3] The sources disagree on the year, so both dates are given here.[^1][^3]

The Goat Peak lookout, five miles south, had no view into the Lost River Valley.[^3] When lightning struck in that area, its watchman would hike the connecting ridge to the unoccupied Setting Sun cab and check for fires before walking back.[^3]

The register says the L-5 cab was removed in 1953.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page and WillhiteWeb say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^3]

Willhite visited in 2022 and found a stovepipe and roof cap, an anchor set in the rock, part of an outhouse, and stacked rocks around the site.[^3] He writes that the phone line running east toward Sweetgrass Butte was never removed, and that some insulators can still be found along the upper ridge.[^3] His older photos were taken before a fire killed most of the trees on that ridge.[^3] The hike is two miles one way, with about 1,700 feet of climb, over terrain he calls open and easy to navigate.[^3]
