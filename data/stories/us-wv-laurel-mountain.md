A 1913–14 biennial report from the state's forest, game and fish warden says that L.L. Bennett agreed to supply a watchman at his own expense for three years at Laurel Hill Station.[^1] No source read for this story says when the first structure went up or who built it.

A 1916 description puts the station on the line between Barbour and Randolph Counties, seven miles from Elkins, and gives its elevation as 3,075 feet.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 2,927 feet (892 m) and places the site in Barbour County.[^2] The two heights disagree, and this story does not choose between them.

In 1916 the station was one story high and was not enclosed.[^1] The station kept a map table, and a metal cover was provided against the weather.[^1] No regular watchman was employed, because a family lived within two hundred feet of the station and went up to the tower several times a day to make observations, reporting any fires they found to the state office.[^1] The same report says very few bad fires occurred there, since the land was thickly settled and good phone service could be had.[^1]

The register says the tower was removed by 1997, with no exact date given.[^2] The site was added to the register on January 23, 2017.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks it as gone.[^3]

The register describes a concrete pad that remains, with remnants of a shed. A communications building and tower stand nearby.[^2] No source describes whether the site is open to the public, so visitors should not assume access.
