Sherman Peak lies on the east flank of Sequoia National Forest, at the margin of the Kern Plateau.[^2] A Fresno Bee item from May 1923 is the earliest source we found for a Forest Service lookout there.[^1] It placed the station on Mount Sherman in Tulare County and said Jennie E. Abbott, then the only woman the Forest Service employed as a lookout, would start work there on June 1.[^1]

Most of the later items concern George McComas, a veteran lookout. A Bakersfield newspaper reported in May 1933 that he had hurt a leg during trail work.[^1] That June a visitor stayed for two weeks with him at his post.[^1] In October 1935 he came back after four months on the peak, and in June 1936 the paper reported he had gone up again for the summer.[^1] The papers spelled his name several ways, including McComas and McCommas.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Sherman Peak tower as gone.[^4] A Peakbagging table gives the site a "LO site" label, a tag it applies when a lookout building is gone and little or nothing of it may survive.[^5]

A SummitPost guide says a fire lookout once stood on the summit, which today holds just a handful of radio transmitters.[^2] An old jeep road, Forest Service Road 33E48, climbs to the top for the people who maintain those towers.[^2] A hiking trail starts at Sherman Pass Vista and runs about 5.2 miles round trip, ending at a high, exposed top where a lookout used to stand.[^3] The Sherman Pass Road is usually passable from late May or early June into mid or late November.[^2]
