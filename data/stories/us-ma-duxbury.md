Captain's Hill in Duxbury, Massachusetts, is the site of the Myles Standish Monument. The 116-foot granite monument has a statue of Captain Myles Standish, the military leader of Plymouth Colony, at its top.[^3] The register says a stone lookout on top of the monument began operating in 1889 as a town-run tower for Duxbury.[^1] The monument was not finished until 1898, so the 1889 start date is unverified.[^3]

The register adds that the lookout was meant to pair with Plymouth's Billington Hill tower, and that staffing ran until 1910.[^1]

An entry dated January 1920 quotes the State Forester's report for 1919. It describes an old windmill tower, formerly in use at Chelmsford, that had been erected on the Myles Standish Reservation with a 10-foot addition.[^2] The quoted entry names only the reservation, not Captain's Hill, so this story does not link the tower to the monument.[^2]

The state has held the monument and grounds since 1920.[^3] Today the site is Myles Standish Monument State Reservation, a public recreation area managed by the state's Department of Conservation and Recreation.[^3] The park has walking trails and picnic tables, and the monument's 125 steps can be climbed when it is open.[^3] No source we checked says what, if anything, remains of the lookout on top of the monument.
