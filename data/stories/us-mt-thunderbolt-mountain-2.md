Thunderbolt Mountain's lookout was a 20-foot pole tower with a glass-enclosed cab, on a peak about 14 miles east-southeast of Deer Lodge, Montana.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com places it in the Helena and Deerlodge national forests, in Powell and Jefferson counties.[^1]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built it. Work began in the summer of 1937, and the structure was finished that fall.[^2] A telephone line and trail were run to the site that year, and the plans included living quarters for the lookout crew.[^2]

Sources disagree on the elevation. Firelookout.com gives 8,597 feet.[^1] Newspaper items from 1937 give 8,000 feet, 8,400 feet and 9,400 feet.[^2]

In July 1938 both the Thunderbolt and Haystack lookouts were staffed to get ready for prevention control.[^2]

Firelookout.com says the tower was destroyed in 1965.[^1] A January 1966 item quoted on montanalookouts.weebly.com reports that a new tower on Jack Mountain, north of Basin, replaced the Thunderbolt station, which was unsafe and was removed.[^2] That replacement, a 53-foot treated-timber tower, was built in 1965 and torn down in 2011.[^3] Jack Mountain is a separate peak, so that tower was never the original Thunderbolt lookout.[^2][^3]

No source we read describes the Thunderbolt Mountain site today, including whether anything remains or whether the public can reach it.
