Tussey Mountain had observation posts before the steel tower.[^2] Around 1910 a tree post, 30 feet above the ground, was set up.[^2] A 1912-13 state forestry report describes an observatory station built on the mountain along the Pennsylvania Furnace road.[^2]

In July 1933 the state's chief forest fire warden ordered three new 80-foot observation towers, one of them for Tussey Mountain in Huntingdon County's Logan State Forest district.[^2] By August a newspaper reported that the three towers had recently been built, the Tussey one among them.[^2] The register gives 1933 as the build year and calls it a steel Aermotor tower.[^1]

A September 1938 Altoona paper reported that a new aerial signal light had been placed atop the tower.[^2] People east of the village were at first taken aback by it, but their unease eased once they saw it turn through white, blue and red.[^2]

The register and the lookout history site both say the tower was removed in 1977.[^1][^2]

A hawk-watch project's site profile says a former fire tower near Pennsylvania Furnace Road was once a popular place to watch hawks, about five miles southwest of the project's current count site.[^3] The count site itself is a powerline right-of-way at the crest of the ridge.[^3] The profile gives no date for that tower, so it is likely, but not certain, that it is this one.[^3] No source I read describes what, if anything, remains at the tower site today.[^1][^2]
