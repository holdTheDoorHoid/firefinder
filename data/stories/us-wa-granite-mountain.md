On Granite Mountain in the Okanogan National Forest, about 8 miles southwest of Conconully, a log cabin with a cupola went up in 1930.[^1][^2] In 1934 panorama photographs were taken from the cabin, which stood 17 feet above the ground.[^3][^4]

Sources differ on how the cabin ended.[^1][^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was removed in 1954, while firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3] A U.S. Geological Survey party visited the summit in 1956 and did not report a lookout building there.[^3] In 1973 a forest helitack crew removed the remains of the old lookout and built a helispot for pre-attack fire control, according to a newspaper report.[^3][^4]

The trail runs all the way to the old lookout site, about 5.5 miles one way from a trailhead off Forest Service roads southwest of Conconully.[^3] Fire hit the upper section of the trail, and deadfall was cut out in 2019, with the Forest Service maintaining the trail.[^3] A 2025 trip report describes much of the upper mountain as burned over.[^5] No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the summit today.
