Mule Peak Lookout stood on a rocky peak in the Sequoia National Forest, in Tulare County, California.[^1][^3] It was a 14-by-14-foot wood-frame cab.[^1][^3] Sources give 1935 or 1936 as the build year, and the register gives the cost as $2,800.[^1][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the cab, and crews packed the materials in by horse and mule.[^1][^3]

The Tule River Indian Reservation, which the lookout overlooks, helped fund it, and it was staffed five days a week during fire season.[^1][^3] A 1948 newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page describes a lookout observer, Mrs. M. Melford, reporting the first condor flight of that season near the station.[^2] By 2011 fire-lookouts.org reported the lookout as unstaffed, although the register still describes it as in active service.[^3][^1]

The Windy Fire, which burned in September 2021, destroyed the lookout.[^1][^4] The Forest Service said it was destroyed early in the fire, even though it had been wrapped in fire-resistant material.[^4] Photos released afterward showed the structure reduced to rubble.[^5]

The register says the lookout could be reached only by trail.[^1] Forest Service trail information still describes a 0.6-mile trail that ends at the lookout tower and says it is open to hikers and horseback riders with no permit needed.[^6] Other accounts put the walk at 1.25 miles from the parking area.[^3] The same trail page warns that recent fires and flooding may have left washouts and downed trees. It does not say whether the structure stands, and the 2021 reports describe it as destroyed.[^6][^4][^5]
