The Padlock Hill tower stood on Blackman Hill in the Town of Caroline, Tompkins County.[^1][^5] The DEC places that hill in Potato Hill State Forest, 12 miles southeast of Ithaca.[^2]

In 1940 the state Conservation Department chose the hill for a fire observation tower.[^1] A crew of ten CCC enrollees from Camp SP-125 at Slaterville spent about two months on the steel structure, which the Ithaca Journal reported as 75 feet high.[^1] A 1941 item gave its height as 68 feet, so the sources disagree.[^1] That item also describes steel construction with a stairway up to a glass-enclosed cab.[^1]

The tower went into service in April 1941.[^1] Russell J. Boyer of Slaterville Springs was appointed observer for the spring fire season, and the item called it the first fire tower erected in that part of the state.[^1]

In the early 1980s the tower was removed to the State Fairgrounds, where it became part of a Department of Environmental Conservation exhibit.[^1] A Central New York hiking list gives the move as 1985, so the sources disagree on the year.[^3] The DEC now describes the tower as part of a permanent exhibit at the fairgrounds.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Padlock Hill site as gone, and the fairgrounds tower as standing.[^5] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on Blackman Hill. The DEC says the forest can be reached from Blackman Hill Road, where there is an unpaved parking area for up to eight vehicles.[^4]
