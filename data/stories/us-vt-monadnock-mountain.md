The Monadnock Mountain fire tower sits on a 3,148-foot peak in the town of Lemington, in Essex County, Vermont.[^1][^2] A Valley News column describes the summit tower as rising above a thick forest.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the tower to 1938 and says the Civilian Conservation Corps put up the steel tower between 1938 and 1940.[^1]

The column says the chimney and foundation of a warden's cabin still stand beside the tower, and that the corps built the cabin in the late 1930s.[^2] The Register says the tower was staffed for about a decade, but it gives no years for that stretch.[^1]

At some point the tower fell into decline. The column says the trail had grown unkempt and the tower had fallen into disrepair, until the NorthWoods Stewardship Center and its conservation corps came to the rescue about ten years before the column ran.[^2]

The land has changed hands as well. The Trust for Public Land acquired 14 parcels in Vermont's Northeast Kingdom from International Paper Realty Corporation, and in 2005 it conserved this 1,471-acre mountaintop and conveyed it to the Town of Lemington for public use.[^3] The Register lists the Town of Lemington as the administrator, and it recorded the tower on March 24, 2020.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Vermont chapter report for 2020 lists the trail and tower as closed to the public.[^4] Its 2021 report still lists the trail as closed.[^5] The sources read do not show a reopening, so visitors should treat the tower as closed unless the Town of Lemington says otherwise.

The sources read say nothing about who staffed the tower during its working years or which fires it watched.
