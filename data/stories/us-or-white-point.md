White Point sits on a ridge in Jackson County, Oregon, about two miles south of Halls Point Lookout.[^1][^4] The Oregon Department of Forestry owns and runs the site, even though it sits inside the Rogue River-Siskiyou National Forest.[^3]

The first tower went up in 1951.[^3] It was a 40-foot tower with a 14-by-14-foot house, and a Southwest District annual report lists its cost as $2,143.17.[^2] In 1974 the state replaced it.[^3] The register describes the new structure as a 30-foot treated timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot flat-top cab.[^1][^3] A contract that year set a price of $1,500 for building the lookout, and one account says the tower itself was recycled from the Burnt Peak lookout.[^2]

Sources disagree on the height of the 1974 tower. The register, Cheryl Hill's page and Fire Lookout all give 30 feet, while the Oregon Forest Lookouts page gives 28 feet.[^1][^3][^4][^2] This story uses 30 feet.

The tower was staffed into the 2010s.[^4] A 2016 visit report on the register says that summer was the last year it was in active service.[^1] Cameras went up by summer 2017, and the tower has not been staffed since.[^1][^3]

I found no source describing a fire the lookout spotted, so this story has no fire record to tell.

Cheryl Hill visited on July 12, 2023.[^3] She reported that the lookout is now a remotely monitored camera site.[^3] She also reports that part of the equipment was stolen when the lookout was broken into the August before her visit.[^3]

Getting there is not simple. The spur road off Road 290 is gated, and the lookout sits 0.1 miles past that gate.[^3] The forest road was overgrown in 2023.[^3] Because the state owns the site, check with the Oregon Department of Forestry before you go, and do not climb the tower.
