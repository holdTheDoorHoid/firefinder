Raspberry Ridge is a lookout in Kananaskis Country, Alberta.[^1] It took over from the Cameron lookout on Mount Burke, which was decommissioned in 1953.[^1] Most people believe the move happened because no road could reach the top of Mount Burke, though the reasons are not clear.[^1]

In 1953 a road was built up to the ridge.[^1] A new lookout, 3.7 m by 3.7 m (12 ft by 12 ft), was staffed for the first time in 1954.[^1] The present building replaced that lookout in 1977.[^1][^2] The register lists it as a cabin, 2 m (6.6 ft) high, at 2,360 m (7,743 ft) elevation.[^2]

Helicopters made the access road unnecessary from the 1980s, and the road was reclaimed in 1986.[^1] A FireNet radio repeater station also sits on the ridge.[^1]

The Friends of Kananaskis Country describe the lookout as both a workplace and a private residence. A locked, gated barbed wire fence encircles the site, roughly 100 m out from the lookout.[^1] The register lists it as active and reached by air.[^2] The province says each of its lookout sites is staffed by an observer, generally from April to October.[^3] No source describes public visits, so treat the site as a private working place.
