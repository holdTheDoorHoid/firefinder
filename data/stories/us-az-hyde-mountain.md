Hyde Mountain Lookout stands in the Prescott National Forest near Camp Wood, in Yavapai County.[^1][^2] A March 1937 newspaper item said the planned lookout would address a shortfall in the Forest Service's fire control system.[^3] The register dates the lookout house to 1936 and calls it an L-5 groundhouse.[^1] The National Register record also gives 1936 as its significant year and lists the Civilian Conservation Corps as architect.[^2] That 1937 item still described the lookout as one that "will be built soon," so the sources disagree on exactly when it went up.[^3]

The earliest staffing the sources name is Wayne Black.[^3] In May 1938 a ranger placed him as lookout on the point, according to a newspaper item quoted on a lookout history site.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the lookout on August 5, 2003,[^1] and the National Register of Historic Places record for the lookout house is dated January 28, 1988.[^2]

Today the register describes Hyde Mountain as semi-active and no longer in service.[^1] A May 2020 visit report found the building in good condition, though it needed paint and a screen door repair, and it noted the lookout was not staffed.[^1]

Getting close takes a hike, not a drive.[^1] The register notes there is no road access, so the last stretch is a hike of roughly an hour.[^1] A trail guide puts the trailhead 43 miles from Prescott, with 18 of those miles on dirt, and gives a start near 5,800 feet and a lookout at 7,270 feet.[^4] The 2020 visitor called the trail rigorous.[^1]
