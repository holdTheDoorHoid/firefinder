The Fargo Fire Tower was built in 1928 by the Speir Pine Lumber Company to protect the pinelands west of the Okefenokee Swamp.[^1][^2] The Register puts the tower at 100 feet, an International Stacey steel design, with a cab seven feet square.[^1]

The tower later passed to the Georgia Forestry Commission, and the Register says it remains in active service at a state ranger station in Clinch County.[^1] The Register's description carries no date, so it does not show how the tower is staffed today. The sources I read do not name any fire it watched.

The town of Fargo was threatened by the Bugaboo fire in April and May 2007.[^3] A Vanishing Georgia post calls the Fargo tower very relevant in the same passage, but the text I could read does not say how it connects to that fire.[^4]

The Register says a large electric star on the side of the cab is lit every Easter, and that the site is used for sunrise services.[^1] The Register also counts the tower as a local tourist attraction.[^1] The sources give no visiting rules and do not say whether the tower can be climbed. Before going to the site, check with the Georgia Forestry Commission, which the Register lists as administrator.[^1] Do not climb the tower.
