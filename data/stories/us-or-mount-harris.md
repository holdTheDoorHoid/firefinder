Mount Harris rises in Union County, about 12 miles northeast of La Grande, and the Oregon Department of Forestry administers the lookout on its summit.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says a lookout first stood there in 1930.[^1]

A 1930 Forest Log item describes a lookout house under construction on the mountain, with a road being built that will let cars come a short distance from the summit, and about four miles of phone line to reach the district warden.[^2] Sources disagree on the tower that followed. Firelookout.com says a 30-foot wooden live-in tower went up in the late 1930s, while Forest Lookouts dates a 14-by-14 cab on a 30-foot tower to 1942.[^2][^3]

In 1957 a new tower with a live-in cab went up at a slightly different spot.[^1] The register describes an open timber structure 40 feet tall, while Forest Lookouts calls it a laminated structure completed in October for $2,000.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com calls it a treated timber tower, so the sources do not agree on materials.[^3]

The two named lookouts in these sources are Al Geiss, described as the lookout on Mt. Harris in August 1937,[^2] and Florence Beck, listed for 1954.[^3] That year rock near the top was blasted away to widen the road for supply trips, and in 1956 a new radio repeater went in on the mountain.[^2]

The old tower was dismantled in 1958, and its salvaged material went into a repeater building under the new one.[^2] Firelookout.com says the lookout has been severely vandalized.[^3] A 2012 visitor recorded its status as abandoned and found the summit crowded with radio towers and antennas, with the lookout itself used to hold some of the radio equipment.[^5] The register added the tower on July 5, 2022.[^1]

A directory of Oregon lookouts lists the tower as standing.[^4] The 2012 account describes a rough road that the visitor left partway, followed by a hike of about 1.8 miles to the top.[^5] The sources do not say whether the road or summit is open to the public today, or whether the tower can be entered. Anyone planning a trip should check with the Oregon Department of Forestry first, and should not assume that an abandoned tower is open to climb.
