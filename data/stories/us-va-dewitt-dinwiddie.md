The DeWitt lookout stands in Dinwiddie County, Virginia, and the Virginia Department of Forestry administers it.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 110-foot Aermotor tower built in the 1930s, and it gives no closer date than the decade.[^1] The Register says it was built to do double duty, spotting fires and watching over woodland on behalf of local lumber yards.[^1]

Later, the tower was staffed only when needed, since Fort Pickett lay nearby.[^1] The sources I read do not name anyone who staffed it, and none records a specific fire at the site.[^1][^2][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^2] A blog post calls it a retired fire lookout.[^3] Read together, the two describe a tower that is still there but no longer used as a lookout.[^2][^3]

The Register registered the tower on January 23, 2017, as US 1164 and VA 30.[^1] None of these sources says whether the public may climb or visit it, or whether it can be rented.[^1][^2][^3] Ask the Virginia Department of Forestry before going, and do not climb the tower.
