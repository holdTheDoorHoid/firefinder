The Sandoval Mesa Lookout is a 50-foot metal tower in Archuleta County, Colorado, at 8,488 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower may be one of a kind. It also says its stair and landing design differs from the major fire tower models.[^1] Its 7-by-7-foot cab is wrapped in a four-foot catwalk, which the register calls unusual for a cab that small.[^1]

The register gives 1970 as the year the tower was built.[^1] It does not say who designed or built it, why it was placed there, or who staffed it or watched for fires from it.[^1] The site was registered on January 21, 2010, as US 871 and CO 11.[^1]

Its later history is unclear. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Colorado list marks the tower as removed, but it gives the same register number.[^2] The register describes the tower in the present tense and does not confirm its current condition, so the two sources disagree, and we could not settle the question from what we could read.[^1][^2]

The Southern Ute Indian Tribe owns and manages the tower, and the register says there is no public access.[^1] Please do not try to reach or climb it. Anyone who wants to know its current condition should ask the tribe directly.
