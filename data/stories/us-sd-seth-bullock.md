The Seth Bullock lookout sits on Scruton Mountain, just southwest of Pactola Lake.[^2][^5] It is named for Seth Bullock, who was the Black Hills National Forest's first supervisor.[^2][^4] The forest announced the new station in December 1938.[^2] By September 1939 the first tower was complete.[^2] It was a 30-foot log tower whose cab, measuring 14 by 14 feet, held living quarters, and the forest planned to man it through the fire season starting the next spring.[^2]

The register describes that first structure as an open wood tower, 30 feet tall, with an L-4 cab.[^1] The forest replaced the wooden superstructure and reconfigured the stairs in 1949.[^3] In July 1954 a two-acre blaze half a mile from the tower was brought under control by a ranger and crew from Pactola.[^2]

The sources disagree about the steel tower that followed. The register dates the current CL-100 cab and steel tower to 1975.[^1] A book cited by westlookouts says the original tower was taken down with explosive charges that year, and that the replacement is a CL-100 all-steel tower.[^2] firelookout.com instead lists a 67-foot all-steel live-in tower in 1966, partly built from a 53-foot tower moved from Mt. Pisgah in Wyoming, and says the wooden tower was destroyed in 1975.[^3] The sources also give different heights, from 53 feet to 67 feet.[^4][^1][^3]

Today the tower stands on Scruton Mountain.[^3] The register listed it in July 2022.[^1] The summit is reached by a gated road off Edelweiss Mountain Road.[^4] A hiking blog found the gate locked and a walk of about 1.8 miles one way, and reported that visitors cannot get onto the tower itself.[^5] No Forest Service statement on the tower's current use or any rental turned up in the sources checked.
