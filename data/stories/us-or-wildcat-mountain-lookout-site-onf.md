Wildcat Mountain, about 16 miles northeast of Prineville in the Ochoco National Forest, had a crow's nest in the 1920s.[^1][^2] In 1932 it was replaced with a 90-foot steel tower and a cabin.[^1][^2]

The Central Oregonian, quoted on an Oregon lookouts index, mentions people working at the station in 1934 and 1936.[^3] A July 1934 item called Don McNely a lookout fireman on Wildcat Mountain, and said he had accepted an appointment on the Umatilla forest.[^3]

In early July 1936, Roy Gray, his wife and their family set out for the Wildcat Lookout Station, where the Forest Service had hired him for the summer.[^3] A July 1937 item reported a wind storm that tore shutters from the lookout tower.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both say the tower and cabin were gone by 1971.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com lists the station's life as 1932 to 1971.[^2] Neither source says how they came down.[^1][^2]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether the summit is open to visitors.
