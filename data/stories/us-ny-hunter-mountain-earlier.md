Hunter Mountain's wooden fire tower went up in 1909, built from timbers found on the mountaintop. It stood about 40 feet below the true summit.[^1] Droughts in 1903 and 1908 brought severe fires that cost thousands of dollars and pushed the public to demand tighter fire control.[^2] The register counts the station among Forest Fire Control's earliest facilities.[^1]

In 1917 a 60-foot steel Aermotor LS-40 tower went up beside the wooden one.[^1] Wikipedia adds that horse teams transported the steel sections up the dirt road from Spruceton.[^2]

The two sources disagree on when the tower moved. The register says it was dismantled and re-erected on the summit in 1950, while Wikipedia dates the move to 1953.[^1][^2] Either way, the tower left the lower site for the summit.[^1][^2]

Wikipedia reports that the first tower's footings can still be seen at the trail junction where it stood.[^2] Neither source describes anything else left at the lower site.

The steel tower itself still stands on the summit.[^1] The register says it has been fully restored and welcomes visitors, and that Committee volunteers cover it on weekends between Memorial and Columbus Day.[^1] The register also notes that NYSDEC-blazed trails lead to it.[^1]
