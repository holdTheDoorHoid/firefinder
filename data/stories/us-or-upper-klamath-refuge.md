The earliest lookout record we found at Upper Klamath Refuge is a Forest Service permit. On June 29, 1935, the Forest Service issued a special use permit for a lookout tower and cabin to help run the refuge, covering 30.05 acres of Forest Service land.[^1]

The refuge dates to April 1928, when Calvin Coolidge set apart 7,560 acres as a refuge for birds and wild animals.[^2] Wikipedia now gives the refuge as about 14,400 acres.[^3] The two figures disagree, and we found nothing that explains the change.

A 1937 newspaper item shows workers taking apart a large steel tower at the biological survey headquarters west of Tule Lake. The tower had been used by game agents to watch for poachers, and the item says it would be moved to the Upper Klamath Lake Preserve.[^1] The sources do not say whether that tower is the one the 1935 permit covered, or when a lookout was first put up at the refuge.

The Forest Lookouts page says that in 1974 a crew from the Cave Junction smokejumper base dismantled the tower and moved it to "that site," without naming the place.[^1]

A 2015 visit found three foundation blocks at the old refuge headquarters, where the page places the tower site. The fourth block and a possible step pad were buried or removed, and no other remains were noted.[^1] Signs at the compound entrances say "No Public Access."[^1] Wikipedia says the refuge is reached only by boat, from Rocky Point, Malone Springs and a few neighboring ramps.[^3] We found no description of the site after 2015.
