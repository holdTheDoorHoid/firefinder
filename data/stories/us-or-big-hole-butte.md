Big Hole Butte sits in Lake County, Oregon, on the Deschutes National Forest, about 19 miles southeast of LaPine.[^1][^2]

A 1970 Bend Bulletin account says a crew built a telephone line from the Summit stage station to the summit in the spring of 1931.[^3] In August 1932 the paper reported that work was about to start on a 20-foot tower with a standard lookout house on top.[^3] The register describes it as a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1932.[^1]

In June 1932 Neil Winkle left for the Fort Rock district, where he was to be the forest fire lookout at Big Hole Butte. He had returned from there by October.[^3] In January 1936 Roderick Blacker was the lookout.[^3] One evening he sighted a plane's flare, drove down the highway to meet a couple whose plane had made a forced landing in an old burn, and brought both of them back to the lookout, where they stayed all night.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1958.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year instead.[^2]

The register's only photo, dated 5/2/2001, shows a foundation block for one tower leg.[^1]

No source we found describes the summit today beyond that photo, or says whether the butte is open to visitors or how to reach it.[^1]
