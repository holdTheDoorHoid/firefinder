Chinquapin Point sits in the Siuslaw National Forest in Lincoln County, about 15 miles southeast of Toledo.[^1][^2] The register describes a 20-foot pole L-4 tower on the point, at 1,104 feet (336 m) of elevation.[^1]

The sources disagree about when the tower went up. A 1934 note cited on the Forest Lookouts page says the tower was built that year.[^3] The Osbornes Project also dates it to 1934.[^4] The register and firelookout.com both put the 20-foot tower in 1936.[^1][^2] Panorama photos were taken there on March 24, 1934 and again on September 16, 1936.[^3][^4]

In August 1936 the Forest Lookouts page recounts that Nell Springer, wife of emergency guard C.D. Springer, was at the lookout while her husband was away on a fire.[^3] She spotted a new fire in a high-hazard area and pinpointed its location well enough that the crew got there as quickly as possible.[^3] The note's author suggested she receive the title of honorary lookout.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1950, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] The Osbornes Project describes the lookout as abandoned by 1950.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the point today, and we found no current access information. Check land ownership and access rules before you go.
