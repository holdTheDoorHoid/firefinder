The Forest Service put a fire lookout on Vulcan Peak in the Siskiyou National Forest in 1941.[^1][^2][^3] A May 1940 guard placement plan described the spot as looking west over country where people had started many fires.[^3] The cabin was a 14-by-14-foot L-4 built on the ground.[^3][^4]

In its first season the lookout was staffed for 100 days, and the observer reported to the Gold Beach station by radio.[^3] A 1964 item, dated July 1 and quoted by the Forest Lookouts history, said the lookout was manned that year.[^3] The same history places the site on the boundary of the Kalmiopsis Wild Area and calls it the farthest-flung station on the Chetco District.[^3]

The cabin came down in 1968.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says it was removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] Oregon Hikers gives only a 1960s date for the removal.[^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today. Oregon Hikers lists a hike to what it calls an old lookout site, and it places the peak in the Kalmiopsis Wilderness.[^4] The same page says the Biscuit Fire of 2002 burned the summit hard.[^4] A 2021 forum thread on that page is titled as saying the lake and peak are completely unaccessible. The thread is unverified, so check conditions before any visit.[^4]
