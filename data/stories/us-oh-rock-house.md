Ohio put up a steel fire tower at Rock House in the summer of 1929.[^1] A December 1929 Athens newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, said hundreds of visitors had already climbed to its observation room.[^1] A March 1930 issue of Forest Worker called it one of three new 65-foot steel fire towers ready in Ohio, the one on Rock House State Park in Hocking County.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register instead places the site in Hocking State Forest, and the sources do not explain the difference.[^2]

In May 1931 a fire guard, Luther Carr, was roofing a building near the tower when he spotted smoke south of the cave.[^1] A survey put the blaze in a hollow some miles off, and rangers cleared a path through the leaves and lit a backfire.[^1] In October 1934 a Chillicothe Gazette item said Ira Oldfield had been selected as towerman at Rock House.[^1]

The Ohio Department of Natural Resources is the agency the register names.[^2] The Ohio fire tower list marks the tower as gone, and the Forest Lookouts page ends its Rock House entry with the single word "Removed," giving no date or method.[^3][^1] The register recorded the site on September 3, 2014.[^2]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today or whether visitors can reach it.
