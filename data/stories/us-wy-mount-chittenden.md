Mount Chittenden is a peak in the Absaroka Range, inside Yellowstone National Park in Park County, Wyoming.[^1][^3] The peak was named for George B. Chittenden, a surveyor, not for Major Hiram M. Chittenden, the Army engineer known for road and bridge work in the park.[^3]

In July 1932 a Teton Valley News item, quoted on a lookout history site, reported that the park had temporarily added a fourth fire outlook on Mount Chittenden, near the east boundary.[^1] The same item named the park's other outlooks as Mounts Washburn, Holmes and Sheridan, and called the Chittenden site the latest of them.[^1]

The park's fire history gives a smaller count. John D. Coffman, the Park Service's fire control expert, found two Yellowstone fire lookouts, one on Mount Washburn and one on Mount Sheridan.[^4] The 1932 item lists two more outlooks than that.[^1][^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wyoming list files Mount Chittenden among its unknown/undocumented sites, not among its lookout structures. It says a site moves to the lookout lists only if evidence shows it was used as a regular wildland fire lookout.[^2]

No source I could read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today.
