Blue Knob got a state fire tower in 1921.[^1] An August 1921 notice named Blue Knob, Bedford County, as one of the first 18 towers to be put up.[^1] A June 1922 Bedford Gazette piece said fifty 60-foot steel towers had been erected in the latter part of the summer of 1921.[^1] An October 1922 Altoona Mirror item described the Blue Knob tower as sixty feet tall.[^1] The register gives 1921 as the build year and calls it a steel Aermotor tower.[^2]

The 1922 Gazette also said the tower stood on the highest point in the state, at 3,165 feet. The register puts the summit at 3,025 feet (922 m), so the two figures disagree.[^1][^2]

Fires came early. In October 1921 five fires were reported in one batch near the tower, and the Kane Republican said there was evidence they had been set on purpose.[^1] In October 1922 a blaze about five miles away could not be seen for some time by the towerman, because of the way the mountains lay.[^1]

A towerman was on duty at the tower.[^1] In 1928 the department decided to remove the lower two flights of steps whenever the towerman was off duty, because of damage that included broken glass and locks.[^1]

The same Mirror item said the tower was open to the public every day until December 1.[^1] An August 1924 Altoona Tribune item said the state forestry department, by then called the Department of Forests and Waters, had erected the tower.[^1]

A National Geodetic Survey station description from 1941 calls it a four-legged steel structure about 75 feet high, with a glass cab and a Civil Aeronautics Bureau airway beacon on the roof.[^1] The register says the tower was relocated in 1951 and removed in 1991.[^2]

The National Park Service transferred the land to Pennsylvania on September 26, 1945, and it became Blue Knob State Park.[^3] Today a park trail starts on the Tower Road just below the summit, and the same road passes a gated road that leads to Herman Point, where transmitter towers stand on its own summit.[^4] No source I found describes what, if anything, remains of the 1921 tower on the summit.
