Battle Mountain Lookout is a 22-foot steel tower about a mile northeast of Hot Springs, in Fall River County, South Dakota.[^1][^2] It stands in the Battle Mountain–Friendshuh Game Production Area and is administered by the South Dakota State Forestry Department.[^1]

Construction was authorized in September 1964, with an estimated cost of $1,000 and $500 of that to come back from Fall River County, according to a newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts site.[^3] A June 1965 item reports that R.C. Hutcheson Steel Co., of Fort Pierce, was approved for contracts at Hot Springs and Pickstown, including $1,491 to erect a 22-foot fire tower on Battle Mountain.[^3] The register says the tower was built in 1965.[^1] It is an open steel tower with a small cab that was meant for observation only.[^1]

I found no record of who staffed the tower, how often it was manned, or any particular fire it helped to spot.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4] The National Historic Lookout Register gave it the number US 1670, SD 9. It was nominated by Brad Eells and registered on July 10, 2022.[^1] The sources disagree on its elevation: the register gives 4,363 feet, while Rex's fire lookout page gives 4434 feet.[^1][^2]

Today the tower sits on ground that the state's fish and game agency manages.[^1] A SummitPost hiking guide says the surrounding area is open year round and that overnight camping is not allowed.[^5] None of the sources says whether the tower itself can be climbed, so check with the state before going near it, and do not climb it.
