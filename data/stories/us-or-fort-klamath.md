The first Fort Klamath lookout, according to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register, was an elevated platform over the community center, mainly in use during the 1940s and 1950s.[^1] A 1949 item quoted on a Rex's Fire Lookout Page calls the post a Grange Hall tower, so the early set-up is described differently from one source to the next.[^2]

Forest Service lookouts were posted there in the late 1940s.[^2] In June 1948 a lookout named James D. Hale was to be stationed at Fort Klamath, and in 1949 the post went to Mr. and Mrs. Brady Broxson.[^2]

The Forest Service had a reason to want the station.[^2] In 1955 the forest felt there was a definite gap in its coverage over the Seven-Mile and Three-Mile areas, and Fire Control approved a lookout in the Fort Klamath area to cover them.[^2]

The steel tower was built in 1958 on the southwest side of town. The register calls it a 20-foot CL-100 steel live-in tower.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association says the tower sat at the bottom of a valley instead of a mountaintop, one of the strangest lookout locations in Oregon.[^3]

The station did detection work.[^2] In July 1964 the tower guard spotted a fire in a logged area and reported it to the Lake of the Woods ranger station, which sent crews.[^2]

In 1970 the tower was airlifted to Sugarpine Mountain. The move also left the Round Butte lookout unnecessary and cut the need for a Yamsay Mountain tower.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register records the structure as relocated from Fort Klamath that year and originally built in 1958.[^4]

The original site has no tower now.[^5] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the spot or whether it is open to visitors. The tower itself stands on Sugarpine Mountain in the Winema National Forest, and the association's Oregon list records it as standing.[^4][^5] It is staffed every summer, according to the association's Sugarpine Mountain page.[^6]
