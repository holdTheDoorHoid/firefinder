Simmons Peak sits in Shoshone County, Idaho, about four miles northeast of Red Ives R.S., at 6,762 feet.[^1][^2] The site began as a camp in 1924, and an L-4 tower went up in 1933.[^1][^2] In 1953 an L-4 structure was set atop a concrete base.[^1][^2] The sources disagree on the 1953 structure: firelookout.com calls it an L-4 cab, Idaho Fire Lookouts an L-4 tower, and the statewide list a two-story cab.[^1][^2][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts names Charlie Kevis for the 1930s, citing Hardship & Happy Times by Bert Russell, and Tom Boehr for 1967, citing the Kellogg Evening News.[^2] Staffing ended in 1972.[^1][^2]

Lightning burned the lookout down in 1984, and it is gone.[^1][^2][^3]

A route guide describes Simmons Ridge Road, FS 346, a 3-mile spur off Red Ives Creek Road that climbs to the site of the former lookout.[^4] The guide places that road in the Idaho Panhandle National Forest, while firelookout.com names the St. Joe National Forest for the lookout; no source explains the difference.[^1][^4] No source describes what remains on the site, such as the concrete base, and none says whether visitors may go there.[^1][^2][^4]
