Snake River Lookout stands inside Yellowstone National Park, about two miles east of the park's South Entrance, on a rise above the Snake River at roughly 8,180 feet.[^1][^2] It is a small 8-by-8-foot open-air wood frame structure, put up by the Civilian Conservation Corps as a secondary lookout — the National Historic Lookout Register calls it the only remaining open-air lookout structure built anywhere in Yellowstone.[^1] Its timbers may have been framed as early as 1934, but the building itself was not erected until 1935, and the register doubts it was even used before 1936.[^1] The National Park Service, which still administers the site, carries it on its books as Building #699.[^1]

Its working life was short. In the register's own words, the lookout was "Most probably no longer used some time in the mid to late 1940's and the trail to the lookout was abandoned decades ago."[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's statewide roster reflects that modest status: it files Snake River among "Lookout Sites / Non-lookout Structures" rather than full towers, labeling it simply a shelter.[^3]

Even so, the little building has survived. Ed Stark photographed it in September 2020 and nominated it to the National Historic Lookout Register, which listed it on February 28, 2021.[^1] With its access trail abandoned long ago and no maintenance program on record, Snake River Lookout today is best treated as a historic backcountry relic rather than a hiking destination. It sits on National Park Service land, and nothing in the record suggests it is signed, maintained, or safe to approach.
