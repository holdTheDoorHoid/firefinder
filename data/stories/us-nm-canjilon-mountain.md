The Canjilon Mountain lookout stood on a high, bald, flat-topped mountain in Rio Arriba County, New Mexico, inside the Carson National Forest.[^2] It was a wooden tower with a platform, and a frame cabin of 10 by 14 feet was built to house the lookout.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page dates the cabin and tower to about 1909. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the district was set up that year and the lookout went up soon after.[^1][^2]

Victor Ortega worked as a fire guard on the mountain during the summer months.[^2] On June 30, 1924, he took shelter from an approaching storm in the cabin.[^2] A bolt of lightning came down the stove pipe and passed through him, and a second bolt struck him on the head and killed him.[^2][^4] His wife was present.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the tower was abandoned in 1922 after several lightning strikes, one of which killed the guard.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page gives the abandonment only as a year in the 1920s, and a January 1925 newspaper report it quotes puts the death in June 1924. The 1922 date and the June 1924 death date do not agree.[^1][^2]

The tower is gone.[^1] The observer's cabin remains, with a cement foundation, a pitched tin roof, and a bunk made from spruce poles.[^1]

The cabin was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on January 28, 1988, and the register record now calls it the Victor Ortega Cabin.[^3][^4] An interpretive sign has been put up at the site to tell the story of the cabin and Ortega.[^4]

A 2020 report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico chapter lists Canjilon among the lookouts marked as needing restoration or rehabilitation.[^3]
