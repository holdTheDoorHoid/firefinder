Piney was a Forest Service lookout tower in Phelps County, Missouri, inside the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1] The Coast and Geodetic Survey described the site in 1947.[^1] The survey places the tower about 2.5 miles east of the Big Piney River and about 13.5 miles southeast of Waynesville.[^1]

The Central US Lookouts page quotes a 1937 News and Tribune report.[^1] It said plans were being completed for a temporary lookout about three miles southeast of Big Piney, an open crow's nest atop a forty-foot pine pole.[^1] The report said the nest had already been built at the Blooming Rose Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) camp.[^1]

In November 1938, The Oak Tree, a Civilian Conservation Corps newspaper of Company 1732 at Camp F-9, named Kenneth Jackson as in charge, with Joe Turner and Pershing Snodgrass.[^1] The item does not name the tower.[^1] In February 1941 the Rolla Herald reported that a cabin was being built at the Piney Lookout Tower for the CCC lookouts to use on duty.[^1]

A note on the 1947 survey description says the lookout tower had been blown down and was to be rebuilt on the same foundation and anchor bolts.[^1] The page marks the lookout as removed.[^1]

A January 2023 article about the Piney Lookout Tower Trail describes a fire tower looming over the trail, surrounded by barbed wire, and says the tower is not stable for climbers.[^2] It also reports that the Forest Service says climbing is off limits.[^2] The article places the trail on the west side of Table Rock Lake, which does not match the Phelps County location the survey gives.[^1][^2]

The removed label conflicts with the 2023 article, but the article's location does not match the survey's, so the sources do not establish whether a tower stands at the surveyed site now.[^1][^2] The sources also do not settle whether the tower on the trail is the CCC-era structure or a later rebuild.[^1][^2] The article gives the hike as about 4.2 miles in total, per All Trails.[^2]
