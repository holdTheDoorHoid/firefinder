Ontario Parks calls the tower the Bannerman Fire Tower.[^1] A park blog post says the lookout dates from the 1930s and was meant to detect forest fires.[^2] The tower sits within Fushimi Lake Provincial Park, in Cochrane District, Ontario, a park that Ontario Parks manages.[^3]

Wikipedia dates the lookout to the 1930s, says it was once sited on the north shore of the lake, and describes its structure as steel.[^3]

The park's blog describes a towerperson on fire watch from May through September.[^1] Another Ontario Parks blog post says the trail passes the site of the old fire-ranger cabin where a towerman and his family made their home.[^2]

Firelookout.ca lists the site as the Sentier (Fushimi Lake) Fire Tower.[^4] Its record describes what is left of the observer's cabin as dilapidated, lying along the trail.[^4] The park's blog names the Bannerman Fire Tower and cabin as the trail's highlight, so the two accounts appear to describe one site.[^1][^4]

Wikipedia says the tower is no longer in use but is still complete.[^3] The Ontario Trails Council counts it among the few original fire lookouts that survive in Ontario.[^5]

The Fire Tower Trail is a 7 km round trip, rated strenuous, that takes about two hours.[^1][^2] The park suggests it for experienced hikers, given a few tough stretches.[^2] The park's own page notes that one of its two hiking trails reaches a fire tower.[^6] Visitors can camp or come for the day during the summer season.[^3]
