Claremont Mountain is two summits, both nearly 7,000 feet (about 2,130 m), about four to five miles south of Quincy in Plumas County.[^1][^4] The Plumas National Forest planned a lookout here in 1907, with a telephone line to the summit and a summer ranger camp.[^1] A November 1907 notice said a fire lookout would be set up for the dry summer, once a branch line was built from Quincy.[^1] A July 2022 trip report says the lookout on Claremont Peak was finished in June 1908 and was among the earliest Forest Service lookouts in California.[^3]

In May 1909 the forest said the station would be kept up for the summer season.[^1] In November 1909 it reported that its officers had put out, or helped put out, 85 fires that season, and that the Claremont station had been useful in finding fires.[^1] In June 1911 a newspaper named Claremont and Mount Hough as the two lookout peaks, each with a telephone link to headquarters, a compass, high-power glasses and fire-location maps.[^1]

A 2022 trip report says the lookout had fallen out of use by the 1930s, that no trace of it can be seen, and that a communications tower now stands on the site.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the Claremont site as gone.[^2]

Bob Burd's trip report describes telecom towers at the top of the higher summit, one with a viewing platform about 30 feet (9 m) off the ground.[^4] On East Claremont he found a benchmark and the remains of a small antenna.[^4] The report describes a dirt forest road to the summits, with one approach under a fire closure.[^4] No source says whether the summits are open to visitors today. The 2022 trip report also says the 2020 Claremont Fire had burned forest along its route.[^3]
