Depot Mountain is in Aroostook County, Maine.[^1] The state built a station on the mountain in 1909, and a living camp was also built at small expense.[^2] Sources disagree about that first station. A 1914 state report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts page, describes it as a platform at the top of a tall spruce tree, while a Maine Memory record calls it a wood tower.[^2][^4]

The platform had become unsafe, so a new steel tower went up in 1914.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page gives its height as 60 feet.[^2] Maine Memory agrees on 60 feet in one record but calls it 63 feet in a slideshow, so the exact height is uncertain.[^4][^3] The 1914 report also mentions a telephone line of about 22 miles to English Lake.[^2]

A watchman was posted there in 1917. Clyde Fox of St. Pamphile, in Quebec, held the job, and Grover Bradford of Sebec was chief warden.[^3] The steel tower was repainted in 1917 and reported to be in good condition.[^2]

Maine Memory says the tower closed in 1973, and another Maine Memory record gives the tower an elevation of 1,300 feet.[^3][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was removed between 1995 and 2005.[^1] The register lists the site as US 103, ME 29, registered on September 15, 2008, with Henry Isenberg as nominator.[^1]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the land is open to visitors.[^1] Anyone interested should check with the landowner before visiting.
