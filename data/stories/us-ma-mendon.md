Mendon's fire tower sits at the highest point on Wigwam Hill, in the town forest near the Millville and Uxbridge town lines, according to a 2012 local history.[^2] In August 1915 the town's fire warden heard that the state would begin building a tower on the hill at once.[^2] The first tower stood 50 feet tall and first went into service in May 1916, with a watchman posted there.[^2] The register says it began operating that year to cover southern Worcester County, working with lookouts in northern Rhode Island to locate fires.[^1]

In 1954 a new 70-foot tower went up about 20 feet taller than the old one, and a public dedication was held on August 20 of that year.[^2] The older tower was later torn down.[^2]

The register gives 1955 as the build year for the present 70-foot steel tower. A photo caption on the same page is dated 1954 and shows the current tower with its predecessor, so the sources disagree by a year.[^1] This story follows the local history's 1954 date.[^2]

The town received $50 in rent from the state's Department of Conservation for the land under the tower.[^2] A photograph from 2011 shows the tower still standing.[^2] The same 2012 local history lists improving Tower Road, and a monitor to oversee the site, among the costs of providing it for recreation.[^2] Statewide, a network of 42 operating fire towers is staffed by trained observers.[^3] Current staffing at Mendon is not confirmed, so please don't climb the tower without permission.
