Miller Hill is a prominent point in Thurston County, about three miles south of Vail.[^1][^2]

The register and firelookout.com both describe a wooden tower from the 1930s, abandoned in 1958.[^3][^2] The register adds that the site may also have been called Weyerhauser 1.[^3]

Two of the lookout listings name the state Division of Forestry as the agency, and one of them pairs it with DNR.[^2][^4] One listing calls the tower gone and another marks it removed.[^2][^4] A trip report describes the summit as the former site of a fire lookout tower.[^1]

The summit lies within the Weyerhaeuser Vail Tree Farm.[^1] A clear-cut around 2014 made the four concrete footings on the summit easy to find, but new trees were planted around them within a short time.[^1] The trip report also says the footings were moved slightly northeast to free up room at the top for logging equipment.[^1]

The summit sits on Weyerhaeuser land, and access is restricted.[^1] The trip report says Weyerhaeuser asks people to hike in only when the gate is closed and no harvest is under way. It also says no one may enter from August 1 to December 31 without a special access permit.[^1] We found no source that describes anything else standing on the site. Check with the owner before going.
