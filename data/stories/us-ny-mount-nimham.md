The fire tower on Mount Nimham in Putnam County went up in 1940, built by the state and the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] Wikipedia credits CCC Camp P-135 of Peekskill with the work and gives the steel tower a height of 82 feet 6 inches, or 25.15 meters.[^2] The mountain is named for Chief Daniel Nimham, and its spelling was recently corrected from the older "Ninham."[^2]

The tower was first staffed in 1941, and that year it recorded 73 fires and 266 visitors.[^2] Fire lookout work stopped after the 1988 season, and early in 1989 the state's Department of Environmental Conservation made the closure official.[^2] In 1994 vandals set fire to the observer's cabin.[^1]

Around the mid-1990s, the Town of Kent rallied volunteers to bring the tower back. The restoration was completed in spring 2005 and dedicated on July 24 of that year.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register had already listed the tower in October 1999.[^1]

It has not been used for fire detection for many years; it now serves as a radio repeater site.[^1] According to the NHLR, the Town of Kent owns the tower, and volunteers help keep it up.[^1] Wikipedia says it is still open to the public.[^2]

To reach it, the Town of Kent's trail guide describes a moderate, hour-long walk from the DEC lot on Gipsy Trail up to the upper parking area.[^3] From there, the hike to the tower takes about 20 to 25 minutes.[^3] The guide says the view from the top is worth the climb.[^3]
