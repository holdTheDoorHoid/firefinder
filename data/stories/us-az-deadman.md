The Deadman lookout stood on a hilltop in the Deadman Wash area, north of the San Francisco Peaks, in the Coconino National Forest.[^1][^2] It was a D-6 cupola-style lookout, and a Forest Service photo caption says it was the sole example of that design in Arizona.[^2]

The 1962 Flagstaff topographic map shows the lookout, but the 1966 O'Leary Peak map does not. That later map shows the O'Leary Peak tower instead.[^1] The writer of a 2020 blog post thinks the O'Leary Peak tower replaced it.[^1] Peakbagging.com's table lists the site as "Deadman (LO site)," a label used when the building is gone or its status is unknown.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists its type as "Ground" and its status as gone.[^4]

The only firsthand account I found is a 2020 blog post, by a writer who visited the site.[^1] The writer reached it by Forest Road 514, looked at the remains, and found that the tower's own foundation is no longer evident.[^1] The writer says the concrete structure on top appears to be a cistern with a building constructed on it, and mentions two benchmarks on the hilltop.[^1]

The road is narrow and cindery, and the writer suggests a four-wheel-drive vehicle.[^1] The writer notes a stone chimney on private land near the road, but no source I read describes a closure or private land at the lookout site.[^1]
