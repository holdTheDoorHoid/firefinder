The Number Four lookout was built in 1928 by the Fisher Forestry and Realty Company, with help from the Conservation Department.[^1] Newspapers from that summer put the steel tower at close to 80 feet, with a glass-enclosed observatory.[^1] Later accounts do not agree on the height. A 2018 reader quoted in the New York chapter's newsletter says it was originally 75 feet, and the DEC says 90 feet.[^2][^3] Wikipedia gives 75 feet too, and names an Aermotor LX25 model that used ladders, not stairs, between its landings.[^4]

The Conservation Department took over running the tower in 1945, and the duties of the Beaver Lake Mountain lookout moved to Number Four.[^4][^1] The same newsletter quotes a 2019 Facebook post by Fred Knauf, who says an observer's cabin and standard stairs arrived in 1945 in place of the ladders.[^2] Wikipedia puts the switch to stairs around 1960, and says the land passed from the landowner to the state in 1958.[^4]

The eastern lookouts record gives 1982 as the final year of operation, and says the tower was removed in 1987, mainly because of vandalism.[^1] Wikipedia dates the closure to the early 1980s, with dismantling after that.[^4]

The DEC says the upper section was moved to Lowville and displayed near the entrance of its demonstration area.[^3] Wikipedia says that section was used to build a demonstration tower beside the DEC office.[^4] The observer's cabin went to Nicks Lake Campground around 1984, according to Wikipedia, and the ranger cabin at Lowville came from Gomer Hill.[^4][^3]

Today the upper section stands at the DEC demonstration area, and the DEC invites visitors to climb to the observation cabin at the top.[^3] The DEC office sits just past the tower, according to its directions.[^3] Only the tower footers remain at the original site.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list gives the Lowville Demonstration Forest tower as the relocated Number Four.[^5]
