Big Flat is a high, flat mountain on South Mountain in Cumberland County, inside Michaux State Forest, and its steel fire tower dates from 1921.[^1][^2][^3] The Pennsylvania Department of Forestry built it as one of 50 planned that year.[^2] A November 1921 notice placed the tower at an elevation of about 2,080 feet and said it was connected to the forester by a commercial telephone line.[^3]

Reports disagree on its height. The Register and the 1921 notice both give 60 feet.[^1][^3] Willhite's history opens with an 80-foot Aermotor tower, then notes that other reports say it was 60 feet tall. A 1942 survey described it as 75 feet high, with a glass-enclosed cab.[^2]

A 1936 newspaper item gave driving directions to the tower and praised the view over the Cumberland Valley. On clear days, it said, the Gettysburg battlefield monuments could be seen.[^3] Observers were recorded at the tower in 1972 and 1973.[^2]

The Register listed the tower on July 30, 2018, nominated by Kristin Reynolds.[^1] A 1952 survey found the external sheeting in poor repair, though the frame was intact and in good condition.[^2] The tower was still there in 2006.[^2] A 2021 online contract called for the demolition of a 60-foot steel tower, a cabin and concrete foundations.[^2] A 2024 visitor found no visible remains at the site and believed the footings were to be removed under that contract.[^2]

The Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry administers the tower.[^1] A 1942 survey described the property as state-owned land.[^2] The site is reached by a road walk off the Appalachian Trail, past several homes, near Shippensburg Road.[^2] Whether the tower still stands is unresolved. The 2021 contract and the 2024 visit suggest it may be gone, but I found no record that the demolition was finished.[^2] Check with the Bureau before you visit, and do not climb any remaining structure.
