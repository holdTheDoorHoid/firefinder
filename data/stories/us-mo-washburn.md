In 1944 the Missouri Conservation Commission bought 40 acres in Barry County, roughly four miles west of Washburn, as the site for a new fire-control lookout.[^1] A September 1944 issue of The Pineville Democrat reported that it would be the third tower in that section and that Elmer Ellis of Pineville was understood to have been hired to man it.[^1]

A temporary pole-type tower put up during the war stood on the site first.[^1] The 100-foot (30.5 m) steel tower that replaced it was completed in September 1955, and a Neosho News item said it overlooked Roaring River State Park and an area reaching from Cassville to Powell.[^1] The state's 1955-56 annual report calls the earlier structure a wooden tower.[^1] The Missouri Department of Conservation says the majority of the state's lookouts stood 100 feet high.[^2]

Bobby Lawrence, who had come over from the Joplin lookout near Spring City a year earlier, was slated to staff the new tower.[^1] The 1955 Neosho News item calls him Bobby; the accounts from 1961 and 1990 call the observer Bob.[^1] In April 1961 Bob Lawrence was serving as an observer at the Washburn tower.[^1] On April 2, 1961, a grass fire, driven out of control by a strong wind, killed Wayne Lawrence, 50.[^1] His brother Bob pulled him from the blazing grass, but he was already dead, and the Barry County coroner said death was due to suffocation.[^1]

A 1990 Springfield News-Leader article said Bob had spent more than 35 fire seasons in a tiny cabin atop the tower, and that he would scramble down a 115-step staircase when he spotted a fire.[^1] The same article gives the tower's height as 1,000 feet, which conflicts with the 100 feet in the September 1955 Neosho News item.[^1]

The state kept up the site's buildings in both the 1950s and the 1960s.[^1] The old tower residence was dismantled in 1956-57 and replaced with a new building, and the towersite garage got new overhead doors in 1968-69.[^1] The annual reports were titled for the Missouri Conservation Commission in 1955-56 and for the Missouri Department of Conservation by 1968-69.[^1]

No source I found describes what remains at the towersite today.
