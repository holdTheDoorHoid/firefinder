Mount June stands in the Umpqua National Forest in Lane County, Oregon.[^1][^2] Its first lookout was a 50-foot pole platform, put up in the 1920s.[^1][^2] Newspapers in late 1925 said the peak would probably become a forest fire lookout point.[^3] A cabin went up in 1934, and in 1949 a 30-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab replaced the cabin quarters.[^1][^2][^3][^5] The Forest Service's trail page says the tower that replaced the pole platform was 50 feet tall, so the height is one point where the sources disagree.[^4]

In August 1962 the News-Review, quoted on the Oregon lookouts page, classed June Mountain as an emergency station.[^3]

The tower came down in 1967.[^1][^2][^3] The register and the Oregon lookouts page say it was removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^3] A hiking group gives the reasons as better fire detection and shrinking maintenance budgets.[^5]

The Forest Service's Mount June Trail climbs to the summit and links to the Sawtooth Trail.[^4] The agency says the structures were abandoned and destroyed, but a stone base is still on the summit, and visitors stand on those ruins when they get there.[^4] The route is a hiking trail on national forest land, so the summit is reached on foot, and no tower is left to climb.[^4]
