The Alamo lookout site sits at 9,270 feet on Alamo Peak, east of Alamogordo in Otero County, in the Lincoln National Forest.[^1][^2] A new tower was replacing the old one there in 1925.[^2]

Papers from that summer describe a new all-steel tower, 35 feet tall, with an observation room glassed in on all sides.[^2] The register and the Forest Service both date the lookout to 1926.[^1][^3] The register calls it an LX-24, while the Forest Service names an Aermotor LX-25 cab on a 35-foot tower.[^1][^3] The sources do not reconcile the 1925 papers with the 1926 date, so we give both.

In May 1933 the Alamogordo News reported that Alex Quick and his family had moved out to the Alamo Lookout tower, where he would keep watch for forest fires.[^2] That is the only staffing detail we found.

Only a benchmark marks the lookout today, according to the Forest Service.[^3] The site now hosts a US Air Force telemetry antenna.[^3] The register's 2015 entry says a Holloman Air Force Base telemetry station was on the site at that time.[^1] The sources do not say when the tower came down.

The register lists the site as US 1604, NM 3. It was registered on August 30, 2015, on the nomination of Mark Gutzman.[^1]

The register says the site was still accessible in 2015, when a telemetry station already occupied it.[^1] The Forest Service page is labeled "Site Open," but a federal telemetry facility now sits where the lookout stood, and we found no source describing public access to it today.[^3] Treat the site as restricted and keep off the telemetry grounds. There is no tower left to climb.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list also marks the tower as gone.[^4]
