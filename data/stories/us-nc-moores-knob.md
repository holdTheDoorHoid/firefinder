Moores Knob is the highest mountain in the Sauratown range, and it sits inside Hanging Rock State Park in Stokes County, North Carolina.[^4][^1]

The first lookout went up in 1938, and the Register describes it as a steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] A camping blog gives an earlier date, 1936, and a height of 60 feet, and credits a Civilian Conservation Corps camp it calls Camp James.[^2] The two sources differ on the date, and this story follows the Register.

In 1951 a two-story stone structure replaced the steel lookout.[^1] The Register says Hurricane Hazel heavily damaged that stone building in 1954.[^1] The blog tells it differently. It says a hurricane put the steel lookout out of commission after only a few years, and it gives no date for the stone replacement, so the Register's 1951 date cannot be checked against it.[^2] A 2018 hiking post goes further and says a fire destroyed an old tower.[^3] The Register is the only source here that dates the replacement, so this story follows its 1951 date.[^1]

According to the Register, the restored stone building is now a viewpoint with no cab, and the state park keeps it as a place for hikers to visit.[^1]

Visitors reach it on foot.[^3] One route starts at the park lake and covers 3.8 miles round trip to the tower on the Moore's Knob Loop Trail.[^3] The stone steps are the hardest part of the route, according to one guide.[^2] The same guide suggests phoning the park to make sure the observation tower is open before you go.[^2]
