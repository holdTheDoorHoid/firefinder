Raum was a steel fire lookout in Pope County, on the Shawnee National Forest.[^1] In April 1936 the Shawnee began buying materials for a dwelling, garage-woodshed, cistern and latrine at the Raum tower site, on the same list as the Deputy and Honey School sites.[^2] A National Archives photograph titled "Photograph of Raum Lookout" is dated June 1941, so the tower was standing by then.[^3]

A listing from about 1942 describes it as the Raum Lookout Station of the U.S. Forest Service, a 100-foot steel tower where visitors were welcome.[^1]

Bob Frakes, a lookout historian quoted in Illinois Country Living, says nearly all of the state's towers were dismantled or sold off in the early 1970s.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Raum tower as gone.[^5]

No source describes what, if anything, is left at the Raum site today, or whether the spot can be reached. For context, the Forest Service says that of the 16 fire lookouts built on the Shawnee National Forest, only the Trigg tower survives.[^6]
