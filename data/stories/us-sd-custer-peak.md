Custer Peak, on the Nemo Ranger District of the Black Hills National Forest in South Dakota, has carried a fire lookout since 1911, the year after a wave of catastrophic wildfires pushed the Forest Service to start building towers across the country.[^1][^3] The first structure on the peak was simple: a wooden cabin with shutters but no glass, sitting on an open platform with no railings.[^1] It was replaced by a cupola-style building, with sources differing on whether that happened around the 1920s or as late as 1935.[^1][^2]

The stone tower that stands on the peak today dates to 1941, built as a two-story, 14-by-14-foot cab with a catwalk, using native stone chosen to blend with the surrounding landscape.[^1][^2] The architect was W. Ellis Groben, who designed the lookout specifically to match its surroundings.[^1]

A number of lookout staff are recorded by name over the decades: Hans Miller in 1921, Frank Tower from 1917 to 1944, James Curry and E.A. Nelson in 1946, Jim Meyers from 1947 to 1948, John Sloss from 1949 to 1963, and Evelyn Sorheim in 1964.[^2]

The tower has needed upkeep over the years; Forest Service employees carried out a stabilization project on it near Deadwood in 2008, and its history notes the ongoing risk of vandalism to historic lookouts like this one.[^3]

Custer Peak Lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 1990.[^1] It remains standing today and is publicly accessible within the Black Hills National Forest, though it is not available as an overnight rental.[^1]
