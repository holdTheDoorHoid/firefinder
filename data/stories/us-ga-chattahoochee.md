The Chattahoochee Lookout Tower is on the Georgia tower list at tnlandforms.us, which places it in Chattahoochee County with an elevation of 612. The list gives no unit.[^1] OpenStreetMap has a tower point with the same name, and its elevation tag of 186 is about 610 feet if the tag is in meters, close to the list's figure.[^2]

A county wildfire plan dated December 2017 places a fire lookout tower at 123 Fire Tower Road in Cusseta-Chattahoochee County. It says the lookout is staffed seven days a week, and it names two fire tower operators assigned there.[^3] The plan gives no map position and does not use the list's name, so the sources do not say outright that the two describe the same structure.[^1][^3]

The county pays for forestry help under yearly agreements with the Georgia Forestry Commission.[^4][^5] In June 2024 the county approved a one-year agreement with the commission for help with detecting, preventing and fighting forest fires, with payments capped at $4,738.58.[^4] A year later it approved the same cap for fire-tower detection and prevention services from July 2025 to June 2026.[^5] Neither article names this tower or its address.[^4][^5]

The newest record I found that could relate to this tower is the June 2025 agreement, which ran through June 2026.[^5] No source describes the tower's condition today or whether visitors may climb it.[^3][^5] The plan describes it as staffed seven days a week in 2017, and the 2025 agreement provides for a fire-tower operator for the county, but neither shows how it is staffed now. Anyone curious about a visit should ask the Georgia Forestry Commission first.[^3][^5]
