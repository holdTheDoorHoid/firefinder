Indian Ridge, in the central Cascades of Oregon's Willamette National Forest, has held a fire lookout since 1918, when an open-sided cupola shelter went up on the ridge.[^1][^2] A decade later, in 1928, that gave way to a 10-by-10-foot "Firechaser" frame cabin topped with a 29-foot third-story observatory in the classic "cathedral style."[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says that cathedral-style structure stayed in service until 1958, firelookout.com gives 1928 for its construction and the newer tower's completion as 1958 as well; either way, the old cathedral tower wasn't maintained afterward, and no examples of it survive today.[^1]

The lookout that still stands now is a different structure: a 15-by-15-foot R-6 flattop cab with a catwalk, set on a 20-foot wooden tower. The National Historic Lookout Register dates it to 1957, while firelookout.com and the Recreation.gov listing both put its construction in 1958.[^1][^2][^3] Forest Service personnel staffed it for fire detection into the early 1970s; it went out of regular service in 1973, though it has since been used occasionally for emergency fire detection.[^1][^3]

The tower was later restored and brought into the Forest Service's cabin rental system.[^1] Today it's bookable through Recreation.gov. Getting there means a car trip up a rocky access road, followed by a narrow stairway rising 30 feet to the cabin; conditions inside are basic, so guests pack in most of their own supplies.[^3] From the ridge, Sand Mountain and Carpenter Mountain are visible with binoculars, and a short unmaintained trail leads out along the ridge through beargrass and huckleberries.[^3]
