Sheep Mountain Lookout stands on a windswept peak in the Bighorn National Forest, about 18 miles southwest of Buffalo, Wyoming, at roughly 9,600 feet.[^1][^2] It's a classic Rocky Mountain lookout: an L-4 cab on a ten-foot stone base, with a catwalk running around the cab.[^1][^2] Plans for it were approved in 1941, but the building wasn't finished until 1950.[^1] The Forest Service's own history of the Bighorn's lookouts gives the same 1950 completion date.[^5] A tourism listing and a Wyoming newspaper piece both credit the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it that year, but the CCC had been disbanded for about eight years by 1950, so that detail is worth treating with some caution rather than as settled fact.[^4][^6]

The lookout wasn't staffed every season. A fire-lookout reference site run by enthusiast Rex Kamstra lists its duty status as "Emergency," meaning it was watched only when fire danger was high rather than continuously.[^2] It kept watch over the surrounding forest until the early 1970s, when its fire-spotting role ended.[^4] It was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on September 1, 2000, as US 352 / WY 8 — one of only three fire lookouts still standing on the Bighorn, along with High Park and Black Mountain.[^1][^3]

The lookout still stands and remains Forest Service property.[^1][^3] It's rented out through Recreation.gov from June 15 to October 20, snow permitting, for about $50 a night.[^4][^6] It's bare-bones inside: one set of bunk beds, a vault toilet, no running water or electricity, and a view that reaches out to the Cloud Peak Wilderness and, on a clear day, Devils Tower.[^4] It's popular enough that bookings fill up months ahead.[^6]
