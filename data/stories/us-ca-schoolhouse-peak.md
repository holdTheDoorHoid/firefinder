The Schoolhouse Peak lookout stands on the Bald Hills ridge in Humboldt County, in what is now Redwood National and State Parks.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps erected its L-601 tower in 1940.[^1] A North Coast Journal writer who visited in 2020 gives 1941 as the construction date.[^3] A federal survey description written in 1942 calls the building a wooden structure about 30 feet high, and says it was still being completed when a 1941 survey station was occupied.[^2] The dates do not agree, and the sources we read do not settle which is right.

The register says the original cab, plan BC-301, was replaced by CDF with a 732-6A design, under a remodel plan numbered 962-2A.[^1] It adds that the cab change and other modifications have seriously diminished the structure's historic integrity.[^1] The register also gives 1976 alongside 1940 as build dates, but it does not say what happened that year.[^1]

The writer who visited in 2020 says the building has since been modernized from its wooden original and now has tall metal walls below an enclosed tower.[^3] The same account describes a man stationed at the lookout during that year's fire season, who told the writer the fires were merging and stood at 2,700 acres.[^3]

Forest Lookouts quotes a January 1965 report that the lookout had been broken into during a flood. Some food, a few tools and a lantern were reported missing.[^2]

Today the register lists the lookout as an interpretive stop for national park visitors.[^1] The 2020 writer rode a road bike up Bald Hills Road and walked the last stretch of loose gravel to the summit.[^3] We found no source that says whether the cab is open or what hours apply, so check with Redwood National and State Parks before going up.
