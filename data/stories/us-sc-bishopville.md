The Bishopville lookout was one of seven new steel fire towers that the State Commission of Forestry reported erecting in the year from July 1937 to June 1938.[^1] The same report credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building additional towers and telephone lines that year, free of charge to the state and to protective associations.[^1] It also lists a set of support buildings at five tower sites, Bishopville among them: a four-room dwelling, a barn, a smoke house, a chicken house and a latrine.[^1]

The lookout network was later wound down. A Post and Courier article says the state retired its towers in the early 1990s, and ties the closure of more than 100 towers around the state to a 1993 change at the Forestry Commission.[^2]

The Eastern US Lookouts page marks the Bishopville tower as removed. It gives no date and no account of how the tower came down.[^1]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the land is open to visitors.[^1]
