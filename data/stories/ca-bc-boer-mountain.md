Canada Lookouts places the Boer Mountain lookout in the Bulkley-Nechako area, at latitude 54.258 and longitude 125.683, with an elevation of 1,395 m (4,577 ft).[^1]

A December 1937 item on that page, quoting the Forest Branch News Letter, reports that two-way communication was set up in September that year between the Burns Lake ranger station and "the new Boer Mountain Lookout Station".[^1] The item credits Assistant Ranger Dick O'Hara with setting it up, and says he built the two home-made sets that were used.[^1] It adds that messages arrived with the clarity of a city telephone call.[^1]

A Lake District News item, which the page dates to May 8, 1985, reports that thieves broke into the B.C. Forest Service lookout on Boer Mountain between April 25 and 27 and took the station's radio, valued at more than $2,000.[^1] Police sources said an investigation into the incident was under way.[^1]

A wildfire-risk project on the mountain lists the Burns Lake Community Forest Ltd. as its organization.[^2] The project covers about 155 hectares that include the Burns Lake Mountain Bike Park, its trails and a small campground, and it says the area is heavily used by recreational hikers.[^2] Wikipedia also notes the trail network on Boer Mountain.[^3]

None of the sources I read says whether a tower still stands on the mountain, or whether the public may visit one.
