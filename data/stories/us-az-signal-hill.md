Signal Hill is a fire lookout inside Grand Canyon National Park in Coconino County, Arizona.[^1] The register says the National Park Service administers it.[^1] The park's own 2009 account says the Park Service constructed the tower in 1929, near Pasture Wash on the South Rim.[^2] The register also gives 1929 and describes a 35-foot Aermotor LS-40 tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1]

The two sources disagree about where it is. The register says the tower overlooks some of the most fire-prone areas on the North Rim, while the park's 2009 page places it near Pasture Wash on the South Rim.[^1][^2] This story follows the park's account, since the park built the tower and administers it.[^1][^2]

A passage quoted on the Forest Lookouts Arizona page, attributed to the Report of the Director of the National Park Service and dated 1926, says a telephone line was built from Pasture Wash Ranger Station to Signal Hill that year and that a fire lookout was established there.[^3] The sources read do not say whether that first lookout stood where the 1929 tower stands.

The park's 2009 page includes a transcript of a July 1995 interview with Roy Lemons, who manned the tower in 1937.[^2]

The park's 2009 page says all four of its lookout towers are inactive and ties that to a shift in fire control policy.[^2] The register says the tower has stopped being used to spot fires.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4] The sources read do not say whether visitors can reach or climb it. Because it sits inside a national park, ask the park before planning any visit, and stay away from the tower if access is closed.
