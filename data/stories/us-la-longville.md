The Longville Fire Tower stood along U.S. 171 in Beauregard Parish, Louisiana, near the town of Longville.[^1][^2]

A 2003 newspaper item says the tower had gone up a little more than 50 years earlier.[^2] That points to construction around 1950 or a little before. This is our estimate, not a recorded build date.

The lookout was a small room, seven feet on each side, roughly 100 feet off the ground.[^2] Mary Morrow worked there and watched for smoke from wildfires.[^2] The same item says hundreds of people went past her workplace every day for more than 30 years.[^2]

The state transportation department had taken the tower over from the forestry office about three or four years before the 2003 item, to prepare for the road work.[^2] The item said the tower was set to come down within weeks, to make way for widening the highway between Gillis and DeRidder to four lanes.[^2]

The Wikipedia list dates the teardown to about 2003, for the widening of U.S. 171 and U.S. 190. It says aerial imagery from 2003 still shows the tower clearly.[^1]

A Facebook group post, which we could read only as a headline, says the state removed the tower "for two lanes" of Highway 171S.[^3] The headline gives no year, so it does not settle when the tower came down, but it does name the state as the agency that removed it.

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site, or whether the public can reach it. Both the Wikipedia list and the Central US Lookouts page report the tower as gone.[^1][^2]
