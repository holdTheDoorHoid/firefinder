House Mountain is a fire lookout near Whitecourt, Alberta.[^2] In September 1938 the forestry branch of the Alberta department of lands and mines put four radio-equipped lookout stations into operation, at an estimated cost of $15,000, according to a Calgary Herald report that Canada Lookouts reproduces.[^1] One of the four stood at House Mountain, in the Whitecourt district. The report said the system was believed to be the first of its kind in Canada.[^1]

A later Alberta Historic Places article cites the House Mountain lookout as an example of a smaller square lookout set on a broad wooden tower base.[^2] Canada Lookouts' Alberta index files House Mountain under the Slave Lake Division instead, so the two sources name different administrative units for it.[^5]

In January 1952 John Givinn of Rimbey replaced Andy Craig, who had died, at the House Mountain tower.[^1] In March 1959 Leo Goulet was appointed lookout man there. The Peace River Record Gazette reported that he had taken a course for tower men at Grande Prairie in the week before the report.[^1]

A replacement steel tower went up in 1953.[^1][^2] Alberta's wildfire lookout layer gives 2015 as the year the House Mountain tower was constructed, with a tower height of 30.0 and no unit stated.[^3]

The same layer marks the lookout as active and records its access mode as drive.[^3] Alberta says observers are stationed at each of its 127 wildfire lookout sites, generally from April to October, and that boreal sites are typically far from neighbouring communities.[^4] No source we read describes the tower or the site as it stands today, and none describes a public visiting arrangement. Treat House Mountain as a working fire lookout, not a visitor site.
