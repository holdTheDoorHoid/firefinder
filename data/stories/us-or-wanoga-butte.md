Wanoga Butte lies in the Deschutes National Forest, about 16 miles southwest of Bend.[^1][^2] The Forest Service put a lookout on its summit in 1932: an L-4 cab with a hip roof and a catwalk, set on a 30-foot pole tower.[^1][^3] Local papers covered the work that summer.[^3] A July report named Paddy Ryan as the planned lookout, though another paper called him Pat Ryan.[^3] By August the man stationed there was Lee Smith, who would have to live in a tent for a month until the house on the tower was ready.[^3] Bears raided that tent twice in 48 hours.[^3]

In 1953 the original pine legs of the tower were replaced with tamarack.[^1][^3] The garage built in 1933 was hauled to Sparks Lake in 1950 and made into a cabin.[^1][^3] Charles Shaw, a lookout on the butte, helped a pilot find wild horses in a 1935 roundup by flashing a mirror at the plane.[^3] In 1948 W.T. Curtis split his duties between Wanoga Butte and the Tumalo Mountain lookout.[^3]

In 1994 an engineering firm inspected the tower. It advised taking off the lower stairs to restrict entry, and noted that some cab beams looked rotten.[^3] In July 2006 a crew of volunteers took the lookout down.[^1][^3] The cab was packed up and stored at the High Desert Museum, south of Bend.[^1][^2] The tower itself was taken apart, and its hardware was set aside for later restorations.[^1]

The plan was to rebuild the cab as a display at the museum once money was raised.[^3] One fire lookout page says that project had not happened.[^2] We found no later source confirming a rebuild, so the cab's present condition is unknown. No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the butte, and we found no information about public access to the site.
