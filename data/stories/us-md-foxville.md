In 1918 the state put up a lookout tower on Catoctin Mountain near Sand Flats, but an unknown arsonist burned its wooden cab that December.[^1] A steel tower planned a few miles farther north on the mountain, near the residence of Warden A.L. Hauver, was completed and put in operation in 1920 as the Foxville Tower.[^1] The sources do not say whether it replaced the Sand Flats lookout. They give its height as 47 feet in the original plan, 50 feet in a 1930 state report and 60 feet in a 1928 newspaper, so they disagree.[^1]

Alice Willard, of near Foxville, was appointed lookout there in 1928, and the Frederick News-Post reported that she was the only woman in Maryland in such a post.[^1] In March 1930, while keeping watch, she saw smoke going up near Foxville along the Smithsburg road. One paper thought children on their way home from Sunday school had set it alight by accident, and that they then put it out with little trouble.[^1] A March 1932 newspaper item described her as keeper of the Foxville tower, which had spotted many Washington County forest fires.[^1]

In 1951 the state finished a new 100-foot all-steel tower on the site of the old one, with a concrete base.[^1][^2] The 47-foot tower that had stood there was dismantled and set on top of the new structure, which rose 53 feet, for 100 feet in all.[^1] It was tied for the tallest lookout on the mountain with Salamander, which is also 100 feet.[^1]

Andrew H. Abraham was back on duty there in 1955.[^1] In 1971 a fire was called in from the Foxville watch tower.[^1]

In October 1976 the tower, owned by the Maryland Forest Service, fell in a strong gust of wind shortly after midnight.[^1][^2] No injuries were reported.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maryland list now marks the site as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, is left at the site today, or whether the public can visit it. The 1976 report puts the tower on Foxville Tower Road, with Catoctin Mountain National Park and Cunningham Falls State Park to its east and Foxville to its north.[^1] The 1951 tower had a concrete base, but no source says whether any of it survives.[^1] Ask the Maryland Forest Service before going to look for the spot.
