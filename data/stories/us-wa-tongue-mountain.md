Tongue Mountain rises at the north end of Juniper Ridge, in Lewis County, Washington.[^4][^1] It sits within the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1][^3]

A lookout went up on the summit in 1934: an L-5 cab, with a 10-by-10-foot shed built at the saddle between the two peaks.[^4] The register gives the same cab and shed, both put up on the site that year.[^1] Panorama photos were taken there on July 20, 1934.[^3] Willhite notes that a wildfire had caused massive destruction on the peak in 1918, and that the CCC cut many snags in the 1930s to keep lightning from striking them.[^4]

The lookout was abandoned in 1947.[^1][^2][^4] Willhite says it was reported gone the following year, in 1948.[^4] The register and FireLookout.com both describe it as gone today.[^1][^2]

Little is left to see on the summit.[^4] The trail's final 100 feet has crumbled away, and a campsite and fire pit sit on top.[^4] He had not come across a photograph of the lookout itself.[^4]

Willhite's directions start at Randle and follow a string of forest roads to a trailhead on FR 2904, at about 3,600 feet.[^4] From there the hike is close to 2 miles with 1,300 feet of climbing.[^4] Willhite considers the final 100-foot scramble suitable for experienced hikers and warns that beginners may find the exposure frightening.[^4] The summit is popular for its views, with the Cispus River valley and three volcanoes standing out.[^4]
