A regional directory of forest lookouts lists a tower named Brookgreen in Georgetown County, South Carolina, and marks it as removed.[^1] The name matches Brookgreen Gardens, a sculpture garden and nature preserve on Waccamaw Neck, but the directory does not say the tower stood on the gardens' grounds.[^1][^3]

For context, the South Carolina Forestry Commission's first fire tower went up near Camden in August 1930.[^2] Most sites covered roughly ten acres and included the tower and a house the operator lived in all year.[^2] Operators did not usually keep watch overnight.[^2] During moderate to high fire danger, operators had to climb after dark to look for glows.[^2]

The Forestry Commission shut its tower network down on October 1, 1993.[^2] A Post and Courier article reports that over 100 towers statewide were shut that year.[^4]

Brookgreen Gardens is described as the nation's first public sculpture garden.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains of a lookout at the site today.
