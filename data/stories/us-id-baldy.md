The peak that is now called Baldy was known as Salmon City Peak in its early years.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page files it under Lemhi County, and a 1913 Ogden Standard item put the lookout about nine miles by telephone line southwest of Salmon.[^2]

The earliest item I found is from 1912. The Lemhi Herald said the summit was the signal station of a fire guard, held by a son of W.F. Bradley.[^2]

In 1913 a new telephone line was being built from the top of Baldy toward a lookout station at the head of Hat Creek.[^2] That December, the Ogden Standard said the lookout had instruments for showing where a distant fire lay.[^2]

In August 1915 a ranger came to town from Baldy to report a small fire. Lightning had recently put the telephone out of commission, so the dispatch went by horseback.[^2]

A Salmon paper said in 1920 that a lookout man would soon take up work on Baldy.[^2]

A 1924 Salmon Forest report describes a 9-by-9-foot lookout building there that was not staffed that season, because other lookouts could see the surrounding country.[^2] A 1982 overview of the forest's lookouts says structures stood on Baldy by 1924, and that most of them were small, about 9 by 9 feet.[^1]

A later listing gives a 6-by-6-foot cab and camp from the 1930s. The sources do not say whether that was the same building as the 1924 one.[^3][^4]

Three listings give the abandonment as 1942, and two of them describe ruins.[^2][^3][^4]

A federal survey mark description, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, calls the summit station a dilapidated wooden shack, about six feet square, kept for emergency use as a lookout. The description is credited to the Coast and Geodetic Survey in 1945.[^2]

No source I read says what, if anything, remains on the summit today.[^2][^3][^4] One site labels the lookout "Drive-Up", but none says whether the public can reach it.[^4]
