Nooseneck Hill Fire Tower stands behind the West Greenwich fire station in Kent County, Rhode Island.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list of former lookout sites marks it as standing.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 60-foot International Derrick tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, first built in 1933 in Wickaboxet State Forest.[^1] Around 1950 it was moved to the fire station.[^1][^2] The register's summary box, though, lists 1950 as the build year, so the page disagrees with itself on that point.[^1]

The register says the tower has not been staffed since before 1950.[^1] A 1952-53 state report, quoted on a lookout history site, mentions a battery-operated set loaned to the West Greenwich fire company for the tower.[^4]

In 2005 a Forest Fire Lookout Association report said the eastern regional conference had been held in Rhode Island that summer. In the same passage it said a restoration grant had been presented to the tower's owners, the West Greenwich fire department, and that the tower was placed on the register.[^5] The register gives June 2, 2005 as the registration date.[^1] The report does not say what the grant paid for.[^5]

A 2016 visit report on the register says the tower, though not fenced, should not be climbed.[^1] A visitor wrote that people at the station said the wood has not been replaced in at least 25 years.[^1] The cab now holds radio equipment, and the fire station's siren sits on a platform in the middle of the tower.[^1]

In December 2024 the Forest Fire Lookout Association posted about the tower after the West Greenwich fire and rescue department shared a post showing the tower lit up and decorated behind the station.[^2] The register lists the West Greenwich Fire Department as its administrator, and the visit report says it should not be climbed.[^1]
