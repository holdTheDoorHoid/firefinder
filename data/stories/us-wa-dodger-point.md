Dodger Point Lookout is a small summit cabin in Olympic National Park, in Clallam County, Washington.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service built it in 1933, and horses packed the materials up to the summit.[^1][^3] The cab is 14 by 14 feet, an L-4 design.[^1]

During World War II the cabin was used as an Aircraft Warning Service station.[^1][^4] Records conflict on when that service ended. One source gives October 5, 1942, as the last day of service, in a 1944 report,[^3] while another passage names Leith and Mary Johnson as the lookouts in 1943.[^3]

The foundation had been failing for more than 15 years, and the previous winter's snow broke five windows.[^5] Crews rebuilt the foundation in 2017, replaced the siding and roof, and re-glazed the windows.[^1][^3][^6] Volunteers assisted with the work, which also received grant funding from Washington's National Park Fund and the REI Foundation.[^1] The lookout has been on the National Register of Historic Places since July 13, 2007.[^1][^4]

The Osborne fire finder and crank telephone are still inside.[^3] Visitors hike in, and the Long Ridge trail is the most accessible route.[^3] Sources give its length as 10 or 13 miles.[^3][^5] One source says the cabin is now used only for emergencies.[^3] Check with the park before planning a visit.
