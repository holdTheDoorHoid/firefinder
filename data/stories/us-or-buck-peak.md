Buck Peak sits in Klamath County, Oregon, within the Winema National Forest, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says its lookout was removed in 1968.[^1] A Forest Service letter from the summer of 1932 proposed a lookout on the peak and noted that about 100 large trees would have to come down to get the coverage the site offered.[^3] The Department of the Interior approved the plan that September.[^3] The tower that followed was 21 feet tall and carried a 14-by-14-foot cab with a hip roof.[^2][^3] The register instead describes a shorter, 10-foot wooden tower and a camp begun in 1915, and the two other sources do not match that account.[^1]

By 1941 the lookout was run from the Lake of the Woods district of the Rogue River National Forest, and Columbia Utilities Company supplied its telephone.[^2][^3] In 1943 lightning struck the tower while the lookout, Francis Bush, was eating dinner.[^2] The strike started a fire that grew to about an acre and a half before crews brought it under control.[^2]

In the early 1950s Jack and Lorraine Holmes lived on the peak with their young daughter.[^2] The site had no electricity, and the family relied on canned and dried foods.[^2] Jack drove to a nearby spring every other day for water.[^2] In 1960 a 19-year-old lookout, Laird Millburn, saw smoke from a fire in logging slash and reported it.[^2] About 40 men held the fire to half an acre.[^2]

The tower's end came in 1968.[^1] An October 30, 1968 memo from the Winema forest supervisor set a November burn.[^3] R.D.M.A. personnel did the burn, and a November memo said it would incur no cost to the project.[^2][^3] The register dates the removal to the same year.[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site now, or whether the public can reach it.
