Icicle Ridge's lookout began as a camp in 1914, about five miles west of Leavenworth in the Wenatchee National Forest.[^1][^2]

In October 1928 a newspaper account of a forest inspection trip said a big rock, blasted flat, was to become the foundation for the lookout house.[^3]

The cabin went up in 1929.[^1] Packers carried 48 loads of lumber up the steep trail by horse that year, and the building was a D-6 cupola cabin.[^4][^1]

Newspapers name a run of summer lookouts through the 1920s and 1930s.[^3] Ralph Koerner was the patrolman in 1924, A.G. Sharp was hired for the summer of 1926, and Frank Allman is named in 1933.[^3] In August 1932 John Nelson, a forest service lookout on the ridge, spotted a fire that burned two cottages in East Wenatchee.[^3] John Kjosness worked summers on the Leavenworth Ranger District from 1934 to 1939, at times as a lookout at several stations including this one.[^5] In 1936 he helped a carpenter build new stairs and a solid catwalk around the lookout house.[^5]

A 1940 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls the structure a tower, while other accounts describe a cabin.[^3][^1]

A 1963 photograph shows the lookout still staffed.[^1] FireLookout.com names Frank McWhirter in 1963 and Wayne Johnson in 1965, the last lookouts I found named.[^2]

The register says the cabin was removed three years after that photograph, which puts it in 1966.[^1] FireLookout.com says it was destroyed.[^2] WillhiteWeb says it lasted until 1966, and a photo blog says it was removed that year.[^4][^5]

None of the pages read describes footings or other remains on the summit today.[^4] The guide calls the summit a former lookout site with a panoramic view, and it mentions a bar that sticks out of the summit. A rope looped around it can serve as a handhold for the last few feet.[^4]

The hike is long. The guide lists a 6-mile route with 4,700 feet of gain, starting at the western edge of Leavenworth, where the paved Icicle Creek Road (Forest Road 76) runs 9.6 miles to the trailhead.[^4] The Fourth of July Creek Trail climbs to the ridge, and the route then follows the Icicle Ridge Trail to the summit rocks.[^4] The guide says the average hiker can get to within 20 feet of the summit, and a short rock climb reaches the top.[^4] No closure is described in the pages I read.
