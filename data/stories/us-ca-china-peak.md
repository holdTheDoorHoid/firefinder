The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says China Peak was completed in 1937 as a California Region 5 cabin on an enclosed timber tower, on the Klamath National Forest in Siskiyou County.[^1] West Lookouts gives a later date, 1939, when a Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Seiad finished the lookout.[^2] A 1952 survey-station description, reproduced on the same page, puts the station about six miles northeast of Happy Camp and calls the lookout a standard 14-foot square, about 25 feet high.[^2]

A December 1928 item, also reprinted there, says airmail pilots regularly dropped papers and magazines to the observer at a China Mountain lookout.[^2] If that item describes this lookout, it predates the register's 1937 date.[^1][^2] Mrs. George Souza worked the lookout for several summers in the early 1960s, based in Seiad Valley.[^2]

The register says the lookout was sold off as surplus and taken apart in 1972.[^1] West Lookouts says it was bought at auction and removed, and that its parts were recycled into a nearby cabin.[^2]

An OpenStreetMap entry still carries the name China Peak Lookout, but it gives no date and does not describe what is standing.[^3] No source read describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.
