Grizzly Mountain Lookout is in Curry County, a few miles from Gold Beach. The sources disagree on the distance: a 1937 newspaper says about five miles back from town, a 1942 article says about four miles east, and firelookout.com says two miles southeast.[^2][^3]

The first station went up in 1936 on a ten-foot tower.[^2][^3] A June 1937 newspaper called it new, said its view was among the finest in Curry County, and noted that it was built the previous fall.[^2] The NHLR dates the original lookout to 1937 instead.[^1]

In 1937 M.S. Brainard of Gold Beach was assigned to the station, but he was recalled after a few days when rain eased the fire danger.[^2] In 1938 the lookout spotted a small boat adrift in heavy seas, and a Coast Guard rescue followed.[^2] Staffing records are thin after that, although a 1941 paper reports that a man named Parker McNeil was sent there that season.[^2]

The tower did not last.[^2] A December 1941 windstorm wrecked the station, and a February 1942 article places the storm in late December.[^2] The NHLR puts the storm in 1942, and a March 1942 article calls it a windstorm "last January," so the sources do not agree on the date.[^1][^2] A 12-by-12 ground cabin that cost $250 replaced it in 1942.[^2] During the Second World War the site was an Aircraft Warning Service post, activated on March 9, 1942.[^1][^2]

A 14-by-14 lookout house was built in 1952, and one mile of new road made the site reachable by car.[^1][^2] The 1952 Oregon Department of Forestry annual report lists $810 for labor and $878.33 for materials.[^2] In 1980 a 10-by-10 concrete block building was added for radio and microwave equipment.[^2] Firelookout.com says the 1952 cab was later turned into an enclosed radio building.[^3]

The site joined the National Historic Lookout Register on June 23, 2022, as US 1619, OR 142.[^1] The NHLR lists the Coos Forest Protection Association as administrator, while firelookout.com calls the cab an Oregon Department of Forestry cab.[^1][^3] None of the pages read say whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^3] Because the cab is now a radio building, anyone interested should check with the agency before going, and should not try to climb or enter the structure.[^3]
