The U.S. Forest Service built a fire tower on South Mountain, in Ripton, Addison County, Vermont, in 1941.[^1][^2] An April 1941 Rutland Daily Herald item said the tower, 41 feet tall, was nearly finished.[^1] It was made of prefabricated timber, protected with creosote against porcupines, and its top was a glassed-in deck 14 feet square.[^1] An October 1941 Brandon Union item put the finished tower just south of Little Notch.[^1]

A 1943 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls the tower wooden and about 50 feet high.[^1] That disagrees with the 41 feet given in the 1941 newspaper.[^1]

A report dated January 19, 1951, says the wind totally destroyed the South Mountain lookout tower in Ripton.[^1] No source I read says a new tower went up on the site.

The peak was renamed for the poet.[^1][^3] Forest Lookouts dates the rename to 1953.[^1] A historical marker for the Robert Frost wayside says Vermont named a mountain after Frost in 1955, so the two sources differ.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the South Mountain tower in Addison County as gone.[^2] No source I read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit, or whether the summit is open to visitors.

On Vermont Route 125, just east of Ripton, the Robert Frost Interpretive Trail is a roadside wayside.[^4] Signs at several points along the trail print about six of Frost's poems.[^4] A historical marker there describes the site as a recreation area set aside in memory of Frost.[^3]
