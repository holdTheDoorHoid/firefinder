Mount Agassiz is a peak in the town of Bethlehem, New Hampshire, and Wikipedia places the town in Grafton County.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association and the Eastern US Lookouts page both list the mountain in Coos County, so the sources disagree on the county.[^2][^3]

White Mountain History says New Hampshire began funding fire lookouts in 1909. It names Mount Agassiz among the summits where a hotel or viewing platform and phone lines were already in place.[^4]

A 1910 report, quoted on the Eastern US Lookouts page, says the telephone lines were already installed and the only expense was a watchman.[^3] On August 30 of that year, a fire set to clear land was seen from Mount Agassiz, twelve miles away. The forest fire warden arrived within two hours, and the report says the fire caused no particular damage.[^3]

A 1930 newspaper item says W. B. Phillips, who owned the land around the summit, had an automobile road built to the top.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Agassiz tower as gone.[^2] The summit is now the location of an observatory built around the site of the old fire tower, according to NH Family Hikes.[^5]

NH Family Hikes says the summit is privately owned and that the owner welcomes hikers. The route follows a private road, which the guide says is hard to find.[^5] A May 2026 hiking blog describes a communication tower near the top, and a stone building that surrounds another communication tower.[^6]
