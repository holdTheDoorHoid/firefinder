The Homestead tower stands about 3.7 miles southeast along U.S. 127 from its junction with U.S. 70 in Crossville, in Cumberland County.[^1] The tower's association, whose president is Brenda King, dates its construction to 1938 and ties it to Franklin Roosevelt’s New Deal.[^2] A photo listing dates the tower to 1937 and 1938 and gives an unlabeled figure of 15 feet.[^1]

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched. A 2014 National Register study of Tennessee Division of Forestry fire towers lists a Homestead tower in Cumberland County among the towers still standing that March.[^3] The association's account and the photo listing do not mention fire watching, and we could not confirm that they describe the same structure.

An April 30, 2025 article reports that the Cumberland County Commission voted unanimously to donate the tower to the Tennessee State Parks system, which plans to run it as a museum.[^2] King said the handover would be a long process and hoped it would be finished within a year.[^2] We found no report that the transfer has been completed.

A listing with a photo from 2008 notes an admission charge.[^1] We could not confirm current hours or prices, so check with the museum or the state park before a visit.
