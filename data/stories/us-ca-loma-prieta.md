The Loma Prieta lookout stood on the summit of Loma Prieta, at 3,795 feet in Santa Clara County.[^1]

The first station went up in 1927 as a wooden tower.[^1] The Monterey Bay Redwood company donated the lumber.[^2] The state forestry commission promised to pay a full-time lookout through the fire season.[^2]

A 1933 Santa Cruz newspaper reported a 60-foot tower completed on the summit, along with living quarters.[^2] Before that, the report said, the lookout had been forced to live in a tent, and James Sharp of Burwell was to be in charge.[^2] The same report called it the first tower ever placed on Loma Prieta, which does not square with the 1927 accounts.[^2]

In October 1934 a plan called for a 35-foot steel tower and said the old wooden tower would be dismantled.[^2] In late 1934 the Division of Forestry leased an acre of land on Loma Prieta from the Monterey Bay Redwood Company and said a new station would replace the outdated structure.[^2] The register describes the 1934 structure as a US Forest Service BC-301 cabin on a 30-foot enclosed steel tower.[^1]

James Sharpe was the lookout at the station in April 1934.[^2] In December 1935 the tower shut for the winter.[^2]

The register says the lookout may have been removed around 1976.[^1]

A recent state notice about fuel clearing describes a Loma Prieta communication tower complex, and it names Mount Umunhum Road and Loma Prieta Road as roads near the work.[^3] No source we read says what, if anything, of the 1934 lookout remains, or whether the summit is open to visitors.
