Granite Peak is a lookout site in the Gila National Forest, Grant County, New Mexico.[^1] Two firemen stationed on the peak put up a small tower in 1917, and a lookout cab was built on the old tower in 1923.[^1]

In 1930 a steel tower and frame cabin went up, the cabin serving as living quarters for the lookout.[^1] Materials were carried 25 miles, by way of the trail up Big Turkey Creek.[^1]

A 1965 U.S. Geological Survey station description, reproduced on the same page, puts a survey marker 50.7 feet (15.5 m) northwest of a Forest Service steel lookout tower.[^1] That suggests the steel tower was still standing then.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list has a Grant County Granite Peak tower at 33.19188, -108.37464, marked as gone.[^2] The list matches by name and county, not by register number, so the link to this peak is likely but not confirmed.

No source I could read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1]
