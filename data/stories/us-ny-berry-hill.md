Berry Hill rises to 1,960 feet in the town of McDonough, Chenango County, New York, just west-northwest of Norwich.[^1][^2][^3] Wikipedia gives the height as 1,962 feet.[^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps put up the tower in 1934, using a structure the federal Forest Service had given New York State.[^2][^3] It was sited to keep an eye on state land that had been replanted and was vulnerable to fire.[^2] Observers first staffed it in 1935.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it the second fire tower in New York that the Corps erected.[^1] The tower stands 59 feet 3 inches tall and is an International Derrick design.[^2]

Berry Hill was an active fire lookout through the 1988 season.[^3] Wikipedia dates the official closure to early 1989.[^2] The Register recorded it on January 4, 1993, on a nomination by Edwin Pierce, a regional ranger with the state environmental agency.[^1] A 1999 management plan for the surrounding state forest named public access as an objective for the tower.[^3]

A June 16, 2017 visit report on the Register says the visiting team could not climb the tower that day because it had no key, so most of the tower could not be inspected.[^1] The same report notes that volunteers maintain the property and had already fixed up the observers' cabin.[^1] It also lists plans to remove the communications equipment and restore the tower.[^1]

A $125,000 restoration, paid for through NY Works, removed all communications equipment from the tower.[^3] The work also added safety fencing and metal grate treads on the stairwell, repainted the whole tower, and fitted new windows in the cab.[^3] Work on the observers' cabin replaced its roof and redid its porch and siding.[^3] The tower reopened to the public on September 13, 2021.[^2][^3]

The state says that, of the fire towers on public land in Central New York, only Berry Hill is open for recreation.[^3] A stone-dust path, which the state calls accessible, runs from the upper parking area to the base.[^3] The grounds also have a picnic table, a bench and an information kiosk.[^3] The fire tower is open to the public year-round, from sunrise to sunset.[^3]
