The tower was already standing when the Coast and Geodetic Survey described it in 1950.[^1] The survey describes a steel tower standing on four legs, roughly 112 feet tall and topped by a glassed-in observation cab, and places the station about five and three-quarter miles east and three-quarters of a mile south of Plain Dealing.[^1] The survey name ends in "LFC", which most likely stands for the Louisiana Forestry Commission.[^1]

In October 1974 the Times reported that Mrs. Margaret Little kept watch from the tower while her husband Lewis was sent to combat the blazes she had seen.[^1]

By October 1984 the tower was unstaffed because staff had quit or retired, but no official closure had been made. The associate state forester, W. D. Mercer, said the tower would resume operations once staff could be hired or reassigned to it.[^1] The same report said future plans would shift detection away from continuous tower watches toward more spotting from the air.[^1]

A tower list revised in 2011 still gives a Plain Dealing Lookout Tower at about 32.897 north and 93.603 west, in Bossier Parish.[^2] No source I found describes the tower or the site today, or says whether visitors may go there.
