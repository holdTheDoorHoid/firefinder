Crane Mountain, in Lake County, Montana, had a Forest Service lookout on the Flathead National Forest, about seven miles southeast of Bigfork.[^1] One listing dates the cabin to 1926 and places it below the summit, about 1.25 miles northeast of the highest point.[^1]

A forestry road toward the lookout was being cleared in 1937, starting from the Tykeson place.[^2] In December 1950 the Plum Creek Logging Co. of Columbia Falls started building that road.[^2] A 1951 Daily Inter Lake item said many sightseers had made their way up the mountain that summer, and that only a short hike from the trail was needed to reach the lookout.[^2]

During World War II the Flathead had women working four of its lookouts, Crane Mountain among them.[^3] In July 1955 the Flathead Courier reported that the Crane lookout was not staffed that summer, and that planes were spotting fires in the area.[^2] By 1957 the Daily Inter Lake said the lookout was no longer maintained and was patrolled from the air daily, sometimes twice.[^2]

A 1956 Coast and Geodetic Survey description places the survey station within a small white house on a bare rock outcrop.[^2]

In the spring of 1972 partygoers ripped apart an old Forest Service outlook on the mountain, the Daily Inter Lake reported that August.[^2] The article quotes a man who said it would be burned soon, because it was ruined.[^2] The article uses the word outlook and does not say whether it means this cabin. firelookout.com lists the cabin as destroyed in 1972.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as gone.[^4] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it, so check with the Flathead National Forest before going near it.
