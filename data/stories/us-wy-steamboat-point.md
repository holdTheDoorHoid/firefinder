Steamboat Point is a rock formation in Sheridan County, Wyoming, on the Bighorn National Forest.[^1][^5] Its name comes from a resemblance: viewed from the south or southeast, the rock looks like a steamboat's bow.[^5]

Civilian Conservation Corps workers built a fire tower on the point.[^3] A National Register of Historic Places continuation sheet on Depression-era federal projects in Wyoming lists Steamboat Point among the CCC accomplishments on the Bighorn National Forest, but it gives no date for that work.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wyoming list files Steamboat Point among its unknown/undocumented sites, not among its lookout structures. It says a site moves to the lookout lists only if evidence shows it was used as a regular wildland fire lookout.[^2]

The point is listed as a day hike.[^4] Sheridan County's guide to its favorite day hikes describes it as a moderate, steep trail of 1.7 miles round trip, with 360-degree views from the top.[^4] SummitPost says the route to the top is a Class 1 trail that circles the rock, and calls it fairly easy.[^5]

No source I could read describes what, if anything, remains of the fire tower on the point today.
