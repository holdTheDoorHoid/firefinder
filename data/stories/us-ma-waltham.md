Waltham's Prospect Hill had a 58-foot fire tower built on city land, and four communities each gave $900 toward it.[^1] Wikipedia dates a fire tower on Big Prospect to 1917.[^2]

In 1942 two-way radios were installed.[^1] In October 1942 a civilian aircraft spotter's carelessness with the tower stove caused the cab to burn.[^1] It was rebuilt over the winter of 1942 to 1943.[^1]

Wikipedia says the U.S. Air Force used Big Prospect for research from 1951.[^2] After it erected a large dish in 1963, the fire tower had to move to Little Prospect.[^2]

A new 68-foot steel tower was built in 1964, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites page calls it the last one standing.[^1] A chain-link fence surrounded it, and wire mesh covered its windows.[^1] Its use ended after the 1977 fire season.[^1]

A 2007 chapter report says the state sold the closed Waltham tower to a private buyer, and that the tower was moved to Tennessee.[^3] None of the sources we read names the Tennessee site, so where that structure stands now is not known from them.[^3]

A community photo page says the military antennas came down in the 1990s, and that a tower on the hill is now the repeater site of the Waltham Amateur Radio Association.[^4] Wikipedia says the relocated fire tower later carried that club's equipment.[^2] Those accounts do not line up cleanly with the report that the closed tower went to Tennessee. No source we read describes any remains of the lookout's base on the hill.[^2][^3][^4]
