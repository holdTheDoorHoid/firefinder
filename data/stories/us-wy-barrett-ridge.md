The lookout tower on Barrett Ridge was finished in 1912. A newspaper report from that October says the Medicine Bow forest supervisor oversaw its construction, and that with its completion the forest had four towers able to spot fires anywhere in it. Each tower was tied to the supervisor's office and the ranger headquarters by telephone.[^1]

In July 1918 a fire lookout station was maintained on the ridge, and a Forest Service telephone line reached a high point there. A family camping on Barrett Creek used that line to call several local people.[^1]

In 1921 district rangers covered a set of backup lookout sites during the worst fire periods, and Barrett Ridge was one of them.[^1]

A 1930 Casper newspaper item, quoted in a 2022 blog post, says towers were then in place on Barrett Ridge, at Long Lake and on Bridger Peak, so that temporary observers could be posted at those points in hazardous conditions.[^2]

Around 1949 the lookout's duties were moved to Slash Ridge.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association now lists Barrett Ridge as a tower that is gone.[^3] The site lies about 15 miles northeast of Riverside in Carbon County, at 9,062 feet.[^4] No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.
