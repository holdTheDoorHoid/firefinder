Triangulation Peak sits in the Willamette National Forest, about 11 miles east of Detroit, Oregon.[^3] In April 1931 a news item, reprinted on a lookout history page, said the peak would be one of eleven new emergency lookout stations that year.[^2] The lookout house went up in 1933 as a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab.[^1][^2][^3] The register says the U.S. Forest Service built it, while the lookout history page credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with the construction.[^1][^2]

A 1934 newspaper item, quoted on the same history page, says Ed Schroeder worked the peak as a Forest Service lookout all summer, then went on to Mill City ahead of fall registration at Oregon State College.[^2]

The register and the lookout history page both say the house was removed in 1967.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year, so the sources differ on how the house ended.[^3]

No source we read describes what remains on the summit today. A hiking site describes the top as the old lookout location, reached by a 2.1-mile hike, and says the vista from it is outstanding.[^5] The Forest Service trailhead page gives the route: follow Triangulation Trail about 1.5 miles to the junction with the Triangulation Peak trail.[^4] The trailhead is also the entry point for the Mount Jefferson Wilderness, where two kinds of entry permit apply from June 15 to October 15.[^4]

The same trailhead page marks the site as closed, under a closure order for the Beachie Creek and Lionshead fires.[^4] It gives no end date, so check with the Detroit Ranger District before you plan a visit.[^4]
