Pony Peak was watched long before it had a cabin. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says a phone line reached the peak by 1912, and a photo caption records a 1925 temporary site.[^1] A December 1924 notice called for new wire on the emergency line to Pony Peak Lookout, and a 1928 record describes it as a secondary lookout, manned in emergencies, though it says it should be a primary one.[^2]

The first structure came in 1934, a Region 5 cabin on an enclosed timber tower, and a CCC crew completed it that year.[^1][^2] The sources do not agree on what stood before then. The 1924 and 1928 notes call the peak a lookout, while the register says the 1934 cabin was the first structure.[^1][^2]

The register says the peak was staffed each year through 1940, again from 1955 to 1960, and used intermittently until 1970.[^1] In 1952 a road to the lookout was under construction.[^3]

The register does not record when the cabin was removed, and it reports only a foundation at the site now.[^1] No source here describes the site beyond that foundation, or says whether the peak is open to visitors.
