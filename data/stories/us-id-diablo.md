Diablo Lookout stands in the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness on the Clearwater National Forest, in Idaho County, Idaho.[^1][^2] The register and firelookout.com both give its elevation as 7,461 feet; Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 7,641.[^1][^2][^3]

The first building on the site was a cabin in 1926, though sources differ on its type.[^1][^2][^3] The register describes a gable-roofed L-4 ground cab built that year, while firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts describe a cabin in 1926 that was replaced with an L-4 cab in 1930.[^1][^2][^3] The present cab is an R-6 design, built in 1965 on a 10-foot concrete base and replacing the older L-4.[^1][^2][^3] Keeping Watch describes the R-6 cab as a 15-by-15-foot design with a flat roof that reaches over the deck for shade.[^4]

Staffing has been by volunteers in recent years.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Steve Kalling in 1992 and Nate Raff, a volunteer, in 2021.[^3] Keeping Watch says lookouts staff the tower during peak fire season, and it features an interview with Bill Moore, who now runs the volunteer program.[^4] Firelookout.com says the cab has been used on an emergency basis since the early 1980s, a detail the other sources do not confirm.[^2]

The pages read name no specific fire spotted from the tower.

The register recorded the lookout on April 24, 2009, on a nomination by Gary Weber.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts says the tower was restored a few years before its 2021 article, and that a memorial cross hangs inside for a young man who died on the mountain in 1995.[^3]

Diablo is a hike-in lookout.[^3] A 2013 trip report describes an 11.5-mile out-and-back route with about 1,700 feet of climbing, starting from the Big Sand Lake trailhead near Elk Summit, and recommends July through October.[^5] The road ends at Elk Summit Recreation Area on the edge of the wilderness.[^5] The register lists the Powell Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] The sources do not say whether visitors may go inside the cab, so check with the district before planning a trip.
