Keller Peak, in the San Bernardino National Forest, rises to about 7,845 feet, and the National Historic Lookout Register calls its lookout the first one registered in California.[^1] The station went up in 1926.[^1][^2] The register's best guess is that the original was a Region 5 4AR cabin on a 20-foot open steel tower, and it says a Region 5 BC-301 cab replaced the original cab in 1954.[^1][^2]

A San Bernardino County newspaper report from November 1926 said the station was expected to be finished before winter snows arrived. The same report said a trail the foresters had spent six weeks completing was being used to pack in building materials.[^3]

In July 1929 the lookout, K. F. Smith, had to leave his post when flames crept up the west slope. Flames surrounded the building, but it survived.[^3]

Newspaper coverage from June 1954 said the old tower had been torn down and a new one was being set up. That account does not match the register's cab-only swap that same year.[^2][^3]

By 1985 the Forest Service had discontinued the lookout, and volunteers took over the watch.[^3]

The register listed the site in November 1991, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register added its record in January 2026.[^1][^2]

Southern California Mountains Foundation volunteers staffed the lookout during fire season and opened it to visitors when someone was on duty.[^1][^4]

The lookout's run ended in September 2024, when the Line Fire destroyed it. Hundred Peaks reports only the steel framework is left.[^1][^4] The register says rebuilding discussions are underway, though that statement carries no date. I found no later report on whether a rebuild is funded.[^1]

Keller Peak Road reaches the summit, and Hundred Peaks calls it a drive-up, though a closed gate adds several miles.[^4] The summit also holds a radio transmitter site.[^4] The lookout itself is gone, so there is no cab to visit.[^4]
