The Civilian Conservation Corps built Park Point Lookout in 1939, and a December 1939 park newsletter says the station was completed that August.[^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a rustic design intended to fit the summit: a 17-foot octagonal floor plan built of native sandstone from Chapin Mesa, hand-shaped to echo the park's other Pueblo Revival buildings.[^1] The lookout sits at the highest point in Mesa Verde National Park, which the register puts at 8,571 feet and the park puts at 8,572 feet.[^1][^4]

The 1939 park notes rate the station among the best of its type, and describe it as another link in the fire suppression chain.[^3] Sleeping and cooking space inside the lookout meant the observer was always on hand.[^3] The observer phoned the fire dispatcher six times a day under ordinary conditions.[^3] When a smoke was sighted, the dispatcher checked visibility maps to see whether another lookout could also see it; a second report of the same smoke from another tower let the dispatcher place the fire on the map.[^3]

Like many lookouts of its generation, the building gradually wore down from exposure and too little annual maintenance.[^2] The park decided to restore it with park funds, and a dedication on June 10, 2009 marked both the finished work and the lookout's listing on the National Historic Lookout Register.[^2][^1]

The register's July 4, 2009 visit found the lookout unstaffed that day.[^1] Rangers said that a wet spring and summer had kept fire danger low, so it might not be staffed that season.[^1] The 2009 account said staffing would continue through the summer, with public access during those months.[^2]

Today the National Park Service says the park still uses the lookout during fire season to pass along updates on lightning, weather and fires.[^4] The road up is not plowed in winter, which limits access then.[^4] The overlook has parking, a trailhead and a vault toilet.[^4] A 2019 condition note in the register said the cab was in good shape, though the walkway around the lookout could use paint and repair.[^1]
