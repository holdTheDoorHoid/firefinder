Grouse Ridge Lookout stands in the Tahoe National Forest in Nevada County, California, at about 7,650 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates it to 1923 and counts it among six surviving 4AR cabs in California.[^1] The 14-by-14-foot cab has a steep roof and five windows on each wall, and the Register says the design came before the L-4 model.[^1]

A 1915 newspaper item reported that the forest planned another primary lookout, most likely on Grouse Ridge or Fall Creek Mountain, to cover upper Yuba headwaters that Banner Mountain did not reach.[^2] The sources read do not show that this plan produced the lookout built later.

In 1925 a newspaper item named Charles Bone as the Forest Service lookout at the station.[^2] In 1943 a district ranger reported storm damage to the lookout and said the road was still blocked by snow and down timber.[^2]

The Register added the lookout on June 4, 1998, as US 283.[^1] A 2009 visit report recommended maintenance soon and noted damage where people had tried to get in.[^1] The same report said it appeared to be used for some radio equipment and was otherwise unstaffed.[^1] The Register's condition entry also marks the lookout as needing paint.[^1]

Restoration talks began in the fall of 2009.[^4] Late snow kept work restricted until August 2010, and that year volunteer contractors completed hantavirus and lead paint remediation.[^4] A 2011 report says the lookout would be restored to its 1924 version rather than its 1964 version, with the radio equipment removed.[^4]

By 2023 district recreation staff had done most of the remaining work, and the project was listed as near completion.[^3] Rental approval stalled, however. A lapse in a recreation advisory committee's approval means new recreation rental facilities cannot be approved or changed.[^3]

The Forest Service's camping page says the forest has nine historic fire lookouts, two of them available to rent, and does not name Grouse Ridge.[^5] A visitor guide last updated in June 2026 says only exterior viewing is allowed unless the lookout is officially opened or listed for rental.[^6]

The final six miles of Grouse Ridge Road are unimproved dirt, and the guide urges a high-clearance vehicle.[^6] The 2009 visit report described the same road as bumpy but easily driven in two-wheel drive, so the sources disagree, and conditions should be checked before going.[^1][^6]

The sources read do not say what condition the tower is in today. Visitors should stay outside the structure and check conditions with the Tahoe National Forest before setting out.
