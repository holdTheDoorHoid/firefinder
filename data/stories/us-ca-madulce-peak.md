Madulce Peak rises in the Santa Barbara backcountry, where a Forest Service page on the Dick Smith Wilderness says it tops a knot of mountains.[^4] The lookout on the peak dates to 1934, when the Santa Barbara National Forest had it built to California Region 5 Plan BC-301: a timber tower 10 feet tall with an open design.[^1][^2] By the end of that August a trail had been completed to it, the Santa Maria Daily Times reported.[^2]

The register says the 1934 lookout replaced an earlier structure that fire destroyed in September 1932.[^1] That month a week-old fire swept north toward the peak, and the Oxnard Daily Courier first reported that the lookout had been destroyed.[^2] Its keeper, Edward Decker, had left the post as the blaze drew near.[^2] The same paper said the next day that the reports of the lookout burning were mistaken.[^2] The register's account and that newspaper correction do not agree.[^1][^2]

In 1972 a windstorm took the roof off the lookout, and its walls then collapsed.[^1] The register says the rest of the structure was removed afterward.[^1] A PeakVisor summit page instead says the old lookout was burned down in the 1970s, so the cause of its loss is disputed.[^3]

Today the register places the site within the Los Padres National Forest.[^1] The Dick Smith Wilderness was set up in 1984 under the California Wilderness Act and covers 64,700 acres.[^4] A 2018 trip report describes foundations of an old lookout on the summit.[^5] The same writer described the trail as faint in most places, with the switchbacks near the top marked mainly by cairns and flagging tape.[^5] No other source read describes the summit now, and no recent survey or photo was found.
