Benham Hill, in Pope County, Illinois, sits inside the Shawnee National Forest.[^1] A Forest Lookouts entry dated c.1938 describes a tower there: a 40-foot telephone pole fitted with a ladder and a crow's nest. The entry says the tower served as a supplementary observation post.[^1]

A historical marker on the Shawnee attributes frequent forest fires in southern Illinois to lightning, field-clearing practices and sparks from passing trains.[^2] Soon after the forest was formed in 1933, a fixed-point detection system went up to spot and respond to emergencies, and fire towers were a core part of it.[^2] In the Shawnee area, Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) crews built sixteen fire towers between 1934 and 1939.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the hill today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
