The Joe B. Shirley Fire Tower was built in 1968 by the Alabama Forestry Commission to replace a wooden lookout from the 1940s.[^1][^3] It is an Aermotor model 100 feet tall, with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and Alabama Living says it is made of galvanized steel.[^1][^2]

It stood at Monte Sano State Park in Huntsville.[^2] A historical marker treats fire towers as emblems of forest conservation, even though aircraft have taken over their original job.[^3] The Register listed the tower on October 18, 2006, on a nomination from Jane Barr of the Monte Sano Historical Association.[^1]

None of the sources read say who staffed the tower or which fires it watched. The Register and the marker both say NASA used the lookout in the 1950s to log telemetry from the Sputnik and Explorer satellites.[^1][^3] That predates the 1968 tower, so the claim most likely refers to the earlier wooden lookout, though no source says so.

For years the tower was a landmark at the park, but visitors could not climb it.[^2] Huntsville businessman Billy Claunch heard it was for sale and set out to buy it.[^2] Alabama Living reported in September 2025 that it had been taken down and was being rebuilt at Fire Tower Camp near Crane Hill in Cullman County.[^2] The historical marker itself is listed in a marker database as being in storage.[^3]

Fire Tower Camp, which rents cabins and a treehouse on Smith Lake, now describes the restored tower as a 109-foot climb.[^4][^5] The Register gives the height as 100 feet, so the two figures disagree, and the sources do not explain whether the rebuild changed the height.[^1][^4] Alabama Living expected the work to be finished in late 2025 or early 2026, but the sources do not confirm an opening date.[^2] Ask the camp about hours, fees and conditions before you go, and do not climb the tower unless the camp says it is open.[^5]
