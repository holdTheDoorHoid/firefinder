Mount Artaban is a summit on Gambier Island, at 614 m (2,015 ft).[^1][^2] From the peak, the view takes in Howe Sound.[^4]

The Forest Service fire lookout on the summit was established in 1957, according to the reserve plan, which gives that date on the strength of a personal communication from John Parminter.[^1] The plan adds that it was probably one of the first Forest Service fire towers, prefabricated off-site and flown in by helicopter as pre-assembled panels.[^1]

The reserve had burned decades before the tower went up: a human-caused fire that ignited on July 9, 1922 burned over the whole reserve.[^3]

The plan says the date of the tower's last use is not known, though it was probably around 1970.[^1]

The plan's text says the tower has come down, with its footings and an iron hook still in place.[^1] Its survey table, by contrast, says the lookout has since been removed.[^1]

A copy of the plan on doczz.net describes a tower that collapsed years ago and left timber and scraps of glass and metal behind, and it recommends clearing up the remains.[^3] No tower stands on the summit now.[^1]

The reserve includes the summit.[^3] In June 2008 the province transferred the land to the Islands Trust Fund under its Free Crown Grant program, for management as a nature reserve.[^3]

Hikers reach the summit on foot from public docks at Camp Fircom, Halkett Bay Marine Park or Camp Artaban, and the walk from Camp Fircom takes about two hours.[^4] There are no campsites. Visitors should pack in and pack out, and keep to the trail and out of private property.[^4]
