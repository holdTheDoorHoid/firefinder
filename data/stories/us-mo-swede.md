The Swede lookout was in Pulaski County, Missouri, and its agency was the Missouri Conservation Commission.[^1] The Central US Lookouts page gives the land description as 37N-13W-21.[^1] It quotes the commission's annual report for fiscal 1948-49, which reports that a 17.6-acre towersite was obtained.[^1]

According to the Missouri Department of Conservation's history of its towers, lookout towers were the best way to spot fires for decades.[^2] Before the commission was formed in 1936, the article adds, blazes frequently ran unchecked through the Missouri Ozarks.[^2]

The article counts 15 towers owned by the state in 1941 and 102 operated by the state in 1966, when the U.S. Forest Service ran 43 more in Missouri.[^2] The state's towers stood between 60 and 120 feet tall, and the article notes they sat on high ground, where they offered a 360-degree view of the land around them.[^2]

No source I could open describes what remains at the Swede site today, or whether the public can reach it.
