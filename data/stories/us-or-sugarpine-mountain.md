The tower that stands on Sugarpine Mountain first stood at Fort Klamath.[^1] The register says it was built there in 1958 as a 20-foot open steel tower with a 13-by-13 cab on top.[^1] In 1969, 32 hours of road and lookout site construction were logged at Sugarpine Mountain.[^2] In 1970 the tower and cab were airlifted from Fort Klamath to the summit.[^2][^3]

Sources differ on how far back the site goes.[^1][^3] The firelookout.com page says the summit was first used as a camp lookout in the 1930s, and that its first permanent structure arrived in 1970.[^3] The register, by contrast, dates the structure itself to 1958, before its move.[^1]

In 1973 the Herald and News reported that a lookout might be stationed on the peak by mid-May if the dry weather held.[^2] Before 1983 the Winema National Forest hired its lookout staff directly.[^2] That year it let the fire-watch job out by contract for the first time, with watch hours from 10 am to 6 pm, June 1 through September 30.[^2] The winning bidder had to bring personal gear and transportation.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on July 6, 2022.[^1] The tower still stands, and a lookout is posted there each summer.[^3][^4] A 2025 visitor found an easy drive to the summit and a lookout on duty who greeted them.[^4] A statewide list of standing lookouts marks the site as accessible to the public.[^5]
