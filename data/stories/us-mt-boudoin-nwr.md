A fire lookout tower stood on the Bowdoin National Wildlife Refuge in Phillips County, Montana.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page gives September 1, 1936, as the date erection began on a 100-foot (30 m) steel tower with a cab.[^1]

The refuge was established the same year, 1936, with a main goal of protecting places where migratory birds rest, feed and breed.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page lists the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service under the refuge name.[^1]

The tower was dismantled in 1987.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the tower site.

The Fish and Wildlife Service page for the refuge has a "Visit Us" section.[^2] Among the activities it lists are wildlife viewing, photography, hiking, cross-country skiing and upland bird and waterfowl hunting.[^2] Weekday hours at the headquarters and visitor center run from 7:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., Monday through Friday, except Federal holidays.[^2] A 15-mile self-guided driving tour circles Lake Bowdoin.[^2]
