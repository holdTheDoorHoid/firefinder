The Rushing Mountain lookout stands on a rounded peak in Tuolumne County, California.[^1] The peak was known as Big Hill before the lookout was founded.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the spot became a fire detection site in 1921.[^1]

Plans for a tower were under way by late 1930.[^2] A newspaper report described a proposed site on the W.H. Rushing ranch, west of Keystone.[^2] Tuolumne and Stanislaus counties each contributed $600, and the state erected the tower.[^2] Newspaper items disagree on who would maintain it: a February 1931 item said the Forest Service would maintain the lookout during the danger season, while a May 1931 item said the state would maintain it.[^2] The Register says the tower is an Aermotor product and gives 1931 as its build year.[^1] A 2011 photo page also dates it to 1931, though it says "probably."[^3]

Sources do not agree on its height.[^2][^3] One 1931 newspaper item says forty feet, another says thirty, and the 2011 photo page records 35 feet.[^2][^3]

A May 1931 item said Ed Tinney of Keystone was to be assigned as watchman in the tower.[^2] In June 1931 the lookout reported a twenty-acre grass fire on a ranch in the Don Pedro country, and that paper names him as Edward H. Tinnen.[^2] In October 1931 a blaze near Knights Ferry began as a permitted grass burn, and Ed Tinney at the lookout discovered it and sent in the alarm.[^2] A 1936 item names Frank Tinney as the lookout and says he had to leave his post when lightning threatened the station.[^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built the residence and garage.[^1] The Register says the house follows a Forest Service plan, but its front entry was altered.[^1] It also says the east end has been enlarged to the full 19-foot width, and that this may have been done in 1970, when the facilities were rented out year round.[^1] A 2011 visit found the house abandoned, and it appeared to have once served as an FM radio transmitter site.[^3] The same visit said the tower looks about the same condition, without saying compared with what.[^3]

The Register says the site is not open to the public, and it sits two miles from a paved road.[^1] Cal Fire's Tuolumne-Calaveras Unit administers it, and the 2011 photo page says the lookout may be staffed as needed during fire season.[^1][^3] Do not plan a visit without checking with Cal Fire first.
