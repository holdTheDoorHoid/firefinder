The Hopkins Mountain fire tower stood in the Monongahela National Forest in Greenbrier County, West Virginia, at 3,318 feet, or 1,011 meters.[^1] The register says it was built in 1935 with Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) labor.[^1][^4] The Forest Service says the CCC built the cabin and fire tower site that year, and Bitter Southerner adds the mountain road to that list.[^2][^3]

The observer's cabin is the part that lasted. Bitter Southerner calls it a historic fire spotter's cabin and gives its size as 14 by 13 feet.[^3] The Forest Service gives 14 by 20 feet and describes the building as virtually unaltered since construction.[^2] We could not settle which size is right.[^2][^3]

The register lists the tower as removed in 1979.[^1] The cabin was renovated in 2016 and made available for rentals in 2017, according to the same register.[^1]

Today the Forest Service lists the cabin among its recreation sites, where people go for the view, wildlife watching, hunting and local history.[^2] Forest Road 139, also called Hopkins Mountain Road, runs about four miles to the cabin.[^2] The Forest Service calls it narrow and very steep, and recommends four-wheel drive, though a passenger vehicle can use it and conditions can change quickly in bad weather.[^2] The Forest Service gives two distances from White Sulphur Springs for the cabin, 17 miles in one place and 15 in another.[^2] Bitter Southerner gives the road as about five miles.[^3]

The writer of that account took a caretaking post at the cabin in October 2020. She says the last few hundred yards of the road lay behind a locked gate, and that she was given the keys.[^3] We could not confirm whether that gate is still locked.

We found no source that describes what, if anything, of the tower still stands at the site.[^1][^2] Anyone planning a visit should check with the Monongahela National Forest first.
