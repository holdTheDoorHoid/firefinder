The tower stands on Sand Lake National Wildlife Refuge, about 20 miles northeast of Aberdeen and near Columbia, in Brown County.[^1][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps put up the steel observation tower.[^3][^5]

In 1935 Sand Lake became a refuge where migratory birds could breed.[^4] Between 1935 and 1939 the CCC worked there on buildings, dikes, roads and the tower.[^4] The marker dates the tower to 1936, and I found no other source for its exact build date.[^3]

At first the tower was meant to watch for fires and poachers and to study bird migration.[^4] The marker says the CCC camp at Sand Lake operated from 1925 through 1939 and held several hundred men.[^3] South Dakota Magazine names Ralph Herseth, who was 26 and supervised the camp. He had 200 men under him when the refuge was added to the national system in 1935.[^5] The brochure's 1935 to 1939 dates for CCC work do not match the marker's 1925 start, and I have used the brochure's dates.[^3][^4]

One listing gives the height as 100 feet.[^2] The marker, the refuge brochure and the magazine say 108.[^3][^4][^5]

There are 132 wooden steps to the top room, which has 83 windows across its four sides.[^3] The marker says the tower is open to visitors during daylight hours.[^3] I found no recent report on its condition.

The western listing is headed Sand Lake NWR Hdqtrs. The refuge brochure's map labels the observation tower and the refuge headquarters as separate places, so I have not tied the tower to the headquarters.[^2][^4]
