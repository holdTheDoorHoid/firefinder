The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a Kelso Mountain tower in Cook County as gone, at 47.919 N, 90.931 W.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service owned the tower, which stood on Kelso Mountain in the Superior National Forest and overlooked Kelso Lake.[^2]

A 2025 compilation of Minnesota fire towers dates it to 1932 and describes it as a ladder tower.[^2] A Forest Lookouts listing gives 1932 as the construction year too.[^3] The compilation adds a possible second name: the Sawbill tower.[^2]

Survey notes from 1934 describe a 90-foot steel tower. A later note, from 2008, says the tower was taken apart.[^2]

According to the compilation, the tower was reached off the Sawbill Trail, and it likely required a canoe trip.[^2] Kelso Lake sits inside the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness. It also lies in the Superior National Forest.[^4] We found no source that describes what remains on the mountain today, so we cannot say whether any footings or other traces survive, or whether the site is open to visitors.
