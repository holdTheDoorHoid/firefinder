The Long Hill fire tower is at 8023 Long Hill Road in Pasadena, Anne Arundel County, Maryland.[^2][^3] A state forestry report, as the Forest Lookouts site quotes it, says the 60-foot steel tower was in service by 1923, and the state's own notice gives the same date.[^1][^3] Maryland's Bureau of Forestry put up fire towers statewide between 1915 and the mid-1940s, and Long Hill dates from that period.[^3]

The tower also served as a radio post.[^1][^4] In June 1934 a newspaper report, which the Forest Lookouts site quotes, described a short-wave set installed in the tower that made contact with a forest warden's automobile during tests.[^1] In the late 1950s the Long Hill Central Two-way Radio Center began at the tower, and the tower operator was often asked to relay messages for the department's headquarters in Annapolis.[^4]

John Shortall had been the towerman before he moved up to a roadside tree warden post.[^4] His wife Kitty then took over as tower operator, and the family lived at the site.[^4] The sources we read name no specific fire the tower watched, though its operators were there to spot smoke and send rangers toward blazes.[^4]

The Department of Natural Resources now proposes to demolish the tower.[^3] Its notice, with comments due by September 30, 2025, says the towers have fallen into disrepair and can no longer be used, and it calls abandoned towers a safety risk.[^3] The Maryland Historical Trust found that demolition would have an adverse effect on historic properties, and asked the department to complete a memorandum of agreement as part of mitigation.[^3] We found no report that the tower has come down since, and the Forest Lookouts list still shows it as standing, so its current condition is unconfirmed.[^2]

Sources also disagree on height. The Forest Lookouts site gives 60 feet, while Kitty Shortall's history page calls the tower 110 feet.[^1][^4] We found no public-access statement for the tower. Given that warning, do not climb it, and check with the department before visiting the site.
