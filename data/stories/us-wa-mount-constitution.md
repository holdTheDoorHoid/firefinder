Mount Constitution Lookout is a stone tower on the summit of Mount Constitution, the highest point in the San Juan Islands, inside Moran State Park on Orcas Island.[^1][^3][^4] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it.[^1][^3] HistoryLink says a crew of 28 men began work in August 1935, topped out the tower on July 7, 1936, and finished it later that year.[^3] Island Histories also dates it to 1935-36.[^4] The register gives 1937 instead.[^1] The register links the design to the watch towers of the Caucasus Mountains and describes a five-story stone tower.[^1] Ellsworth Storey, a Seattle architect, is named as designer.[^3] Island Histories says Roland Koepf did the design under Storey's direction.[^4]

The sources also differ on height. Island Histories gives 53 feet, while firelookout.com gives 45 feet.[^2][^4]

The tower served as a state lookout.[^2] The register says the Division of Forestry staffed it until 1967, when more than 10,000 visitors a year led to a second, more isolated tower used only for detection.[^1] Firelookout.com puts the change in the mid-1960s and says a replacement called Little Summit Lookout, 1.5 miles south, was built in 1966.[^2] The register reports that the replacement no longer stands.[^1] Over the years the stone tower also served as an observation and radio tower during World War II, and it was home to the KVOS television station in the 1960s and 1970s.[^4] I found no notable fires at the tower in these sources.

In 1973 Orcas Island residents halted a plan to tear the tower down. KVOS agreed to restore it and relocated its equipment to a steel tower alongside.[^4] The National Register of Historic Places listed the tower in 1974.[^3][^4] Local contractor Rolf Eriksen oversaw restoration in 2003 and 2004.[^3] In May 2004 former Camp Moran CCC workers attended a rededication.[^3]

Today the tower is a popular state park attraction.[^3][^4] The register says the state parks commission maintains it.[^1] Visitors can drive to the top, or park at the bottom and hike or bike up about seven miles.[^4]
