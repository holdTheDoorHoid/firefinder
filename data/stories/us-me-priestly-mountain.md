Priestly Mountain sits in Piscataquis County, in Township 10, Range 13.[^1] The state built a lookout there in 1910: a log tower of twelve upright logs, 22 feet high, on the highest bluff of the mountain.[^1] By 1919 it had a house on top for the watchman.[^1] A living camp was built on the shore of Priestly Lake.[^1]

A 1914 report called the station still in good condition.[^1] In 1917 a chief warden reported repairs to the tower and to the watchman's camp.[^1]

A 1920 inventory listed a 20-foot wooden tower.[^1] In April 1929 a Presque Isle newspaper reported plans to replace it with a new 24-foot steel tower.[^1] The sources we read do not confirm that the steel tower was built.

The lookout came down in July 2009.[^2] The Maine chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association reported that the tower had no cab and held some state radio systems. It also reported that the U.S. Department of Homeland Security then built a new radio complex on the site, with a shelter and a new steel tower.[^2] Forest Lookouts ties the removal to a new communication complex.[^1]

A press release from Senator Susan Collins's office, dated January 27, 2009, had described an agreement between the State of Maine and Customs and Border Protection to upgrade the radio tower on this mountain.[^3] The release said the federal agency would gain site access for a new tower.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^4] We found no source from after 2009 that describes the site today, so we cannot say what stands there now or whether visitors can get near it.
