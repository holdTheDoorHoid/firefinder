The Missouri Conservation Commission bought 40 acres near Camdenton in 1941 for a fire tower.[^2] The steel came from an old Eldon city water tower. That tower had been moved to Sunrise Beach in 1930, served there briefly as a fire lookout, and was then moved about three miles northwest of Camdenton.[^2]

The Register dates the tower to 1942 and describes it as a 60-foot steel tower on the 40-acre site.[^1] A 1942 Commission report says a 60-foot steel and wood tower was completed.[^2] An October 1941 newspaper item said construction had stopped because defense orders delayed materials, and that the tower would be open to the public once finished.[^2]

An October 1941 newspaper item planned a towerman on duty in the tower at all times, except in wet or rainy weather.[^2] A watchman's dwelling went up in 1946.[^1] In fiscal 1970 the cab was overhauled.[^1] The Department's annual report for fiscal 1969–70 said a major cab overhaul and new blacktop had the site ready for thousands of visitors a year.[^2] The Register lists the tower as registered on January 15, 2018.[^1]

The sources disagree on height. The Register and Forest Lookouts say 60 feet, but the Department's current site page calls the tower 80 feet tall.[^1][^2][^3] The 60-foot figure is kept here, and the gap is flagged.

Today the Camdenton Conservation Service Center site covers 45 mostly wooded acres, with an archery range and a hiking trail.[^3] The Department says visitors can walk up the tower for views of the surrounding countryside.[^3] Public use is limited to what postings authorize, and special hours apply.[^3] I did not find a rental listing for the tower.
