Beaver Fire Tower stands on Beaver Mountain, the highest point in Cherokee County, Oklahoma.[^4] It sits in the Cookson Hills Wildlife Management Area in the northeast of the state.[^1] The Aermotor tower stands 100 feet tall and carries a metal cab, 7 by 7 feet.[^1] It went up in 1950.[^1][^3]

A 1960 newspaper report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, describes a ranger named Jess Morton manning the tower, and says he could raise a warning about trespassers or any sign of fire.[^3] A roofed hollow stump, big enough to hold four people, serves as the visitor registration site.[^1]

The lookout was in use until 1997.[^1] Patrick McDowell of the Oklahoma Forestry Services nominated it, and the National Historic Lookout Register recorded the registration on November 7, 1997.[^1]

The tower's later history is less clear. A News On 6 report said the state forestry agency has little use for most of its fire towers and treats this one as surplus.[^2] The same report called the lookout abandoned, said the 100-foot structure is still sound, and floated moving it to the Keystone Ancient Forest Preserve in Sand Springs.[^2] A Sand Springs news item says the city has bought a disused 100-foot tower from Cookson for $108,000 and planned to bring it to the forest.[^5] The item does not name the tower, but its details match Beaver's, and the register lists it.[^5][^1] I could not confirm whether the move has happened, so the tower's current location is unclear.

The summit is reached by road, but whether you can drive up depends on the current gate schedule, operations at the wildlife area and any controlled hunts.[^4] I found no source saying the tower is open to climbers. Do not climb it unless the state confirms it is open and safe, and check with the Oklahoma Forestry Services or the Cookson area before you go.
