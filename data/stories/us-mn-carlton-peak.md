Carlton Peak is a rocky hill about a mile northwest of Tofte, on Lake Superior's North Shore.[^1][^2] A Superior National Forest brochure says Edward and C. Lester Tofte built a fire tower on the peak in 1946.[^2] A 1952 Coast and Geodetic Survey description puts the station at roughly 35 feet, built of steel and anchored on four legs set in bedrock.[^1][^4]

According to the brochure, the tower was taken down in the mid-1960s.[^2] When the survey mark was recovered in 2004, the tower had been removed, though remnants of its base could still be seen.[^1]

Today Carlton Peak is a day-use area in Temperance River State Park.[^2][^3] The brochure describes a hiking-only route of about three miles round trip from the Britton Peak trailhead, with the Temperance River valley and Lake Superior in view.[^2] Wikipedia says visitors need a current state parks sticker.[^3] The brochure, which is dated 2004, also says remnants of the fire tower can still be found on the peak, so the site may have changed since.[^2]
