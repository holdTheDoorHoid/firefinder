The lookout stands on Santa Lucia Peak in Monterey County, a peak that the Forest Service trail page calls Junipero Serra Peak.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register dates the tower to 1935 and says a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin sat on an open steel tower about 30 feet tall.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building the 30-foot steel tower and its wooden cab, and it cites a book on Los Padres lookouts by Irma Oksen Reaves.[^3]

We found no source that names anyone who staffed the lookout or any fire it spotted.[^1][^3]

The Register says the tower is still there, while the date the cabin came down is unknown.[^1] A hiking blog calls the site a ruin of a lookout that was abandoned in 1979.[^4] The Register does not give that 1979 date, and we could not confirm it from another source.[^1][^4]

The Register added the site to its list on December 20, 2019, and gives the elevation as 5,842 feet (1,781 meters).[^1]

Today, the Forest Service page for the Santa Lucia Peak Trail describes a 2.3-mile route through the Ventana Wilderness to the summit, and it lists the trail as closed in August 2026 because of the Timber and Plaskett fires.[^2] The wilderness bans motorized use, and the forest does not issue wilderness permits.[^2] Please do not climb or visit the tower while the route is closed, and check the Forest Service notice before planning any trip.[^2]
