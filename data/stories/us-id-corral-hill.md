Corral Hill got its start in 1929, when the Nez Perce National Forest put up a simple 40-foot pole tower with a tent cabin at its base so a lookout had somewhere to sleep.[^1] That arrangement lasted a couple of decades before the Forest Service replaced it in 1953 with the structure still standing today: a 41-foot treated timber tower carrying a standard L-4 cab and catwalk.[^1][^2]

The tower has had people in it on and off ever since. At some point in the 1970s it was dropped to emergency-use status rather than staffed every season, but it came back to regular summer staffing in the 2000s and has stayed active during fire season since.[^3] Among the named staff are Bob Anderson, who watched from Corral Hill in 1979 while a colleague named Kris worked nearby at Coolwater, and more recently Kevin Roach, a former wildland firefighter who took the job partly so he could have his kids with him for the summer.[^3] Writer Nancy Canyon also lived there in the early 1970s and later wrote about the experience in her book "Struck, A Season on a Fire Lookout."[^3]

Corral Hill was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on April 24, 2009, as US 783.[^1] It sits at about 5,968 feet on the Nez Perce National Forest and remains an active Forest Service lookout rather than a rental, so the structure is not generally open for overnight stays the way some retired towers are.[^1][^3] Visitors researching a trip should check current conditions and access with the Nez Perce-Clearwater National Forests before heading up, since a staffed fire tower is still a working facility during the season.
