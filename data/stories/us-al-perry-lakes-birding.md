The birding tower at Perry Lakes Park, in Perry County, Alabama, is a 100-foot Aermotor MC-39 tower that stood somewhere else before it came to the park.[^1][^3]

The register gives 1936 as the build year, but its description says the Civilian Conservation Corps erected the tower in the late 1930s, under the name Rumley Lookout in Sumter County.[^1] We could not confirm the Rumley name or the original site from any other source. The magazine and park pages do not say where the tower first stood.[^2][^3]

The Alabama Forestry Commission donated the tower, which had been an abandoned fire tower beside a road.[^2][^3] Students from the Rural Studio at Auburn University took it apart and rebuilt it in the park.[^2][^4] The register credits students from Judson College as well, and dates the move and restoration to 2005.[^1] Metropolis says the park was ready for a student team to take on the project in 2004.[^2]

The sources disagree on the price. Metropolis quotes a student saying the Forestry Commission would give them the tower for a dollar, while Ruth Arts says a team of four students bought it for $10.[^2][^4] Ruth Arts also says the team spent 15 days taking the tower down piece by piece, then 45 days rebuilding it with new railings and observation platforms.[^4]

The register says the tower was rebuilt without its 7-by-7-foot cab, which gives birders an open view of the canopy, and bills it as the tallest tower in the country for watching birds.[^1]

Perry Lakes Park has no admission charge during daylight hours, and its gate is unlocked.[^3] To drive in, turn in at the park entrance on AL-175 and take the dirt road through the gate.[^3] Ruth Arts likens the open-air stairway up the tower to a 10-story building, and the author describes climbing it on a winter morning.[^4] The register says the Perry County Commission administers the tower, and that it was listed on December 16, 2009.[^1]
