Chase Mountain sits about 14 miles southwest of Klamath Falls, and its lookout history reaches back to the 1920s.[^1][^2] In 1924 the lookout, Reuben Gardner, reported several forest fires, and a lookout cabin was planned for the summit in 1925.[^2] The first structure seems to have been an observation platform on a 12-by-12 hip-roofed frame cabin, and a ground cabin for living quarters followed in 1928.[^2][^4]

The steel tower that stands today went up in 1951. Willhite describes a new all-steel 20-foot lookout built by Pade and Holland of Klamath Falls, and Cherylhill says it is the tower still standing.[^2][^3] The NHLR credits Holland Sheet Metal Co. with the 1951 build and says the older 10-by-10 ground house moved to Hambelton Butte in 1949, while Willhite puts that move in 1951.[^4][^2] Willhite and the Forest Lookouts page both list a new tower in 1935, a year the NHLR does not mention.[^2][^5]

The lookout was staffed for 180 days in 1940 and reported to the Clover Station by radio and telephone.[^2] In 1943 Thelma Duke became the area's first female lookout.[^2] Firelookout says it was staffed every summer into the 2010s.[^1] In 1950 a logging train's brake shoe threw sparks that set fire to a Weyerhaeuser trestle near Spencer Creek, and Klamath Forest Protective Association lookouts on Chase Mountain and Buck Peak spotted the smoke.[^2]

Crews took the living quarters down in 1964 because they had decayed.[^2] The tower got new windows and flooring in 2004.[^3] When Cherylhill visited in June 2022, the lookout was no longer staffed and carried equipment and cameras.[^3] The 2026 directory lists it as unmanned.[^6]

The NHLR listed it on March 31, 2003 as US 485, and says much of the view from it lies in California.[^4]

To reach it, turn south from Highway 66 west of Keno onto Hamacker Mountain Road. After 5.6 paved miles, an unmarked gravel road branches off to the right; take it 1.9 miles to the summit.[^3] The gravel is good until the last 0.2 miles, which get rougher.[^3] Because the tower now holds equipment, treat it as a site to view from the road, not a lookout to climb.
