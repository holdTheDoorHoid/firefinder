Sliderock was a fire lookout on Sliderock Mountain, 21 miles north-northwest of Philipsburg in Granite County, Montana.[^1] Ron Kemnow's page lists the Lolo/Admin. Deer Lodge National Forest as the agency.[^4]

The site was first developed in 1919 with a 35-foot pole platform and a small scale-model log cabin.[^1] Firelookout.com says a 40-foot pole L-4 tower was built there in 1935.[^1] The Fort Missoula museum says the lookout was built about 1930, and a Montana historic marker says it was manufactured in Columbia Falls that year.[^2][^3] The museum adds that similar lookouts were prefabricated and delivered to a four-state region, to be assembled on a tower or on bare ground.[^2]

We found no record of who staffed this lookout or of any fire it watched.

The museum says the lookout was taken apart on its mountain and brought to the museum in 1983, with help from the Friends of the Historical Museum and the Missoula Chapter of the Society of American Foresters.[^2] The marker also gives 1983.[^3] Kemnow's page describes the move from the original site as one to put the lookout on display at Fort Missoula.[^4] Firelookout.com instead says the cab and top two-thirds went there in 1984.[^1] The sources disagree on the year and on how much of the lookout was moved.

Today the lookout stands on the museum grounds, on what the marker calls its own "mini-Sliderock" mountain.[^2][^3] According to the marker, the first section of stairs has been removed, so the lookout sits lower than it would have at its original location.[^3] The museum's page refers to standing on the landing between the two flights of stairs, but we found no hours or climbing rules there. Ask the museum before you go.[^2]
