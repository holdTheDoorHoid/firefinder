The National Historic Lookout Register describes the Ramer Fire Tower in McNairy County as an Aermotor-style tower, registered on December 30, 2022.[^1] It gives the elevation as 645 feet and names the Tennessee Division of Forestry as administrator.[^1] The register gives no build date and does not name a model.[^1]

Two Division designs, the LS-40 and the MC-39, were among its most widely built types, but the register does not say which design this tower follows.[^3][^1]

Tennessee Landforms lists it as Ramer, numbered 8-20 and standing.[^2] That number comes from the same 1976 list of Division towers.[^2] A March 2014 count of surviving Division towers, published in a National Register study, also lists a Ramer tower in McNairy County.[^3]

The register says the tower seems to sit on private land.[^1] Landforms also notes that many towers are gone and most sites are now private land.[^2] We found no published history of its construction, staffing or fires. Because the land appears to be private, this story does not suggest visiting or climbing it.[^1]
