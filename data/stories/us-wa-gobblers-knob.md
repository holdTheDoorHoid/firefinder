Gobbler's Knob Lookout sits in the far western reaches of Mount Rainier National Park, closer to the mountain's peak than any of the park's other three surviving lookouts.[^1] It was built in 1933, a roughly 14-by-14-foot, two-story frame cabin that came out of the National Park Service's in-house design shop, the Branch of Plans and Design, while Edwin A. Nickel held the post of acting chief architect.[^2] The design follows the Park Service's "rustic" style used across Mount Rainier, with a live-in cab and catwalk on the upper level and storage below, and cables anchored to buried deadmen to keep the building from blowing off the ridge in wind.[^1][^2]

The lookout was staffed into the 1980s as part of the park's fire-detection network.[^3] Like several of Rainier's lookouts, it was hit hard by a 2006 windstorm that also damaged the Mount Fremont lookout; both were later repaired.[^2]

Gobbler's Knob was added to the National Register of Historic Places on March 13, 1991, and it's counted within the Mount Rainier National Historic Landmark District, which recognizes the park's broader collection of Park Service-designed rustic buildings.[^2]

The structure still stands and has been restored.[^1] It's no longer staffed full-time for fire detection, but the park now uses it as a popular hiking destination and a standby lookout point worked by backcountry rangers when conditions call for it.[^1] Because it sits within Mount Rainier National Park, any visit means a hike in on park trails rather than a drive to the door; check current National Park Service guidance before planning a trip, since specific trailhead and distance details weren't confirmed in the sources reviewed here.
