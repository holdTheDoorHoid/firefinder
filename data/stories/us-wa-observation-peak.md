Observation Peak, in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest and the Trapper Creek Wilderness, was the site of a fire lookout.[^1][^2] The register describes an L-4 cab built in 1934 that replaced a D-6 cupola cabin from the 1920s.[^1] Willhite is less sure about that earlier cabin. The lookout inventory mentions it, but a 1934 panoramic survey went to nearby Sister Rocks and seemed to skip this peak. The author wonders whether a cupola cabin stood here before 1934, though a dismantled cupola may be lying on the ground in a 1937 panorama.[^3]

Willhite also places the peak in operation in August 1924, as either a cupola or a camp, which is before the 1934 cab.[^3] That month, a fire that patrols passing the spot daily or twice a day had missed for three months was found only after two days of low humidity.[^4] The lookout located it promptly and accurately, which kept the fire to Class A, although the elapsed time from origin to end was 92 days and 23 hours.[^4] In May 1926 the Morning Oregonian reported that a six-mile trail from Government Mineral Springs would probably be finished early in July.[^4] A Morning Oregonian item from August 1934, quoted on one lookout history page, describes seven women hiking to the lookout as guests of Mrs. N.H. Crowell, the wife of the summer fire watchman.[^4] Panoramic photographs of Observation Peak were taken on July 7, 1937.[^3][^4]

The sources all place the end of the cab in the 1960s, but they differ on how it ended. The Forest Service says the lookout was abandoned then.[^2] The register says the L-4 was removed, while firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed.[^1][^5][^3]

Trail 132A is about half a mile long and leads to the summit, which the Forest Service calls the site of an old fire lookout.[^2] Trail 132 ends on Road 58 at the Observation Peak Trailhead.[^2] The Forest Service page says the trail is open to hikers and horses, but the same page carries a "Trail Temporarily Closed" banner, so check conditions before you go.[^2] No source I found describes what, if anything, remains on the summit. Willhite also describes the forested pass between Observation Peak and Sister Rocks. Old campsites there remain as compacted soil, though trees have grown over the old berry fields.[^3]
