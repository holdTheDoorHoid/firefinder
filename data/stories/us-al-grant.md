The Alabama Forestry Commission is listed as the agency for a lookout called Grant, in Marshall County, Alabama.[^1] Statewide, the first lookouts of the early 20th century were platforms set in trees.[^2] Soon after, the forest service began putting up wooden towers made from creosote wood.[^2] Many of those wooden towers were later swapped for steel ones during the New Deal.[^2] When the United States entered World War II, the Civilian Conservation Corps stopped building towers, and forestry staff took over, putting up new ones during the war and for a few decades after.[^2] The Eastern U.S. Lookouts page gives no description of what remains at the Grant site today.[^1] Probably fewer than 75 of Alabama's historic lookout structures remain standing today.[^2] Many are in rough shape and unsafe, and climbing them is against the law.[^2] Please do not climb any tower you find.
