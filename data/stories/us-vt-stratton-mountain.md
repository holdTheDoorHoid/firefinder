The first lookout on Stratton Mountain went up in the spring of 1914, when the Stratton Mountain Club, working with the Vermont State Forester, put a steel tower on the summit.[^1][^3] Most of the mountain was privately owned timber land at the time, which made fire detection urgent.[^5] A winter storm partly wrecked that first tower in 1920 and 1921, yet what was left of it stayed in use.[^5] The sources disagree on its height: the register and the Green Mountain Club's shelter history say 60 feet, while the nomination says 65.[^1][^3][^5]

The present tower dates to 1934, when Civilian Conservation Corps crews put up a 55-foot Aermotor steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^5] The old tower was taken down in 1936.[^5] A watchman's cabin went up in 1928.[^1][^3] The nomination says a sawmill owner, Leon Stocker, probably built it, and the Green Mountain Club's shelter history credits the timber owners' association and the state forestry department with the work.[^3][^5]

Resident watchmen once lived on site during the April-to-October fire season.[^3] According to the nomination, the tower was one of the last three Vermont towers still spotting fires, until it shut down in 1982.[^3] I found no record that the tower helped stop any particular fire.[^3][^5]

Ownership is another point of disagreement. The nomination dates the Forest Service's acquisition of a large tract, including the south summit, to 1986, while the register puts the purchase of the land and tower in 1990.[^3][^1] The tower was fixed up and painted in 1988 to serve as a sightseeing spot.[^3] The state later retired it as an active fire station, and the Green Mountain Club took over the 1928 cabin, which has sheltered hikers and caretakers since.[^5] The tower entered the National Register of Historic Places in 1992.[^5]

Today the Forest Service says visitors may go up the renovated tower, which it puts at 70 feet. A Green Mountain Club caretaker is posted at the summit in summer and autumn.[^4] The register still gives 55 feet, so the height is another point where sources differ.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^2] The route leaves from a parking area and follows the white-blazed Appalachian and Long Trails; about 1.4 miles in, it crosses Forest Road 341, where the steeper section begins.[^4] I did not find a rental listing for the tower or the cabin.[^4][^5]
