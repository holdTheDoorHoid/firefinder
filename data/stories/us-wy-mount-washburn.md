Mount Washburn Lookout stands near the top of Mount Washburn in Yellowstone National Park, about 22 miles southeast of Mammoth Hot Springs in Park County, Wyoming.[^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the site at 10,243 feet, while the Park Service gives the peak as 10,219 feet.[^1][^3]

A stone shelter and fire lookout went up on the summit in 1921, built with a $2,500 appropriation.[^4] Its windows proved too small over time, and the Park Service decided in 1939 to tear the building down and start again.[^4][^5] Construction began in June 1939.[^1] The register says the new lookout was finished the following summer, in 1940, but the Historic American Buildings Survey gives 1941, and the register's own build field also says 1941.[^1][^5]

The new building is a three-story structure of mostly reinforced concrete with Art Deco touches, though its top observation level is steel.[^5] The other New Deal lookouts in Yellowstone used native stone and log, so this one stood apart.[^5] The first floor was meant for public restrooms, the second floor was originally the spotter's residence and storage, and the top floor is the observation level.[^5]

John D. Coffman, the Park Service's first wildland fire suppression expert, had called the older structure inadequate back in 1929.[^5] In 1935 Theodore Robb served as ranger naturalist and fire lookout at the top, guiding visitors while watching for fires.[^4] A three-mile telephone line was run from Dunraven Pass to the station in 1922, and by 1930 hundreds of tourists made the trip each day.[^4]

In 1980 the Park Service modified the building, replacing a wood addition with a concrete wing that holds telecommunications equipment and an interpretive center.[^5] The lookout is still a working station, and the Park Service says it is continuously staffed from mid-June until the fire season ends.[^3]

Visitors can hike to the top from two trails, starting at the Dunraven Pass trailhead and the Chittenden Road parking area.[^3] The Park Service calls it one of the most popular day hiking destinations in Yellowstone, and interpretive exhibits sit inside the base of the lookout.[^3]
