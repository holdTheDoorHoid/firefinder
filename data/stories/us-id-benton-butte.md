The earliest lookout the sources name on Benton Butte was a pole tower with a cabin, run by the Clearwater Timber Protective Association.[^1][^3] The sources disagree on when it went up. The association's 1947 report dates the tower to 1928, while the Idaho lookout sites give 1930.[^1][^2][^3] An aerial photograph dated 1931 shows the lookout, so it was standing by then.[^4]

In 1946 the association said its two towers on Benton Butte and Whiskey Butte were built in 1931 and needed new poles.[^3] In 1947 it replaced two of its oldest wooden lookouts, Benton Butte and Whiskey Butte, with 75-foot steel towers from war surplus.[^3] The Idaho sites date the steel tower to 1948 and also list a 100-foot steel tower in 1935, but the association's reports describe wooden lookouts until the 1947 replacement.[^1][^2][^3]

A 1954 slide description notes a very small fire that a lightning strike started on the butte.[^4] By 1957 the lookout was listed as emergency-only.[^1][^2]

The lookout is gone.[^1][^5] The Idaho site lists the spot as drive-up.[^2] No source describes what remains at the site today.
