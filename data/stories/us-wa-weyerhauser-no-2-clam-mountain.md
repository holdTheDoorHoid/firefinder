The Weyerhaeuser No. 2 lookout, also called the Clam Mountain lookout, stood near the summit of Clam Mountain in Lewis County, Washington, just over the county line.[^3] Willhite notes that maps labeled it Weyerhaeuser #2.[^3]

It was built in 1940 as a wooden tower with a live-in cab.[^1][^2][^3] In 1941 a USGS survey party recorded the station mark at the center of a cabin on a lot that had been logged.[^3] The lookout was abandoned in the 1950s.[^1][^2][^3] The 1952 aerial photographs appear to show nothing there, Willhite says.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register names the company as administrator and records the site as registered on April 6, 2020.[^1]

The site is on the company's Vail Tree Farm, and a permit is required to enter.[^3] A motorized permit allows driving right to the lookout site. Willhite, on a non-motorized permit, parked at the Sorenson Road gate.[^3] The area was logged again around 2007.[^3] When Willhite visited in 2018, the trees around the site were still small. He found two footings that had been moved a long way to clear space for logging equipment that used the spot as a landing.[^3] The register calls the lookout gone, and the washingtonlookouts list marks it as removed.[^1][^2] We found no source that describes public access.[^1][^2][^3]
