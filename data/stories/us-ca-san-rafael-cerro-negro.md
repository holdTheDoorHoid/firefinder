San Rafael Lookout, now called Cerro Negro Lookout, stands in Cherry Canyon Park in Los Angeles County.[^1] The Los Angeles County Fire Department had it constructed in 1934.[^1] A county annual report from June 1934 says towers went up on Verdugo and West San Rafael Peaks, and a November 1934 newspaper item says a new lookout on San Rafael peak was going up to replace a temporary station used during the previous fire season.[^2]

The Register says the lookout appears to be a standard L-4 design, with a 14-by-14-foot cab on a 30-foot steel K-brace tower.[^1] Its walls and windows have since been covered with exterior paneling, and asphalt roofing has replaced the wood shakes.[^1] A Chrysler Victory air raid siren stands nearby on a short steel tower.[^1]

The sources I read name no fires the lookout watched and no staff who worked in it. The county ran the lookout for 26 years.[^1]

A county annual report for the year ending June 1945 says the site got a fence to protect its radio equipment and a power line to run it.[^2] In 1960, according to the Register, the county handed the lookout to the Glendale City Fire Department for communications use.[^1] A December 1960 newspaper item, quoting a county supervisor, describes a license for Glendale to install a radio receiver on the county-owned site, and says a Civil Defense siren will remain operable under county and federal control.[^2] The Register's wording suggests a handover, but the newspaper's account is more specific.[^1][^2]

Today the Register describes a fenced, working communications site, now managed by the City of La Canada Flintridge.[^1] Trails in Cherry Canyon Park may reach the area, but the site itself is closed to the public.[^1] The Register listed the site on June 28, 2017.[^1]
