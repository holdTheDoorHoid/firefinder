Hungry Peak Lookout Site sits in Skamania County, Washington, on land the U.S. Forest Service administers within the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1] Its register entry describes an L-5 cab built in the 1930s.[^1] Willhite dates the cab to the early 1930s and says the summit benchmark went in during 1937.[^3]

Panoramic photographs from 1935 show a flagpole and some sheds.[^3] A 1940 photo on Willhite's page carries the caption "Columbia National Forest," while the register now places the site in the Gifford Pinchot.[^3][^1]

The sources disagree on how the cab ended. The register says it was abandoned and then removed.[^1] Willhite reports that a USGS party in 1964 said the lookout house had been destroyed.[^3] The firelookout.com summary lists the 1930s L-5 cab as gone, with no removal date.[^2]

Today, trees have overtaken the old views, though Willhite's captions record a view of Mount St. Helens in October 1999.[^3] His photos show small cliffs around the summit, a steel anchor rising from a buried footing, and an anchor bolt farther down the hillside.[^3] Willhite calls the place a former lookout site, and his captions describe no standing structure.[^3]

Willhite describes driving a forest road to an old circular loop just below the site, then walking a 20-foot trail to the site.[^3] He says the final spur is very hidden, with grass and tree branches hiding the road after it leaves the main road.[^3] None of the sources read says the site is closed or permit-only, but none states its legal access status outright.[^1][^2][^3]
