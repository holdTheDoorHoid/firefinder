The Big Hill Fire Lookout Tower stands on the Cumberland Plateau in Marion County, at an elevation of 2,032 feet.[^1] In 1934 the state signed a contract with G. Sherman and the heirs of A.R. Hall for the tower site, and a deed from them to the state that year is on record.[^1] The Tennessee Division of Forestry put the tower up around 1947 as part of its fire management program.[^1]

The date is an estimate.[^1] The nomination notes that Division files point to 1948, but the tower shows on a 1947 USGS map and was not on the 1936 one.[^1] The nomination says it appears to be an Aermotor MC-39 type, and a National Register study of the state's towers lists it in that group.[^1][^2] Its four-sided steel frame is sixty feet tall, and a seven-by-seven-foot observation cab sits on top.[^1]

The tower was staffed constantly through the high fire season until the 1970s.[^1] After the mid-1970s, forestry staff came to rely more on spotter planes and 911 reports, and the tower was no longer continually manned.[^1] Staff still climb it occasionally to spot fires when needed.[^1] We found no named fire that the tower is recorded as spotting.

The tower has not been moved since it went up, and it keeps its original design features.[^1] The changes are small: the wooden stair treads were replaced in kind, and two modern radio antennas were added.[^1] A radio tower and equipment building sit nearby, both inside a barbed-wire fence.[^1] Three of the five towers that once stood in Marion County still stand, and Big Hill is one of them.[^1]

The National Register listed the tower on June 28, 2021.[^1] The state owns the land, and the nomination describes ongoing forestry use but no public visiting arrangement.[^1] Tennessee Landforms advises against climbing these towers, because steps are often missing or decayed.[^3] Please do not climb it, and ask the Division of Forestry before going near it. We found no rental or lodging use for the tower.
