Crotched Mountain, in Francestown,[^1] got a new state lookout station during the 1915–1916 biennium.[^2] The first station was an open-top wooden structure about 20 feet high, with a small cabin.[^2] In 1921 the state called the Crotched station an emergency post, and in 1922 it was one of five stations temporarily or permanently discontinued, on the belief that its service was worth keeping only in emergencies.[^2]

The lookout came back as a steel tower. A Peterborough Transcript note says a new 45-foot steel tower replaced the one abandoned in 1921.[^2] A Portsmouth Herald item dated January 3, 1930 describes the reopening with a 45-foot steel tower and a two-room cabin.[^2] The two accounts date the rebuild differently, and the sources do not settle it.

Before the ski area, a woods road ran almost to the summit.[^3] A photo dated September 14, 1943 shows someone sitting on the roof of the fire warden's cabin at the tower.[^3] A later writer says it was popular to climb the steps into the cabin on top for the view, and calls the tower a Forest Service one.[^3]

The tower and warden's cabin are gone.[^3] Communication towers and buildings now stand on the summit.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Hampshire list marks the tower as gone.[^4]

Today the Crotched Mountain Ski & Ride is on the northern slopes, and a rehabilitation center sits on part of the southern ridge in Greenfield.[^1] No source I read describes any remains of the lookout itself, such as footings.
