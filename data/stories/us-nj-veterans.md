The Veterans Fire Tower stands at the Forest Resource Education Center and State Tree Nursery, off East Veterans Highway in Jackson Township, Ocean County.[^1][^2] The state dedicated it on March 25, 2026, and it is the first new fire tower the state has put up in 78 years.[^1][^2] Its builder was Fitzpatrick & Associates of Eatontown.[^1] The project cost about $2 million, paid from corporate business tax money set aside under the Preserve New Jersey Act.[^1][^2]

The tower is 133 feet tall, according to the Pine Barrens Tribune and the body of the Shore News article.[^1][^2] One Shore News headline says 173 feet instead. Because the article itself and the other report agree on 133, we use that figure.[^1][^2]

The tower replaces the Lakewood Fire Tower, a structure dating to 1944 that stood about five miles east in Lakewood.[^1][^2] That tower stood on private land. The Forest Fire Service's 100-year lease on that property expired in 2024, the landowner asked for its removal, and the old tower was taken out of service in July 2024 and then dismantled.[^2] Officials said the Jackson site has a better vantage point, since a nearby water tower had partly blocked views from the Lakewood tower.[^2]

The sources we read do not name the observers who will work the new tower, and they describe no fire it has spotted yet. The Forest Fire Service says observers often report fires several minutes before the first 911 call comes in.[^2] The state also counts additional funding for upgraded equipment and expanded staffing among recent investments in the Forest Fire Service.[^1]

The state also dedicated the tower to New Jersey veterans to mark the nation's 250th anniversary.[^1][^2] The sources we read describe no history for the new tower beyond its dedication.

OpenStreetMap maps it as a New Jersey Forest Fire Service observation tower in Jackson Township, based on a state press release, but its mapper did not confirm the location on site.[^3] We did not find any public visiting arrangement, and the sources do not say whether visitors may go up. Ask the Forest Fire Service before you go, and do not assume you may climb it.
