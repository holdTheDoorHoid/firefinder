The Summersville fire tower stood in Texas County, Missouri, inside the Gist Ranch Conservation Area, southwest of the junction of Highways 4430 and 4434.[^4]

The sources disagree on when the land was acquired.[^1][^4] A compiled list of Missouri lookouts gives 80 acres for a towersite acquired in 1942.[^1] The survey says the property was acquired in August 1952, as a donation from its previous owners.[^4]

The compiled list records an undated 52-foot wood tower. It also records an oil derrick converted for lookout use in 1946, and a replacement tower erected in fiscal 1952-53.[^1]

The survey dates the standing tower to December 1953, names the Aermotor Company as builder, and gives its height as 87 feet.[^4] The 2015 MDC plan for the Texas County towersites gives the same 1953 date and 87-foot height.[^3]

The fiscal 1952-53 replacement on the compiled list and the survey's December 1953 date do not match.[^1][^4] The survey also judges the current tower the probable only one to have stood at the site.[^4]

The survey describes the frame as galvanized steel, with a steel ladder on the north elevation and a pyramid roof.[^4] It also says the tower was assessed for the National Register of Historic Places and recommended eligible for its historic and architectural significance.[^4]

The 2015 plan gives wildfire detection as the main purpose of the Texas County towersites.[^3]

The department's Summersville page now reports that the tower was sold and is gone from the site.[^2] The site lies inside the Gist Ranch Conservation Area, where the survey lists camping, hunting and fishing as activities.[^2][^4]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the site now.
