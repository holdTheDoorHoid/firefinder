The Forest Lookouts site lists the Louisiana Forestry Commission as the agency for the Luna tower, which stood in Ouachita Parish.[^1] When the National Geodetic Survey described the site in 1971, it placed the tower about 2.4 miles north-northwest of Luna, a mile west of State Highway 557.[^2]

That 1971 description calls the observation cabin square and metal, with a silver finish and windows on all sides. It gives the cabin's height as 100 feet and says it is supported on four channel-steel legs.[^2]

On June 15, 1973, intruders tore down the fence around the tower, ripped several maps inside it, and took an electric fan and a Bible. The account comes from a Monroe News-Star report that the Forest Lookouts site quotes, and the report calls it the Luna Fire Tower in West Monroe.[^1] The survey places the tower about 2.4 miles from Luna, and the sources do not explain the difference.[^2][^1]

The survey mark, set at the top of the cabin roof, was recorded as recovered in good condition in 1990 and again in 1991.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts site now lists the tower as removed, without a date.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site or whether the public can reach it, so ask locally before going there.

[^1]: Luna - Forest Lookouts, Forest Lookouts, https://centraluslookouts.weebly.com/luna.html (accessed 2026-10-10).
[^2]: Station CQ2742, Luna Lookout Tower, National Geodetic Survey datasheet, https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/cgi-bin/ds_mark.prl?PidBox=CQ2742 (accessed 2026-10-10).
