American Ridge, also called Goat Peak, is a former lookout site in Yakima County, Washington.[^1][^3] A lookout with an L-4 cab went up here in 1933, and a storage shed stood 20 feet below it.[^1][^2][^4]

Sources differ on the forest. One names the Snoqualmie National Forest, while another gives the Wenatchee National Forest and says it was originally the Snoqualmie.[^2][^3]

In 1955 William George, 18, of Selah, was stationed at the lookout.[^3][^4] He radioed the district ranger at Naches that he was unwell, and a Yakima doctor diagnosed appendicitis.[^3][^4] A Forest Service patrolman was sent up with a pack train, but after a doctor warned that a horseback trip could be risky for George, the district ranger asked the Navy for help.[^3][^4] A Navy helicopter from Whidbey Island flew him out. Its pilot kept the wheels on rocks beside the building, with the rear wheels over a 1,000-foot drop.[^3][^4]

The Register says the cab was removed in 1968.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] Forest Lookouts also gives 1968 as the removal year.[^3] Willhite adds that a USGS party in 1971 reported the lookout house had been destroyed by fire.[^4] The sources do not agree on how the lookout ended.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Visit Rainier says the lookout was dismantled in the 1960s, though the views remain from high points along the trail.[^5] The hike to the peak starts at Hells Crossing, east of Chinook Pass, and runs about 18 miles round trip.[^5] The trail crosses the William O. Douglas Wilderness, and the guide mentions a Northwest Forest Pass and a permit filled out at the trailhead.[^5] We found no source that describes what, if anything, is left on the peak today.
