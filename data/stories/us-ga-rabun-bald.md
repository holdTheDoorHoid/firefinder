Rabun Bald is the tallest mountain in Rabun County, Georgia, and its summit sits inside the Chattahoochee National Forest.[^1][^3] The Chattooga Conservancy says the summit held the county's first firetower.[^4]

The National Historic Lookout Register dates that first tower to the 1930s and credits Nick Nicholson, Georgia's first forest ranger, with building it.[^1] The Chattooga Conservancy gives the same decade.[^4] An older item complicates the picture. A November 13, 1922 Atlanta Constitution report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, describes a newly completed Forest Service lookout at the very top of the peak: a fourteen-foot tower topped by a glass house.[^2] The sources read do not say whether that earlier structure was the one the 1930s accounts describe.

The Forest Service ran the tower for decades. Wikipedia says it operated until the early 1970s, and the Chattooga Conservancy says the agency managed it for the next forty years.[^3][^4] The sources read name no lookouts who staffed it and no fires it watched.

After the tower went out of service, a Youth Conservation Corps crew dismantled its uppermost part, a metal-framed glass cab that sat on a stone base.[^3] The crew left the stone base intact and replaced the cab with a railed wooden observation platform.[^3] The Chattooga Conservancy says that platform remains today.[^4] The sources do not say when the change was made.

Hikers reach the summit on foot.[^3][^4] The Chattooga Conservancy gives a 4.2-mile round trip with about 1,100 feet of climbing and rates it moderate to difficult. Wikipedia gives 5.8 miles round trip on the Rabun Bald Trail.[^3][^4] The two distances disagree, so check a current trail guide before you set out. Wikipedia says that on clear days, hikers at the summit tower can see more than 100 miles.[^3]

The sources do not say whether the platform has set hours or climbing rules, and none mention a gate. Stay on the trail, and do not climb past any fence or closure you find.
