Bald Mountain in Dedham, Hancock County, Maine, already had a permanent camp in 1910.[^1] On October 27, 1921, Forest Commissioner Samuel T. Dana announced that the department had placed an order for a 47-foot steel tower.[^1] The selectmen of Dedham, Otis and Mariaville requested the tower and were to pay roughly 40 percent of its cost.[^1]

An April 1922 Ellsworth American item reported that a fire tower was going up and that A. F. Moore, a surveyor, would serve as its lookout.[^1] The same paper reported in November 1922 that Fred Barnes had worked as the summer lookout.[^1]

A 1923 panoramic map is centered on the tower.[^2] In 1930 the state tested a fire district covering sixteen towns that the Dedham-Bald lookout watched over, with a district fire warden in charge. The state forest commissioner's annual report called the experiment highly successful.[^1]

Sources differ on the height. The 1921 order says 47 feet, while the Appalachian Mountain Club article gives 60 feet.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts history dates the removal to November 2009 and says the structure was to be moved to Canada the following year.[^1] The AMC article says the 60-foot tower, erected in 1921, was taken down in 2009 and transported to Canada.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list marks the tower as gone.[^5]

Maine by Foot, a trail guide posted in 2019, still describes a walk to "the old fire tower at the top" and lists "remnants of the past" among the sights.[^4] The AMC article is more specific. On the short walk to the ledges near the summit, it says, hikers pass concrete supports where the old tower once stood, and the summit now carries seven communication towers.[^3]

Hikers reach the summit by a short trail of about 0.6 to 0.7 miles.[^3][^4] Directions from the Dedham Fire Department run about 3.7 miles along Bald Mountain Road to the trailhead on the left, and the route is steep on bedrock near the top.[^3][^4]
