West Fork Butte Lookout stands in the Lolo National Forest, about 20 miles southwest of Missoula.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service built the cab in 1934: an L-4 ground house with a hip roof.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it "the only pre-1936 design hip-roof L-4 ground house lookout remaining in western Montana."[^2]

The Forest Service kept the lookout staffed into the late 1980s.[^2] After it went out of active fire-watch service, the Montana Chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association took on an extensive renovation.[^2] FFLA member Gary Weber then nominated it to the National Historic Lookout Register, where it was registered on June 5, 1996, as US 173, MT 15.[^2]

Today the Forest Service rents the cabin overnight through recreation.gov. It sleeps up to four people on bunk beds, with a propane cook stove, a wood stove for heat, and a vault toilet outside.[^3] From December 1 through May 1, Forest Road 37 is closed, so winter visitors reach it by hiking, skiing, or snowmobiling roughly 7.5 miles.[^3] The rest of the year the road is open but can still be "muddy, snowy or icy," and guests are on their own for travel and supplies.[^3]
