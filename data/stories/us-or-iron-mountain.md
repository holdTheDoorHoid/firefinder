Iron Mountain is a rock pinnacle above Highway 20 in the Willamette National Forest, in Linn County.[^1] The summit got a fire finder in 1929 and an L-4 ground house in 1933.[^1][^2]

The summit still had a lookout in 1940, when a newspaper item described an airplane dropping a Sunday paper, wrapped in white cloth, to the lookout.[^3] A July 1939 item had already named the season's lookout fireman.[^3]

In February 1976 a windstorm destroyed the house.[^3] That spring the Forest Service announced plans to move a lookout tower from Herman Peak to the summit, to be flown in by helicopter.[^3] The NHLR dates the arrival of its R-6 flat-top cab to 1976.[^1] The sources name the donor site differently, as Herman Peak in one place and Herman Lookout in another.[^3][^2]

A Forest Service fire lookout died after an apparent fall near the cabin in August 1990, which suggests the post was still staffed then.[^3]

The structure came down in 2007, and the sources differ on how.[^1] The NHLR says the cab was knocked down and burned in place because of vandalism.[^1] A 2007 Oregon chapter report says members made four trips to salvage items before it was brought down.[^4] An Oregon lookouts page says it was declared a health hazard and torn down, with a viewing platform for hikers planned in its place.[^3] Two lookout pages date the removal to 2007.[^2][^5]

No source describes what remains on the summit today, and none confirms that the planned platform was built.[^3] The NHLR calls the summit a popular hiking and wildflower destination, but no current source confirms access.[^1]
