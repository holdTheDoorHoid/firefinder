The Larose fire tower is listed at the headquarters of the Larose Forest, at Bourget in Clarence-Rockland, Ontario.[^1] The forest lies about 60 km (37 mi) east of Ottawa, and it sits across the line between Clarence-Rockland and The Nation municipality.[^2]

The forest grew out of a replanting drive.[^2] The area had been known as the Bourget Desert, and a replanting project started in the 1920s, led by Ferdinand Larose.[^2] The first planting, 40 hectares (99 acres) of red pine, came in 1928.[^2] While the Russell County Council owned the land, the Ontario Department of Lands and Forests managed it.[^2]

A caption on a photograph dated around June 1971 says the tower was staffed to guard the Larose Forest in the Kemptville District, southeast of Ottawa.[^3] Across Ontario, towers were used less and less from the 1960s, once aerial spotting and fire crews took over their work.[^4]

Today the register places the tower at the forest headquarters.[^1] A fan-run guide to Ontario's towers lists a tower in the Larose Forest among the popular originals that sightseers still use.[^4] People use the forest for hiking, mountain biking, snowshoeing and cross-country skiing.[^2] The register does not say whether the tower itself is open to visitors.[^1]
