Anderson Mountain is in Catawba County, North Carolina.[^2] Vernon Otto Sipe, an orchardist native to Conover, bought most of the ridge in 1943 and planned a one-hundred-unit development he called Anderson Park.[^1] That development never came to fruition, but Sipe lived to see a fire prevention tower erected at the summit.[^1]

The tower was completed in early 1951.[^1] The article says it was the only tower ever put up in Catawba County and the only all-wood tower in North Carolina.[^1] It stood on four telephone poles and was less than thirty feet (9 m) tall.[^1]

At first the lookout had no radio or telephone, which limited its effectiveness.[^1] A radio bought in October 1952 cost so much that the tower closed for the fall fire season.[^1] Early in 1953 the tower got a radio and telephone line, and the county also found money for a full-time staff.[^1] In the early 1950s, county forester Jim Ledbetter was responsible for the tower's upkeep and staffing.[^1]

In 1954 a new steel tower, seventy-two feet (22 m) tall, replaced the wooden one and rose above the treeline.[^1] According to the article, the lookout answered hundreds of fire calls in the decade that followed.[^1] It also quotes an earlier article, which the piece does not date, naming Clarence "Smokey" Stroupe as the man still manning the tower.[^1]

Yet by 1967 modern technology was threatening the tower's usefulness, and staffing became sparse.[^1] By March 1969 the tower had not been staffed for over a year and a half.[^1] Vandalism grew so severe that the doors and windows were boarded up whenever no staff was on site, and a chain across Tower Road kept visitors and vandals from driving up the ridge.[^1] Sporadic use continued for a time, but the article says the lookout's heyday was over.[^1]

The article, dated April 16, 2026, describes the lookout tower as still standing, fenced off and on private property.[^1] Cell towers now sit beside it, enclosed by barbed-wire fences.[^1] We found no source saying the public may visit, and this story does not encourage climbing it.
