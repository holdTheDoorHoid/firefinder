Clear Creek Lookout was an L-5 cab on a 10-foot log base, built in 1932 on a rock promontory with cliffs on three sides.[^1][^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register, which added the site on June 23, 2012, lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator and places it in Skamania County.[^1] Firelookout.com puts the site about nine miles east of Mount St. Helens, in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^2] The register gives its elevation as 3,105 feet (946 m), while Willhite's page gives 3,000 feet.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts page also calls the site Clear Creek Butte.[^4]

None of the pages we read says who built the cab, why this spot was chosen, or who staffed it. Willhite's notes mention a fire that cleared the trees from the ridge, but they give no date for it.[^3] Willhite's photo captions date north and southeast views to August 14, 1936.[^3] The Forest Lookouts list notes panorama photos taken on that same day.[^4]

The register says it is unclear when the cab was removed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association list shows the two-story cab as gone.[^5] Willhite's notes say not much remains, and the photo captions list parts of the base, metal parts and a coiled phone line.[^3]

Getting there is a short drive and walk.[^3] From the paved FR 25, Willhite's route takes the northern branch, forest road FR 2573, for roughly 1.7 miles to a bend with parking, where the trail begins.[^3] The walk follows the abandoned Wildcat Trail.[^3] Willhite lists the distance as 0.4 miles, with a few hundred feet of climbing round trip.[^3] Willhite reports that trees are returning to the site, which has cliffs on three sides.[^3] We found no source that describes the view from the site today, and no source that gives a current access rule or closure.
