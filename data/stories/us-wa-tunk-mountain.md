Tunk Mountain stands in Okanogan County, Washington, roughly 15 miles southeast of Tonasket.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service built the summit's first lookout in 1933 to spot fires.[^3] Its original cab was an L-4 model on a 40-foot pole tower.[^1][^3]

In the 1950s Washington's Department of Natural Resources (DNR) took over the tower.[^1] A 40-foot timber tower replaced the pole in 1966; its cab measures 14 by 14 feet.[^1][^3]

A list on firelookout.com names lookout staff from 1952 to 1962, with 1953 and 1954 marked not staffed.[^2] In 2005 a contractor hired by the Forest Service spent about a month in the tower while Bonaparte Lookout was under renovation.[^4] That year the local chapter also took over management and did some maintenance.[^4] NHLR tells a different story: it says American Resources bought the lookout in 2004 and staffed it for a month in 2005.[^1] In 2021 TrailChick reports that the lookout was temporarily staffed from Bonaparte, whose own lookout had to evacuate during the Spur/Walker Creek Fire.[^3]

After years of neglect, the tower was declared surplus in the 1990s.[^3] NHLR says W.R. Morrison then bought it and kept it on site under a special use permit.[^1] firelookout.com dates a private purchase to the mid-1990s and says ownership had passed to the State of Washington before that.[^2]

Today TrailChick, last updated in March 2026, says the lookout is privately owned and locked, although the summit area is open to the public and volunteers maintain the structure.[^3] NHLR still lists the state's Department of Natural Resources as administrator, so the sources disagree about who controls the site now.[^1] On a November 2017 visit the road was in reasonably good repair, but snow turned the visitor back a mile short of the lookout.[^3] The cab is locked, so do not expect to go inside.[^3]
