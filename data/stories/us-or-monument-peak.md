Monument Peak sits in the Willamette National Forest in Linn County, Oregon, about eight miles west-southwest of Detroit.[^3] The Forest Lookouts history places an alidade on the peak around 1914, and a lookout house in 1921.[^1] The Register starts its story with a camp in 1916, which it says had a cabin added in 1921.[^2]

The Linn County Fire Patrol association ran the station in its later years.[^1] By August 1942 it had completed a new lookout house on the peak, and Charles Graves was to be the first lookout to occupy it.[^1] The Register describes that cab as built in 1942 on the middle summit.[^2] Ed Cox and Henry Thompson went up that September to staff the station until the war ended.[^1]

The station was also a wartime AWS post, activated on August 13, 1942, and deactivated on September 22, 1943.[^1] In 1946 a survey said the 14-by-14 house sat on a concrete base and needed paint.[^1]

Mike Hobson was in charge of the lookout station around 1930.[^1] Albert Simons and his wife later staffed it through the late 1940s and into the 1950s.[^1] A 1956 report says they were on duty at the Monument Peak lookout station that year.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts history and the Register both date the removal to 1973.[^1][^2] firelookout.com says the cab was destroyed that year.[^3] The Register lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the peak today, or whether the public can reach it.
