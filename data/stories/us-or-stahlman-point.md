Stahlman Point sits just above Detroit Lake, about two miles south of the town of Detroit, in the Willamette National Forest.[^1][^2] The register describes an L-4 cabin perched on a short tower, put up in 1948 and taken away in 1966. From there a lookout could see up the valleys that drain into the lake, areas the mountaintop lookouts could not see.[^1]

A Eugene paper reported in May 1947 that a lookout was planned for the point, with work set to start on July 1.[^3] The register dates the cabin to 1948, a year after that planned start. The same clipping says trail crews were already busy preparing the summit, but no source we read says when construction actually began.[^1][^3]

The cabin had lookouts in the early 1950s.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com lists Clara Young as known lookout personnel in 1952.[^2] In July of that year The Oregon Statesman called her a veteran fire watcher in the Detroit ranger district.[^3] In June 1953 the same paper reported that the Detroit district's first lookout of the season had manned Stahlman Point.[^3] In September 1955 a forest guard from Marion Forks was brought in as a stand-in while the regular lookout went back to school.[^3]

The register says the cabin was removed in 1966, but firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year, so the two sources disagree on how it ended.[^1][^2] A hiking guide says the tower has since been demolished, though its foundation remains at the summit as a spot for group lunches.[^5]

The Forest Service lists Stahlman Point Trail #3449 as open, and says it ends at the former lookout site.[^4] The same notice adds that the Beachie Creek and Lionshead fires reached the area around the trail.[^4] The hiking guide describes the final climb as steep and rocky, and says the summit view takes in a wide swath of damage from the 2020 Lionshead Fire.[^5] The trail starts at the Stahlman Point Trailhead, reached from Highway 22 by Blowout Road (FR 10).[^5]
