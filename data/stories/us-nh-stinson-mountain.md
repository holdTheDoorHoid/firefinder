Stinson Mountain stands above Stinson Lake in Rumney, Grafton County.[^1][^2] Neither the fire tower nor the ski trail remains.[^2]

A display on the summit, quoted in one trip report, says the tower was established in 1911 by the New Hampshire Timberland Owners.[^3] It was a 16-foot wooden structure.[^3] The display also records a 27-foot steel tower, built in 1927.[^3]

In August 1939 the Portsmouth Herald reported that the White Mountain National Forest had taken over operation of three towers from the State Forestry Department, with Stinson Mountain in Rumney among them.[^1] An April 1941 item in the Groton Times reported that Frank Woodward of Rumney had officially taken up his post in the tower on April 13, the first time that year.[^1]

The summit display says the tower stayed in service until after 1967, and that it was dismantled and removed in 1985.[^3] The Forest Lookouts entry also marks it as removed.[^1]

Hikers and snowmobilers now reach the summit by separate trails, and the hiking route begins at a trailhead on Lower Doetown Road.[^2][^3] A winter trip report says the old tower's concrete foundations could still be seen through deep snow near the summit.[^3] The ski history page lists the summit ski trail under the White Mountain National Forest.[^2] The New Hampshire lookout list marks the tower as gone.[^4] Beyond these trip reports and listings, no source we read describes the site, so a visitor should expect trails rather than a tower.
