Mount Bailey lies immediately west of Diamond Lake, and it was used as a lookout point for years before a proper cabin arrived.[^3] In 1917 a newspaper reported that R.C. Agee would take charge of a government lookout station there.[^3] The Forest Service finished a telephone line to the summit in 1921.[^3] Permanent lines were out of the question because of winter snow, so the wire lay on the ground and was taken up each fall.[^3] A temporary station, with Arthur McIntosh of Riddle in charge, was planned for that year.[^3]

The permanent cabin went up in 1923, a D-6 cupola cabin.[^1][^2] Its pieces were cut in Roseburg, numbered, shipped to Medford and trucked toward Diamond Lake, and mules carried them the last fourteen miles to the top.[^3] In early September 1923 Grant McLaughlin was building it, having brought the sawn lumber in by pack horse.[^3]

A 1922 report said the station had helped detect a number of fires before they caused damage.[^3] In July 1928 Paul Geddes was the forest service lookout, and 17 visitors climbed to the summit that Sunday to meet him there.[^3]

Sources disagree about how the cabin ended.[^1][^2] Firetower.org says it was removed in 1952, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] The Oregon Websites and Watersheds Project also says it was destroyed in 1952, and it dates the lookout to 1917, years before the cabin.[^4]

Today no source we found describes what, if anything, is left on the summit.[^5] The Forest Service still lists a hiking trail to the top, where visitors find a crater, a rock garden, and a rock wall with a window overlooking Mount Thielsen.[^5]
