Turkey Butte Lookout sits in Coconino County, Arizona, in the Coconino National Forest.[^1] The Register says the lookout overlooks red-rock country close to Sedona.[^1] The Register describes a 32-foot Aermotor MC-24 tower built in 1937 and notes that this design is rare outside New Mexico and Arizona.[^1] The cab measures 14 feet by 14 feet.[^1] The Register says the cab is wood with no catwalk, and that it is based on the L-4 design but appears quite different.[^1] A Peakbagging photo page gives the same height, build year and cab size, and calls the tower steel.[^2]

The Register recorded the lookout on April 1, 2001, as US 374, AZ 19, with Dave Lorenz, director of the AZ/NM FFLA, as its nominator.[^1] The Register gives the elevation as 7,330 feet, while Peakbagging gives 7,374 feet. This story uses the Register's figure.[^1][^2]

Peakbagging's Arizona lookout table, revised October 2, 2024, codes Turkey Butte as usually staffed by the Forest Service.[^3] None of the sources read names the people who staffed it or a specific fire it watched. The trail page says the tower was set up to watch over Coconino National Forest and the nearby Sycamore Canyon and Secret Mountain areas. It adds that wildland fires have affected the surrounding forest over the years.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list shows the tower as standing under the same register number.[^5] Peakbagging's photo, taken July 1, 2024, calls it an active fire lookout.[^2] No source read describes a closure, rebuild, or move.[^1][^2][^4][^5]

The Register also says the site is well known to visitors.[^1] The trail page describes a 6.8-mile off-road route, rated easy and scenic, that begins near Forest Road 231 (Woody Mountain Road). It calls the route a primary access corridor to the lookout.[^4] It also says the route is not winter maintained and can become muddy and slick when wet.[^4] The sources read do not say whether visitors may enter the cab or climb the tower, so check with the Forest Service before a visit, and stay off any tower that is closed.
