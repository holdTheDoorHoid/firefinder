The Sky Top site above Lake Mohonk had a state lookout station as early as 1912, when the Conservation Commission set one up at that spot.[^2] The stone tower there now was built as a memorial to Albert K. Smiley, founder of the Mohonk Mountain House.[^2][^5] The Register says the Smiley family built it between 1921 and 1923.[^1] A 2023 Hudson Valley One article says a committee of family members and guests designed it after the state proposed a steel tower in 1919.[^5]

The tower was dedicated in August 1923.[^2] It is square and built of stone, with each face about thirty feet wide.[^2] According to the resort, its stone came from the ridge, and the pit left under the tower became a reservoir that is still there.[^4] Sources disagree on its height: the 1923 Kingston newspaper gives 65 feet, and the 2023 article gives 60 feet.[^2][^5]

In 1924 the state's tower at Sky Top was in the charge of Nathan Osborne.[^2] That summer, 14,000 people registered at the tower and 36 fires were reported from it.[^2] A 1926 article spelled the name Osborn.[^2]

The tower served as a fire lookout until 1971.[^1][^5] It is still standing, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list marks it that way.[^3] The Mohonk Mountain House now lists a moderate walk to the tower from the Lake Porch, and says that on a clear day the observation deck gives a view of six states.[^4]

The Register says visitors must be guests of the Preserve.[^1] It places the tower at the Mohonk Preserve and lists it under Private Lands, while a 2023 article says the Smiley family has owned Mohonk Mountain House across six generations.[^1][^5] Ask the resort before going, and do not climb the tower without permission.
