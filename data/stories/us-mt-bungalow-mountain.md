Bungalow Mountain was a fire lookout on the Flathead National Forest, and its site lies inside the Bob Marshall Wilderness.[^1] Firelookout.com says the site was developed with a camp in the 1920s, and that a gable-roofed L-4 cab was built there in 1930.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as a ground lookout that is gone.[^3]

Most of the staffing record comes from old newspaper clippings that the Forest Lookouts Montana page reprints.[^2] In August 1937 the Daily Inter Lake reported that a lookout named Roy Root had helped a girl with a sore throat at the station, which was the nearest place a call for help could be made.[^2] Firelookout.com names George Hossack as lookout in 1948, Harvey Robe in 1957 and Thomas Eck in 1958.[^1]

In August 1948 Hossack put out a timber fire by himself, after climbing six miles through brush and timber to reach it.[^2] In 1950 the Hungry Horse News called the lookout the most remote one in the country.[^2] The latest clipping on the page, from August 1966, says the lookout made two reports to the Forest Service in a single morning.[^2]

Firelookout.com says the cab was destroyed in 1971.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page lists it as removed in 1971.[^2] Neither source says how the cab was destroyed or taken down, and the two sources use different words for the same year.

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it. A 1948 clipping put the station four miles from the Continental Divide and 68 miles from any settlement.[^2] A 1949 clipping called it the most remote lookout in the country, and said it was the furthest from any motorized travel.[^2]
