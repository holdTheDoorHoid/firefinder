Diamond Mountain lies about three miles northeast of Sloatsburg in Rockland County, and it carried two fire towers, one after the other.[^1][^2]

The first was a steel tower built in 1935 by the Palisades State Park Commission, using steel from the old Bear Mountain tower.[^1] It came down in 1955, and its steel went into storage.[^1] In 1966 the Park Commission bought and erected an 80-foot Aermotor LS 40 tower with louvered aluminum windows in its observation cab. That tower stood for twenty years before it was removed in 1986.[^1]

The park stopped running its fire towers in the early 1980s, citing manpower shortages and budget restrictions.[^1] In 1989 the state Department of Environmental Conservation deactivated most of its fire observation stations, and the detection system that included the Diamond Mountain towers came to a halt.[^1] The list says no photos of either tower have been located. Some of the steel was later reused at the Sterling Fire Tower when Sterling Forest State Park was created in 1998.[^1]

Today the summit is reached by hiking trails in Harriman State Park.[^3] A 2020 hiking account describes a yellow-blazed Diamond Mountain Tower Trail and a climb to the top on the HTS Trail.[^3] That account does not say whether any footings, steel, or other part of the tower remains on the summit, and no source describes the tower site now.[^3]
