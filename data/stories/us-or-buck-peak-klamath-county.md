Buck Peak is a lookout site in Klamath County, Oregon, at 6,081 feet (1,853 meters).[^1] The register places it in the Winema National Forest.[^1]

A camp was started on the site in 1915.[^1] The register dates a wooden L-4 tower there to 1932.[^1] WillhiteWeb describes a 21-foot tower built by the Crater National Forest, with an L-4A cab measuring 14 by 14 feet under a hip roof.[^4] The register describes a 10-foot tower, which conflicts with that 21-foot height.[^1][^4]

The Department of the Interior approved the lookout without objections, in a letter dated September 3, 1932.[^3] On September 27, 1932, the Medford Mail Tribune reported that a lookout house was being built in the Dead Indian district.[^3]

In 1933, a panorama was shot from 24 feet above ground.[^4] In 1940, during one early storm, the lookout alone logged 124 lightning strikes.[^4] In 1941 the lookout was controlled by the Rogue River National Forest's Lake of the Woods district, and it operated 110 days.[^4]

Francis Bush staffed the lookout in 1943. One Sunday, lightning struck the tower while he was eating dinner, and the fire it started spread over about an acre and a half before crews controlled it.[^4]

Jack and Lorraine Holmes staffed the lookout in the early 1950s. In 1960, Laird Millburn, then 19, staffed it.[^4] The tower got new legs and bracing in 1962.[^4]

WillhiteWeb says the lookout was disposed of by burning in November 1968.[^4] The register says it was removed in 1968, and Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2]

WillhiteWeb lists the site as drive-up, but no source we found describes what remains there today or whether the road to it is open.[^4]
