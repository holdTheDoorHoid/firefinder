A July 1950 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says the Virginia Forest Service had recently finished a 100-foot steel fire tower at Ninde in King George County.[^1] The item calls it the 110th tower the Forest Service had erected.[^1] The chief forest warden, Lloyd Farmer, said the tower would help enforce the 4 o'clock burning law and would also be used for regular fire spotting.[^1]

The lookout list calls the site King George (Circle) and gives its coordinates as 38.26899 and -77.13837.[^2] Wikipedia places the community of Ninde several kilometres east of those coordinates, at 38°16′16″N 77°3′18″W.[^3] The 1950 item names Ninde and the list uses the name Circle, and the sources do not show whether they describe the same spot.[^1][^2]

The lookout list now marks the tower as gone.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. The sources also do not say whether the land is open to visitors, so anyone planning a visit should first find out who owns it.
