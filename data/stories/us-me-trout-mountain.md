A Maine Memory caption says the lookout station moved to Trout Mountain in 1931. The caption sits beside a photo of the Black Cat Mountain tower, so it may not describe the structure that stood on the summit later.[^4] The FFLA list shows an older Trout Mountain tower as gone, and the summit now sits inside the Nature Conservancy's Trout Mountain Preserve beside Baxter State Park.[^6][^1] The Conservancy says that older tower now lies in ruins on the forest floor.[^1]

The sources give the summit's elevation two ways: 1,409 feet in the Conservancy's description and 1,427 feet in the builder's account.[^1][^2] A fire tower that stood there was closed in the 1970s, and the builder says safety concerns were the reason.[^2][^3]

The present tower went up in 2020.[^3] The Conservancy's Maine chapter hired David Vana of Davana LLC to build it.[^2] He used the middle sections of a 120-foot tower that had been in use until 2017, when he bought it.[^2] His account says that tower dated from the 1940s and came from Alabama.[^2] The Conservancy's page says the tower "started life in the longleaf pine forests of Alabama."[^1] The FFLA lists the tower as "Relocated from Alabama," but that label tells only part of the story.[^6] The builder's account describes a rebuild: six tons of original galvanized steel were stripped and recoated for the salvaged pieces, and four tons of newly galvanized steel went into parts to complete the Maine installation.[^2]

The tower is about 80 feet tall.[^1][^3] A 2.7-mile hike reaches the summit, and the climb ends with six flights of stairs and a 360-degree view of Katahdin.[^3] The Conservancy warns visitors to use caution and says any climb is at their own risk.[^1]

After the tower went up in July, some people objected that it shone in the sun and ruined the view of Katahdin.[^5] Vana told the Adirondack Explorer that the zinc finish would lose its gleam in about a year if nothing was done to it.[^5]
