Grizzly Peak's steel lookout went up on the summit in 1924, after a major blaze in 1923 prompted its construction. A 1960 report says it was built the year after Berkeley's disastrous fire.[^2]

The register describes a 7-by-7 observation cabin on a 40-foot open steel tower, built with funds from the Contra Costa Hill Fire Protective Association and the City of Berkeley.[^1] The newspapers disagree on the height: a 1924 item says 35 feet, a 1925 item says 40 feet, and a 1960 item gives 60 feet for the tower and a one-story watchman's house, while another 1960 item says 40 feet.[^2]

Glen Gadbury, the state forester, was named fire warden in 1924, and Harry Piper was the watchman by 1925.[^2] A tile house with Mission architecture stood beside the tower.[^2] A 1960 report calls Piper the first man on the hill and Gene Smith the tower's third boss, and says Smith spotted 160 fires in the Berkeley Hills during his years on the peak.[^2]

Money was a recurring problem. State aid had been withdrawn by March 1933, when the city manager proposed that Berkeley pay the full salary of a lookout on Grizzly Peak.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed in 1960 when funding was withdrawn to staff the lookout.[^1] The 1960 Oakland Tribune reports give a different reason: the acting city manager told the council that maintenance was no longer needed, and the razing was set to take effect June 30, when the city's lease expires.[^2] A 1962 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, reports a steel tower on the summit in good condition, which leaves the removal date unclear.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the summit today.[^1][^2] A 2020 Claremont Canyon Conservancy post describes spots on Grizzly Peak Boulevard that it calls the lookouts as places people gather for the sunset. It says newly installed signs there read 'No Smoking; Closed from 9 PM-6 AM,' and that Oakland stepped up patrols to educate people about them.[^3] No source ties those spots to the old tower.
