Tower Mountain Lookout sits on the Spokane Indian Reservation in Stevens County, Washington.[^5] The steel tower went up in 1975. It stands 53 feet tall with a live-in cab, and the National Historic Lookout Register calls it the site's first tower.[^1][^2][^3] The pages I could reach do not say why it was built.

The name can confuse visitors. Roadside signs call the place Lookout Mountain, but the tower itself is labelled Tower Mountain, and a hillside nearby rises higher.[^4][^1]

The lookout has had staff on hand every summer.[^1] TrailChick's page, last updated in March 2024, reports that staff are still on duty each fire season.[^3] None of the sources I could reach mentions a notable fire at the lookout. TrailChick also says it is the summit's only lookout building.[^3]

As far as these sources show, it is still standing and in use. None of the sources I could reach mentions it being moved, burned or closed.

The recommended route starts on the paved West End Road, reached from Highway 25 northeast of Fort Spokane, then turns onto the gravel Sand Creek Road.[^3][^4] The summit is about 6.5 miles from there.[^3] Road conditions shift with the season and recent rain, though sources say most cars can get there.[^4] Because the lookout sits on the Spokane Indian Reservation, check access with the reservation before you go. None of the pages I read gives a visitor rule.
