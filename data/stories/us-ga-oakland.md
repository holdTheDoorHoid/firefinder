The Oakland Fire Tower stands on the Oconee National Forest in Greene County, Georgia.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service built it in 1942, and the Register lists the agency as its administrator, with the Oconee Ranger District as cooperator.[^1]

The Register describes an 80-foot tower with a metal cab that is 7 feet by 7 feet.[^1] It was added to the Register on July 26, 2007.[^1]

The Register marks the tower as staffed, but it names no lookout, gives no staffing dates and says nothing about which fires the tower watched.[^1]

The latest condition report found, dated October 9, 2016, was written by a volunteer steward who had taken on the role.[^1] The fenced site has a gate locked with Rayle EMC padlocks, and the steward had no access inside the fence.[^1] From outside the locked gate, the steward used binoculars and reported that the glass in the cab appeared intact, with rust on the metal sides and roof.[^1] The steward also counted four antennas of unknown ownership on the tower, and noted a small building inside the fence with power and antenna lines running to it.[^1]

The Register gives no public-access terms and records no NHLR sign posted.[^1] The 2016 report describes a locked gate and no NHLR sign, so the site appears closed to visitors.[^1] The Register lists the Oconee Ranger District as cooperator, so check with that office before planning any visit, and do not try to climb the tower.[^1]

[^1]: Oakland Fire Tower, National Historic Lookout Register (nhlr.org), http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/ga/oakland-fire-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
