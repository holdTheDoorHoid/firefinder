The Mathison Peak lookout stood a few miles east of Mendocino, near the head of Little River.[^1]

In late summer 1934, Ukiah newspapers reported that a 30-foot tower was going up on the Mathison place, with Frank Mathison put in charge of the new station.[^1] An earlier notice, from 1927, names a "Mattson lookout station" near the Ellison schoolhouse on the Littleriver road. The sources do not say whether that was the same place.[^1]

Frank Mathison was the lookout in June 1935.[^1] By May 1949 the station had opened for the season with Miss Emma Mathison in charge.[^1] A 1956 entry on the same page says the lookout worker also served as a Ground Observer Corps Skywatch observer.[^1]

Malcolm Macdonald, who wrote about burns on his family's land near the Albion River, says Emma Mathison often occupied the lookout in the two decades after World War II.[^2] He recalls that she might phone his family when smoke from one of their burns caught her attention.[^2][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the Mathison Peak site as gone, with no date.[^4] In his 2017 essay, Macdonald recalls that when he was a youngster in the late 1950s, a fire lookout stood only a couple of miles from his family's burns.[^3]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
