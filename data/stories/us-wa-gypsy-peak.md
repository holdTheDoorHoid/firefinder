Gypsy Peak rises in the Salmo-Priest Wilderness of Pend Oreille County, Washington.[^1][^2] Wikipedia gives its height as more than 7,320 feet,[^2] and WillhiteWeb puts it at 7,326 feet and calls it the highest peak in Eastern Washington.[^3] The two heights differ only slightly.[^2][^3]

The Crowell Ridge lookout, a gable-roofed L-4 cab built in 1929, had a patrol point on Gypsy Peak about three miles north-northeast of it.[^1][^4] The register calls that point a patrol lookout.[^1] WillhiteWeb says the peak was reported as a patrol point tied to the Crowell Ridge lookout, and its author saw no sign of a phone line during a traverse of the ridge.[^3] The same author also noticed a few trees near the summit that had been cut long ago, possibly for unrelated reasons.[^3]

WillhiteWeb's undated trail notes describe hiking routes to the summit.[^3] It names the Bear Pasture trailhead as the quickest start, notes that the road to it is closed to hikers from August 15 to protect grizzly habitat, and gives 3.5 miles one way with 1,800 feet of gain.[^3] It also notes that there is no water on the ridge, and advises picking a day with no thunderstorm activity, because the walker will spend a long stretch on the highest ridge around.[^3]

WillhiteWeb's page describes no standing structure on the summit.[^3]
