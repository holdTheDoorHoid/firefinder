Sources do not agree on the name or the summit. The Canada Lookouts website lists the tower as Norberg/Griffin Lake, on Batchawana Mountain near Batchawana.[^1] Ontario High Points, a peak-listing site, puts the tower on the higher peak it calls Griffin Lake Peak, a name it says is not official. It notes that Ministry of Natural Resources maps label the lower summit Batchawana Mountain.[^2]

A clipping headed July 23, 1959, reprinted on the Canada Lookouts page, says the Griffin Lake lookout tower reported smoke in the Adelaide Lake area.[^1] Sparks from a snapped power line started the blaze, and rangers kept it to about 0.6 hectares (1.5 acres).[^1] Officers could not tell whether a bear caught in the line had broken it or only come to it afterward.[^1]

A January 2009 Ontario High Points post says a well-preserved fire tower marks the broad summit of the peak it calls Griffin Lake Peak.[^2] SummitPost says the Norberg / Griffin Lake fire tower stands on the summit.[^3] A list of still-standing Ontario towers includes the Norberg/ Griffin Lake Tower on Batchawana Mountain. The same list says that, as of 2013, the Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources had decided to take down many of the towers that were still standing. It adds that the ministry no longer maintains them and asks people not to climb them.[^4]

The Superior Hiking blog describes an 11 km round trip, rated moderate. From Sault Ste. Marie, the route runs north on Highway 17 for 53 km and then turns onto Mile 38 Road.[^5] From the parking spot, the walk goes 2.2 km along a gravel road, then 3 km on a trail to the tower. According to the blog, the entire route follows an old road.[^5]

The same blog describes the place as the former location of an old forest fire lookout, so the sources disagree on whether a tower still stands there today.[^5]
