Black Rock is a summit in the Umpqua National Forest, in Douglas County, Oregon.[^1] The earliest lookout on the Umpqua, dated to 1899, was little more than a viewpoint with a compass.[^3] By about 1911 a sturdier arrangement stood there: an alidade mounted on a pole platform, used to pinpoint fires.[^3]

The Register dates the first cab to 1911.[^1] The newspapers tell a different story. In September 1914 a paper reported that work on the glass house atop the summit was being pushed toward completion.[^3] A February 1915 account described a small station with walls of stone and concrete, plate glass above them, and timbers hauled up the mountain.[^3] The sources do not agree on when the glass house went up, so the story gives both dates.

The lookout gained a cupola and a new roof in 1926.[^1][^3] In 1938 an L-4 cab was built on top of the old lower walls.[^1][^2][^3]

Lookouts named in the newspapers include J. H. Bosard, assigned to the post in 1917; Minerva Bidwell, who served in 1919; and Ernest Taylor, stationed there in 1935.[^3] In 1941 the lookout was staffed for 80 days and reported over a forest telephone line to the Big Camas Ranger Station.[^3] A September 1942 "Report on Lookout and Guard Stations" described the trip as a 65-mile drive from Roseburg, followed by an eight-mile trail to the summit.[^3]

The fate of the cab is also reported differently. The Register says it was removed in 1965, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page says the Forest Service set fire to the structure once it was taken out of use.[^3]

The concrete foundation appears to remain on the summit.[^3][^4] A 2007 account on the Forest Lookouts page says it has weathered the elements.[^3] A hiker's blog post dated June 21, 2024 says the old foundation was still there.[^4] That hiker also wrote that the access road, Road 950, runs 2.6 miles to Whitehorse Meadows Trailhead, and that the old trail still appears on topo maps, though the Forest Service had given it up at some point.[^4] We found no agency statement on whether the summit is open to visitors, so check with the Umpqua National Forest before going.
