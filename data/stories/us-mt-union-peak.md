Union Peak Lookout is a groundhouse on top of Union Peak in Granite County, Montana, about 28 miles east of Missoula.[^1][^2][^3] The present cab measures 15 by 15 feet and has a gable-flattop roof with a slight pitch, and the Register calls its design unique.[^1]

The Blackfoot Forest Protective set up a crow's nest tree platform on the peak in 1930.[^1] The Register says a 70-foot steel tower replaced it five years later.[^1] The firelookout.com page describes a 72-foot steel tower and tent cabin, first used in the 1930s, instead.[^2]

Dating the present cab is harder. The Register says the groundhouse was built in 1963.[^1] The firelookout.com page says the present ground cab was built in 1952.[^2] The two sources do not agree, and the pages read do not settle it.

In 1973 the Montana Department of Natural Resources and Conservation took over responsibility for the lookout.[^1] The Register lists the state's Forestry Division as the administrator, and gives March 31, 2003 as the registration date, under US 476, MT 37.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^4] The firelookout.com page says the current staffing status is not known.[^2]

The Register gives the elevation as 6,723 feet, while firelookout.com gives 6,810 feet.[^1][^2]

The marker database places the lookout at the end of Union Peak Road.[^3] The pages read do not say whether the public can climb the tower or stay in the cab. Check with DNRC before planning a visit.
