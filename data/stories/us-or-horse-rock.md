Horse Rock stands on the divide between the Calapooia and Mohawk rivers, south of Crawfordsville in Linn County, Oregon.[^1][^3] The Linn Fire Patrol association kept a lookout station on the summit, and a 1913 newspaper report names Elmer Fox as the person in charge of it.[^3]

The register dates the cabin on the site to 1939.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same year.[^2] The station was older than that cabin, though, because the 1913 report already describes a lookout there.[^3]

Telephones were a problem for the station. A 1913 report described a line already built from Crawfordsville to Horse Rock.[^3] A 1936 item in The Forest Log, however, described an 18-mile line from Holley being built to the rock, and recalled that a lookout once had to walk seven miles to reach a phone after spotting a fire.[^3] The 1913 line and the 1936 account of a seven-mile walk are hard to reconcile, and neither says when the station first had a telephone.

A June 1940 note in The Forest Log said William Newton had held the Horse Rock post for twelve years and died on April 28.[^3]

Both the register and firelookout.com put the end of the cabin in the 1940s. The register says it was removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2]

The register gives the site's elevation as 2,864 feet (873 metres).[^1] Wikipedia describes Horse Rock Ridge as a Bureau of Land Management research natural area, gives 2,864 feet as the top of its elevation range, and notes a hiking trail to the ridge summit.[^4] None of our sources says whether the old cabin site lies inside that area. No source we read describes what remains at the site today or whether the public can reach it, so check with the managing agency before you go looking.
