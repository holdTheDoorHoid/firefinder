A 1921 state agriculture report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts history, records a new tower and telephone line on Westmore Mountain that year.[^1] The same history says the New England Forest Emergency fund later built a new steel tower on Bald Mountain, in work it dates to 1938 to 1940; whether that replaced the 1921 tower is not stated.[^1] The Register gives the summit's elevation as 3,304 feet and lists March 14, 2014 as its registration date.[^2] A 2014 chapter report says the tower was added to the Register that year.[^3]

The tower and summit cabin were restored in 2002, according to a 2016 chapter report.[^4] Later chapter reports describe roof trouble. A 2008 report said wind damage had left the lookout's roof in need of repair, and that the fix was due in 2008.[^5] The 2010 report said the tower roof still needed replacing, and the 2012 report said it needed repair, with no plans offered.[^6][^7]

A 2013 chapter report says a major Northeast Kingdom project restored the Ranger's Cabin on Bald Mountain in Westmore.[^8]

An August 2016 visit report on the Register describes plenty of graffiti in the cab, a few bullet holes, and part of the roof blown away, while also calling the restored cabin in great condition.[^2] The chapter's own October 2016 hike found the tower and summit cabin in good shape.[^4] The two accounts differ on the condition of the cab that year.

A 2022 chapter report says the tower roof was replaced the year before, which reads as 2021.[^9] A 2024 renovation replaced the tower's wooden components with white oak.[^10] In 2025 the summit cabin received a new metal roof, and interior railings were installed on the tower.[^11]

The 2022 report counts Bald among the state-owned towers it studied.[^9] Bald Mountain is also the highest peak in Willoughby State Forest, which the Vermont Department of Forests, Parks, and Recreation manages.[^12]

The Register's visit report says directions to the tower are signed, and that blazes mark the Long Pond Trail.[^2] The newest chapter report read is from 2025, so current public access and any closures are not confirmed here. Check with the state forest office before planning a visit, and do not climb a tower that is closed.
