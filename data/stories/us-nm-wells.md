The Wells Lookout stands on the Jicarilla Reservation in Rio Arriba County, New Mexico, at an elevation of 8,120 feet.[^1] The site first served as a lookout in 1930, and International Derrick Co. supplied parts for it in 1931.[^1]

In June 1932 a 60-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab went up. The register gives its reported cost as $92 and marks that figure with an exclamation point.[^1]

The following year the site was staffed by a "lookout-fireman."[^1] According to the register, that label referred to a towerman who would respond first to any smoke he reported that lay close by.[^1]

Our sources do not say who staffed the tower after that, or name any fire it watched.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list shows the tower as standing.[^2] The register added it on September 1, 1999, as US 319 and NM 7.[^1]

The Bureau of Indian Affairs administers the site.[^1] Our sources describe no public access, so anyone interested should ask the tribal government before going near it.
