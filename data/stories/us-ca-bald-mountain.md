Bald Mountain is a fire lookout on the Sequoia National Forest, at 9,339 feet (2,847 m) above sea level.[^1] The Forest Service calls it a manned lookout tower in the Domeland Wilderness.[^2]

The register says the Sequoia National Forest replaced an earlier lookout here in 1954. The cab and the 40-foot (12.2 m) Aermotor tower were hauled in by mule train, and the road did not reach the mountain for another decade.[^1] The cab is a 14-by-14-foot metal cab with a corrugated flat roof.[^1]

Fire-lookouts.org gives different dates. It puts the present structure at 1955 and says the site came back into use in 1951.[^3] It also says an earlier lookout, shown on a 1935 map at another spot, was abandoned when a lookout at Sherman Peak was built in 1936.[^3]

Logging roads crossed the plateau in 1967, and fire-lookouts.org says the lookout became accessible to the public that year.[^3] The register and fire-lookouts.org also disagree on its listing date: June 10, 2001 in the register, and April 21, 1999 on fire-lookouts.org.[^1][^3]

By 2009 the lookout was in trouble. A report summarized on fire-lookouts.org quotes the Forest's engineering assessment as finding it "not worth the liability or repair," and says the Forest planned to remove the cab in spring 2010.[^3] Removal was delayed for lack of funding. In 2011 the lookout came off the threatened list after volunteer groups sought to save it.[^3]

Today the Forest Service lists summer visiting hours from 9:30 am until 6 pm, Thursday through Monday, and requires pets to be on a 6-foot leash.[^2] To reach the lookout, take the Bald Mountain Lookout road from Sherman Pass Road.[^2] The register says it is still in active service, and often used to relay fire traffic for the BLM and the Inyo National Forest.[^1]

The sources disagree on the county. The register places the lookout in Tulare County, while a western lookout list files it under Kern County and the Sequoia National Forest.[^1][^4]
