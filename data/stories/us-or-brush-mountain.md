The Brush Mountain lookout began in 1913, when a sighting instrument called an alidade was set on the summit and a cabin went up a mile to the east.[^1][^2] The site lies about 20 miles east-northeast of Ashland, in the Rogue River National Forest.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on when the cabin gave way to a tree tower. The register says 1916, and firelookout.com says 1915.[^1][^2] The tower was a crow's nest about 104 feet up, reached by a spiral ladder of wooden pegs and set in a spar tree.[^1][^3][^5] The observer built the ladder at odd moments and without help, packing the pegs and poles up the mountain on his back.[^3] A 1925 note names Dan Pederson as the man who built it.[^3] A stone cabin followed about 1918.[^1][^2]

Dan Pederson was sent to the summit in the summer of 1915, when he was 40.[^3] A 1971 Herald and News column says he spent 11 years on the mountain.[^3] In July 1918 the lookout phoned the forest supervisor's office in Medford about a new fire near the state line. The fire lay in another association's territory, so no crew went out.[^3] In 1924 the lookout duties moved to the Old Baldy lookout.[^3]

Sources disagree on when the lookout ended. The register and firelookout.com say the site was abandoned about 1930.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as Abandoned.[^4] A Show n Tell sheet dates the lookout to 1916 through 1930.[^5] The 1971 column's eleven years, counted from 1915, would end around 1926.[^3] By 1971 the tall Shasta fir had tumbled, and the column said the lookout was no more.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com describe the site as now in ruins.[^1][^2] A caption on the cover of a Forest Service history of the forest describes a replica of the lookout tree on Brush Mountain. The sources we read do not say where that replica is or whether it still stands.[^6] They also do not say whether the summit is open to visitors.
