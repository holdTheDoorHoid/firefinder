Cowee Bald Lookout stands on the Cowee Bald summit in Jackson County, North Carolina, inside the Nantahala National Forest.[^1] The Register gives the lookout site an elevation of 4,892 feet.[^1] Other sources give the peak as 4,944 feet.[^2][^3] The sources do not explain the gap.

Sources disagree about when the tower went up. The Register dates it to 1936 and describes a 40-foot steel tower with a cab fit for living in, plus a catwalk.[^1] BusyHiker says it was built in 1933 and is 30 feet tall.[^3] An online photo album credits a Civilian Conservation Corps company from the F-20 camp with the 1936 build, and it also gives the height as 30 feet.[^2] The Register says the tower was rebuilt in 1980.[^1]

The Register says the lookout has not been staffed on a regular basis since the 1960s.[^1] It says the North Carolina and U.S. Forest Services now share it for emergencies.[^1] The photo album says it was used for fire detection until the mid-1990s, which does not match the Register.[^2] None of the sources name a fire it watched.

A September 2020 visit report on the Register's site found the tower and its access road in excellent condition.[^1] BusyHiker also says it remains in good condition.[^3]

Getting there takes a long drive on FR 70 from Franklin.[^3] Park at the turnaround before the gate, then walk the gravel road for about half a mile.[^3] The top cab is normally locked, and BusyHiker calls the views from the stairs limited in one passage and decent in another.[^3] BusyHiker warns that one or more gates are locked in winter, and the hike to the summit could then be many miles.[^3] The September 2020 visit report says its author did not notice any signage on the tower.[^1] Check current conditions with the Nantahala National Forest before going.

The Register listed the tower on December 30, 1993, as US 71, NC 3.[^1] Joe Nicholson of the Wayah Ranger District nominated it.[^1]
