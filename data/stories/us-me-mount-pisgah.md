Mount Pisgah sits in the towns of Winthrop and Wayne, in Kennebec County, Maine.[^3][^1] The Maine Forest Service built a 60-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab on the summit in 1949.[^2][^1][^3] The register describes it as an Aermotor tower.[^1]

The Kennebec Land Trust says the tower was in use from 1949 to 1991, about four decades.[^3] A Forest Lookouts summary dates its decommissioning to 1992, so the two sources differ by a year.[^2]

By August 2008 the Town of Winthrop owned the tower, which it managed jointly with the Kennebec Land Trust. The trust holds a conservation easement on the property.[^2][^3] A 2012 entry in the same summary reports that vandals pulled a safety rail away from the cab's entry hole.[^2]

Today the 94-acre tower property, owned by the town, sits inside the trust's 1,100-acre conservation area.[^3] A 0.7-mile Tower Trail climbs to the tower, which the trust describes as open to the public.[^3][^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4]
