Livingstone Fire Lookout stands on Thrift Peak in the Oldman River area of Alberta, within Bob Creek Wildland Provincial Park.[^1][^2] An archive of Alberta lookouts files the site under Livingston Ridge, in the Bow Crow Forest.[^3]

The original lookout building on the ridge was a 12 x 12 wood frame structure, built in 1956.[^3] A Calgary Herald report, which the archive quotes, says forestry crews spent about ten days in July 1975 blasting rock for the foundation of a new lookout a few yards away.[^3]

When a group climbed the peak in June 2009, the lookout observer greeted them on the helipad and shared tales of the lookout.[^1] According to the lookout log the writer read, the lookout is rarely visited.[^1]

Alberta reports 127 wildfire lookout sites, each staffed by a lookout observer who usually works there from April to October.[^4]

The most recent first-hand account we found is from June 2009.[^1] A trail listing calls the route to Thrift Peak moderate and one way, within the park.[^2] The 2009 writer says the usual approach means crossing the Oldman River and walking several kilometres of road.[^1] We found no source that says whether the lookout building is open to visitors today.
