The lookout first stood on Red Mountain, in the upper Cow Creek country of the Umpqua National Forest in Douglas County, Oregon, before it was moved to the Tiller Ranger Station.[^1][^4]

Sources disagree on what was there first. The National Historic Lookout Register calls the early structure a tree tower that the Umpqua National Forest built around 1920.[^1] A former-sites listing for the mountain says a crow's nest went up there about 1920.[^2] The Roaming Civic adds that a 25-foot platform crow's nest with a tent camp was developed there in 1921.[^3]

In 1928 a D-6 style cupola cabin, 12 feet by 12 feet, replaced that early setup.[^1][^2] The cabin was staffed into the 1960s.[^1][^3] It then sat unused on the mountain until it was moved to Tiller in the fall of 1985.[^3][^4] Restoration work was completed in June 1986, and community volunteers did more work in 2000.[^3]

The register added the cabin in August 2011 as US 911, OR 116, and it describes the building as restored as a historic exhibit.[^1] The former-sites listing for the original mountain location is a separate record, US 896, OR 263.[^2]

Today the cabin is part of the Tiller Historical Trail, reached by a short trail from the parking lot at the ranger station.[^3][^4] The Forest Service asks visitors to stop by the station to see whether the lookout is unlocked.[^4] A visitor in May 2024 found it closed up and advised asking about access Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 3:30 p.m.[^3] That visitor's hours differ from the Forest Service listing, which gives Thursday and Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., so check before you go.[^3][^4]
