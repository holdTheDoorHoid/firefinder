The Roxbury lookout stood on Painter Hill, on Painter Ridge in the town of Roxbury, in Litchfield County.[^1] A 1951 newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page calls the tower 62 feet high on a ridge it puts at 1,100 feet.[^1] The tower had a telephone, a topographic map and instruments for pinpointing smoke, and the item calls it one of the central hubs of the state's forest fire operation under the Connecticut Park and Forest Commission.[^1]

A 1951 item says the tower operator was on duty from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.[^1] The 1951 observer, a two-season veteran, lived at the tower's foot in a green and white portable cabin with his dog, which went up the 62-foot stairway to the observation room.[^1] In 1956 Mrs. Willard Vaill of Warren came back for her third season as observer. A 1960 item says Mrs. Willard Vaille, Jr., of Warren was on duty again.[^1]

The 1951 item said the public could visit in the spring and autumn fire seasons, and that people were always invited.[^1] A 1956 item says people could visit during duty hours, and that radio connected the tower with others across the state.[^1]

No source we found describes the site now, so it is not known whether the tower or any footings remain, or whether the hilltop is open to visitors.

[^1]: Painter Hill - FOREST LOOKOUTS, Forest Lookouts (eastern US), https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/painter-hill.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
