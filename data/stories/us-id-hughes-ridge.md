The site began in 1926 with a crow's nest in a tree, plus a second for patrol four miles to the north.[^2][^3] A 1932 Spokane newspaper item, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts history site, describes a new station on poles 60 feet high under construction near Hughes Meadows.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts and firelookout.com instead date a 40-foot L-4 tower to about 1933, and the sources do not reconcile the two accounts.[^2][^3] The present tower went up in 1953.[^2][^3] The register calls it a 41-foot wooden tower with a 14-by-14-foot live-in cab.[^1]

The lookout sits in an out-of-the-way part of the Idaho Panhandle National Forests, which the register says is home to woodland caribou and grizzly bears.[^1] Named lookouts include Vern Marschall, who staffed it in the summers of 1965 and 1966, Kenneth Perkins in 1967 and Jean Duncan in 1970.[^1][^2][^3] In 1977 a Coos Bay newspaper item, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, describes Janelle Peek taking a summer job as a Forest Service lookout there.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts and firelookout.com name the Kaniksu National Forest instead.[^2][^3]

The register listed the lookout on August 1, 1995, and describes it as the last surviving L-R tower in Boundary County.[^1][^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists it as a hike-in staffed lookout.[^2] Firelookout.com reports summer staffing.[^3]
