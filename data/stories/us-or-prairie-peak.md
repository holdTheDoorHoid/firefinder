Prairie Mountain, also listed as Prairie Peak, is a summit in the Coast Range on the line between Lane and Benton counties.[^1] In 1913 a newspaper reported plans for a trail, a telephone line and a lookout point on the peak, and by 1914 the trail and telephone line had been built.[^1]

The register and firelookout.com say a platform tower and camp went up in 1913, and that a D-6 cupola cabin replaced them in 1932.[^2][^3] The Oregon collection, however, places the first lookout house in 1920 and 1921.[^1] In 1920 John Rust and his brother hauled the building materials over a seven-mile trail, and a carpenter, Jack Sandburn, put up the building.[^1] In 1921 more material went up on horseback.[^1]

In 1914 a Weeks Law warden was stationed on the summit through the summer.[^1] In 1917 H. M. Mayo, a lookout there, reported 160 forest fires in sight that summer.[^1] In 1924 V. G. Backman, the lookout, reported 76 fires between mid-June and July 31.[^1]

In 1946 the Polk-Benton District of the State Board of Forestry, which had only just been organized, was to take over and keep up the station under an agreement.[^1] The 1958 season brought a new three-story lookout, built as a joint project of Western Lane and Polk-Benton.[^1] The two districts split the materials bill evenly, which came to $2,214.74.[^1]

According to the collection, the Kyle Brothers bought the old cupola building, moved it about a quarter mile, and then used it as a line shack for cattle grazing on the peak.[^1] The register and firelookout.com put the move at half a mile east.[^2][^3]

The register describes the 1958 tower as aluminum-sided, while firelookout.com calls it wooden R-6.[^2][^3] The register says the tower was gone by 1985, and firelookout.com gives 1985 as the year it was gone.[^2][^3] A Forest Fire Lookout Association county list also marks it gone.[^4]

Around 2012 the long unused site was reactivated with Forest Watch cameras.[^1] Forest Watch is a wildfire smoke detection system that the Oregon Department of Forestry and the three Forest Protective Associations operate.[^5] The sources consulted do not describe what remains on the summit now, or whether the public can reach it.
