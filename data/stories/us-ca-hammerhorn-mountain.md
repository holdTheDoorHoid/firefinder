Hammerhorn Mountain rises to about 2,300 metres in the Mendocino National Forest, in Trinity County.[^1] Sources disagree on when its lookout went up.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says news reports place construction in late 1921, with the cabin probably in service for the 1922 fire season, and it calls the building a California Region 5 Plan 4A ground cabin.[^1] A November 1921 item in a Mariposa paper backs the early date. It says a deputy forest supervisor had been overseeing the building of the lookout house at Hammerhorn mountain.[^2] The register's own "Built" field, though, reads 1924.[^1]

The station had seasonal lookouts.[^2] In July 1930 C.M. Bucknell left to spend the summer there, and in December 1930 a 1,200-gallon water tank was being built at the lookout.[^2] The Forest Service named him again for the 1932 season.[^2] In September 1946 a patrol flight dropped mail at the station, but strong winds and high flying speed sent the parcels down in rough country about a quarter mile from the lookout.[^2] Mrs. Williams, who was there, had trouble finding them.[^2]

The register gives no removal date.[^1] A 1948 photo shows the cabin in rough condition.[^1]

Today the summit is a small graveled flat.[^3] A June 2026 hiking trip report found only a few remnants of the lookout, plus old nails gathered at one rock.[^3] The site lies on national forest land, and a 1942 survey description places the summit in the Middle Eel Primitive Area.[^1][^2] Visitors reach it on foot, and the last stretch is a short scramble, about 10 feet, with plenty of hand and foot holds.[^3]
