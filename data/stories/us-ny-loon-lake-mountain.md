Loon Lake Mountain's fire tower dates to 1917, when a steel-frame Aermotor tower about 35 feet tall went up on the summit in Franklin County.[^3][^4] It replaced a wooden tower that Wikipedia says was built in 1912.[^3] The register tells a different early story: it says the tower was reconstructed in 1927 after a hurricane blew it down.[^1] Wikipedia does not mention a 1927 rebuild, so that part is unconfirmed.[^3]

The tower was added to the National Register of Historic Places on December 5, 2008, as part of a multiple listing of New York Forest Preserve fire observation stations.[^3][^4] It has been unused since 1971, and its stairs were removed to keep people from climbing it.[^3] The condition reports are not recent. A 2007 visit found the steel and cab structurally sound but in need of considerable work before public use, and a 2018 visit found the windows, wood and paint in poor shape.[^1]

NY DEC rebuilt the hiking trail in 2013, and the register calls the hike a popular destination.[^1] A 2018 visit found the parking lot well plowed and the trail well marked, though it noted that active logging may be going on in the area.[^1] DEC says the tower has not yet been restored, though the summit still offers views.[^2] Across New York, fire towers began to be phased out in the 1980s.[^2]

Visitors can hike the trail, but the tower itself is not open to climbing, since its stairs were removed.[^1][^3] Treat the structure as closed.
