The Garden Mountain Fire Tower, also called ET-12 and the Bays Mountain Park Fire Tower, was built in 1937 by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1][^3] It stands on Holston River Mountain, the ridge that borders Bays Mountain Park, although a 1953 newspaper placed the lookout atop Bays Mountain.[^3][^4] The register gives its elevation as 2,393 feet, and a regional table gives 2,405 feet.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree on height. The register and the Bays Mountain history site both say the tower is 100 feet tall.[^1][^3] The 1953 Times-News describes a 60-foot tower with a seven-foot-square lookout shelter at the top.[^4]

The Bays Mountain history site says the tower served the Forestry Service into the 1960s.[^3] In December 1953 the Times-News profiled lookout Govan Smith Jr., who spent five long months each year in the shelter.[^4] He used a walkie-talkie to reach the fire department at Holston Ordnance Works, and a box telephone to reach neighboring towers.[^4] He also made three weather checks a day at a state station below the tower.[^4] Nights were spent in a one-room cabin that the state built near the foot of the tower.[^4]

The register lists the tower as US 1401, TN 37.[^1] Don Gouge nominated it, and the register recorded it on March 24, 2020.[^1] The register also says the tower overlooks Kingsport, and it lists the City of Kingsport as the administering body.[^1]

The register lists the location as Bays Mountain Park.[^1] Visit Kingsport has advertised a ranger-guided early-arrival hike of 3.3 miles round trip.[^5] The final stretch of the hike is steep, with steps and switchbacks, and the tower is not ADA compliant.[^5] Visitors can hike up inside it.[^5] Check with the City of Kingsport or Bays Mountain Park before you go, because hike rules and hours can change.
