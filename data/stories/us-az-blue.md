Blue Lookout stands on the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest in Greenlee County, Arizona, at about 9,346 feet [^1][^2]. It was built in 1933 as an Aermotor MC-24 lookout on a tower about 31 feet 6 inches tall [^2]. The National Historic Lookout Register calls it a sister structure to Big Lake Lookout and says it was also built in 1933 [^1].

A July 1922 newsletter item describes a bank robber who was chased to the lookout and shot by lawmen at the guard cabin some weeks earlier [^2]. A Forest Service trail guide dates a robbery at a bank in McNary to 1921, and says a posse member shot the man as he stepped out of the cabin with his hands up [^3]. The two accounts agree on the cabin but not on the date, so I have not settled it.

The cab was remodeled in the 1960s, and its windows and siding were replaced [^2]. In June 2011 the Wallow Fire badly hit the northwest part of the Blue Range Primitive Area, where the trail runs [^3]. None of the pages I read says the lookout was damaged.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list still shows the tower as standing [^4].

Today the register says the lookout can be reached by trail [^1]. The trail is open to hikers and horseback riders, but motorized and mechanized travel is prohibited [^3]. I found no record of who staffs the lookout now, and no source says whether visitors may climb the tower itself. Check with the Apache-Sitgreaves before you go.
