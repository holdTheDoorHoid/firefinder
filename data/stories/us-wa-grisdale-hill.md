Grisdale Hill Lookout stood on a hill in Mason County, Washington, in the Olympic National Forest, about seven miles southwest of Hoodsport.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register gives its elevation as 1,511 feet.[^1]

No start date is known. Willhite Web says so directly, and says newspaper articles show the lookout existed on August 3, 1939.[^3] The lookout was often called Camp 3 Lookout. Willhite Web says it stood above the Simpson Logging Company's Camp 3 and looked directly down on the camp.[^3]

A Shelton-Mason County Journal item that Willhite Web places in the same 1939 passage names Johnnie Boyce as stationed at the Forest Service lookout at Camp 3. The quoted item gives no date of its own.[^3] During the Second World War the Aircraft Warning Service used the lookout, and its observers moved down to the logging camp in winter.[^3]

Willhite Web says 1942 Aircraft Warning Service records show that the lookout house was moved from Grisdale Hill to Dennie Ahl Hill, a few miles away, because the logging company thought the new spot had a better view.[^3] The guide reads a 1946 newspaper item about fire near the old lookout at Camp 3 as meaning the lookout was already gone.[^3] Firelookout.com lists a tower on Dennie Ahl Hill, built in 1943 and destroyed in 1971.[^5] The sources do not say whether that tower is the house moved in 1942.[^3][^5] Firelookout.com marks the Grisdale site as a 1940s tower that was abandoned.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register registered it on January 11, 2012, as US 1183, WA 84.[^1]

Willhite Web tells visitors to look for old lookout footings at the northwest end of the summit, about 15 feet down near the base of the original summit.[^3] It also says the ground on top has been heavily altered.[^3] OpenStreetMap maps the spot as the ruins of a tower.[^4] We found no source that dates these observations, so we cannot say what the site looks like now. The guide's route follows Forest Service roads, and it says gates likely block the way.[^3]
