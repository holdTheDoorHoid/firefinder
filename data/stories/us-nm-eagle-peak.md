Eagle Peak Lookout stands on the summit of Eagle Peak in the Gila National Forest, about 18 miles east of Reserve, New Mexico.[^3] At 9,801 feet, it sits on the Tularosa Mountains' highest peak.[^1][^3]

An earlier tower was built on the peak in 1929.[^2][^3] The current lookout replaced it in 1955 with a Forest Service Standard Plan CL-100 series cab, a 14-by-14-foot steel cab on a 10-foot base.[^1][^2][^3] The sources disagree on the base. The Register calls it a concrete blockhouse, while a Forest Service history quoted by a lookout-history site describes a cinder-block base that doubles as a storage room.[^1][^2]

A 1967 Silver City Daily Press item noted that a lookout was stationed on the peak as of April 15, with a second person about to join him.[^2] The lookout was evaluated for the National Register in 1988 and found not eligible because of its age.[^5] A 2020 chapter report put its age at 65 and called for re-evaluation and possible nomination.[^5] The same report says seasonal Forest Service staff work the tower.[^5]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed it on May 22, 2022, with Mark Gutzman as nominator.[^1] Wikipedia calls it an active Forest Service lookout, and the Register page includes a June 2024 photo.[^3][^1]

Reaching it means taking Wilson Canyon Trail #768 to Forest Road 38, also called Eagle Peak Road.[^4] The Forest Service measures the trail-and-road route at 1.5 miles one way.[^4] A road guide says the road is unpaved and has two sharp hairpin turns near its end.[^6]

The sources do not say whether the cab is open to visitors. Treat it as an active fire lookout and check with the Reserve Ranger District before going.
