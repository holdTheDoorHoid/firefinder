Frog Lake Butte sits about 11 miles south of Mount Hood, inside the Mount Hood National Forest.[^2][^3] The U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^1]

A crow's nest lookout and camp went up on the butte in 1929.[^1] In 1932 a 75-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab and a cabin replaced it.[^1][^3]

A telephone line about two and a half miles long was built in 1931, linking Frog Lake Campground with the summit.[^2] The forest guard camped at the campground, hiked up in the morning to watch from the summit all day, and at day's end returned to the campground, where he also did maintenance.[^2]

The register says the tower was moved in 1967 to Goat Mtn., where it became a radio tower.[^1] A 2012 trip report says the lookout was removed in 1967 and taken to Goat Mountain.[^4] Neither source names which Goat Mountain is meant, or whether the steel still stands there.[^1][^4]

A 2012 visitor found a cell phone tower standing where the lookout had been.[^4] The road to the top, Road 290, was 2.5 miles of gravel with the last half rough, and the same report says visitors could either hike up or drive.[^4] No source here describes the site after 2012, so it is not known whether anything else remains on the butte or whether the road is open to visitors now.[^4]
