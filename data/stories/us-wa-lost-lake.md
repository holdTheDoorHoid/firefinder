Lost Lake Lookout stood on the high point of Coal Creek Mountain, a long ridge just inside the Goat Rocks Wilderness in Lewis County, Washington.[^4][^1]

The site was first used as an observation point around 1922, with a tent cabin.[^1][^4] An L-4 cab replaced the tent in 1934.[^1][^4]

In August 1931 lightning struck the cabin while Raymond Campbell was on duty.[^3] According to a Centralia Daily Chronicle report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, he was stunned but suffered no injury.[^3] Martha Hardy was the lookout in 1944.[^4] WillhiteWeb says she later wrote a best-selling book about her earlier lookout work at Tatoosh.[^4] A 1936 account quoted on the same site says the lookout welcomed 62 visitors that season.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the cab was removed in the 1960s, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed then.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says both structures, the L-4 cab and an earlier one just below the summit, had been destroyed by the 1960s.[^4]

Topo maps label the high point Lost Lake Benchmark, and no source we read describes what remains of the lookout there.[^4]

The summit is reached on foot.[^4] The Coal Creek route is 6.5 miles one way, with 3,600 feet of gain.[^4] WillhiteWeb describes the Packwood Lake route as a weekend trip, with a first-night campsite on the shores of the lakes.[^4] WillhiteWeb reports that a 2015 washout on Purcell Creek took out the road, so hikers should plan to hike from the washout point.[^4]
