The fire tower (tour à feu) at Saint-Rémi-d'Amherst, in the Laurentides, was erected in 1930, according to the register and the Quebec heritage record.[^1][^2] The municipality of Amherst calls its tower a remnant of a forest-protection system. Its page on the tower recalls that detection had depended on patrollers on foot, on horseback or by canoe, and that in 1907 the head of the protection service proposed a network of observation towers as an alternative.[^3]

The register says the tower stayed in service for decades before the municipality restored it in 1997 and took ownership.[^1] A visitor's account on On va se promener gives other years: the municipality bought the tower in 1987 and restored it in 1996.[^4] The two accounts disagree on the restoration year.

Today the tower stands restored within the forest, and the register says the public may not climb it.[^1] A trail of about 1.5 km leads to it from chemin Saint-Louis, and the municipality maintains it.[^4][^5] Access is free.[^5] The visitor's account gives May to November as the access period, warns about hunting season, and says that climbing the tower is forbidden.[^4]
