A tree observation post about 50 feet above the ground went up at Goodall, in Tioga County, Pennsylvania, in 1910 or 1911.[^1][^2]

According to the register, the first lookout was built in a tree in 1910, and a steel Aermotor tower took its place in 1920.[^2] A September 1919 notice set out plans for a 60-foot steel tower, with stairs and a closed top, above Asaph Run at the head of Goodall hollow.[^1] Sources disagree on its height. The 1919 plan says sixty feet, while a 1968 survey station description calls the tower about 90 feet high and places it 8 miles west-northwest of Wellsboro.[^1]

The same notice planned a direct phone link to the Forestry department's Asaph office and to the homes of nearby fire wardens.[^1] In 1936 enrollees from Darling Run Camp were building a 10,200-foot road to the tower, and the Wellsboro Agitator expected the finished road to make access easier.[^1] In April 1949 the Wellsboro Gazette named Rex Sherman as towerman on the Goodall tower.[^1]

In November 1949 a bear hunter from Harrisburg went missing after his party returned to the cars.[^1] Wellsboro firemen set a 500-watt spotlight on top of the tower. The hunter headed toward the light, came out a short distance from the tower at about 6:30 the next morning, and found members of the search party.[^1]

The register records the steel tower's removal in 1983,[^2] and the Forest Lookouts timeline gives the same year.[^1]

A trail-mapping listing for Goodall Road says the road climbs steeply, with switchbacks, to the site of a former lookout tower, and that the tower's cabin still stands in good condition.[^3] That listing dates the tower and cabin to between 1910 and 1920 and says the tower was demolished in 1983.[^3] No source we read says whether the site is open to the public. The listing says the road gets no winter maintenance, so check conditions before going.[^3]
