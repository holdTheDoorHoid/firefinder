Lookout Point Lookout sits on a high point north of Spokane, Washington, now covered in timber, above a fire-prone edge where town meets wildland.[^1][^4] The register describes a 40-foot wooden tower with a flattop cab of 14 by 14 feet, a catwalk, and a DNR design.[^1]

The sources do not agree on when the lookout was built. The register says the tower was first built on Mica Peak near the Idaho border and moved to this site in 1956.[^1] A lookout-site page for Mica Peak says a state tower replaced the first Mica Peak lookout in 1954, and that the replacement was moved here in 1955.[^3] Firelookout.com says a nearly identical tower stood here in 1956 and that the present tower was built in 1976.[^2] Willhite agrees on 1976 and says the current structure was moved to this site that year.[^4] The sources give 1955, 1956 and 1976 for the move or build, and this story does not settle which is right.

A 1957 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout-history site, mentions a fire spotter planning to work on Lookout Point that June.[^5] According to firelookout.com, the lookout had staff every season up to 1992, was shut the next year, and came back for the 1994 fire season.[^2] Willhite gives the same dates.[^4] The register gives 1995 as the last year it was staffed.[^1] The register listed it on November 18, 1999.[^1] I found no notable fires at the lookout in these sources.

The lookout still stands, but nobody has staffed it since the mid-1990s.[^2][^6] A 1999 housing development opened the road that climbs the hill, yet firelookout.com says the tower still sits on private property and visitors go at their own risk.[^2] Willhite describes it as lying behind the gate of a community called River Bluff Ranch, where signs discourage visitors from going the last few hundred feet.[^4] Because it stands on private land behind a gate, it should not be climbed.
