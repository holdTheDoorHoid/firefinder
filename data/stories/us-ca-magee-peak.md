Magee Peak is a summit on the crater rim of the Crater Peak volcano, in Shasta County within the Lassen National Forest.[^1][^2]

An early eruption of Lassen Peak smashed the forest bureau's lookout on its summit, so the bureau built a substitute station on Magee Peak, about ten miles north of Lassen.[^1] A telephone line reached the Magee summit in 1915, with a lookout planned for the next season.[^1] In July 1917 a contractor, J.G. Fritz, held the construction contract.[^1] By late July the station was finished: a 14-foot-square building with glass walls about six feet high, where a forest guard sat in a wheeled chair and turned to watch every direction.[^1] Construction material had to be packed in on mules for eight miles.[^1]

The newspapers do not agree on the height. One 1917 item gives 8,025 feet, another about 10,000 feet, and two 1918 items give 7,000 and 9,000 feet.[^1]

W.S. Hays of Montgomery Creek was stationed there as lookout in 1917.[^1] In June 1918 Mrs. Todd Williams of Portland set out to take the post, which a newspaper said several men had turned down after a trial, finding it lonely and high up.[^1] A 1919 item, which spells her name Ted, calls her the first woman lookout in the Lassen forest.[^1]

T.D. Goodman was on the summit by 1923, when he phoned in word that the volcano had erupted.[^1] In 1930 he took up his duties for the fire season, and in October he moved off after snow, going down to his winter residence at Balls Ferry.[^1] A 1932 item says he had been there about 17 years, which would reach back before the Williams seasons. The items do not reconcile.[^1]

The fate is unclear. The Kemnow page marks the site as removed, with no date.[^1] A climbing guide says the building burned down long ago and that remnants can still be seen on the summit.[^2] The two accounts disagree about how it ended.[^1][^2]

Today the climbing guide says the Forest Service no longer maintains the Magee trail.[^2] Downed trees lie along it and at the trailhead, and the road is still passable, though the route continues to suffer.[^2]
