The Forest Service built a lookout on the summit of Teanaway Butte in 1940: a wooden L-4 cab on a 10-foot tower.[^1][^3] Willhite's 1935 pictures show many trees cut from the summit while a lookout was being planned, and the same page suggests a fire finder was already there, perhaps with a crow's nest tree.[^3] The only lookout person named in these sources is R.A. Spence, listed for 1933, years before the cab went up.[^2]

The state took over the tower in 1944 for fire duties.[^1][^3] By the mid-1950s it was reported abandoned, and in 1968 it was destroyed.[^1][^3] A Willhite photo caption calls one 1968 picture a burning.[^3]

The tower itself is gone.[^1][^3] The FFLOS entry says a tree platform is still there.[^1] Willhite's photo list includes one captioned "Lookout Footings."[^3]

Today the route is a walk.[^3] Willhite says to park where Road 230 turns off the Middle Fork Teanaway road. A gate past the first bend bars motor vehicles.[^3] The road runs about two miles to a ridge, and the last few hundred feet are a switchback hiking trail.[^3] The page adds that snowshoeing there is usually a winter activity.[^3]
