A lookout stood on Berryessa Peak in Napa County, at 3,057 feet, east of Lake Berryessa.[^1][^2] The register dates it to 1948.[^1] An Internet Archive photograph from the California Division of Forestry collection shows the crowd at its dedication ceremony in June 1948.[^5]

The register calls it a CDF 809R design, a 30-foot K-brace steel tower with an enclosed cab.[^1] The register says it was one of only four towers of its kind, and that only two of the four survive.[^1] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator.[^1] West Lookouts files it under the California Department of Forestry, and Wikipedia calls it the CAL FIRE Berryessa lookout tower.[^2][^4]

The Rumsey Fire burned from October 10 to 16, 2004, and officials said it was arson.[^4] A Ukiah Daily Journal report, quoted by West Lookouts, says the fire destroyed the lookout as it moved toward Lake Berryessa.[^2] A 2004 Forest Fire Lookout Association western-region report also says the lookout was completely lost to wildfire.[^3]

The register dates the lookout's destruction to 2004 and names the Rumsey Fire as the cause.[^1]

No source I read describes what, if anything, is left of the lookout today, or whether the public can visit the site.
