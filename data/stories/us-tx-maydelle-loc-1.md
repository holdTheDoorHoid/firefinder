The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list marks Maydelle (Loc 1), in Cherokee County, as gone.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page for Maydelle describes a Texas Forest Service station four miles west of the town and one-tenth of a mile north of U.S. Highway 84.[^2] It describes a standard-type steel lookout tower, painted gray and about 110 feet high.[^2]

The same description says the tower was built in 1933 and is Tower No. 8 of the Texas Forest Service.[^2] A June 1939 item from the Rusk Cherokeean calls it Tower No. 8 in Maydelle State Forest and places it four miles southwest of town.[^2] The survey and the newspaper agree on the distance but not the direction.[^2]

A February 1946 item credits Forest Patrolman Ray Sides at the Maydelle lookout with reporting and putting out one of nine fires in Cherokee County that January.[^2] The item says that fire was caused by an incendiarist, meaning someone who set it on purpose.[^2] An April 1946 item gives the patrolman's name as Roy Sides.[^2] In January 1952 the Alto Herald reported that J.A. Benge, who had managed the Alto tower, was moving to Maydelle to take over the fire tower there.[^2]

The association also lists a second Maydelle site, Loc 2, as standing.[^1] A Texas lookout towers list last revised in January 2011 has two Maydelle entries.[^3] One, at 31.81958, -95.30931, is almost exactly where the association puts Loc 2.[^3] The other, at 31.78400, -95.36503, is at the Loc 1 coordinates and cites survey datasheet BY2737, the station the Forest Lookouts page describes.[^2][^3] Read together, those two pages point the 1933 tower to Loc 1.[^2][^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the Loc 1 site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2]

[^2]: Maydelle, Forest Lookouts, https://centraluslookouts.weebly.com/maydelle.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
