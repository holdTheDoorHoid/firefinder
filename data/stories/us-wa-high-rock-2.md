High Rock Lookout sits on the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, just south of Mount Rainier National Park, on the edge of a sheer 1,200-foot cliff.[^1] The Forest Service built the 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab, with its gable roof and surrounding catwalk, and staffed it the same year it was completed.[^1] Sources differ slightly on the exact year: the National Historic Lookout Register lists 1929, while the Fire Lookout Museum's page gives 1930.[^1][^2]

Longtime observer Bud Panco became closely associated with the lookout over his years watching from it, and it was staffed seasonally into the mid-2000s.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register presented its certificate for the site in June 2001, with the honor going to District Ranger Harry Cody and to Panco himself.[^1]

After staffing ended, the building sat exposed to the weather and to visitors. A 2017 site visit described broken and boarded-up windows, graffiti, mouse droppings, a missing basement door, and wood reinforcements added to shore up a sagging ceiling, though the structure itself still seemed sound.[^1] The firefinder was reported still in place inside.[^1]

By 2021, the cab had been removed from the site for off-site restoration, according to the Fire Lookout Museum, and the National Historic Lookout Register's 2024 note confirms the cabin was still away from the tower undergoing repair at that point.[^1][^2]

As of the most recent reporting, the cabin remains off-site for restoration rather than back on its tower base; the site is not rented out, and it functions as a historic site maintained by the Forest Service, reachable by a roughly 1.6-mile trail that's typically clear of snow by early July.[^1] Signage along the approach road is sparse, so hikers should carry a map rather than rely on signs alone.[^1] Visitors can view the lookout and its dramatic cliffside perch, but given the building's fragile, in-progress restoration, it's best treated as a place to look at, not climb on.
