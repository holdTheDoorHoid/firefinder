Pelican Butte rises near Rocky Point in Klamath County, Oregon, about 27 miles northwest of Klamath Falls, in the Winema National Forest.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com dates the first lookout here, a cable-braced pole L-4 tower, to 1935.[^3] A lookout history page quotes 1934 newspapers describing a 15-foot steel tower erected for panorama photos and a lookout station still under construction that summer, so the start date is not fully settled.[^2]

In the late 1940s and early 1950s married couples staffed the lookout.[^2] A 1948 notice offered the post to a married couple with a car, and in 1949 a couple was to man the 14-by-14-foot station.[^2] In 1952 the road stayed drifted shut until mid-August, when the lookout shoveled through four-foot drifts to open it.[^2] A 1941 report says the Pelican Butte lookout felt an earth tremor.[^2]

The first tower gave way to a timber L-4 cab in 1954.[^1][^2] More than a decade later a live-in R-6 cab replaced it. The register says this came about twelve years after 1954, while firelookout.com dates it to 1965.[^1][^3] In 1971 a Pacific Northwest Bell unit was removed, and a later memo says it would not be reinstalled.[^2] A 1980 newspaper piece described a combined tower and communications building, built by a private company on leased Forest Service land, with a lookout to be stationed there in season. The same page gives a 39-foot steel tower, which does not match the 20-foot height in later sources.[^1][^2][^3]

The present metal cab dates to 1986.[^1][^3] The register says a cable company wanted the summit as a relay station and offered the new cab as part of the deal.[^1] Firelookout.com says the 20-foot all-steel tower is now used only during emergencies.[^3]

The register says the lookout is currently on standby.[^1] Cheryl Hill's list of standing Oregon lookouts shows it as unmanned, and as a site the public can legally reach. That does not mean the cab is open to visitors.[^4] The register also notes that members of the Forest Fire Lookout Association voted it Oregon's ugliest lookout, even though its history is colorful.[^1]

None of the sources says whether the public may enter the cab or climb the tower. A 1967 survey description gives directions along a rough, bladed road to the tower, but no current road status turned up.[^2]
