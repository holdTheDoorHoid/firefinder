The Potash Lookout Tower is in Randolph County, Alabama.[^1] The Register says locals call it the Tin Shop Tower, a name we could not trace further.[^1] The Alabama Forestry Commission constructed it.[^1] The Register gives two dates: its description says 1949, and its built field says 1950.[^1] We found no source that settles which is right.

It is a 100-foot Aermotor with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] The Register does not name the exact model. A 1819 News profile says the Aermotor MC-39 was the most common steel lookout model in Alabama's forests, followed by the LS-40, and that other models were also put up in the state.[^2]

The Register lists the tower as US 861 and AL 39.[^1] It was registered on January 7, 2009, and the site sits at 1,332 feet, or 406 meters.[^1]

We found no source that names anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched. The 1819 News profile says that when a lookout stopped being staffed, the structure stayed standing. It gives the most common explanation for the decline as aircraft spotting fires more efficiently.[^2]

The Register does not say whether the tower still stands, what condition it is in, or whether the public can visit.[^1] It names the Alabama Forestry Commission as administrator.[^1] Check with the agency before going near the site.
