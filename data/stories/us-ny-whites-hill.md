Whites Hill is a 1,427-foot peak in the northern Adirondacks, southeast of Parishville in St. Lawrence County.[^1] In 1950 the state Conservation Department put up an 80-foot Aermotor LS40 steel lookout tower on its summit.[^1][^2] A list of the state's fire towers compiled by Bill Starr gives the same model and height, and says the tower opened that same year.[^2]

The tower began lookout work in 1951, and that first season logged 3 fires and 588 visitors.[^1] Before 1970 the state ran it part-time, on a need-by-need basis, with fire wardens sent out on assignment from the district forest ranger.[^2]

Sources disagree on when the lookout ended.[^1][^2] The state list says the tower was decommissioned at the end of the 1970 season.[^2] Wikipedia says operations stopped after the 1971 season, because aircraft were doing more of the spotting, and that the tower was later taken down.[^1]

Wikipedia says the observer's cabin was later moved to the state Department of Environmental Conservation's Trout Lake Shop, where Operations Unit personnel use it.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list also says parts of Whites Hill went into two platform towers at Wilson Hill Wildlife Management Area.[^3] We found no second source for that reuse.

The association's list marks the Whites Hill tower as gone.[^3] No source we found describes what remains on the summit today, or whether the hill is open to visitors. The cabin is now at a state shop, not on the mountain.[^1]
