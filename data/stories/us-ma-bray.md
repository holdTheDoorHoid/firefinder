Bray Lookout Tower stands in Mount Tom State Reservation, in Holyoke, Massachusetts, in Hampshire County.[^1] The register calls it the easiest tower on the reservation to reach, and says a car can drive right up to it.[^1] To get there, take Christopher Clark Road from Easthampton or Reservation Road from Holyoke.[^1]

The state's Department of Conservation and Recreation (DCR) administers the tower.[^1][^3] The register describes it as a steel tower, painted green, about 42 feet tall.[^1] A photo on the register page was taken in March 2026, so the tower was standing then.[^1] The register lists it as registered on May 26, 2026, on the nomination of Tori Bonazoli.[^1]

We could not find when Bray was built. The register compares it with the Goat Peak tower, which it says was built in 1928 and is very similar.[^1] We found no source that says Bray was used to spot fires. The register says people still use it to watch the yearly hawk and raptor migration.[^1] An observation tower on nearby Goat Peak is maintained for that same raptor watching.[^2]

Sources disagree about the reservation's official lookouts. The register counts Bray as one of the reservation's two official lookouts.[^1] A Mt. Tom Range web page says the reservation has two viewing towers, one overlooking Westhampton and one on Goat Peak. It mentions a tower at the Visitor Center but does not mention Bray.[^3] A Holyoke tour listing names Bray Tower among the reservation's sites.[^4]

We found no statement on whether Bray can be climbed, so check with the reservation before climbing.
