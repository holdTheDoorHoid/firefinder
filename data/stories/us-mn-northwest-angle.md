The Northwest Angle is a peninsula that extends from Canada into Lake of the Woods. Water, not land, separates it from the rest of the United States.[^1] Survey notes from 1934 describe a state fire tower at Warroad, on the mainland, as an 80-foot tower of all-steel construction with a hexagon-shaped enclosed cabin at the top.[^1][^2] The same entry also says it was about 100 feet high, and the later Lake of the Woods list gives 80 feet, so the height is in doubt.[^2][^3]

The tower was dismantled in 1946 and later moved to the Angle.[^2] It was erected there in 1951 as an 80-foot ladder tower, numbered 75 in the county list.[^3] The Roseau County list files its owner under the Minnesota Forest Service, which it says is now part of the state's Department of Natural Resources.[^2]

The tower stood at a pulpwood camp operated for Minnesota & Ontario Paper Co., known as Mando.[^1] A 1950s article reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page says the state owned the tower, but Mando operated it to protect its timber.[^1] During fire season the tower was manned, and the camp itself was manned in winter as well as summer.[^1] Aircraft reports from the camp were radioed across the water to Warroad.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Northwest Angle tower as gone.[^4] The county compilation calls it the northernmost lookout tower in the lower 48 states until it was removed.[^3]

The association's list places the site at about 49.22 north and 95.12 west.[^4] We found no source that describes what remains there today or whether visitors can reach it.[^4]
