The Black Mountain lookout stands on the mountain of the same name, in the Black Range district of the Gila National Forest in Catron County, New Mexico, at about 9,303 feet.[^1][^3] The tower was erected in 1934 and is 30 feet tall, an Aermotor MC-24 model with a wooden cab measuring 12 by 12 feet, according to the Register and a 2020 state chapter report.[^1][^3]

An observer's cabin was built on the site earlier, in 1925, according to the 2020 report.[^3] The two dates belong to different structures, the cabin and the tower, so they should not be read as conflicting build years.[^1][^3]

In 1978 all of the tower's original windows were swapped out.[^3] The 2020 report says that change ruled the tower out in the 1988 National Register review, because the review's starting date would have been 1978, when the lookout was only 10 years old.[^3] The cabin, by contrast, was judged eligible and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on January 28, 1988.[^3]

A 1977 item in the Silver City Daily Press, quoted on a lookout history site, mentioned a Forest Service lookout working on the mountain that year.[^2]

Sources disagree on height. The Register and the 2020 report give 30 feet, but a 1966 federal survey description says the station sits at the center of a 50-foot, four-legged structure that holds up the cabin.[^1][^2][^3]

The Register recorded the lookout in May 2022 as US 1583, NM 41.[^1] The 2020 report recommended either re-evaluating and nominating the tower using its 1934 date, or restoring the windows and then nominating it.[^3]

The sources give no public visiting rules. The 2020 report says the lookout is actively staffed.[^3] Check with the Black Range Ranger District before visiting, and do not climb the tower without permission.
