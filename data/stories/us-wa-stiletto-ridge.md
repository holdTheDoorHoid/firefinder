The register and FireLookout.com both date the Stiletto Ridge cab to 1931. It was a gabled L-4 cab at the west end of a spur ridge.[^1][^2] Will Hite's page says it was said to be built that year.[^4] The register places it in the Wenatchee National Forest and FireLookout.com agrees, but the Forest Lookouts page names the Chelan National Forest, and the sources do not reconcile the two.[^1][^2][^3]

Before the cab, a forest guard named Kenneth Carmody was sent to the peak for the summer of 1930. A Wenatchee newspaper said he would be stationed there the entire summer, and Will Hite's page thinks the 1930 setup was probably only a camp and a firefinder stand.[^3][^4]

Panoramic photos were shot from the roof in 1934, and the Forest Lookouts page dates them to September 9, 1934.[^3][^4]

The cab appears to have ended in 1952.[^1] The register says a photo that likely shows it being prepared for burning dates from that year.[^1] FireLookout.com lists its dates as 1931 to 1952.[^2] Will Hite's page reports a belief that the cab was burned in 1952, while the Forest Lookouts page only lists it as removed.[^3][^4]

The register places the site today within North Cascades National Park.[^1] Will Hite's page reports that only a fire finder post, some rock steps and a rock wall windbreak are still standing.[^4] A photo on that page is captioned 2025.[^4]

Will Hite describes the site as easy to reach from the highway.[^4] A Washington Trails Association listing describes a climb from the Bridge Creek Trailhead on Highway 20, at 4,540 feet, to vistas at the site.[^4][^5]
