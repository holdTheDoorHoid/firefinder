The lookout trees of the Kaibab National Forest are what remains of an improvised fire-watch system that relied on tall trees for high views.[^1] Forest Service workers picked a tall tree near a high point, put a ladder against its side, sawed off the top, and built a platform on what was left of it.[^2] Many such lookouts were set up between 1905 and 1920.[^1] The Cooper Ridge Lookout Tree is one of eleven trees that could be located, and all eleven were added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 13, 1992.[^1][^2]

The Cooper Ridge tree stands west of Forest Road 248, about 0.7 miles north of Highway 67, on a nameless ridge northeast of Moquitch Canyon in the North Kaibab Ranger District.[^3] A 1990 nomination describes a three-trunked ponderosa pine about 75 feet tall, with five sections of ladder still on its east side, fastened with spikes.[^3] The nomination says neither the build date nor the abandonment date is known yet, and suggests oral history might supply them.[^3] The coordinates in our record match the Cooper Ridge tree listed on Wikipedia.[^1]

These sources describe the system in general terms rather than this tree. Atlas Obscura says each platform was staffed by two people, a lookout and a patrolman, and that steel towers eventually replaced the trees.[^2] No source we read gives staffing or notable fires for Cooper Ridge itself.

The nomination says the tree had been abandoned for about 30 years by 1990, yet it was still alive with its ladder in good condition.[^3] The same form says lookout trees on the North Kaibab fell out of use in the late 1960s, a date that does not quite fit the 30-year figure.[^3] No source we read confirms the tree's condition today or describes public access to it. Atlas Obscura's map coordinates lead to the Tusayan Lookout Tree instead, which it says is likely the most accessible; for most of the trees, it warns, you will need seasonally passable dirt roads and sometimes a hike.[^2]
