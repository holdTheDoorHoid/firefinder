Rising above the flat sand country of central Wisconsin, the Friendship tower is also historically known as Skyline.[^1] It went up in December 1939 as a 100-foot Wisconsin standard tower, reached by an external ladder enclosed in its own protective cage.[^1]

For decades afterward it did the plain, essential work of a fire lookout. Rather than being staffed year-round, Friendship was watched intermittently each spring fire season, until 2015, when the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources decommissioned its statewide lookout network.[^1]

The tower itself was left standing. In December 2017 it was added to the National Historic Lookout Register as US 1259, WI 45, a recognition of its place in the state's firefighting history rather than any renewed fire duty.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's own Wisconsin inventory likewise lists it simply as still standing.[^2] No source consulted here describes current public access or a maintained trail, so visitors should treat the tower as unstaffed infrastructure on DNR land rather than an open climb, and check locally before planning a visit.
