Mount Baldy is a prominent hill 44 km southeast of Wawa, rising above the Agawa River.[^1] A hiker's trip report from August 2014 says an old fire tower stands on the bare-rock summit.[^1]

The report adds that most Ontario peaks lack the clear, all-round outlook that Baldy offers.[^1] The writers parked at a pull-off near an overgrown skidder path on Crossover Road, then bushwhacked about 1.2 km from the road to the top.[^1]

A fan site's list of still-standing Ontario towers places the Mount Baldy Tower at Flagg and Gould Lakes, in Tabobondung Township.[^2] The entry carries an asterisk, which the list's legend explains: the cupola has been removed and the tower is now used as a communications tower.[^2]

The site also reports that, as of 2013, the Ontario MNR had decided to take down many of these towers.[^2] It says climbing one is hazardous, that the MNR has removed the bottom 6.1 m (20 ft) of ladder and no longer maintains the towers, and it asks visitors to refrain from climbing.[^2]

The most recent first-hand description in these sources is the 2014 report.[^1] The fan-site list is undated, so the tower's condition today is unclear.[^2]
