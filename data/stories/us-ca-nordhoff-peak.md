Nordhoff Peak sits above the Ojai Valley in Ventura County, at about 4,481 feet, inside the Los Padres National Forest.[^1][^2] The Forest Service started work on a lookout station there in June 1935, according to a newspaper report quoted on the Forest Lookouts site.[^3] Its first cab was made of wood.[^2]

Observer John V. Tracy was on duty at Nordhoff Lookout in September 1938, and he reported a smoke that turned out to be a house fire in North Hollywood, about 90 miles away.[^3] In November 1956 the Forest Service ran a Ground Observer Corps post out of the lookout.[^3]

Carey says the first cab was lost in the 1948 Wheeler Springs Fire.[^2] A September 1948 newspaper report says a fire burned the Nordhoff Peak lookout tower, but it does not name that fire.[^3] A replacement tower and cabin were flown in by helicopter from Rincon Mountain around 1950.[^1][^2] That replacement burned in the 1970s.[^1][^2] The registry says it was not replaced.[^1]

Carey wrote in 2012 that only the steel frame stands, and a 2017 trail write-up describes the site as the leftovers of the old tower.[^2][^4] The frame sits above a campground.[^2] Carey gives the approach as seven miles on the Gridley Trail, and the 2017 write-up puts a round trip from Ojai along the Pratt Trail at about 12 miles.[^2][^4] We found no current rule for the frame, and we do not recommend climbing it.
