Arctic Point Lookout sits atop a 7,500-foot peak in Idaho's Payette National Forest, inside what is now the Frank Church Wilderness.[^1] The Forest Service put up a 72-foot Aermotor steel tower here, with a 7-by-7-foot cab on top, in either 1935 or 1936 — sources disagree, with the National Historic Lookout Register giving 1935 and the National Register of Historic Places documentation giving 1936.[^1][^2] A Rocky Mountain-style log cabin was added at the base in 1939 to house the lookout staff.[^2][^3]

For most of its working life the tower served its ordinary purpose, spotting wildfires in one of the most remote corners of the Lower 48.[^1] During World War II, though, it also had an unusual job: watching for Japanese fire balloons, the hydrogen-filled incendiary devices Japan launched toward the Pacific Northwest between late 1944 and early 1945 in hopes the jet stream would carry them onto American forests.[^1] The lookout continued to be staffed into the 1990s; Idaho Fire Lookouts' known-staff list names Mike Taylor as the last recorded staffer, and the tower remained in service until 1997, when it was decommissioned.[^2][^3]

The tower earned a spot on the National Register of Historic Places in 1994 and joined the National Historic Lookout Register in 1998.[^1][^2] It survived the following decades with no major changes beyond a helipad, but by the 2010s the ground cabin needed real attention — a bear tore up its porch logs and decking in 2013, and the structure was showing its age.[^1] Because the site can only be reached by an 18-mile trail on foot or horseback, repairs have been slow going.[^1] Payette National Forest staff, along with volunteers and packers, have been running a multi-year stabilization effort, including an 11-day, roughly 100-mile pack trip in 2020 to shore up the cabin.[^1] In the 2020s the cabin has also been wrapped in protective material more than once as nearby wildfires approached.[^2]

Arctic Point is not staffed and is not available to rent; it stands as a preserved historic structure deep in designated wilderness, reachable only by a long, difficult trail.
