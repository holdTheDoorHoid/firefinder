The Mozark Mountain fire tower was built in 1928 in Tucker County, on the Monongahela National Forest.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register's history of the forest's fire towers places it on the Cheat Ranger District.[^3]

Early accounts do not agree on the start date. A 1949 column, which spells the mountain Musark, says a man hauled lumber for a twenty-five-foot fire tower there twenty-eight years before, which would point to about 1921.[^2] The same column names a Mr. Showalter as the first fire tower worker on the mountain. He worked under Charles E. Long, the forest's first ranger, whom the column calls a brother-in-law.[^2]

The register gives 1928 as the build year and records the tower as removed.[^1] The only physical evidence I found is a photo of footings taken in October 2008.[^1] The site was added to the register in September 2008, nominated by Henry Isenberg.[^1]

The Firelookout.org list of West Virginia towers also marks it as gone.[^4] The site is administered by the U.S. Forest Service.[^1] I found no source that describes its access or what stands there now beyond those 2008 footings.[^1]
