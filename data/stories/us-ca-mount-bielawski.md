Mount Bielawski Lookout stands on Castle Rock Ridge in the Santa Cruz Mountains, and the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection's Santa Clara Unit is listed as its administrator.[^1][^3] The sources disagree on the county. The Register's location line says Santa Clara County, but its description places the station just inside Santa Cruz County.[^1] A 1957 newspaper item places the site on the boundary between the two counties.[^2] Wikipedia says the peak lies in both counties and is the highest point in Santa Cruz County.[^3]

The state board of forestry built the tower in 1922.[^2] Three counties each put up $500 for materials, and the board supplied the labor.[^2] Press items give its height as 60 feet.[^2] The Register calls it the oldest Aermotor Company tower in California and says it was the first fire-detection structure paid for by the state.[^1][^2] The press says Mr. and Mrs. Amos Smead provided the land on a ninety-nine-year arrangement at a dollar a year, described as rent.[^2]

The first lookout named in the press was Almon E. Smead, though the same papers also call him A.E. Smead and Amos Smead.[^2] Fred Herring was the lookout by 1936, and a 1957 item says the station had run for 35 consecutive fire seasons.[^2]

The cab was remodeled in 1955.[^1] The original dwelling came down in 1969, and the present residence was built in 1970.[^1] The Register notes that the tower was altered, and an inside stairway now replaces the old ladder.[^1] It was registered on October 26, 2019, as US 1343, CA 166.[^1]

The sources do not say whether the public may go up to the lookout. Wikipedia notes that the summit is densely forested and gives no outlook.[^3] The Register describes a residence and communications equipment near the lookout,[^1] so anyone interested should check with Cal Fire before visiting.
