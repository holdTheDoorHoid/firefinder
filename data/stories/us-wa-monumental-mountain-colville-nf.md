The Monumental Mountain lookout site sits in the Colville National Forest in Pend Oreille County, Washington, at 5,700 feet.[^1][^2] The first lookout there came in 1926, when a D-6 cupola cabin stood 600 feet north of the summit and served as a detection site.[^1][^2]

A 1930 item from The Newport Miner, quoted on Rex's Fire Lookout Page, reports that Harry Casselman had taken the lookout job on Monumental Peak.[^3] A 1935 item from the same paper describes a road up Harvey Creek to the lookout station, and says the road would be extended to connect with another road across the divide.[^3] We found no other staffing record in the sources we read.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1939 a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced the cupola cabin.[^1][^2]

The tower came down in 1958.[^1][^2][^3] Firetower.org and Rex's page say it was removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^3] None of the sources says how it ended.

Do not mix this site up with a different Monumental Mountain tower in Stevens County.[^4] Rod Fosback bought that one and rebuilt its upper 10 feet on his property east of Colville.[^4][^5]

We found no source that describes what remains at the Colville National Forest summit today. Firetower.org gives an elevation and a map location, and says nothing about the ground now.[^1] Public access is not described anywhere we looked, so expect a site with no tower standing on it.
