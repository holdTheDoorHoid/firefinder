Ensign Peak rises just north of downtown Salt Lake City.[^2] By July 1948 a fire lookout station stood on its summit, according to a Salt Lake Telegram story reprinted by the Forest Lookouts history site.[^1] The lookout is credited to the Salt Lake City Water Department.[^1]

The story follows a young boy who hiked two miles uphill from home to the station.[^1] A water department ranger, Dick Raybould, drove up to the lookout and found him there.[^1] After Raybould identified himself, he carried on with his duties, and the two spent the day touring the lookout stations together.[^1]

Do not confuse this with the Walker Bank Building lookout. That lookout sat atop the 15-story Walker Bank Building in Salt Lake City. The Wasatch National Forest and the city water department set it up in 1939, and it stayed in use until about 1948.[^5]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Ensign Peak station as abandoned.[^4]

Today the summit lies within Ensign Peak Nature Park, a city-owned park, and a popular trail climbs to it.[^3] Wikipedia gives the trail as a 0.47-mile hike.[^3] It also reports that a stone monument went up on the summit in 1934.[^3] No source we read describes a lookout structure, or any remains of one, on the summit today.
