The Missouri Conservation Commission acquired a four-acre towersite for its Lincoln lookout in Benton County.[^1] A state annual report for fiscal 1948-49, quoted on the Central US Lookouts page, records that acquisition.[^1] By February 1950, a Sedalia Democrat report says, a 60-foot lookout tower had been built and was in operation.[^1] The paper called it the second such tower in Benton County and placed it about four miles southeast on the Lakeview Heights road.[^1]

The report named Ted Edwards of Warsaw as the man in charge.[^1] Each towerman was furnished a jeep with firefighting gear, including a 180-gallon water tank and pump.[^1] Towermen would be at their posts day and night in early spring and in the autumn and winter months, when the danger is greatest.[^1] Radio equipment linked the tower to others, with headquarters at Camdenton.[^1] Its running costs were to come from fishing and hunting license sales.[^1] From the windows, the report added, the towns of Lincoln, Ionia, Cole Camp and Stover could be seen.[^1]

Ted Edwards started with the Commission in November 1949 and was assigned to the Lincoln tower.[^1] He died at home in Warsaw on August 26, 1958, after a long illness, according to an obituary in The Index that the page quotes.[^1] The obituary gives his age as 51 and says he had served at Lincoln for the past nine years.[^1]

In fiscal 1970-71, a state annual report quoted on the page records a new steel tower going up at Lincoln.[^1]

No source we found describes what remains at the site today.

[^1]: Lincoln, Forest Lookouts, Central US Lookouts, https://centraluslookouts.weebly.com/lincoln.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
