Park Butte Lookout stands on a ridge above timberline, on Mount Baker's south side, in the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1]

The Forest Service built it in the fall of 1932 to a standard L-4 plan. The small crew included C.A. Larsen and William Thompson, both of whom had worked as fire lookouts, plus a hired carpenter.[^2] Packer Roy Evans brought the pre-cut materials in on pack horses, in about 20 trips.[^2] The builders' names are stamped into the concrete foundation pads.[^2]

Sources disagree on the year. Firelookout.com says 1933, but the register and the nomination both say 1932, and the 1932 date appears in the concrete itself.[^3][^1][^2]

The lookout was used for fire detection until 1961.[^2] The Skagit Alpine Club's history says staff also worked as plane spotters during the Second World War.[^4] In the 1970s, when Mount Baker looked likely to erupt, the restored lookout became an observatory for volcano monitors.[^1]

In 1962 the Skagit Alpine Club took over the cabin under a special use permit with the Forest Service, and the club has maintained it for recreation since.[^2] The register also notes its National Register of Historic Places listing.[^1]

Today the Forest Service lists the site as open 24 hours a day, reached from the Park Butte Trailhead and Trail 603.[^5] The nomination describes a 3.5-mile trail that crosses Sulphur Creek and glacial meltwater.[^2] Use is first come, first served.[^5] A recreation pass is required at the trailhead from May 1 through October 31, and the Forest Service says no fee applies to the site itself.[^5] The lookout sits inside the Mount Baker Wilderness, where motorized and mechanized equipment is not allowed and campfires are prohibited.[^5] The register lists it as available for rental.[^1] Check Forest Service alerts before you go.
