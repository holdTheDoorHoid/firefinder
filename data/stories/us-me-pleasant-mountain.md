Pleasant Mountain stretches along the line between Bridgton and Denmark in Oxford County, Maine, and a Maine Forest Service fire tower stands on its main summit.[^1][^3]

Before the tower, a hotel stood on the summit from 1873 to 1907, and it gave the peak its old name, House Peak.[^3] The state's Forest Commissioner later erected a steel lookout in the town of Denmark. The 1920 annual report gives it as forty-eight feet tall. It adds that the crew also erected a camp and put up more than three miles of telephone line.[^2] A 1922 state panoramic map is centered on the tower.[^4]

In 1939 the Bath Independent said the summit tower was manned through the summer season. It described a summit room walled in glass, about eight feet on a side, with a view of about 20 miles to the naked eye.[^2] In July 1943 the Bridgton News reported a Portland paper's notice that an observation post was to be set up near the tower, with a chief observer from Denmark. The notice says it was planned, and we did not confirm that it opened.[^2]

The tower's current condition is unclear. The Press Herald describes the fire tower as boarded up.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, without the asterisk it uses for towers that have lost their cabs.[^1] A trail site describes a glass-enclosed cab at the summit.[^5] We did not find when the tower was last staffed or boarded up.

The Loon Echo Land Trust manages about 10 miles of trail on the mountain, reached from two trailheads on each side.[^3] The Ledges Trail is the shortest route to the summit at 1.8 miles.[^3] Because the tower is boarded up, we do not encourage climbing it. Check with the land trust about the summit before you go.
