Howard Butte Lookout is an open timber tower, 100 feet tall, in Wallowa County.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1941.[^1] Its design is a 7-by-7 observation cab on a catwalk, with a small residence cabin built alongside.[^1]

A fuller timeline comes from Forest Lookouts, which describes the CCC work as a main project in June 1941 and says the tower and a one-room cabin were built in 1942.[^2] Firelookout.com also gives 1941 but describes a 6-by-6 cab, so the sources disagree on the cab's size.[^1][^3]

Firelookout.com lists the people it knows worked the lookout: Mrs. Riley Chandler in 1954, Riley Chandler in 1957 and 1959, Jim Lewis in 1961, and Joan Sines from 1968 to 1970.[^3] Forest Lookouts records building work rather than fire reports, including repainting in 1953, a new foundation under the cabin in 1957, and metal roofing in 1965.[^2] No source consulted names a specific fire the lookout spotted.

Firelookout.com says the tower saw emergency use into the 1980s and is now abandoned, though that page is undated.[^3] In 2019 a camera for spotting fires went up on the site.[^2] A newspaper report from that year describes the camera on a 110-foot metal tower beside the older wooden lookout.[^5] The same article dates the wooden tower to 1946, which conflicts with the 1941 and 1942 accounts.[^5]

The National Historic Lookout Register added the tower on July 3, 2022.[^1]

A directory of Oregon lookouts lists the tower as standing.[^4] The 2019 article describes communications equipment on the site that serves the Oregon Department of Forestry and other state and county agencies.[^5] The site's two acres were deeded to the Oregon Department of Forestry by a company then called the Bowman-Hicks Lumber Co.[^5] The sources do not say whether visitors may reach or climb the tower, or whether it can be rented. Anyone planning to approach it should check first with the Wallowa Unit of the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^5]
