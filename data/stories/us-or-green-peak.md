Green Peak sits in Benton County, Oregon, and the state forestry department kept a lookout on the summit from the early 1940s until 1974.[^1][^3]

Newspaper accounts disagree on where the lookout stood.[^3] They place it six miles west of Monroe, northwest of Monroe, west of Dawson, or west of Bellfountain.[^3]

In 1942 district warden Ray Oglesby said he would put up a temporary 30-foot tower on the peak. An 18-by-28-foot cabin was finished that August to house a five-man emergency crew.[^3]

A permanent replacement followed in 1950.[^3] An April 1950 Capital Journal item said the new lookout would be 40 feet high, with a garage on the ground floor, crew quarters above and a 14-by-14-foot glass lookout room on top.[^3] A late-July 1950 item called the newly built tower aluminum, which meant no yearly repainting of the kind wooden towers needed.[^3] The register and firelookout.com date the tower to 1951 and describe a three-story, enclosed, live-in tower belonging to the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^1][^2]

In 1947 Anthony Kelly was the lookout, and thieves took the station's short-wave radio while he slept in the cabin about 300 yards away.[^3] A 1950 photo caption shows him on the catwalk with the district warden.[^3] In February 1951 district warden W.M. Curtis reported that vandals had smashed a window and left newspapers burning on the floor.[^3] Curtis also said the radio was taken two years earlier, which conflicts with the 1947 date.[^3] In 1966 the station was first to spot four fires that season.[^3]

The lookout was removed in 1974, according to the register and the Forest Lookouts site.[^1][^3] firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the public can visit the site.[^1][^2][^3]
