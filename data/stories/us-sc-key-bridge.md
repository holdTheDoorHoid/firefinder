The Key Bridge tower stood in Sumter National Forest in McCormick County, South Carolina, according to the former-lookout register.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site places it in Edgefield County instead.[^2]

A newspaper item from August 1935, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, said a 100-foot steel tower would go up one to two miles from Key Bridge, near where Stevens and Turkey creeks meet.[^2] The same site says men from the Edgefield CCC camp put up a 100-foot steel tower in 1935.[^2] The former-lookout register credits the U.S. Forest Service with building the tower in 1935.[^1]

In October 1936 the Index-Journal reported that Norman Cook had been the Key Bridge lookout for 14 months and had worked Christmas and the other holidays.[^2] An October 1937 item reported upkeep and landscaping work at the Key Bridge lookout station.[^2] The register says the tower is no longer standing.[^1]

The name Key Bridge now mostly belongs to a bridge.[^3] It is a steel truss originally built in 1912 and rebuilt in 2007 by the South Carolina Army National Guard and the Forest Service.[^3] Today pedestrians can cross it to reach the Wine-Turkey Creek trail, which is used for biking and hiking.[^3] The register's coordinates for the tower sit about half a kilometre from the bridge marker's coordinates, yet the 1935 plan put the tower one to two miles from the bridge, so the exact spot is unclear.[^1][^2][^3]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the tower site, or whether that site is open to the public. Of the sources we read, only the bridge is described as open to foot traffic.[^3]
