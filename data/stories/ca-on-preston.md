Preston Fire Tower is in Algonquin Park, in Nipissing District, Ontario.[^1][^2] Its first tower went up in 1922 at Kitty Lake.[^1] That site proved unsatisfactory, and the tower was moved to a hilltop in 1932.[^1][^2] The hilltop, at an elevation of about 579 m (1,900 ft), is the highest spot in Algonquin Park.[^1][^2]

The register gives 1922 as the year built, but the park says the tower itself was put up on the hilltop in 1932.[^1][^2] Men from the J.R. Booth lumber company helped move the tower's parts to the new site, according to the park.[^2]

Towermen lived in a cabin about 2.4 km (1.5 miles) east of the tower.[^1][^2] The park now calls it the Tattler Lake Ranger Cabin.[^2] TOURduPARK, a website about Algonquin Park, dates the cabin to 1939, but the park says it has no record of when the cabin was built.[^3][^2]

By 1964, an old report in the park archives said the tower swayed noticeably in the wind and that its roof apparently needed fixing.[^1][^2] The tower was probably last used in 1970.[^1] A directive issued in 1971 set out that the tower, and most likely the cabin, should be dismantled.[^1][^2] The park reviewed all of its historic sites between 1971 and 1973, and a 1973 report on the cabin took a more favourable view.[^2]

No source confirms whether the tower was taken down.

The cabin still stands.[^1][^2] The park lists it among its backcountry ranger cabins and says a new roof and other improvements have since been made.[^2] It can be reached only by canoe, from the Shall Lake or Lake Opeongo access points, as there is no vehicle access.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the tower on the hilltop.
