Cement Ridge has carried a fire lookout in some form since the years right after the catastrophic 1910 fires that pushed the Forest Service to build towers nationwide. Sources differ on the exact start: the National Historic Lookout Register dates the first primitive structure to 1911, while firelookout.com describes a 1912 log cabin and pole platform.[^1][^2] Either way, it was one of the earliest lookouts in the Black Hills, alongside Harney Peak (now Black Elk Peak) and Bear Mountain.[^1]

A second structure, a crow's-nest cab with glassed-in sides, went up around 1921.[^2][^3] In 1935 the Civilian Conservation Corps added a rock cellar just west of the tower site, and five years later, in 1940, CCC crews tore down the aging crow's-nest and built the two-story lookout that still stands today; firelookout.com dates the construction to 1940-1941.[^1][^3][^2] That makes the current building the third lookout on the peak, in the Black Hills National Forest about a mile from the Wyoming-South Dakota line.[^1][^2]

Named lookout staff recorded over the decades include Hans Miller in 1923-24, Vernon Tollefson for early seasons from 1936 to 1950, Jane Russell in 1975, and Barb Peterson from 2009 to 2017.[^2] One local story credits an early lookout named Tom Sawyer as the first to drive a vehicle to the summit, inching up with a sack of potatoes wedged behind the tires for traction while he cleared brush ahead of it.[^1]

Cement Ridge was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on December 30, 1993.[^1][^3] Unlike many historic lookouts that have been retired to shelter or rental duty, this one is still a working fire lookout, staffed each fire season by the Forest Service, and it remains publicly accessible.[^1]
