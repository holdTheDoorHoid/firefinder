West Virginia's state government put up a fire tower on a narrow rock outcrop on Beaver Lick Mountain, east of Watoga State Park in Pocahontas County.[^1] A 1926 Charleston Gazette item said a tower on Beaver Lick Mountain was served by the Marlinton telephone office.[^2] The state transferred the tower to the Monongahela National Forest on November 15, 1948.[^3]

Its all-metal cab sat atop a short steel tower about 25 feet tall, which put it among the shortest fire towers on the Forest.[^1][^3] A 1953 Pocahontas Times item described a bear turning over rocks beneath the White Rock tower while a man was out taking the sun on its steps.[^2] The last year a lookout staffed it was 1973.[^2]

By the end of the 1970s the Forest had phased out staffing its towers, after turning to aerial patrols in the 1960s and early 1970s.[^1] A Monongahela fire-tower history says most of its towers were torn down, with only concrete piers and steps left as evidence they had stood.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout index marks the Beaver Lick site as Tower Gone.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site in January 2017.[^3]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains on the outcrop today, or whether visitors can reach it.
