Big Knob was a South Carolina Forestry Commission fire lookout in Laurens County.[^1] The Coast and Geodetic Survey placed its station about 7 miles (11 km) northwest of Laurens and about 2.5 miles (4 km) south of Gray Court, on a prominent wooded hill that the survey called the highest in the vicinity.[^1][^2]

The survey described the station in 1957. It was the apex of the roof of a glass-enclosed room on four steel legs, with the roof about 80 feet (24 m) above the ground.[^2] A Laurens County historic context study describes Big Knob and Little Knob, in the northern part of the county, as two granite monadnocks. I could not confirm that the study means this hill.[^4]

Across South Carolina, the commission's history says women staffed most towers from the 1960s on, and their husbands often worked as firefighters.[^3]

The commission ended its fire tower network on October 1, 1993.[^3] The lookout-history site lists Big Knob as removed.[^1] A post in a fire-tower enthusiasts' Facebook group, however, says a lookout tower stands on Big Knob.[^5]

Two sources disagree on what is there now, and none I found says whether the hill is open to visitors.
