Warren County Lookout stands near Liberia, about 4.5 miles south of Warrenton, in Warren County, North Carolina.[^1][^2] It is the only fire tower in that county.[^2] The Aermotor Company of Chicago built it in 1932, and its four-sided steel truss frame uses bolted connections.[^2]

Sources disagree about how tall it is. Wikipedia and the National Historic Lookout Register both describe a tower about 100 feet high, but a 1932 state report, as quoted by Eastern US Lookouts, gives 80 feet.[^2][^1][^3] That same report describes an inside stairway and a glass-enclosed cab.[^3] Eastern US Lookouts also points out that the measurements in the 2000 National Register form do not agree with one another.[^3]

The register says the tower was the first state forestry lookout in North Carolina staffed by a woman, and it names Bessie Haithcock as that observer.[^1] Her husband, Earlie, helped build the tower on part of his farm and staffed it until his death.[^1]

Sources do not agree on when the tower stopped being staffed. The register says it was in active use through 1990.[^1] Wikipedia says it was operated until 1993,[^2] and Eastern US Lookouts calls 1993 the last year it was staffed.[^3] None of the sources consulted names a particular fire that the tower spotted.

The register recorded the tower on March 30, 1997.[^1] It was added to the National Register of Historic Places on March 3, 2000.[^2]

Today the inside has been restored and is kept as a historic display at the Bessie Haithcock Forestry Center.[^1] The register lists the N.C. Division of Forest Resources as the agency that administers the tower.[^1] None of the sources says whether the tower itself is open to visitors, so check with the Division before planning a visit.
