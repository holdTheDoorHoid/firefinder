The Goat Mountain lookout site is in Whatcom County, Washington, on the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1][^3] A 20-foot tower with an L-4 cab went up on the site in 1934.[^1][^2][^4] A lookout-history website says it was likely built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^4]

Two sources say the lookout was abandoned in 1956.[^1][^2] The lookout-history website says the sources it found differ, giving either 1956 for abandonment or about 1963 for removal.[^4]

An undated visit reported on the same website found that all four footings and at least one anchor point remain, along with the usual rusty nails, hinges and glass.[^4] Several telephone line insulators are also found beside the main trail, on the climb up.[^4]

Getting there takes a hike. The lookout-history website describes an unsigned, unmaintained trail that switchbacks to the right a few minutes past the Mount Baker Wilderness sign, at about 3,800 feet.[^4] It runs about three-quarters of a mile up to the rocky knoll where the lookout once stood.[^4] A Washington Trails Association hike listing describes an unsigned trail past the wilderness sign that leads to a former lookout site, and says the lookout no longer stands.[^5] No closure is described in the sources we read.
