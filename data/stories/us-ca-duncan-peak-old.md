Duncan Peak (old) was a ground-based fire lookout on a granite peak in the Foresthill district of the Tahoe National Forest, in Placer County.[^2][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a ground site that is now gone.[^4]

A 1941 newspaper dated the old lookout to 1913.[^2] The register gives an earlier 10-foot tower, built between 1910 and 1915 on the Little Bald Mountain site, and a 1917 ground house just north of the current structure.[^1] A lookout information sheet that names the Foresthill Ranger District says the tower was rebuilt in 1917 on the actual Duncan Peak, just north of the current lookout site.[^3] The early dates do not fully line up. The 1941 paper gives 1913, the register and the information sheet both give 1910 to 1915 for the first structure, and the register calls the 1917 building a ground house where the information sheet calls it a tower.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1915 a Truckee paper described it as a primary lookout in the Foresthill district.[^2] A 1920 letter to the Placer Herald described a wooden station with open sides, a table, a circular chart and telephone lines, at 7,170 feet.[^2]

In September 1941 the Nevada State Journal reported that a new lookout on Bald Mountain, roughly a mile from Robertson Flat, was to take over from the old Duncan Peak Lookout.[^2] The register says the 1917 ground house was dismantled in 1941.[^1] The Duncan Peak name stayed with the Little Bald Mountain site, where the present groundhouse was built in 1943.[^1][^3]

The register's photo list includes a 2009 picture captioned "Site of 1917 Cabin."[^1] The register places the 1917 ground house just north of the current structure, but the caption does not say whether it is the same building or what remains there.[^1] No source describes what remains of the old peak today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
