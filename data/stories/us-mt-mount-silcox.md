Mount Silcox stands about four miles northeast of Thompson Falls in Sanders County, on the Lolo National Forest.[^1][^2] The forest service chose the peak as a lookout site in 1907 and named it for F. A. Silcox, the first supervisor of the Cabinet reserve.[^2]

A log cabin went up on the summit in 1908. A July report said it was finished apart from the shake roof.[^1][^2][^3] In 1923 a 35-foot steel windmill tower went up to improve the view.[^1][^3] In 1925 a standard D-6 cupola cabin took the place of the old cabin and tower.[^2] It sat on a rock foundation about half a mile from the summit.[^3] Firelookout.com dates that cupola cabin to 1926 and puts it half a mile southwest of the summit.[^1]

Lookouts were posted on the peak through the 1910s and 1920s.[^2] Newspapers name Oscar H. Moen as the summer lookout in 1914 and say Fred Irvin was set to take the job in 1930.[^2] A 1914 newspaper credited the lookout with spotting dozens of fires that were put out before they did damage.[^3]

In 1935 routine watch on Mount Silcox was discontinued, and the peak was kept for emergencies. A lookout on Clark Mountain across the river from Thompson Falls took its place.[^2][^3] The Forest Service gave up the lookout in 1952.[^3] Through 1965 the public could still use it with a Montana Fish and Game permit.[^3] In October 1967 teenagers trying to get a fire going in the old stove set the wooden building ablaze by accident, and it burned down.[^3] Firelookout.com also gives 1967 as the year it was destroyed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the site as gone.[^4]

According to a Thompson Falls community post, remnants of the original telephone line, including a few ceramic insulators, could still be found as late as the 1990s on the peak and elsewhere along Trail 450.[^3] No source describes what remains today, and none says whether the site is open to visitors.
