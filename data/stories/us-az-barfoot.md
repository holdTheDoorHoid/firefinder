The Barfoot Lookout was a 14-by-14-foot wood-frame ground cabin on Buena Vista Peak in the Chiricahua Mountains.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1935,[^3] but Wikipedia calls the CCC attribution a hypothesis.[^1] The cabin stood at 8,703 feet (2,653 meters), and it had a roof-catch cistern.[^3][^4]

In June 1946 William J. Fry, lookout on what a 1946 newspaper item calls Barefoot peak, reported that smoke from a fire two miles away had engulfed his station.[^2] The lookout was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1988, as one of a group of forest fire lookouts in the Southwestern Region.[^5][^1] The two sources disagree on the date: the National Park Service weekly list gives January 28, 1988, and Wikipedia gives July 28, 1988.[^5][^1]

In early June 2011 the Horseshoe Two Fire overran the lookout.[^2] A June 8, 2011 item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, listed Barfoot Lookout among the buildings destroyed during the fast fire run.[^2] The Register lists the lookout as removed in June 2011, but its text says the fire destroyed it.[^3]

The only remnant the sources describe is the foundation.[^4][^3] The Register places the site in Coronado National Forest, administered by the U.S. Forest Service, but none of the sources we read says whether the foundation is open to visitors.[^3]
