The McGaughey Lookout Tower stands in Whitfield County, Georgia, in the extreme north of the state, less than five miles from the Tennessee line.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it an early fire tower.[^1] The Register gives no build year and does not describe the tower's design.[^1]

According to the Register, which cites the Georgia Forestry Commission, the tower was first staffed by a Mrs. Crow, whose family owned the land it stands on.[^1] A 2016 obituary, carried on a Find a Grave memorial, says she served 36 years as the lookout there and was the first female lookout the Commission had in Georgia.[^2]

The accounts do not fully agree on the timeline.[^1][^2] The Register says the Commission's lease of the land ended and the tower was set for removal, and that Mrs. Crow's son bought it from the state around 2009.[^1] The Register also places the removal and the sale after her death, yet dates the sale to around 2009, while the obituary records her death in October 2016.[^1][^2] The sources do not explain the mismatch.[^1][^2]

A visit report dated February 8, 2019, says the tower had been privately bought about ten years earlier, is no longer maintained for safety and is not open to the public.[^1] The visitor found the road gated twice and fenced, and could not climb the tower.[^1] As of 2019, the Register says the tower is owned by the son's widow.[^1]

Today the Register says no access is allowed.[^1] The newest report found is from 2019, so the tower's current condition is unknown.[^1] Stay off the private road, and do not climb the tower.[^1]
