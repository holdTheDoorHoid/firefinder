In November 1937 the U.S. Forest Service finished a 40-foot secondary lookout on Melcher Hill, in Hardin County on the Shawnee National Forest, south of what the report calls the Wye.[^1] The Hardin County Independent report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, describes it as nothing more than a telephone pole, braced by three guy wires and fitted with a small ladder to climb.[^1] An Illinois Country Living profile of Bob Frakes, a historian of fire lookouts, describes the early Melcher Hill tower as little more than a platform perched on a telephone pole.[^3]

The report says the tower was meant to sight fires and would be manned during hazardous fire conditions. The Rosiclare Ranger district planned four secondary towers, and this was the first.[^1] Frakes says pole towers like this one were used for dry-season and backup coverage, a pole braced by wires with a platform on top.[^4] A photograph he describes shows a fire finder under a canvas cover on the Melcher Hill platform.[^4]

The report said John Rotes would man the Melcher Hill tower, which was to have a telephone. The other three planned towers were to have radios.[^1]

A 1938 photograph, credited to the National Archives, shows Jack McQueen standing on Melcher Hill's emergency platform.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Melcher Hill as a platform that is gone.[^2] The same profile says Frakes visited 17 Illinois sites before nearly all of the state's towers were removed or sold during the early 1970s.[^3]

No source describes what remains at the Melcher Hill site today, or whether it is open to visitors. The Forest Service says that of the 16 fire lookouts put up on the Shawnee National Forest, only Trigg Lookout Tower remains.[^5]
