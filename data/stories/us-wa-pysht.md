Pysht Lookout Site is in Clallam County, about eight miles southeast of Clallam Bay.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as the administrator.[^1]

A state list of lookouts from 1942 records the site as a Cowan-type lookout. Willhite says that type is a two-story cabin about 10 by 10.[^4] The register dates the tower to 1942.[^1]

In September 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service activated the site under the Port Angeles Filter Center.[^3] Willhite notes that the service used existing state structures and built a 14 x 32 cabin for sleeping quarters, kept apart from the lookout.[^3][^4] When the service ended, the State kept the site.[^3]

The tower is gone.[^1][^2] The register says it was built in 1942 and is now gone, and FireLookout.com's entry reads only "1942: tower. Gone."[^1][^2]

Willhite describes the summit as a large, open area with no growth.[^4] A previous lookout group was told this was where the buildings were.[^4]

Willhite's access notes cover the Pysht Tree Farm, bought in 1886 and still run by its original owners.[^4] He says access needs permission, though the road is paved to the gate at the community of Pysht. His guide gives a hiking or biking distance of about four miles one way, without saying where it starts.[^4]

The sources describe no remains at the site beyond the open summit.[^4]
