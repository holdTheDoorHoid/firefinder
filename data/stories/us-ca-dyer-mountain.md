Dyer Mountain Lookout stands in the Lassen National Forest in Lassen County, California, at about 7,474 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin on a 15-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1]

The register and Forest Lookouts both give 1934 as the build year.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts says a Conservation Corps crew erected it with lumber from the Red River Lumber Company, and that pack horses delivered the supplies to the site.[^2]

High winds tore its west shutters off in June 1937.[^2] That September, a spark from a flatcar's brake shoe started a fire that destroyed a logging trestle over Dry Creek, and the Dyer Mountain lookout was the first to report it.[^2]

Forest Lookouts quotes 1955 and 1956 notices naming John and Lillian Dulvick as summer staff.[^2]

State funding ran short in the early 1990s.[^2] A 1992 Lassen County Times item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said the cut came in the 1991-92 fiscal year and that "severity" funds had covered the 1991 season.[^2] The lookout opened on June 15, 1992, funded for two months.[^2] The sources do not give one end date for state support. An October 1996 item still called the lookout state funded,[^2] and the register quotes CAL FIRE's 2011-2012 plan as saying the permanent cut came about 15 years before that plan.[^1]

The register says CAL FIRE used the tower as a lookout until 2007.[^1] When staffed, the register adds, it served the Almanor and Eagle Lake ranger districts and a Plumas National Forest district.[^1]

Its lower room now houses state and federal radio equipment.[^1] CAL FIRE's 2011-2012 plan calls the building unsafe.[^1] Guy wires sagged on one side, stair boards were cracked and splintered, and the catwalk was separating from the main structure.[^1] The plan says the Forest Service had stopped regular maintenance and might pursue demolition rather than restoration.[^1] CAL FIRE also took the lookout off its list of historic buildings to preserve.[^1]

The lookout joined the register on October 31, 2019, as US 1376.[^1] In 2020 the Forest Service proposed a new special use permit so CAL FIRE could install a 10-by-10-foot metal sea container at the Dyer Mountain Communications Site and move its radio equipment out of the lookout.[^3] The project page lists the decision as signed November 7, 2020, and the project as completed.[^3]

The register's text dates from the 2011-2012 plan, so it may be out of date. The newest source I found is a 2020 Forest Service page.[^3] The register calls the building unsafe for use, so I would not advise climbing it. I found no source describing public visits.[^1]
