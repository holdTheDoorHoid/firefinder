Dry Hill is a Jackson County lookout in the Shawnee National Forest.[^1] A listing from about 1942 gives its height as 100 feet, lists its purpose as fire detection, and places it roughly half a mile southwest of Camp Glenn.[^1]

The tower was in use by April 1936.[^1] That month the Daily Independent reported that 37 people had climbed the tower for its view over the previous month.[^1] That August the same paper said the tower's garage was finished and the house was nearly done.[^1] It also said work would soon start on a telephone line linking the Dry Hill tower with the Fountain Bluff tower and the camp.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Dry Hill as gone.[^2] In August 1972 the Southern Illinoisan ran a notice seeking bids for the sale of this tower and seven other steel lookouts on the Shawnee National Forest.[^1] A 2021 Illinois Country Living article reports that nearly all Illinois towers were removed or sold off in the early 1970s.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts site marks the tower as removed.[^1] No source I found describes what remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.
