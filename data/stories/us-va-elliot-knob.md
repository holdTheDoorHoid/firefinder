Elliott Knob is the highest point on North Mountain, about 3.5 miles northwest of Staunton, Virginia, and it sits inside the George Washington National Forest.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service looks after the site.[^1]

The lookout on the summit went up in 1948 to replace an earlier tower from the 1920s.[^1][^4] Its cab is a metal live-in room about 14 feet square, with a catwalk.[^1] SummitPost puts the tower at 30 feet, and the register's figure is also 30.[^1][^4] A 1957 survey description quoted by Forest Lookouts says the tower is about 45 feet and gives the steel house on top as 12 feet, so the height is not settled.[^2]

The register listed the lookout on August 18, 1994, and counts it among just three surviving lookouts of its kind in Virginia.[^1]

It still stands, and the Virginia lookouts list on firelookout.org marks it as standing.[^6] Wikipedia describes the lookout as closed, and says the tower is enclosed by a fence with barbed wire at the top.[^3] A jeep trail climbs the mountain from the east, but it is closed to public vehicles, and the Forest Service uses it to reach the tower.[^3] Visitors go on foot.[^3] A hiking guide describes an iron gate at the start of the route that restricts motor vehicles.[^5] Wikipedia adds that several other trails also reach the top.[^3]

The register says a road or a trail gets you there. That fits with the jeep trail being open to walkers but not to cars.[^1][^3][^5]
