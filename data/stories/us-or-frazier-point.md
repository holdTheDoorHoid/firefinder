Frazier Point sits in the Malheur National Forest in Grant County, Oregon, about 27 miles southeast of John Day.[^2][^1] The first lookout was on a rocky point about half a mile away, set up as an emergency post in the early 1930s.[^2][^3] Panorama photos were taken from that first site in October 1935.[^3] The present tower stands about half a mile north of it.[^3] Most sources date the 100-foot treated timber tower to 1936, but an archive file quoted by Forest Lookouts says it was completed in 1937.[^2][^3][^4][^5] A January 7, 1937 newspaper item describes the tower as already built by CCC enrollees and says the lookout would use it the next summer.[^3]

Lookouts worked the tower every season through 2008, and staffing ended that year.[^2][^4] Forest Lookouts quotes a 1943 Burns Times-Herald item that names Merlin J. McMullen as the lookout.[^3] The sources read do not describe any fire the lookout spotted.

The original cabin was lost to fire in the 1970s, and the garage was later turned into housing.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register listed the site on January 8, 2003.[^1] In July 2019 an inspection notice marked the tower as condemned for major deficiencies, and the Forest Service has it on a plan to tear it down.[^1][^4]

When the NHLR and The Roaming Civic recorded the site, the stairs were boarded over to keep people off the tower.[^1][^4] A gravel spur, NF-849, has no gate and leads about one mile to the site.[^4][^5] A 2026 directory lists the site as abandoned but legally accessible, a column that means legal access to the site rather than to the lookout itself.[^6] Visitors should stay off the condemned tower, and anyone planning a trip should check with the Malheur National Forest first.
