The Gambrill Fire Tower stands in Frederick County, Maryland, two miles north of Gambrill State Park, on Gambrill Road.[^1][^2] A Maryland Historical Trust form written for the Salamander Fire Tower site dates it to the mid-1940s, around 1945.[^1] The same form counts five fire towers built in Frederick County. Two sat inside the city's watershed, and the other three, Gambrill among them, did not.[^1]

The form describes the state's towers in general terms: four long metal legs, an interior staircase, an enclosed cab, and heights between 60 and 120 feet.[^1] It gives no height or cab details for Gambrill itself.[^1] The form links the towers to a statewide fire-detection system.[^1] One tower, the form says, could watch roughly ten miles around it, and two towers together could fix a fire's position.[^1]

The sources do not record who staffed Gambrill or when. The form notes that by the late 1950s many towers had stopped being staffed on a regular schedule, a general trend rather than a record for this tower.[^1] The sources I could reach name no particular fire at this tower.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, with an asterisk. Its footnote says the asterisk marks towers that keep their structure after the cab is gone.[^2] The state form calls Gambrill the only fire tower in Frederick County that remains intact.[^1] That sits oddly beside the association's note that the cab is gone, and neither source describes the tower's present condition in detail.[^1][^2]

Neither source says who manages the land or whether the public may visit. The association's entry gives no access note, and I found no current staffing record.[^2] Do not climb the tower, and check with whoever manages the land before planning any visit.

[^2]: Maryland Fire Lookout Towers, Forest Fire Lookout Association, https://firelookout.org/lookouts/us/md/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
