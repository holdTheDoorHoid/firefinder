Issaqueena Tower is on the Clemson Experimental Forest in Pickens County.[^1] According to the Register, the 100-foot Aermotor, with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, dates to 1938 and was put up by the CCC and WPA.[^1] The forest it stands in is a product of a New Deal land project around Clemson College, which bought nearly 30,000 acres of worn-out farmland and built fire towers and recreation facilities.[^2]

The Register calls the tower and its grounds a local attraction and an illustration of modern rural living in the 1930s, a setting that also included the area's first electricity.[^1] It does not say who staffed the tower or for how long.[^1] The Register listed the tower on July 27, 2006, on a nomination from S. Knight Cox.[^1]

The wider recreation area around Lake Issaqueena had a rough stretch early on, with vandalism and neglect, and it was used as a bombing range during World War II.[^4] The Register does not describe the tower's condition today, or whether it is still in use.[^1]

Hikers, horseback riders and cyclists are limited to designated roads and trails on the forest.[^3] The forest rules say the Issaqueena Lake Road is closed between November 1 and March 15, or as needed.[^3] Whether the tower itself is open to visitors is not stated in the Register or the forest rules.[^1][^3]
