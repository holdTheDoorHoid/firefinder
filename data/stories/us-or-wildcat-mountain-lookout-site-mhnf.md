A rag camp with a fire finder and a telephone was set up on Wildcat Mountain in 1928, in Clackamas County within the Mount Hood National Forest.[^1][^2] In 1931 an L-4 cab replaced it.[^1][^2]

A newspaper item from August 1931, copied onto an Oregon lookouts index, said the new station was well underway and that a string of pack horses was hauling building material up the mountain. It named Milton Beal as the lookout.[^3]

The sources disagree on when the lookout ended. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was removed in 1952.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed in 1953.[^2]

The Oregon Hikers field guide describes an out-and-back walk up the Douglas Trail from the Douglas Trailhead. The hike begins on a logging spur that can be tricky to find for first-timers, and it ends at an unmarked spur to the summit.[^4] The guide also says a 1930s Forest Service lookout once stood there, and that the view once extended in every direction. Trees are now obscuring the northern views, including the view of Mount Hood.[^4]

A 2013 blog post describes the top as a small rocky clearing surrounded by trees. The post does not mention any remains of the lookout.[^5]
