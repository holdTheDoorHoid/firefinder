The first fire tower on Robbins Hill in Chelmsford went up in 1911. It was 30 feet high and reached by a ladder.[^1] The state forester's 1918 report says the state had kept a tower on the hill for six years and calls that earlier one a small windmill-style lookout with a ladder.[^2] In 1918 the state bought the summit and put up a 75-foot tower.[^2] The Parkhurst column gives that 1918 tower's height as 68 feet, so the sources disagree.[^1]

The hurricane of September 21, 1938 destroyed the 1918 tower.[^2] The present tower went up in 1939 to replace it. It is 68 feet tall, made of steel, and has a 10-by-10-foot cab.[^2][^1] The column places its erection in the summer after the storm.[^1] The Register gives 1939 as the build year, yet its description says the tower was constructed in 1938.[^3] The Register also calls the present tower the fourth on the site, and the column agrees.[^3][^1] The accounts I read name only three towers, from 1911, 1918 and 1939, so the missing one is still unidentified.[^1][^2]

Staffing shows up only in scraps. The 1940 section of the town's fire department history says the new rearmament program was already causing an extreme shortage of help at fires.[^4] The same section says several of its most experienced tower operators, including its own local operator, were now on new work and would not be available for the year ahead.[^4] The forest warden's 1940 report tallied 124 actual fires and more than 20 investigations of calls made from the tower.[^4] The 1941 section of the same history credits the tower with letting the town spot fires and pin down where they began.[^4] In a column from about 1986, the present observer said the tower served only to elevate someone else's antennae, which had no bearing on fire work.[^1]

The column says the wooden cab was completely replaced about 1970.[^1] In 2015 the state's forest fire control maintenance crew repaired minor vandalism at the tower, according to the Southern New England chapter report.[^5]

The newest source I read is a 2019 blog post, which describes a quick climb to the top.[^6] That is one visitor's account, not an access rule. I found nothing that says whether the tower is open to the public today, who staffs it now, or whether it can be rented. The Register lists the administering agency as the Mass. Bureau of Forest Fire Control.[^3] Check with that agency or the town before any visit, and do not climb a tower that is closed or posted private.
