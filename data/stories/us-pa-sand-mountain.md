The Sand Mountain Fire Tower stands on Sand Mountain in Union County, Pennsylvania, and the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry administers it today.[^1] The first tower went up in 1917. The state Department of Forestry bought a steel observation tower from the Aermotor Company of Chicago and had it erected that August and September.[^2] Officials called it the first of a planned series of about a hundred towers across the state's forests, and a line about four miles long was built from the Forest House to the tower in 1918.[^2]

Sources disagree on that first tower's height. A 1917 contract described a sixty-three-foot tower, while a report from the same year gave sixty feet, and the register also lists a 60-foot Aermotor.[^1][^2]

In its early years the lookout was staffed. A state publication, Forest Leaves, covering 1919 to 1922, says an observer stayed on the tower day and night through the dangerous fire season.[^2] None of the sources I checked credit the tower with a specific fire.

The 1917 tower was replaced in 1950 by an 80-foot Aermotor that had first been built at Eastville, also called Hartley, in 1938 and then moved to this ridge.[^1][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1951 for the replacement, so the two sources differ by a year.[^4] The National Historic Lookout Register added the tower on December 6, 2017, as US 1253, PA 22.[^1]

A visit report dated June 4, 2022, found the tower in very bad shape. Its plexiglass had all been broken out, the door was gone, and several of the bottom flights of stairs had been torn away.[^1] A large communications tower sits beside it.[^1] The same report says access to the tower is prohibited, that the tower was unstaffed, and that the district was waiting for funding to remove it.[^1] Treat it as closed, and do not climb the structure.
