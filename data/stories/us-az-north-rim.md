North Rim Lookout is a 75-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, first built in 1928 on Bright Angel Point [^1]. The Forest Service had already put a wooden lookout platform on that point before Grand Canyon National Park was created in 1919 [^2]. Later, the Park Service replaced those platforms with steel towers at Bright Angel and Hopi points [^2]. In 1933 the 1928 tower was moved to its current summit site and became North Rim Lookout [^1][^3].

The park's early managers were worried about finding and fighting fires from the 1920s through the 1960s [^2]. According to the register, the tower has stopped serving as a fire detection post [^1]. The pages read here name no single fire at this tower. A travel guide says Edward Abbey worked as a seasonal fire lookout here in the late 1960s and 1970s [^3].

The National Park Service calls all four of its Grand Canyon lookout towers on the register inactive [^2]. The register listed this one on April 25, 2009 [^1], and the listing was recognized at a ceremony on July 4, 2009 [^2].

The summit is reached by an unpaved road that a travel guide says needs a high-clearance 4x4 [^3]. The same guide says the road is not plowed in winter [^3]. These sources do not confirm current public access. The register and the Park Service place the tower in Grand Canyon National Park [^1][^2], while the travel guide puts it within the Kaibab National Forest [^3]. This story follows the register's reading.
