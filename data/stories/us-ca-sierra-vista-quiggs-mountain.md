Sierra Vista is a fire lookout on a ridge in Calaveras County, about four miles northeast of San Andreas.[^6] The hill was first called Quigg's Mountain, and Cal Fire now administers the site.[^1][^2][^6]

The first lookout went up in 1931.[^2][^3] It was a 125-foot sugar pine, topped at about 75 feet, with a crow's nest built on it.[^2] Newspapers from that spring said a station was being set up on Mt. Ararat, that it would carry the name Sierra Vista Lookout, and that a steel tower was expected the following year.[^3] A 1986 historical society bulletin says the tree served until 1934, when the steel tower replaced it.[^2] The state's own register dates the tower to 1931, so the sources disagree on that point.[^1]

Dave and Mary Serra were lookouts there during the 1932 and 1933 fire seasons.[^2] They lived in a small cabin below the lookout and kept watch seven days a week, driving to town once a week for groceries and mail.[^2] Don Wriggley had been the first lookout stationed at the site.[^2] Later, Dave Serra and a Civilian Conservation Corps crew ran a single-wire phone line from the lookout to San Andreas.[^2] The register says the house was built with ECW funds and the garage by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1]

The sources read do not name any fire the lookout spotted.

In 2019 Cal Fire proposed replacing aging telecommunications infrastructure on the site with a new tower. Its notice listed an abandoned residence and shed for removal.[^4] The following year an internet provider began broadcasting from the lookout tower, which also hosts an AlertWildfire camera.[^5] In 2025 Cal Fire planned brush clearing along Sierra Vista Lookout Road so that trucks could reach the tower for maintenance and infrastructure restoration.[^6]

The tower is still listed as standing, and it hosts wireless broadcast equipment.[^5][^7] The sources read say nothing about public visits, and none describe a rental. Check with Cal Fire before planning any trip up the road.
