The Brattleboro water department built the Pleasant Valley fire tower in 1943 to watch over its reservoir and the town's watershed.[^1] A Brattleboro Reformer report from that July described it as a new tower that the department put up with Water Superintendent Justin A. Holden in charge.[^1] It was a 34-foot wooden structure in three stories, with a 12-foot square base, a 10-foot second floor and an 8-foot top deck.[^1] Water department employees did the work and sawed the lumber except for clapboards and window frames. The cost was about $1,100.[^1]

The tower was manned part of the time. Wayne Yeaw, a long-serving town and water department worker, was to be stationed there some of the time, and in dry spells the superintendent and Herbert Ingalls were to spend alternate Sundays there.[^1] In October 1947 the superintendent said it had been manned for some time because the woods were so dry.[^1]

The later notices are mostly about damage. A 1973 item says the town owned the tower, which stood on Pleasant Valley Road.[^1] In January 1974 police were told it had been "shot up," with damage to its door and telephone lines.[^1] A break-in and vandalism were reported in 1979.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Vermont list still shows it as standing.[^2] I did not find a recent report on its condition or on whether the public may climb it. The reservoir supplies Brattleboro's water, so check with the town before visiting.[^3]
