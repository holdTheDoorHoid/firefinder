Quigg Peak rises to 8,419 feet in Granite County, Montana, about 17 miles east of Stevensville, in the Lolo National Forest.[^1]

The firelookout.com page describes a log and frame cupola cabin built in 1928.[^1] Rex's Forest Lookouts page, though, quotes 1921 and 1923 newspapers that refer to a lookout on the peak.[^2] A Billings Gazette item from September 1921 described a station as still to be built, and a Missoulian item later that month called the site a combination lookout.[^2] The sources disagree on when a lookout first stood there, and no source explains the gap.

The newspaper items also name people who were on the lookout or assigned to it.[^2] In 1923 Carter Kem of Indiana had spent that fire season on the lookout, and in 1926 Jack Thurmond of Georgia was to be stationed there for the summer.[^2]

The firelookout.com page says the cabin was destroyed in 1968.[^1] Rex's page lists the site as removed, without a date.[^2] The two sources differ on how it ended.

No source describes what, if anything, remains on Quigg Peak today, or whether the peak is open to visitors.
