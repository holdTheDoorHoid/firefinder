The Red Mountain lookout stands on a ridge that runs roughly north to south, inside Six Rivers National Forest in Del Norte County.[^1] The register dates it to 1951.[^1] That October the Redwood Journal Press-Dispatch reported that the first cooperative fire lookout in the Humboldt-Del Norte area had been set up that year, with five companies and the Forest Service sharing the costs.[^2] The cab sits on a 30-foot tower of the 809R design, which the register says is believed to be exclusive to the California Department of Forestry (CDF). It also says only six late-style 30-foot 809R towers exist.[^1] A 1966 survey description puts the cab floor 30 feet above the ground.[^2]

Vandals struck in May 1954.[^2] The Humboldt Standard reported that the station's windows had been stripped of all glass, with damage put at $250, and that four young men had been arrested and had confessed.[^2] The register says the lookout is staffed during fire season.[^1] I did not find a notable fire or a staffing list for this lookout in the sources I read.

By the time of the register, the ridge also held several communications vaults, radio towers and microwave facilities, which the register calls the only significant detractors from the property's overall integrity.[^1] A National Park Service release dated December 29, 2017, now archived, said state-owned facilities on the ridge must be removed when a Forest Service special-use permit expires on December 31, 2022.[^3] A state budget request for fiscal 2025-26 sought funding to relocate the communications site.[^4] None of these sources says what will happen to the lookout cab itself.

The register gives a registration date of November 27, 2019.[^1] It describes the site as accessible to the public, about six miles from the nearest paved road.[^1] I found no source saying whether the cab is open to climbing, so treat it as a working fire lookout and check with Cal Fire before you go.
