The Winnebago lookout stood in Thurston County, Nebraska, on the Winnebago Indian Reservation.[^1] A Sunday Journal and Star story from November 7, 1937, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says the first of a series of government-built towers in the county was completed in August.[^1] The steel-frame outlook tower carried a crow's nest 70 feet above the ground, on a high bluff overlooking the Missouri River. From the nest, the reservation could be seen from about 600 feet above the river bed.[^1]

Leland Decora, 31, a Winnebago Indian, was the boss of the tower.[^1] His job was to watch for forest fires and for woodcutting done without a permit, and he spent eight hours a day in the crow's nest.[^1] He made his reports by telephone to the agency at Winnebago, and wrote up the weather and forest conditions at the end of each day.[^1] Their three-room house, built from cedar slabs about 300 feet from the tower, was furnished for Mr. and Mrs. Decora.[^1]

The article says the tower was built under a government "CC-ID Indian division project," and that a delegation of twelve councilmen of the Winnebago tribe controlled the forestry work.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower at 42.22844, -96.36437 and marks it gone.[^2] The Forest Lookouts site marks it removed.[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors may go there.
