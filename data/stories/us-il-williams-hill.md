Williams Hill is the highest spot in southern Illinois, at 1,065 feet. It sits in the northeast corner of Pope County.[^1]

A c.1942 description places a Forest Service lookout station, with a 60-foot steel tower, on the hill two miles due west of Herod.[^1]

Notices from 1948 and 1955 describe an annual Easter service at the tower. In March 1948 the American Sunday School Union announced that its sunrise service would move there from the High Knob Tower in Hardin County, where it had been held for two years.[^1] A March 30, 1955 notice set the service at 3 p.m. on Easter Sunday, April 10.[^1]

A July 3, 1958 newspaper column reported that the two buildings at the site had been moved away and that the Forest Service did not man the tower in summer.[^1] The columnist climbed the tower on some new steps, but found the little building at the top kept locked. The picnic area in the grove had three tables and four cooking spots.[^1]

On August 18, 1972, an advertisement in the Southern Illinoisan invited bids to buy this tower and seven other steel towers on the Shawnee National Forest.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Illinois list marks the tower as gone.[^2] The Forest Service says the Trigg Lookout Tower is the sole survivor of the 16 fire lookouts built on the Shawnee National Forest.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
