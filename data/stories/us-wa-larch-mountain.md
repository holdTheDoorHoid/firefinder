The Larch Mountain lookout in Clark County, Washington, went up in 1952 as a 40-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The state's forestry division ran it, and its annual report lists the tower as forty feet tall with a cab measuring 14 by 14 feet.[^1][^3]

In November 1952, during the Yacolt burn fire, Charles Briggs sent most of the updates on the blaze out over the radio from the lookout.[^3] He was forced out once the blaze crossed into Clark County and started up the mountain's east flank.[^3]

In May 1955 State Forester Beckim predicted a late opening, saying drifts seven feet deep were holding his crews back.[^3] Its usual opening date was April 15.[^3]

In August 1969 the state discontinued the lookout, along with four others on the Battle Ground district, because aerial patrols now handled fire detection.[^3] It stayed in service as a weather station, reporting on the east winds that push through the Columbia Gorge.[^3][^4]

The lookout tower was destroyed in 1974.[^2][^4] The register says it was removed that year.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site on June 24, 2012.[^1]

A National Geodetic Survey record in Clark County is labelled LARCH MTN RADAR TOWER.[^5] The record has no station description, so it does not show what, if anything, stands on the mountain today.[^3][^5]

The mountain sits inside the Yacolt Burn State Forest and has a network of gravel roads running across it.[^6] Willhite says a sound road can be walked up from the Larch Mountain Trailhead, and that a gate on the L-1500 road, a short way below it, is shut in winter.[^4] There is no lookout tower to climb.[^4]

Willhite points out that a better-known Larch Mountain lookout stands across the Columbia Gorge in Oregon, a separate site.[^4]
