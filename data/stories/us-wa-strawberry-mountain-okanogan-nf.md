The Strawberry Mountain lookout stood in Okanogan County, Washington, in the Okanogan National Forest at 4,742 feet.[^1][^2] Willhite notes there were two Strawberry Mountains with lookouts in the county.[^3] This one lies a few miles northeast of Mount Bonaparte, between Lost Lake and Bonaparte Lake.[^3] One list places it in the Colville National Forest, but the register, firelookout.com and the trip report all say Okanogan.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

A 1934 newspaper report described an emergency tower under construction, with a road to it.[^3][^4] The register describes a 40-foot pole tower and L-5 cabin living quarters, built that year.[^1][^3]

During the war the site was used for aircraft detection, from August 21, 1942 to July 1, 1943.[^3]

In 1954 the tower was shortened to 10 feet and used to train lookouts.[^1][^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1963. Firelookout.com and the trip report say it was destroyed or burned that year.[^1][^2][^3]

The trip report does not give a date in the text we read. It shows two footings at the site, with a third almost buried.[^3] We found no source that describes a standing structure. The route starts at Lost Lake, a hike of just under a mile each way with about 650 feet of climb.[^3] Most sources name the land as Okanogan National Forest, and none we read mentions a closure, but we found no official access rule, so check with the forest before you go.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
