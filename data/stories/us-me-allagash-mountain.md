The Allagash Mountain lookout is in Piscataquis County, Maine.[^1][^2] A 1916 forest commissioner's report describes a lookout station built on the mountain, with a camp at its foot for the watchman.[^2]

A 1919 report describes the mountain's tower as a log tower built in 1916.[^2] By 1920 the inventory listed a 20-foot wooden tower.[^2] A July 1924 newspaper item said a 27-foot tower would be erected there.[^2] The pages we read do not confirm that it was finished.[^2]

Observers once watched towers like this one for smoke on high fire danger days.[^3] We found no notable fire tied to this tower in the sources we read. The Bangor Daily News column places Allagash among the last towers taken out of service, and ties the final shutdowns to a budget crisis in 1991.[^3] We did not find an exact closing year for this tower.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list shows the tower as standing.[^1] By November 2020 the Maine Bureau of Parks and Lands had recently installed a new cab on it, according to a Bangor Daily News column from that month.[^3] A 2020 year-end report from the same association also lists the Allagash cab replacement among fire-tower projects that the state historic preservation office reviewed that year.[^4] Kleinschmidt Associates designed the new cabs, which were patterned on the one that stood on Deboullie Mountain.[^3] Bureau staff and volunteers stabilized the steel towers, and a helicopter company from Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, was hired to lift the cabs into place.[^3]

The cab is set up for recreational visitors, and the column's headline says hikers can visit.[^3] The pages we read do not describe a trail or road to the site, and we found no closure notice. Check with the Bureau of Parks and Lands before you go.
