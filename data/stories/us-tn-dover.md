The Dover Lookout Tower is in Stewart County, Tennessee.[^1] On October 7, 1955, the state bought twelve 80-foot steel lookout towers for about $36,000, and one of them was to be built at this site.[^1] The listing for the tower places it under the Tennessee Division of Forestry's District 6.[^1]

We could not find the year the tower went up, and the sources we read give no build date.

A former worker, writing on a Land Between the Lakes blog, says they worked at the Division's towers in Stewart and Houston counties from the summer of 1975 until the summer of 1976.[^2] The same account says they helped paint this tower from top to bottom, and that the work included fighting fires and keeping the grass and weeds cut.[^2] That is one personal account, and we could not check it against state records.

Tennessee Landforms lists the tower as standing.[^3] The same site notes that many old towers have been removed and that most of their sites are now private property.[^3] We found no National Register listing for this tower and no recent photo or news report of it.

We do not know who owns the site today, or whether visitors may go up. Tennessee Landforms advises against climbing the towers, because steps are often missing or decayed.[^3] Please do not climb it. The listing places it under the Division of Forestry, so ask there before going near the site. We found no rental use.
