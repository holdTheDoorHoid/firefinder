In March 1930, the Forest Worker reported that Gibbs Bros. & Co., the Foster Lumber Co., and the Delta Land & Timber Co. had agreed to work with the Texas Forest Service on a protection unit in southern Walker and northern Montgomery Counties.[^1] The unit's 111,293 acres of timberland belonged to the three companies, with an almost equal area held by smaller owners.[^1] Ahead of the 1930 fire season, a 97-foot steel tower had been put up on a high point roughly 13 miles south of Huntsville.[^1]

A 1942 Forest Service description of the station, quoted on the Central US Lookouts page from a National Geodetic Survey record, places it in District 8 of the Sam Houston National Forest, about 4.6 miles west of New Waverly.[^1] It describes the tower as galvanized steel with an enclosed crow's nest, about 110 feet high.[^1]

The 1930 notice describes the protection work as a cooperative effort with the Texas Forest Service, and the 1942 description places the station inside Sam Houston National Forest.[^1] The 97-foot height from 1930 and the 110-foot height from 1942 disagree, and the sources do not say which is right.[^1]

According to Pastmaps, a 1959 USGS topographic map of the area, published in 1960, shows the Bath Lookout and the Moores Grove Lookout alongside other fire towers.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks the Moores Grove tower as gone, at 30.54345 north and 95.55762 west.[^2]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2]
