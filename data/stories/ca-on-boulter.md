Boulter Fire Tower is a heavy steel tower at an elevation of 421 m (1381 ft), recorded in unorganized South Nipissing, Nipissing District, in northeastern Ontario.[^1] The sources do not agree on the district. The Canada Lookouts index places the tower in Parry Sound District, and the fan list gives only the place name, Bonfield.[^1][^2][^3]

Most of Ontario's fire towers went up between the 1920s and the 1950s, under the Department of Lands and Forests, which became the Ministry of Natural Resources in 1972.[^4] According to the record, a 1963 Department of Lands and Forests tower detection map places a 30.5 m (100 ft) steel tower on the site.[^1] The record also notes a 1985 proposal to use the tower as a repeater station, with information credited to Dave Fluri of the MNR.[^1]

The record says the tower no longer appears on North Bay topographic map 31L, but it does not say whether the tower still stands.[^1] The fan list marks Boulter with an asterisk, which its key explains as a tower whose cupola has been removed and which is now used as a communications tower.[^3]

The fan list says the MNR no longer maintains these towers and asks people not to climb them.[^3] No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
