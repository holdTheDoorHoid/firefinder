The Slate Mountain lookout stood on a bare-topped summit in Trinity County, California, between Slate Creek and South Slate Creek, about eight miles east of Trinity Center.[^1]

The Dunsmuir News reported in September 1932 that a lookout station had been put up on the mountain. It was one of two new stations in the Shasta National Forest that summer, and summer workers were building roads to reach them.[^1] A November 19, 1932 item in The Searchlight reported that a new lookout house was finished there and gave the tower a height of 26 feet.[^1]

A 1953 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls the structure a wooden tower, 22 feet square at the base and about 45 feet high, painted white.[^1] A 1954 recovery note says its lower part is enclosed and forms a garage and storage room.[^1] The 26-foot and 45-foot figures do not agree, and we found no source that explains the gap.[^1]

The 1954 note calls the tower property of the California State Forest Service. The header of the Forest Lookouts page names the Trinity National Forest, while the September 1932 item names the Shasta National Forest.[^1]

The Dunsmuir News also named people working on the mountain in the 1940s. In July 1944, Harry Ellison, who came from Weed, was in his second year as the lookout, and he was accompanied by his dog, Chang.[^1] A June 1945 item mentions Frank Silva, of Mount Shasta, as "doing his best" on Slate Mountain, but it does not say what his job was.[^1]

The Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the Slate Mountain entry, listed for Shasta and Trinity counties, as gone.[^2] We found no source that says when or how the tower went.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, is left at the site, or whether visitors may reach it.[^1][^2] An OpenStreetMap point named Slate Mountain Lookout, with a fire_lookout tag, sits at almost the same coordinates as the list entry, and its edit note reports work on the roads up the mountain.[^2][^3] A map point does not show what stands on the ground or whether those roads are open to the public.[^3]

A different Slate Mountain lookout, in El Dorado County, is on the Former Fire Lookout Sites register. It is a separate site, and nothing in this story applies to it.[^4]
