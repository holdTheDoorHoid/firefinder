Musquacook Mountain, in Aroostook County, is listed by the Forest Fire Lookout Association as a tower that is gone.[^1]

Maine's fire lookouts trace back to the 1903 fires, which burned more than 200,000 acres.[^2] Within two years, landowners with timber tracts had raised observation towers on their own land, and by 1909 the state had taken over those privately financed towers.[^2] Maine's towers were linked by telephone, and each was staffed by a single observer who watched for smoke and fire.[^2]

From 1950 the towers saw less use as better detection methods, aircraft in particular, took over the job.[^3] Maine kept its lookouts going longer than most states, dropping the network in 1991 after a budget crisis.[^2]

No source I found describes what remains on Musquacook Mountain today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
