Hungry Creek Peak sits at about 1,445 metres in the Klamath National Forest of Siskiyou County, roughly 650 yards from the Oregon border.[^1] The register calls it the farthest north lookout site in California.[^1] A 1932 diary entry mentions an older Hungry Creek lookout, so an earlier lookout of that name came before the present cabin.[^2]

The register dates the present lookout to 1937.[^1] The design was a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin, raised on a 10-foot timber tower.[^1] The Klamath National Forest's chronological history, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says the land was deeded for the lookout site, with title accepted in February 1937, and that a Civilian Conservation Corps camp built a new lookout in 1938.[^2] The two accounts disagree on the year.

In July 1934, during a heavy lightning storm, a CCC enrollee from Hilt was sent up as an emergency lookout.[^2] Clara Williams, whose husband, Ranger John Williams, had died in 1940 while saving a young girl from drowning, took the post in June 1954 and served there for 17 years.[^2]

The register puts the removal before 1986.[^1] The foundation is still on the site, and a 2009 photo in the register shows it.[^1]

Today, the site sits on Klamath National Forest land.[^1] The sources read describe no route to it and say nothing about whether it is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
