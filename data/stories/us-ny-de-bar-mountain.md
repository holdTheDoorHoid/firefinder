Debar Mountain lies north-northeast of Paul Smiths in Franklin County, and it carried a fire lookout from 1912.[^3][^1] The Forest Lookouts history says the Conservation Commission established a lookout there that year.[^1] Wikipedia says the first structure was a wood tower.[^3] In 1918 a 35-foot steel tower was bought and erected, which Wikipedia identifies as an Aermotor LS40.[^1][^3]

A new observer's cabin went up in 1922. That was the year the state adopted a standard 12-by-16-foot cabin plan, and DeBar was one of five stations that received new cabins under it.[^1][^2]

In July 1928 a snowstorm on the summit was watched by State Fire Observer Forest E. Winters and four climbers, according to a Syracuse Herald item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page.[^1]

The state shut the lookout in 1970 and took the tower down in 1979.[^4] Wikipedia says the lookout operations ended at the close of the 1970 season, as aerial detection grew.[^3]

Today the mountain is the tallest spot in the 122,100-acre Debar Mountain Wild Forest.[^4] A hike write-up in the Adirondack Explorer describes a state-maintained route to the former tower site, 7.4 miles out and back with 1,600 feet of climbing, and says hikers can expect at least a partial view from the site.[^4] No source I read says whether the tower's footings or the 1922 cabin survive on the summit.[^1][^3][^4]
