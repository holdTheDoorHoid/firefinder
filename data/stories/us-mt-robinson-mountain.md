Robinson Mountain Lookout stands at 7,404 feet in the Kootenai National Forest, a few miles south of the Canadian border.[^2][^3] The association counts it among the few gable-roof cupola lookouts still standing. That design was once popular in Montana and northern Idaho.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says a few hip-roof cupola lookouts remain, and even fewer have gable roofs.[^1]

The cabin is a 12-by-14-foot building of shake and frame, completed in 1929.[^1][^3] One lookout guide also lists a camp at the site in 1921.[^2] The sources I could reach do not describe who staffed the lookout or which fires it watched. The register says the site was difficult to reach and often fogged in.[^1]

The cabin was abandoned in 1948.[^1] It came back in 1983: the register says it was restored as a Kootenai National Forest rental, while the association says the forest restored it to serviceable condition.[^1][^3] It was added to the register on May 12, 2000.[^1]

A recent condition assessment by volunteers, with help from Ksanka Ranger District staff, found the structure in good condition.[^3] It will still need work on specific structural problems, along with continued upkeep.[^3]

Current access is unclear.[^4] A campground listing run by a third party says the lookout is not on Recreation.gov, that it may be used without fees, and that reaching it takes a nearly two-mile hike after remote forest roads.[^4] Road conditions at that elevation change with the seasons.[^4] Check with the Forest Service before you go.
