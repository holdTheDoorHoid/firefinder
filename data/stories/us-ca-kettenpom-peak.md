The Kettenpom Peak lookout stood in Trinity County, within the Six Rivers National Forest.[^1] In December 1935 the Forest Service was hauling material to build it, and the Blue Lake Advocate reported a 14 x 14 frame building raised on steel, 20 feet above the ground.[^2] The register dates the lookout to 1936 and describes a cabin built to California Region 5 Plan BC-301, set on an open, 17-foot timber tower.[^1]

The sources do not agree on the tower itself. A 1942 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description portrays it as a white timber structure on eight wooden legs, 30 feet tall.[^2] The register's 17-foot figure and the 1935 steel frame point elsewhere, so the height is unsettled.[^1][^2]

The register listed the lookout on October 29, 2019, before the fire.[^1] The lookout was destroyed in the August Complex fires of 2020.[^3][^4] A Forest Fire Lookout Association chapter report says the Six Rivers tower was destroyed during those fires.[^3] A peakbagging page adds that the complex burned over one million acres from August to November 2020.[^4]

A peakbagging page says the lookout is reached by road and trail.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site now, or whether visitors can reach it. Check with the Six Rivers National Forest before planning a trip.
