In 1937 a schoolboy named Austin Post built a 10-foot wooden tower with a 6-by-6-foot cab on a rounded granite knob on the north shore of Lake Chelan, west of town. He called the spot Sage Brush Point.[^3]

Post's own account, a segment from *My Forest Service Days*, says the work was done mostly after school, with his friend Greg Hastings. The materials were driftwood from the beach, old flume boards and a neighbor's prop pile.[^3] A windstorm knocked most of the tower down soon after it was finished.[^3] Post and Hastings rebuilt it, and Greg's father, Francis Hastings, located four real posts and delivered them to the site.[^3]

Sim Beeson, the U.S. Forest Service assistant ranger, spotted the tower, then took the two boys to the Forest Service office and gave them a firefinder, maps, a flag and highway signs.[^3] Post thought the visit came in the summer of 1937 or 1938, and Beeson wrote it up for the Chelan Valley Mirror.[^3] Post had no Forest Service job when he built the tower.[^3] Willhite doubts the Forest Service ever used it for official work.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com both date the tower to 1937 and say it was gone by 1952.[^1][^2] The register lists the site as privately owned.[^1]

According to Willhite, the ground has been orchard land for years.[^3] We found no source that describes what remains there or whether the public can visit. The land is private, so ask the owner before going near it.[^1]
