The Fly Gap lookout was a U.S. Forest Service tower on Ozark National Forest land in Franklin County, Arkansas.[^1] When the National Geodetic Survey described it in 1971, it had a four-legged steel frame with a cabin on top, painted orange, and the survey put its height at about 75 feet.[^1] The survey placed its station at the center of the tower's base, about 18 miles north of Ozark and 1.25 miles east of State Highway 23.[^1]

The survey's directions ran north from Ozark on State Highway 23, past the Forest Service's Cass work center. From there the route followed a graded road east, then a side road with a sign for the tower, and finally a bladed road south for 0.15 mile to the station.[^1] They are the only directions in this record, and they date from 1971.[^1]

The 1971 survey is the newest description this record has found.[^1] It does not say whether the tower still stands, what roads lead to it now, or whether the public may go there.[^1] Anyone planning a trip should ask the Ozark National Forest about access first, and should not climb a tower unless the agency says it is open.

[^1]: Fly Gap, Forest Lookouts (reproducing the National Geodetic Survey station description for PID FG1930), https://centraluslookouts.weebly.com/fly-gap.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
