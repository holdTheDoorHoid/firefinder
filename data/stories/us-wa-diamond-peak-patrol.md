The Diamond Peak summit lookout was a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1934 and used into the late 1950s.[^1][^2] It was destroyed about 1960.[^1][^2] A patrol platform tower stood about three-quarters of a mile from the summit, past the 4,413 point, to look into a blind spot the summit could not see.[^1][^2] Willhite has a photograph of the patrol tower from 1982.[^1] The patrol tower is listed under the Kaniksu National Forest.[^3]

The two sources disagree about the patrol tower's fate. TrailChick reports that the Forest Fire Lookout Association confirmed it was lost either before or during the Diamond Watch fire in 2022.[^2] Willhite's page has no date and says the tower still stands but is expected to fall or burn soon.[^1] The story follows the association's account, which is the more specific of the two.

TrailChick's 2018 visit found footings and a helipad at the old summit site, about half a mile from the patrol tower.[^2] Willhite's undated photo captions mention a patrol tower footing and a patrol point in poor condition, but no source I read describes the site today.[^1] The walk is 4.2 miles round trip from a gated road just southeast of Petit Lake.[^2] It involves moderate bushwhacking and a faint trail across the ridge, and it passes the old summit site on the way.[^2]
