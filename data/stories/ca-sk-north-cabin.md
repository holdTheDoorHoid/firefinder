The North Cabin Fire Tower is a steel pivot-base mast, 24.4 m (80 ft) tall, standing in the Nisbet Provincial Forest in Saskatchewan.[^1] The register gives its elevation as 520 m (1,706 ft) and says it was built in 1934.[^1] The Saskatchewan Public Safety Agency administers it.[^1]

A plywood cupola painted white and red sat on top of the tower. A small cabin at the foot of the tower gave the observer somewhere to live while on duty.[^1] A new cupola, slightly larger than the first, went on in 1956.[^1][^2] A Department of Natural Resources annual report for 1963-64, as quoted by Canada Lookouts, says the tower was completely overhauled in 1963.[^3]

In 1989 the North Cabin Fire reached the site. The blaze destroyed the cabin and cupola and left the steel tower twisted.[^1][^2] The register says the site was rebuilt over the summer of 1990.[^1]

By 2013 the province had moved to camera monitoring for fire detection.[^1]

Today the register says the tower is still standing, fenced off and well maintained. The cabin is no longer on site.[^1] For the site as it is now, the register is the only source I found.[^1]
