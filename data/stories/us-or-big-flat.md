The Big Flat lookout stood about six miles south of Wallowa, in Wallowa County, Oregon, at 5,500 feet.[^1][^3] In 1973 the Oregon Department of Forestry set up a 30-foot steel tower and trailer there to replace the Tope Creek lookout.[^1][^2][^3] A Wallowa County newspaper called the tower a cooperative state and federal Forest Service project, so the sources are not fully clear on who ran it.[^3]

On the night of October 6, 1973, a windstorm blew the unfinished tower over.[^3] It still had no fire-fighting equipment or windows, and its guy wires had not been tied on yet.[^3] The newspaper put the loss at about $4,000 in materials and labor.[^3] The state's 1973 forestry annual report said the tower had been rebuilt at its Wallowa headquarters and would be hauled back to the site.[^3] The same paper quoted a state forestry spokesman as saying no decision had been reached on rebuilding it at the same site.[^3] The tower was back in service for the 1974 fire season.[^1][^3]

Afterward the tower went to the Wallowa Fire Department, where it served as a hose tower.[^1][^2] The lookout was no longer standing by 1990.[^1][^2]

The sources we read do not describe what, if anything, remains at the original site. None says whether the old tower still stands at the Wallowa Fire Department, and none says whether the public can reach the site.[^1][^2][^3]
