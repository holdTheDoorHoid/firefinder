El Morro Lookout takes its name from the sandstone bluff a few miles away that has drawn travelers for centuries. That landmark sits "on an ancient east–west trail," where a pool of water at the base of the rock made it a waypoint long before it became El Morro National Monument, the inscriptions left there by passing explorers now protected by the National Park Service.[^1] The lookout tower itself is far less documented than its famous namesake. The only confirmed record of it found in this research is a single line in the Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico inventory, which lists it simply as "ElMorro Valencia 35.04229 -108.31338 Tower Standing" — a tower-type structure, still standing, with no county or township details beyond that.[^2] It carries no listing in the National Historic Lookout Register or the Forest Fire Lookout Site register, so no builder, construction year, design, or staffing history has surfaced. The county given, Valencia, may also be out of date: its coordinates sit only a few miles from El Morro National Monument, which today is in Cibola County, not Valencia.[^1] The surrounding country is part of the Cibola National Forest, whose Mount Taylor Ranger District "manages land in northern Cibola, southern McKinley, and western Sandoval counties," though no source ties the lookout to a specific ranger station, crew, or year of construction.[^3] Whether it is presently staffed, maintained, or open to visitors is undocumented; anyone wanting to see it should check with the Cibola National Forest rather than assume it can be climbed.
