Rhoda Point is a lookout site in Idaho County, Idaho.[^1] A county lookout list places it in the Nez Perce National Forest.[^1]

Its early history can be read two ways. The Idaho Fire Lookouts page for End Butte says that in 1922 a platform tower and a log cabin stood at Rhoda Point, about two miles southeast of the End Butte camp.[^2] A 1981 history, quoted on the county list, gives 1937 as the year the point was first manned.[^1] That season, William Pointer and a crew of two strung a telephone line from End Butte to the point.[^1] On a hilltop strewn with boulders, the crew raised a 20-foot observation tower, and not far away they built a small log cabin with a roof of split cedar shakes.[^1]

The cabin was roughly 8 by 10 feet. Its main job was storing camp gear over the off-season, though the lookout also slept in it.[^1] Cooking happened outside, under a tent and fly.[^1]

Ranger Millard Evenson oversaw the district that year, and Daland Broker staffed the point in the 1938 and 1939 seasons.[^1] Staffing ran through the 1941 season; after that the point was taken off the fire plan.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Idaho list gives Rhoda Point as a platform and marks it gone.[^3] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the point today, or whether visitors can reach it.
