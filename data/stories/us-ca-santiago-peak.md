Santiago Peak, the highest point in the Santa Ana Mountains and in Orange County, California, had a fire lookout by 1912.[^1][^2][^4] Newspaper items quoted by CaliforniaLookouts.weebly.com describe a plan to protect the mountains better against fire, a small cabin on the peak, and a telephone line to a fire warden's house in the Trabuco.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the first tower to 1913, while FFLA-SDRC gives 1914.[^2][^3]

The first structure was a two-level, ground-mounted building with a peaked roof, according to the register.[^2] A 1930 newspaper item already refers to a modern 75-foot tower on the peak.[^1] FFLA-SDRC says the Civilian Conservation Corps removed the original tower in 1935. That date does not sit easily with the 1930 reference, and the sources do not say which structure the CCC took down.[^1][^3]

Lookouts appear by name in the early newspaper items. Eugene Davis was the lookout in July 1912. A January 1913 item says Andrew Joplin was lookout man on the peak during the previous fire season.[^1] A 1929 item credits the Santiago Peak lookout with helping keep three lightning fires small.[^1]

In the late 1940s the tower was lowered to 20 feet.[^2] In 1951 it was replaced by a 35-foot steel tower with a cab.[^2][^3] The two sources disagree on the cab: the register gives 13 by 13 feet, and FFLA-SDRC gives 10 feet.[^2][^3] The Forest Service stopped using the lookout in the late 1970s or early 1980s.[^2] In 1964 AT&T built a telephone relay building on the site, and a tower and cab were added to its roof. AT&T removed that cab by 1988. The tower and catwalk are still standing on the relay building.[^2]

Today the peak is a communications site. The register says the peak is the busiest communications site in the country.[^2] FFLA-SDRC says the remaining structure is surrounded by communications facilities, and Wikipedia says an antenna farm sits on top of the peak.[^3][^4] The sources we read do not describe public access, so we list the site as restricted. We do not recommend climbing the relay building or the tower, since they are part of an active facility. The register lists the site as registered on February 17, 2009.[^2]
