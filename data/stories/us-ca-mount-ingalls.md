Mount Ingalls, at 8,372 feet, is the high point of Plumas County.[^1] A lookout station was on the summit by July 1922, when the Plumas National-Bulletin reported that Ed Merry was working there near the Walker Mine.[^2]

The tower the register dates to 1936 was a 10-foot enclosed timber tower built to a California Region 5 plan.[^1] The Quincy marker credits Company 989 of the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it in 1935, so the two sources differ by a year.[^3][^1]

Merry resigned in August 1930, and Clark Hart took the post in June 1936.[^2] A 1943 item names Mrs. Harry Kelley as stationed at the lookout, and by 1946 Lois Olds, an artist, had spent three years there.[^2] In 1960 Mount Ingalls was one of three Northern California lookouts chosen for a time-lapse camera, part of a study with the State Forestry Division and the Weather Bureau.[^2]

In September 1962 a high-velocity bullet broke apart on a post of the catwalk and struck lookout Jim Bailey in six places. He stayed at his post until a replacement could be sent.[^2]

In September 1976 the station was closed down. The Portola Reporter said it would not reopen the next year except in emergencies, such as when lightning was likely.[^2] In 1984 the structure was taken apart and moved to the Plumas County Fairgrounds in Quincy.[^1][^2] The marker says a Navy Reserve construction battalion, the "Sea Bees," dismantled and rebuilt it there.[^3]

The register calls the Quincy structure a fire-detection interpretive site, and the marker stands on the fairgrounds grounds, reached from Fairgrounds Road south of Lee Road.[^1][^3] The sources read give no current hours for the display.

At the summit, a hiking guide says that all that is left of the lookout today is the foundation.[^4] The same guide says the tower was removed intact and placed in a museum in Portola, which conflicts with the Quincy record.[^4][^1] Hikers reach the trailhead by logging roads that begin near Lake Davis, and the round trip from the four-way intersection where most park is a little over two miles.[^4]
