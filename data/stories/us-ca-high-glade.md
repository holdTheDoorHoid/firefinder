High Glade Lookout stands near the southern boundary of the Mendocino National Forest in Lake County, California, on High Glade Road.[^3] The Register gives its elevation as 4,841 feet and says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1935 from Forest Service plans.[^1]

The sources disagree on the size of the cab. The Register describes a standard 14-by-14-foot cab.[^1] The Forest Service describes the rebuilt cabin as 10 by 10 feet on a 35-foot steel tower, essentially the same size and shape as the previous structure.[^3][^4]

A December 1940 item in the California Ranger, quoted on a California lookout history page, reports that lookout-fireman Delmar O. Tilley, stationed at High Glade, had volunteered and been accepted for military service.[^2] Before the Ranch Fire, the lookout was staffed seasonally through the summer and fall.[^3]

In August 1996 a wildfire overran the station. The Ukiah Daily Journal reported that the station had been evacuated before the fire arrived.[^2]

The Register accepted the lookout on March 20, 2016 as US 1123, CA 109, nominated by Bill Ulmer.[^1]

In July 2018 the Ranch Fire tore through the southern half of the forest.[^4] The Register says the cab was destroyed about July 31, 2018, and that the steel tower remains at the site.[^1] A 2026 Forest Service feature instead says the tower burned to the ground.[^4] The sources do not agree on whether the steel tower survived.

The design and rebuild were funded in 2022 with disaster supplemental money.[^3] The Forest Service announced on October 11, 2024 that contractors were beginning reconstruction, and that the construction site would be closed to the public.[^3] The Forest Service says the lookout was rebuilt in 2025.[^4]

The new building has power and running water. A heavy-duty cable runs from the top of the tower to the ground to protect the building from lightning strikes.[^4] In 2026 a long-time lookout who had served at High Glade before began a seasonal shift there with a newcomer to the work.[^4]

The 2024 release asked visitors to avoid High Glade Road during construction.[^3] The sources read do not state current public visiting rules. Because the lookout is staffed for fire detection, check with the Mendocino National Forest before planning a trip.
