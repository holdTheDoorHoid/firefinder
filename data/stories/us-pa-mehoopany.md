The Mehoopany tower went up in 1923.[^1][^2] The Eastern US lookouts history describes it as a 60-foot (18 m) steel tower with a 7 by 7 foot cab.[^1] It stood in Forkston Township, Wyoming County.[^2]

In July 1946 the Lackawanna District was checking its fire towers before the fall fire season, and Mehoopany was on the list for inspection the following week.[^1]

Charlie Womer watched for fires from the tower for 13 years.[^2] His last season was 2016.[^2] A March 2017 newspaper report said the tower was staffed during the fire season and was scheduled to be replaced.[^2] Its entrance was a trapdoor, and the planned replacement towers would have a catwalk and a regular door instead.[^2]

In September 2017 the state Department of Conservation and Natural Resources (DCNR) began a $4.6 million project to replace 16 lookout towers on state forest land.[^3] A PA Environment Digest report on the project lists Mehoopany, in Pinchot State Forest, among the towers targeted for replacement.[^3]

We found no source that says whether the replacement has been built, whether the 1923 tower has come down, or whether the site is open to visitors. Check with DCNR before you go to the site.
