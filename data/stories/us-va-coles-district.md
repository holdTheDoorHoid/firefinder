The Civilian Conservation Corps camp known as Camp Recovery, officially Camp P-71, stood at Canova in Prince William County.[^2] Its first enrollees arrived on 15 October 1933.[^3]

The camp's program built two 100-foot steel towers, one here and one in Fairfax County.[^2] The register dates the Prince William County tower to 1934.[^1] Equipment on each tower included a glass-enclosed platform, phone lines, field glasses, and gear for placing a fire on a map.[^2]

A local recollection holds that the Ellicott brothers manned the tower.[^2] The register says local fire departments later used the tower, which was taken apart and sold in 1988.[^1]

The reliquary says 9-1-1 phone service made the tower unnecessary.[^2] It dates the dismantling to 1988 and says that, as of its January 2008 issue, the tower was in South Carolina.[^2]

None of the camp's buildings are left, the marker says.[^3] The Coles District Volunteer Fire and Rescue station, at 13712 Dumfries Road, sits just north of where the tower stood.[^2] A county marker from 2014 stands on Dumfries Road, about a tenth of a mile north of Hoadly Road.[^3] No tower remains on the site, and no source describes public access to the station grounds.[^1][^2]
