The Los Angeles County Forestry Department built a lookout on Blue Ridge, in the San Gabriel Mountains, in 1928.[^1][^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 60-foot open X-brace steel tower with an 8-by-8 observation cabin.[^1] A stone residence cabin was also built on the site.[^1] The register says the lookout "may have been cooperatively constructed and staffed" by the county fire department and the Angeles National Forest, as many county lookouts were.[^1]

Two items the register quotes show the station at work. A June 1932 newspaper item says the Blue Ridge station spotted a fire in the Lake Arrowhead area.[^1] A 1933 item says the forest used Blue Ridge to test a new radio system, "using the tower or the stone cabin at ground level."[^1] That wording is hard to square with the 1930 move described below, and these sources do not settle it.[^1][^4]

In 1930 the county took the tower down and moved it to Bodle Peak in the Malibu Hills.[^3][^4] A county forester, quoted in a 1932 publication, said the move was meant to protect a mountain area that was highly hazardous and dotted with summer homes.[^3] The register pages differ on timing. The Blue Ridge entry says the tower was rebuilt on Bodle Peak "in 1930 or later," while the Bodle Peak entry says it was re-assembled there during 1932.[^1][^4] Angeles Adventures gives the moved tower a height of 50 feet, against the register's 60 feet for the original.[^3][^1]

The Bodle Peak entry adds that the tower was dismantled again and re-assembled on Triunfo Peak sometime after 1934.[^4]

Today the register, which lists the site as registered on November 2, 2019, describes the stone residence cabin as remaining and used to store communications equipment.[^1] No source we read describes a tower standing on the site today, or whether the public may visit the site. Check with the Angeles National Forest before going there.
