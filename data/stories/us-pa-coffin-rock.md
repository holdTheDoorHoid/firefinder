Coffin Rock sits in the remote woods of Noyes Township, Clinton County, and the name comes from a big rock nearby that looks roughly like a coffin.[^1] A 60-foot steel tower went up there in 1921.[^1][^2] At its base was a cabin where a local forestry man lived and kept watch over the woods around the clock, and the Express named J.M. Hall of Renovo as the man picked for that job.[^1]

A 1926 Express piece repeated the claim that the view was among the finest in the state.[^1] In 1939 the tower was raised from 60 to 86 feet, which improved the Department of Forestry's outlook.[^1][^2] The sources do not fully agree on heights. A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1958 puts the tower at about 80 feet overall.[^2] I did not find a record of any particular fire tied to this tower in the sources I checked.

A tornado destroyed that tower in 1985.[^1][^2][^3] Two years later a new one went up, a 93-foot Aermotor with an unusual full catwalk around its 7-foot-square cab. The Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry built it.[^1][^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it the newest lookout in Pennsylvania and registered it in September 2013.[^3] A separate register, the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register, lists the 1921 tower's site as no longer standing and registered it in September 2008.[^4]

An Express article from April 2024 still describes a fire tower on the rock, which suggests the 1987 tower is standing.[^1] Shoemaker Ridge Road lies just south of the rock, and Route 144 lies to the west.[^1] I did not find a current statement on whether the public may climb the tower or drive up to it, so check with the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry before you go.
