Riansares Mountain, in Clinton County's Bald Eagle State Forest, got a steel fire tower in 1921.[^1][^3] A December 1921 paper called it the new steel fire tower and said a road to the summit had just been completed.[^2] Two springs were nearby, and the forestry department had built a cabin near the tower.[^2] A state forest ranger, Russel E. Berry of Loganton, was in charge.[^2]

A 1936 paper gave the tower as 60 feet tall and noted that a CCC camp was nearby.[^2] The name is spelled differently across sources: the 1921 paper writes Rainares, and the 1936 paper writes Mount Riansares.[^2]

Staffing is documented in the late 1930s.[^2] In 1937 Harry E. Lamey was watchman at the tower.[^2] In 1938 he was hired as towerman, a job meant to last until the threat of fire passed.[^2]

The register and a photo caption both date the tower's removal to the 1990s.[^1][^4] The caption adds that the cabin is still in use.[^4]

A 2023 volunteer cleanup notice says the summit is reached by road: drivers are told to go to the top of the mountain and park near a communications tower.[^3] The same notice mentions a former fire tower house with a 911 address on Riansares Road.[^3] The sources do not say outright that this is the same building as the cabin in the photo caption.[^3][^4] The Eastern lookouts page files the site under Bald Eagle State Park, while the 2023 notice and the caption both place it in Bald Eagle State Forest.[^2][^3][^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains of the tower footings.
