Polallie Mountain sits in the Wenatchee National Forest in Kittitas County, Washington, at 5,482 feet, just inside the Alpine Lakes Wilderness.[^1][^4]

A log cabin with an open-air cupola on its roof stood on the ridge by about 1921.[^1][^2][^4] A May 1930 item in the Wenatchee Daily World, quoted on a lookout history site, said a lookout cabin would be built on Polallie Ridge that year.[^3] The register says a 25-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced the cabin in 1936.[^1][^2] Willhite dates the tower to 1937.[^4]

Firelookout.com lists Clifford Stipp as lookout personnel in 1933, the only name it gives.[^2]

Sources disagree on how the lookout ended. According to the register, it was abandoned in 1947 and no longer stands.[^1] Willhite says the inventory points to 1947 as the year the tower was abandoned, yet his images show the lookout still there well beyond that year, and a 1964 hiking guide mentions it.[^4] Firelookout.com says it was abandoned in 1947 and burned in 1965.[^2]

The register says the lookout is gone.[^1] Willhite's photos show a footing with relics collected around it, plus an old cabin foundation.[^4] Pavement covers the road to the trailhead except its final mile, which is good gravel.[^4] From there, Willhite starts up the Pete Lake Trail, turns onto the Tired Creek Trail, and climbs 2.3 miles to a ridgetop, where the Polallie Ridge Trail runs to the site 0.2 miles farther on.[^4] The site is reached on foot.[^4]
