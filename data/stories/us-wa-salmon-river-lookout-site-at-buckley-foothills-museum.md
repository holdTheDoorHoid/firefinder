The Salmon River lookout began in 1965 as a platform and a trailer on a ridge just inside Quinault tribal land, near Lake Quinault in the southern Olympics.[^1][^2] In 1967 a two-story live-in cab for the Washington Department of Natural Resources replaced that setup.[^3][^1] A state biennial report, as quoted on a Washington lookout history site, dates a tower erected between 1966 and 1968, so the years differ a little between accounts.[^2]

Two newspaper items describe the lookout's role. A 1965 article anticipated seven lookouts being manned in June of that year, and a 1969 item said three forest lookouts were to be staffed to back up aerial detection, with Salmon River Ridge as one station.[^1][^2] Both are announcements of plans, so they do not tell us how long the lookout was staffed.[^2][^1]

The cab went to the Buckley Museum in 2000.[^3][^2][^1] A 2021 blog post says the Quinault Nation donated the structure to the Foothills Historical Society in 2000, and that it was later dismantled and hauled by pickup and flatbed trailer to Van Hoof Park.[^4] Volunteers and many locals then rebuilt it on a log tower, using wooden steps where the original had cinderblock.[^4] The former ridge site has a large amount of rubble from buildings on its north side, though a visitor did not think it came from a lookout.[^1]

Today the lookout stands in Buckley, across from the main museum building on North River Road.[^1][^4] The blog says it has been restored and is kept up for education.[^4] When the author visited on October 31, 2021, the cab was shut, and the author thought it opens during events and in summer.[^4] Ask the museum about its hours and whether the cab is open before you go.
