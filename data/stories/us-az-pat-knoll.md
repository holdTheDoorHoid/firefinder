Pat Knoll is a summit in Apache County, Arizona, inside the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest.[^1][^2] Sources give its elevation as 9,484 feet (TopoQuest) and 9,379 feet (Dangerous Roads), so we have not picked one.[^2][^3]

The earliest account of a lookout here that we found is from May 1917.[^1] Staff in Springerville, at the forest supervisor's office, saw a large plume of smoke. The Pat Knoll station, along with two other lookouts, phoned the report in.[^1] The fire was close to the station, so the lookout, Ellis Wiltbank, was sent at once.[^1] Forest rangers arrived about two hours later. Livestock men had already begun fighting it. The fire was brought under control after it burned 100 acres.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a crow's nest at the site as gone.[^4] The sources we read do not describe what, if anything, remains at the site today.

Forest Road 90 runs to the summit from the paved AZ-261. The route is about five miles long, and the road is unpaved.[^3] A road guide says the route shuts down for winter, when heavy snow makes it impassable. It advises checking for gate closures before a trip.[^3]
