Boyer Mountain is a summit in Pend Oreille County, about 18 miles west of Newport, on the Kaniksu and Colville national forests.[^1][^2] The register gives its elevation as 5,252 feet, while WillhiteWeb gives 5,280.[^1][^4]

The register says the summit was developed in 1925 with a log cabin and a platform observation tower on the south end.[^1] A cupola cabin went up on the north end in 1929.[^1][^4] A Division of Forestry report quoted by the Forest Lookouts page says Boyer Mountain was one of three new lookouts put into operation in 1929.[^3] The sources disagree on when the lookout began, so we give both dates.

In 1937 a 72-foot treated timber tower with an L-6 cab and frame living quarters went up on the south summit.[^1][^4][^3]

A June 1930 Newport Miner item said Ernest Hart was to be stationed at Boyer.[^3] WillhiteWeb also names him as the lookout in 1930.[^4] Earle Gray was the lookout in 1957 and 1958.[^2][^4] In July 1942 the Newport Miner reported that the eight lookout stations in the Newport district were being manned that week, with Boyer Mountain first.[^3] The tower received two-way radio equipment in 1938.[^3][^4]

In September 1951 a Statesman Examiner item said state officials called in one lookout on Boyer Mountain while keeping five others running.[^3] In 1960 the Spokane Daily Chronicle reported that Boyer would not be manned that year, and its money would go to plane patrols after lightning storms.[^3][^4] We found no fire reports from the lookout in the sources we read.

Vandals toppled the tower in 1975, and it was never replaced.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Washington list shows the site as a tower that is gone.[^5]

WillhiteWeb's photo captions show footings marked 2026 at the south summit, and cab remnants about 80 feet from those footings.[^4] The sources do not say whether anything else remains.

Access is restricted. Most roads in the area are gated, and the route WillhiteWeb describes ends at an old gate with a large tank trap just beyond.[^4] A wildlife closure area permanently ended the road.[^4] The route runs up Middle Fork Road for about a mile and then Power Lake Road for roughly eight miles, and a trail covers the last part.[^4] The page says the route was still usable as of 2026, though the final quarter mile of trail is overgrowing quickly.[^4] We found no current closure notice or permit rule in the sources, so check with the land managers before going.
