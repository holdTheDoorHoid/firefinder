Granite Mountain is in the Salmon-Challis National Forest, in Lemhi County, Idaho.[^1] Its history starts before the cabin. By 1923 a lookout man was working there from a tent with an alidade, a sighting tool.[^2] The Idaho Heritage Trust says the site was the one place the smoke chaser went without horses, because the steep ground offered little water or grass.[^1] A 1924 forest report says no horses were kept at the station.[^2]

The cab is an L-4 design, which the National Historic Lookout Register calls a classic live-in lookout.[^3] Most sources date it to 1934.[^3][^4][^5][^2] The Idaho Heritage Trust instead gives 1929 and a standard R4 Plan 80.[^1] I have used 1934 because four sources agree on it, but the date is not fully settled.

A local history page quotes old newspaper notices about who worked the station.[^2] In 1935 a notice says a lookout's wife stayed a few days with her husband, who was stationed there.[^2] In the summer of 1939 a Mr. Allen was stationed there.[^2] In 1943 two women, Chloe and Joy Bradley, ran the station and its fire weather instruments.[^2] In 1959 the forest held its summer fire fighting school at the lookout.[^2]

NHLR says the lookout was active for 30 years and went on standby in the 1960s.[^3] Firelookout.com dates its last emergency use to 1960.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts describes emergency service in the 1960s.[^5] Restoration began in June 2002, with a new wood roof, new shingles and removal of exterior lead-based paint.[^2] NHLR says the work was done with help from the North Fork Volunteer Fire Department, Passport In Time and the Forest Heritage staff.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 2002 in its timeline but calls the restoration a 2003 project.[^5]

The National Historic Lookout Register records the lookout as registered on September 18, 2003.[^3] Firelookout.com says the restored cabin was planned for the Recreation Rental program.[^4] I could not confirm that it is rentable now. The forest's camping page lists one lookout among its overnight rentals but does not name it.[^6] Check with the Salmon-Challis National Forest before planning a trip, since the sources do not say how the cabin is reached.
