Thrapp Mountain, in Okanogan County, carried a 40-foot tower with an L-4 cab on its summit.[^1][^2] The tower went up in 1933, and the sources date its end to 1958.[^1][^3]

The earliest name is Ted Healam, listed as the lookout in 1932, a year before the tower was built.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb also records panorama photographs taken from the site in 1934.[^3]

The sources disagree on how the tower ended. FFLOS says it was removed in 1958, while Firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com adds that the ruins on the summit later burned in a forest fire in the 2010s.[^2]

A blog post dated April 25, 2021 describes a visit at which only four concrete footings were left, two of them covered by fallen timber.[^4] The author says lookout artifacts stayed on the summit until fires in 2014, and that the 2014 Carlton Complex Fire burned the forest from Finley Canyon to Thrapp.[^4] The sources do not agree on what survived.

Today the route described runs on NF-41 and Forest Road 250. The author drove about 6.5 miles up NF-41 from near the Loup to the Forest Road 250 junction, then walked about two miles east and south, with a short bushwhack near the top.[^4] WillhiteWeb says Forest Road 41 is paved for about 20 miles.[^3] We found no source saying whether the land is open to visitors. FFLOS lists the administrator as Washington State Schools.[^1]
