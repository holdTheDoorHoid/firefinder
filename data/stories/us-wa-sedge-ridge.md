Sedge Ridge, in Yakima County, was one of three new eastern Washington lookouts that the state Division of Forestry put into operation in 1929.[^3] The two-story building, which the register nicknames a "smokehouse," was built that year.[^1][^2][^4]

One lookout, Tom Bigley, recalled that the firefinder and bunk were upstairs, with the cookstove and storage below.[^4] The view took in the city of Yakima to the east, and Mount Adams and the Cascades to the north and west.[^4]

In August 1958 the Mt. Adams Sun profiled a young woman who was working the lookout that summer.[^3][^4] The paper said the lookout was perched on a rocky point above the Ahtanum Guard Station.[^3][^4]

The lookout was given up in 1961, and the register lists it as gone.[^1][^4] The firelookout.com page says it has since been removed.[^2]

A Willhite page, with photos from July 2019, says there is a small communications building on the summit.[^4] Willhite calls the site a drive-up, and says most cars should be fine if taken slowly.[^4] Timber harvests can change the roads at any time, the page warns.[^4]
