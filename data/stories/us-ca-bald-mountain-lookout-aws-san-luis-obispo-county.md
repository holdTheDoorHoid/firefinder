Bald Mountain in San Luis Obispo County held a wartime post rather than a conventional fire tower.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a standard Aircraft Warning Service post, with a ground cabin and a cupola, put up in 1942 or 1943.[^1] The register says the site had two purposes: spotting enemy aircraft and detecting fires.[^1]

The Forest Service built, supplied and staffed the post, and the Army paid the agency back for all of its costs.[^1] Operators were to report aircraft activity, and every fire or smoke, to Forest Service dispatch, and the aircraft information was then passed on to the Army.[^1] The Aircraft Warning Service that the post belonged to was deactivated on May 29, 1944.[^2]

The register gives the removal date as unknown. It recorded the site on April 23, 2020, on the nomination of Brad Eells, as US 2043, CA 149.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the site as gone.[^3]

We found no source that describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
