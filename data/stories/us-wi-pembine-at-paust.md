The Governor Thompson Fire Lookout Tower, also called the Pembine/Paust Tower, began near Pembine in Marinette County, Wisconsin.[^1][^2] The state's Wisconsin Historical Society record says it was built at its original location in November 1933.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1934 and credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building the 100-foot Aermotor tower for the state.[^1] The two sources disagree by a year, and only the Register names the CCC.[^1][^2]

The Register's 100-foot height is the only height in these sources.[^1] The Historical Society record lists the wall material as metal and names the International Stacey Company in its architect field, a name the Register does not mention.[^2][^1]

The tower was an active lookout until the 1980s, according to the Historical Society and the Register.[^2][^1] Its use goes back further: a November 1936 newspaper item, quoted on a page about the Faithorn lookout in Michigan, reports that a telephone line was being strung between the Wisconsin tower near Pembine and the Michigan tower at Faithorn.[^3] The sources read do not name any lookout or any fire the tower watched.

In 1990 the state declared the tower surplus and sold it to Dale Paust.[^1][^2] Paust moved it to his Woods Lake resort, and the Historical Society dates the move to 1991.[^1][^2] The state later bought the resort and made it a state park in 2000, according to the Register and Wikipedia.[^1][^4] Wikipedia says the park's main parcel was the former Paust Woods Lake Resort and that the park opened in 2005.[^4]

The Register lists the tower's location as Governor Thompson State Park and records its registration as US 107, WI 2 on September 9, 2003.[^1] No source says whether the tower itself is open to visitors or can be climbed, so ask the park before planning a trip to it.
