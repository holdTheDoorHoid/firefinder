The Big Crow Lake fire tower stood in Algonquin Provincial Park.[^1] Firelookout.ca has two entries for it, one titled Big Crow Lake and one titled Freswick.[^1][^2] According to the park, it was the final fire tower still up in Algonquin.[^4]

The park says the tower, the fire ranger's cabin and a boathouse were all built in 1956.[^3][^4] The tower sat about 805 m (2,640 ft) from the cabin, up a steep slope.[^3] Its frame was metal and its top was a wooden cupola, a design the park also describes for its other steel fire towers.[^2][^5] The park says the tower was used for fire detection up to the mid-1970s, and that it was one of the last in Ontario to be used that way.[^3]

Firelookout.ca says lightning hit the wooden shelter at the top in May 2011, and that the fire burned it, leaving the metal tower standing.[^2] The park gives 2010 as the year the cupola was struck and burnt.[^4] One Firelookout.ca entry dates the burn to 2011 and says only the frame survived.[^1]

Today little survives besides the base and the metal frame, and charred wood lies at the foot of the tower.[^1][^2] Firelookout.ca says the site can be reached by hiking.[^1] Visitors need to reserve the fire ranger's cabin, the park says.[^3]

A separate Fire Tower Trail, a 200 m (660 ft) round-trip walk, sits at the entrance to the Algonquin Visitor Centre.[^5] It shows a replica cupola, not the original tower.[^5]
