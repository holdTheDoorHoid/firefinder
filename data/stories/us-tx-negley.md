The Negley lookout stands north of Clarksville, in Red River County, Texas.[^2][^1] In early 1937 the Texas Forestry Service was building a steel tower there, inside a fire protection zone it had set up in the forest belt north of town.[^1] Work was due to resume that week, and the excavation for the concrete foundation was already finished.[^1]

The first newspaper report said the finished tower would stand 100 feet high, with a glass-enclosed watch room at the top.[^1] A report twelve days later gave the height as 110 feet and said the tower would be finished that week, with phone lines to follow.[^1] The two figures disagree. We have recorded the later one, but we could not check either against the tower itself.

W.J. Leatherwood was named patrolman for the area in 1937, and a later report calls him the watchman, with Hugh Sheppard as relief man.[^1] Juanita Westbrook took over the tower in February 1953 and worked it as a radio operator, watching for fires.[^1] A 1954 newspaper said she guarded 58,000 acres in northeastern Texas, and that most people who started the 126-step climb gave up about halfway.[^1] In January 1966 Jerry Westbrook was crew leader for the Negley crew area and its tower, which served the northern part of Red River County.[^1] That is the last staffing we found.

The clippings do not name any particular fire the tower spotted. A 1954 article explains how the tower operator tells the two apart. A legal fire is a rancher's brush burn. An illegal one is started on purpose or by accident. Illegal fires are the damaging kind, and the lookout had to pick them out early.[^1]

The Texas list from the Forest Fire Lookout Association still marks the tower as standing.[^2] We found no record that it was removed. The newspaper pieces we read end in 1966, so the years since are not documented in the sources we read.[^1][^2]

We found no public access statement for Negley. Anyone interested should ask the Texas Forest Service before going, and should not climb the tower without permission.
