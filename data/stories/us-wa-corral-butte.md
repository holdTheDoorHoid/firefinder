Corral Butte is in the Okanogan National Forest, about 14 miles west of Loomis.[^3] A ground-mounted L-4 cab went up there in 1933.[^1][^2][^3] In 1934 panoramic photos were shot from its roof.[^2]

In July 1937, the Wenatchee Daily World reported a lightning strike at Corral Butte that the lookout put out without help.[^2][^4]

In 1956 a U.S. Geological Survey party noted the lookout house still standing.[^2] Around 1957 the cab was reported gone, and the Fire Lookout page gives 1957 as the year.[^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register instead says the cab was removed in the 1960s.[^1]

In July 2001 the site appears to have been used again in connection with the Thirty-mile Fire, a blaze that killed four young firefighters, according to a 2001 account quoted on WillhiteWeb and in the register.[^1][^2] The road had been newly reworked, and the gate had been repaired and fitted with a new lock.[^2]

No source describes what remains at the site now. WillhiteWeb says the simplest approach comes from the Loomis area, by a gated road alongside Forest Road 39, and the hike is a half mile up the road from a locked gate.[^2] A SummitPost page with a photo dated 2009 says the peak once carried a fire lookout on its summit.[^5]

WillhiteWeb describes a locked gate and a half-mile walk, but it does not say whether the gate is open now.[^2] Ask the Okanogan National Forest before you go.
