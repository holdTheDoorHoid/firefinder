North Point sits on a ridge in Olympic National Forest, north of the Snider Ranger Station and about seven miles west of Lake Crescent in Clallam County.[^2][^3][^4] The cab is an L-4 design, and it was built in 1939, according to the register, TrailChick and firelookout.com.[^1][^2][^3]

A federal survey record from 1954 names a "North Point Lookout House," which suggests the building was already there by then.[^4]

The register says the cab was used for several years, then only sporadically in the 1950s, before it was abandoned in 1969.[^1][^2] A few newspaper items from that stretch survive. In May 1956 a Port Angeles paper reported that the lookout was staffed on an emergency basis, and that old snowdrifts still blocked the access road in the woods.[^4] In December 1959 a speaker said the Forest Service planned to develop the site, which was reachable by a forest road.[^4] In 1965 Bryon Loucks observed and reported fires from the lookout.[^4]

The register suggests North Point may first have been a patrol point for Kloshe Nanich, a former lookout only half a mile away, and that its higher site likely replaced that older one. The register words this as a belief, not a fact.[^1][^2]

The cab stopped serving as a lookout in 1969.[^1][^2][^3] Later it was converted to radio use. The register calls the result a communication tower, and firelookout.com calls it a radio communication building.[^1][^3] Both the register and TrailChick still treat the building as standing where it was first put up.[^1][^2]

The register lists the lookout as registered on April 13, 2022.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^5] To reach the site, drive Forest Road 3041 to the Kloshe Nanich parking area, then walk about half a mile, passing a closed gate along the way.[^2] TrailChick warns that the road is steep and narrow in places and says high clearance is likely recommended.[^2] None of the sources read for this story describes public entry into the building, which is used for radio communication.[^1][^3] Treat it as a working site rather than a place to climb.
