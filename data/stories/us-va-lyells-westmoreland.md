A July 1938 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says the State Conservation Commission had completed a 100-foot lookout tower at Lyells.[^1] A 1955 item on the same page says the tower was built in 1937 with C.C.C. labor, and that its timber was creosoted pine.[^1]

The two items disagree about height.[^1] The 1938 report gives 100 feet, while the 1955 item says the tower stood 120 feet tall, the tallest of the fire towers in the Tappahannock District.[^1] The 1955 item adds that on a clear day both the Rappahannock and Potomac Rivers could be seen from the top, and that the tower stood on the farm of Bert Edwards.[^1]

The sources also disagree about the county.[^1][^2][^3] The lookout list and the Forest Lookouts page put the tower in Westmoreland County.[^1][^2] The 1938 item says Richmond County, and Wikipedia describes Lyells as a community in Richmond County.[^1][^3]

The 1955 item reports that the wooden tower was pulled down on Monday, July 11, 1955.[^1] It was the only one of the state's last four wooden fire towers in Tidewater Virginia.[^1] Plans were already under way for a steel tower to replace it, with construction set for August by the Virginia Forest Service.[^1] The lookout list now marks the site as gone.[^2]

The sources do not describe what remains at the Lyells site today, or whether the land is open to visitors.
