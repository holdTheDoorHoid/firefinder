The West Brookfield Fire Tower stands on Ragged Hill in the town of West Brookfield, Worcester County, Massachusetts. The Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control administers it, and the National Historic Lookout Register lists it.[^1][^2]

A 50-foot tower was bought for the site in 1917, but the state postponed putting it up for lack of money.[^2] The next year a 48-foot tower with a 10-by-10-foot room at the top went up on what the state report calls Ragged Mountain.[^2] The tower also needed three miles of telephone line to connect with the New England Telephone and Telegraph Company at West Brookfield.[^2] The state held the tower back in 1918 because fire danger that year was low.[^2]

The 1917 and 1918 state reports give different heights for the same tower: 50 feet when it was bought, 48 feet when it was erected. The Register also gives the original tower as 48 feet.[^1][^2]

The Great New England Hurricane destroyed the lookout on September 21, 1938.[^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps built its replacement in 1939, a 60-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10-foot wooden cab perched on top.[^1][^2]

The sources I read do not say who staffed the tower, how long it was manned, or what fires it watched.[^1][^2][^3]

The Register gives a registration date of December 15, 2004.[^1] In 2015 a state maintenance crew made repairs on nine towers, and at West Brookfield the electrical wiring to the cab and tower lines was completed.[^3]

The Register describes the tower in the present tense and gives its elevation as 1,201 feet (366 meters).[^1] Nothing I read reports its condition after 2015, says whether the public can visit or climb it, or mentions a rental.[^1][^2][^3] Before going up, check with the Bureau of Forest Fire Control or the town, and stay off any tower you have not been cleared to enter.
