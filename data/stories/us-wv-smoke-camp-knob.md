A lookout tower went up on Smoke Camp Knob in Pocahontas County, West Virginia, in 1928.[^1] The register describes it as a 30-foot steel tower administered by the U.S. Forest Service on the Monongahela National Forest.[^1] A register photo shows an earlier tree-stand tower from 1927.[^1]

During construction, the cook relied on a pack burro called the Jenny to fetch water from a nearby spring. A Forest Service Bulletin digest dated April 1928, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page, says she was later fatally kicked by another animal on her last trip and buried at the foot of the tower.[^2]

Both the register and Forest Lookouts date the end of staffing to 1950.[^1][^2]

Forest Lookouts marks the tower as removed. The register says only the footers are left.[^1][^2]

Today the site is reached on foot, on national forest land.[^1][^3] A hiking guide describes a 4.2-mile loop from West Virginia Route 28 that climbs by the Smoke Camp Trail to the old tower site. The Rothkugel Loop, which the guide says begins the hike, has interpretive signs.[^3] An exhibit at the bottom of the mountain shows a photo of the first and second towers and says the larger one, built in 1928, lasted into the 1950s.[^3] The same guide says concrete posts that once held up the tower can still be seen near a circle with a small picnic area, where the road ends.[^3] It also says parking at the site is nonexistent, requiring a short road walk, and that room for two or three cars is on Forest Service Road 54, a little off WV 28.[^3]
