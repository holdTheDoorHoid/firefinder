The Rajneesh Ranch Lookout was built in 1985 by the commune on the Big Muddy Ranch in Wasco County, Oregon.[^1] The register says the ranch was bought in 1980, while the Center for Land Use Interpretation says 1981, so the sources disagree on that date.[^1][^2] The Center describes Rajneeshpuram, an intentional community in an isolated valley, as created from the ground up in the 1980s for followers of Bhagwan Shree Rajneesh, and says as many as 7,000 people moved there.[^2]

The register describes a two-story wooden ground-house lookout with a catwalk, put up to spot fires while the commune was still under construction.[^1] A fire lookout listing gives different dimensions: a six-foot wooden tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab.[^3]

The Bhagwan was deported in 1985, and the property was vacated.[^2] The register says the followers left in the 1990s, that much of the commune later burned in wildfires, and that the lookout stands empty.[^1] The Center's account ties the vacating to the 1985 deportation, and says the government eventually seized the property and sold it at auction in 1988.[^2]

Dennis Washington bought the ranch in 1991.[^2] In 1996 he gave it to a Christian group that ran it as a youth camp.[^2] The register names Big Creek Muddy Ranch as the lookout's administrator, and the sources do not explain how that name connects to the camp.[^1]

Today the site is private land. A fire lookout listing says the property is now known as the Big Muddy Ranch, and a standing-lookouts directory marks the site as private and not publicly accessible.[^3][^4] The sources describe no public route, rental or permission to visit. Treat the site as closed to the public, and do not try to reach or climb the lookout.

The register accepted the lookout on March 1, 2003, as US 471, OR 68.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list still shows it as standing, though its coordinates differ from the register's.[^1][^5]
