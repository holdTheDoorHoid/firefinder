In April 1936 a committee on protection of the state's most serious forest-fire sector recommended three new lookout stations for the Tillamook burn area, and a newspaper item of that month said one would be set up on Hembre Ridge, in the western part of the 1933 burned area.[^3]

Sources give different dates and designs for the tower that followed. Oregon Lookouts says a 40-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab went up in 1949.[^3] A September 1951 item it quotes calls the finished lookout an aluminum house on a 40-foot tower.[^3] The register says a 40-foot pole live-in tower was built around 1950,[^1] and firelookout.com dates a 40-foot pole live-in tower to 1952.[^2]

Oregon Lookouts says the lookout was abandoned in 1971 and its tower removed in 1973, and the register also gives 1973 as the removal year.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com instead says the tower was destroyed in 1965.[^2] The sources do not reconcile that date, and none gives a reason for the removal.[^1][^2][^3]

The site lies about 15 miles east of Tillamook, on the Oregon North Coast, and the Oregon Department of Forestry administers it.[^1][^2] No source I read describes what remains of the tower or its footings today, and none describes access.[^1][^2][^3] The register page carries a 2007 photo of the lookout site, credited to Howard Verschoor; the caption gives no further detail.[^1]
