Chinks Peak, now called Chinese Peak, rises 6,791 feet about six miles east-southeast of Pocatello, Idaho, on land the Bureau of Land Management manages.[^1][^2] The Geographic Names Information System changed the name in 2001, after objections to an ethnic slur in the old one.[^4]

A lookout station was set up on the summit in 1944.[^3] That June a crew was building a fire road to the top, and in July a grazing district range examiner reported he had established a new station there.[^3] The lookout websites date the first cab to 1947, a two-story, 10-by-10-foot building.[^1][^2]

In January 1957 a fire burned down the watch station, a 12-by-12-foot building, and county officers suspected arson.[^3] A January 1960 report mentioned reconstruction of the lookout.[^3] The newspaper's size does not match the websites' 10-by-10-foot cab, so the two accounts may describe different buildings.[^1][^2][^3]

The 1962 cab replaced the 1947 one.[^1] It had two stories, was built of concrete and frame, and had windows described as flat and inverted.[^1][^2]

Living quarters improved in 1952, when the BLM brought a modern trailer up the peak by bulldozer.[^3] In June 1958 a newspaper reported that the station would get its first direct telephone line. In November 1958 the BLM proposed withdrawing 80 acres for lookout and radio facilities.[^3]

The staff lists name Cecil and Lillie Dixon from 1948 to 1966, and Robert and Sandra Rands for 1967.[^1][^2] Newspapers say Cecil Dixon had run the lookout for 19 summers, and report his return for 1968 and his presence on the peak in 1969, which the lists leave out.[^3]

In early June 1968 the BLM found that vandals had smashed one side of the glass cab and demolished the downstairs door. When the damage was done is not known. Teenagers who held parties there were blamed.[^3] In 1974, as a cooperative fire contract in the Pocatello area was being ended, the BLM said it would keep maintaining the lookout.[^3]

The lookout websites list the cab as destroyed in 2003.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page lists the 2003 event as "Removed (Kresek)" and gives no detail.[^3] The sources disagree on whether the cab was destroyed or taken down.[^1][^2][^3]

Wikipedia still describes the summit as a fire lookout site, and a road or bike trail reaches it.[^4][^5] A climbing guide describes a 4.7-mile drive that starts on Barton Road in south Pocatello.[^5] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the 1962 cab on the summit.
