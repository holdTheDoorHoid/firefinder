The Snake Ridge lookout went up in 1939 on a ridgetop south of Nelsonville, Ohio, in the Wayne National Forest.[^1][^2][^3][^5] Civilian Conservation Corps labor built it.[^2] It was a 100-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree about the county of that first site. The Forest Service marker places it in Athens County, southwest of Nelsonville, while the Forest Lookouts page and the Forest Fire Lookout Association list say Hocking County.[^3][^2][^7]

The marker says the towers were staffed during the spring and fall fire seasons, when blazes were most common, and that they were in active use until the 1970s.[^3] An October 7, 1975 Logan Daily News item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says regular use stopped in 1972, when the Forest Service began spotting fires from the air. The item adds that the tower was still maintained for use on severe fire-hazard days.[^2]

The register says that in 1964 the top 20 feet and the cab went to Hocking College, where prospective firefighters could do exercise drills on the top section.[^1][^4] Other sources date the dismantling to the 1970s.[^4][^5] The Forest Service reacquired the tower and rebuilt the full 100 feet at the Wayne National Forest headquarters in 2007.[^1][^5] In 2010 the Ohio chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association reported that the tower had been relocated, opened next to the headquarters and certified by the association.[^6]

The original ridgetop tower is gone. The Forest Fire Lookout Association list marks the Hocking site as gone, and we found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the ridge.[^7]

The rebuilt tower and its marker are at the Wayne National Forest headquarters, beside U.S. 33.[^3] The Forest Service says the tower now holds radio gear and other communications equipment and is not open to visitors.[^5] A third-party guide says it is open for limited public use, but that page carries no date.[^4] Check with the forest before you go, and do not climb it.
