The Bear Gulch station, on the Lolo National Forest in Mineral County, dates to 1925, when sources describe a log cabin with a crow's nest.[^1][^2]

A 1928 Daily Missoulian report, quoted on a Montana lookout page, named Bear Gulch as one of the new Lolo forest structures, slated for a lookout cabin and tower.[^2] The 1925 entries mention a crow's nest and no tower, and the sources do not reconcile the two accounts.[^1][^2]

In July 1926 a newspaper reported that a watchman, Lee L. Mauer, had been struck by lightning at a station it called the Bear Creek lookout, near Superior.[^2] According to the article, he was unconscious for around 90 minutes, and the strike destroyed the station telephone.[^2] Whether that station is the same as Bear Gulch is unclear from the article, although the Bear Gulch page on the Montana Forest Lookouts site files the story there.[^2]

In 1935 a Missoulian report, also quoted on that page, said a lookout at the Bear Gulch station, John Mattovich, shot a 90-pound mountain lion he met while on patrol.[^2]

The station was abandoned in 1948.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list also records it as abandoned.[^3] The sources do not describe what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the spot is open to visitors.
