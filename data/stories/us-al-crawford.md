The Crawford tower stood at the intersection of Shades Crest and Farley roads in Bluff Park, in Hoover, Alabama.[^2] The register describes it as a 100-foot steel Aermotor tower in Jefferson County.[^1] It was named for George Gordon Crawford, the late president of the Tennessee Coal, Iron and Railroad Company.[^3]

The sources disagree about when it went up. A May 1941 item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, calls the tower new and says it was the fourth the State Department of Conservation had put up in Jefferson County.[^3] The Hoover Sun says the tower came down in 1992 after 63 years on the job, which would put its start around 1929.[^2]

The 1941 item also describes Shades Mountain as the scene of forest fires in recent years.[^3]

A 1971 survey description, quoted on the same page, calls the station an Alabama state fire lookout tower: a four-legged steel structure topped by an observation cabin, about 100 feet high.[^3]

The Sun reports that the tower was torn down and misplaced in 1992, but its cab was removed and hidden on city property.[^2] A member of the Hoover Fire Department found the cab on a lot at Fire Station No. 4 on Municipal Drive, and a restoration project that began in May 2011 was completed by a partnership between the Historical Society and the city.[^2]

The register says the tower is no longer at its original site, and the restored cab is in a small park near the Hoover Fire Department at 1591 Patton Chapel Road.[^1] The Sun reports that the Historical Society dedicated the cab on October 31 at its new location across from Hoover Fire Station No. 2.[^2] The Sun's report of the ceremony is dated December 31, 2012.[^2]

Delores Wilkinson, the society's president, says visitors can walk up to the cab from their car, and the site has a historic marker.[^2] No source I read describes what, if anything, remains at the original Shades Crest and Farley site.[^1]

The register lists the site as US 1614, AL 3, nominated by Andrew Zerbe and registered May 27, 2016.[^1]
