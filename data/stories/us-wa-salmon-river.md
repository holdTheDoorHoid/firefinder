The Salmon River lookout stood about three miles northwest of Lake Quinault in Grays Harbor County, Washington.[^1][^2] The Washington Department of Natural Resources administered the site.[^1] A platform and trailer went up there in 1965, and in 1967 they gave way to a two-story DNR live-in cab.[^1][^2]

A DNR biennial report, quoted on Rex's page, puts the erection of a lookout tower in 1966 to 1968. That range does not match the 1965 and 1967 dates on the other two pages, and the sources do not settle it.[^3]

In April 1969 a Port Angeles newspaper said three forest lookouts were to be staffed to back up aerial detection.[^3] Salmon River Ridge was one of those stations.[^3] We found no later staffing record in the sources we read.

In 2000 the cab was moved to the museum in Buckley.[^1][^2][^3] Firetower.org and firelookout.com call it the Buckley Museum, and WillhiteWeb calls it the Buckley Foothills Museum. The museum's own contact page gives its name as the Foothills Historical Museum, at 130 N. River Ave.[^1][^2][^4][^5]

The Buckley museum in these sources is most likely the Foothills Historical Museum, which is open on Sundays from 1 to 4 p.m. and Tuesday through Thursday from noon to 4 p.m., with admission by donation.[^4] The museum is shut for Thanksgiving and from December 15 through February 1.[^4] The contact page shows no date, so check the hours before you visit.[^4] None of the pages we read says whether the cab is on display or what condition it is in, and we found no source describing the original site today.[^1]
