Dickson Lookout stands on Barnabe Mountain near Lagunitas in Marin County, inside Samuel P. Taylor State Park.[^1][^2] The Dickson family gave the summit and a right-of-way for a service road to the Tamalpais Forest Fire District in 1938, and the district passed the land to the county in 1941.[^2][^3]

Grading of the access road began in December 1938.[^2] A Mill Valley newspaper reported in May 1939 that the station was nearly finished and that a lookout had already moved in.[^2] A May 1940 notice called the lookout newly built and set its dedication for June 9.[^2] The notice described living quarters for the lookout and his wife, alongside the observation tower.[^2]

The NHLR dates the first structure to 1935, describing a 14-by-14-foot flattop on a two-story tower that was rebuilt in 1975.[^1] A rental listing repeats the same 1935 and 1975 details.[^3] The 1938 to 1940 newspaper accounts describe a station being built then, so the sources conflict on the first date.[^2]

In 1961 a newspaper reported that a fireman was found dead at the foot of a stairway at the lookout. The report said he had been making repairs in preparation for opening the lookout that week.[^2]

The NHLR describes it as one of a small number of active county lookouts in the United States, administered by the Marin County Fire Department.[^1] In 2011 county supervisors agreed to rededicate it as the Dickson Fire Lookout, at the Dickson family's request.[^3]

A Marin County Fire post on NextDoor describes a Volunteer Lookout Program. It says the county operates two lookouts, one on Mount Tamalpais and the Dickson Lookout on Mount Barnabe, and that lookouts are staffed seven days a week from June through late November.[^4] Hiking trails and fire roads in the state park make the top of Mount Barnabe easy to reach on foot.[^3] The lookout itself is an active county fire facility, and no source reviewed says the tower is open to climbing, so please stay out of it.[^1][^4]
