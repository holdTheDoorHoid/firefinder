The Jasper Peak fire tower stands on a summit near Soudan, in northeastern Minnesota.[^4] The Register places it in Saint Louis County and describes the current tower as a 60-foot steel tower with stairs inside, built in 1934.[^1] A 1934 record on the Central US Lookouts site gives the same height and year.[^2] A local history site also dates the steel tower to 1934.[^3]

The summit had a lookout before that.[^1] A local history site says a wooden tower about 10 feet tall went up on the top in 1919, at the request of forest ranger Fred P. Murphy.[^3] The Register says the 1934 tower was not the first on the site.[^1] A 1920 state report called the lookout perhaps the most valuable observation post in the state.[^2]

The sources disagree on the height.[^1][^2] A 1936 federal survey note describes a 100-foot steel tower, which does not match the 60 feet in the Register and the 1934 record.[^2] We have used 60 feet.

The peak was once named Chester Peak, after a professor who surveyed the iron ranges. Its present name refers to red jasper, the main component of the bedrock.[^4][^3] The state used the tower for fire watch into the 1990s, and watchers climbed up with a radio to look for smoke.[^3]

The Bois Forte Band bought the land from the state in 1996.[^3] The band later traded it back to the state for land elsewhere.[^3] The Register added the tower on June 5, 2018, and names the Minnesota Department of Natural Resources as its administrator.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list shows it as standing.[^5] The Tower-Soudan Historical Society lists the lookout as a historic site at 5080 Minnesota 169.[^6]

The sources read do not say whether the public may climb the tower today. Check with the DNR before you go, and do not climb it unless you have confirmed it is open.
