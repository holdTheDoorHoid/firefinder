The first Mormon Lake lookout was a wooden tower raised in 1915 at a reported cost of $139.[^1][^3] A later history page gives the cost as $139.63 and dates the tower to 1914 to 1918.[^2] In 1927 a steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab replaced it.[^2] The NHLR gives the height as 48 feet, while the 2018 federal review gives 50.[^1][^3]

The federal review dates the cabin to 1928, and it was entered on the National Register on January 28, 1988.[^3] The same review found the tower ineligible for the Register because it had lost historic integrity.[^3]

In June 1920 the lookout fire guard, J.E. Wiley, found a fire near Allen Lake and traced it to a camping party, in charge of L.V. Rush.[^2] Rush was let off with a fine of $10.[^2] In summer 1953 Mr. and Mrs. Sam Saunders were stationed at the lookout.[^2]

The cabin, which deteriorated from vandalism, weathering and lack of routine maintenance, collapsed in December 2018.[^3] Earlier, the Forest had removed the lowest tower stair treads to deter public access.[^3] The Coconino National Forest proposed removing the tower and the cabin remains.[^3] In a letter dated February 20, 2019, the state historic preservation officer concurred with the Forest's decision to delist the cabin from the Register.[^3] One history page says the tower came down in 2019.[^2] The NHLR lists it as removed by 2024, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was demolished by 2024.[^1][^4]

According to the register, debris is all that remains of the tower.[^4] No source I read describes what is left of the cabin, or whether visitors can reach the site now.
