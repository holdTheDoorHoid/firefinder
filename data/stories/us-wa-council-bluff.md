Council Bluff is a summit in Skamania County, Washington, at 5,180 feet (1,579 meters).[^1] A lookout went up there in the 1930s.[^1] A 1931 newspaper item, quoted on the Washington Lookouts site, reported that Jim Green and John Peters, Jr. were packing in the material for a lookout house on the bluff.[^2] Willhite's history says the lookout was built in 1931 and 1932. It was a gable-roofed L-4 cab on the ground, not on a tower.[^3]

The first lookout was Clarence McMahan.[^3] Willhite also reports a panorama taken from the ground in 1934, and says Francis (Pitman) McMahan and Gena Hickey manned the station in 1943.[^3]

The sources disagree about how the cab ended. Willhite thinks it was probably abandoned in the 1950s, and that a survey party later reported it demolished, with the loss before 1964.[^3] Firetower.org says it was removed in the 1960s, and FireLookout.com says it was destroyed in that decade.[^1][^4]

The sources do not describe what, if anything, is left on the summit today. Willhite's photos from 2018 show the site looking north, but his text does not mention footings or a foundation.[^3]

The land is National Forest, and the summit is reached on foot. Willhite describes a hike of 1.5 miles with 950 feet of climbing, and says the trip can start right from the Council Lake campground.[^1][^3]
