Seahorn sits in Overton County, Tennessee, at an elevation of 1,962 feet (598 m).[^1] The statewide list of Tennessee lookout towers marks the tower there as removed.[^1]

Tennessee's forestry division ran a fire detection network built around its lookout towers from 1933 to 1975.[^2] A National Register of Historic Places multiple property document on the division's lookout towers says that many of the 208 towers once in that network are gone or slated for demolition, owing to disuse or to leases with private landowners that expired.[^2] The same document adds that people who visit surviving towers are often cautioned about the hazards of climbing them.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Seahorn site today, or whether the land is open to the public.
