The Okanogan Post Office lookout was not a tower. It was a rooftop firefinder station on a building in downtown Okanogan, Washington.[^4][^5]

The main evidence for its start is a 1930 panoramic photo.[^4] Ray Kresek's 2014 note, quoted on SummitPost, says it was taken from the roof of the post office building in October 1930.[^5] Kresek wrote that the photo only shows a lookout was proposed, and does not prove one stood there.[^5] An inventory entry at the Okanogan supervisor's office does list the post office building as having a lookout in 1930.[^4] Willhite and TrailChick both accept that a lookout stood on the roof.[^4][^3] Willhite suggests it may have been used only for training.[^4]

The building's name is also unclear. Firelookout.com and Forest Lookouts use the post office name.[^1][^2] A 2014 note quoted on SummitPost says the lookout was atop the First National Bank building, and that the bank building is now part of the Rawson's store.[^5] The sources do not agree on which building held it.

Firelookout.com says the firefinder was abandoned in the 1940s, and Forest Lookouts lists it as removed, without a year.[^1][^2]

The building is still standing and looks much as it did, but it no longer holds a post office or Forest Service office.[^5] A hiking guide places it at the southwest corner of 2nd Avenue and Queen Street, where Rawson's Department Store now operates.[^3] Willhite lists the site as drive-up, on a paved road.[^4] The guide recommends a self-guided Okanogan Historical Walk and says the county museum is not far away.[^3]
