Dry Creek Lookout stood in Lewis County, in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, at 3,815 feet.[^1][^2] Sources put it four to five miles from Packwood.[^4] A 1960 newspaper said four miles west, and a 1956 article said five miles south.[^4]

The first structure went up in 1935 as an L-5 cab.[^3][^2][^1] Willhite says panoramic photos were taken from 16 feet above the ground in 1936.[^3] In 1947 a 20-foot timber tower with an L-4 cab on top was built.[^3][^1][^2]

Heavy snow in 1954 lasted into July, and Dry Creek, at low elevation, was the first Packwood District lookout to open.[^3][^4] In 1956 assistant Ranger Jack Fowler locked up the lookout for the season, the last one in the district to close.[^4][^3] In June 1960 the lookout was manned on June 13 after two weeks of dry weather. Wet weather returned, and the fire watcher was brought back down.[^3]

The last structure was removed in 1966.[^3] Firelookout.com and the Register both list the lookout as gone that year, and the Forest Lookouts page lists it as removed.[^2][^1][^4]

Today, Willhite says both footings from the two structures are still there.[^3] His photo captions mention large anchor bolts in the cliffs below the site, burned batteries, and fencing left near the summit.[^3]

To reach the trail, drive Highway 12 west from Packwood for four miles to FR20, Smith Creek Road.[^3] Follow it 0.3 miles to a sign for the Dry Creek Trail and some parking on the side.[^3] Willhite gives the trail as 3.5 miles and says it is open to motorcycles, hikers, horses, and bicycles.[^3] Early on, the trail crosses Dry Creek on a log.[^3]
