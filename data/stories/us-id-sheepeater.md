Sheepeater Lookout stands on Sheepeater Peak in the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness, on the Payette National Forest in Idaho.[^1][^2] Sources put the summit at either 8,421 or 8,486 feet.[^1][^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it one of the oldest lookouts still in use in the wilderness, and among the most remote.[^1]

The first building on the summit was a hip-roofed log cabin. One source dates it to 1925, and another says the late 1920s.[^2][^3] Two listings date an L-4 cab on the site to 1934. Keeping Watch also gives 1934 as the build year, though it labels that cabin R-6 rather than L-4.[^3][^4][^5] The Register instead places an L-4 ground house there in 1957, so the early dates are not settled.[^1]

In 1976 a metal cab moved from the Lookout Mountain site replaced the older structure.[^3][^4] The University of Idaho archive file ties that steel cab to Gate City Steel.[^2] The Register describes the current cab as a 14-by-14-foot flat-roof CL-100 on a six-foot stone base.[^1] Keeping Watch calls the cabin an R-6 design, 15 feet square, and says the R-6 design generally came after the L-4.[^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts keeps a list of known staff, with entries scattered from 1963 through 2024.[^4]

One episode in the sources stands out: a 1954 paint mishap. In a 1999 account, former Chamberlain District Ranger Val Simpson described an inexperienced young man sent up to the lookout with a gallon of yellow paint. The young man was meant to repaint the roof numbers, but he also painted the rocks around the cab yellow, using the whole gallon.[^2] Within days, another lookout went up to the site to set things right. When the supervisor returned two weeks later, the yellow rocks had been turned under.[^2]

Keeping Watch describes burned forest in view from the lookout, but no source read for this story names a fire the lookout spotted or one that damaged it.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

The sources describe an active lookout, and none reports that it was abandoned, burned or moved after 1976.[^1][^3][^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts labels the site "Hike-In Staffed."[^4] Keeping Watch says it is staffed seasonally, and firelookout.com says it is staffed in the summer.[^5][^3] The Register's phrase "still in full-time use" does not square with those seasonal descriptions.[^1][^3][^5] No source says whether visitors may enter the cab, and none mentions a rental.[^1][^3][^4][^5] Anyone planning a visit should check with the Payette National Forest first, since Keeping Watch says the lookout is staffed only during peak fire season.[^5]
