The Bear River Migratory Bird Refuge put up two steel observation towers in August 1934, one at its Headquarters site and one at the Perry entrance.[^1] The Headquarters tower was a 40-foot structure.[^1]

In July 1935 a 100-foot steel tower replaced it at Headquarters, and the 40-foot tower was dismantled and rebuilt at the North End station.[^1] The Utah Fire Lookouts page by WillhiteWeb dates the 100-foot tower to 1934 instead, and it identifies the North End station as the Perry Entrance.[^3] Both pages describe the towers as useful for observation and patrol work.[^1][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Utah list marks the Headquarters tower as gone.[^2]

In the 1980s, rising water from the Great Salt Lake flooded the Headquarters area.[^1] Forest Lookouts says the flooding took all the Headquarters structures, including the lookout tower.[^1] Friends of the Bear River Refuge dates the disaster to 1983, when flooding reduced the refuge to ruins, and says restoration could not begin until 1989.[^4]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the old Headquarters site today. The refuge is located in Brigham City, Utah, and its visitor center sits about a quarter mile from Interstate 15 at exit 363.[^5] Wikipedia dates a new wildlife education center off the interstate to 2006.[^6] The visitor center is open Tuesday through Saturday, and the 12-mile auto tour route is open from sunrise to sunset every day.[^5]
