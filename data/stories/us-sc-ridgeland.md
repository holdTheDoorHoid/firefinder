The Ridgeland lookout stood in Jasper County, South Carolina, near the town of Ridgeland.[^1][^2] Its story starts in October 1931, when the State Forester went to Ridgeland one night to organize a protective association for a 30,000-acre unit.[^2] The Press and Standard reported that the state would erect a fire tower for the unit, and that landowners, the state and the federal government shared the cost of such groups.[^2]

The state's commission report says the tower was erected in November 1931.[^2] It was a 100-foot steel lookout tower.[^2] By late summer 1932 it was connected by telephone with stills in the unit.[^2]

The sources also disagree on how many owners took part. The state report counts five property owners in the unit, while The Columbia Record says six were involved.[^2] The register gives the site's elevation as 89 feet (27 meters).[^1]

The state closed its fire tower system on October 1, 1993.[^3] That date covers the whole system, and no source ties it to this tower. The register and Forest Lookouts both list this tower as removed, but neither gives a date.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
