Grouse Point is a former lookout site in the Clearwater National Forest, about three miles north of the Lochsa Ranger Station, at 5,000 feet.[^1][^2]

The point was first used in 1933, when a tent camp went up with a lookout man, Russell Stadtman, and a crew built a 30-foot tower with an L-4 cabin that same year.[^4][^3][^1] The sources call the frame a pole, round timber or log tower, so the exact build is unclear.[^1][^2][^4]

The tower was staffed for the only time in 1934. That August the Pete King Fire burned it down.[^4][^1][^2][^3] Lightning started the fire early on August 11, during a storm that also sparked other blazes nearby.[^5] Two Civilian Conservation Corps crew members were killed by falling trees on the fire, the Wildland Fire Lessons Learned Center reports.[^5]

Nobody staffed the point after that, and the site fell out of use.[^4] No source I found describes what remains at the site today. The Idaho Fire Lookouts page lists the site as hike-in.[^1]
