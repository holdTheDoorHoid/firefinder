The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maryland list includes a tower it calls Blackwater NWR – Wolf Pit Pond, in Dorchester County [^1]. Two other towers on the refuge, Gum Island and Little Blackwater, appear on the same list, and the association marks all three as gone [^1].

The association also says it is unclear whether several towers on wildlife refuges were for fire detection or for wildlife observation. It kept such towers listed anyway, because definitive proof is lacking and it counts the Fish and Wildlife Service as a federal wildland fire agency [^2].

The refuge dates to 1933, when it was founded as a sanctuary for migrating waterfowl on the Atlantic Flyway [^3]. None of the sources checked describes what, if anything, is left at the Wolf Pit Pond site today.

Visitors can drive, bike or walk the refuge's 4-mile Wildlife Drive with a daily or annual pass, though most of it is closed on some deer-hunt dates [^3].

[^3]: Blackwater National Wildlife Refuge, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, https://www.fws.gov/refuge/blackwater (accessed 2026-10-10).
