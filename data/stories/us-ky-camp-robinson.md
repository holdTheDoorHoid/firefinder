The Camp Robinson Lookout Tower stands in Robinson Forest, a University of Kentucky forest in Breathitt County, Kentucky.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 100-foot Aermotor tower.[^1]

The Register says Kentucky's woodlands once had more than 100 state towers, and that this is the one still in active service.[^1] It says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower in 1934, replacing two wooden towers and a site called Tip Rock within the 15,000-acre forest.[^1] The university's history says the Corps took part in projects in the forest between 1933 and 1937 that included fire towers, bridges and firebreaks.[^2]

The Register says the university's Department of Forestry staffs the tower, but it gives no date for that statement. The other pages read do not describe current staffing.[^1][^2][^3]

The Register gives February 2, 1996 as the registration date, under US 158, KY 2. Robert Muller of the university's forestry department nominated it.[^1]

None of the pages read name fires the tower watched, and none say it has been moved, burned or restored.[^1][^2][^3]

The university's public access page describes the Boardinghouse Trail as a 0.9-mile route that climbs 660 feet before it reaches the fire tower on the hilltop.[^3] The page does not say whether visitors may climb the tower itself, so ask the university before going up.[^3]
