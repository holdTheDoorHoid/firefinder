The steel lookout at Pequot Lakes, Minnesota, was built by Civilian Conservation Corps crews working for the state's forestry division.[^2] The nomination dates the current tower to 1935, when CCC labor replaced a first lookout that had gone up in late 1927.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1934 as the build year, so the two sources disagree by one year.[^1] The tower stands 100 feet tall and has an enclosed cab about seven feet square.[^2]

For years it was a working fire lookout.[^2] The nomination says that from about 2008 to 2014 the tower served visitors instead of spotting fires.[^2] A May 6, 2015 news item shows retired DNR forester Keith Simar still keeping watch from it for fires, so the record on that stretch is mixed.[^4] A 2019 MPR article says Simar started his career as a DNR forester nearly half a century before that article.[^3]

The DNR later quit using the tower and shut it to visitors several years before 2019, citing safety and liability worries.[^3] In 2018 the DNR sold the tower and about 40 acres to Crow Wing County for a single dollar.[^3][^5] The county then put about $50,000 into repairs, among them new stair treads, windows, guard rails and mesh to keep people from falling.[^3]

Today the tower is the centerpiece of Paul M. Thiede Fire Tower Park, named after a former county commissioner who championed saving it.[^1][^5] According to the park's visitor guide, the public may climb the tower, and the grounds include more than 3,000 feet of walking paths.[^5] The guide says the view from the top reaches about 20 miles across the surrounding landscape.[^5]
