Stone Mountain is an exposed granite summit about nine miles east-northeast of Decatur, Georgia.[^1] By January 1958 a 40-foot (12 m) fire tower stood on its summit, and a lookout named Inez Shubert climbed it to scan for wildfires.[^1]

A newspaper report from that month, carried by the Independent of Long Beach, California, called her a "towerman" for the Georgia State Forestry Service. She had been on the job for three years, and she was one of a small number of American women to hold such a post. She described her station as having "all the comforts of home", with a radio, a heater and a telephone. She said she had largely got used to the tower swaying in high winds.[^1]

A December 1963 report, credited to The Kansas City Times, describes the same work. Mrs. Shubert, then 36, was a fire spotter working from the fire tower on the summit.[^1] The sheer face of the mountain is impassable, but she drove a jeep up the steep, stony trail on the back on every day that rain or ice did not cover the ground.[^1]

A 1973 National Geodetic Survey note, reproduced on the same page, places a survey station 202 feet north of an observation tower on the summit.[^1] The note does not say whether that was the fire tower.[^1]

Earlier, Aaron Cloud, a businessman, put up a 165-foot (50 m) wooden observation tower on the summit in 1838.[^2] A storm brought it down, and a far smaller tower took its place in 1851.[^2]

Stone Mountain Park opened on April 14, 1965, and the state of Georgia owns it today.[^2] Visitors can walk up the western side to the summit, or ride the Skyride aerial tram.[^2] No source describes the fire tower, or any remains of it, on the summit today.
