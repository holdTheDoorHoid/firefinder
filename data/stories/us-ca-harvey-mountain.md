Harvey Mountain Lookout stands on the Lassen National Forest in Lassen County, California, and the Register lists its elevation as 7,317 feet.[^1][^4]

The Register says it is the only known surviving example of a 4A cab built to the original 1918 design. Heavy iron straps cover the wood between its windows.[^1] The cab was built in 1919.[^1][^4]

Lookouts were posted on the summit before 1919.[^2] A 1915 newspaper item, quoted on a California lookout history page, reports that Luther S. York had resigned from the station and that Earl Slater took over for the rest of the season.[^2]

In 1934 a steel Aermotor fire tower went up beside the cab, and the cab served as quarters.[^1] The photo page says that tower was later removed, and the Register says fire watch moved back to the ground cabin.[^1][^4]

A 1937 report from the Lassen Advocate, quoted on the same history page, says the lookout spotted a blaze in the Termo district about 30 miles away. Equipment reached the scene within an hour and 20 minutes.[^2]

The Register accepted the lookout on May 29, 2003 as US 517, CA 55.[^1] A Forest Fire Lookout Association list gives the number as US 516, CA 54, so the two lists disagree.[^6]

In 2020 the Forest Fire Lookout Association gave a $500 grant for materials to replace the catwalk deck boards and top handrails and to refurbish the shutters. Fire season and COVID protocols pushed that work back a year.[^3] Vickie Lamoureux, a long-time FFLA member and former Northern California director, staffed the site that season.[^3]

In October 2023 the Forest Service wrote that Lamoureux was staffing the Harvey Mountain tower that year, her 42nd summer as a lookout.[^5] An inspection note from 1953 also called the lookout in good condition.[^2]

A photo page describes the cab as in good condition and staffed during the fire season.[^4] The sources read do not say whether the public may visit or climb to it. Because it is a working fire lookout, anyone planning a trip should check with the Lassen National Forest first.
