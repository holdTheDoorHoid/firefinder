The Two Rock lookout stands on a forested summit about eight miles west of Willits, in Mendocino County.[^1][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register describes the station as an 809R design on a 30-foot steel tower, and says the design is thought to be used only by the state's forestry department.[^1] A March 2026 local news report says the tower was built in 1966 as a summer post, where one person watched for smoke.[^2]

An earlier station on the same summit appears in a 1932 newspaper item, which says a lookout was being built on the ranch of D.T. Bennitt, who had donated the site to the forestry department.[^3] Neither source read says whether that station was replaced by the 1966 tower.[^2][^3]

The lookout was staffed during the fire season.[^3] James Frazier was named as its lookout in 1935, and Bert Gibson held the post in 1938.[^3] In 1956 Mrs. Russel Horn watched from the tower. That year the lookout also served the Ground Observer Corps Skywatch program, and a newspaper reported that Horn was woken one night when a meteor passed the window.[^3]

Volunteers from the Willits area reopened the lookout in 1989, after state funding for it had been lost.[^3] A 1990 entry says it would not be staffed that fire season because of budget cuts.[^3]

The register says the tower was permanently closed in 1994, and that the land and structure were handed back to the private landowner who had leased the land.[^1] Local news says the tower was registered as a historical lookout in 2020.[^2]

The 30-foot tower still stands on the forested summit.[^4] Two wildfire early-detection cameras had been installed at the lookout and were reported active on March 31, 2026.[^4] An AI system monitors the images, and Cal Fire's Howard Forest dispatch center will be alerted to any sign of fire.[^4] A photo caption from February 2026 said the cameras would soon go in the tower.[^2]

The register lists the site as private land.[^1] It is not open to the public, and it sits 3.1 miles behind a locked gate.[^1] Visitors should not try to reach or climb the tower.
