Whiskey Peak lies in Josephine County, Oregon, on the Rogue River National Forest, about 28 miles southwest of Medford.[^1][^2] The first lookout house went up in 1924, according to a Rogue River National Forest history quoted by Oregon Lookouts.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register instead dates a small frame cabin on the summit to 1922.[^1]

A fire destroyed that house in October 1929 while the lookout, Cal Winningham, was outside repairing a chimney.[^3] His belongings and the government supplies on hand burned with it.[^3] The replacement, a ground-mounted, gable-roof L-4 cab, was finished in October 1930.[^1][^3] The plan called for a concrete floor.[^3] Crews hauled sand and gravel up from the Big Applegate by tractor and pack horse.[^3]

Lookouts in the 1930s included Mr. and Mrs. George Ellis in 1935.[^3] In August 1936 a rifle accident seriously injured the lookout, James MacLean.[^3] Two CCC men from Camp Applegate covered the post until Jim Arnold took over.[^3] From August 1942 to October 1943 the site was an Aircraft Warning Service post, and two observers manned it through one winter.[^3] Afterward the Forest Service kept the structures for fire spotting.[^3]

The cab sat abandoned for several years.[^1] In 1989 it was salvaged for the new Sand Mountain Lookout near Sisters.[^1][^2] The Sand Mountain Society says it chose the Whisky Peak cab because its grange-hall style matched the original Sand Mountain lookout, even though the cab was badly run down and the other option, Pearsoll Peak, was in better shape.[^4] Helicopter support was arranged to fly the bundled pieces out.[^4]

No cab stands on Whiskey Peak now.[^1] A 2019 Native Plant Society field-trip guide says the old lookout foundation still remains on the summit.[^5] The same guide describes a short, steep trail from Forest Service roads that are mostly in good shape, with limited parking at the trailhead.[^5] The Forest Service lists the route as Whisky Peak Trail #910.[^6] The sources we read do not describe current closures or conditions, so check with the Rogue River–Siskiyou National Forest before going.
