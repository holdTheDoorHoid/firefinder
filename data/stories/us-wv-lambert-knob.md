Lambert Knob stands in Wyoming County, at about 2,360 feet of elevation.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built its 86-foot tower, but it gives no build year, and I found no other source that does.[^1] The West Virginia Division of Forestry operated the lookout until, according to the register, it was closed in 1991.[^1] A state encyclopedia entry says the state's forest fire towers were closed in 1990, so the two dates disagree.[^3]

The state's network began when the first fire towers went up in 1916. Towers were a response to damaging wildfires that followed the logging of the original forests.[^3]

The register also lists Pocahontas Land Corporation as a cooperator.[^1] Its one visit report, from August 2017, calls the tower's condition very poor after vandalism and a long period of neglect, and says the gate was not locked.[^1] The West Virginia list of lookouts from the Forest Fire Lookout Association still marks it as standing.[^2] I found no current report on access, staffing or condition, so I cannot say whether the public may visit. Given that 2017 report, please do not climb it.
