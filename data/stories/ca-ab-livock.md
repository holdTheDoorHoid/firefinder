The provincial lookout dataset gives 2017 as the year the present Livock tower was built.[^1] It is a steel tower 30 m (98 ft) high, standing at an elevation of 650 m (2,133 ft).[^1] OpenStreetMap, the volunteer-built map, lists the site as the Livock Fire Tower.[^2]

Steel began to replace timber in lookout towers in the 1920s.[^3] A Wildfire Information Officer quoted in the Lakeside Leader says Alberta has 107 steel towers topped with a cupola, a structure that shields the observer from the weather and houses the specialized gear.[^4]

Alberta's wildfire program says each site has a lookout observer on watch every day, usually from April through October.[^5]

The dataset lists the tower as active, with an access mode it labels fly.[^1] A map database also shows a cabin shelter and a helipad nearby.[^6] No source describes the site today, and none says whether the public may visit it.
