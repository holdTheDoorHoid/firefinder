Spanish Peak rises to about 6,871 feet in the Ochoco National Forest, in Wheeler County.[^1][^2][^5] The summit was once called Bald Mountain.[^3] A camp went up on top in 1921, and a 14-by-14-foot cab followed in 1923.[^2][^4]

A May 1923 newspaper item said the planned house would be the first standard lookout house on the Ochoco forest.[^3] The lookout register gives 1930 for the cab instead, so the dates disagree.[^1] In December 1931 the lookout house was raised onto 20-foot stilts late in the fire season, for better visibility.[^3]

The old house gave way to a timber tower in 1938.[^2][^4] The sources disagree about its size. A 1938 newspaper item described a 40-foot tower, while the register and firelookout.com give 50 feet.[^3][^1][^2] The register also dates the swap to 1939.[^1]

Martin Statey moved into the new house in 1924, and Floyd Jones was reporting fires from it by 1927.[^3] In August 1930, lookout Marvin Angle reported a timber fire about two miles southwest of Derr station.[^3] An August 1934 newspaper item said a forest radio station on the peak kept daily contact with Tamarack Mountain, on the Umatilla forest, and Fields Peak, on the Malheur.[^3] The last staffing we found is from 1952, when sisters Eudora and Leora Houston were posted there.[^2][^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1968.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] Oregon Hikers links its removal to a drop in the use of lookouts.[^4]

We found no source that describes a standing structure today. Oregon Hikers reports that concrete footings can still be seen on the summit, and that a rough, unmaintained road runs to the top.[^4] Outdoor Pilgrim says a visitor with a moderately high clearance vehicle can drive to the summit on National Forest roads.[^5] The Forest Service may close some land access from December 1 to May 1 because of bald eagles.[^5]
