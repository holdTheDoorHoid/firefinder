The Pioneer Tower stands in Pickens County, Alabama, and the Alabama Forestry Commission administers it.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 899, AL 57 and gives its elevation as 134 feet (41 meters).[^1] A Forest Lookouts listing files it under the name Pleasant Grove.[^2]

The Register tells a more tangled story about dates. It says the first Pioneer-Grove fire tower was destroyed by a tornado in 1947, and that a 100-foot Aermotor MC-39 with a 7-by-7-foot cab replaced it in 1966.[^1] Its own Built field, however, reads 1947.[^1] The description suggests the 1947 date belongs to the tower that was lost, but we found no second source to confirm the tornado or either date.

A 1819 News column by Thomas Kaufmann names the Aermotor MC-39 as the steel lookout design most often found in Alabama's forests.[^3] Kaufmann also nominated this tower to the Register, which accepted it on January 3, 2011.[^1]

The sources we read say nothing about who staffed the Pioneer Tower, which fires it watched, or whether the public can visit or climb it today. Anyone planning a trip should check with the Alabama Forestry Commission first.
