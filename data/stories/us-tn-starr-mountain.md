The fire tower on Starr Mountain is in McMinn County, Tennessee, at about 2,205 feet, according to the Tennessee Landforms list.[^1] The list numbers it 3-4 and marks it standing.[^1] The mountain is named for Caleb Starr, who owned it in the latter days of the Cherokee Nation.[^3]

The sources do not give a build date, a builder, or a height for the tower. The state's Division of Forestry property document lists a tower on Starr Mountain among those still standing as of March 26, 2014.[^2] That same document says the division built 208 fire towers between about 1930 and 1970.[^2]

The sources disagree about its condition. A photo caption on a local history page about the mountain calls the fire tower abandoned.[^3] The 2014 list has it standing, and no newer state record appears in the sources.[^2]

A mountain-bike guide describes a gravel road that climbs steeply for about a quarter mile to the tower, which offers views to the west.[^4] The guide gives the tower's elevation as 2,200 feet, close to the state figure.[^1][^4]

The sources do not say whether the tower is open to visitors or who owns the land. The same document cautions that climbing surviving towers is dangerous, and that they are frequently in poor shape.[^2] Do not climb this tower. Check with the land manager before going, and treat its condition as unknown.
