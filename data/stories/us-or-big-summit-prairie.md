Big Summit Prairie is a large meadow in the Ochoco Mountains, about thirty miles east of Prineville, Oregon.[^1] Its name comes from its size: it is larger than Little Summit Prairie, a smaller spot roughly twelve miles east of it.[^1]

Homesteaders first took up land there in the mid-1880s, and livestock was the main way the settlers made a living.[^1] In the 1950s the Hudspeth family acquired all of it and grazed their cattle there each summer.[^1]

Today most of the prairie is privately owned and is not open to the public.[^1] Forest Service roads 22 and 42 reach its edges and the land around it, where visitors come to see wildflowers and birds.[^1] The Oregon Encyclopedia article does not describe a lookout tower on the prairie.[^1]
