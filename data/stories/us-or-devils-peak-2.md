Devils Peak is a 7,582-foot summit on the Cascade divide, about 19 miles south-southwest of Crater Lake, in Klamath and Douglas counties, Oregon.[^1][^2][^3]

Newspapers from 1915 name E.W. Smith, an assistant ranger, as the lookout man on the summit.[^3]

The sources disagree about what stood there before 1930. The register and a 2017 hiker's trip report both describe a cabin on the summit from 1917.[^1][^4] A July 1930 newspaper item said the spot had never held a lookout building and that the lookout had worked out of a tent.[^3]

In 1930 the Crater National Forest was getting a ready-made lookout building from Portland, to be set on a concrete foundation that summer.[^3] The register calls it an L-4 cab built that year.[^1][^2]

Newspapers name lookouts for the station in 1933 (Virgil Clark), 1935 (Carl Cobleigh), 1938 (Ralph Ellis) and 1941 (Norman Hayes Jr.).[^3] In 1944 Ellsworth Robison was sent up to stay alone until mid-September, with supplies packed in by mule every fifteen days.[^3] Richard Barber was stationed there in 1951.[^3]

In September 1950 two lookouts, Devil's Peak and Pelican Butte, reported a blaze, and the Devil's Peak lookout reached it first.[^3]

In June 1961 every Rogue River lookout except this one was manned. In 1963 the summit was still snowbound, and the station would probably be manned that week.[^3]

The register says the L-4 cab was removed in 1968.[^1] FireLookout.com and the 2017 trip report also give 1968.[^2][^4] The Oregon Lookouts history page dates the removal to 1969, when a forestry crew burned the structure.[^3] The sources disagree by one year.

The 2017 trip report found only broken concrete and corroded metal bands where the lookout had stood, and describes a use trail that now leads from the Pacific Crest Trail over the summit.[^4] It is one hiker's account, not an agency record. No source we read says whether the public may visit, so check with the Forest Service before going.
