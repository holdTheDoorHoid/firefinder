Deming Lookout stood on Deming Mountain, about two miles north of the town of Deming in Whatcom County.[^1][^2][^3] The register dates the first lookout here to 1929, when an 80-foot pole tower was in use.[^1] From August 1942 to October 1943 the site also housed an Aircraft Warning Service station.[^3] After that the state kept using it as a fire lookout.[^3]

In 1947 a 75-foot steel tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab went up.[^1][^3] The Weebly page says it replaced a small tower built of native materials, which does not match the register's 80-foot pole tower, so the two sources disagree about what stood there before.[^1][^3] The lookout's living quarters came from an abandoned Aircraft Warning Service building.[^3]

The register says a 40-foot wooden DNR tower went up in 1965 and came down in 1985.[^1] The Weebly page gives the replacement years as 1964 to 1966.[^3] The tower was removed in 1985, and the cab went to the museum at Forks.[^1][^3] A WillhiteWeb page says the cab was built in 1965 and removed to Forks in 1985. It also says the lookout came from near Bellingham, moved in sections on a lowboy, and was put back together at the Forks Timber Museum.[^4]

Today the cab is on display at the museum.[^1] The register calls it the Forks Museum, and the Willhite page calls it the Forks Timber Museum.[^1][^4] The register does not say whether the display is open to visitors.[^1] We found no source that describes the mountain site today.
