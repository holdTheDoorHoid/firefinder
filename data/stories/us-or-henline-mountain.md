The Henline Mountain lookout stood on a ridge in the Willamette National Forest, in Marion County, Oregon, at about 4,116 feet.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places the Forest Service cabin on the south-southeast end of that ridge, about a mile from the summit.[^1]

A lookout station was already in use on the mountain by 1930.[^3] A 1930 newspaper item describes a young man who spent that summer alone on the mountain, with 14 visitors in all, and whose provisions were packed up every ten days over a distance of 15 miles.[^3] An October 1933 newspaper item said a lookout house was being finished on the mountain.[^3] The register and the Oregon Hikers field guide both date the cabin to 1934.[^1][^4] They differ on who built it: the register credits the Forest Service, while the field guide says the Civilian Conservation Corps probably did.[^1][^4] The cab was an L-4 design.[^2]

A lookout was posted there in the summer of 1945.[^3] In August 1960 a lookout returning to his post at about 3:30 a.m. fell roughly 20 feet from the trail. X-rays showed no broken bones, but the newspaper reported that his legs were temporarily paralyzed.[^3] The lookout was last staffed in 1963, and the cabin was removed in 1967.[^3][^4]

Today the old site is the end of the Henline Mountain Trail #3352, so hikers can reach it on foot.[^4] The field guide lists Mount Beachie, Battle Ax and Mount Jefferson among the peaks in view.[^4] The register lists the Forest Service as administrator.[^1] We found no source that describes footings, a foundation or any other remains at the site, so we cannot say what is left there now.
