The Steuben lookout stood near the small community of Steuben in northern Schoolcraft County, on the Hiawatha National Forest.[^1][^2][^3]

The Forest Lookouts site records that a steel tower was erected there in 1921, citing a conservation department report from 1922.[^1] A different account credits the Civilian Conservation Corps: enrollees at Camp Steuben, which opened on May 4, 1933, built a fire lookout tower near the town.[^2] The two accounts do not match, and the sources do not show whether one replaced the other.[^1][^2] In April 1929 the Escanaba Daily Press reported that buildings to house the fire lookouts had been erected at Steuben and Tie Hill.[^1]

Eugene Scott and Ernest Edwards began work as fire wardens on the Steuben tower in the spring of 1930.[^1] The next day they found and put out a fire at Martha Lake.[^1] According to Scott's 1958 obituary, he spent 29 years as the Steuben towerman before retiring from the U.S. Forest Service.[^1]

The state owned the tower before the Forest Service took it over, and the Forest Service used it from 1937.[^3] A U.S. Weather Bureau also used it to gather weather information, a use that had been dropped about five years before November 1967.[^3] That autumn the Forest Service began scheduled airplane flights, and its new air detection system was set to replace eight fire towers on the Hiawatha, Steuben among them.[^3]

No source describes the site today, including whether a tower or any footings remain there.
