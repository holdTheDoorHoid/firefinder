Smoothing Iron Ridge, in the Umatilla National Forest in Asotin County, Washington, once held an emergency lookout cabin.[^1][^4] One source calls it an L-5 cabin.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the cabin to 1935, and it dates archive panoramas of the site to August 7 of that year.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page, which carries the name Rex's Fire Lookout Page, also gives 1935.[^4]

Willhite Web gives an earlier date, 1933. A Civilian Conservation Corps camp was in the Asotin District that year, and one of its projects was building the emergency lookout house.[^3] The sources disagree by two years.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

During the 1940s the cabin was used in emergencies.[^1][^2]

In 1950 the cabin was moved off the ridge to a farm to the northeast. firelookout.com gives the distance as 4.5 miles and Willhite Web as 5.[^2][^3] In 2003 the farm was sold to the Washington Department of Fish & Wildlife and the Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation.[^3] During the sale, the Schlee family moved the cabin to family property on a different ranch, which is now in Whitman County.[^3] firelookout.com likewise says it went to a private farm in Whitman County in 2003.[^2]

The register, which covers the ridge site, says the cabin "is now gone."[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Washington list shows the relocated cabin as standing on private land in Whitman County.[^5] Willhite Web's 2021 account describes it in good condition in a safe location in Whitman County.[^3]

The cabin is on private property.[^3][^5] Willhite Web reports that Ralph and Peggy Owen visited the cabin at its current site in 2021, with special permission.[^3]

The original ridge is National Forest ground.[^1][^3] Willhite Web gives directions from Pomeroy: take 15th Street south, then FR-44 to Round Prairie, where you park and walk. The walk is 500 to 1,000 feet of easy going.[^3] The foundation has been hard to find, and no source describes a structure on the ridge now.[^3]
