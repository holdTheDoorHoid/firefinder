Mount Defiance is a peak near the Columbia River Gorge, and it carried a fire lookout from the mid-1920s until about 1971.[^5][^1]

The first lookout was a tree crow's nest with a tent, which firetower.org and firelookout.com date to about 1925.[^1][^2][^4] A January 1924 item gives an earlier start, saying a ranger established a lookout station there during 1923.[^3]

In June 1928 a young couple planned to spend their honeymoon at the summit, with the groom serving as lookout until September.[^3] In 1934 a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced the crow's nest.[^1][^2][^3]

The Forest Service began replacing the lookout house and tower in May 1952, and a later historical note says the work was completed in June.[^3]

Sources disagree about the 1959 windstorm. Three say the tower was destroyed, while the Oregon lookouts history says it was severely damaged.[^1][^2][^4][^3] A February 1960 entry in that history said a new cabin would sit on the 40-foot tower ahead of the 1960 season.[^3]

A 41-foot timber tower with a flat-top R-6 cab replaced it in 1962.[^1][^2][^3] The station was deactivated in 1964.[^3]

Three sources say the last tower was gone by 1971, while the Oregon lookouts history says it was burned that winter.[^1][^2][^4][^3]

Today a hiking blog reports that the summit still has communication buildings, and the writer thinks foundation remnants can be found by looking.[^4] Climbing the route means gaining about 5,000 feet across six miles, starting at the Starvation Creek Trailhead.[^5] The north and west slopes fall within the Mark O. Hatfield Wilderness.[^5] The route had only recently reopened following the 2017 Eagle Creek Fire.[^4]
