Dennis Hill is in the town of Norfolk, in Litchfield County, Connecticut.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register gives the site an elevation of 1,640 feet, while a 2014 visitor's page gives 1,627 feet.[^1][^4]

The first lookout station on the hill was established by State Forester Samuel Spring about 1911, according to a state report quoted on the Forest Lookouts site.[^2] The register says the original tower stood on top of Dr. Dennis's summer home there, though it gives no year for that tower.[^1]

The summer home belonged to Dr. Frederick Shepard Dennis, a New York City physician who spent many summers in Norfolk.[^3] Wikipedia dates Dennis's commission of the summit bungalow to 1908, when he engaged the architect Alfredo S.G. Taylor.[^3] A 1955 Bridgeport Telegram piece, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, describes that building as an octagonal stone structure with a wooden stair on its outer side that climbs to a small viewing platform.[^2]

The station was in use in the 1920s.[^2] A 1921 item counted one of Connecticut's four lookout towers in operation at Dennis Hill.[^2] A 1924 report said the station looked over the county's northwest portion in clear weather, though it was too distant to be of much use.[^2] We found no record of who staffed it or when it stopped.

Dr. Dennis gave the estate to the state. The 1955 piece gives 1934 as the year, and Wikipedia gives 1935.[^2][^3] The park is administered today by the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection.[^3]

The platform's later history is less clear. A 2014 visitor's page says an observation deck once sat on the roof of the summit pavilion, and that the roof and decking burned. It gives no date for the fire.[^4] According to the register, a replica went up on the site after a photo of the original tower turned up in 1992, but the register does not date the replica itself.[^1] We found no source that describes a replica on the summit today.

Today the summit is part of Dennis Hill State Park, which the 2014 visitor's page calls a free state park.[^4] The summit road is open from late April to early November, and you can hike up at any time.[^4] The page describes a large open-air pavilion on the summit, and says the state keeps the view to the south and east clear of obstructions.[^4] Anyone who wants to see the summit should check the park's current rules first, and should not climb any structure that is closed or looks unsafe.
