Cable Mountain is a peak northeast of Georgetown Lake.[^1] In June 1937 the Forest Service began building a fire lookout there: a frame cabin atop a 20-foot log tower, with a telephone line to be strung and a trail to be blazed to the lookout.[^1] A temporary camp served as the lookout while the work went on.[^1] By early September 1937 the lookout, a 14-foot-square tower with living quarters, had been completed and accepted.[^1] Rex Kamstra's list gives 1934 and calls it a 20-foot pole L-4 tower.[^2] The 1937 newspaper reports describe the building work, so this story follows them.[^1]

The lookout was staffed during its first season.[^1] A lookout observer took over the tower in September 1937.[^1] In late September 1939 the paper reported that John Ringdahl would leave the post, and that his departure would mark the official close of that year's fire season.[^1] In 1940 the lookout was the only one in its district, and the paper says visitors who climbed to it could be given membership cards in the Ancient Order of Squirrels.[^1] In July 1943 Anna Beth Glaser, 19, of Anaconda, was named the first woman to serve as a lookout in the Deer Lodge National Forest and assigned to the peak.[^1]

The papers also mention a weather incident. In June 1938 lightning destroyed a tree near the lookout, and the forest supervisor said the lookout itself was not damaged.[^1]

The sources disagree on how it ended.[^1][^2] Kamstra's page says it was destroyed in 1978.[^2] Ron Kemnow's listing says it was removed and gives no date.[^1] The Montana lookout list marks it as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to the public.
