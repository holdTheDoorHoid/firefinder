Oregon Butte Lookout sits on a high, pinnacled butte in the Gospel Hump Wilderness, in the Nez Perce National Forest of Idaho County.[^1][^4] The name comes from the unobstructed view of Oregon's Wallowa Mountains from the summit.[^5] Reaching the lookout takes a hike of about five miles each way.[^5][^1]

The site started as a tent camp in the early 1920s. In 1929 a cupola cabin became the first permanent lookout.[^5][^1] Sources differ on how that cabin was built. The Register and the Forest Service describe it as log, firelookout.com as a shake cabin, and a local history as gable-roofed. All of them mention a cupola.[^1][^5][^2][^4]

The cabin was replaced in 1963 by an R-6 flat-top cab set on a seven-foot base.[^1][^2][^5] Sources disagree on what that base is. The National Historic Lookout Register and firelookout.com say concrete, while a local history page describes a cinder-block enclosed storage area.[^1][^2][^4] A 1946 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls the lookout house of that time a standard 12-by-14-foot Forest Service building.[^4]

Staffing is reported in different ways. The Register says the lookout is staffed every season, and an undated Forest Service post says the 1963 cab remains staffed to this day.[^1][^5] firelookout.com says it is staffed in the summer.[^2] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on August 25, 1997.[^1][^3]

Today the lookout is reached on foot, and Idaho lookout records tag it as "Hike-In Staffed."[^3] The Forest Service notes that Oregon Butte Lake can be visited on the same hike.[^5]
