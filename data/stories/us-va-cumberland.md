The Cumberland Fire Tower stands behind the Cumberland Court House in Cumberland County, Virginia.[^1] The Register says the steel tower was built in 1941 to replace a wooden tower from 1938 that stood 66 feet high.[^1]

The sources disagree on the early history. A February 1937 Civilian Conservation Corps camp item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, describes a 100-foot steel tower already constructed near the courthouse.[^2] That does not match the Register's sequence, and the sources read here cannot settle which account is right.[^1][^2]

A 1941 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, also quoted there, calls it a four-legged steel structure about 85 feet high, with a survey station on top.[^2]

The sources read do not describe any notable fires. A July 2019 visit report on the Register says the writer doubted anyone occupied the tower, which was badly infested with bees and wasps.[^1]

A photo caption says the tower has been retired from lookout duty, although it supports some local government communications antennas.[^4]

The Register lists the administrator as the Virginia Department of Conservation and Recreation, although the photo's title calls it a Department of Forestry tower.[^1][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^3]

The sources do not say whether the public may enter the grounds or climb the tower, so please do not climb it or go onto restricted property to see it.
