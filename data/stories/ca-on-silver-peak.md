Silver Peak, the highest peak of the La Cloche Mountains, sits inside Killarney Provincial Park.[^1] The name refers to the silvery glint of the white quartzite rock when sunlight hits it.[^1]

The tower was made of wood.[^2] A photo page about Ontario's fire towers says that, at the system's height in the 1950s, this was one of only two wooden towers among 320.[^2]

The tower itself is gone, and only its footings remain.[^1] According to Ontario High Points, the highest spot on the ridge, near the trail, is marked by the foundations of a former fire tower.[^1] That photo page adds that the footings survive.[^2] A 2011 blog post asked whether the concrete footings near the end of the trail came from a fire tower, since they seem too close together compared with those still standing at Maple Mountain and Ishpatina Ridge. The author was not sure they came from a fire tower, but thought they were for some kind of tower.[^3]

Today there is no tower to climb.[^1] One of the most common routes pairs a 5.7 km hike to the summit with a 3.3 km canoe paddle from Bell Lake, and can be done in a day.[^1] At some times of year, Bell Lake may have no parking permits, so visitors must buy them at the George Lake Park Office.[^1] No source I found describes the footings' condition today.
