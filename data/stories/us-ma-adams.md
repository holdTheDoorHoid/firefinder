The register calls it the Adams Tower Site.[^1] It stood on the summit of Mount Greylock in Berkshire County, the highest point in Massachusetts.[^1] A 50-foot observation tower was already there, and in 1911 a lookout cab was built on top of it.[^2] The register calls that tower iron, but the Mount Greylock Lookout history page says steel.[^1][^2]

The register dates the first operation to 1911.[^1] The Willhite history gives October 17 as the day operations started.[^2] Thomas Buxton, who had just finished his work as fire lookout on Greylock, met deputy warden John Murdock Jr. at the end of that short first season, on November 11.[^2] Murdock judged the short season a good one, because a considerable number of people had been warned of fires nearby.[^2]

The station was discontinued after the 1913 fire season because of poor visibility, and the tower was often in the clouds.[^1][^2] Two fire towers replaced it: Borden Mountain in Savoy and Berlin Mountain in Williamstown.[^2]

After that the cab came off, and the steel tower stayed up as an ordinary observation tower.[^2] None of the sources we read says whether that tower still stands.

Visitors can drive to the summit today and climb the Veterans War Memorial Tower.[^2] The Appalachian Trail crosses the summit.[^2] We found no source describing what remains of the 1911 lookout itself.
