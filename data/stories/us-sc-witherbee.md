The Witherbee lookout is a 100-foot Aermotor steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, in Berkeley County near Cordesville, South Carolina.[^1][^2]

The register says the tower first stood at the Bethera site, with a ground house, in the 1930s, and went to the Witherbee Ranger Station in the Francis Marion National Forest in 1952.[^1] A Former Fire Lookout Sites entry for Bethera says the tower was moved to Witherbee in 1952 and that only the footers are left there.[^3] The news story calls the 1952 location the Witherbee Ranger District Office, so the two sources name it differently but appear to describe the same place.[^1][^2]

The Forest Service used the tower actively until 1989.[^1][^2] The register says few lookouts of this kind survive from South Carolina's former network, and that this one keeps its original design and materials.[^1] It was added to the register on December 1, 1998.[^1]

A news story, which our copy shows without a date, says a Forest Service archaeologist unlocked a trap door into the enclosed cab. Inside, he found a dusty book of maps for marking fire locations and a pair of binoculars, and he took them back to his office.[^2] The same story says the tower is still standing and recommended for listing on the National Register of Historic Places.[^2]

We found no report on the tower's condition after that story. A 2024 Forest Service decision covered the demolition of two administrative buildings at the Witherbee station, and its project summary does not mention the tower.[^4]

The tower is at the Witherbee station, a Forest Service site, and we found no public visit route.[^1][^4] Please do not climb it. The Francis Marion Ranger District leads this project, so ask it before going near the station.[^4]
