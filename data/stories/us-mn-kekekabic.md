The Kekekabic lookout stood near Kekekabic Lake in the Superior National Forest's Kawishiwi district, in what is now the Boundary Waters wilderness.[^1][^3] A January 1937 newspaper report announced its completion and called it likely the most remote lookout in northern Minnesota.[^1] Its steel came in over frozen lakes the winter before, and concrete and lumber arrived later by hydroplane.[^1]

The tower was 100 feet tall according to the 1937 report and the state list, although a later article gives 110 feet.[^1][^4][^3] It had no telephone line. Messages went out by radio to the ranger station, and getting there meant crossing lakes and portages by canoe or flying in.[^1]

Bill Rom signed on to man the tower in 1939, the year after the trail was finished.[^3] One observer was posted there for months at a stretch, living in the Kekekabic cabin.[^3] The tower operated for about 20 years, and by the late 1950s aircraft surveillance had largely replaced fire towers, especially in remote areas.[^3]

A Minnesota list of fire lookouts says the Forest Service likely destroyed the tower, leaving it lying in the woods, and that it was probably damaged during removal.[^4] A visitor who hiked in during the 1970s saw the tower toppled.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as gone, and the Minnesota list says it is no longer standing.[^5][^4]

Today the site lies in the wilderness.[^3] The Kekekabic Trail, originally built to reach four fire towers, is kept up by volunteers, and roughly 100 people use it each season.[^2][^3] A visitor who returned in 2011 could not find the tower and thinks it may be hidden among trees felled in the 1999 blowdown.[^2] The cabin still stands.[^2][^3] We found no source that describes the tower site in more detail, or says whether the site has any restrictions.[^2][^4][^5]
