Copperhead Fire Tower stands on a hill in Shawnee State Forest in Scioto County, Ohio.[^1][^4] The state put it up in 1924, and Division of Forestry records describe it as the first fire tower built in Ohio.[^1][^2] A set of 1924 clippings, collected on a forest lookout history site, describes a 60-foot steel tower with stairs and an observation cabin at the top.[^3] It was placed to protect the state forest and the nearby Roosevelt Game Preserve.[^3] The same clippings say the tower was delivered in August and that a telephone line was being built from the forest headquarters at Pine Flat.[^3]

The sources do not agree on the hill's height. The 1924 clippings give 1,300 feet above sea level, and the register lists the site at 1,246 feet (380 m).[^3][^4] Portsmouth says the hill is more than 1,200 feet, and the Scenic Scioto Heritage Trail gives 1,200 feet.[^1][^5]

In 1934 a Chillicothe Gazette item said James Morgan would man the tower, with Isom Morgan as his substitute.[^3] A 1948 newspaper item said a short-wave station would be set up in the tower.[^3] The sources do not say when the tower stopped being staffed, and none of them name a particular fire it helped fight.

Aircraft spotting began in the 1940s, and the state gradually retired its fire towers.[^1] This tower is the only one still standing in the forest.[^2] It joined the National Historic Lookout Register in 1991.[^4] A 2003 visit report on the register found the stairs appeared to be rotting, the steel sound but bare of paint, and no glass or shutters left in the cab.[^4] The sources give no date for a restoration, but the later pages call the tower recently restored.[^1][^2]

Today visitors can climb to the top at their own risk.[^1][^2] Standing at the top, a visitor can look all the way around, with forest and hills in every direction.[^1][^2] The sources give no hours or parking details, so check with the Ohio Department of Natural Resources before you go.
