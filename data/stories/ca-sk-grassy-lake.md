The Website Service listing places the Grassy Lake tower at 53-42-49.4 N, 104-08-28.4 W, and gives its height as 32 m (105 ft) of light steel.[^1] The same listing says the site had three tower locations over the years, so those coordinates may match only one of them.[^1]

The first lookout in the area was a wooden tower from the early 1920s, built under the federal Dominion Forest Service. It stood roughly a mile west and four miles north of the Torch River School.[^2] The article calls the third Grassy Lake tower, raised in 1938 some two miles north of the school, the first all-steel one; it was first staffed that year.[^2] The listing records a new cupola in 1954.[^1] A provincial annual report for 1963 to 1964 says the tower was completely overhauled in 1963.[^3]

Gary Parker, the first provincial ranger for the Grassy Lake district, worked from a headquarters at the tower site and held the post from 1931 to 1941.[^2] Walter Kratz reached the district around the late 1930s, was a towerman by the early 1940s, and stayed until his retirement in 1965.[^2] In 1961, a severe forest fire north of Torch River and Moose Run burned for most of that season, and Syd Stubbington was the officer in charge.[^2]

The local history says the third tower served until 1973.[^2] A replacement steel tower opened about five miles north in 1974.[^2] The old one was later decommissioned and eventually dismantled.[^2] The Website Service page gives about 1971 for the new tower, and the 2009 article gives both 1971 and 1974, so the year is unsettled.[^1][^2] The article adds that the fourth tower was later closed and taken down, as were most other Saskatchewan fire towers.[^2]

No tower stands there now.[^2] The 2009 article places the Grassy Lake Fire Cache on the former tower yard and says the field officer's quarters and most other original buildings are still there.[^2] That article is the newest source found, so no source describes the site after 2009, and none says whether the cache is open to visitors.
