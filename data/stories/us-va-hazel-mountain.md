The Hazel Mountain Fire Tower lies on the boundary between Dickenson and Russell counties, atop the Clinch Mountain range.[^1] The Register dates completion to August 31, 1939, when the Virginia Department of Forestry finished the 90-foot Aermotor tower.[^1]

A 2008 visit report describes a concrete base marked 8/31/49, which does not match the 1939 completion date. The sources read do not explain the difference.[^1]

The sources describe no notable fires. Visit reports from 2008 and 2018 both say the tower was not staffed.[^1]

Forest Lookouts reports that a January 2003 auction sold the tower on condition that it be removed.[^2] A 2008 visit report says the writer thought they had read that it was to be sold and taken down, but was not certain.[^1] Visit reports from 2008 and 2018 nonetheless describe the tower as in place, and the 2008 report calls it in very good shape for its age.[^1] The 2018 report says the lower stair section had been removed so that people could not reach the cab.[^1]

The Register lists the administrator as a private owner, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^1][^3] The Register accepted it on January 3, 2015, as US 1093, VA 23, nominated by Jeff Flanary.[^1]

The tower stands beside a paved road, but its lowest stairs have been removed and its owner is private. Please do not try to climb it.[^1]
