The cab that now sits at Molson was once on Franson Peak, in Ferry County, Washington.[^1] The first facility on the peak went up in 1934 and carried an L-5 cab.[^2] In the 1950s, a 15-foot tower of treated timber with an L-4 cab took its place.[^2] The lookout was last staffed in 1995, and it hosted an Aircraft Warning Service station from March through October 1942.[^2]

When the current 15-foot tower with a DNR live-in cab went up in 1986, the older L-4 cab went to the Molson Museum.[^2][^1]

Today the cab stands at ground level in the ghost town of Molson, east of Oroville, beside the Schoolhouse Museum.[^1] The sources differ slightly on the drive. Okanogan Country puts Molson Road about 9 miles along Chesaw Road from Oroville, while the Molson Museums say 10 miles.[^1][^3] The Molson Museums say there is no fee to visit either museum, though donations are welcome.[^3] The Schoolhouse is open from Memorial Day to Labor Day, 10a to 5p, seven days a week.[^3] The Molson Museums do not plow access routes during snow events.[^3]

The summit is a different trip. Okanogan Country says a 4x4 can make the drive to Franson Peak, though the Molson cab is the simpler way to see one of these old cabs.[^1]
