Bear Creek Point Lookout is in Valley County, Idaho, at 8,629 feet, inside the River of No Return Wilderness.[^1][^2] Its cab was built in 1934 to the L-5 design, and every source we read gives that year.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The sources disagree on its size. The register calls it a 10-by-10-foot groundhouse, while the Forest Lookouts page describes a two-story, 12-by-12 log cabin.[^1][^2][^3]

Staffing records are thin. A July 1951 clipping, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, says a Charles L. Powell, Jr., of Kennewick, Washington, had taken over the "Bear Creek lookout." The clipping does not say which Bear Creek lookout it means.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts says it does not yet have a documented staffing history for this lookout.[^4] The same page also reproduces a 1945 survey note that places a "Bear Creek Pt." lookout tower and cabin in the Nez Perce National Forest. The register and the Forest Lookouts page both put Bear Creek Point in the Salmon or Salmon-Challis National Forest, so this survey note may describe a different point with the same name.[^1][^3] We found no notable fires tied to this lookout.

The cab was abandoned in the 1950s, and it is still standing.[^1][^2] The register recorded it on November 20, 2008, as US 748, ID 50, nominated by Jill Osborn.[^1]

Today the site is reached only by trail, within the wilderness.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers it, and Idaho Fire Lookouts calls it a hike-in site.[^1][^4] Because the sources describe an abandoned cab on foot, check with the Forest Service about current conditions and access before planning a visit. We found no source about rental.
