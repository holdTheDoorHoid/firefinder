A fire tower went up on Bodmin Hill in 1926, 9.7 km (six miles) east of Big River, in Big River No. 555, Saskatchewan.[^1][^3] The Big River district history describes that tower as a wooden structure, only 11 m (35 ft) high.[^1]

The history says the old tower was torn down at the end of Arnold Fonos's stint as towerman (1963 to 1965), and an 18.3 m (60 ft) steel tower was built in its place.[^1] A Department of Natural Resources annual report for 1965-66, quoted on Canada Lookouts, says the old lookout was swapped for a modern steel tower.[^2] The register's Bodmin entry gives the height as 18.3 m (60 ft).[^4]

Bruce McTaggart took over Bodmin Tower in 1945 and retired in 1962; during his time there he sent daily weather reports to Prince Albert over a two-way radio link.[^1] Stan Brettle took over in 1965 and stayed until 1979, when he retired.[^1] A towermen list gives his years as 1966 to 1978, so the two accounts differ.[^5] Bernice Reimer was towerperson from 1980 to 1998.[^1] John Fonos took over in May 1999 and retired in September 2001.[^1]

The history says there were safety concerns about the towers. In August 2001 all fire towers in the Big River district were condemned, and the towermen were given other duties.[^1] The history lists Bodmin among the district's towers that operated until their closure that year.[^1]

The history places the Bodmin tower of 2001 about 8 km south of Big River, while it puts the 1926 tower six miles east of town.[^1] The two register entries agree on latitude but not longitude: Firelookout.ca gives 53.783, -106.94833, and the Firetowers of Saskatchewan list gives 53-46-58.8 X 106-58-54.0.[^3][^4]

A note on the Firelookout.ca record questions whether the tower is still standing.[^3] None of the five sources cited says what remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
