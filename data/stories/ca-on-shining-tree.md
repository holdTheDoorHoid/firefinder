The firelookout.ca record places the Shining Tree Fire Tower in unorganized North Sudbury, Sudbury District, at an elevation of 459 m (1506 ft).[^1]

The record describes a steel tower with a square cupola.[^1] The tower also appears on a 1977 Gogama topographic map sheet, and on the Department of Lands and Forests Tower Detection System Map of 1963.[^1]

Ontario's first lookout towers went up in the 1920s, and by 1962 the province counted 316 steel towers in service.[^3] Most of Ontario's towers were built between the 1920s and the 1950s.[^2] Those towers were administered by the Department of Lands and Forests, which was succeeded by the Ministry of Natural Resources in 1972.[^2]

Many towers have since been demolished or put to other uses.[^3]

No source here describes what stands at the Shining Tree site today, or whether the public can reach it.
