A lookout went up on Clayton Point in 1933.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The site is in Douglas County, Oregon, on the Umpqua National Forest, roughly 30 air miles east-southeast of Roseburg.[^3][^4]

The sources disagree about the design. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and Firelookout.com describe a 20-foot pole L-4 tower.[^1][^2] The Oregon lookouts history and the Forest Fire Lookout Association describe an L-5 style cab measuring 10 by 10 feet, on a 20-foot round timber tower.[^3][^4] The Association adds that the legs look a little heavier than the standard 20-foot pole legs of lookouts from later in the 1930s.[^4]

Panorama photos were taken at the site on July 8, 1938.[^3] A 1942 photograph shared by the Association shows the lookout almost ten years after it was built.[^4]

The register added the site on November 24, 2010, and says the tower was gone by 1958.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same end date.[^2] The Oregon lookouts page marks the tower as removed but gives no date.[^3] The Association suggests that the dense concentration of lookouts in the region probably led to its demise by 1958.[^4]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey station described in 1953 sat 43 feet south of the remains of a lookout tower, on the southern slope of a timbered hill about 13 miles north of Tiller.[^3] That is the only on-the-ground description we found. No source we read describes what is left at the site today, such as footings or a foundation, and none says whether the site is open to visitors.
