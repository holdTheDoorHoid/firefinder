The Christoff lookout was an L-5 cab on the summit of Huckleberry Mountain, just above the town of Greenwater in King County, Washington.[^1][^4] The blog says it was first called Huckleberry Lookout, and that the Christoff name came from a disused ranger station at the trail's base, adopted to avoid confusion with another Huckleberry Mountain Lookout on the Mineral District.[^4]

Sources differ on the year it went up. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both give 1935.[^1][^2] The blog says 1934, and a Forest Lookouts page lists panorama photos taken on August 13, 1934.[^4][^3]

The register says the cab was removed in 1958, while firelookout.com and the blog say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^4]

The Forest Service says Christoff Trail 1171 leads to the site of an old, abandoned fire lookout on Huckleberry Mountain.[^5] The directions run east on State Highway 410 to Slippery Creek Road, then along spur roads, with the last stretch on a gated road for about a mile to the trailhead.[^5]

Part of the route runs through a fresh clear-cut on private land, though the blog says the trail sits on a public easement.[^5][^4] The Forest Service lists the trail as open to motorcycles but not to other off-highway vehicles.[^5]

The blog author reached the old lookout site on September 17, 2012, and found it.[^4] The register says the site itself looks south toward Mount Rainier, and the Forest Service says the climb rewards hikers with the White River valley and Mount Rainier in view.[^1][^5]

None of the written sources we read describes what, if anything, remains of the cab.
