The Batsto Manor House Lookout is a cab on the roof of the iron master's mansion at Batsto Village State Historic Site, in Burlington County, New Jersey.[^1][^4] The register says the New Jersey Forest Fire Service noticed the imposing mansion while it was choosing lookout sites. For many years before that, people had watched for fires from the mansion's widow's walk during dry spells.[^1]

The register dates the enclosed wooden cab to 1919.[^1] Yet a state annual report for 1917-18 already refers to a lookout station at Batsto, and the 1919-20 report says a lookout room on the mansion's tower had been in service since April 1.[^4] The 1919 date may therefore refer only to the enclosed cab.[^1][^4]

The sources do not agree on the cab's size. The register gives 9 feet by 9 feet for the mansion cab, but its Batsto Station entry describes a 10-by-10-foot wooden cab added in 1919.[^1][^2] A newspaper item quoted on a lookout history site describes a glass-sided room about eight feet square.[^4]

Julia Cramer got the job.[^3] A Batsto history article says the state fire warden advertised the post in 1921, and that Julia, then 19, was determined to get it.[^3] A newspaper of the time said she was the state's only woman in a post of that kind.[^4] In June 1921, a newspaper reported that a girl lookout's alertness had kept a small forest fire from reaching the Amatol arsenal reservation.[^4] The Batsto history article also says how long she served is not known.[^3]

According to the register, the cab was in use for fire detection until 1924, when the nearby Batsto Fire Tower was built.[^1] The station entry says that tower went into service in 1924.[^2] The sources I read do not say what became of the mansion cab after that.[^1]

The register places the lookout at Batsto Village State Historic Site, which the New Jersey Division of Parks and Forestry administers.[^1] A Wikipedia list of New Jersey fire towers files the Batsto Mansion House Station under its heading for towers in active service, but the list does not describe the cab's condition.[^5] No source I read says whether visitors can go up to the cab, so please ask the site before planning a visit.[^1]
