A short item from the Winnipeg Free Press, dated December 26, 1963 and quoted on the Canada Lookouts website, is the only source we found on Waboose.[^1] It describes the fire tower at Waboose as lying 306 km (190 miles) northeast of Port Arthur, Ontario.[^1]

The item says that for the previous five years the forest ranger at the tower had been a woman.[^1] The ranger at the time was Mrs. Theresa Linard of Nipigon, who had recently been transferred and promoted.[^1]

Getting to work there meant a flight of more than 112 km (70 miles), then a hike of about 4 km (2.5 miles) through the bush. Groceries were air-dropped.[^1]

Most of Ontario's fire towers were built between the 1920s and the 1950s. They came under the authority of the former Department of Lands and Forests, which was succeeded by the Ministry of Natural Resources in 1972.[^2]

No source we found describes the Waboose site today. We cannot say whether the tower still stands, whether anything remains of it, or whether visitors can reach the place.
