The Savannah Lookout Tower stands in Hardin County, Tennessee, and the National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 1768, TN 70.[^1] The Register gives the site elevation as 774 feet.[^1] A Tennessee Landforms list places a Hardin County tower called Bee Rock at 784 feet.[^2] Forest Lookouts also lists a Bee Rock tower in Hardin County.[^4] None of these sources says outright that Bee Rock and Savannah are one structure. The shared county and the elevations, 10 feet apart, make it plausible but unconfirmed.

The Register describes it as a 60-foot International Derrick tower.[^1] A National Register study of Tennessee Division of Forestry lookout towers says the International Derrick Model 1933 and the Aermotor MC-39 were close to identical.[^3] No source we read says why the tower was put on this site.

The Register lists the build date as unknown.[^1] The same study says most of the division's towers went up between the late 1940s and about 1960.[^3] That is a statewide pattern, not a record for this tower, so we have no build year for it.

None of the sources we read names who staffed the tower or which fires it watched.

The Register's site visit of November 25, 2023 found the cab without a roof or windows.[^1] Tennessee Landforms, which has no Savannah entry, lists the Bee Rock tower as standing.[^2]

The Register names the Tennessee Division of Forestry as the agency that administers the tower.[^1] The sources do not say whether the public may visit or climb it. Ask the division before going to the site, and do not climb the tower unless it is open to visitors.
