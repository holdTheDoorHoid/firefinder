The Demond Hill fire tower stands on the highest hill near Raco, in Chippewa County, Michigan.[^1] The register says it sits in the Hiawatha National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1]

The build date is uncertain. A June 1923 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says a new ranger station and fire tower were being completed on Demond Hill.[^2] The register gives the build date as circa 1925.[^1] We record 1923 as the earliest documented date, and the editor should check the difference.

The tower was part of the fire work around Raco during the Great Depression. The register says the tower and the Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Raco split fire suppression duties.[^1] A May 1940 newspaper item said CCC enrollees from that camp would man the tower.[^2] By 1942 Raco was the district's last camp, and the register puts the area it protected at 151,140 acres.[^1]

The register added the tower on May 22, 2022, as US 1576, MI 7.[^3] Its page includes a 2021 photograph of the tower, the most recent evidence we found.[^1] We could not confirm its condition or current use, or whether visitors can reach or climb it. Check with the Hiawatha National Forest before visiting, and do not climb the tower unless it is clearly open to the public.
