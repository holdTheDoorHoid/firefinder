Love's Lookout takes its name from Wesley Love, a Jacksonville resident who bought much of this ridge in 1904 and planted a 600-acre peach orchard on it.[^2] After Love died in 1925, his widow gave 22 acres of the property to the state of Texas, hoping it would become a state park, but the state never built one.[^2] In 1934 the City of Jacksonville bought another 20 acres alongside Love's donated land and combined the two tracts into a city park.[^2] Workers from the federal Works Progress Administration then built out the site, adding picnic grounds, a stone amphitheater used for sunrise services and other events, a swimming pool, and a dance pavilion, much of it faced in local Cherokee County red rock.[^2]

The fire tower came soon after. The Texas Forest Service put up a 125-foot steel Aermotor tower topped with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab in 1935.[^1] It watched over the East Texas pineywoods, part of a network that once numbered more than a hundred such towers across the region keeping lookout for wildfire.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register's own entry says the tower stayed in service until 1970;[^1] a Texas Escapes history of the site instead says it kept working into the 1980s, until spotter planes made ground-based lookouts unnecessary.[^2] The two accounts do not agree on when the tower's fire-watching days ended.

The tower still stands on the hilltop today, owned by the City of Jacksonville, and the ridge remains a public park.[^1][^2] It was entered into the National Historic Lookout Register on May 30, 1996, nominated by the Texas Forest Service's Henderson office, and was the first Texas lookout on that register.[^1] The structure itself is no longer open to climb,[^2] so visitors see it from the ground, alongside the park's other WPA-era stonework and the long view out over the pines that first drew people to the hill.
