A 66-foot lookout tower had recently gone up on Cumberland Mountain, a short distance north of Ewing in Lee County, Virginia, according to a July 1938 newspaper report that Forest Lookouts reprints.[^1] The tower was a prefabricated wooden design, assembled from blueprints.[^1] The local branch of the U.S. Forestry Service built it in cooperation with the Tennessee Valley Authority.[^1] A Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Jonesville had already cut a mountain trail up to the site.[^1]

An 8-by-8-foot cabin sat on top, and the report expected a view of about 20 miles on a clear day.[^1] The state owned the tower and planned to appoint a lookout to watch for forest fires.[^1] Earlier, the summit had served federal geodetic surveys, and its signal lights could be seen in Kentucky and Tennessee.[^1]

Forest Lookouts marks the tower as removed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list shows it as gone.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on Cumberland Mountain today, and none says whether the spot is open to visitors.
