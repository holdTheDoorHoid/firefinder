Sunday Peak, the high point of the Greenhorn Mountains, rises to 8,295 feet.[^1] By 1920 the Sequoia National Forest had a lookout on the peak.[^2] A district newsletter that August reported that an air patrol had delivered mail to the lookout in July, and that its keeper welcomed daily mail.[^2] An October 1921 item reports that lightning destroyed the flagpole at the station, which stood 30 feet from the cabin.[^2] SummitPost and the Tobias Peak history both date the lookout to about 1921.[^3][^4] Because the 1920 item already describes a station in use, the sources disagree about when it began.[^2][^3][^4]

In September 1922 a newspaper profile placed Mrs. Jennie Abbott on watch at the lookout and described the small log cabin as hers to care for.[^2] Later items name R. Smith of Porterville, posted there in 1928, and Lawrence Anderson of Springville, there for the summer of 1933.[^2] A 1934 report gives the lookout an elevation of 8,200 feet and counts more than 400 visitors since June 1.[^2] The trail guide gives the summit as 8,295 feet, and the sources do not explain the difference.[^1][^2] In June 1936 the Sunday Peak lookout reported a grass fire near Glennville, and CCC men from Camp Fulton put it out.[^2]

Two sources date the switch to Tobias Peak to 1935.[^3][^4] SummitPost says Tobias Peak was judged the superior vantage point in 1935, and the Sunday Peak lookout was dropped.[^3] The Tobias history says Sunday Peak was considered a hazard and was burned by the Forest Service in 1954, and SummitPost gives the same burning date.[^4][^3] A trail guide dates the destruction of the building to the 1950s.[^5] A June 1936 item still calls the Sunday Peak station a lookout, so the 1935 date may be too early.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^6] The Peakbagging table lists a lookout site on the peak, and its key describes such sites as ones whose building is gone, possibly leaving nothing to find.[^7]

No source we read describes footings, a foundation or other remains on the summit. A trail guide says that once Tobias Peak took over, the Sunday Peak tower was destroyed, and that the peak has reverted to a natural condition.[^5] To reach the top, hikers leave from a trailhead on Portuguese Pass Road and cover roughly 3.4 miles round trip, climbing about 1,100 feet.[^1] A June 2022 trail update reports that the path reopened after the 2021 French and Windy fires, though no official maintenance had been done as of early June.[^5]
