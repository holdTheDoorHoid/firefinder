New York's conservation department put an 80-foot Aermotor LS40 fire tower on the summit of Bramley Mountain in 1950.[^1][^2] The Friends' history spells the maker Aeromotor and says a three-room observer's cabin, a radio antenna and a picnic table went up nearby.[^2]

Observers watched from the tower for about 20 years.[^2] The Friends' history names three observers who served between 1950 and 1970, including Theresa "Que" Aitken, one of the few female fire observers in the state.[^2] A visitor recalled an observer spotting a fire during their stay.[^2]

The state closed the tower at the end of the 1970 season, as aerial detection took over the job.[^1][^6] In 1975 it was offered for sale on condition that the buyer remove it, and Delhi dairy farmer Pete Clark paid $50.[^1][^2][^6] The Clark family took the tower apart and stored the pieces.[^1][^2] They marked the parts for a rebuild that never happened, partly out of insurance worries.[^2]

A local club formed in 1997 to bring the tower back.[^6] In January 2020 a Friends of Bramley Mountain group formed, and the Clark family donated the tower to it.[^1] The summit is owned by NYC's Department of Environmental Protection, which supported putting the tower back on its property.[^1][^2] NHLR says the tower returned to the mountaintop in fall 2024.[^1] The Friends' history gives November 2024 as the completion date.[^2][^4]

A certificate of completion followed in early January 2025.[^2] The FFLA New York newsletter from that January reported an initial soft opening, after "the last piece of paperwork was finally received."[^3] The newsletter had promised a larger ribbon cutting in the spring.[^3] The Friends' site says more than 60 people attended the ribbon cutting, and it has posted video, including the full speeches from the May 2 opening ceremony.[^5]

In 2025 volunteer stewards staffed the tower for 47 days, every weekend and holiday from Memorial Day through Indigenous Peoples' Day.[^5] Stewards open and close the cab and are at the summit from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., weather permitting.[^5] Visitors have climbed it, and on the busiest day of the season 86 people did.[^5]

The Friends counted 2,217 hikers on the trail from June through August 2025, up from 844 over the same dates in 2024.[^5] The trailhead is at 3276 Glen Burnie Road, Delhi.[^5] Pop-up openings are announced on social media, and the tower was added to the state's 2026 Catskill Fire Tower Challenge.[^5]
