The Kettlefoot tower stands on Doe Mountain, inside the Cherokee National Forest near Mountain City, in Johnson County, Tennessee.[^1][^2] The steel tower stands 60 feet, with a square cab on top, and the site sits at an elevation of 3,889 feet.[^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower in 1936, according to the National Register nomination and the Clio summary.[^2][^4] The National Historic Lookouts Register gives 1948 instead, a date the sources I read do not support.[^1] In August 1933, the Kingsport Times reported that a Kettle Foot fire tower would soon go up in the Holston and Iron Mountain area, and a compilation of Eastern lookouts quotes that report.[^5] The nomination describes the tower as an Aermotor MC-39 standard plan, and says the steel carries Carnegie Steel Company markings.[^2]

The register attributes the name to a bear that got a foot stuck in a kettle.[^1] I could not confirm that story from another source.

Clio says the Tennessee Department of Forestry used the tower until the mid-1970s, by which point aerial spotting and 911 calls had made it obsolete.[^4] The nomination says very little history survives and that the Tennessee Division of Forestry holds no tower records.[^2] The National Park Service lists the tower on the National Register of Historic Places, and its NPS record shows a publication date of November 20, 2015.[^3][^4] The register had added it to its own list on September 2, 2014.[^1]

The register's 2021 visit report says the tower was restored in 2019, though the windows are missing.[^1] The state intends to fold the tower into a multiple-use recreation area it is developing.[^1] Clio says trail and tower repairs are still being planned.[^4] Clio also says that Doe Mountain Recreation Area Multi-Use Trail 25 is the sole route to the site.[^4] One 2021 visitor hiked 4.5 miles from the park's North Entrance because the ATVs and motorbikes were all rented out.[^1]

Check trail conditions with the Doe Mountain Recreation Area before you go. The sources do not say whether the cab is open to visitors, so do not climb it without permission.
