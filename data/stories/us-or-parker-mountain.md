Parker Mountain sits in Klamath County, Oregon, and has carried a fire lookout since the Civilian Conservation Corps put up a round timber tower there in 1934.[^1][^3][^4] The Oregon state record says the site was first constructed in 1934 and rebuilt in 1956.[^5] The National Historic Lookout Register says the 1934 tower was upgraded in 1958.[^1]

Staffing records are thin, but a few items survive. An October 1936 newspaper item names John Colvard as the Parker lookout, and the station was staffed for 180 days in 1940.[^3] A July 1934 Klamath Herald story, quoted by Forest Lookouts, credits the Parker Mountain lookout with the first report, in Klamath Falls, of the fire that gutted Dorris, California.[^3] A 1937 account calls the tower's design rather unique and says it was made of long poles.[^3]

The tower that stood before 1997 did not survive the mid-1990s.[^3][^1] Forest Lookouts dates an arson fire to 1995.[^3] The Register and the state record say vandals destroyed the tower in 1996, while Cheryl Hill says arsonists did it that year.[^1][^5][^4] A replacement went up in 1997, built by Steve Barrows Construction Company to an Oregon Department of Forestry design.[^3]

The sources disagree on the new tower's height. The Register, firelookout.com and the state record give 50 feet, while Forest Lookouts and Cheryl Hill give 40 feet.[^1][^2][^5][^3][^4] The Register describes a 14-by-14-foot flat-top cab, and Forest Lookouts gives 15 by 15 feet with a wood cab.[^1][^3]

Today the picture is patchy. Firelookout.com says the tower is staffed every summer.[^2] When Cheryl Hill visited in June 2022, it was locked and not yet staffed for the season.[^4] Access crosses land owned by Green Diamond.[^4] A sign at the gate says the company grants recreational access that can be revoked at any time, and the walk from the gate to the lookout is about 0.7 mile.[^4] The Register says the flat-top cab and catwalk are still in active service.[^1] None of these sources says whether visitors may climb the tower, so ask the Oregon Department of Forestry and the landowner before any visit.[^1]
