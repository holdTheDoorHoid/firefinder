Bee Mountain Lookout is in the Ouachita National Forest in Polk County, Arkansas.[^1][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1938, and the register calls it nearly identical to Tall Peak, which a 2007 update on the register page calls its twin.[^1][^2][^3]

Its design was unusual. According to the register, the tower has a single-story stone base, a wooden cab that people could live in, and a catwalk, and the style was found only in the Forest Service's Southern Region.[^1][^4] Preserve Arkansas, however, says that stone-and-wood design is more often seen in western states.[^4]

The register gives the cab as 12 by 12 feet, as does the Central US Lookouts page, but a 2007 update says about 14 by 14 feet and calls the tower two-story, so the sources disagree on both points.[^1][^2]

The district first planned a stabilization job that would replace the cedar shingles and redo the stairs and catwalk, with help from local volunteers and Forest Service employees.[^1] Partway through, the plan became a full restoration, and the wooden cab was dismantled.[^1] The 2000 ice storm then stopped the work, and as of 2007 only the catwalk had been rebuilt.[^1]

The register page still says the site is on the National Register of Historic Places. The 2007 update says the tower was delisted in 2005 because too few of its essential physical features remain for it to meet the eligibility criteria.[^1] I follow the delisting, since the Encyclopedia of Arkansas names three National Register towers in the state and Bee Mountain is not among them.[^5]

I found no source that says when the tower last watched for fires or who staffed it.[^1][^2] A July 2019 visit report says there is no staff, the first-floor door and both windows are gone, the top structure is missing, and the planks spanning the ceiling seem questionable.[^1]

The register still lists it as a registered lookout.[^1] The same July 2019 report describes a very rough, unsigned path that an experienced ATV driver can manage, and says hikers should watch for overgrowth and insects.[^1] No source I read says the site is closed, but the structure is in poor shape, so stay off it and check with the Mena Ranger District before you go.[^1]
