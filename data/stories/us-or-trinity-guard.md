The Trinity Guard Station lookout went up in 1926: a 68-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6 cab, about a mile south of Fish Lake in Baker County.[^1][^2][^3] The register also lists a log cabin built alongside it.[^1] The site sits on Wallowa-Whitman National Forest land, administered by the U.S. Forest Service.[^1]

The tower had lookout staff in the 1930s and 1940s.[^3] A 1937 item in the Eastern Oregon News said LaVerne Leep was stationed there that year.[^3] In 1942 Tommy Leep was kicked by a horse while on duty, and Carl Rhoda looked after the lookout and the Fish Lake station while he was on crutches.[^3]

In 1946 the lookout was assigned to Bud O'Toole.[^3] That August his car overturned on the narrow road back to the station.[^3] He was not seriously hurt, and Tommy Leep took charge of the station for a while.[^3]

The register says the tower and its cabin were removed in 1953, while firelookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page say they were destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3]

The Forest Lookouts page says some remnants were still visible at the site, though it does not say when that was recorded.[^3] The sources do not say how a visitor would reach the site, and none says a standing lookout survives.
