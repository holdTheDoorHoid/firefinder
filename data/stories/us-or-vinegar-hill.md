Vinegar Hill Lookout stood in the Malheur National Forest in Grant County, Oregon, about 24 miles northeast of John Day, at 8,131 feet.[^1][^2]

The Forest Lookouts page says a lookout was maintained on the hill in 1911, connected by telephone to the central office.[^3] A cupola cabin went up on the summit in 1916.[^1][^2][^3] A U.S. Geological Survey station on the summit was destroyed when that house went up.[^3]

In 1941 an L-4 cab replaced the cupola.[^1][^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts page gives that date too, but a 1971 notice it quotes says the building was built in 1942 and used until 1969.[^3]

The Baker Democrat Herald reported in July 1933 that Charles Arrington would work as the ranger at the lookout that summer.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry and Firelookout.com both say the L-4 cab was removed in 1971.[^1][^2] In September 1971 the Malheur National Forest sought bids on five structures in the Bates-Prairie City area.[^3] It described this one as 14 feet square and set on the ground rather than on a tower, and said aerial observation had replaced the manned fire-prevention station.[^3]

A 1936 station description, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the survey station sits at the center of the lookout house.[^3] The National Geodetic Survey listing for that station carries the code for a surface mark reported destroyed.[^4]

No source we read describes what remains of the lookout itself, or whether the summit is open to visitors.
