Mount Electra rises 2,260 feet in the Town of Webb, Herkimer County, and was once known as Rock Lake Mountain.[^1] The Webb Estate built a steel Aermotor LS40 lookout, about 60 feet tall, on the mountain around 1920.[^1] The New York chapter's list describes the tower as privately owned and says its build date cannot be confirmed.[^2]

The Webb family did the hiring, while the state helped pay the observer under their agreement.[^1] The chapter's list says the tower worked alongside state fire towers when fire danger ran high.[^2] Harriet Rega was engaged by the State and Nehasane Park to staff the mountain from 1924 to 1930.[^2] In 1942, the state covered at least part of one observer's pay, according to Department records.[^2]

By the late 1970s the mountain and tower had become part of the New York State Forest Preserve, and the land was placed in the Five Ponds Wilderness Area.[^1] That designation classed the tower as a non-conforming structure.[^1] The tower was taken apart in 1989 and left on the summit.[^1] The chapter's list agrees that it was dismantled and left in place, and the county list marks the tower gone.[^2][^3]

No source read describes what, if anything, stands on the summit now. The peak is part of the New York State Forest Preserve, yet no source read describes how the summit can be reached today.[^1]
