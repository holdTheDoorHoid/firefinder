Delyle Ridge Lookout was a ground cabin in the Kaniksu National Forest, about five miles south-southeast of Clark Fork in Bonner County, Idaho.[^1][^3] From the site's cliff, visitors look out over the Clark Fork and the Cabinet Mountains.[^2]

The cabin was an L-4 design. Fire Lookout and Forest Lookouts date it to 1935, while Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 1934, so the build year is not settled.[^1][^2][^3]

Forest Lookouts quotes an August 23, 1935 Northern Idaho News item on this lookout's page.[^3] It names a lookout scout, Paul Schravondyk, at the "DeLisle peak ranger station."[^3] The station's name differs from the lookout's, so the link rests on that page.

The cabin was abandoned in the 1950s.[^1][^2][^3] In 1976 the ranger at Sandpoint sold it while clearing the district's lookouts, and only the door, windows and shutters were removed, according to Gary Weber, who first visited in 1970.[^2] Fire Lookout says the cabin was at one point used as a site for hacking falcons, and Weber describes a box built on one side for that purpose.[^1][^2]

The Western Region's 2008 annual report says the cabin collapsed after more than 50 years of abandonment.[^4] Fire Lookout dates the collapse to 2008, under heavy winter snow, while Weber and Forest Lookouts date it to the winter of 2007–08.[^1][^2][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts describes the site as a hike-in, calling the trail beautiful and gentle, and says a visit can turn up remnants of the past scattered around the site.[^2] None of the cited sources describes in detail what stands there today, and none mentions a closure.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
