Big Frog was a fire tower in Polk County, Tennessee, and it shares its name with Big Frog Mountain.[^1][^2] The Tennessee Landforms table gives the tower an elevation of 4,220 feet, close to the 4,224-foot (1,287 m) summit that Wikipedia gives for the mountain.[^1][^2] The mountain lies within the Cherokee National Forest.[^2]

A historical marker near Ducktown describes the forest's towers in general.[^3] It places the peak of fire-tower use in the Cherokee National Forest in the 1960s, and notes that the Oswald Dome tower was one of 17 towers used to spot fires.[^3] It adds that spotter planes were used more as equipment improved, and dates the end of regular tower staffing in the forest to the late 1980s.[^3]

The tower table lists Big Frog as removed.[^1] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.

The mountain itself is open to hikers, and the Forest Service, which manages it as a black bear reserve, presents it as a place for backpacking, day hiking and hunting.[^4] It names Big Frog Trail #64 as the route to the top and says no permit is needed in the Big Frog region.[^4] Groups in the wilderness are limited to 12 people.[^4]
