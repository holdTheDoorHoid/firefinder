Squaw Mountain, also known as Big Moose Mountain, lies in Piscataquis County, Maine, near Greenville.[^1][^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it Maine's first lookout and one of the nation's oldest such sites.[^1] A 2024 report from WABI says it was the country's first fire tower to operate continuously.[^3]

The first tower went up in 1905.[^1][^3] The register says the M.G. Shaw Lumber Co. built it.[^1] Maine Memory says the original wooden tower began operating there on June 10, 1905, and that William Hilton of Greenville was its first watchman. Its photograph, however, shows a later tower rather than that original wooden one.[^2]

A metal tower replaced the original wooden structure in 1919, according to a state report that WABI quotes.[^3] Forest Lookouts quotes a 1919 annual report for a 12-foot steel tower.[^4] The register gives a different date for the tower that stood later. It dates the present steel structure, with its wooden cab, to 1959.[^1]

Watchmen kept a camp near the tower and staffed it for years.[^3] WABI says the fire watchmen kept a constant presence until 1976.[^3] Maine Memory says the lookout was shut permanently in 1968, and aircraft patrols took over its work.[^2] We did not find a source that explains the gap.

The tower came off the summit in 2011, according to Forest Lookouts. WABI says the cab was removed by helicopter that year.[^4][^3] Forest Lookouts dates the airlift to October 11, 2011, and says the Maine Forest Service delivered the tower to the Natural Resources Education Center south of Greenville.[^4] Maine Memory puts the move in October 2012.[^2] The sources disagree on the date, but they agree the tower, or at least its cab, went to Greenville.[^2][^3][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list shows the summit tower as gone.[^5]

WABI reported in 2024 that the cab still stands at the Moosehead Lake Region Visitors Center in Greenville.[^3] Forest Lookouts quotes a 2011 Bangor Daily News story saying the rebuilt lookout would be opened to the public, but we did not find a newer notice about hours or access.[^4] The same list marks the relocated Squaw Mountain tower as standing.[^5] We found no source that describes what remains on the summit, so we cannot say what is left there or whether the site is open to visitors.
