The first Cold Mountain lookout stood in the Krassel District of the Payette National Forest, about 2.5 miles south of Cold Meadows Guard Station.[^1] Firelookout.com puts the site at 8,084 feet.[^2]

The Historic American Buildings Survey report describes three structures there: a privy built in 1934, the tower built in 1935, and a log cabin built in 1936 and 1937.[^1] Pack animals carried the prefabricated tower in.[^1] The tower was a 72-foot steel Aermotor with a metal cab measuring 7 by 7 feet.[^2][^3]

Sources disagree about the tower's year. Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts both give 1934, while HABS and the register say 1935.[^2][^4][^1][^3] HABS dates the privy to 1934 and the tower to 1935, and this story follows HABS.[^1]

The register says Norman Borlaug, who later won a Nobel Prize, staffed the tower the year after the cabin went up, which places him there in 1937.[^3] Firelookout.com lists Leonard Rowland as a lookout in 1939.[^2]

The tower came down in 1994.[^1][^2][^3] HABS says it was taken off the site under a contract that began August 1, 1994.[^1] HABS documented the site in April 1996 and found the privy and log cabin still standing.[^1] The register says a wildfire burned the cabin in 2000.[^3]

The tower was moved to private property near McCall, Idaho, close to the shore of Payette Lake.[^4][^3] A private party restored its top 20 feet in 2007.[^3][^5] The register lists Richard Holm, Jr. as owner and says the lookout was registered on January 2, 2008.[^3]

Apart from the 2000 fire, no source we read describes the original site after the 1996 survey. HABS places it inside the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness.[^1] We found nothing that says what, if anything, is left there now. Because the tower is on private land, please do not visit or climb it without the owner's permission.
