Aldrich Butte sits in Grant County, about 25 miles west of John Day, and it lies inside the Malheur National Forest.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Oregon Department of Forestry built and administers the tower.[^1]

The site was first used as a lookout in 1934, and firelookout.com says the 1934 cab was an L-4 design.[^1][^2] A three-story frame cab with a live-in space went up in 1959, and vandals burned it the same year.[^1][^2] The present lookout was built in 1960 as an identical replacement, to the same standard.[^1][^2] That standard is a three-story ODF flat-roof design with a 14-by-14-foot cab and a catwalk.[^1]

The tower was registered with the National Historic Lookout Register on January 8, 2003.[^1] Beyond the 1959 vandalism, none of the sources we read records a fire at this site.[^1][^2]

Sources do not agree on who staffs it. The register says the Oregon Department of Forestry staffs it.[^1] An undated firelookout.com page says the Ochoco National Forest staffs it every summer.[^2] A forest notice from August 15, 2023 says the Bureau of Land Management mans it, and it lists the lookout apart from the forest's own staffed towers.[^3] That notice is the newest source we found, so it is the best guide for now.[^3]

The register describes the tower as in active service, with coverage of state, BLM and private land.[^1] The 2023 notice says public parking is available at the lookouts it describes, and that visitors are welcome during daylight hours. It asks people to respect the staff after hours.[^3] We found nothing saying whether the cab itself can be entered, so check with the agency before you go.
