Mount Chase, near Patten in Penobscot County, Maine, had a wooden fire tower from 1909.[^1][^3][^4] A one-room cabin for the watchman went up below the summit that year. It served until 1922, when a bigger cabin took its place.[^2]

In 1917 a steel tower replaced the wooden one. The 1917 annual report of the forest commissioner gives its height as 16 feet.[^1][^3] The 1920 inventory lists a 12-foot steel tower instead, so the height is disputed.[^1] Earlier, a 1912 state report had called the station in good condition. In November 1916 a chief warden relayed advice that the lookout house needed a floor and windows.[^1]

The steel tower was taken down in 2001 and moved to the Patten Lumbermen's Museum, where it is now on view.[^2][^3][^4] The museum's home page lists admission rates, but we did not confirm whether the tower display is covered by them.[^5]

The summit no longer has a fire tower.[^4] A radio tower and a communications building stand there, and a metal U.S. Geological Survey marker is stamped into the bedrock between the footings of the old tower.[^2][^3] Concrete corners where the tower was anchored are still visible, and the wooden remains of a lightning shelter lie near the summit.[^2] The watchman's cabin is partly standing: a 2018 blog post describes its interior as dilapidated, while a later hike report says most of the cabin is still standing.[^2][^3]

Hikers reach the summit on a side trail of about 1.7 miles that starts where Mountain Road meets the Shin Pond ATV Trail.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list marks the Chase Mountain site as gone.[^6] We found no closure notice, but the sources do not name who owns the land, so check before you go.
