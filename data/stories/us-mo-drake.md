The Missouri Conservation Commission ran the Drake lookout in Gasconade County.[^1] In fiscal 1950-51 the commission acquired an 11.36-acre towersite at Drake.[^1] In fiscal 1952-53 a new tower was erected on that site, according to the commission's annual reports as quoted on the Central US Lookouts page.[^1]

The Missouri Department of Conservation says that for decades fire towers were its leading way of spotting fires.[^2] In 1966 the department ran 102 towers statewide, and the U.S. Forest Service ran 43 in Missouri.[^2]

In October 1953 a newspaper item, quoted on the Central US Lookouts page, reported that the ranger at the Drake lookout tower had spotted what looked to him like a burning flue.[^1] He hurried to the spot his instruments indicated and found that nearly 10 acres of woodland had already burned.[^1] The paper said careless hunters were thought to have lit a fire inside a large, hollow green tree, and that the tree burned until it fell and started the woods fire.[^1]

No source describes the Drake site today, including whether a tower still stands or whether the public can visit.[^1]
