Haskill Mountain, in Flathead County, Montana, carried a lookout that old newspapers call the Haskell mountain lookout.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com dates the first structure to 1927, a log cabin, and lists a 10-foot wooden tower in 1961.[^1]

The Northern Montana Forestry association had a lookout man at the site by 1936.[^2] In May of that year the Daily Inter Lake reported that Pete Calder would work the Haskell lookout that season.[^2] In July 1937 Calder fell ill and was taken to Kalispell, and Harry Fulmer took over his post.[^2] In June 1938 the paper reported that placing a lookout on Haskell mountain completed the association's fire setup, with Fulmer in charge.[^2] That September the paper noted that no lookout would be sent to fill the vacancy left when the regular guard fell ill.[^2]

Firelookout.com lists Bill Livingston in 1949 and Boone Isbell in 1952 as known lookout staff.[^1] From 1961 to 1967 the Flathead Courier names Eugene Woody at the Haskell Pass lookout. In 1961 the paper places it near Marion, and in 1965 out of Kila.[^2] A 1965 item says rain made an attendant unnecessary.[^2] The Courier calls the station Haskell Pass, and none of the sources says whether that is the same station as Haskell Mountain.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^3] Firelookout.com dates its end to 1997 and does not say how the tower went.[^1] Firelookout.com names the Montana DNRC as the agency.[^1]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether the public can reach it. A visitor should not assume that any structure is still there.
