The Register places the Beardstown Lookout Tower in Perry County, Tennessee, at an elevation of 823 feet (251 meters).[^1]

The Register says the tower was built around 1950 and is administered by the Tennessee Division of Forestry.[^1] Its height is about 73 feet, and the structure is steel, of Aermotor make.[^1] The Register does not say why it went up. A National Register study of the division's fire towers says the Aermotor LS-40 design was widely used by the division until the mid-1970s.[^2] We could not tell from the sources whether Beardstown is that model.

According to the Register, its use ran through the early 1970s.[^1] We found no notable fires tied to it.

The tower's later fate is unclear. A Tennessee lookout-tower list names a Lobelville tower in Perry County and marks it as removed.[^3] The National Register study lists a Lobelville tower in Perry County among the towers still standing on March 26, 2014.[^2] The sources do not show whether either entry describes the Beardstown tower. The Register's photo of the tower is dated March 2018.[^1]

We found no public access information for the site. The Register notes that two modern communications towers share the ground.[^1] Anyone interested should ask the Tennessee Division of Forestry before going there.
