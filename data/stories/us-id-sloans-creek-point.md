Sloans Creek Point, also called Sloans Point, stood in the Payette National Forest, 14 miles southeast of McCall, Idaho.[^1][^2] The Register dates it to 1933 and says wagons brought in its materials.[^2] The lookout had a log cabin residence and a 72-foot Aermotor tower with a metal cab about seven feet square.[^2][^3]

Sources give different end dates for its staffing.[^2][^3][^4] The Register says staffing continued into the 1970s.[^2] A marker at the museum gives 1934 to 1970.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the 1970s as the point it stopped being staffed.[^3]

The log cabin was removed in 1994.[^2] In 2000, volunteers took the tower apart and rebuilt its cab and 10 feet of tower at the Central Idaho Historical Museum in McCall.[^2][^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 2008 as the year it joined the National Historic Lookout Register.[^3]

Today the rebuilt lookout stands on the museum grounds in McCall.[^4] A McCall visitor listing names a cab and tower from Sloan Point among the museum's exhibits, while the Register describes the rebuilt part as the cab and 10 feet of tower.[^5][^2] The museum says private tours of its grounds run through October 15, with at least three days' notice.[^6] No source we read says whether the cab can be entered, so ask the museum before you visit. No source describes what, if anything, remains at the original site today.
