Mount Emily stands about seven miles northeast of Brookings in Curry County, Oregon.[^3] A telephone line was built up the mountain in 1917, and Mrs. Nellie W. Milbury kept watch on Mount Emily during the 1919 season.[^2]

Sources disagree on the first cabin. The register gives a D-6 cupola cabin built in 1922.[^1] A September 1922 Siskiyou Bulletin item said the lookout had not yet been constructed, and a 1923 Gold Beach paper described a new building at the station.[^2] Lightning struck the cabin in 1925, tore two sides off the roof and broke the windows, and the cabin was rebuilt.[^1][^2]

The station got a new Osborne fire finder in 1928, and that July the lookout, Will Lane, cooked a noon dinner for a hiking party.[^2] It was staffed for 100 days in 1941, and the Rauches spent the summer of 1944 on the lookout.[^2] In 1963 Miriam Christensen was the only woman lookout on the Chetco District.[^2]

On September 9, 1942, a Japanese floatplane, launched from a submarine, dropped two incendiary bombs in an attempt to start a forest fire.[^4] Howard "Razz" Gardner, stationed on Mount Emily, saw the smoke and reported it.[^4][^5] A Herald and News account quoted on the lookout history site spells his name Gardiner.[^2] The bombs were dropped from the wrong height, so only a few small fires started, and two men on the scene kept them under control.[^4] According to Wikipedia, no enemy aircraft had bombed the contiguous United States before this attack.[^4]

In 1948 a 20-foot treated timber L-4 tower replaced the cupola.[^1] The register says it was removed in 1973. Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^3]

The U.S. Forest Service built a trail to the Wheeler Ridge bombing site in 1975, and a viewing platform there lets visitors see the remains of the site.[^5] The National Register of Historic Places listed the bombing site in 2006.[^5] No source I read describes what remains of the lookout tower on the summit.
