Bell Mountain stands in the Siuslaw National Forest, in Tillamook County, Oregon.[^1] Crews were still building a trail and a telephone line toward the summit in 1923.[^2] In September 1925 a new tower was being built for a lookout named William Land, and a Eugene newspaper put its height at 70 feet and said it was built from peeled fir poles.[^2] A January 1926 newspaper item put its cost at $406.[^2] The next year an executive order set aside 80 acres in Section 11 for use as a lookout station.[^2] That fall the Bell Mountain lookout was kept open after the other Siuslaw lookouts had been called in for the season.[^2]

The register and Rex's page place a 15-foot L-4 tower in 1935 and an 80-foot pole tower in 1936.[^1][^3] Newspapers from 1934 tell a different sequence: a plan to rebuild or replace the 70-foot tower, and an 80-foot tower with a 10-by-10-foot house on top nearing completion that October.[^2]

Records show the lookout was activated on March 19, 1942, under the Portland Filter Center.[^2] In 1951 a newspaper reported that women worked as lookouts on five of the district's six peaks, and that its only male observer was on Bell Mountain.[^2] In 1958 a Forestry Department pickup went over a 50-foot embankment, and its driver used the truck's two-way radio to call the lookout for help.[^2]

The register says both towers were gone before 1963.[^1] Rex's page carries the heading "R.I.P. 1935-?" and gives no later date.[^3]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^3] A survey description from 1936 places a reference disk 47.2 feet south of the tower's center, but no source says whether that disk survives.[^2]
