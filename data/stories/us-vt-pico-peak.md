Pico Peak, in Rutland County, Vermont, was presented to Mortimer R. Proctor as a birthday present in 1910.[^3] In 1914 Proctor paid for a tower, a cabin and a phone line on the mountain, and the peak then counted as a lookout site.[^1] A state report says Proctor gave the tower to the Forest Service to operate as a fire lookout.[^1]

In 1925 the wooden tower was condemned as unsafe.[^1] The Rutland Daily Herald reported a new steel tower on exactly the same site, with steel parts from the Aermotor company of Chicago.[^1] The paper put the old tower at 20 feet and the new one at 35.[^1]

Dana Rowe was picked to staff the new tower as its lookout.[^1] A 1935 newspaper still had him on the summit, and a 1939 paper named Clark Prior as the attendant.[^1] In 1932 almost all of the glass in the tower's top windows was smashed on purpose.[^1] A 1956 Ogden, Utah, newspaper profiled Caroline Parmenter, describing her as the sole female fire lookout east of the Mississippi River.[^1] The same profile said she had worked for the Vermont Forest Service since 1953.[^1]

The tower's time on Pico ended with a move.[^1] A state report for 1963 and 1964 says renewing the state's lease on Pico had become difficult, so the tower was moved to Killington, a state-owned summit.[^1] Forest Lookouts reports that the structure was taken apart on Pico and set up again on Killington.[^2] WillhiteWeb dates that move to 1963 and says the Killington tower was discontinued in the fall of 1984.[^5] The same state report says the tower had gone up on Pico around 1914.[^1] The 1925 newspaper, however, describes a new steel tower replacing the wooden one, so the sources do not make clear which structure went to Killington.[^1]

The Pico ski area has been part of Killington Ski Resort since December 1997.[^3] A 0.4-mile path, the Pico Link Trail, leads to the summit.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Pico tower as gone.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout's base on the summit.
