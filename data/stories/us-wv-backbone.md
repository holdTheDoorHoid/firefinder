Backbone Mountain, in Tucker County and within the Monongahela National Forest, was the site of West Virginia's first fire tower.[^1][^2] The state built that first tower in 1922, and the national forest later took it over.[^2][^4] A West Virginia tourism guide puts the state's first fire tower at about 1916, which does not match the Forest Service date.[^5]

The tower was used until 1963, when it was replaced by a steel tower named Olson Tower.[^2][^3] The register says the new tower went up on approximately the same site.[^1] The register lists the original tower as removed.[^1] A 1935 photograph in the register shows the old tower and a cabin.[^1]

The Olson Tower stands on the summit today.[^2] No source describes what remains of the 1922 tower itself. The Forest Service says the cab is closed to the public, but visitors may climb to just below it.[^2] Wikipedia says the whole walkway to the top is accessible, which differs from the Forest Service.[^3] The Forest Service says the tower is open when roads are snow-free, while the tourism guide gives mid-April to December.[^2][^5] The Forest Service directions end at a parking lot below the tower, reached along Forest Road 717.[^2]
