Sandy Lake is a steel lookout tower in Alberta, about 30 m (98 ft) tall, at an elevation of 631.5 m (2,072 ft).[^1] The provincial wildfire database gives 2012 as the year the lookout was constructed.[^1]

Alberta's lookouts are either alpine cabins on mountaintops or steel towers on the high points across the boreal forest, sited where the view helps staff detect fires.[^2] The province's forest service grew the lookout network further, especially in the 1950s, and a telephone line had to be in place before a lookout could be permanently staffed.[^3]

The database marks the tower as active.[^1] It lists the access mode as "fly," which points to air access rather than a road. Members of the public may not climb lookout towers, for safety reasons, and the living quarters at a lookout are the observer's home, reserved for private use.[^1][^2] Beyond that record, no source describes the site as it stands today.[^1]
