Vista Grande Lookout went up in 1914 for the Cleveland National Forest.[^1] The register suggests a modified California Plan 4A cabin, or perhaps an original DuBois 12-by-12, on a low, open timber tower.[^1] In 1926 the San Jacinto Ranger District passed to the San Bernardino National Forest.[^1]

The register does not know exactly where the lookout stood.[^1] It puts the spot near the Vista Grande Fire Station, a little to the northwest, and says the later Ranger Peak lookout was not built there.[^1] It also gives no removal date, and it notes that later lookouts went up at Ranger Peak, Barton Peak and Black Mountain.[^1]

The Forest Service's history of its Vista Grande Hotshots says the name dates to the 1930s, when the spot was a California Conservation Corps camp.[^2] The place sits near the small mountain community of Idyllwild.[^2] In 1974 a second Hotshot crew was added, and the Vista Grande Guard Station was chosen as its location.[^2] No source we read says what, if anything, remains of the lookout, or whether the spot is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
