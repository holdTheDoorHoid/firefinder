Elmore Mountain Fire Tower stands at the summit of Elmore Mountain, in Elmore State Park in Lamoille County, Vermont.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^3]

The state built the tower in 1939, according to Wikipedia.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register credits a federal forestry task force, set up after the 1938 hurricane, with the tower, a residence cabin and an underground phone line.[^4] A state report covering 1938 to 1940 describes a 45-foot, 9-inch steel tower paid for with New England Forest Emergency funds, and a cabin being built near its top by the CCC.[^5]

The register spells the county "Lemoille" and gives the elevation as 2,287 feet (697 m).[^4] Wikipedia puts the summit at 2,608 feet (795 m).[^1] The sources do not explain the gap, so the tower's exact elevation is unsettled.

The earliest staffing I found is an April 24, 1958 Burlington Free Press item reporting that Collins Parker had gone to Elmore to work at the Elmore Mountain lookout.[^5] The register says the lookout was in use until 1974.[^4]

The register calls the tower a well-recognized local landmark.[^4] The Vermont Division of Forestry first constructed it, the Division of State Parks now maintains it, and the register listed it on November 24, 1995.[^4]

In the 2025 work, Elmore Mountain's tower received interior cab railings.[^6]

The park has hiking trails that lead to the tower at the summit.[^1] A May 2, 2025 news report says Elmore Mountain would see shorter closures while workers installed railings, with the work expected to run into spring 2026.[^7] I found no rental notice for the tower. The sources do not say whether visitors may climb into the cab, so check with the state park before going up.
