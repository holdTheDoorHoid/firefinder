Spanish Peak sits in the Bucks Lake Wilderness in Plumas County, on land the Plumas National Forest administers.[^1][^3] The register says a lookout had been on the summit since 1907, and that a ground cabin stood there before 1920.[^1] In 1934 a BC-201 cabin replaced the older building.[^1] The new cabin was set slightly southeast of it, and the old one stayed up for a while.[^1]

A Plumas history quoted in a 2012 trip report gives a different sequence: built in 1913, rebuilt in 1922 and modified in 1933.[^3] The register and that history do not agree, so both versions are shown here.[^1][^3]

Newspapers name a run of summer lookouts.[^2] Wm. Konradi was on duty in 1914, when he reported another eruption of Mount Lassen.[^2] L.C. Chase was stationed on the peak in 1920, and a 1924 paper said he had held the post for seven years.[^2] Clarence Woolsey needed pack horses to reach his post in 1930, and a 1932 item calls Jack Woolsey a longtime lookout there before he moved to Pilot Peak.[^2] Eunice M. Olsen was assigned to the peak in 1943, Adele Demes was stationed there in 1947, and Mrs. Jean Rippey spent the summer of 1963 on duty.[^2]

The closing date is disputed. The Plumas history names Doris Rippy as the last lookout and says the station closed on December 2, 1966.[^3] A 1971 paper warned that budget cuts could shut the lookout down, at least for the next season, which does not fit that date.[^2] In October 1973 the Forest Service offered the buildings for sale, on the condition that everything above ground level be removed from the site.[^2] The register says the structures were removed around 1973.[^1]

The 2012 trip report says only some concrete and cable remained, on a small steep hill before a cliff.[^3] The same report says a wooden sign marks the spur to the peak, and that the walk up it is easy.[^3] We found no source that describes the site after 2012.
