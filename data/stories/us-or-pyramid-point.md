Pyramid Point was a three-story fire lookout in the Izee area of Grant County, about 32 miles south of Dayville.[^2][^3] The Oregon Department of Forestry leased the site in 1953 for a new lookout.[^2] Crews from the John Day unit, with help from Prineville, started work on June 2, 1954.[^2] By that summer the building had living quarters and could be used for fire detection, although lightning protection was still unfinished.[^2]

The building was metal-covered and stacked in three levels: a garage at the bottom, living quarters in the middle, and the lookout on top.[^2] The district's annual report put the labor at $1,207 and the materials at $2,142, a total of $3,349.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register dates the lookout to 1954 and records its registration in 2011.[^1]

The station was manned for the 1954 season.[^2] A hailstorm in 1957 left dents in the aluminum siding, some up to two inches across.[^2] In 1964 four fires were reported from the station.[^2]

According to the register, the lookout was put to emergency use in 1971 and is now gone.[^1] Oregon Lookouts lists it as removed, without a date.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list also marks it as gone.[^4]

No source describes what, if anything, is left at the site today. The register says there is no public access.[^1]
