Hutch Mountain sits in the Coconino National Forest, southeast of Flagstaff.[^3] The first lookout there was a wooden tower from the early 1920s.[^1] In September 1935 the forest supervisor asked to put a tower on Hutch Mountain in place of the one on Mahan, because Hutch is higher and sees more country.[^2] A later memo said a 30-foot structure would be ample.[^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the present tower in 1936, replacing the wooden one.[^1][^3]

The register describes a 31-foot Aermotor MC-24 tower with a 12-by-12-foot wooden cab, built without a catwalk.[^1] The 1935 plans called for a 30-foot tower, so the figures differ slightly. This story uses the register's 31 feet.[^1][^2] A steel catwalk went up in the fall of 2008.[^2]

I found no notable fire at the lookout in these sources. A 2018 travel blog says the tower still works as a major manned viewpoint for fires in the Flagstaff area.[^5] An Arizona Highways article says a lookout on duty will likely give visitors permission to climb the tower.[^3]

The register listed the lookout on March 15, 2006.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a standing tower.[^6] I found no record of a move, a burn or a closure in the sources I read.

Getting there means leaving Forest Highway 3, also called Lake Mary Road, and turning onto Forest Road 135B for the summit.[^4][^3] The road is typically open from late spring to autumn and closed in winter by snow.[^4] Do not climb without permission from the lookout on duty.[^3]
