The Los Angeles County Forestry Department built a fire lookout on Oat Mountain in 1923.[^1][^3] It is the highest peak in the Santa Susana Mountains and overlooks the San Fernando, Santa Clarita and Simi valleys.[^1]

An August 1923 newspaper account describes a standard Moreland Road Runner truck hauling the 3,000-pound steel observation tower up a five-mile grade to the summit.[^2] The register describes that first tower as a 30-foot X-brace steel structure with an 8-by-8 observation cab. The Angeles guide says it was 36 feet high.[^3][^1]

In February 1924 a newspaper said F. A. Graves was serving as towerman, and linked the early staffing to a run of fires.[^2] Victor Oliver Lopez is named as the lookout who stayed the longest, from 1924 to 1941.[^1] In the Second World War the site was manned around the clock for aircraft warning, and spotters shared the tower.[^1] The guide also names John C. Hofer (1949), Fay L. Willett (1951) and Ray Smith (1963) as later lookouts.[^1]

A 1937 county annual report says work had begun on a thirty-foot steel tower to replace the old one.[^2] The Angeles guide says the replacement was ten feet taller and put the living quarters on one level.[^1] A 1950 county report mentions improvements to the lookout tower and a radio setup.[^2]

By 1951 the Pacific Telephone Company had decided to install a microwave relay station at the site. The Angeles guide says the company took down the old tower and added a lookout room to its own antenna tower.[^1] The register gives the same sequence: a telephone relay tower with a lookout cabin on top took the place of the first structure.[^3]

The lookout was in use through at least 1968, when a brush fire north of Chatsworth burned about 2,100 acres and briefly endangered the tower.[^3][^2] The Angeles guide says the lookout served until 1973, when smog cut the visibility and it was closed.[^1]

The summit also held a Nike missile site, LA-88, built in 1956, with its control area half a mile from the top.[^1] The guide says a few of its buildings remain, in deteriorated condition.[^1]

The lookout is no longer a fire lookout, but its cab still stands. The Angeles guide lists the structure as standing and converted into an antenna tower.[^1] The register says KABC-TV mounted a Doppler 7000 radar dome on the old cab.[^3] The guide says the summit now holds microwave relay antennas, Doppler weather gear and oil wells.[^1] A paved road reaches the summit, and the area lies within Michael D. Antonovich Regional Park.[^1] No source describes public access to the cab, which sits on an equipment site.
