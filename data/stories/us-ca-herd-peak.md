Herd Peak's lookout house went up in August 1933, west of Grass Lake in Siskiyou County.[^1][^2] A local history page built from Dunsmuir newspaper clippings reports that a foreman and thirteen men from the Sims C.C.C. camp finished it in twelve working days, and that it was the first building put up under the president's emergency conservation work program.[^2] The register classifies it as a classic L-4 groundhouse, 14 feet square, with a wooden catwalk.[^1] A 1952 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description gives a single-story Forest Service design about 15 feet tall.[^2]

The register spells it Herd Mountain Lookout, and one 1934 clipping spells the summit Hurd's peak, so the name varies from source to source.[^1][^2]

Earl J. Fain was stationed there in 1934, and once the season closed he and his wife moved to Dunsmuir for the winter.[^2] Bessie Cates spent four and a half months on watch in 1945 and was back for a third straight season in 1947.[^2] Leona Andrus returned to the station in October 1958 after a stint at Ball Mountain Lookout.[^2] The register names Robert D. Clark as the person staffing the tower on Sundays and Mondays from May to October.[^1] An account of a 2019 visit describes Clark as the tower's sole resident. The two accounts differ, and the sources do not say which pattern applies today.[^3]

The sources we read name no particular fire that the lookout spotted.

The register describes the lookout as kept in good shape and close to its original state, and it notes a cell phone tower about 200 feet away.[^1] The register gives April 21, 1999 as the date the lookout was registered.[^1] The sources we read do not record any move, rebuild or closure.

Today the register lists the lookout as staffed on Sundays and Mondays from May to October.[^1] Anyone who wants to visit should ask the Klamath National Forest first and should not climb into the cab uninvited.
