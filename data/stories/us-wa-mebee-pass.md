Mebee Pass Lookout is a small L-5 cab in the Okanogan National Forest, about 18 miles west of Mazama.[^1][^2] The L-5 cabs were prefabricated. The Forest Service typically moved them to secondary lookout sites by horse or mule, and crews staffed them temporarily when fire danger ran high.[^1] firelookout.com considers it the final known L-5 cab to survive.[^2] Sources disagree on the build year: firelookout.com says 1933, while TrailChick gives 1934 for the current structure.[^2][^3]

The sources read do not name a lookout who worked here or record a fire the cab helped spot.[^1][^2] firelookout.com says the cab was likely abandoned in 1941.[^2]

By 2002, two members of the Forest Fire Lookout Association who hiked in found the cab badly neglected, and a helicopter airlifted supplies for emergency repairs.[^1] firelookout.com also records some preservation work that year.[^2] In 2013, John Scurlock, a group of volunteers and Methow Valley District Ranger Mike Liu set about clearing the trail. Crews cut through hundreds of fallen trees, and they rigged a log crossing over Granite Creek, where a bridge had been out for years.[^1] The 2013 chapter report says the work included brushing out at least eight miles of trail to the lookout.[^4]

Work included a new roof, a repaired foundation and walls, and a lightning rod.[^1] The crew had $3,000 in donations, and helicopters flew in 1,100 pounds of equipment.[^1] The chapter donated $500, and the Chuck Butler Memorial Lookout Restoration Fund contributed $200.[^4] The Methow Valley FFLA credits the Friends of Mebee Pass with the final restoration work, done in 2013.[^5] The site was registered on the National Historic Lookout Register on April 12, 2022.[^1]

The Methow Valley FFLA says new shutters are coming, rebuilt from old plans to replace temporary plywood ones.[^5] The group is also working with the Methow Valley Ranger District on a plan to fly the shutters and other supplies in.[^5] The cached post gives only the day and month, so the year of that plan is not confirmed here.

TrailChick lists it as a hike-in and calls the trip to it an adventure.[^3] The sources read say nothing about rentals, current rules for entering the cab, or recent trail reports. Check with the Methow Valley Ranger District before planning a trip.
