Ash Peak sits in Sequoia National Park, in Tulare County.[^1] A fire lookout was in use there by 1929.[^2] A newspaper notice from that August, about the reopened North Fork Road, said visitors would find the trip to the Ash Peak station of particular interest, and a 1932 notice speaks of the tower's completion in 1929.[^2]

The register tells a different start. It dates the site's first structure to 1934: an observation-only cabin on a 40-foot steel tower, with a residence cabin as well.[^1] The sources do not agree on the first lookout, so this story keeps both dates.[^1][^2]

J. G. Wade had charge of the tower for three seasons by 1930.[^2] He then left, and his brother-in-law Homer Cooper took over.[^2] George Magley was the lookout in 1933.[^2] In spring 1934 he was expected to move to a new tower planned at Little Baldy.[^2] In 1931 a truck brought the station's water to the end of the trail, and it was carried the last two miles to the tower.[^2]

In 1938 the Ash Peak station went back into use instead of the newer Little Baldy station.[^2] The old site was judged better for spotting brush and grass fires in the lower foothills, and its cabin and steel observation tower made the work easier to live with.[^2] Paul Ortlipp was given charge for that fire season, and Charles Miller was due to take the post in 1944.[^2]

The register says the structure was replaced in 1966 by a CL-100 cabin on a steel K-brace tower.[^1] It gives no removal date for that structure.[^1]

No source we found describes what remains on Ash Peak today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1]
