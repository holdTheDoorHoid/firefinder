Lewis Peak once held a fire lookout in Shenandoah National Park, in Rockingham County, Virginia.[^1] Willhite's site record gives the site an elevation of 2,760 feet.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a platform tower there, now gone.[^2]

Willhite calls it a secondary lookout. It was a wooden platform with no roof, set on a single tree pole. A fire finder sat on top of that pole, and a ladder of 12 steps climbed to a square platform enclosed by two tiers of pole railings.[^1] Those details come secondhand: Willhite credits a reliable source who had seen a photo in the park archives.[^1]

Willhite's notes cite two old maps. A 1937 map shows Lewis Peak with no trail. A 1965 map marks only a summit trail.[^1]

Willhite has not located the site on any map. He has seen no written account of footings or remnants.[^1]

According to a trail guide, a 2006 fire left the summit open, with views in every direction.[^3] The same guide says the hike is less traveled than others in the park, though its views are spectacular.[^3] The route starts at the Browns Gap parking area on Skyline Drive and runs about nine miles round trip.[^3] The park's entrance fee applies.[^4]
