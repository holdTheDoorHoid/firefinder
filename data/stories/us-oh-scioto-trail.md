The Scioto Trail Fire Tower stands in Ross County, Ohio, and overlooks part of the Scioto Trail State Forest.[^1][^2] It was built in 1925 and is a 60-foot Aermotor tower topped with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1][^2] A historical plaque says fire wardens and foresters watched for fires from the tower across Ohio.[^2]

It belongs to a wider push in Ohio. Ohio's first fire tower went up in 1922, and Civilian Conservation Corps crews helped build fire towers from 1933 to 1935.[^6] The early record of this particular tower is a little tangled. A 1936 newspaper item reported plans for a 25-foot observation tower on Two Mile Hill, open to the public, and I could not tie that plan to the 60-foot tower.[^4] The register and the plaque both date the 60-foot tower to 1925.[^1][^2]

Most of what the record says about daily work comes from old newspaper clippings. An October 1934 item named G. P. Schilder as the towerman at the Scioto Trail Forest.[^4] In April 1938 the Ohio Division of Forestry put a fire weather station at the tower, with a weather vane, rain gauge, anemometer and hydro-thermograph, to collect data for forecasting serious forest fire periods.[^4] In April 1941 a fire that burned nearly 50 acres of Ross County land was contained, according to Harold Lump of the tower.[^4]

The sources describe the tower as still standing. A historical plaque entry says so, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Ohio list marks it standing.[^2][^5] The register entry names the Ohio Division of Forestry and the Ohio Fire Tower Restoration Project as cooperators, but describes no specific restoration work.[^1] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on July 20, 2007.[^1]

A register visit in June 2010 found the cabin open and in good condition, with directions to the tower signed from the road, and recorded that the site was not staffed.[^1] An Ohio Department of Natural Resources map says a fire tower and picnic area on the forest are the sites of many reunions and family outings.[^3] The sources do not say whether the public may climb the tower today, so check with the state forest office before planning a special trip.
