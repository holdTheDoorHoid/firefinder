The Montana list calls this lookout Elbow Mountain, while the local history calls the site Elbow Point.[^2][^3]

In 1926 a cupola cabin with a shake frame went up on Elbow Point, above the northwest side of Elbow Lake. The lake was renamed Lindbergh Lake in 1928.[^2] A 1919 forest fire had left the point with little tree cover in the 1920s, and the site itself commanded a striking view of the surrounding drainages.[^2]

In 1942 the original gave way to an L-4 cabin, a standard design, set on a 30-foot tower.[^1][^2] Before World War II, Swan Valley residents Ed Beck, Hans Monrad and Bob Martin worked at the site.[^2] Husband and wife Henry and Hazel Drenth served as summer observers in 1952 and 1953.[^2] The firelookout.com page also lists Mr. and Mrs. David Devoto in 1957 and Mr. and Mrs. William Hunt in 1958.[^1]

Cousins Elaine and Nola Kauffman worked the lookout in the early 1970s.[^2] In 1971 Nola spotted smoke to the southeast of the lookout and called it in. Someone was sent to check, and the smoke came from a woman burning trash in her fireplace.[^2] On another night, lightning struck a dead snag near Lindbergh Lake.[^2] Elaine spotted the flames and pinpointed the spot, and a ground crew later mopped the fire up.[^2]

In 1977 the Forest Service took the lookout down.[^2] The firelookout.com page says it was destroyed that year, so the two sources differ on how it ended.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the site as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors may go there. An OpenStreetMap point still carries the name Elbow Lookout Tower, but that is a map entry and does not show what, if anything, stands on the point.[^4]
