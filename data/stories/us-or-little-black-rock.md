Little Black Rock lies in Douglas County, Oregon, on the Umpqua National Forest, at an elevation of 4,538 feet.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the lookout to 1930, when an L-5 cab was built, and says that cab was removed in 1966.[^1] Another lookout-history site gives the same two years but calls the 1966 end a destruction.[^2]

A 1935 news item, quoted on a lookout-history site, reports that the telephone line between Little Black Rock and Diamond Rock was out of commission in the same emergency, and that a storm caused static and other interference.[^3] George Norman, the lookout at the Little Black Rock emergency station, kept in touch with Bruce Ferguson at Diamond Rock, about 25 miles away, by portable voice radio.[^3] The paper said radio had been a key help in that emergency.[^3]

No source I read describes what, if anything, is left on the ground today. A Forest Service trail called Little Black Rock, numbered 1562, is a 0.7-mile trail in the Umpqua National Forest.[^4] I could not confirm from a source I read that it reaches the old lookout ground, and none says whether the site is open to visitors.
