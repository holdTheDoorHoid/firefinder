Bacon Point is a low site on the boundary between the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest and the Ross Lake National Recreational Area, in Skagit County, Washington, at 1,710 feet.[^1][^4] A lookout was built there in 1934, and the Forest Service administered it.[^1][^4] The registry and firelookout.com describe a 35-foot L-4 tower.[^1][^2] Willhite says the 1935 panoramas, taken from the catwalk, put the height at 40 feet, but he thinks 35 feet is more likely.[^4]

Panoramic photographs were taken from the site on July 20, 1935.[^3][^4]

During World War II the site was an Aircraft Warning Service station. It was activated on March 21, 1942, and deactivated on October 16, 1943.[^3][^4] Willhite says the service added a sleeping cabin that cost $350.[^4] After the station closed, the Forest Service kept the site for fire detection.[^3]

The sources give 1956 as the year the tower came down. The registry and Washington Lookouts say it was removed, firelookout.com says it was destroyed, and Willhite says the cab was removed or destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Willhite thinks the tower poles probably stood until 10 to 20 years ago, which does not fit the 1956 date well.[^4]

Willhite found the views gone, but relics remain. He describes one footing still above ground and a tower leg that fell between two trees.[^4] The land is National Forest, and he reached the site by an old road and then open forest. He lists it at 1.5 miles with about 1,000 feet of climb.[^4] Washouts make the road impassable a short way past the first switchback, and his trip report mentions no permit.[^4]
