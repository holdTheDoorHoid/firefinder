Charlie Peak, also called Castaic Peak, is a lookout site in Los Angeles County.[^1][^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps erected the tower in 1934, according to the Forest Lookouts history.[^1]

The Los Angeles County Forestry Department staffed the lookout in 1965. The Forest Lookouts page ties that opening to the filling of Castaic Reservoir, and says the U.S. Forest Service required the post while state water money paid for it.[^1] Wikipedia's article on Castaic Dam gives a later date, saying construction of the dam was completed in 1973.[^3] The two accounts do not agree, and we could not settle which is right, so treat the 1965 start and its link to the reservoir with caution.

The lookout closed on September 1, 1971. The page cites a shortage of money, smog and improved communications as the reasons.[^1]

The Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the tower as gone but does not say when it came down.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, stands on the peak today, or whether the site is open to visitors. Do not climb a structure you find there without first checking who manages the land.
