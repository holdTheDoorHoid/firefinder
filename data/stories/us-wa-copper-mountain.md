Copper Mountain Lookout sits deep in what is now North Cascades National Park, surrounded by jagged peaks and broken glaciers. The U.S. Forest Service built it in the early 1930s as a Mount Baker National Forest lookout and wilderness guard station; the National Historic Lookout Register dates construction to 1932, while the National Register of Historic Places dates the current building to 1934, a common split between an earlier post and the structure still standing today.[^1][^2] It's a classic L-4 ground cabin, 14.25 feet square, with a shingled gable roof, prominent lightning rods, and large windows protected by awning-style shutters on each side.[^2]

The lookout was staffed every summer as both a fire lookout and a backcountry guard post, and during the winter of 1943 the Aircraft Warning Service used it to watch for enemy aircraft, a wartime duty shared by many remote lookouts of that era.[^2][^3] When North Cascades National Park was created in 1968, the Forest Service transferred the entire area, Copper Mountain included, to the National Park Service, which has kept it staffed each summer since.[^1] It's one of only three Forest Service-era lookouts still standing within the park.[^2]

Copper Mountain was added to the National Register of Historic Places on February 10, 1989, and also carries a National Historic Lookout Register listing.[^2][^3] It sits well off the beaten path: the NHLR notes it's reachable only by a ten-mile trail, reflecting the remote, glacier-ringed terrain the lookout was built to watch over.[^1] Because it remains an active NPS lookout staffed in summer, it isn't available as a public rental, and visitors should check current conditions and access rules with North Cascades National Park before attempting the approach.
