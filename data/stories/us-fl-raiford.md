In May 1937 The Florida Times-Union reported that a Union County fire-tower site had been secured.[^1] According to the item, the State Board of Institutions gave 10 acres of prison-farm land, and the board approved the location at its meeting.[^1] Plans called for the tower on the farm's west side, on State Road No. 48.[^1]

Reforestation work had reached Raiford Prison before this.[^2] A first-person recollection in a Florida Division of Forestry history booklet says the author's group was given nursery space there for trials of some 21 species.[^2]

By 1932 the booklet counts 22 lookout towers on poles 45 to 70 feet (14 to 21 m) tall, staffed part time by cooperators and Forest Service crews.[^2]

Two tower lists name a Raiford Lookout Tower in Union County.[^3][^4] The Topo Zone listing gives its approximate elevation as 131 feet (40 m).[^4] Our sources do not show that the 1937 plan became this tower, or when it was built.[^1][^3][^4]

No source we found describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can visit.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
