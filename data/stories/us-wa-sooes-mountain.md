The Sooes Mountain lookout was on the summit by 1957, but the sources disagree about where its steel tower came from and how tall it was. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives a 90-foot steel tower with a cabin, built that year.[^1] Eric Willhite says the tower came to the summit from the much lower Neah Lookout site.[^2] The Neah Lookout record describes the same 1957 move and gives the tower as 80 feet tall.[^3] Willhite, citing the Kresek Lookout Inventory, says the Neah tower began as a 1942 Coast Guard tower meant to spot aircraft.[^4]

A 1964 Port Angeles Evening News item, quoted on the Washington Lookouts site, said seven lookouts were slated to be staffed that June, and Sooes was one of them.[^5]

The tower was down by 1973, and the accounts differ on how that happened.[^1][^6] Willhite says it was dropped or fell.[^2] The Neah record says the tower was removed in 1973 and was left lying in the forest.[^3]

Ruins of the tower are still on the summit.[^6][^2] Willhite's photo captions list tower footings and the steps to the tower stairs.[^2] The register names the Washington Division of Forestry and DNR as administrator,[^1] but Willhite places the access on Makah tribal land and advises against visiting without permission.[^2] He adds that the Makah Recreation Pass does not admit visitors to the forest roads.[^2]
