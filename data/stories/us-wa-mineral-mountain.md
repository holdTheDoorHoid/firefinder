A 12-by-12-foot lookout cabin went up on Mineral Mountain, in Stevens County within the Colville National Forest, in 1929.[^1][^2][^3] The Republic News-Miner called it a new lookout site with a view over the low country along the Columbia, between Marcus and Northport.[^3] Two men packed the lumber in on horses over a rough six-mile trail, and by October Mike Moran was finishing the building.[^3]

Newspapers name a lookout man on the mountain in 1928, a year before the cabin went up.[^3] WillhiteWeb describes a lookout camp there that year.[^4] Fred Dahl, previously doing maintenance at the First Thought lookout, moved to Mineral Mountain that June.[^3] In July 1928 the lookout spotted several fires in deserted ranch buildings near Boyds, and two rangers brought them under control.[^3]

The lookout for 1929 was J. Williams of Kettle Falls.[^2][^3]

The sources agree the cabin came down in the 1940s, but not how. Firetower.org says it was removed.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^2] WillhiteWeb says it was "removed or destroyed" and does not say which.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today. A National Geodetic Survey listing, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, still carries the name "Mineral Lookout House." The entry gives that name, a survey ID, the state and county, and a map name, but nothing about what stands there.[^3]
