The Bonheur Tower went up in 1941, about 25 km east of Ignace, on land traded with the Canadian Pacific Railway.[^1] Materials and ranger labour came to $1,500.[^1] The marker explains the logic behind these towers. They were judged the best general means of detecting fires where communication was good, because a towerman who came to know his ground could notice smoke as soon as it appeared.[^1]

The tower stayed in service until 1969.[^1] The marker names four towermen who staffed it in those years: Frank Machimity, Gaylord Rundle, Roger Grouette and John Ross.[^1] A 2010 Ignace Driftwood excerpt quoted on the marker page says the tower detection system began to decline in the late 1960s, when a decision was made that aerial detection was a more cost-effective way of spotting forest fires. By the early 1970s, it says, lookout towers were being taken out of operation.[^1]

The tower did not stay where it was built.[^1] In 1991 it was dismantled and rebuilt at Ignace.[^1][^3] Its original height was about 24 m (80 ft) and its rebuilt height about 20 m (66 ft), according to the marker.[^1] A Township of Ignace consulting agreement, undated in the copy we read, sets a $75,000 budget for dismantling and re-erection.[^2]

The rebuilt tower now stands as a fire tower exhibit on the south side of the Ignace Public Library, and the historical marker is there too.[^1] The marker can be reached from Main Street, which is Trans-Canada Highway (Provincial Highway 17).[^1] No source read describes the original site, about 25 km east of Ignace, as it looks today.
