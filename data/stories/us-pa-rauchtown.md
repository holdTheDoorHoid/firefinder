Rauchtown's state fire tower went up in 1935, built by CCC workers on a mountain a few miles south of the village in Clinton County.[^1][^2] Sources disagree on the mountain's name.[^2] A February 1935 notice says Catfish Mountain, while an April notice says Kalbfleish Mountain. Both put it about two to three miles south of Rauchtown.[^2]

The steel parts were sent from Chicago to Jersey Shore and stored at CCC Camp 127 until the concrete foundations could be built.[^2] The register calls it a steel I-S tower.[^1] An April 1935 paper described it as the latest type of air motor tower, 80 feet high.[^2] It was meant to close a gap in the lookout network, because the land there could not be seen from other towers in that section.[^2]

That spring Frank Aumiller of Jersey Shore was appointed towerman for the new tower. He went on duty for the spring fire season.[^2]

The register says the steel tower was removed in 1973.[^1] A 1955 item describes an 85-foot tower about five miles from Rauchtown, maintained by Ralph Rauch, a descendant of the town's founder.[^2] It does not say whether that was the state tower.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today.
