Heaven's Peak Lookout stands in the backcountry of Glacier National Park, in Flathead County, Montana, about 7 miles northwest of Logan Pass.[^1][^4] Its story begins in 1940, when the Park Service contracted a local builder, Ole Norden, to build it as a Public Works Administration project.[^2] Construction had still not begun by April 1942.[^2] Norden pointed to ill health, wartime labor shortages, rising costs and the Park's failure to finish the trail to the site, and the Park Service released him from the contract in late 1943.[^2]

Actual construction began on July 23, 1945, with an estimated final cost of $4,700, almost double the original contract figure.[^2] The lookout is credited to conscientious objectors in the Civilian Public Service.[^3][^4] The finished cab is 12 feet by 12 feet with a flat roof, a stone foundation and a stone-wall catwalk.[^3]

The lookout was in operation from 1945 through the 1953 fire season, when the Park Service gave it up in favor of aerial detection.[^4] The firelookout.com guide gives the same end year.[^1]

A 2012 news story recounts a man who said he worked on the trail in 1942 and found a lookout already standing, and who guessed it was built in 1940 or 1941. Park records do not support him.[^5] They show trail work beginning in 1939 and a final construction report filed in 1945.[^5]

The lookout was added to the National Register of Historic Places on December 19, 1986.[^6] It was registered on the National Historic Lookout Register on September 15, 1991.[^3] In 2012, park crews and volunteers repaired the roof and floor, a job the Glacier National Park Fund covered with $32,000.[^5] The firelookout.com guide says it was restored that year, while the news story describes stabilization work due to finish the following year.[^1][^5]

As of the 2012 report, the trail to the lookout had long been abandoned, and the building had not been manned for years.[^5] Park planning documents describe a historic structure losing its original fabric through lack of maintenance.[^4] Anyone thinking of a visit should check current conditions and access with Glacier National Park first.
