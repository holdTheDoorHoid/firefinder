Hamilton Mountain is a peak in the Adirondack Mountains of New York, west-northwest of the hamlet of Wells.[^1] Wikipedia gives its height as 3,215 feet.[^1] A 1976 newspaper report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, gives 3,250 feet.[^2]

The Forest, Fish and Game Commission built a lookout on the peak in October 1909.[^1] It was an 18-foot wooden tower.[^1] It cost $694.51.[^2] State fire superintendents had decided to add it to the six observation stations already in the Adirondacks and the three in the Catskills.[^2]

In 1916 a 50-foot steel tower replaced the wooden one.[^1][^2] Wikipedia names the new tower as an Aermotor LL25.[^1] A new cabin went up at the site in 1924.[^2]

Sources disagree about when the lookout stopped being used. Wikipedia says the tower was shut once the 1971 lookout season ended, because aerial detection had improved.[^1] A 1976 report says it had been closed for six years.[^2]

In December 1976 the state took the tower down, because it did not fit the forever wild rules for the Adirondack Park.[^2][^3] Commissioner Peter Berle said it was the first of eight towers to come down.[^2]

Accounts of the take-down differ. The Weekly Express says a strong wind had twisted the tower on its base, so crews lowered it to the ground with cat and guy ropes. They planned to dismantle it there and fly the parts out by helicopter.[^3] Forest Lookouts, quoting The Post-Standard, says DEC crews unbolted two of its legs and pulled it over with a power winch.[^2]

Dates vary too. Wikipedia puts the removal around 1977,[^1] and a 2004 forum post says the tower was flown off in 1977 or 1978.[^4] The December 1976 reports look like the most precise.[^2][^3] The cabin was to stay on the peak until summer 1977, when it was due to be removed.[^3]

No official source we found describes the summit today. A 2013 forum post says the summit has no view now, though a ledge a short way south has an excellent southerly view. It also says an old trail is still visible at the top but is being reclaimed by the forest.[^4] A 2017 trip report says the summit is overgrown with no views, and that the writer found many relics of the old tower there.[^4] A list of New York lookouts marks the tower as gone.[^5]

The tower stood in the Silver Lake Wilderness Area.[^1] That wilderness is forest preserve land.[^6] A 2004 post says the trail is on private property.[^4] A 2017 climber says private property on the west side ruled out that approach, and that the area where the old trail had been is heavily posted.[^4] We found no official source on access to the summit. Stay off posted private land.
