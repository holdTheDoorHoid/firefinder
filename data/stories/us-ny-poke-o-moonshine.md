Poke-O-Moonshine rises to about 2,180 feet in the town of Chesterfield, in Essex County, on state Forest Preserve land.[^2][^3] The site was first used as a fire lookout in 1912, and the state's roster names its first observer that May.[^1][^4]

The steel tower that stands there dates from 1917. It was made in prefabricated form by Aermotor, the windmill company.[^1][^3] The register gives its height as 35 feet, while the National Register description says 40 feet.[^1][^3] The observer's cabin, possibly a Civilian Conservation Corps build from 1936, was burned in 1991 and left only a fireplace and foundation.[^3][^2]

The roster's last observer served the 1988 season, and its notes say the tower was decommissioned at the end of that season.[^4] Wikipedia says DEC closed the tower in 1988.[^2]

After the closure, DEC announced in 1995 that it planned to dismantle the tower, and a local group formed to raise money to save it.[^2] The Town of Chesterfield adopted a resolution in 1997 backing renovation.[^1] Wikipedia says the tower was renovated and reopened three years after the 1995 announcement.[^2] An NHLR photo caption, however, dates a restoration to 2003, so the sources do not agree on the year.[^1] The National Register listed the station on September 23, 2001.[^3]

Today the cab stays shut except when a steward is on duty.[^1] The Friends of Poke-O-Moonshine engage a summit steward every season, and they have been working on the historic Ranger's Trail since 2011.[^1] Many hikers reach the top by one of two paths that start at U.S. Route 9.[^2] The Ranger's Trail, the long-used public route, is about 1.2 miles to the top but climbs roughly 1,280 feet.[^2] Climbing on the cliffs is limited during the peregrine falcon nesting season.[^2]
