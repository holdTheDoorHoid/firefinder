An essay in Our State magazine places a fire tower at the top of Dave's Mountain, the hill above Asheboro in Randolph County, North Carolina,[^2] and gives its height as 100 feet (30 m).[^1] The same essay says the tower was built in 1950.[^1] It is a personal essay rather than a survey record, and we found no other source for that date, so treat it as one account. A statewide list of towers places the mountain at 35.72638 north and 79.82916 west, in Randolph County.[^2] The Forest Lookouts listing files the tower under the state's Division of Forest Resources.[^3]

The essay also recalls the author's father climbing the tower in the early 1950s.[^1] As a Scout earning a merit badge, he was required to spend a week observing a ranger from the tower, and the pair kept watch over the land below for any sign of fire.[^1] A local history blog described the forest fire watch tower at the mountain's crest in 2007, alongside cell phone antennas.[^4]

None of the sources we read describes the tower's condition today, or whether the public may climb it. The essay uses the present tense, but its date is not in our copy, so it cannot show the tower's state now. Check with the state forest service before going, and do not climb the tower without permission.
