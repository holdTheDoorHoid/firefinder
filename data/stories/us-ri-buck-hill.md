The Buck Hill Fire Tower stands in the town of Burrillville, Rhode Island, on land that the state's Department of Environmental Management administers.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists its build year as 1938 and describes a 65-foot steel tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab.[^1]

The story may begin earlier, though. A 1937 study reported in a state annual report found that a Buck Hill site would round out coverage in the area, and the Forest Lookouts site says the Blackstone Valley tower was moved there as a result.[^2] A former-sites register entry for the Blackstone Valley tower, at Durfee Hill, describes a 65-foot steel tower that began operating in 1926 and stopped after the 1937 season.[^3] No source explains the gap between the 1937 move and the 1938 date, so the 1938 date most likely marks when the tower went up on Buck Hill. That is an inference, not a documented fact.

The Forest Lookouts site quotes a 1952 state report that lists general clean-up and minor repairs at the tower among that year's work highlights at Pulaski State Forest.[^2] Visit notes on the Register's page say the state quit staffing its towers in the 1990s and record that Buck Hill was not staffed in 2016 or 2017.[^1] The sources read name no particular fire that the tower watched. The Register's description claims that during the Second World War the tower monitored German radio transmissions and passed what it heard about enemy movements to Allied forces around the D-Day landings. No other source read supports that account, so treat it as the Register's claim.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, with no asterisk, the mark it uses for towers that have lost their cab.[^4] The 2016 visit report says the cab showed no obvious damage.[^1]

The 2017 visit report calls the tower in good general condition, though it needs paint. It notes that some upper stairs are a bit soft, and it lists a rotted cab door, cracked or shot-out glass, a spongy cab floor in places, and a bee and hornet problem. Several antennae are bolted to the railing around the cab.[^1]

The Register's notes say there is no public access and no signed directions to the tower.[^1] Anyone interested should check with the Department of Environmental Management before going, and should not climb a tower that is closed or off limits.
