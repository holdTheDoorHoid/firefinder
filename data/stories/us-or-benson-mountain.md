The Benson Mountain lookout stood about four miles south of Clatskanie in Columbia County, Oregon, at roughly 1,850 feet.[^1][^2] The Oregon Department of Forestry administered it, and the former-lookout register lists it as US 670.[^1]

The register says a 40-foot pole platform and a ground cabin went up in 1934.[^1][^2] A March 1935 note in The Forest Log, quoted by Forest Lookouts, credits the Camp Boyington, a camp set up in late 1933, with a secondary lookout on Benson Mountain.[^3] A May 1942 inspection report describes something taller: a 45-foot tower with a 7-by-7 cab, beside a 12-by-16 ground cabin.[^3] Because that inspection describes the structure in detail, we use 45 feet here.

Mr. and Mrs. George Jones were the observers named in that 1942 report.[^3] A 1943 newspaper item said they were in charge of the lookout, and in 1945 they went back up for the season.[^3] In 1950 Chester Hall of the state fire patrol moved back to the area, and in 1951 he and his wife manned their old post again.[^3]

In 1954 the lookout's duties passed to a new lookout on Clatskanie Mountain.[^3] That replacement was dismantled in 1984 and later rebuilt at Hopkins Demonstration Forest in Beavercreek.[^4] A 2021 blog post says it stands right in the parking lot there.[^4]

The Benson platform and cabin are gone.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts marks the lookout "Removed" but gives no date for it.[^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the Benson Mountain site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2] Because the register names the Oregon Department of Forestry as administrator, that is the agency to ask before going there.[^1]
