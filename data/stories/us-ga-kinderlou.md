The Georgia lookout list has an entry named kinderlou in Lowndes County, placed at 30.79958, -83.35439.[^1] By our calculation from the two coordinate pairs, a separate entry named old kinderlou sits about 1.6 km away, and the list gives it no note.[^1]

A 2012 photo essay says a 54-foot tower was a fire lookout in Kinderlou Forest between 1939 and 1993.[^2] The Georgia Encyclopedia calls the structure at Grand Bay the Kinderlou Tower.[^3]

Today the tower marks the end of a half-mile boardwalk at the Grand Bay Wetland Education Center, near Valdosta.[^3] The essay says Valdosta businessman Harley Langdale, Jr., donated it to the state.[^2] The Encyclopedia says the structure was once a fire tower above Langdale Forest Products' timberland, and that it has since been adapted so visitors can observe the wetland's plants and animals.[^3]

The list's grand bay entry carries the note "Ron GA097-relocated," and GA097 is the kinderlou line, so the list appears to tie the two together. The same list says names for towers 73 and above are the compiler's own.[^1] Taken together, the sources suggest the tower was moved from Kinderlou Forest to Grand Bay, though no source we found dates the move.[^1][^2][^3]

The Georgia Department of Natural Resources page for the Grand Bay wildlife area carries a notice posted 10/8/26 that the boardwalk is closed, with no reopening date given.[^4] The Encyclopedia describes the boardwalk as ending at the tower.[^3] An undated alert on the same page gives gate hours for the boardwalk and tower, but this story follows the dated notice.[^4]

No source we found describes what remains at the Kinderlou Forest site today, or whether that site is open to the public.
