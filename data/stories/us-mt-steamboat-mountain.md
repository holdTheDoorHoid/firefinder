Steamboat Mountain sits in the Lewis and Clark National Forest.[^1][^2] FireLookout.com puts the lookout 17 miles southwest of Augusta, but a 1960 survey description places the summit 13 miles south and 12 miles east of town, so the two sources disagree on direction.[^1][^2]

Work on the site was under way by June 1930.[^2] A ranger named Harris had a crew building a trail around the mountain, so people could reach the new station planned for the top.[^2] A forest telephone line ran from the Elk Creek Ranger Station that summer, and by late August the trail and phone line reached the lookout camp on the summit.[^2]

The cabin itself came later.[^2] The Independent Record reported on December 15, 1933 that a new lookout house had been built there.[^2] FireLookout.com dates its L-4 cabin to 1934 instead.[^1] A 1960 survey describes a white-painted wood building about 14 feet square and roughly 14 feet tall.[^2]

FireLookout.com records the cabin as abandoned in 1973.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list and FireLookout.com both mark the site as gone.[^1][^3]

None of the three sources I read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
