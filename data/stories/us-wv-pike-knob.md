A 2026 guide to the Monongahela's former fire towers says a 90-foot steel tower stood on the site of Pike Knob in the 1930s.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register lists the site in Pendleton County and records its entry on January 3, 2015.[^1] The register gives the site an elevation of 4,290 feet (1,308 m).[^1]

The site sits inside The Nature Conservancy's Pike Knob Preserve, on North Fork Mountain.[^1][^3]

Sources differ on who runs the land.[^1][^2] The register names The Nature Conservancy as administrator.[^1] The Monongahela guide describes the area as jointly managed by the Forest Service and the Conservancy, as a research preserve.[^2]

The register records the tower as removed, though its footings and a concrete staircase are still there.[^1] In 2007 the watchman's cabin was still standing, in poor repair.[^1]

Today the Conservancy says the summit is now a small clearing where a tower once stood, and that the watchman's cabin is reduced to ruins.[^3] The preserve is open year-round during daylight hours.[^3]

Partway up, the old road enters national forest land.[^3] Past about two miles, the road is too rough for a passenger car, so the Conservancy advises pulling off and hiking up.[^3] The trail itself is unmarked and rocky, a 3-mile out-and-back, with the former tower site roughly two miles in.[^2] No tower remains to climb, so a visit means a hike to the old site.[^1][^2]
