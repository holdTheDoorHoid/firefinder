The firelookout.ca record for Mount St. Patrick gives the tower's address as Tweed, Ontario.[^1] A fan site's district notes list the tower under the Tweed Fire District, which they say was formed in 1922.[^2]

Those notes give the tower as light steel, about 24 m (80 ft) tall, and say the district's towers, this one among them, were built in 1922.[^2]

Firelookout.ca describes an octagonal cupola and says the tower could be one of the first 11 steel towers built in Ontario.[^1] Goold, Shapely & Muir of Brantford supplied the steel, and the ground was held on a 99-year lease from Daniel C. Hunt.[^1]

The first observer, James Guiney, reportedly rigged up pulleys and weights to spare himself the long climb. One day a weight came loose and sent him down much faster than he intended, so he gave up the apparatus and went back up the ladder.[^1] The record attributes this account to a 1946 issue of Sylva Magazine.[^1]

Firelookout.ca also notes that the tower appears on the Pembroke topographic map, sheet 31F, third edition of 1977.[^1]

A fan site's list of still-standing Ontario towers, which refers to MNR decisions as of 2013, does not include Mount St. Patrick.[^3]

No source describes the site today. These sources do not show whether any tower, footing or other trace remains, or whether the public may visit.
