Estelle Mountain, between Corona and Lake Elsinore in Riverside County, got a fire lookout in 1935.[^2] The California Division of Forestry and the Civilian Conservation Corps built it, and the cab was a 14-by-14 design on a 30-foot steel tower.[^1][^2]

A November 1936 ranger newsletter item, quoted on a lookout history site, says the lookout spotted a fire eight miles away and sent a crew to a bonfire at that exact spot.[^4] An October 1936 item in the same newsletter says a Forest Service lookout hosted about twenty Scouts in the tower.[^4]

The tower was taken apart and moved to the Hemet-Ryan Air Attack Base, about 1958.[^1][^2][^3][^5] The sources do not agree on the exact year. The register says about 1958, the FFLA history page says before 1960, and the marker and a photo site both say 1958.[^1][^2][^3][^5] The Forest Service started operating at Hemet-Ryan in 1957.[^2]

Today the lookout stands at the air base and still works there as its ground control tower.[^2][^5] A historical marker at the tower says it was built in 1935 on Estelle Mountain and moved in 1958.[^5] The register listed it on December 16, 2008.[^1]

The original mountaintop site is now a preserve, though the two sources name it differently.[^2][^3] A high-voltage transmission line also runs over the spot.[^3]

Because the tower sits at a working air base,[^2][^5] the sources do not describe public visits. Check with the base before going.
