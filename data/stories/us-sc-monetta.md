In March 1936 the South Carolina Forestry Commission bought a six-acre tract on U.S. Highway 1, three miles north of Aiken, for a new steel fire tower. The article describes the tower as still to be built.[^1]

It was to be the fifth steel tower in Aiken County, part of a state program to protect county forests from fire.[^1]

The site was described as the highest point in Aiken County. The planned tower was 80 feet (24.4 m) tall, and a tower house and outbuildings for the towerman were to be built on the same tract.[^1]

The article said the new tower would leave the county well protected for fire prevention, with similar towers already at Wagener, Graniteville, Oskwood and Silverton.[^1]

A towerman was to be named once the tower was up. The article places the tower in the district of Ranger U.G. Ouattlebaum, of the Monetta section.[^1]

The eastern U.S. lookouts record marks the Monetta tower as removed.[^1] Statewide, the Forestry Commission shut its network of fire towers on October 1, 1993.[^2]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the Monetta tower site today, or whether the public can visit it.
