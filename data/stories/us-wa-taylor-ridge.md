Taylor Ridge lies about 20 miles northeast of Republic, Washington, in Ferry County and the Colville National Forest.[^1][^2] Sources disagree on the start date. The register and Rex's page both say a log cabin and a 30-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6 cab went up in 1928.[^1][^2]

Willhite says the site was likely a camp used for fire detection before 1930.[^4] In late September 1930, the Republic News-Miner reported that Robert Foote, the road construction superintendent, was building a tower and cabin on the ridge.[^3]

Willhite describes a 40-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6 cab, and a log cabin for living quarters at its base. The register and Rex's page give 30 feet for the tower.[^4][^1][^2]

In July 1931 a paper reported that Ed Sizemore had finished the tower and cabin and moved to Swan Lake for the summer.[^3][^4]

The Aeneas Creek Fire of 1934 covered much of the Taylor Ridge area.[^4]

In 1942 the U.S. Army used the site as an Aircraft Warning Service lookout, activated March 30 and deactivated August 3.[^3][^4]

The register says the tower and cabin came down in the 1940s. Rex's page says the site was destroyed in that decade, and Willhite dates the abandonment or destruction to later in that decade.[^1][^2][^4]

None of the pages I read describes what remains at the site today.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Willhite says the author had not visited the site.[^4] A 2015 note on that page says the Forest Fire Lookout Association planned to check a recent fire loss that involved the original outhouse and a phone line on a tripod.[^4] The author does not know what the association found.[^4]

Check with the Colville National Forest before you go, because none of these pages says whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
