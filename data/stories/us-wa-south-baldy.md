The summit of South Baldy, in the Colville National Forest northeast of Usk, has held four lookouts.[^1][^4] The register dates the first lookout to a one-room log cabin in 1909. A second log cabin followed in 1915, and a cupola lookout in 1929.[^1][^2] The tower standing now is a 41-foot wooden tower with a 14-by-14-foot R-6 flat-roofed cab, built in 1960.[^1][^3]

In July 1925 a lightning strike in the cabin killed lookout Paul Blickensderfer, who had been sleeping on its floor.[^4] His companion, Orin Blake, was stunned and suffered slight burns.[^4] Two 1925 newspaper reports spell the name differently and disagree about when the storm struck.[^4]

The lookout was staffed most summers until 2010.[^3][^4] In that year thieves took the copper lightning-protection wire off the tower, and it could not be staffed that season.[^3][^4]

In August 2015 a forest fire surrounded the tower.[^2][^4] Crews burned out the area around it as a precaution, and it survived.[^2][^4]

The register's own page marks the lookout as not staffed.[^1] A Forest Service page for the nearby South Skookum Lake Campground, however, says the lookout is still staffed in summer and describes it as a popular day trip.[^5] I could not settle that disagreement from these sources.

Getting there means a rocky road from Usk, and Willhite says high clearance is probably needed.[^3] A visitor's comment on the register page says they took the stairs but could not reach the catwalk, and that the tower needs work.[^1] I could not confirm whether the tower is open to visitors now.
