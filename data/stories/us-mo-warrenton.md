Ozark wildfires often raged out of control until the Missouri Conservation Commission was created in 1936.[^1] For decades, lookout towers were the most effective way to detect fires.[^1] They stood on high ground, where a watcher had a 360-degree view and could see smoke from wildfires.[^1]

In February 1941 the State Conservation Commission announced a new forest protection district run from Warrenton, with a fire tower to be built there.[^2] The commission acquired ten acres for the site that year.[^2]

In April 1942 a temporary wooden tower was going up on the site, two miles southwest of town, where its lookout was to work closely with the one at the Mineola tower.[^2] That wooden tower was 30 feet (9.1 m) tall.[^2]

In August 1951 work was under way on an all-steel tower on Pinckney Ridge that would replace the wooden one.[^2] The new tower's cab floor was to sit 68 feet (21 m) up.[^2] The same newspaper item noted that a picnic table and fireplace had been built for public use.[^2]

In January 1959 Herbert Hurt, of Warrenton, accepted a post as forest towerman and was to be assigned to the Warrenton tower, replacing Norman Smith, who had resigned.[^2] In fiscal 1975–76 the garage at the tower site got new overhead doors.[^2]

In early May 1979 the Missouri Air National Guard moved the surplus tower from Warrenton to Fort Leonard Wood.[^2] The Conservation Department had handed it to the base for gunnery observation.[^2]

No source describes the Warrenton towersite today. The 1979 move is the last record of the tower itself.[^2] No source says whether it still stands at the base.
