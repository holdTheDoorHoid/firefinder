Calamity Butte Lookout stands on the Malheur National Forest in Harney County, Oregon, at about 6,700 feet.[^1][^3][^4] The first lookout cab went up in 1927.[^2][^4] Forest Lookouts quotes Malheur historical notes saying that cabin was built on Bald Butte, and that the point was later renamed Calamity Butte.[^2] That first cab is described as a D-5 design ground house.[^2][^4]

The register describes the cab differently. It calls the wooden, ground-level 14-by-14 structure a "Supervisor Hall Special" built on a revised California design, and an early version of the L-4 model.[^1] The sources do not settle which label is right, but the 1927 cab is still on the summit.[^2][^3][^4]

The current lookout is a 50-foot tower with an octagonal cab. Work began in 1996, and the new tower was completed in 2001. The old 1927 cabin was left on the site.[^2][^3][^4] In September 2004 the new tower, the old ground cabin, a garage and a vault toilet all underwent a visual inspection.[^2] The register lists the site as US 73, OR 10, registered on January 30, 1994.[^1]

Firelookout.com says it is staffed every summer, and Every Lookout in Oregon recorded it as staffed on a July 2021 visit.[^3][^4] No fire at the site appears in the sources reviewed.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The drive to the top is about three miles of rough, rocky forest road.[^4] Because it is still an active Forest Service lookout, check with the Malheur National Forest before visiting.[^1] None of the sources give visitor rules or mention a rental.[^1][^3][^4]
