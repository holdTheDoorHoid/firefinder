Park Ridge Lookout sits at the southern end of Park Ridge, just east of Grant Grove in Kings Canyon National Park, and people have used the spot to watch for fires since 1916.[^2][^1] The first version was an open-air site with a lean-to for shelter.[^2][^3]

A two-story wooden lookout came later. The register dates it to 1929, while the National Park Service's account says 1934.[^1][^3] The Forest Service and the Park Service shared management of the site until 1940, when the Park Service took sole charge.[^2]

In 1964 the wooden building was replaced by a steel tower about 20 feet high, set about a quarter mile southwest of the old site.[^2][^3] The register describes a 13-foot-square cab that went into service in 1967.[^1] Buck Rock and the National Park Service article both give the steel tower as 14 feet square.[^2][^3]

From June 1956 until she retired in October 1974, Mattie Simms was the firewatcher for every fire season.[^2] The Park Service article spells her name Sims.[^3] Staffing was sporadic after that, and the lookout closed for good in 1996.[^2]

The Buck Rock Foundation led the effort to nominate the lookout for the National Historic Lookout Register, working with the two parks.[^3] The register dates the listing to October 19, 2010, and a ceremony at the lookout on August 3, 2013 honored the new listing.[^1][^3] Under a cooperative agreement with the Park Service, the lookout reopened in 2004, and foundation volunteers have covered every fire season since.[^2]

Today visitors can come daily from 10:30 am to 5 pm in fire season, typically mid-May through October, though the lookout may close at any time without notice for fire activity, weather or other reasons.[^2] There is no vehicle access, so visitors hike five miles round trip from Panoramic Point or take a five-mile loop from the Manzanita Trail.[^2] There is no water or toilet on site, and visitors are asked to pack out their trash.[^2] When it is staffed, the cab serves as the firewatcher's workplace and home, and staffing normally runs around the clock.[^2]
