The Tillamook Forest Center is a visitor center in the Tillamook State Forest, owned and operated by the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^2] It sits on the Wilson River Highway in the heart of what was the Tillamook Burn.[^4] The center opened to the public on Saturday, April 1, 2006, the first day of Oregon Arbor Week.[^4][^5]

The tower is a replica, not a working lookout.[^2] The tower was planned as part of the center's design and built for the site, to reflect the wildfire history that helped turn the forest into a state forest.[^5] The Roaming Civic post says the 2006 build was a teaching replica, with no real record of fighting fire on the forest.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it among the replica lookouts.[^1] The blog notes that the association and many enthusiasts would not count it as a true lookout, because it was built for display.[^1][^2]

The tower stands 40 feet (12 m) tall, outside the building.[^3] Visitors can climb it when the center is open, and admission is free, though donations are welcome.[^2] The center's 250-foot (76 m) suspension bridge crosses the Wilson River to the Wilson River Trail.[^3] The Roaming Civic post says the center reopened after the pandemic with limited seasonal hours, and it advises checking the hours before going.[^2]
