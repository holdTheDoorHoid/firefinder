Davis Peak lies in Kittitas County near Roslyn, on Okanogan-Wenatchee National Forest land managed by the Forest Service.[^1] The lookout on the peak was built in 1934 as an L-4 cab set on the ground, and panoramic photographs were taken from its roof that first year.[^1][^3]

Marge Lumsden, a schoolteacher and fourth-generation resident of the Roslyn area, occupied the lookout in 1944.[^1][^3] The register says she put in 16 years as a fire lookout on Davis in total.[^1] Willhite says her last season was 1965, when so much snow fell that she had to be landed on the peak by helicopter.[^3] The Fire Lookout Association's page lists Aaron Jurgens in 1933 and Marianna Kearney in 1964 as known personnel, with no context for either name.[^2] The 1933 entry comes a year before the 1934 cab.[^1][^2]

The lookout came down in 1968.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register and SummitPost say it was removed, the Fire Lookout Association says it was destroyed, and Willhite says it was destroyed or removed that year.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

SummitPost says the lookout no longer stands, and that what remains is "a semi structure that is like a large rock windbreak."[^4] Willhite calls the spot "the remnants of a former lookout site."[^3] No source we read describes the site in more detail.

Directions start in Roslyn, on the Salmon la Sac Road (State Route 903), and end at the Paris Creek/Davis Peak Trailhead.[^3] Willhite calls the trail long, steep and dry, and describes the hike as 5.5 miles with 3,900 feet of gain.[^3]

One web page carries the Davis Peak name but describes Powderhorn, a different lookout about nine miles northeast of Woodland.[^5] Keep the two apart.
