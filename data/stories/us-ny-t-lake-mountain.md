T-Lake Mountain stands in the Adirondacks, north-northwest of Witherbee in Hamilton County.[^2] The state set up a fire-watch post there in 1916, when the Conservation Commission put up a 50-foot Aermotor LL25 lookout tower.[^1][^2] A state annual report from that year says a new mountain station near Piseco Lake was in operation for part of the season.[^1]

The first tower had no stairs, only a ladder up the outside.[^2] Wooden steps were added inside in 1918 or 1919, and in 1931 a self-supporting staircase, a tower within a tower, replaced them.[^2]

The sources disagree about its height. A 1924 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, calls the tower one hundred feet high.[^1] Wikipedia gives fifty feet.[^2]

Emerson Nye was the observer. The newspaper calls him the veteran observer of the post, and says he had been spotting fires there for about ten years when he resigned in 1928.[^1] The article gives his age as 78.[^1]

In 1923 the state built several new observers' cabins, and T-Lake was one of the places that got one.[^3]

The state closed the post at the end of the 1970 season, after aerial detection had made lookouts less necessary.[^2] Wikipedia also calls the tower a non-conforming structure in the newly established West Canada Lakes Wilderness Area. Residents around Piseco Lake asked the state several times, without success, to keep it for air traffic control at Piseco Airport.[^2]

In 1977 Army engineers tested shaped explosive charges on the tower, which brought it down. A few weeks later, the remains were removed by helicopter.[^2]

The tower is gone.[^4] No source we read describes the summit today, says what if anything remains there, or says whether the land is open to visitors, so we cannot say what a visit would show.
