The Missouri Conservation Commission acquired a 40-acre towersite in Bollinger County in fiscal 1947–48.[^1] A Mexico Ledger item from September 1949 described the Grassy and Gipsy towers, both on the Sam A. Baker district, as new, and said they were placed so the commission's foresters could protect the forest from fire.[^1][^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register spells the name Gypsy and lists the site as US 1594, MO 2, registered March 16, 2015, on the nomination of Jerry Lutes.[^3] Missouri's own records use Gipsy.[^4][^5] A January 2006 visit report on the register page says the tower was idle but still kept up by the Conservation Department, and that a locked gate blocked the access road.[^3] By 2018 the department's area plan said the site had historically been used for wildfire detection and for setting up a radio tower, and that its purpose today is to maintain that radio tower for communications.[^5]

Today the 40-acre site is a Department of Conservation area, and the department's page lists its hours as 4:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.[^4] The directions point west from Route TT onto a private lane in the southwest corner of Bollinger County.[^4] The 2018 plan describes the site as mainly a stand of shortleaf pine, plus the radio tower.[^5] No source we read describes any lookout structure, or its remains, on the site today.
