The Salem Fire Tower stands on private land northeast of Salem, in Washington County, Indiana.[^2] It sits outside any state park or forest, though the Jackson-Washington State Forest is close by.[^1]

The tower did not begin on this site. A 1937 annual report from the state Division of Conservation mentions the Borden tower, which the Indiana Fire Towers site places in Clark County.[^3] In 1954 the Indiana Division of Forestry moved the tower to its present spot.[^1] The register quotes an August 1954 newspaper notice. It put the new site about eight miles north of Salem and said the tower would link a tower in Jackson State Forest with one in Clark County State Forest.[^1]

After the move the tower was renamed the Salem Fire Tower. The register says it helped the state guard more than 150,000 acres of timber against wildfire.[^1] It is a four-legged steel tower, painted silver, and the register gives its height as 80 feet.[^1]

Sources disagree on the height. A National Geodetic Survey datasheet from 1947 describes the Borden tower as about 85 feet high, five feet more than the 1954 notice and the register give.[^1][^3] At the old Borden site the tower was gone by 1966, when a survey mark there recorded only the footings remaining.[^3]

Today the Indiana Fire Towers site lists it as standing on private land, with no public access.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association also lists it as standing.[^4] The register was added on August 2, 2026.[^1]
