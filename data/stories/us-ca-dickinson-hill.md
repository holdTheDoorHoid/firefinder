Dickinson Hill was a fire lookout in California, in the area of Stanislaus and Merced counties. Forest Lookouts places it in Merced County, while the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it under Stanislaus County.[^1][^2]

By late June 1941 a lookout station had gone up about six miles east of Montpellier, on the grounds of the former Dickenson school.[^1] Newspaper excerpts reproduced on Forest Lookouts say the station was providing fire protection to the eastern part of Stanislaus County and adjacent Merced County territory during the grain harvest, and that a thirty-foot tower had been erected for it.[^1]

Robert Johnson of Oakland was engaged to staff the lookout under the forestry service and went on duty the Saturday before the June 25, 1941 report.[^1] The two counties' supervisors set aside money for communications with the Montpellier fire station.[^1]

A Stanislaus County board order dated June 10, 1941 committed $200 toward maintaining the lookout, on condition that Merced County match it. A similar order is dated May 11, 1942.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2]
