Sugarloaf Fire Tower stands near the summit of Sugarloaf Mountain, a 1,089-foot hill in the Ozark National Forest in Stone County, Arkansas.[^1] A Civilian Conservation Corps crew built the whole hilltop complex in 1937: a 100-foot steel tower topped by a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, plus a residence for the watchman, an outhouse, and a stone cellar dug into the hillside for storage.[^1][^2][^3] Pairing a working fire lookout with a built residence for the watchman was unusual enough that it helped the site earn historic status decades later.[^3]

The tower is administered by the U.S. Forest Service, with the Sylamore Ranger District as cooperator.[^1] The whole hilltop group was added to the National Register of Historic Places on September 11, 1995, as the Sugarloaf Fire Tower Historic District.[^3] It joined the National Historic Lookout Register on August 20, 1996, as US 186, AR 5.[^1]

No one has staffed the tower in recent memory. Volunteer visit reports from 2012 and 2023 both record it as unstaffed, and both describe a site slipping into disrepair alongside continued use by radio equipment.[^1] In 2012, a visitor found the "locked radio room seems to still be active, other buildings boarded up and first set of stairs has been removed."[^1] By 2023, a visitor reported the stairs still solid but the cab glass gone, with antennas attached to the outside of the cab. The old stone house beside the tower, once boarded up, had since been broken into, and a small cement bunker-like structure dug into the ground nearby may once have served as storage.[^1]

Getting there takes some effort now: a locked gate blocks the roughly mile-long gravel road to the tower, forcing most visitors to walk the last stretch and climb about 500 feet in elevation.[^1] The tower and its CCC-built outbuildings are still standing on Forest Service land, but there is no sign the climb to the cab itself is sanctioned, and the deteriorated stairs and missing glass are reasons for caution rather than encouragement to go up.
