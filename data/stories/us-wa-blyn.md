Blyn Lookout stood on Blyn Mountain, two miles southeast of the town of Blyn, in Clallam County, Washington.[^1][^2][^4] The first lookout was a 90-foot pole tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab and a ground house for living quarters. It was built in 1934.[^1][^2][^3] Weebly dates the start of the work to 1933, when the Washington Forest Fire Association helped the Civilian Conservation Corps build it.[^4]

In October 1934 a windstorm picked up the ground cabin and hurled it against one of the tower legs.[^3][^4] Willhite says the cabin was rebuilt in the spring of 1935.[^3]

The Aircraft Warning Service used the site from 1942, and Willhite says the use ran through 1944.[^3][^4]

The sources disagree most on Olga Hughett's years at Blyn.[^3][^4] Willhite says she staffed the lookout from 1950 to 1965.[^3] Newspaper items quoted on Weebly show her there in 1957, call 1960 her ninth year, and say she had been the lookout for 14 years by 1966.[^4] Those counts do not match Willhite's start year, and I did not settle which is right.[^3][^4]

In 1960 a 40-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot live-in cab replaced the first tower.[^1][^3][^4] A 1966 newspaper item puts the height at 45 feet.[^4] That year Ann Forest spent the summer as the lookout, and Karen Ronning took over after Forest left for school.[^4]

Weebly lists the tower as removed in 1969.[^4] Willhite says Olga Hughett bought the lookout for $138 after it was taken out of service, and that her husband rebuilt its top in her front yard.[^3] The cab and top 10 feet went to Beausite Lake in the 1970s.[^1][^3][^5]

Willhite says the tower was owned by Jefferson County but stood on property leased to the Kiwanis club. The club uses the property as a camp for special needs children, though it did not use the tower.[^3] Later he says the cab went to private property at Beausite Lake.[^3] A relocated-lookouts list gives the current place as Camp Beausite NW.[^5] Willhite credits Irene Potter with a photo dated 2000, captioned as taken after the move.[^3]

At the summit, Willhite's captions say all footings are still in place.[^3] That caption has no date.[^3] He calls the summit a drive-up on good gravel roads with no gates, and says snow or fallen trees are the only things likely to block the way.[^3]

The Register lists the Washington State Division of Forestry/DNR as the agency that administers the site.[^1] None of these pages mentions a closure.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
