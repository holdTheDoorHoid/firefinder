In 1931 the state set up a lookout station on top of a building in Redding.[^1] A May 26, 1931 Searchlight report called it "a lookout station on top of the old high school, or county offices building," where W. J. Hufford had his headquarters.[^1] A canvas shielded the lookouts from the sun in 1931, a 1932 Searchlight report says, but trouble came when the winds rose.[^1]

Lookouts on the building spotted two fires in early July 1931, one on Clear Creek and one in the upper Churn Creek valley, and each burned about five acres.[^1] A July 9, 1931 Searchlight report says the Redding lookout located a 30-acre fire in grass and oak timber near the Parkville schoolhouse on Bear Creek the afternoon before.[^1]

In 1932 a new station 14 feet square went up on the County Offices Building on Placer Street, and it cost $100.[^1] Hufford was lining up his forces for the 1932 fire season, and that June the Courier-Free Press reported that Thomas Boswell of Redding would be the lookout in the new observatory.[^1]

The May 1931 report names two possible buildings, the old high school or the county offices building. The 1932 reports name the County Offices Building on Placer Street and do not mention a high school.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the Redding rooftop site as gone.[^2] No source we read says whether the building or the 1932 station is still there, or whether anyone can reach the roof.[^1]
