Minton Peak, in Montana's Kootenai National Forest, takes its name from Col. David Minton, an Ohio cavalryman who homesteaded near the mouth of Minton Creek and is buried there.[^2][^3] The first lookout on the peak was a 10-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1932.[^2] That structure was later replaced by a new tower, which sources date either to the 1950s or to 1968; the National Historic Lookout Register gives 1968, while the Fire Lookout website describes a treated-timber tower going up in the 1950s, so the exact year of this middle structure is unclear.[^1][^2]

The lookout was staffed into the early 1970s. Fire Lookout's personnel listing names Helen Ahlgren as a staffer in 1967, and the site records 1973 as the year the lookout was last staffed.[^2]

In 2011 the Forest Service completely rebuilt the lookout, putting up a new 5-foot tower topped with a replica L-4 cab.[^1][^2] It was added to the agency's recreation rental program and listed on the National Historic Lookout Register in 2014.[^1]

Today Minton Peak Lookout sits at 5,351 feet and is reachable by car, with the rental season running from late June through mid-October.[^3] It sleeps up to four guests, though there's no water on-site, so visitors need to pack in their own supplies and pack out their trash.[^3] Reservations go through Recreation.gov under the Kootenai National Forest.[^3]
