Mount Buchanan rises a few kilometres northeast of Kaslo, in the Kokanee Range of the Selkirk Mountains.[^2][^3] The first lookout on the summit was built in 1920.[^1] The site takes its name from George O. Buchanan, an American in the lumber trade who moved to the area in 1889 and worked to improve fire control.[^1]

In 1965 a new cab replaced the original. It measures 4.3 m by 4.3 m (14 ft by 14 ft), has a catwalk, and sits on a 5.5 m (18 ft) concrete block tower.[^1]

A 2022 hiking trip report says the lookout operated from 1941 to 1982.[^4] The register entry says it is no longer used for fire detection, but it gives no end date for its operation.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed it on August 15, 1998.[^1] The province later restored it.[^2] A 2014 provincial release said substantial exterior and interior restoration was complete, and that the structure had been rebuilt to current safety standards.[^2]

By then the second floor had been set up as a diorama of a working lookout from the 1950s and 1960s. The ministry, individuals, families and community archives donated many of the old items, including a fully functional Osborne Fire Finder.[^2]

In 2014 the province said visitors cannot go inside, but can look in through windows from the outside catwalk.[^2] The 1998 register entry said it was open to the public in summer months, and a travel site describes it as locked, so the sources differ on how much of the inside a visitor can see.[^1][^5]

The surrounding recreation site has a 2 km viewpoint trail, an accessible toilet for wheelchair users, and two small campsites.[^2]

The sources also disagree about the road and the height. A recreation page says 2WD vehicles can use the road.[^3] A 2022 trip report found it in good shape for a 4x4 with some clearance.[^4] A travel site recommends high-clearance vehicles for the rough last stretch.[^5] The province gives the elevation as 1,912 metres (6,273 feet).[^2] The recreation page gives 1,908 metres (6,260 feet), and the travel site gives about 1,945 metres (6,380 feet).[^3][^5]
