Shivigny Mountain rises about 22 miles east-northeast of Roseburg in Douglas County, Oregon, at 3,782 feet, on the Umpqua National Forest.[^1] In 1933 a tent camp lookout was set up there, with Tom Scanlon as the lookout.[^2]

Work on a permanent lookout followed that fall.[^2] A Civilian Conservation Corps crew was to build a 14-by-14 lookout before the snow came, and by October 23 the exterior was finished, with the interior put off until the next summer.[^2] A later September report gave the size as 10 by 10 instead, so the newspaper accounts disagree.[^2] One listing dates an L-5 cab to 1933.[^1]

The 1948 state report lists a 54-foot steel tower with a 14 x 14 cabin, at a cost of $2,746.[^2] The other listing gives the steel tower as 50 feet.[^1] The tower was first manned in 1949, by U.S. Forest Service lookouts.[^2] The state forester's office had acquired it from an Idaho bombing range no longer in use, and a 1949 newspaper reported that Arthur Rankin, a North Umpqua Ranger District lookout, would live in the cabin on top that summer.[^2]

The lookout was discontinued after the 1977 fire season.[^2] The tower came down in the fall of 1978 and went up again at the Baughman lookout the next year.[^2][^4]

No source describes what remains on the Shivigny site today. The county directory lists its tower as gone.[^3]

The steel tower now stands at Baughman, on Weyerhaeuser-owned ground west of Roseburg.[^5] The register entry calls the lookout in active service.[^4] A 2026 visit report lists it as not staffed.[^5] The two accounts disagree on current use.[^4][^5] Anyone planning a visit should know that reaching the lookout requires a recreation permit for the Coos Bay–Millicoma district.[^5]
