Grass Mountain stands in Lane County, about 12 miles south-southwest of Oakridge, on land shared by the Willamette and Umpqua national forests.[^2][^1] A camp was set up on the summit in the 1920s, and a 40-foot wooden tower with a ground cabin went up in 1933.[^1] Eugene newspapers that August gave the height as 50 feet and then 52 feet. They reported that Civilian Conservation Corps men were at work on the 52-foot tower, and that every bit of material was packed nine miles up the Packard Creek trail.[^3]

In June 1949 an air service dropped 61 pounds of food and supplies to the lookout, Don McChesney, because snow kept packhorses from reaching the station.[^3] By 1967 the Forest Service described the tower as in good condition but not manned, and it wanted to get rid of the structure.[^3]

In 1962 the Forest Service replaced the old ground house and tower with a treated timber tower with an R-6 cab.[^3] Mules hauled about 48,000 pounds of materials to the summit.[^3] Sources give that tower 40 or 41 feet.[^1][^3] In October 1968 a Forest Service engineer said the newer structure might be salvaged for use on Logger Butte, with a decision due by July 1969.[^3] The 1962 tower was torn down by 1982.[^1]

The sources disagree about what happened next. The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register and firelookout.com say the tower, minus its cab, was moved to nearby Warner Ridge in 1986.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page says an attempt to move it failed, and salvaged materials went into a new tower on Warner Ridge.[^3]

We found no source that describes what remains at the original summit today. The Warner Ridge tower, which Recreation.gov lists as Warner Mountain Lookout, is a replica cupola-style cabin on a 40-foot tower.[^5][^4] Firelookout.com says it is staffed in summer and rented in winter. Its road is not plowed in winter, so visitors ski, snowshoe or ride snowmobiles 2 to 10 miles to reach it.[^4][^5]
