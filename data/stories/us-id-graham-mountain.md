Graham Mountain, in the Coeur d'Alene National Forest, got its first lookout in 1922, when a log cabin and a windmill tower with a platform went up.[^1][^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts dates a 30-foot pole L-4 tower to 1930.[^2]

In June 1930, a Spokane Daily Chronicle item reported that lightning struck the station, split its roof and damaged the structure. The same storm also set off five fires, all of them seen from the station.[^3] A late-July 1931 report said a new tower and observatory had just been completed: a 60-foot tower with a 7-by-7-foot enclosed observatory.[^3][^2]

Sources disagree on when the 60-foot tower was built. The 1931 report is the earliest dated account, while firelookout.com says about 1933, and Forest Lookouts lists an L-6 cab added to a 60-foot patrol tower around 1933.[^3][^1][^2]

Named staff include T. R. Peterson, as lookout, and Eric Swanson, as smoke chaser, in 1930. Tilmer Peterson is named in 1931, and Norman Harvey in 1944.[^1][^2][^3]

The tower was destroyed in 1955, according to all three lookout listings.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com also notes a crow's nest patrol on nearby Nira Point, two miles north-northeast.[^1]

The summit still shows traces of the lookout.[^2][^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts describes concrete footings, collapsed cabins and scattered debris near the top, based on a hike in late summer 2022.[^2] A 2023 post on the same site reports that debris from more than three decades of lookout use still litters the peak.[^4]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as hike-in.[^2] A 2016 hiking guide describes a loop of about 17 miles that reaches the summit. It lists the trail as open to hikers, and says camping is possible amid the remains of an old lookout. The trailhead is reached from the Kingston exit of I-90 by heading north on FH9.[^5] No source gives current rules for the summit.
