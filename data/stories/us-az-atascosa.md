Atascosa Lookout stood on Atascosa Mountain in the Coronado National Forest, about 25 miles west of Nogales and four miles north of the border.[^3]

The Forest Service built it between 1930 and 1933 as a 14-by-14-foot wooden groundhouse with a modified hip roof.[^1][^2][^3] A 1989 regional survey gives the year as 1930 or 1933.[^3] The outhouse and a stone cistern were built in 1933, and gutters on the roof fed rainwater into the cistern.[^1][^3]

In June 1944 the lookout on Atascosa Peak reported a blaze.[^3] The center of the fire could not be located, and it was unclear whose land it was burning on.[^3] In 1967 Dorothy Williams worked alone there in a lookout that a Tucson newspaper described as 12 by 12 feet.[^3] According to the paper, she was the only woman doing this job alone in the Coronado National Forest.[^3]

The lookout was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1988.[^2][^5] The National Historic Lookout Register listed it on December 30, 1996.[^1]

By 1989 it was no longer used for fire detection.[^3] That year's regional survey described it as a rest area for hikers, reachable only by trail.[^3]

The register says the Murphy Fire destroyed it on June 6, 2011, and Wikipedia gives the same date.[^1][^2] A lookout history page gives June 5 instead.[^3] KOLD News 13 reported that the lookout had been renovated between 1999 and 2001 and had large windows and a porch.[^4]

The Forest Lookouts page says the fire also destroyed the outhouse, but none of the sources checked says whether the stone cistern survived, or whether the site is open to visitors now.[^3][^4]
