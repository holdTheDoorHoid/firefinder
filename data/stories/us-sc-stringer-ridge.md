Stringer Ridge Lookout stands in a rural part of Anderson County, South Carolina, near Clemson University.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 120-foot tower that may be a Bethlehem Steel model, and it dates the build to about 1936 to 1938.[^1] The South Carolina Forestry Commission administers the site.[^1]

The Forestry Commission's first tower had gone up near Camden in August 1930, six to eight years before Stringer Ridge was built.[^1][^2] Many early towers in the state were wooden, and the wooden ones were eventually swapped out for steel.[^2]

Most tower sites in the state covered about ten acres and included the tower itself plus a year-round home for the operator.[^2] On days of high fire danger, an operator could spend up to ten hours a day in the cab, and starting in the 1960s most South Carolina operators were women.[^2] No source I checked names Stringer Ridge's operators or a particular fire it spotted.

The Forestry Commission ended its fire tower system on October 1, 1993.[^2] The register dates the abandonment of Stringer Ridge to 1993 and notes that the floor and some windows are missing.[^1] Both sources point to 1993, so the tower seems to have gone quiet in the same year the statewide system ended.

The register lists the lookout as US 1145, SC 32. It was nominated by Michael T. Finch, Jr., and registered on November 27, 2016.[^1] None of the sources I checked say whether the site is open to visitors. Do not try to climb it. The register notes that its floor is missing.[^1]
