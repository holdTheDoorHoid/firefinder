Hemlock Butte sits on the Idaho/Clearwater County line in the Clearwater National Forest, at about 6,053 feet.[^1] Sources disagree on when the first lookout went up. The National Historic Lookout Register lists a roofed platform in 1919, while firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts date a cupola cabin to 1921.[^1][^2][^3]

The register describes a D-6 cupola cabin in 1923, then an L-4 cab on a 50-foot pole tower in 1934.[^1] The present tower went up in 1954.[^1][^2][^3] The register and firelookout.com describe an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts calls it a flat cab.[^3] The sources give its height three different ways: 54 feet on the register, 53 feet on firelookout.com and 41 feet on Idaho Fire Lookouts.[^1][^2][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists a known staff member for 1948.[^3] That predates the present tower, so the entry probably refers to an earlier structure on the site. firelookout.com says the tower is staffed every summer.[^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts, in a post dated August 9, 2021, says it is manned during fire season.[^3]

The register says the tower is still in service and was registered on April 6, 2009.[^1] It names the U.S. Forest Service as the administrator.[^1]

Idaho Fire Lookouts calls the site a drive-up staffed lookout.[^3] Because the tower is manned during fire season, check with the Clearwater National Forest before you plan a visit.[^3]
