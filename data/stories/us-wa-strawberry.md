Bill Roe built the first lookout on Strawberry Mountain in 1926, a shake-sided cupola cabin in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1][^3][^4] A September 1926 item quoted on a Washington lookout history page describes Roe living on the peak and milling his own lumber in a one-man sawmill.[^2] Several Washington lookouts share the Strawberry Mountain name, so Hite identifies this one by its position at the edge of Mount St. Helens National Volcanic Monument.[^3]

In 1931 a 15-foot pole tower with a gable-roofed L-4 cab replaced the cupola.[^1][^3]

In May 1964 a newspaper notice quoted on the same history page said that three Spirit Lake district lookouts, Strawberry Mountain among them, would be closed that summer except for possible emergency use.[^2]

The sources disagree on how the 1931 tower ended. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register says it was removed in the 1950s, and its listing page says it was destroyed in that decade.[^1][^4] Hite also places the removal in the 1950s. He notes that a USGS party mentioned the lookout in 1953, and he reports a trailhead sign that gives the lookout's years as 1931 to 1967.[^3]

Today Hite describes cable and possible buried footings on the summit, along with metal, battery cores, nails and glass.[^3] Two of his photos are captioned as views from the lookout site, toward Mount St. Helens and Mount Adams, and he calls the view spectacular.[^3] The Mountaineers list Mount Saint Helens National Volcanic Monument as the land manager and say a Northwest Forest Pass is needed to park.[^5] Hite puts the hike at under a mile from a parking spot on the pass.[^3] No source describes a standing structure at the site today, and none says the summit is closed.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
