The Civilian Conservation Corps built the stone fire tower that stands on the highest point of Fort Mountain, inside Fort Mountain State Park near Chatsworth.[^2][^5][^7] CCC camp 468 was working at Fort Mountain in 1934.[^2] A state report and an engineering firm's article both give 1935 as the year the tower was completed.[^2][^3] The Register gives 1938 and a Forest Lookouts listing gives 1936, so the sources disagree on the build year.[^1][^4] The tower is 50 feet tall, and its original top was a wood-and-glass room.[^1][^4]

The tower served as a fire tower until the early 1960s, when a modern steel tower went up on a nearby mountain.[^2][^3] A 2018 visit report on the Register page answers yes to a staffing question, but the form does not say whether that meant a fire watch or park staff.[^1]

In 1971 the top burned, and the state report says the stone tower then fell into disuse.[^2][^3] The Register blames vandals for the fire.[^1] The state and engineering sources do not give a cause.[^2][^3] The Georgia Trust says the tower lay in ruins for more than 40 years.[^5]

The state restored the tower in 2014 and 2015.[^2] Crews rebuilt the cupola in Southern Cypress, reconstructed the entrance and interior steps, and worked from historic photographs.[^2][^5] The state report says about $189,000 was allocated in 2014.[^2] An engineering firm's article describes a $225,000 restoration project, finished in the fall of 2015.[^3] The sources do not reconcile the two figures.[^2][^3] The Register dates the restoration to around 2018, but a photo on that page is captioned as an April 2016 picture taken after the restoration.[^1]

The Register lists the tower as US 943 and GA 13, registered on May 25, 2012 and nominated by park manager Brian Ensley.[^1] A 2018 visit report answered yes for the shutters, stairs and glass, and said park staff maintain the tower and give guided tours of it.[^1] The park's trail listing describes a one-mile loop: up the West Overlook Trail, then back by the CCC Stone Tower Trail.[^6] The park's main page lists the stone fire tower with a "weather permitting" note, so check before you go.[^7]
