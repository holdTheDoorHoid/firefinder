Mica Peak sits in Spokane County, about 16 miles east-southeast of Spokane.[^2] Its first lookout went up in 1942: a 40-foot wooden tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab, and a cabin at its base.[^1][^3]

In 1944 the lookout got a short-wave radio that linked it to lookouts in Stevens, Pend Oreille and Spokane counties.[^3] On July 15, 1949, the lookout saw smoke and radioed fire crews.[^3] The smoke was seven miles from the tower, where a resident was burning grass and yard trimmings.[^3]

In 1954 a 40-foot wooden DNR tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab replaced the first one.[^1][^3] In 1955 the Air Force acquired 20 acres on the peak and built a radar station there, and the new tower had to be removed.[^3] Wayne Rieper and Paul Anderson had built the replacement, and Rieper was the one who hauled it to Lookout Point, north of Spokane.[^3]

The sources disagree on when the tower reached Lookout Point. The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says 1955,[^1] the National Historic Lookout Register says 1956,[^4] and Will Hite's Lookout Point page says 1976.[^5]

Lookout Point sits on a timbered highpoint just north of Spokane.[^5] Will Hite's page says staffing ran from 1976 to 1992, with the lookout reopened for the 1994 fire season.[^5] The National Historic Lookout Register says it was staffed until 1995.[^4] It is now inside a gated community called River Bluff Ranch.[^5] The guide notes that signs in the last few hundred feet discourage visitors, so treat it as private.[^5]

The Mica Peak summit is a different place now.[^3] Will Hite's guide describes an FAA dome on the summit, and says park maps mark the summit off limits.[^3] Nothing in the sources describes a lookout tower on the summit today.[^3]
