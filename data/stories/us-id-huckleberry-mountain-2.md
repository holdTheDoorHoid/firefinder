Huckleberry Mountain Lookout stood in Kootenai County, about eight miles northeast of Coeur d'Alene, in the Coeur d'Alene National Forest.[^1][^2] The lookout sat at 4,865 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

Around 1920 the site had a crow's nest observation platform on a pole.[^1][^4] From 1928 to 1931 a 60-foot pole tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab was built.[^1][^3] An inspection memo dated October 5, 1928 describes a 60-foot tower under construction, its frame complete. The memo says the tower was normally designed at 48 feet, but the builder raised it to 60, mainly because timber of that length was obtainable.[^3][^4]

Patrol points lay 1.5 miles southwest on Treasure Mountain and 1.5 miles east along the ridge.[^1][^3]

One site account, on willhiteweb.com, says the site may also have had a log cabin, with a cupola, in 1931.[^3]

The tower was removed in 1950.[^1][^2] The willhiteweb.com account says it was "removed or destroyed" that year, so the sources do not clearly agree on whether it was taken down or lost.[^3]

Today the site is overgrown with trees, which has ended the views, according to the same account.[^3] Its writer reached it on foot from Canfield Mountain, several miles west, and says that in summer a far nearer road route begins at paved Fernan Saddle.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as hike-in.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower, or whether the public may visit.
