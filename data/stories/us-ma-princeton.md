The Princeton Fire Tower stands on Wachusett Mountain, in the Wachusett State Reservation in Worcester County.[^1][^2] Its story starts in a hotel. When the state set up a fire watch in 1911, it staffed an observation room on top of the Summit House, a mountain hotel.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites page says the site was Massachusetts's first state lookout, and that the Summit House observation room was its first tower.[^2]

The watch was busy. In April 1912 the station's warden, J. Harry Allen, spotted a fire three miles west of Princeton center, where a barn was struck by lightning and destroyed, with a loss of $300.[^3] A 1915 newspaper item, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, said 485 fires were reported from the Wachusett tower the year before, the most of any of the state's 24 observation stations.[^3]

Observers came and went. In 1923 a newspaper said Mr. Skinner of Princeton would take over from James Maley, a longtime observer whose age made daily watching too hard.[^3] By 1961 daily watches had been halted for three years, though the tower was still maintained on high-fire days. The public could no longer climb the old wooden stairway because of fire hazard.[^3]

The sources disagree on when the Summit House burned. The register and the Forest Lookouts page say 1965.[^1][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites page dates the lookout room to 1911 through 1965.[^2] The same page says the hotel burned around 1970, while a Princeton Historical Society sheet gives December 18, 1970.[^2][^4] A steel tower 70 feet high, with a 10-by-10-foot cab, replaced it on the site in 1966.[^1][^3]

The register's 2012 visit report describes a visitor observation deck that was "Brand new... not yet open," and says the cab will not be open to ordinary visitors. It also says the "current tower" was to be dismantled.[^1] The same register still says the 1966 tower "continues in active service."[^1] No source here confirms whether the new deck opened, or whether the 1966 structure is the one standing today.

An undated Gardner News report, read on Yahoo, says the state runs 42 fire towers, manned every day of fire season and on days when the danger stays at or above Class 3, "High."[^5] That report places the tower in Westminster, while the register names it the Princeton Fire Tower.[^5][^1]

Do not climb the tower. The 2012 visit report says the cab is not for public use, so check with the state before any visit.[^1]
