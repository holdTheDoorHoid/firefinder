Mount Ingalls, at 8,372 feet, is the high point of Plumas County, California.[^1] A lookout station was already on the summit by 1922, when the Plumas National-Bulletin reported that Ed Merry was working there as lookout, near the Walker Mine.[^2] The register dates the tower that stood on the summit to 1936. It was a 10-foot enclosed timber tower built to a California Region 5 plan.[^1] The Quincy marker credits a Civilian Conservation Corps company with building it in 1935, so the two sources disagree by a year.[^1][^3]

Several lookouts are named in the newspaper items quoted on the Forest Lookouts page.[^2] Ed Merry resigned in 1930, and Clark Hart took the post in June 1936.[^2] In 1962 a lookout named Jim Bailey was hurt when a bullet broke apart on the catwalk and struck him in six places.[^2] He stayed at his post until a replacement could be sent.[^2]

In September 1976 the station was closed down, with no reopening planned for the following season apart from emergencies.[^2] In 1984 the structure was taken apart and moved to the fairgrounds in Quincy.[^1][^2] The marker says it was dismantled and rebuilt by a Navy Reserve construction battalion.[^3]

The register, which listed the lookout in 2019, calls it a fire detection interpretive site at the fairgrounds.[^1] The marker stands on the fairgrounds grounds. Access is via Fairgrounds Road, south of Lee Road.[^3] I did not find current visiting hours or a recent condition report, so check with the fairgrounds or the Plumas National Forest before you go.
