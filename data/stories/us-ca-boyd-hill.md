In June 1937 a Civilian Conservation Corps spike camp on Willow Creek was set up to build a road from Hayden Hill that would reach the Boyd Mountain lookout, with a Forest Service telephone line also planned.[^1] The notice calls the station Boyd Mountain; later records call it Boyd Hill.[^1] That summer a guard, G.M. Johnson, was to be stationed there.[^1]

A 1944 project plan describes a lookout with living quarters below it, plus a one-car garage for tools and fuel.[^1] Between 1942 and 1946 the lookout logged just two first discoveries.[^1] A 1947 review said Boyd Hill was "just not paying its way" as a detection point. It said moving detection to Mahogany Ridge should be seriously considered, and that a fire crew setup should replace the station.[^1]

In August 1977 two lightning fires joined into the Gerig fire near Bieber. It threatened the Boyd Hill Lookout, but firefighters stopped it before it did any damage.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page lists the lookout as removed, and the Fire Lookout Association's California list marks it gone.[^1][^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the site today, and none says whether the site is open to visitors. The Forest Lookouts page names the Modoc National Forest as the agency.[^1]
