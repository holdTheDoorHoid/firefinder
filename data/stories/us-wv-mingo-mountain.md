The tower is called Mingo Mountain, but it stands on Horsepen Mountain.[^2] The register says the summit serves as the Mingo-Logan county line in southern West Virginia.[^1] The register places the tower in Logan County, while the Forest Lookouts page is headed Mingo County.[^1][^3]

A 1926 newspaper item said the tower would be served by a state forest telephone line.[^3] The register gives the build year as 1926.[^1]

The tower dates to 1926, but the road came much later. It was not built until a Civilian Conservation Corps camp, P-70 Camp Mingo, was established on July 29, 1935, on Pidgeon Creek about eight miles away.[^2] That camp's main work over its two years was building the road.[^2]

In April 1958 a 300-acre forest fire came within a few yards of the observer's cabin at the foot of the tower.[^3] The county forest protector was out fighting fires, and his wife, on duty at the tower above the cabin, spotted the blaze.[^3]

The register says the tower was staffed until the end of the state fire tower system in 1990.[^1] A former ranger's account gives the closure as December 1990, when all state towers were permanently closed.[^2]

During the author's time as ranger, Norfolk & Western Railway put up a microwave tower on the site.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4] In an Autumn 2018 article, the former ranger reported that the cabin had disappeared, that the tower stood but could no longer be climbed, and that the road now needs 4-wheel drive.[^2] I found no source newer than that article about the tower's condition, so treat its current state as unverified, and do not try to climb it.
