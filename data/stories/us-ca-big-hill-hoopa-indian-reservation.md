Big Hill Lookout stands on the Hoopa Indian Reservation in Humboldt County, California.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the Bureau of Indian Affairs as its administrator and says very little is known about it.[^1] The Register dates the lookout to the 1930s, according to a 1986 survey by Thornton, and it does not give a single year.[^1] It also says Big Hill was the only structure in that survey built by the Bureau of Indian Affairs in California.[^1]

The Register says its design is similar to Green Mountain Lookout in Mariposa County, a 1942 structure built by the state forestry agency.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site lists Big Hill under Humboldt County but gives no history beyond that listing.[^2]

None of the sources read says who watched from the lookout, when it was last used, or what its condition is now. The Register registered it on October 19, 2019, as US 1316 and CA 139.[^1]

The reservation is tribal land.[^3] An 1864 treaty with the United States acknowledged Hupa sovereignty over the land, and the federal government named the reservation the Hoopa Valley Indian Reservation.[^3] No source describes public visits to Big Hill, so do not assume public access; ask the tribe or the Bureau of Indian Affairs before going near it.
