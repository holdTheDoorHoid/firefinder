Red Butte Lookout stands on a lava-capped butte on the Coconino Plateau, in Coconino County, Arizona, south of Grand Canyon National Park.[^1][^3] The U.S. Forest Service administers it, and the Tusayan Ranger District is listed as a cooperator.[^1]

A May 1913 newspaper report described a planned telephone line, about 17 miles long, running to Red Butte.[^2] The butte was to be the principal fire-watch station for the Grand Canyon division of the Tusayan forest.[^2] The sources read do not say when a tower first went up on the butte.

The tower standing there now was built in the early 1980s.[^3] According to the Forest Service, the design departs from its standard CL-100 plan: the cab is 14 by 14 feet, and the tower has a metal catwalk and two water cisterns.[^3] The register adds that the windows are tinted and the cab has a fixed roof overhang that gives shade.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register registered the lookout on May 1, 2001, as US 386, AZ 23.[^1]

The Forest Service says the lookout is manned in fire season, when it helps spot new starts, monitor smoke and relay communications.[^3] The trail to the summit is listed as open and runs 1.25 miles one way.[^3] The register gives the trail as 1.5 miles, so the two sources disagree on its length.[^1] From Tusayan, drive south on US Highway 64/180, turn left onto Forest Road 320, then left onto Forest Road 340, and right onto Forest Road 340A to the trailhead.[^3] The sources read do not say whether visitors may go up into the cab. Because it is an active, staffed lookout, check with the Tusayan Ranger District before planning a climb.
