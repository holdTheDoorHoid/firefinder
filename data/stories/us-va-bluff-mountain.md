The Bluff Mountain fire tower stood on the summit of Bluff Mountain in the Blue Ridge, in Rockbridge County and in the George Washington National Forest.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on when it went up. The register gives 1930 and says the tower looked down on the town of Buena Vista.[^1] The Appalachian Trail history site says the U.S. Forest Service built a tower there around 1917.[^3] A News Leader item dated September 3, 1932, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said two new lookout towers would be constructed, one of them on Bluff Mountain. The item described a planned forty-foot steel tower, with a 14-by-14-foot cabin on top for living quarters and an observatory.[^2][^3]

A 1933 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description refers to the fire tower on the summit and gives the survey mark's distance from its southwest leg as 8.5 feet.[^2] A photograph captioned July 1969 shows the lookout tower.[^3]

The register says the tower came down in the early 2000s because it might harm the nearby Punchbowl Shelter on the Appalachian Trail.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks it as gone.[^5]

Accounts differ on what is left. The Lexington visitor guide says the foundation and steps still remain, while the Appalachian Trail history site says only the footings remain.[^4][^3] Neither source gives a date for its description.

Visitors reach the summit on foot, on a hike the Lexington guide puts at about two miles, reached partly by a short section of the Appalachian Trail.[^4] The same guide notes a side trail to Punchbowl Shelter about four-tenths of a mile in.[^4] There is no tower to climb.
