Bear Mountain Lookout sits at 7,184 feet in the Clearwater National Forest in north-central Idaho.[^1][^2] The site's fire-watch history goes back further than the current structure: a log cabin with a crow's-nest platform was built there in 1922, and that cabin reportedly still stands at the site today.[^1] In 1935 it was joined by a 45-foot pole tower carrying an L-6 cab.[^1][^2]

The building visitors see now dates to 1951: a 53-foot treated-timber tower topped by an L-4 cab with a catwalk, a design the National Historic Lookout Register calls a classic example of the type.[^1][^2] It replaced the 1935 pole tower.[^1]

Records of who staffed the lookout are thin but go back decades. Someone was posted there as early as 1924, and Bud Moore and Owen Johnson — both later written about in accounts of Fire Lookouts of the Northwest — staffed it through much of 1935 to 1938.[^3] Steve Kalling served in 1982–1983.[^3] Beyond those names, no source consulted gives a full staffing record or says when, if ever, the lookout stopped being used for fire detection.

Bear Mountain was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on April 24, 2009.[^1] It remains a working lookout today, listed as hike-in and staffed each summer by the Forest Service.[^2][^3] Reaching it requires hiking in rather than driving to the door, consistent with its backcountry Clearwater National Forest setting. No source consulted mentions the tower being available for public rental.
