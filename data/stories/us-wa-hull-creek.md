Hull Creek began as a wartime post.[^3] Willhite's site says the Aircraft Warning Service built a two-story, 10-by-10-foot cab there in 1942.[^1][^3] A March 1942 service document quoted on that page describes a crew building a low-standard access road to the lookout point, expected to be about two miles long.[^3]

The Washington Lookouts site quotes a 1944 service report that says the station used existing state-owned facilities, with the service adding a 12-by-20 garage.[^4] Willhite says the garage was added the following year.[^3] The register and Willhite credit the service with the cab, so the sources differ on who first built the site.[^1][^3][^4]

After the war the site was used for fire detection.[^3] Nellie and Clair Smith worked there in the mid-1950s.[^1] A 1956 newspaper item quoted on the Washington Lookouts site says Florence Sanders was back at her post that year.[^4] FireLookout.com also lists her for 1956.[^2]

The cab was abandoned in 1962.[^1][^3] FireLookout.com links that to the nearby K.O. Point lookout going up.[^2] A 1962 newspaper item quoted on the Washington Lookouts site says K.O. Point replaced the old Hull lookout, which sat lower on the next ridge north.[^4]

Today, the register says the cab is now gone, and it lists the Washington Division of Forestry and DNR as administrator.[^1] Willhite describes a gravel road that reaches a spot just below the old site, then a climb through the woods to the summit ridge, where the old road runs along the top.[^3] The photo captions on that page mention an old stovepipe and the old low-standard road to the summit.[^3] No source we read describes what remains of the cab itself, and the sources do not say whether the land is open to visitors.
