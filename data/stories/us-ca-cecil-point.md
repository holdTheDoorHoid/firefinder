The Cecil Point lookout stood in Siskiyou County, California, on land the Klamath National Forest administers.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register lists its build years as 1934 and 1954.[^1] Its first structure was a BC-301 cabin, a California Region 5 plan, on a 10-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1]

The lookout was in use by July 1935.[^2] A diary entry from that month, quoted in the Klamath forest's chronological history, records plans to go into the lookout with Walter Carruthers and install a fire finder and map.[^2] That August, Carruthers resigned, and Ed Farnsworth took over the station.[^2]

In 1939 the California Ranger reported that lookout Johnny Roff had spotted a small blaze during a lightning storm.[^2] Radio contact with the lookout guided a crew to it.[^2]

William Campbell had been the Cecil Point lookout for several years before he moved to the Blue Ridge station for the 1946 season.[^2] The Siskiyou Daily News said the Cecil Point station was several miles off the road, reached by a trail it called extremely steep.[^2] Paul Farmer went to the lookout in 1951.[^2] In October 1963 the Blue Lake Advocate reported that Eileen Johnson had come down from the lookout after serving there for the last four summers.[^2]

In 1954 the R1M, a Region 1 mobile cupola trailer, replaced the original cabin.[^1]

The register gives no removal date, but notes that the original foundation is still visible.[^1] No source we read says whether anything else stands at the site now, or whether the public may visit it.[^1] Anyone curious should check with the Klamath National Forest before heading out.
