A lookout on Grizzly Peak was staffed by 1912, a 1913 newspaper reported, and that paper put the peak about twenty miles from McCloud. The register dates it to 1915 at the latest, saying the peak has hosted a lookout since at least that year.[^2][^1]

In July 1913 a supervisor of the Shasta National Forest hired a pack train to haul 15,000 pounds of lumber for three new lookout houses, one of them on Grizzly Peak. The plan called for 12-by-12 houses, each topped by an eight-foot octagonal glass tower, and the buildings were expected to be ready by August 1.[^2]

In 1915 a Red Bluff paper named W.S. Lofton as the Forest Service lookout at the peak.[^2] A new station went up in 1923. The material had to come in by pack train for fourteen miles, and the register also says a new structure was built that year.[^2][^1]

In 1932 a Dunsmuir paper reported a Forest Service road being built toward the peak, where a fire lookout was posted.[^2] The register says the final building went up in 1953 on a five-foot block tower.[^1]

In the 1950s the lookout was staffed by Pearl Cowan, who was flown up to her tower in 1955, and by Lenore Broze. In 1958 Broze had no human visitors for more than four weeks, until shovelers opened the road that snow had blocked.[^2]

The register says the vandalized lookout building was reported removed by 2012, yet a 2017 photo shows a structure at the site.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history says the vandalized remains were removed in 2012.[^2] A 2007 trip report heard of possible demolition through the "lookout grapevine" and noted that requests for additional information were not returned.[^3]

No source describes the site today with confidence. The same report says snow keeps the lookout road closed until spring.[^3] Before going, check with the Shasta-Trinity National Forest about whether anything still stands and whether the road is open.
