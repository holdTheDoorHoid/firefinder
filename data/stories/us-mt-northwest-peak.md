Northwest Peak Lookout sits in the Yaak area of the Kootenai National Forest, in Lincoln County, just south of the Canadian border.[^1][^3][^4] The site was first used as a patrol camp in 1921.[^1][^3] In 1929 a one-room cab, 14 feet by 14, was built there. It had a gabled roof and was a prototype of the L-4 design that later became common across the West.[^1][^3][^5]

The cab served as a joint home and office for seasonal fire lookouts employed by the Forest Service.[^5] The agency abandoned it for fire detection in 1955, and after that the building was left to the weather.[^1][^3][^5]

The sources disagree on when it was restored. The register and a 2026 association summary say it was restored in the 1980s as a historic site.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com says restoration took place through 2000.[^2] The register listed the lookout on May 12, 2000, as US 347, MT 29.[^1]

Recent work has been steady. In 2024 all the windows were flown off by helicopter and refurbished over the winter.[^3][^4] In 2025 the windows went back up in June, volunteers stabilized all four walls to address major structural deficiencies, and they began putting back the ceiling, which had been removed and reinforced in 2017.[^3][^5]

The 2026 project to reinstall windows, replace siding and install new shutters was rescheduled to 2027 because of helicopter availability and increased wildfire activity.[^4]

An August 2025 Daily Inter Lake article described the one-room cabin as worn down, with out-of-square shutters and unsteady walls, and said steel cables held it to the mountainside.[^5] The windows and shutters are still unfinished, so the sensible way to see it is from a distance.[^4] The sources do not describe public access, so do not enter or climb it.
