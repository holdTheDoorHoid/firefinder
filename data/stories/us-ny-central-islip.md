The Central Islip fire tower stood in Suffolk County, and the state's list now marks it as gone.[^2] The state classes it as a platform tower.[^2] A 1933 newspaper notice gave its height as 70 feet.[^1]

In April 1922 the Brooklyn Daily Eagle reported that a state official backed a plan by Fire Warden Wohlfarth of Central Islip.[^1] The plan was to move the tower from the South Side Club's property to his own land, where it would give a wider view.[^1] The sources do not say whether the move was made.

In April 1933 Robert Wohlfarth, the Central Islip fire warden, asked the Islip Town Board to move the 70-foot tower from the yard of his late father's home to his own back yard.[^1] His father had used the tower when he was fire warden.[^1] The 1922 and 1933 notices may describe the same tower and family, but the sources do not say so.

We found no removal date and no account of how the tower ended. No source describes the site today, so we cannot say what, if anything, remains there or whether the land is open to visitors.
