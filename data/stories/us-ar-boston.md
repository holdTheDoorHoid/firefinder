The Boston lookout stood in Madison County, inside the Ozark National Forest's boundary, on land that belonged to the U.S. Forestry Service.[^1] A 1971 federal survey described a steel tower with four legs and a metal cabin mounted on top, about 85 feet overall.[^1] The survey station lay roughly one mile to the south and three miles to the east of Pettigrew.[^1]

In March 1940, a social item in the Madison County Record said a man was running the Muddy Gap lookout, which the item also called the Boston Lookout.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts directory marks the tower as removed, with no date.[^1] A federal survey listing for the station, PID FG1910, carries a code that means the surface mark was reported destroyed.[^2] A second row for the same station carries a code meaning no geodetic control.[^2] The listing gives no date for either.[^2]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
