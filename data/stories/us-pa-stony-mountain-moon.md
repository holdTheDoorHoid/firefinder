The Stony Mountain "Moon" Lookout stands on a ridge in Dauphin County, and its history depends a little on which tower you mean.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the standing lookout to 1922, when the Philadelphia & Reading Coal & Iron Company built it as a 100-foot steel tower.[^1] Other sources put the first tower on the mountain at 1923, a 60-foot Blaw-Knox built about two miles northeast along the Appalachian Trail, and say the tower standing today went up in 1967 further down the ridge.[^2][^3]

The early tower was put up and staffed by fire wardens working for the Philadelphia & Reading Coal & Iron Company.[^3] The Stony Valley history site says those wardens first protected the area, and that by the mid-1900s it needed some extra help.[^4]

The first tower did not stay on its site.[^4] The Stony Valley history site says it was taken down by the 1970s. A replacement went up further down the mountain, which the site suggests gives the better outlook.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the older site and says its tower was replaced in 1967.[^2] Today a small campsite sits at the old location, with space for around three tents.[^3]

The standing tower is administered by the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry, and the Register added it on January 15, 2018.[^1] It sits in State Game Lands No. 211, where the Stony Valley site calls it the tallest structure.[^4] Reaching it means walking.[^4] From a game land gate on Ellendale Road, the Stony Valley site has visitors walk about two miles up Rattling Run Road and the Horse-Shoe Trail, then roughly half a mile more.[^4] The Register puts the hike at four hours and says people commonly gather there and camp.[^1] The two sources measure the walk differently, one by time and one by distance, so its length is unclear.[^1][^4]

Stony Valley says it is not advisable to climb the tower.[^4] Willhite reports that people climb it regardless of requests not to.[^3]
