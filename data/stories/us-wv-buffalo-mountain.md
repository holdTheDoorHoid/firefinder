The Buffalo Mountain lookout stood in Mingo County, West Virginia, at 1,897 feet (578 meters).[^1] The West Virginia Division of Forestry administered it.[^1]

The register dates the tower to 1961, when it went up under the State Temporary Employment Program, known as STEP.[^1][^2] A November 1963 newspaper account, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said the post had several structures, one of them a tall steel tower.[^2]

The same account named the observer as Emory Hypes, a state Natural Resources Department employee, who was on duty at the tower.[^2] When a fire came toward the post one Thursday night, he radioed that it was approaching his tower. He then climbed down and, using a rake, dug a line around the property; according to the account, the flames stopped there.[^2] The same account puts the post on the West Virginia-Kentucky border, between Williamson and Chattaroy.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register listed the site on January 26, 2017, on the nomination of Brian Powell.[^1]

The statewide list on firelookout.org marks the tower as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the hilltop is open to visitors.[^1]
