The Negro Mountain tower was on the ridge known as Negro Mountain, at 3,213 feet above sea level, the highest elevation in Pennsylvania.[^2][^3] Mount Davis, the state's highest point, lies on the crest of that ridge.[^3]

The register of former lookouts gives 1916 as the build year for the steel Aermotor tower and says it is no longer standing.[^1] A December 1921 newspaper report already describes a six-mile telephone line to the tower and an observer on duty there.[^2] A 1934 report, however, says the Pennsylvania Department of Forests and Waters had erected a forest fire observation tower on the site, and that the tower commands a view of 400,000 acres of forest land.[^2] The sources do not reconcile these dates.[^1][^2]

A Mount Davis page places a metal observation tower, fitted with a regional relief map, close to the true high point.[^3] A state forest article says a 1930s-era observation tower at the high point is being fitted with new signs for visitors who cannot climb it.[^4] Neither source says whether that is the structure the register calls gone, so the register's status is marked disputed.[^3][^4]

Mount Davis may be reached by car or on several hiking trails in the Forbes State Forest.[^3]
