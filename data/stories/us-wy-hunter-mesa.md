Hunter Mesa sits in Johnson County, in the Bighorn National Forest, about 10 miles west of Buffalo.[^1][^2]

A fire report dated July 5, 1939 describes a trip on July 4 to the "Hunter Mesa LO location." The fire was found on arrival at the mesa, under the edge of a ridge and out of the wind.[^1]

The tower and lookout house went up in 1940.[^1] A history of the Bighorn National Forest, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says a crew was erecting them a short distance north of the Hunter Ranger Station in April 1940.[^1] A Civilian Conservation Corps camp's inspection report from October 1940 says the lookout was completed.[^1] The Forest Service says the Corps built fire lookouts in the Bighorn National Forest between 1938 and 1940.[^4] Hunter Mesa's 1940 date puts it at the end of that span, but the sources do not name the crew that built it.

Memos to the regional forester give visitor counts: 70 for the 1956 season, and 55 for 1957, a figure the 1957 memo credits to the lookout.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page marks the tower as removed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wyoming list shows it as a tower that is gone.[^3]

No source describes what remains at the site now, or whether it is open to the public.

The Forest Service names three surviving historic lookouts on the Bighorn National Forest: Sheep Mountain, Black Mountain and James T. Saban. Hunter Mesa is not one of them.[^4][^5]
