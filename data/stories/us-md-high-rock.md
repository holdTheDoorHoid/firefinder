The High Rock Tower stands in Savage River State Forest in Garrett County, Maryland, and the Maryland DNR Forest Service administers it.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register dates the tower to 1934 and describes a 90-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] The register also says the tower has been repainted recently, is used for communications and emergency detection, and invites volunteers to get in touch about staffing times.[^1] It was registered on June 17, 2007.[^1]

A visit report posted on that same register page tells a different story. Written in April 2017, it calls the tower poor and unmaintained, notes that its windows have no glass, and describes a very rusty cab exterior. The writer doubts that volunteers use it and reports a local's account that the tower has not been actively staffed since the 1980s, with the observer's cabin removed before then.[^1] The same writer found gaps in the upper landings, which need replacing, and a slightly spongy floor.[^1]

The state's Fire Tower Report says High Rock stands on what it calls an LU tract. That land could revert to the federal government if it is not kept in appropriate use, and the report says the site lies within large tracts of public land.[^2]

Given the mixed picture, this history does not encourage climbing the tower. Check with the state forest before planning a visit, because the sources do not describe any current staffing or repair schedule.[^1]

[^2]: Fire Tower Report, Maryland Department of Natural Resources, https://dnr.maryland.gov/forests/documents/fire/Fire_Tower_Report.pdf (accessed 2026-10-08).
