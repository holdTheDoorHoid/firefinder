The Avenales site was a wartime post, not a fire tower in the usual sense.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes it as a standard Aircraft Warning Service post, with a ground cabin and a cupola, built in 1942 or 1943.[^1] The post had two jobs: spotting enemy aircraft and detecting fires.[^1]

The Forest Service built, supplied and staffed the post, and the Army repaid the agency for its costs.[^1] Operators were to report aircraft activity, and every fire or smoke, to Forest Service dispatch, and the aircraft information was then passed on to the Army.[^1] The service behind the post was a civilian branch of the Army's Ground Observer Corps, and it was deactivated on May 29, 1944.[^2]

The register gives the removal date as unknown.[^1] It records the site as registered on April 23, 2020, on the nomination of Brad Eells, under the number US 2042, CA 148.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the site as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.
