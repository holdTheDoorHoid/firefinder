The state's Conservation and Development Commission procured and erected a 120-foot steel fire lookout on the Windsor road, just west of the Isle of Wight courthouse.[^1] It was placed in operation in December 1936, at no cost to Isle of Wight County, and a Smithfield paper called it one of the three highest towers in the state.[^1]

In 1937 the tower was looked after by R. H. 'Doc' Holland, who spent dry days in it watching for smoke.[^1] A 1955 newspaper notice sought a part-time seasonal lookout for spring and fall dry days, at $6 a day.[^1]

The tower came down in January 1993, after about 57 years at its home site. Its new owners, who owned Southwind Shooting Farms, planned to reassemble it about a mile away, where the structure was to be built into their clay target range.[^1]

A 1992 article places Southwind two miles west of the courthouse on Route 644, at 10134 Fire Tower Road. It describes the range as open to the public, with a $20 fee and a mandatory guide for each shooter.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's state list still marks the 1936 tower as gone.[^3] None of the sources we read says whether the reassembled tower still stands at the range, or whether the range operates today.
