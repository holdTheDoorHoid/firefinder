Angeles Adventures says the Los Angeles County Forestry Department put up a fire lookout on Parker Mountain in 1935, and places the mountain in Acton.[^2] Forest Lookouts quotes a November 1934 newspaper item saying the lookout was well under way, and says the department constructed it in the spring of 1935.[^1] Angeles Adventures also describes a 20-foot-high, enclosed timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot wood cab built to a 1934 Region 5 Forest Service standard.[^2]

Lookout staff named in the sources were Theo L. Mercy in 1938 and Cecil G. Harrell in 1949.[^2]

Forest Lookouts records a closure on July 22, 1969, and says helicopters had made the tower obsolete.[^1] Angeles Adventures says it stayed in service only until 1969 because helicopter detection was far better suited to a city that was growing and getting smoggier, and it lists the tower as demolished.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks it gone.[^4]

Today a concrete structure from the lookout days still sits at the top, according to Angeles Adventures, and a hiking listing also mentions a concrete foundation from the old lookout.[^2][^3] Angeles Adventures says most of the mountain is public land managed by the Bureau of Land Management, but the roads around its base are private and parking is hard.[^2]
