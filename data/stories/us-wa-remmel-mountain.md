The summit of Remmel Mountain, now inside the Pasayten Wilderness, once held a fire lookout, and the first building there is hard to date.[^1][^4] WillhiteWeb says an International Boundary Commission party reported a trail and a lookout house on the summit in 1904, and that other survey parties reported a lookout building and trail in 1925.[^4]

The register gives a ground-mounted Region-5 cab built in 1930.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page quotes a Wenatchee Daily World item from that August, saying Ed Allen was putting up a new lookout station on the peak.[^3] That summer Keith Patterson served as the lookout, the same paper reported in September.[^3]

An August 1932 item called it the top lookout in the Chelan National Forest and gave its altitude as 8,690 feet. The register now lists the summit at 8,685 feet.[^3][^1] During World War II the lookout served as an Aircraft Warning Service station for July and August 1942.[^4][^3]

A 1950 survey party said the Forest Service had abandoned the lookout house because of safety factors.[^4] The lookout inventory suggests 1952 as the year the cab was destroyed.[^4] The FFLOS register says it was removed, while firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say destroyed.[^1][^2][^4]

None of the sources consulted describes what remains on the summit today. WillhiteWeb says a former lookout once stood on the summit, which is why a trail goes there.[^4] The WTA, however, lists an abandoned side trail to the old lookout location, so check trail conditions before you go.[^5]

The site is in the Pasayten Wilderness, and hikers reach it from trailheads.[^1][^4] WillhiteWeb lists the Thirtymile, Cathedral Driveway and Andrews Creek trailheads, with a hike of about 16 miles one way.[^4] It adds that Andrews Creek burned and was closed for many years, and that trail conditions and fires may decide which trailhead makes sense.[^4]
