The Oregon Department of Forestry lookout on Little Grayback sits on a high point in Josephine County, about eight miles east of Cave Junction.[^1][^2][^4] A 1980 district annual report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says construction began on July 1, 1980, with completion expected by the start of the 1981 fire season.[^2] Rex's Fire Lookout Page puts the site nine miles east of Cave Junction.[^3]

A Forest Service helicopter carried in the materials. The same report says many came from an older lookout on Waldo, and that the new lookout covers more ground for detection than Waldo.[^2] The sources do not agree on the name of that older lookout. Forest Lookouts calls it Waldo Mountain, while the register and Rex's Fire Lookout Page say Waldo Hill.[^1][^2][^3] The register also says the 14-by-14-foot flattop cab was moved to this site from Waldo Hill in 1981.[^1] Cheryl Hill's 2020 visit account describes a ground cabin built in 1980 and finished in 1981, with much of the material from Waldo Hill.[^4] The register gives a build year of 1971, which the other pages we read do not support.[^1]

Rex's Fire Lookout Page says the cab had summer staffing into the 2010s.[^3] By the 2020 visit it had lost its staff and was boarded up, and it hosted cameras from the EVS Fire Watch system.[^4] The register says the cab is still in use for research on electronic fire detection, and it was registered on September 4, 2011.[^1] The 2026 directory lists it as unmanned.[^5]

The directory says the site can be legally reached by the public.[^5] The 2020 visitor drove a rough gravel road of about five and a half miles, found a gate partly open, and walked the last steep half-mile.[^4] A surveillance warning was posted on site.[^4] The cab was boarded up when that visit was made, so do not try to enter it.[^4]
