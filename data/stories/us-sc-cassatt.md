The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Cassatt fire tower in 1936. The tower was a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40 steel design with a 7-by-7 cab. Its builders were enlistees posted at the Cassatt State Forest in Kershaw County.[^1][^2] The state had received the forest by deed in 1935, and for a time it hosted a C.C.C. camp.[^1]

In 1947 a state history of the forest noted that the 462-acre property had a fire lookout tower on it.[^1]

By the 1990s the tower had been sold to a private party.[^1] The register says the South Carolina Forestry Commission sold it to Billy McIntosh in the late 1990s.[^2] He moved it to Sumter County, reconditioned it and set it up again on his property.[^2] The register also says the tower had been reconstructed to 40 feet on its original concrete footings, down from its original 100 feet.[^2]

The register, entered in 2013, places the tower in Sumter County at US 969 and SC 22, on Billy McIntosh's property, and describes it as a spot for hunting and watching wildlife.[^2] We could not confirm what is at the original Cassatt State Forest site today. The register's mention of the original concrete footings does not say where they are.[^2]

[^1]: Cassatt - Forest Lookouts, Eastern US Lookouts (a compilation of a 1936 summary and a 1947 state history), https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/cassatt.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Cassatt Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/sc/cassatt-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-21).
