The Bearwallow Mountain lookout was erected in 1923 on the Glenwood Ranger District of the Gila National Forest, in Catron County, New Mexico.[^1][^2] It was an Aermotor LX-24 with a 7-by-7-foot wooden cab, and it stood 35 feet tall.[^1][^2]

The early record is thin. A June 1913 Albuquerque Journal passage, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, mentions a supervisor driving from a "Bear Wallows lookout." That hints at an earlier lookout in the same area, but no source describes it.[^2] In April 1967, a Silver City paper reported that a married couple planned to take up their post at the lookout.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register (NHLR) describes a 1984 overhaul. The original windows were replaced, new stairs went in, and steel now covers the roof and exterior walls.[^1] Forest Lookouts also reports that the standard ladder was replaced by interior stairs, and that windows and siding have been upgraded over the years.[^2]

The tower is part of a complex that also includes two log cabins and a log shed.[^1] Wikipedia gives two build dates for the cabins and shed. Its lead credits the Works Progress Administration with building them in 1940, while its infobox lists 1923.[^3] The sources I found do not settle which is right. The complex was added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 28, 1988.[^3] In 2006 the Bear Fire crossed Bearwallow Mountain, and Wikipedia says it threatened the buildings.[^3]

The NHLR registered the tower on May 22, 2022, as US 1585, NM 43, and lists the U.S. Forest Service as its administrator.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list shows it as standing.[^4] Wikipedia places the complex in Bearwallow Park near Mogollon.[^3] I did not find a source that says whether the tower is staffed now or open to visitors, so anyone planning a trip should check with the Gila National Forest first.
