The summit of Beech Hill is about one mile from downtown Keene, New Hampshire.[^1] In October 1890, a 35-foot (10.7 m) wooden observation tower went up on the top. The tower took the name Horatian Tower from its owner, Horace L. Goodnow.[^1]

Goodnow also put up a six-bedroom summer hotel on the hill and laid out streets for a vacation-home development he called Horatian Park, but very few homes were built.[^1] Over time, a trip to the tower became a favorite pastime for people from Keene and for out-of-town visitors.[^1] Admission was 15 cents, and across ten seasons, from 1890 through 1899, thousands of people signed the guest book.[^1] No visits were logged after November 1899.[^1]

The sources disagree about what stood on the summit. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Beech Hill among its non-lookout structures, as an observation tower, and marks it gone.[^3] NH Family Hikes, however, lists a Beech Hill site at Keene among New Hampshire's fire towers, at 1,053 feet.[^2]

A hike report from May 2026 describes a network of trails on the hill. It says the old tower site now holds a communication tower, with an old foundation just past the summit. It also says everything there is fenced in, though an earlier line says the tower area is not fenced off.[^4] The report lists parking for two to four cars.[^4] Local residents and the city of Keene set aside 180 acres of the hill as open space in the decades from the 1970s into the 1990s.[^1] The land is now the Beech Hill Preserve.[^1] The tower has been gone for decades, but the preserve still has views over downtown Keene.[^1]
