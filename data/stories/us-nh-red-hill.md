The State of New Hampshire built the Red Hill lookout in 1927 as a replacement for the Mount Israel lookout, in the town of Moultonborough in Carroll County.[^1] Eastern lookouts says a 27-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10 cab went up that year, paid for with $1,175 in private money, and that the state then maintained and staffed it.[^2]

The sources disagree about a 1972 change. The register says the tower was raised to 55 feet and given an observation platform for visitors.[^1] Eastern lookouts says a 10-foot extension brought the height to 37 feet.[^2] We could not settle which is right.

The state closed the tower in 1981.[^1] Eastern lookouts says the Town of Moultonborough then signed a lease, and the local fire department took over detection duties.[^2]

The register records the tower on August 5, 2003.[^1]

In 2009 the town received a restoration grant from the Forest Fire Lookout Association to help repaint the tower.[^3] The 2011 chapter report says inspections in June and November found no work done on the tower and no attempt to paint the cab or steelwork.[^3] In December 2011 the town's selectmen voted to end the lease and hand the site back to the Lakes Region Conservation Trust, the landowner.[^3]

In 2012 the effort to run the tower did not succeed, and the grant money was sent to the association's treasurer to close the matter.[^4]

A 2016 visit report answered yes to "Needs paint?" and reported rot in the wood of every landing, with the boards not properly fastened.[^1]

The 2025 chapter report says a private association still operates the tower.[^5] The register says it is the only active tower in New Hampshire not owned by the state, and a sign marks the trailhead.[^1] We found no current statement that the stairs or cab are open to visitors. Because the 2016 report describes rotting landings, this story does not encourage climbing.[^1]
