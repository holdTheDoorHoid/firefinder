The Ritter Butte Lookout stands in Grant County, Oregon, just off U.S. Highway 395.[^1][^2] The Oregon Department of Forestry put it into service in 1950 as a three-story enclosed tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab.[^1][^3]

The sources disagree on how it was built.[^1][^3] The Register describes a wooden tower with a garage, but a 1950 district report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, calls it an aluminum tower.[^1][^3]

Forest Lookouts quotes a 1960 season with 17 fire reports.[^3] Firelookout.com says the lookout was staffed into the 1980s, then used for emergencies, and was staffed full time again from 2012.[^2] Cheryl Hill's June 2016 visit listed it as staffed, but no one was there, and she heard that staffing would start the next week.[^4] A 2017 entry says it was not staffed during the fire season and that a fire detection camera system had been installed.[^3]

The Register's entry adds that an engine is stationed at the site on high fire days.[^1]

In June 2016, during a Friday visit by attendees of the FFLA Western Conference, a caravan of cars trekked up to the butte, according to Cheryl Hill's account.[^4] None of the sources says whether the public may go inside the tower.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Because the Register ties the site to an engine on high fire days, check with the Oregon Department of Forestry before planning a visit.[^1]
