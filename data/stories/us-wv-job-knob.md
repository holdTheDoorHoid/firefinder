Job Knob's site sits at 4,347 feet (1,325 meters) in Greenbrier County, West Virginia.[^1] The register records an enclosed tower there, with a build span of 1913 to 1914.[^1][^2] It names the West Virginia Fish, Game & Forestry Commission as the agency that administered the site.[^1]

A state warden's biennial report covering 1913-14 says that H.E. Williams donated all the lumber for the Job Knob station and delivered it on the ground at the site.[^3] A later report entry, which the transcription dates to 1916, places the station in Williamsburg District, nineteen miles from Richwood, under the supervision of David Frame.[^3] Its watchman, James H. McMillion, lived nine miles from the station and spent each night there.[^3] The same entry puts the station's view at roughly 800 square miles.[^3]

The two sources disagree on elevation. The register gives 4,347 feet, while the 1916 entry gives 4,388 feet, and neither source explains the difference.[^1][^3]

The register now lists the tower as gone.[^2] The register's image is a 1915 photo from a Fish, Game & Forestry Commission report, but no source we found describes what remains at the site today or whether it can be visited.[^1]
