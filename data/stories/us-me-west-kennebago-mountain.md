West Kennebago Mountain in Oxford County, Maine, had a Forest Service fire lookout on its summit from 1911 until the tower came down in 2012.[^1][^2] The first station was a wooden tower, and a telephone line of about 2.5 miles served it.[^1]

In 1914 a 24-foot steel tower replaced the wooden one, with the watchman's house on top.[^1][^3] A 1925 letter printed in the Daily Kennebec Journal describes the tower as 40 feet high. The sources do not agree on its height, and we cannot tell whether the letter describes the same structure.[^1]

Lancelot Cooper worked as the watchman in 1917, and Wallace S. Houston held the post in 1919.[^3] In 1925 and again in 1926, Lewis Blake, a state fire warden, and his wife spent the summer at the lookout. Letters from the couple were printed in the Daily Kennebec Journal.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page gives May 18, 2012 as the day the tower was removed.[^1] The association's 2012 Maine report says the tower was to be taken down so a new communications tower could take its place, as part of the state's MSCommNet radio project. A helicopter lift planned for Saturday, May 19 failed, because the steel was too heavy and the cab too rotted to carry. The tower was cut into pieces and sent to scrap.[^2] The two sources give different dates, and we could not settle which is right.

The fire tower is gone. A birder who surveyed the mountain wrote that the Forest Service had burned the fire warden's cabin to prevent vandalism, and he later photographed the remnants on the cabin site.[^4] He also noted a helicopter landing platform higher on the trail.[^4] A hiker who reached the summit later reported a partly erected tower, footings for other structures, and many trees cut back on the summit.[^5] We found no source that says whether the summit is open to the public now, so check before you go.
