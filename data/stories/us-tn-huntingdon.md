The Huntingdon tower stood in Carroll County, on ground the Tennessee Landforms list puts at 548 feet.[^1] The list's elevations come from USGS terrain data, so the figure describes the ground, not the tower.[^1] The list gives this tower the code 8-8.[^1] That code is a district and tower number from a 1976 list of state forest towers, and the same list says the Division of Forestry had 208 tower sites around that time.[^1]

A National Register study of the Division's towers says they were the visible part of its fire detection system from 1933 to 1975.[^2]

The Landforms list marks the Huntingdon tower as removed.[^1] That fits a wider pattern. The National Register study says many of the 208 towers have since been taken down or are slated for demolition, either because they fell out of use or because leases with private landowners ran out.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Huntingdon site, or whether the public can reach it. The list says most former tower locations are now private property, but it does not say this one is.[^1] It also advises against climbing the towers, because steps are often gone or rotten.[^1] Please ask the landowner before going onto any land that may be private.

[^1]: Tennessee lookout towers, tnlandforms.us (Tom Dunigan), https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers, 1933 to 1975, National Park Service, National Register of Historic Places, https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/64501237.pdf (accessed 2026-10-21).
