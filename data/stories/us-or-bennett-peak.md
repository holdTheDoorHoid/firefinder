Bennett Peak is a summit in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest in Baker County, at 7,099 feet.[^1] The register describes a cabin with a fire finder on its roof, dating from the 1920s, that may have been swapped for a tower in 1932.[^1] Firelookout.com dates the cabin to the 1910s, so the two sources disagree.[^2] A lookout history page quoting old newspaper items includes a July 1914 item naming a forester to take charge of the station, where a new fire finder was to be placed.[^3]

In August 1915, lightning storms caused three forest fires within two days, and lookout man Robert W. Quinn reported them by telephone to the Baker forestry office.[^3] A September 23, 1915 newspaper item said a fire reported from the lookout was on East Eagle Creek, threatening a large amount of valuable timber.[^3] In July 1935, a lookout-dispatcher named Fred Groom went up to the peak, according to a 1935 item quoted on the history page.[^3]

Both the register and firelookout.com say the site was abandoned in 1948.[^1][^2] The register adds that the site is now gone.[^1] Neither source says when the cabin came down or what, if anything, stands on the summit today.[^1][^2]

No source found describes the site as it is now, and none says whether it is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
