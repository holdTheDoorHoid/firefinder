Moddersville was a village in the Missaukee County countryside, along Thirteen Mile Road.[^1] Its name comes from the Modders family, who came from the Netherlands in 1878. The name was made official after a post office opened in 1890, with Wyn Modders as first postmaster.[^1]

In March 1939 a Clare Sentinel item, quoted on the Eastern US Lookouts page, referred to the Moddersville Fire Tower. It reported that enrollees from Camp Houghton Lake were starting six miles of telephone line from the tower to the main line at the camp.[^2] The paper said Michigan's CCC boys had spent the past six years building a telephone network that linked forest fire towers, and that every Michigan State CCC camp ran such work as part of its program.[^2]

The Michigan Fire Towers history page says aerial surveillance and population expansion brought about the decline of the state's towers in 1962, and that Michigan began selling them off in 1970.[^3]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains of the Moddersville tower or its site today, or whether the site is open to visitors. The October 2023 village article names one present-day business, the Moddersville Store and Pizzeria, at South Thirteenth Mile Road and East Finkle Road.[^1]
