Illinois Peak straddles the Montana-Idaho line, in Mineral County, Montana, and Shoshone County, Idaho.[^1][^3] It also sits where the Lolo and St. Joe national forests meet.[^3]

In July 1915 the Mineral Independent reported that Forester Phillips had gone to the head of Cedar Creek with a government pack train to set up a lookout station on the peak. Calvin MacDonald would be left in charge.[^2] Firelookout.com instead dates the first structures to 1913: a log cabin about half a mile south on the divide and a pole platform at the summit.[^1]

A steel Aermotor tower went up in 1938, its materials packed in by mule.[^1][^2] The Montana page describes it as about 72 feet tall.[^2] Firelookout.com and a 1943 newspaper item quoted on the Montana page both give 60 feet.[^1][^2]

Newspaper notices from 1934 to 1947 name a few men at the peak. Calvin MacDonald was the lookout man in 1934, and a 1935 item had Bud LaCombe down from it. Charles Kern, 17, was a lookout there in 1943, and Leeland Spencer was stationed as a lookout in 1947.[^2]

In 1974 the tower was taken apart, and pack animals carried it off the peak. A helicopter was judged too costly.[^2] Firelookout.com lists the site as gone in 1976.[^1]

A climbers' guide says only foundation blocks remain near the summit, where a fire lookout once stood.[^3] The same guide describes a relatively easy class 1 trail from Freezeout Pass, a round trip of roughly five miles.[^3] No agency source we read describes the site today, including whether visitors may go there.
