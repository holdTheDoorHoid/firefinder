Bald Mountain stands on the Siskiyou National Forest in Josephine County, Oregon, about 29 miles west of Grants Pass.[^1] A May 1914 item from the Siskiyou Bulletin, quoted by Forest Lookouts, describes opening a trail to the peak and choosing a lookout station at the end of a telephone line.[^2]

The lookout house went up in 1929.[^1][^2] In March that year the Sunday Oregonian reported plans for a 12-foot-square house with an observation tower.[^2] By September a Grants Pass paper said Henry Harrison of the Forest Service was building it, with lumber packed in from Selma by horses.[^2] That paper put the lookout 58 miles from Grants Pass, not the 29 miles firelookout.com gives.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com also dates the building to 1929, but calls it a "Cathedral" cabin.[^1]

George Wadsworth of Kerby was the lookout fireman in 1931.[^2] A July 1931 item says he held a fire on the slope until crews could reach it, and that about 250 acres of small trees burned.[^2] In 1932 a sick prospector who had come to the station died there.[^2] A 1941 entry records the lookout as staffed for 84 days.[^2]

Firelookout.com gives the dates as 1929 to 1967, with a question mark after the later year, and says the cabin was abandoned in 1967.[^1] Forest Lookouts says the station was activated on June 10, 1943, for the Roseburg Filter Center, and later lists it as removed, with no date.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the site gone.[^3]

We found no source describing what, if anything, remains on the summit today. An Oregon Hikers guide describes a trail junction that leads up to the old lookout site atop Bald Mountain, which it gives as 3,975 feet.[^4] SummitPost places the peak inside the Kalmiopsis Wilderness, which it says a national forest administers, and calls the climb a long day hike on trails.[^5] It also says the peak is among the most frequently visited, because it lies along the Illinois River Trail.[^5] Firelookout.com gives the elevation as 3,730 feet, so the sources do not agree on the height.[^1]
