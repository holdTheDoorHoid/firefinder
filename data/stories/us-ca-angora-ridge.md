The Angora Ridge lookout sits on a moraine between Fallen Leaf Lake and Lake Valley, in the Lake Tahoe Basin.[^1][^2] The Lake Tahoe Basin Management Unit of the U.S. Forest Service manages the site, and the National Historic Lookout Register says it "first went into service in 1914."[^2] A plaque about the lookout gives a different start: it dates the first lookout to 1924 and says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the current structure in 1935.[^3] The same plaque credits forester Coert duBois's 1914 idea for fire lookouts, and says this one followed his design.[^3] The register notes that the three ground-level buildings put up on the site over time all kept their original designs.[^2]

Lookouts watched from the site in the 1920s and 1930s, and it drew crowds.[^1] A 1925 report names Lookout Waterhouse, and a 1931 item names Clisby Loomis as the Forest Service lookout at Angora.[^1] In July 1936, 900 people registered at the lookout.[^1]

In 1965, lightning struck the lookout tower directly twice, but no one was hurt and the steel tower was not damaged.[^1] That item describes a steel tower, while the register describes ground-level buildings.[^1][^2] The sources read do not say when, or whether, that tower was removed.[^1][^2]

On June 24, 2007, the Angora Fire started on Forest Service land near Seneca Pond.[^4] Before it was contained, it had burned about 3,100 acres and destroyed 254 structures.[^4] A 2007 Forest Fire Lookout Association report says the fire started near the unstaffed lookout, which "almost succumbed to the conflagration."[^5] The sources do not say whether the lookout was damaged.[^5]

A 2009 association list marks the site "Abandoned" and notes that it was unstaffed during the fire.[^6] The register says the lookout "is now closed but still stands," although trees now block part of the view.[^2] Photos from June 2010 show two lookout structures and a garage on the site.[^1]

Today, Angora Ridge Road (Forest Road 1214) has seasonal access, typically from mid-May to mid-November, conditions and weather permitting.[^7] The Forest Service says the lookout "can be accessed seasonally" from that road.[^7] The register says the lookout is closed, and the sources read do not say whether it is open to visitors.[^2] Please stay off the structures.
