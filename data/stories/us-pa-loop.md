The Loop tower was built on Tussey Mountain at Lytle's Knob, on a spur of the mountain that the sources call the Loop. The site is about 2,500 feet above sea level.[^1] The district forester, T. Roy Morton, chose it from three sites he suggested; the other two were Short Mountain and Canoe Mountain.[^1]

A steel tower 60 feet high was finished in 1922. A notice dated November 7, 1922, says it had been completed the week before, and that Eugene Bradley had just been named its tower man.[^1] Bradley came over from the Greenwood Fire Tower, where he had been in charge for six months.[^1] A 1924 notice still describes the 60-foot tower when a Blair County chapter of the Pennsylvania Alpine Club planned to climb it.[^1]

The sources disagree on the height. The Loop page on the same site says the tower was 78 feet tall, and it gives no source for that figure.[^2] The 1922 and 1924 notices both say 60 feet.[^1]

The two Forest Lookouts pages also disagree on the county. One places the site in Huntingdon County and the other in Blair County.[^1][^2] The state game lands along the ridge, State Game Lands 118, run through both counties.[^3]

The sources do not say whether the tower still stands or whether its cab may be climbed, and none describes its condition. The game lands are open for general use year round.[^4] Treat the tower as off limits until you have checked with the land manager.
