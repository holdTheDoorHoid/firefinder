Kosmos Lookout stood on Kosmos Hill in Lewis County, Washington.[^3] In 1947 a World War II surplus steel tower went up there, with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and the site also had a house for living quarters.[^1][^2][^4] The state's annual forestry report gave the steel tower a height of 75 feet and a 14-by-18-foot cabin on the ground, and it said the tower filled one of the last gaps in protection across western Washington.[^3] Willhite gives the height as 90 feet, or 75.[^4]

The register, the Fire Lookout website and Willhite all date the replacement to 1963, when a 50-foot wooden live-in tower took the steel tower's place on the same spot.[^1][^2][^4] A state report covering 1960 to 1962 says a crew completed nine lookouts in those years, one of them at Kosmos.[^3] That does not match the 1963 date, so the sources disagree on when the wooden tower went up.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

In the summer of 1955 a newspaper reported that the state forestry service had added the Kosmos lookout.[^3] Mrs. V. Bridges staffed it and phoned the filter center directly about any aircraft she sighted.[^3]

According to the Fire Lookout website and Willhite, the wooden tower was destroyed in 1968.[^2][^4] The register says it was removed that year.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site on September 28, 2010.[^1]

The register says the site is now owned by Champion Timber of Orting, Washington.[^1] Willhite found the foundations of the 1947 house and tower still in place, and the 1963 tower's footings overlap the older ones.[^4]

Willhite's route starts on Highway 12 east of Morton and continues onto a road with a gate partway up.[^4] In 2015 the gate was posted as open to non-motorized users, and the posting changes while logging is underway.[^4]
