Chisholm is a steel fire lookout in Alberta, 30 m (98 ft) tall and set on a site 685.6 m (2,249 ft) above sea level.[^1] The provincial register gives 2018 as the year the tower was built, and it lists the tower as active and reached by road.[^1]

The Chisholm fire lookout was already in service in 2001.[^2] On May 23 that year, a wildfire broke out ten kilometres to the south of the hamlet of Chisholm.[^2] An oil-industry worker saw the blaze and reported it to the lookout at 9:35 PM.[^2] The blaze ultimately burned an area of 104,000 hectares (257,000 acres).[^2] A low ridge hid the ignition point from the lookout operator.[^2]

In 2007 the Alberta government asked researchers at FPInnovations to design a camera system that could watch ground the lookout could not see.[^2] A 2015 magazine article describes a portable, collapsible tower in the first installation.[^2] A 2015 FPInnovations report by Colleen Mooney says the camera system built for the lookout in 2007 and 2008 worked.[^3] The same report says researchers were later asked to build one that did not rely on a CN Rail trackside tower.[^3] Lacking any other tower, the researchers placed the cameras on a portable tower.[^3] The magazine and the report disagree about the first setup, and this story reports both accounts.[^2][^3]

A 2013 FPInnovations project page gives 2009 for the portable tower system at the lookout, so the sources give different years for it.[^4] By 2015 the camera system still operated at the lookout, and a second system was under development.[^2]

Alberta operates 127 fire lookouts.[^2] A lookout observer staffs each site, usually from April to October.[^5] Both the Chisholm hamlet and the starting point of the 2001 fire sit inside one of this lookout's blind areas, which the magazine calls a constant concern for Alberta's detection program.[^2]

The register still lists the tower as active, with road access.[^1] No source found describes the site as it stands today, or says whether the public may visit it.
