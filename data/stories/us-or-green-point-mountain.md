Green Point Mountain is a local name for a high point on Green Point Ridge in the Mt. Hood National Forest, about 12 miles southwest of Hood River, Oregon.[^2][^4] The Forest Service built a lookout on the summit in 1930.[^1] firelookout.com also lists 1930.[^2]

An October 12, 1934 item in The Dam Chronicle, quoted on a lookout-history page, said the road from a ranch out of Hood River to the lookout station was due for improvement.[^3]

Both listings now mark the lookout as gone, and neither gives a year for its removal.[^1][^2] The site entered the Former Fire Lookout Sites register on August 25, 2010.[^1] The register puts the summit at 4,730 feet (1,442 m), while the Oregon Hikers field guide gives 4,737 feet.[^1][^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today. The Oregon Hikers field guide describes the summit as a high point with broad views, and says the Gorton Creek Trail runs very close to the top.[^4] It puts the summit 9.6 miles on foot from Herman Creek Trailhead and places it in the Mark O. Hatfield Wilderness, with no closure mentioned.[^4]
