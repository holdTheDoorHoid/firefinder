The steel lookout at Tulaby, in Mahnomen County, has an early history that the sources do not fully agree on [^1][^2]. A lookout history site's Minnesota list gives 1934 as the year a steel tower was erected there [^1]. The county survey lists 1935 in its table, but its notes say the tower was moved to its present site in 1934 from an unknown location [^2]. The same survey names the Minnesota Forest Service as owner, a body that is now the forestry division of the state Department of Natural Resources [^2].

A 1964 survey describes a four-legged steel structure that holds a small cabin on top [^2]. It gives the height as about 100 feet, although the table in the same survey lists 86 feet, and it places the tower in White Earth State Forest [^2]. The table also marks the design as a stairway tower [^2].

I found no record of who staffed Tulaby or of any fire it spotted, so that part of its story is still open.

The 1964 survey called the tower standing [^2], and the Forest Fire Lookout Association list still shows it standing [^3]. The 1964 survey is the most recent on-site description I found, so the tower's present condition is unconfirmed.

I could not find a current statement about public access or the tower's condition. Before planning a visit, check with the land manager, and do not climb the tower without clear permission.

[^2]: Mahnomen County Minnesota Fire Lookout Towers, Wikimedia Commons, https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/40/Mahnomen_County_Fire_Lookout_Towers.pdf (accessed 2026-10-09).
