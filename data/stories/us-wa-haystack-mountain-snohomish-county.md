The Haystack Mountain lookout in Snohomish County stood about 11 miles east-southeast of Monroe.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places the site in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1]

In 1957 a 40-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab was built on the site.[^1][^2] firelookout.com puts the tower two miles west of the summit.[^2] A SummitPost climbing page says the lookout never actually stood on the summit.[^4] The register and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's list give coordinates about 5.7 kilometers apart, so the exact spot is uncertain.[^1][^3]

According to the register, the tower was later abandoned and no longer stands.[^1] firelookout.com lists it as abandoned too.[^2]

A Washington Trails Association trip report from June 2022 describes a Haystack Ridge Lookout Site that appears to be this one.[^5] The writer says the lookout has been gone for a long time and mentions two old radio shacks that appear to be out of use. The access road runs along Ben Howard Road, 1.8 miles south of Sultan. It is gated and unmarked, and the report describes the land as private timberland.[^5] Non-motorized day use is allowed under the Forest Legacy program, and the gate can close at any time, so the report advises against driving up.[^5]

Eric Willhite's guide to Ten-Four Mountain, under the heading Haystack Lookout & Ten-Four Mountain, also calls the spot the former lookout site, reached by road to Airplane Lake and then up.[^6]

The register lists the Forest Service as administrator, but the trip report describes private timberland, so check who manages the land before going.[^1][^5]
