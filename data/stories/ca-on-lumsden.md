Labelle Lake Peak, in the Greater Sudbury region of Ontario, once held both a ski hill and the Lumsden fire tower.[^1] The peak's listed height is 487 m (1,598 ft).[^1]

Across Ontario, most of the lookout towers went up between the 1920s and the 1950s.[^2] Ontario's Department of Lands and Forests oversaw them, and in 1972 it was renamed the Ministry of Natural Resources.[^2]

A trip report posted in May 2016 found little on the summit beyond rusted ski-lift remains and weathered footings from the fire tower.[^1] That is the newest account of the summit I found, so no source confirms what it looks like today. The summit also gives views south over the Sudbury Basin.[^1]

Getting there starts at Nelson Lake Road. The writer recommends stopping where the road becomes rough, since only high-clearance vehicles should continue.[^1] From there a rough ATV trail climbs roughly 120 m (almost 400 ft) to the summit, about 1.1 km from the road.[^1] A reader comment dated September 2018 says the road is in much better shape to reach.[^1]

[^1]: Labelle Lake Peak, Ontario High Points, https://www.ontariohighpoints.com/labelle-lake-peak/ (accessed 2026-10-20).
[^2]: Ontario's Fire Tower Lookouts: Preserving Their History, Clay (fire tower history site), https://forestrangers.github.io/ontariofiretowers/ (accessed 2026-10-20).
