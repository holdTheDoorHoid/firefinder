The Pyramid Peak lookout site sits at 5,715 feet in King County, Washington, about 29 miles east of Buckley.[^1][^2] The land is part of the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1]

The earliest structure we found was a cupola cabin, built in 1923.[^1][^2] The Greenwater hiking guide describes it as among the first lookouts in the region, and says the cupola was the source of panoramic photos taken in 1938.[^3]

A 1928 photograph in the guide shows Jess Rose upstairs in the cupola, holding the shutter.[^3] The guide also reports that Rose once fell from the cupola, through a floor opening, to the level below.[^3] We found no other lookout names in the sources we read.

The register and firelookout.com say an L-4 cab replaced the cupola in 1948, and that the lookout had disappeared by 1965.[^1][^2] Neither says how it ended. The hiking guide says the Forest Service burned the cupola in 1948, and that an L-4 it may have put up was lost sometime between the 1950s and the early 1960s.[^3] A blog post from January 2026 says the L-4 burned in the early 1960s and that by 1968 the site had fallen out of use.[^4] The sources do not agree on the dates or the sequence.

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site on October 17, 2010, on the nomination of Irene Potter.[^1] Captioned photos in the guide, dated 2023, show lookout site anchors and what may be remnants of an outhouse or cellar along the west ridge.[^3] The blog describes a steep 0.7-mile route along Pyramid Peak Trail #1160 to the summit. The guide gives one mile on gravel roads.[^3][^4] Neither mentions a closure, so we list the site as open to hikers. Check current conditions with the Forest Service before you go.
