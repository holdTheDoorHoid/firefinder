The tower stood on Cornell Hill in Luther Forest.[^1] The New York Conservation Commission erected a 60-foot Aermotor fire tower there in 1924.[^2] Sources disagree on the model. Bill Starr and the Town of Wilton say LS-40, while the National Historic Lookout Register says LS-20.[^1][^4][^2]

Noah LaCasse was the first forest fire observer, working the tower from 1924 to 1935.[^1] The state ran the lookout under an easement with Luther Forest until the end of the 1970 fire season, when the tower was decommissioned.[^1] The Saratogian names Jim Crammond as the last observer but gives no date.[^3]

Afterward, the sources differ on who owned the tower. Starr says Luther Forest acquired it for a nominal payment.[^1] The Saratogian says William Mackay bought it from the state for $1 in 1976.[^3] Starr also reports that the tower was subject to vandalism after the state gave it up.[^1]

In August 2010, Dave Vana's crew started taking the tower apart at Cornell Hill.[^1] Luther Forest Corp. covered the restoration cost and handed the tower over to the Town of Wilton.[^3] The tower went up again at Camp Saratoga in December 2010, although the National Historic Lookout Register dates the relocation to 2011.[^2] A dedication followed on May 14, 2011.[^1]

Today the tower stands at Camp Saratoga, 93 Scout Road, in the Wilton Wildlife Preserve & Park.[^4][^2] According to the Town of Wilton, the tower is open on the third weekend of each month from April through October, 10am to 3pm, and also on October 2 and 3 during foliage season.[^4] In 2011 the Saratogian reported that a volunteer guide had to go along with visitors.[^3]

No source we read describes the original Cornell Hill site today, after the tower was taken down.
