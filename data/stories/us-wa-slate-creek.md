Slate Creek's lookout stood on Lead Hill, in the Colville National Forest, about 11 miles northeast of Metaline Falls in Pend Oreille County.[^1][^2][^3] The summit sits at 5,474 feet.[^1]

A lookout went up on the summit in 1931, built to the L-4 design.[^1][^3] The sources disagree on what kind of structure it was. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register calls it a tower, while WillhiteWeb calls it a cab.[^1][^3]

The sources name one person who worked the lookout, and only for 1932. A Metaline Falls newspaper item from that year, quoted by two of the sources, placed Wayne Myers on a lookout job on what the paper called the Kaniksu forest, stationed just above the Leadhill camp on Slate Creek.[^3][^4]

The register and WillhiteWeb both say the lookout was removed in the 1950s.[^1][^3] Fire Lookout's page says it was destroyed in that decade instead, so the sources do not agree on how it ended.[^2]

The only remains the sources describe are a lookout footing set beside a tree on the summit, shown in WillhiteWeb's photos. The page describes conditions as of 2024.[^3]

Getting there means a walk past gates. The signed Slate Creek Road leaves Highway 31 north of Metaline Falls.[^3] A gate at the highway blocks the road from April 1 to June 30. From July, you can drive about 7 miles before a locked gate.[^3] Past the gate, WillhiteWeb calls the route easy road walking. It lists the hike at 2.5 miles one way, with 1,100 feet of climbing.[^3]
