Bear Camp Basin, also called Bear Camp, is a lookout site at 4,973 feet in Curry County, Oregon, on the Siskiyou National Forest.[^1][^2][^3]

The earliest structure is hard to date. A 1914 newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says Charles Simons had built some kind of tower at his lookout, which gave a much better view.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register instead dates a 10-foot platform to around 1918, and firelookout.com gives about 1918 as well.[^1][^2]

A shake cabin built in 1924 replaced the platform.[^1][^2] A 1915 item described a cabin being built on the California Bulletin plan, and the sources do not say whether that was the same building.[^3] In 1932 an L-4 cab replaced the shake cabin.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts describes that year's house as an Aladdin style lookout house.[^3]

Charles Simons was back on the job at Bear Camp in 1919, after military training.[^3] The Siskiyou Bulletin reported in 1932 that J.P. Emery had spent each summer since 1927 in lookout work.[^3] In 1941 the station was staffed for 100 days.[^3]

In September 1932 Emery was sent to two small fires near his station and put both out.[^3] In August 1933 lookout Lawson Thomas reported a smoke to the Rand Ranger Station, and a 15-man crew was on its way to the fire by 7:35 in the morning.[^3]

Forest Lookouts also lists an Aircraft Warning Service station, George 8-2, that was active from June 10 to October 16, 1943.[^3]

The register says the L-4 cab was removed in 1965.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2]

No source we read describes what remains of the lookout at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
