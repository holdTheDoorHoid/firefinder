Sugar Mountain lies in the Monashee Mountains, in the Regional District of North Okanagan, British Columbia.[^1][^2] A hiking blog says that, compared with neighbouring Monashee summits, this one stands out for its intact, abandoned lookout station.[^2]

Firelookout.ca gives the elevation as 2,162 m (7,093 ft), while a Lumby Valley Times column gives about 2,184 m (7,165 ft), so the sources differ slightly.[^1][^3]

The first building is dated two ways. The Lumby Valley Times column says a temporary structure went up in 1927 and was later upgraded to a typical 1940s-style lookout tower.[^3] Firelookout.ca says it was built sometime in the 1930s.[^1]

In the 1940s, before Sugar Lake was flooded, Lavington orchardist Erskine Burnett and Bill Fraser set out for the Sugar Mountain Forest Fire Lookout.[^3] Tom Patrick held the lookout post when Erskine took these photographs.[^3]

The column says a 2008 demolition had been planned, but the building was later saved and restored.[^3] Firelookout.ca dates the restoration to around 2006, which is earlier than that 2008 demolition date, so the two accounts do not agree on the timing.[^1]

To reach it, the blog's directions start in Cherryville, where you turn north at Frank's General Store onto Sugar Lake Road.[^2] The blog says that road stays in good condition year-round and can be driven in a 2WD vehicle.[^2] Just before Sugar Lake, turn right onto the Kate Creek forestry road, and park 17.6 km from Sugar Lake Road at the obviously logged road that climbs the mountain.[^2] From the trailhead the top is roughly 10 km away, on a route the blog says is better classified as an ATV trail.[^2] Kate Creek road is generally not plowed in winter unless logging is happening in the area.[^2]
