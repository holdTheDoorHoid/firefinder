In October 1950 the Post-Star reported that a state fire tower for the southern part of Washington County was about to go up. A tower had been shipped to the Town of Jackson and was being stored temporarily on a private property there while the state picked a site, and Mount Colfax was generally considered the likely site, though two other sites were also under consideration.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1950 as the build year.[^2] A Times Record piece from April 1951, though, still describes the tower as being built on Mount Colfax, so the year it went up is uncertain.[^1]

The tower is an 80-foot Aermotor LS40 steel structure.[^2][^1] The 1951 paper said it would be 81 feet high, the tallest in that section.[^1]

That same paper named Ernest Bennett as the tower's observer. The state's observer roster lists Ernest L. Bennett from April 1951 to November 1966, and Jennie B. Bennett from April 1967 to November 1970.[^1][^3]

The tower was closed for the season by November 1953, and emergency fire calls were to go to the ranger in Hudson Falls.[^1]

The Register says the tower was closed to the public in 1970.[^2] The state roster says it ceased operations that same year.[^3] It did not come down. According to the roster, the structure was later used to carry two-way radio gear and antennas for several agencies.[^3]

Today the Register describes a fenced site where the tower carries radio repeater gear for the state Department of Environmental Conservation.[^2] The Register lists that department as its administrator, and it was registered on December 23, 2022, as US 1700, NY 52, nominated by Laurie Rankin.[^2] Because it is fenced and closed to the public, it is not a place to visit or climb.
