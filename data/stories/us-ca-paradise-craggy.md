The National Historic Lookout Register dates the first Paradise Craggy lookout to 1935, a Civilian Conservation Corps era station built for the U.S. Forest Service.[^1] The Forest Lookouts collection points to earlier work. A 1931 item mentions a road being built to the Paradise lookout, and a Klamath National Forest chronology entry dated February 23, 1932 describes a telephone line running to a new lookout house, though the same entry names Saturday, September 27th, so that year is uncertain.[^2] The sources disagree on the date, and this story keeps both.

The California Department of Forestry took over the lookout in 1948.[^1] In November 1948 a Dunsmuir paper reported that Mrs. E. E. Boehm had recently completed a season there, working out of Yreka.[^2] In 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service ran an observation post from the lookout.[^2]

In 1969 a CDF-designed cab, Plan Number 284, replaced an earlier structure the register calls ECW.[^1] Lightning destroyed that cab in 1989.[^1] The present cab is based on the 732-GA plan, and the register says no other cab in CDF&FP's detection system is a "ground cab."[^1]

The register lists the site as administered by the Siskiyou Unit of Cal Fire and says it was registered on October 27, 2019.[^1] The pages I could reach do not say whether the lookout is staffed today or whether the public can visit it, so check with Cal Fire before planning a trip.
