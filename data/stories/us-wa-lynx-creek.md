Lynx Creek, also called Lynx Mountain, is a lookout site in Ferry County, Washington, on the Colville Indian Reservation.[^1][^2] The earliest structure listed there is a log cabin just below the summit, dated to 1926 in the Kresek inventory.[^1][^2]

The same inventory lists a frame cab on the west summit in 1932 and a 30-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab in 1938.[^1][^2] It says the tower was abandoned in 1952.[^1][^2]

The earliest staffing record we found is a September 14, 1931 newspaper item, quoted on two lookout sites, which reports that the Cody Butte and Lynx Creek lookouts were relieved after rain on Saturday night.[^3][^4]

The sources disagree on what is still standing. Firelookout.com lists the lookout as gone, but notes that the 1926 cabin was still there in 2014.[^1] A hiking guide who visited in 2019 says the cabin still stands, while noting that the lookout community debates whether it counts as a standing lookout in its original place.[^2] Willhite describes the cabin as living quarters for the lookout on the summit, and does not list it as a standing lookout.[^4]

In July 2021 the Summit Trail Fire burned through much of the Lynx Creek drainage.[^2] The hiking guide says lightning started it on July 12 and that it burned nearly 50,000 acres.[^2] The same guide says crews reportedly applied structure protection to the cabin, and that it survived. That is the only account we read of the cabin's fate in the fire.[^2]

Willhite found no footings and no steel at the summit, and suggests that any steel tower was removed from the site.[^4] He rates access as suitable for most cars if driven carefully, with a hike of one to two miles and about 600 feet of climb.[^4] The hiking guide warns that fire damage and road conditions could make the site harder to reach.[^2]

The site is on the Colville Indian Reservation.[^1][^2] The Colville Tribes list reservation permits and passes for purchase.[^5] None of the sources we read says which one applies here, so ask the Tribes before you go.
