Ojibway Knoll sits in the Kaniksu National Forest in Pend Oreille County, about 15 miles north-northwest of Newport.[^1][^2] The lookout was a crow's nest.[^1][^2] The register dates it to 1936, when a crow's nest stood on the site with a garage cabin an eighth of a mile to the west.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb, citing what it calls the Kresek inventory, gives the same year and says the crow's nest was abandoned in 1948.[^3]

Willhite visited the knoll and found two bed frames, about 90 percent buried by earth.[^3] He noted that the south side of the summit still has some open areas, and he thought the base of the crow's nest tree might still be discoverable.[^3] Besides the bed frames, his photo captions list a phone line and metal sheets.[^3]

Willhite's route starts at Usk and crosses the Pend Oreille River on Kings Lake Road.[^3] It then follows forest road FR50 up to a ridge and continues on a steep, rocky track to a saddle between two knobs.[^3] From the end of the track it is about a quarter mile of hiking, then a bushwhack to the top.[^3] He advises a high-clearance vehicle.[^3] None of the three pages mentions a closure.[^1][^2][^3]
