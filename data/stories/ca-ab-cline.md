Cline Fire Lookout is in David Thompson Country, Alberta, at about 2,050 m (6,726 ft).[^1] A hiker's trip report dated March 15, 2014 gives that place and height.[^1] The writer calls the lookout a bump that barely clears the treeline on the southwest corner of the Mount Michener massif, and says the name is official.[^1] In 2014 the writer found a big fire lookout structure and a helicopter pad on the summit.[^1]

Before Abraham Lake was formed, people went up often, the writer says. By the time of the trip the trails had degraded, and reaching the top meant bushwhacking almost from the bottom up.[^1]

Alberta's government counts 127 wildfire lookout sites in the boreal forest and along the Rocky Mountains.[^2] The province says the sites are picked for a clear view, which helps spot and report fires.[^2] Alberta Wildfire's blog describes lookout observers as watchers for the forestry service across alpine, foothill and boreal Alberta.[^3]

The 2014 report is the only source we read that describes the summit. No source we read says what is there today or whether the public may visit. Ask the province before going, and do not climb or enter any structure you find without permission.
