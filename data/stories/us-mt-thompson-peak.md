Thompson Peak stands in Sanders County, about 13 miles northwest of Hot Springs, in the Lolo National Forest, at an elevation of 7,460 feet.[^1] The site's record places a 30-foot pole L-4 tower there in 1934.[^1] The same entry marks the tower as gone in 1965 and gives no detail about how it came down.[^1]

One detail survives in the record: a crow's nest about three miles west, on Cook, is listed as still there.[^1] It sits three miles from the peak, so it is not on the summit.[^1]

Sanders County is not the only place with a Thompson Peak.[^2] The state list shows a separate Thompson Peak in Mineral County, with a three-story cab still standing.[^2] One Montana history site files a Thompson Peak under Sanders County, yet it also quotes a June 1947 newspaper item about a Thompson peak near Superior, so some of its details may describe a different peak.[^3]

No source read for this story describes the Sanders County summit today.[^1][^2][^3] None says whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
