Gore Mountain lies in Avery's Gore, an uninhabited gore of Essex County, about 6 miles south of the Canada–US border.[^3]

The lookout story starts in 1912. The Vermont state forester's fourth annual report says local landowners in northern Essex County had arranged to equip a station on the mountain. It adds that a telephone line was under construction and that a trail already reached the summit, where a lookout tower was planned.[^1] Around 1924 the state listed a new steel tower at Gore as constructed or under construction.[^1] In 1936 the 121st Company of the Civilian Conservation Corps erected a steel tower with stairs and a glass-enclosed cab to replace an old tower, and built a new cabin for the watchman.[^1]

Upkeep is recorded through the 1930s and 1940s. Two new foundation piers were poured because of settling in the early 1940s, and the telephone line was rebuilt by 1950.[^1]

Watchmen appear in newspaper and state report items. Walter Harlow finished work at the tower in November 1945.[^1] Lyle Hopkins arrived at the tower on April 15, 1953, and in 1956 he was still named as the watchman at Gore.[^1]

A 1968 federal survey description, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, put the survey station at the center of the top of a lookout tower about 60 feet high.[^1] The Vermont lookout list now marks the tower as gone, and the Forest Lookouts page gives its status as "Removed."[^1][^2]

A hiker's trip log, quoted on a New England hiking forum, describes the summit as a clearing where only the foundations of an old fire tower are left, beside a fire warden's cabin that is still usable.[^4] The log itself carries no date, and the forum thread was last edited in August 2007.[^4] The same forum post says the trailhead is on Route 114 near the north end of Norton Pond.[^4] We found no source on who owns the summit or whether visitors are welcome there.
