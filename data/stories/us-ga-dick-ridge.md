The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Dick Ridge fire tower in 1941.[^1][^2] The ridge is in Walker County, Georgia, in the Chattahoochee National Forest.[^2] One tower list places the site in Whitfield County instead.[^3] The tower stood 60 feet tall on treated wooden poles, with an 8-by-8-foot wooden cab and a catwalk with handrails around the outside.[^1]

The district's history says construction on the Chestnut Mountain and Dick Ridge towers was about to begin.[^1] It describes a Forest Service telephone system linking the lookout towers and other key points to a CCC camp and the district ranger's office.[^1]

The tower had at least one operator.[^1] An interview printed in the same history names Harry Copeland as the man on Dick's Ridge. He lived just off the mountain and kept the post until he left for Bowaters.[^1] The speaker dates that move only loosely, to around the early 1950s.[^1]

The district history says the tower was dismantled in 1960.[^1]

No source we found describes what remains at the site today. The same history mentions a 6.2-mile loop, the Chickamauga Creek Trail, which circles Dick Ridge from the end of Ponder Creek Road. It is limited to foot travel.[^1] The history does not say when that trail description was written, so it may be out of date.
