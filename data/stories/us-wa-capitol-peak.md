Capitol Peak is about 12 miles southwest of Olympia, in Thurston County.[^2] The Washington State Division of Forestry/DNR administered its lookout.[^1]

The first tower went up in 1929. It was a tripod set on four topped trees, with a floor 84 feet above the ground.[^1][^3][^4] The register gives the tower's height as 114 feet, while a 1930 Timberman account, quoted by the Forest Lookouts page and by Willhite, gives 119 feet from the ground to the lookout floor, so the sources disagree.[^1][^3][^4] The Washington Forest Fire Association's 1934 report called the design temporary, and the register says the tower blew down that year.[^1][^3] Willhite cites the same report as suggesting it was brought down in 1934.[^4]

A 100-foot creosoted timber tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab followed in 1935.[^1][^3]

The register says the U.S. Army removed that tower in 1942 to make room for a radar station, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2] The record gets tangled after that. The register, citing one source, says an 83-foot tower built in 1943 blew down in 1946 and that a 68-foot wooden tower replaced it the same year.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page quotes a 1946 forestry report describing an 83-foot ring tower built to replace one demolished by wind.[^3]

In 1954 a 45-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab went up.[^1][^2] The register says the last tower was removed in 1967 during the DNR's first round of phasing out lookouts, but Willhite's photo captions say the tower removal came in 1968.[^1][^2][^4]

Willhite's page includes a photo note labeled 2020 that describes a gate up to the communication and lookout site, with a large parking area, where many visitors walk the road to the view, though Willhite adds "while it lasts."[^4] A photo caption there says two tower footings are still at the summit, and the caption gives no date.[^4] A 1975 survey team noted several microwave and communication towers around the top.[^4] Willhite lists the access as good gravel roads, but gives no rules for the gate.[^4]
