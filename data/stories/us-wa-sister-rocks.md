Sister Rocks is a pair of rocky prominences beside the Trapper Creek Wilderness, in Skamania County, Washington.[^2][^3] A 1912 Columbia National Forest map already marks a lookout symbol there.[^1] WillhiteWeb suggests the site may have been one of the earliest in the area, since it sits just north of the 1902 Yacolt Burn.[^1]

The lookout was an L-4 cab, reported as built in 1931.[^3][^4] A panoramic view of the site taken in 1934 is credited to the National Archives' Pacific Northwest Region.[^3]

The cab is gone now.[^3][^4] Willhite reports that the main center firefinder post still stands.[^1] Visitors walk in: his route leaves the Observation Peak Trail and turns onto the Sister Rocks Trail, a hike he lists as 1.6 miles.[^1] An Oregon Hikers guide lists the walk as a short hike to a rocky viewpoint, and notes a vertical rusting pipe on the higher rock, though nobody seems to know what it was for.[^2] The site lies within the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^3]
