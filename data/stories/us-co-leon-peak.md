The Leon Peak Lookout sits on Leon Peak in the Grand Mesa National Forest, in Mesa County, Colorado, at about 11,196 feet.[^1]

The register says the lookout was designed in 1911, before lookout designs had been standardized, and that it is built of logs and corrugated sheet metal.[^1] Newspapers from August 1911 describe a 12-foot-square house with a cupola, nearing completion on the summit. Ranger Clay Wintersteen put it up and was due to have charge of it that season.[^2] The register gives 1912 as the build year, while a 2014 trip report says it was built in 1911 or 1912.[^1][^4]

Staff were posted there in the early years. Carl Pfeiffer was sent up to take charge in August 1912, and Ben Suchre was at the station in September 1913.[^2] In late September 1913 a local paper reported numerous smokes from the lookout and no fires.[^2] In September 1914 Forest Guard Bloss was sent to the station for the fire-danger period.[^2]

A 2014 trip report says guards were stationed there only through the summer of 1915, and that constant lightning strikes forced it out of use.[^4] The register says the station was dropped after a few years, citing frequent lightning.[^1]

The register says the log frame still stands after a century of severe weather.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as standing, and uses an asterisk for a structure whose cab is no longer in place.[^3]

Visitors reach the summit on foot.[^4][^5] One guide gives the hike as five miles round trip from a trailhead near Trickle Park Reservoir.[^5] Another guide warns that the drive to the trailhead may involve rough roads.[^6] A trip report describes leaving the trail for a ridge of boulders, and warns that the frame may not bear a climber's weight.[^4] The sources read say nothing about whether the Forest Service has closed the site or offers it for rent.
