Baseline Mountain sits in the foothills of the Rockies, in Alberta.[^4] The original lookout on the mountain was a small wooden groundhouse, 3 m (10 ft) square, that the federal Canadian Forest Service built in the 1920s.[^1][^3] Provincial staff ran it until the late 1940s.[^1]

Then a new 24 m (80 ft) tower and cabin went up about 0.8 km (half a mile) to the west, and the groundhouse was abandoned.[^1] The register says the successor is still in active service as an Alberta Land & Forest Service lookout.[^1]

Travel Alberta gives a different timeline. It says the fire tower was upgraded in the 1990s to a new site on the adjacent summit, with an access road.[^4] The register's late-1940s date and that 1990s date do not agree, and the sources we read do not explain the gap. No source we read gives a firm build year for the tower that stands on the summit today.

The tower is staffed in season, usually from May to October.[^3] Travel Alberta says people can reach the summit on foot, in four-wheel-drive vehicles or on off-highway vehicles, using the main access road or side trails.[^4] A route guide places an active fire lookout at the top of Baseline Mountain and names the Baseline service road as the way up.[^3] We found no source that says the public may climb the active tower, and none says it is closed. Do not climb it.

The original groundhouse is no longer in use.[^1] A trail guide calls the old lookout a worthy destination and mentions a small shelter at the top.[^2]
