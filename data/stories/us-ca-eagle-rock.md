Eagle Rock is a summit behind Boulder Creek in Santa Cruz County, California, in the Santa Cruz Mountains.[^1][^4] In November 1934 a lookout tower was going up there as one link in the county's fire suppression system.[^1] A newspaper of the day said men from the Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Big Basin had been put to work on it.[^1] A 2018 report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association says the tower was built in 1934 by the CCC.[^2]

A July 1938 Santa Cruz Evening News item previewed the dedication of the new tower for the next day.[^1] The same item placed the tower on the Locatelli ranch toward the northern end of Empire Grade.[^1]

A 1975 newspaper story said that veteran lookout Clifford "Mac" McLaughlin had spent 17 years at the tower for the state forestry agency, and that he had reluctantly given up the post.[^1] The lookout was a California Department of Forestry lookout, though the 1975 story and a local wiki call the agency the California Division of Forestry.[^1][^3]

By 2018 the old lookout was decommissioned, and a local wiki says it had been boarded up for years.[^2][^3] On August 4, 2018, it burned to the ground.[^2][^3][^4] The fire spread into vegetation and grew to about half an acre to an acre, and Cal Fire named it the Eagle Fire.[^2] Cal Fire said the fire was currently under investigation.[^2]

A local wiki says the lookout site is in Little Basin, and that Hewlett-Packard transferred ownership of Little Basin to the Peninsula Open Space Trust and the Sempervirens Fund in 2007.[^3] The same wiki describes the Eagle Rock Trail as a difficult 3.5-mile round trip, and says Little Basin State Park cannot serve as a parking spot for it.[^3] A guide page describes the summit road as unpaved and says 4WD vehicles are required.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, and no current notice on whether visitors may go there.
