The Georgia Forestry Commission's history says the first fire observation tower went up on the Ware Hotel in Waycross, possibly as a result of a 1928 forestry fair in that city.[^1]

A Georgia lookout tower list places a tower named "curry" in Decatur County, at about 30.9 degrees north and 84.4 degrees west.[^2] The name is a label rather than a record. The list says its names for towers numbered 73 and above are its compiler's own, and this one is numbered 149.[^2]

Since the late 1980s, Georgia has cut its tower staff in favor of air patrols.[^3] A forestry commission spreadsheet from earlier in 2006 showed that 137 of the state's 144 still-existing towers were no longer staffed.[^3] Of those, 119 were listed as antenna towers, which are usually unoccupied, though forestry staff climb up now and then during fire season.[^3]

Across the state, towers were being disposed of through auctions, scrap sales and sales to hunting clubs and similar groups.[^3]

No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
