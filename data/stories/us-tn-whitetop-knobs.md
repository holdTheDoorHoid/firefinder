Whitetop Knobs sits in Sullivan County, Tennessee, at about 2,054 feet.[^2] A Tennessean report dated December 9, 1913, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said the government had finished its first fire lookout in the Appalachian Forest reserve, in the area the report calls White Top, near Bristol.[^1] The page ties that tower to Whitetop Knobs.[^1]

The newspaper said the tower stood on a high peak, tall enough that someone familiar with the country could locate a fire in North Carolina, Virginia or Tennessee through a field glass.[^1] It said the towers were built of heavy timbers, anchored twelve feet underground, and that several more were to be erected and connected by telephone.[^1]

Both pages list the tower as removed.[^1][^2] Neither describes what, if anything, remains on the high ground today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2]
