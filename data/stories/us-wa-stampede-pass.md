The Stampede Pass lookout was built in 1934 in King County, Washington, and its site sits at 3,963 feet, or about 1,208 meters.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association registers it as US 1299.[^1] Its entry gives a 40-foot treated timber L-4 tower on a small peak roughly three-quarters of a mile southeast of the pass.[^1] A hobby guide gives the same 1934 date and describes a wood tower of 40 feet with an L-4 cab, set on a knoll south of the pass.[^4] The sources we read do not say why it was put up.

Our sources do not name anyone who staffed the lookout, and none describes a notable fire it watched. A 20-foot L-4 tower reportedly stood about a mile north of it for a time.[^1] From August 1942 to September 1943 the Aircraft Warning Service used the site.[^3][^4] A ground cabin was built there, because the lofty tower was chilly and noisy in gusts.[^4]

In 1974 a helicopter moved the tower to Camp Waskowitz, a conservation camp near North Bend, where it was kept as a permanent display.[^1][^2] A hobby guide says the military called the move a training exercise and carried the tower in two parts, about 20 miles from the pass to North Bend.[^4] A Forest Lookouts page says only the cab was flown there and set atop a new tower.[^3] The sources disagree on whether the whole tower or only the cab was moved, and we could not settle it.

The original site is a small knoll near the pass, and hikers can reach it along the PCT as well as by road.[^4] Travel is mostly on good gravel, though the last stretch has potholes and ruts.[^4] A visitor in 2014 saw two or more concrete footings still on the site, and we found no later description of it.[^4] A list at Firelookout.org marks the pass site as having no tower.[^5]

The Camp Waskowitz tower is still listed as standing, and the sources describe it as used for education.[^5][^3] We found nothing on whether visitors may go to the camp or see the tower, so ask the camp before going. Please do not climb any tower without permission.
