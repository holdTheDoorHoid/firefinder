The Comers Rock lookout went up in 1931, according to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register.[^1] The register describes a wooden cab, 19 by 14 feet, set on stone.[^1] A local history recounts a 1934 Appalachian Trail guide that mentioned a fire tower at the summit.[^3] The same history cites Forest Service documents that date the overlook's development to the 1930s, when it served as a fire tower.[^3]

According to the register, a private owner bought the cab in 1956.[^1] Forest Lookouts words the same year as a private purchase of the lookout site.[^2] The local history dates a remodel of the site into an observation platform to the 1960s.[^3]

Today the register lists the site as a public scenic lookout, part of the Mount Rogers National Recreation Area in the Jefferson National Forest.[^1] Per the register, a concrete slab was poured over the footer, with stone pillars and wooden rails above it.[^1] The Forest Service's recreation area there includes a campground and a historic picnic area, and its recreation page lists 360-degree views from the overlook and the former fire tower.[^4]

The Forest Service says the campground is a short walk from long-distance hiking in the Little Dry Run Wilderness.[^4] The register puts the summit in Wythe County, while the local history places the Comers Rock community in northern Grayson County.[^1][^3]
