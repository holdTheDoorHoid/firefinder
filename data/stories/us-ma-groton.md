The register places the Groton Fire Tower on Gibbet Hill in the town of Groton, Middlesex County.[^1] The first lookout on the site was a 70-foot wooden tower furnished by the U.S. Forest Service in 1941.[^1] A Lowell paper had reported in 1940 that a lookout was to be built there that fall, and in September 1941 a Fitchburg paper noted a man attending the "fire watch tower on Gibbet hill."[^2]

The register dates the steel tower that followed to 1957. It stands 68 feet tall, with a 10-by-10-foot wooden cab and, beneath it, a smaller cab.[^1] In April 1957 a Lowell paper reported charges of patronage in choosing men to staff the area's forest fire towers.[^2]

The stone ruin on the same hill is a different structure.[^3][^4] General William Bancroft began building a retirement home there in 1906. He planned a castle-like mansion beside a modest bungalow, but his money ran out before construction was finished.[^3] A firecracker accident on the Fourth of July in 1932 burned much of the building, and the stone shell left behind is what locals call Bancroft's Castle.[^4] A trail page titled "Bancroft's Castle, Groton Fire Tower" runs the two together in its title, though its text describes the castle ruin and the steel tower separately.[^4]

The register entry dates from December 2004.[^1] A 2026 trail write-up describes the 68-foot steel tower as staffed in fire season, and says its writers were invited up by the lookout operator.[^4] That is one secondhand account, and I found no official record of the tower's condition since 2004. The owners of Gibbet Hill welcome visitors to the property and its trails, but no source I read says whether the public may climb the tower, so ask the owners before going up.[^3]
