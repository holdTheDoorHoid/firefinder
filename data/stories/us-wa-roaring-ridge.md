Roaring Ridge rises about five miles south of Snoqualmie Pass.[^1] Driving east over the pass on I-90, it stands out as a peak across Keechelus Lake.[^2] The register places the site in the Wenatchee National Forest, administered by the U.S. Forest Service.[^3]

The register lists the first lookout here as an L-4 tower built in 1937.[^3] A later site account argues that something stood on the summit earlier, because panoramic photos taken on August 10, 1934 show a platform about 40 feet above the ground.[^2]

In 1948 a 40-foot treated timber L-4 tower replaced it.[^3][^1] The willhiteweb.com account gives 1948 or 1958 for that change, and notes that the USGS recorded the lookout as new when it benchmarked the summit in 1958.[^2]

The register says the 1948 tower was removed in 1965.[^3] The firelookout.com page says it was destroyed in 1968, and a hiking blog says the towers on the peak were removed as late as 1968.[^1][^4] The willhiteweb.com account gives 1965 or 1968 and reports that the USGS described the older lookout as torn down.[^2]

Today the summit is turning into forest, and what remains is wreckage rather than a tower.[^2] The willhiteweb.com account describes heavy wreckage from both structures, two sets of footings beside each other, and plenty of decayed debris hidden in the brush to the west.[^2] A photo on the register is labelled "Remnants July 2000."[^3]

To get there, the willhiteweb.com account says to take I-90 Exit 62, turn onto the unsigned Roaring Ridge Road, and follow it about 3.5 miles to the end. It recommends high clearance because of rough spots.[^2] A minor trail climbs the north ridge from the road's end.[^2] The site sits on national forest land and is reached by road and trail, so it is public, though no source confirms current rules.[^3][^2]
