Quebec's old network of fire towers has mostly gone. A 2015 SOPFEU blog post says a few still exist, though most were destroyed for safety reasons.[^1] The post calls the towers tours d'observation, or observation towers, and describes them as the backbone of Quebec's forest-fire detection.[^1]

The network's history, as the post tells it, starts in 1910, when the first tower used to spot forest fires in Quebec went up south of Lac Matapédia in the Gaspésie.[^1] It peaked in the late 1930s with more than 500 towers.[^1] Typically they had a steel frame topped by an octagonal wooden cab where the watchman worked.[^1]

The post also describes the tower on Mont du Général-Allard in the present tense. According to the post, the tower is 18.3 m (60 ft) tall and stands on the summit of Mont Général Allard, on the Valcartier military base.[^1] The mountain's official name is Mont du Général-Allard, in the municipality of Saint-Gabriel-de-Valcartier.[^2] OpenStreetMap maps the spot as a fire lookout.[^3]

The post calls this the last tower in use in Quebec and says it was used until 1981.[^1] It also says detection aircraft were not allowed to patrol the area, so the watchman still had a reason to be there.[^1] Those two statements do not sit easily together, and the post does not explain how the watch ended.[^1]

No source found describes the tower today, and none says whether the public can reach the summit. The tower sits on a military base, so check before going.[^1]
