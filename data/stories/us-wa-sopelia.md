The Sopelia site got its cab in 1959: an R-6 flat cab raised on concrete pillars.[^1][^2] The register says it took the place of the nearby Simcoe Butte Lookout.[^1] Firelookout and Willhite both say it was the site's sole lookout structure.[^2][^3]

Sources agree that the cab burned in 2013.[^1][^2][^3] Washington Lookouts dates the burn to July 25, 2013, when the Mile Marker 28 Fire overran it.[^4] Willhite says it sat unused for many years, while the register describes several years of abandonment.[^3][^1]

Willhite says the land belongs to a private timber owner, and he lists access as limited to the landowner, with no trespass.[^3] He adds that the burned lookout is visible from the air, and that the site name is laid out in white rocks.[^3] The register and Firelookout both place the site on the Yakama Indian Reservation,[^1][^2] and the register names the Bureau of Indian Affairs as administrator.[^1] The sources do not agree on who owns or manages the ground, so Willhite's no-trespass note is the one to heed.
