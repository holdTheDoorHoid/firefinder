The Bald Knob fire tower is in Cass Scenic Railroad State Park, in Pocahontas County, West Virginia.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register notes that only a handful of U.S. mountaintop lookouts can be reached by steam train, and this one is among them. The Register listed the tower on December 15, 1995, as US 149, WV 4.[^1]

In 1916 the site was a watch station. A description of that year, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the station had a watchman who lived there while on duty, and that with no road, visitors had to walk in from Cass.[^2]

The dates of the tower itself are disputed. The Register says it was built in the early 1930s, on land then owned by the Mower Lumber Company.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page quotes a note that a 53-foot Blaw-Knox steel tower was erected in 1923, and a February 1930 newspaper that calls the top of the knob the new steel fire tower.[^2] We could not tell whether the 1923 structure is the tower standing today. The Register gives the height as 47 feet, while the Forest Lookouts page gives 53.[^1][^2]

Watch duty ended in 1977. The Register says the lookout was staffed until then, and the Forest Lookouts page gives 1977 as the last year it was staffed.[^1][^2] In April 1958, when snow blocked the road, a newspaper quoted on the same page reported that staff from the Paddy's Knob tower reached Bald Knob on snowshoes to man it.[^2]

The state park was established in 1963 and took over the former company town of Cass.[^3] A 2021 article says the park also took possession of the Bald Knob tower and cabin.[^3] According to the park's superintendent, the cabin was rentable until about 2011 to 2015, when the railroad's operations changed, and after that it stood unused.[^3] The owner of Autumn Breeze Stables made repairs to the cabin, and by June 2021 it was open for booking.[^3] The park sought a contractor for the tower, which was still under construction then.[^3]

Excursion trains pause for about 20 minutes at the Bald Knob overlook.[^4] A railroad history page says the overlook is not the summit, and that a fire tower at the summit was abandoned years ago, once trees grew tall enough to hide the view.[^4] We found no later source on the tower's condition, and the 2021 article describes its repair as still in progress.[^3] Check with Cass Scenic Railroad State Park before you plan a visit, and do not climb the tower unless the park says it is open.
