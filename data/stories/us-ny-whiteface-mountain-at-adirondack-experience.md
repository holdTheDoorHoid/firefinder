The state set up an observation station on the summit of Whiteface Mountain in July 1909. At first there was no tower, only a pole frame under a canvas tent that sheltered the observer.[^1] A 1910 state report gives the cost of the lookout as $1,559.31.[^2] In 1919 a 22-foot steel tower replaced the pole station.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on the build year. A 1972 newspaper item calls the dismantled tower "built in 1909."[^2] The marker page includes the line "Erected 1909," but its text says the steel tower was made in 1919.[^1]

Observers came and went through the 1910s and 1920s. Sam Cheltham was fire observer until November 1, 1918, and was back on the job by May 1919.[^2] George Marshall held the post in 1920.[^2] The state adopted standard observer's cabin plans in 1922, and a new cabin on Whiteface that year followed the 12-by-16-foot plan.[^3][^2]

Work also reached the summit. A CCC crew drilled holes in the rocky peak for steel poles, part of a new telephone line from the base to the tower in 1934.[^2] In 1929 five caddy boys damaged the tower. A district ranger let them off if they bought a new window sash, and they did.[^2] By 1946 U.S. weather observers also worked on the peak, and Riley Lawrence was fire observer on the state tower for the rest of that season.[^2]

A 1942 Coast and Geodetic Survey description calls the tower a structural steel lookout about 50 feet high, which does not match the 22 feet given for 1919.[^2]

The steel tower came down in May 1972. A newspaper item from that month says crews had taken it apart into roughly 200 pieces.[^2] The marker page says it was dismantled that year under a wilderness plan and re-erected at the Adirondack Museum in 1973.[^1] The same page says a stone observation tower built in 1935 supplanted the steel one, which conflicts with the 1942 description and the 1972 item.[^1][^2]

Today the tower is an outdoor exhibit at the Adirondack Museum on Blue Mountain Lake.[^4] The Adirondack Architectural Heritage list calls the museum the Adirondack Experience.[^5] The state's fire tower page says the tower looks out over the museum grounds and the mountains and lakes around it.[^4] The marker is displayed with it.[^1] The sources I read do not say whether visitors can climb the cab or what admission applies, so ask the museum before you go up.
