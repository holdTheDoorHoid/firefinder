Bunker Hill sits in the Eldorado National Forest, west of Lake Tahoe, at about 7,500 feet.[^1][^5] The Forest Service planned a lookout there after a 1928 road survey.[^3] By 1932 a lookout station was in use, and Joe Baumann was the lookout there.[^3]

The first station was a live-in cab, 14 by 14 feet.[^1] Later the site was picked for a stone tower. The NHLR says it was one of three sites chosen for this kind of stone tower, and Campflare says only three structures like it stand in California's national forests.[^1][^5] HistoriCorps says the design came from Region 5 architect Kepler Johnson in 1939, that the stone base was finished by 1942, and that the wooden observation cab went on in 1949.[^2] The NHLR gives 1942 as the build year and names Joe Bauman as the builder.[^1]

A 2006 visitor noted the lookout looked manned seasonally, probably during lightning storms.[^1] No source read names a particular fire the tower watched.

The same 2006 visitor counted three antennas on the building, which would indicate a Forest Service repeater site.[^1] HistoriCorps ran volunteer sessions in 2024 for roof repair, removing the brick chimney, and fixing water damage, with repointing and paint to follow.[^2] Campflare says that work was finished in 2024.[^5] A 2018 report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association also notes that an unused $9.38 of a 2016 grant to the site was returned.[^4]

Campflare expected the lookout to open as a recreation rental in 2025, but it lists the site as closed and says the site is not listed on Recreation.gov.[^5] The 2006 visit report says the road is gated quite a way from the lookout.[^1] Check with the Eldorado National Forest before planning a visit, and do not enter or climb the tower unless the Forest Service has opened it to visitors.[^1][^5]
