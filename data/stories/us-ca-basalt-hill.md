The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1947 as the build year for Basalt Hill Lookout, and Wikipedia says 1947 or 1945.[^1][^2] A 1945 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, reports a station already running on the hill with Vern Yarborough in charge.[^3] The Register's own description says the lookout was built in the 1950s, so the sources do not agree on when it went up.[^1]

The hill had a job to do. A January 1948 Modesto Bee clipping explains that fires in the valley and along the north slope of the Diablo Range had been hard to pin down from Pacheco Peak. A post on Basalt Hill would form a triangle with Pacheco Peak and Call Mountain, so that the lines of sight from all three could cross at a fire's exact location.[^3] The same clipping says the new tower was still being built, a 16-foot-square structure with a garage on the ground floor, living quarters above, and a glass-walled observation room on top, its platform 20 feet off the ground.[^3] The Register describes the cab as a 16-by-16-foot octagon on a 20-foot enclosed tower.[^1]

Forrest Eckler was on the job during 1947 and was set to be the lookout for the coming season, according to the clipping.[^3] A May 1946 item calls an "East Basalt lookout" newly in operation, but the sources do not say whether that was the same post.[^3]

The Register lists Cal Fire as the administrator.[^1][^2] A hiking trip report says the summit is a short, easy hike from San Luis Reservoir State Recreation Area, up a rough track past telephone poles toward radio towers and the lookout.[^4] None of the sources says whether the cab is open to visitors or who staffs it now, so ask Cal Fire before going up to the structure itself.
