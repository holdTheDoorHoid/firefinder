Fire towers have stood beside Route 6 in South Wellfleet since 1927, when the state put up a tower to help protect the lower Cape from fire.[^1] The tower cost $2,000, mostly covered by the Commonwealth.[^1] The towns of Wellfleet, Orleans, Eastham and Truro each added smaller shares.[^1]

The register's account of that first tower differs from the local history. It calls the first structure an iron tower of 68 feet, and says it was the last of eight original towers built to guard Cape Cod.[^2] The blog quotes a 1927 newspaper report describing a twenty-foot-square steel tower, which does not match the register's iron tower.[^1] These sources do not settle which account is right.

A 70-foot steel tower with a wooden cab replaced it in 1960.[^2] The register calls that structure the present tower.[^2] The blog says Richard Baker of South Wellfleet was expected to be the watchman at the 1927 tower.[^1] None of the sources read name a particular fire it spotted.

A state survey of Cape and Islands fire observation towers says there are currently seven active towers in the same geographic area as Nickerson State Park, and it lists the Wellfleet tower among them.[^3] The survey text does not show its date, so 'currently' rests on the survey alone. The survey says the tower sits on a small rise at 1947 State Highway (Route 6), on land owned by the Department of Conservation and Recreation. It also says vehicle access crosses private property.[^3] The register lists the site at 11 feet, while the survey's elevation range starts at 76 feet in Wellfleet. I could not settle which figure is right.[^2][^3]

No public visiting or climbing information turned up. Anyone curious should admire the tower from the road and ask the state before going onto the property.
