The Old Baldy lookout stood on Lookout Mountain, a peak just northwest of Baldy Village.[^1] The register says the site was also called "Old Baldy Lookout," and that its enclosed timber tower, 20 feet (6.1 m) high, went up in 1915.[^1] No lookout was ever located on the summit of Mount San Antonio itself.[^1]

A June 1915 newspaper item reported that the government forestry service had put a lookout station on a peak near Mount San Antonio, and that E.E. Cook would be posted there with one job: spotting forest fires visible from his perch.[^3] The Arcadia Tribune reported in January 1918 that Cook spent eight months of each year at the station.[^3] In 1916 The Timberman described a signal at the Fuego Vista Lookout Station: when a fire needed a ranger, the lookout set off three sticks of dynamite, and the ranger was to hurry to the nearest telephone.[^3]

In January 1918 a severe windstorm broke the tower's glass and emptied the room of its contents. Cook spent the night outdoors on the mountainside. The next morning, the paper reported, he was able to return to the damaged tower.[^3]

The register says severe weather hurt the lookout in 1926 or 1927. In 1927 a tower on Sunset Peak, lower down San Antonio Canyon, took its place.[^1] A 1927 newspaper report said the old station was to be dismantled.[^3]

The Sunset Peak tower stood across the saddle north of the Lookout Mountain site.[^2] The register gives 1927 as its build year and 1974 as its removal year.[^2] Photographs suggest the cab was an L-4 or BC-301 model on a 20-foot (6.1 m) steel tower.[^2] In December 1938 a fire came close to the tower, but lookout Sherman Foster kept to his post, the register says.[^2] Reports say the cab and tower were taken down and moved to the Ontario Air Tanker Base in 1974 or 1975. Their final fate is unknown.[^2]

The register's 2013 photographs show the tower footings and a house foundation on Sunset Peak, and corrugated metal that may cover a cistern.[^2] Sunset Peak lies in the Angeles National Forest and the San Gabriel Mountains National Monument.[^2] No source found describes the Lookout Mountain site as it is now. The sources do not say whether the Sunset Peak remains are open to visitors.

The name Fuego Vista is unconfirmed. The register says Lookout Mountain has been suggested as the same site as the Fuego Vista Lookout, though it adds that the two structures appear to differ in tower height and window details.[^1]

Mount San Antonio sits on the border of Los Angeles and San Bernardino counties, and the register places the old lookout site in Los Angeles County.[^4][^1]
