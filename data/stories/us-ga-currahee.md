The Currahee lookout stood on the summit of Currahee Mountain, in Stephens County near Toccoa.[^2][^3] The Georgia tower list gives its coordinates as 34.52927, -83.37600 and its elevation as 1,731 feet (527.6 m).[^1]

The Works Progress Administration built the tower in the 1930s.[^2] A local history says the agency built a road to within a short distance of the summit, then a parking lot, rock steps up to the tower, and a metal fire lookout on the top. It also built a house for the ranger and his family, further down the mountain.[^2]

Seaborn Farmer was the first ranger to man the tower, and he lived in the ranger house.[^2] In 1942 the 506th Parachute Infantry received its training at the foot of the mountain. Its soldiers ran three miles up, climbed the rock steps, circled the tower, and ran three miles back down.[^2] The U.S. Army had chosen Currahee as the site for its first Parachute Infantry Training Center, Camp Toccoa.[^3]

A local history says the lookout tower has been taken down, and the summit now holds numerous other communication towers.[^2] It also says the ranger house and rock steps still remain.[^2]

The lookout list gives the tower 1,731 feet, while the county gives the summit as 1,735 feet, so the two figures may refer to slightly different points.[^1][^3]

Today the mountain is a popular destination for rock climbing and rappelling, and it hosts an annual race, the Currahee Challenge, each fall.[^3] Part of it lies within the Chattahoochee National Forest.[^3] One of the radio towers at the top carries NOAA Weather Radio station WWH24, which serves parts of northeast Georgia and upstate South Carolina.[^3] Trail maps are available from the Toccoa-Stephens County Chamber of Commerce, in the historic train depot downtown.[^3]
