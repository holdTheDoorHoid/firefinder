Mount Holmes stands in Yellowstone National Park, in Park County, Wyoming.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the building as US 578 and WY 12.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register lists the site as US 1940 and WY 2.[^2] Both registers give the elevation as 10,256 feet (3,126 meters).[^1][^2] Firelookout.com gives 10,336 feet,[^3] and the 1935 Pinedale Roundup gives 10,300 feet,[^4] so the elevation is not settled.

The registers give 1930 as the build year.[^1][^2] The park's own 2019 press release, as reported by Montana Public Radio, says the lookout was built in 1931 and renovated in 1998.[^5] Firelookout.com also gives 1931.[^3] The sources disagree, and we could not settle the year.

The register describes a one-story, 16-by-16-foot building on a concrete foundation, with a rubble-stone base, a frame upper story, and a hip roof.[^1] The sources we read do not name who built it.

In August 1934, the Helena Independent reported that Ed Hall was stationed on the summit and that a red fox visited the station every day for the meat and scraps he put out.[^4] A year later, The Pinedale Roundup reported that Mr. and Mrs. Edward Hall, married less than a year, had taken over the summit station for about two and a half months.[^4]

The park's release says the lookout was one of its staffed stations until 2007.[^5] Firelookout.com says it was last staffed in 2007.[^3]

On July 16, 2019, lightning struck the lookout, and the park said it burned to the ground.[^5] The fire also damaged a park radio repeater.[^5] The register lists the lookout as removed in 2019.[^1]

We found no source that says what, if anything, remains on the summit. The register's description mentions a concrete foundation, but no source we read says whether it survived the fire.[^1]

After the fire, the park closed the Mount Holmes Trail, west of its junction with the Trilobite Lake Trail, and the summit. The closure was to stay in effect until unsafe conditions were assessed and mitigated.[^5] We found no later notice, so check with Yellowstone National Park before any visit. The lookout is gone, so there is no building to climb.[^5]
