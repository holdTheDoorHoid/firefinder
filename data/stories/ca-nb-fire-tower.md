Mount Champlain is the name of a lookout site in New Brunswick, on the boundary between Kings and Queens.[^1] The Canada Lookouts history gives the summit as 446 m (1,462 ft), converted from the source's feet.[^1] The first entry it records for the summit is a cupola cabin in 1930.[^1]

A 1950 entry records that the telephone link to the lookout station was changed from a grounded circuit to a metallic one, which improved communication.[^1] The entry's source is the 114th Annual Report of the Department of Lands and Mines.[^1]

OpenStreetMap has a record named Fire Tower, tagged as an observation tower.[^2] Its only name is Fire Tower, so the sources do not tie it to the Mount Champlain site.[^2] It was last edited more than a decade ago, so it does not show what stands on the summit now.[^2]

No source I found describes what remains on the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
