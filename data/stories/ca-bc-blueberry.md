Canada Lookouts places the Blueberry lookout in the Peace River area at latitude 56.721 and longitude 121.765, with an elevation of 846 m (2,776 ft).[^1] A second Canada Lookouts page under the same name is labelled Kootenay-Boundary and gives other coordinates, at 2,363 m (7,753 ft).[^2] That page also carries a notice placing its tower 2 km south of Wonowan, on the Alaska Highway.[^2] I found no source that says the two pages describe the same tower, so the location stays unconfirmed.

Building the Alaska Highway from 1942 added to the forest-protection burden in the Peace River area, and the Forest Service added more staff in 1943.[^3]

The 1979 notice describes the tower as 18.3 m (60 ft) high, with pole legs and bolted bracing of about 10 by 15 cm (4 by 6 in). At its top was a plywood lookout building of 2.4 by 2.4 m (8 by 8 ft).[^2]

In July 1979 the notice invited offers to buy the tower "as is and where is" for removal or demolition, with the work to finish within 30 days of a bid being accepted and the site left tidy.[^2] The sources I found do not record what happened after that offer.[^2]

No source I found describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit it.
