Panny is a steel fire lookout tower, 30 m (98 ft) tall, standing at 625 m (2,051 ft) above sea level, according to Alberta Wildfire's lookout layer.[^1] The layer dates the present structure to 2019.[^1] Steel came into use for lookout towers in the 1920s, and wood gradually gave way to it.[^2]

A closed MERX tender, numbered 19PTC116, is titled "Wildfire Observation Tower Replacements - Pinto and Panny Towers", so Panny is one of two towers it names for replacement.[^3] Alberta reports that each of its 127 wildfire lookout sites has a staff member on watch daily, usually from April through October.[^4]

The layer lists Panny as active, with access mode fly, and marks its location as not verified.[^1] No source I found describes the site beyond that listing. Alberta describes the sites as remote, and staff there have to plan for many contingencies, including a curious bear.[^4] Treat Panny as an active, staffed lookout, not a place to visit.
