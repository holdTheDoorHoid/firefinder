A new fire tower and cabin at Sayer's Hill near Lake Pocatello in Orange County were made ready for public viewing in August 1930.[^1] Orange County's board of supervisors paid half the cost of the cabin, and a private gift paid the other half.[^1]

During August 1930, more than 300 people visited the tower and looked out from its platform, 60 feet above the ground. On a typical day, visibility from the platform reached about 40 miles.[^1] The tower was one of a proposed five to cover the district, and the state's 1930 report says it protects the eastern and southern slopes of the Shawangunk range.[^1] It was to be open to visitors when the observer was on duty, usually from nine to five.[^1]

In 1946 the Lake Pocatello property owners' association sought support to keep the fire tower and its watchman going.[^1]

The state's lookout list now marks the tower as gone, but neither source says when or how it came down.[^2][^1] The 1930 newspaper calls the site Sayer's Hill, while the state report names Pocatello Mountain, so the two names may refer to the same place.[^1] No source read describes what, if anything, is left on the site today, or whether the mountain is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
