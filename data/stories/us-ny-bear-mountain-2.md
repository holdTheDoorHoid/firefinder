The State Park Commission put up a steel fire tower on the true summit of Bear Mountain in 1923, working with the State Conservation Department.[^1] The register describes it as a 60-foot tower with nine landings, staffed by park rangers.[^1]

The dates do not all agree. A 1929 newspaper, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says the top of Bear Mountain had recently been raised eighty feet by a steel fire observation tower.[^2] The register's 1923 date and that 1929 account do not match.[^1][^2]

The register gives 1930 for its removal. Its main text hedges this as "it's said" and suggests the nearby Perkins Memorial Tower, under construction roughly 300 feet away, may have been the reason.[^1] A 1932 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, also quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, still calls the steel frame tower about 65 feet high on the summit.[^2] A 2005 geocaching note there says it was gone before 1965.[^2]

The stone Perkins Memorial Tower was finished in 1933 under the CCC. It stands about 300 feet west of the old tower, and the Appalachian Trail runs past its base.[^1] The register calls it private, though the public may visit it.[^1]

Today the steel tower is gone.[^1] The register says its footings still exist on the rock face at the summit, though it does not date that observation.[^1] The summit lies within Harriman/Bear Mountain State Park.[^1]
