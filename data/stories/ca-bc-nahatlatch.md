The Nahatlatch Fire Lookout stood near Boston Bar, in the Fraser Canyon of British Columbia.[^2][^3] The recreation site lies at 1,190 m (3,900 ft), and from Highway 1 near Boston Bar the tower was visible on clear days.[^1]

Recreation Sites and Trails BC dates the original tower to between 1956 and 1958.[^2] A club that later rebuilt it says the BC Forest Service built it in the late 1950s.[^3] The tower was a tapered wooden design, and a road was cut to it in the 1960s to supply the observer.[^2][^1]

Observers were posted at the tower from 1957 until the early 1970s, when better fire detection made human watching less necessary.[^1][^2] Officially decommissioned in 1993, the tower soon drew hikers, ATV clubs and geocachers.[^1][^2]

Heavy snow and gradual structural decline brought the tower down in the winter of 2011.[^1] The Four Wheel Drive Association and the Southwest All Terrain Trails ATV Club rebuilt it, and the rebuild matched the original's look and dimensions.[^1]

The sources differ on when the rebuild was finished. Our West Coast says the tower was rebuilt in 2012, while a Canada Lookouts timeline says work began in August 2012 and was completed in September 2013.[^1][^4] The club said the rebuild came almost 11 years before the fire.[^3] The lookout also became an official BC recreation site in 2013.[^1]

On 17 August 2023 the Kookipi Creek wildfire consumed the lookout.[^1][^2] The fire had started from lightning on 8 July.[^3] Strong winds pushed it north over the Nahatlatch River and up the far slope, where it engulfed the tower.[^3]

In 2023 the association's communications director said the site is now visited by many hundreds of people each year.[^2] The Four Wheel Drive Association of BC said it intended to rebuild the tower and was seeking volunteers.[^1] No source I could reach describes the site as it stands now.
