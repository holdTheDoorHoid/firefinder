Reddish Knob rises to 4,397 feet in Augusta County, Virginia, within the George Washington National Forest.[^1] The register says a fire tower stood at Reddish Knob from the 1920s until 1975.[^1] The Friends of Shenandoah Mountain say the road up was built to help detect and control fires.[^3]

In May 1937 Forest Service towermen were on duty at the lookout.[^2] A Harrisonburg newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page reports that 138 visitors signed in at the lookout cabin over that weekend.[^2]

A Daily News Record item dated October 14, 1960, reports that a stolen two-way radio and a portable field telephone from the lookout tower had been found.[^2]

The register gives 1975 as the removal year and says the tower came down after persistent problems with vandalism.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page gives the same year.[^2] The register and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list both show the tower as gone.[^1][^4]

A photo on the register page, credited to Brian Powell, shows the tower's foundations as they looked in June 2009.[^1]

Today the summit is a scenic viewpoint at the end of a road.[^3] The Friends of Shenandoah Mountain describe a one-lane paved road that ends at a paved parking lot, with views across the mountains and valleys around it. They add that the road is often icy in winter and early spring.[^3] Wikipedia also notes that a narrow paved road leads to the summit from Harrisonburg.[^5]

We found no source that describes the foundations today, or whether anything else remains at the summit.
