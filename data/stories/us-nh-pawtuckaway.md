The Pawtuckaway lookout stands on the south knob of Mount Pawtuckaway, inside the state park of the same name in Rockingham County, New Hampshire.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as the state's first steel lookout.[^1] Lookout service began at the site in 1913, and the 25-foot tower went up in 1914.[^1]

A 1914 forestry commissioner's report said the steel tower was only slightly dearer than a wooden one, offered the watchman more protection, and was nearly permanent.[^2] In 1920 the state acquired the Goodrich land on the mountain.[^2] A January 1930 notice said the Goodrich house had been linked to the lookout station, and that the old house would be occupied by the forestry watchman in fire season.[^2] A 1938 death notice recorded that a fire warden had spent 14 years as the mountain's lookout.[^2]

The Register records a 1930 modification, a 10-foot raise in 1971, and a new cab in 1976.[^1] A 2016 chapter report records a fresh cab and a new coat of paint that year.[^3] The Register lists the tower as still in use.[^1]

Today the tower stands inside Pawtuckaway State Park, at the summit of South Mountain.[^1][^4] The park's trail sheet says hikers covering 2.4 miles reach the 908-foot summit and are rewarded with a wide outlook from the tower.[^4] A 2020 trip report says the three flights to the top are quick to climb but steep, and advises care.[^5] A 2023 visitor found many people on the tower and chose to stay below.[^6] The Register gives the site elevation as 549 feet (167 m), which does not match the 908-foot summit the park uses, so that figure should be read with caution.[^1][^4] No source found a closure notice, and no source found a current staffing schedule, so check with the park before going up.
