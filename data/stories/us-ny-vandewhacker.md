The Vandewhacker Fire Tower stands on the summit of Vanderwhacker Mountain in Essex County, New York.[^2][^3] The state gives the summit as 3,386 feet, while Wikipedia gives 3,389.[^3][^2] The name is spelled both ways in the record. The NHLR uses "Vandewhacker," while the state and Wikipedia use "Vanderwhacker."[^1][^2][^3]

The Conservation Commission put up a wooden observation tower on the mountain in May 1911.[^2] In 1918 a 35-foot steel Aermotor tower replaced it, and a new cabin was built for the observer.[^1][^2][^4] Hikers on the trail today pass two cabins that were used by the fire observer.[^6]

Sources disagree on when fire watching ended. The NHLR says the tower was staffed for fire spotting through 1971.[^1] Wikipedia says fire watching stopped at the end of the 1988 season, and that the state officially closed the tower in early 1989.[^2]

A 2005 Lake Placid News article calls the tower a restored Adirondack fire tower, and says a state Environmental Protection Fund grant helped pay for the restoration.[^5] The NHLR registered the tower on June 12, 2018.[^1]

The Vanderwhacker Mountain Wild Forest is open for recreation year-round.[^3] The state describes a 2.5-mile Tower Trail that climbs 1,650 feet from the Tower Trailhead to the summit.[^3] Trees cover the summit, so the cab is the only vantage point.[^3] The NHLR warns that the road to the trailhead is often rough for low-clearance vehicles, and suggests walking the extra 2.6 miles along it.[^1]

One hiking guide describes climbing the tower.[^6] We found no current statement that the cab is open to visitors, and Wikipedia reports an official closure in 1989.[^2] Check with the state's Department of Environmental Conservation before you climb.
