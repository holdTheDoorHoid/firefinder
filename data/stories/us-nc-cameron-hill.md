Cameron Hill, in western Harnett County, is where the North Carolina Forest Service put up its first fire lookout tower, finished in September 1926.[^1][^2] The corner supports were 60-foot (18 m) juniper poles, and an 8 x 8 foot glassed cabin sat on top.[^1] A 1926-28 state report gives the tower as 65 feet (20 m) tall, while the September 1926 account describes the 60-foot poles.[^1] A July 1926 newspaper report, reprinted on a blog, said the tower offered an open view across a wide area.[^2]

Local association dues and federal cooperative money paid for the tower and about 50 miles of connecting telephone line.[^1]

In August 1938 a 100-foot (30 m) creosoted wood tower was erected on the hill. A 1940 state report credits SCS Camp 18 as a cooperative project, though the Forest Lookouts page says the CCC erected it.[^1] A steel tower replaced the wooden one in 1951.[^1]

In March 1965 an Air Force cargo plane dropped a three-quarter-ton truck from 1,500 feet, and it landed about 200 feet from the tower.[^1] A Statesville newspaper quoted William Holder, who was in the fire tower with his wife at the time.[^1]

The tnlandforms.us list of North Carolina towers has two Cameron Hill entries: "Cameron Hill (old)" at 35.28027, -79.09888, and "Cameron Hill" at 35.27972, -79.10749, a little to the west.[^3] The sources do not say which entry matches the tower described above, so this story covers the hill's lookout history only.

No source found describes what stands on Cameron Hill today, or whether the public can reach it.
