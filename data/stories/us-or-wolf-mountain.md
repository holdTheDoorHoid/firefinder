Wolf Mountain stands in Wheeler County, Oregon, inside the Ochoco National Forest.[^1] In 1920 an 18-year-old from Portland took up duties as the primary lookout there, perched in a treetop on a rough platform put up for him.[^2] The next summer a Forest Service ranger was building a lookout tower on the summit.[^2]

Sources disagree on what came first. One history dates a 50-foot wooden tower and cabin to 1926, with a crow's nest tree platform added in 1931.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register places the wooden tower one mile west of the present site and says a crow's nest went up one mile east in 1932.[^1] The present tower, about 107 feet of treated timber with an L-4 cab, was built in 1947; one source gives 108 feet.[^1][^2][^3] Its materials had been bought in 1942 and stored at Derr Guard Station until postwar manpower allowed the build.[^3]

Early records show a working lookout. The lookout reported one fire during the 1927 season.[^2] A 1952 inspection found the tower and house in excellent condition, though the cables had too much sag.[^2]

As of the 2011 register entry, the present tower was manned every summer and still relied on an older telephone setup with radio as a backup.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers the lookout, with the Paulina Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] The Register listed it on February 9, 2011, nominated by Howard Verschoor.[^1]

I could not find a source that says whether the cab is open to visitors. The newest details I found date from the 2011 registration, so check with the Paulina Ranger District before you plan a trip. Treat it as a working lookout rather than a place to climb.
