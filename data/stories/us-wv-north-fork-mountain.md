The North Fork Mountain Tower Site stood in Pendleton County, West Virginia, on Monongahela National Forest land, at 3,795 feet (1,157 m).[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers the land.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register lists the site as US 164 and WV 14.[^1]

Photos on the register, dated 1935 and credited to the National Archives, show an original wooden tower and its steel replacement.[^1] The register added the site on September 16, 2008, on the nomination of Henry Isenberg.[^1]

Do not confuse this site with Pike Knob, another lookout on North Fork Mountain, which has its own listing at different coordinates.[^2][^4] As part of a fire program that followed a March 1930 fire on the mountain, a 90-foot steel fire tower was built on Pike Knob.[^4] A Mon Forest guide describes remains of that tower inside the Nature Conservancy's preserve.[^3]

The register says the tower has been removed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's West Virginia list, which includes Pendleton County, also marks it as gone.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today.[^1] The register does not say whether the site is open to the public.[^1] A Mon Forest guide names only three former towers that remain accessible in their original locations, and this one is not among them.[^3]
