Pilot Knob is a fire lookout in Cumberland County, Tennessee, at an elevation of 2,021 feet.[^1] Tennessee Landforms lists it as standing.[^1] We found no build date, design or builder for this tower in the sources we checked.

Tennessee also has a Pilot Knob tower in Clay County. Landforms lists that one as a separate entry, so the two should not be mixed up.[^1]

A 2014 National Register study of Tennessee's Division of Forestry fire lookout towers lists Pilot Knob in Cumberland County among the towers standing on March 26, 2014.[^2] The same study credits the Division with 208 lookout towers built from roughly 1930 to 1970.[^2] It also says the Civilian Conservation Corps helped put up many of the Division's 1930s towers.[^2] We could not confirm that Pilot Knob was one of those.

We found no staffing history or notable fire reports for this tower. The 2014 list is the latest dated record we found for it.

Tennessee Landforms advises visitors not to climb its towers, because steps are frequently missing or decayed.[^1] We found no public visiting arrangement for this tower. Please do not climb it, and check with the Tennessee Division of Forestry before going to the site.

[^1]: Tennessee Lookout Towers, Tennessee Landforms, https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers, 1933 to 1975, National Register of Historic Places multiple property documentation form, National Park Service, https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/64501237.pdf (accessed 2026-10-09).
