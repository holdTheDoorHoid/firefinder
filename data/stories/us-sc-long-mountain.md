Long Mountain Tower is in Oconee County, South Carolina, inside the Sumter National Forest.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the tower to around 1935. It is a 54-foot Aermotor LS-40 design with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab and a low hip roof.[^1] Its windows are arranged in an unusual pattern.[^1]

The U.S. Forest Service administered the tower, and the register says the agency used it until the 1980s.[^1] The register does not name any lookout who worked there, and we found no account of a fire it spotted.[^1]

The register gives the elevation as 2,021 feet, or 616 meters, and lists the tower as US 961, SC 15.[^1] It was added to the register on January 27, 2013, on the nomination of Ron Stafford.[^1]

The newest report we found is a 2020 blog post.[^2] The writer entered the cab and says the tower was open "probably by mistake." The writer adds that many fire towers are not open.[^2] The sources we read do not say whether the tower may be climbed, so please do not climb it unless the Forest Service confirms it is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
