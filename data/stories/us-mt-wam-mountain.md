Wam Mountain Lookout sits atop a 7,121-foot peak in the Whitefish Range, within the Kootenai National Forest. The Forest Service built it in 1931 as a gable-roofed L-4 cab, a style the National Historic Lookout Register calls "one of the last" of its kind.[^1] A patrol point once stood about a mile northeast along the same ridge, though no further detail on it survives in the sources checked here.[^2]

The lookout was used for fire detection only into the 1950s, after which it was retired from active duty.[^1][^2] Unlike many lookouts of its era, it was never torn down or lost to fire, and both sources checked here credit that survival to its remote, hard-to-reach location.[^1]

At some point after its working life ended, the Forest Service restored the cabin and made it available through the Recreation Rental program, and it was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on May 12, 2000, as US 350.[^1][^2] More recently, though, Recreation.gov's own listing for the cabin now carries a notice reading simply "No longer a rental," with no further explanation given.[^3] That leaves its current public-access status unclear: it may still be standing and reachable by trail, but as of this writing it does not appear to be bookable as an overnight rental the way Meadow Peak and Sex Peak are.

A hiking route, the roughly third-of-a-mile Wam Lookout Trail, climbs from a trailhead off a Forest Service road to the summit.[^4] Visitors should check current Forest Service and Recreation.gov postings before planning a trip, since recent storm damage has closed or washed out other roads and trails elsewhere in the Kootenai National Forest.[^5]
