Gazelle Mountain, in Siskiyou County, got a lookout in 1933 that was built for the Shasta National Forest.[^1] The register describes a USFS Plan R1 cabin atop a 20-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1] In 1951 administration of the lookout passed from the Shasta National Forest to the Klamath National Forest.[^1][^2]

Few staff names survive in the sources. A 1959 diary entry, quoted in a Klamath forest chronology, records a flash from the oven of a gas stove that burned a woman at the lookout.[^2] A doctor treated her and called the injuries superficial.[^2] In October 1961 the Siskiyou Daily News reported that a bear cub had made daily appearances at the station that week.[^2] The paper named Mrs. Nancy Rich as the Klamath National Forest lookout there.[^2]

The register says the lookout structure was removed sometime before 1986.[^1] Only the foundation remains, according to the register.[^1] The sources do not say who took the structure down or why.

The register names the Klamath National Forest as administrator.[^1] We found no source that describes the foundation in more detail or says whether the site is open to visitors. Anyone who wants to see it should check with the Klamath National Forest first.

[^2]: Gazelle Mountain, Forest Lookouts (California Lookouts), https://californialookouts.weebly.com/gazelle-mountain.html (accessed 2026-10-09).
