Warland Peak sits about 20 miles east-northeast of Libby, in Lincoln County, Montana, inside the Kootenai National Forest.[^1][^2] The US Forest Service administers the lookout, which was developed in 1934 with a 100-foot steel tower and a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^2] A shorter 10-foot pole tower carried an L-4 cab for living quarters.[^2] A memoir from the early days of Forest Service work describes those quarters as built by Civilian Conservation Corps crews on 12-foot tower legs, so the two accounts differ on the pole's height.[^3]

The same memoir places its author at the Warland Ranger Station from spring 1939 and names Rex Fleming as the lookout at that time.[^3] When a fire broke out at the head of Cripple Horse Creek, the lookout was away from the tower for two days.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower has not been used since the late 1960s.[^2]

The small L-4 cab collapsed over the winter of 2012 to 2013.[^2] A Forest Fire Lookout Association chapter report from 2013 says it was found collapsed and presumably came down the winter before.[^4] The Association's Montana list still shows the Warland Peak tower as standing.[^5]

The site was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on January 9, 2021, as US 1506, MT 84.[^2] We did not find a current access or rental notice from the Forest Service, so we cannot say whether the tower is open to visitors. Check with the Kootenai National Forest before you go, and do not climb the tower unless it is clearly open to the public.
