Bald Mountain, in Polk County, Oregon, is the county's highest summit.[^1] A Forest Lookouts history places a station on its summit by 1913, and a 1914 state report describes a telephone line running to the lookout station on top of Bald Mountain.[^1] In 1916 the Polk County Fire Patrol Association told its manager to build a new station there, and by 1918 a crew had nearly finished a high tower on the summit.[^1]

The tower was rebuilt more than once. In 1932 the district warden put up a new tower of about 60 feet on the spot where an old one had decayed.[^1] Winter winds blew a 66-foot tower over in December 1933, and the warden put the repairs at a little over $100.[^1]

The sources disagree on the tower's height and order. The Register says a pole tower went up in 1941 and was replaced by a 40-foot tower.[^2] A June 1942 inspection instead describes a 90-foot tower without a cab.[^1] Both the Forest Lookouts history and firelookout.com date a 40-foot tower to 1947.[^1][^3]

After a federal wireless receiver was installed in 1921, T.C. Stockwell ran it from the station.[^1] William Caldwell was the lookout in 1924 and 1925.[^1] In 1944 Lyle Thomas was the fire association's lookout there.[^1]

In July 1922 a blaze climbed the west slope of the mountain.[^1] The Morning Oregonian reported that the lookout had left his post, but the district warden said the lookout was still on duty.[^1] In June 1925 a fire threatened a sawmill and the town of Valsetz, and Caldwell reported on it by telephone.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts history says the lookout was removed in 1969.[^1] The Register and firelookout.com put its abandonment in 1971 and say it no longer stands.[^2][^3] The dates differ, and we could not settle which is right.

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit, and nothing we read says whether the public can reach it. The Register lists the Oregon Department of Forestry as administrator, so ask the department before going up. There is no tower left to climb.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts history calls the site Bald Mountain, while the Register and firelookout.com use Monmouth Bald.[^1][^2][^3] The 1947 40-foot tower in both accounts suggests they describe one summit, though no source says so outright.[^1][^3] The distance from Dallas also differs: a 1922 newspaper says about 23 miles, and firelookout.com says 14 miles.[^1][^3]
