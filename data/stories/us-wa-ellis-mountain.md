The Ellis Mountain lookout stood on a high ridge in Clallam County, at about 2,338 feet.[^4][^2] The register dates its L-4 cab to 1952.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page quotes a Forestry Division annual report, which calls the 1952 building a replacement 14 x 14 ground house.[^3][^4]

Firelookout.com says the cab replaced an earlier lookout one mile north.[^2] Willhite found 1952 aerial photos that show the cleared lookout area but no structures, so an earlier lookout is unconfirmed.[^4]

A 1958 Port Angeles Evening News item, quoted by the same Forest Lookouts page, says Mrs. Norman Cowan served as emergency lookout on Ellis Mountain from Friday to Monday that August.[^3] An April 1964 item said seven lookouts would be manned that June, and Ellis Mountain was one of them.[^3]

The lookout was removed in 1970.[^1][^3][^4] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2]

Willhite's photo captions show an anchor bolt and a battery dump just below the fence of the lookout site.[^4] He places the lookout on a lower knob near the summit's communications station, and no source we read describes a standing structure there today.[^4]

Public roads reach almost to the site.[^4] A gate sits partway up the road, and Willhite found it open on his visit.[^4] From the parking area at the top, he says the site is about 0.1 miles past the gate.[^4] His directions start at Highway 101 and run north on Highway 112.[^4] The sources do not say whether visitors are welcome at the communications site, so check locally before going.
