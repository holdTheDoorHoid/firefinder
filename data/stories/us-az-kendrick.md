Kendrick Lookout sits atop Kendrick Peak in the Kaibab National Forest, northwest of Flagstaff, at just over 10,200 feet.[^1] Fire detection on the peak goes back to the early 1900s: a log cabin was built about a quarter mile below the summit in 1911, originally as sleeping quarters, and was first used as a fire lookout the following year.[^1][^2] The register calls it the second-oldest fire-detection structure in Arizona or New Mexico.[^1] An L-4-style cab went up on the peak itself in the early 1930s.[^1] That structure was later replaced by the tower still in use today: a 10-foot steel tower carrying a 14-by-14-foot flat-roofed cab and catwalk, built in 1964.[^1]

The 1994 nomination to the National Historic Lookout Register credits Forest Archaeologist Teri Cleeland, FFLA Arizona/New Mexico director Dave Lorenz, former lookout Gary McElfresh, and Chalender District Ranger Gene Waldrip; it was registered that February as NHLR US 78, AZ 1.[^1] The older cabin had already been listed on the National Register of Historic Places since January 1988, under the name "Kendrick Lookout Tower and Cabin."[^2]

The cabin has twice been wrapped in protective fire-shelter material to save it from wildfire: during the Pumpkin Fire in 2000 and the Boundary Fire in 2017. Both times it survived, though other, unrelated cabin ruins nearby burned.[^2]

Sources disagree on the tower's current state. The NHLR register (last updated in 2016) describes it as in active service, and Wikipedia separately reports that the Forest Service's own description has the tower open to visitors in summer when staffed.[^1][^2] Wikipedia also says that as of mid-2024 the tower appeared damaged and likely abandoned, though it cites no source for that claim.[^2] Nothing in the sources gathered here resolves that conflict, so a visitor should expect uncertainty about whether it is currently staffed. The Forest Service's own trail page for the route makes no mention of current staffing either way. Kendrick Mountain Trail #22, about 27 miles from Williams, Arizona, climbs to the summit area where the structures stand.[^3]
