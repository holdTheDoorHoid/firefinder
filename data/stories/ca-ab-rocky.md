Alberta's wildfire register lists a steel tower at Rocky.[^1] It is 30 m (98 ft) tall and stands at an elevation of 1,106 m (3,629 ft), at about 52.52 N, 114.88 W.[^1] The register gives 2017 as the year it was built.[^1]

Alberta says it places lookouts where visibility is good enough to spot fires and report them.[^2] The province has 127 such sites, spread across its boreal forest and the Rockies.[^3] Each has an observer on duty who keeps watch for wildfire all day, generally from April to October.[^3] The observer's main job is catching smoke and fire early and reporting what they see, lightning included.[^4]

The register still lists Rocky as active.[^1] It gives the access mode as drive, meaning the tower is reached by road, and it flags a weather station at the site.[^1] No source describes public visits, so treat Rocky as a working fire-watch station rather than a place to visit or climb.
