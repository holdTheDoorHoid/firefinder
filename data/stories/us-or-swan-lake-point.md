In 1930 the Klamath Agency's forestry report recorded the purchase of an 80-foot steel tower for a planned Swan Lake Point lookout, meant to close a blind area in the reservation's coverage.[^2] The tower and its freight were expected to cost about $1,140.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the tower and its cabin to 1932, and the 1932 report said the steel tower would be erected that July.[^1][^2] A separate ground cabin, built for living quarters, was finished on December 27, 1934, for $839.60.[^2]

Most sources put the tower at 80 feet, but an NGS data sheet quoted on the Oregon Forest Lookouts page gives 90 feet.[^1][^2]

In the summer of 1947 Claude Parazoo and his wife, with their family, were stationed at the lookout; by early September Mrs. Parazoo was moving to the Klamath Agency lookout for the rest of the season.[^2] By 1973 the Chiloquin district no longer manned it through the whole fire season, and it was staffed only when fire danger peaked.[^2]

In September 1962 the guard at the lookout spotted a brush fire near Lone Pine.[^2] By the time a six-man crew put it out, the fire had burned eight-tenths of an acre.[^2] A 1965 memo argued that the station needed replacing and warned that a new one might take several years to finish.[^2]

The register and firelookout.com both say the tower was gone by 1976.[^1][^3] The Oregon Forest Lookouts page gives a later date, saying the lookout was dismantled in 1979.[^2]

Forest Service decisions from 2011 and 2014 treat the summit as a communications site.[^5][^4] The 2011 decision covered upgrades to the facilities there, and the 2014 radio station project described a new building, tower, propane tanks and an access road, along with removing existing structures and rehabilitating the site.[^5][^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, of the lookout remains, and none says whether the public may visit the summit.
