By 1960 a steel lookout tower stood on the Crab Orchard refuge, about 11 miles southwest of Marion, Illinois. A federal survey that year described it and noted that it stood on refuge property.[^1]

The survey described a steel tower on four legs, with a viewing room at the top, and put its height at roughly 110 feet.[^1] A 1962 item in the Southern Illinoisan said a district of Junior Woman's Clubs had first planned a lookout at the lake, but the steel alone was quoted at $6,000.[^1] A 1963 item put a Fish and Wildlife Service tower in the refuge's southwest corner.[^1]

Sources disagree on the height. The survey's 110 feet differs from the 100 feet that Illinois Country Living gives.[^1][^2]

The same article says Gordon Tripp staffed the tower for 20 straight years.[^2] His family lived in a government house at the base.[^2] His daughter remembered the Osborne Fire Finder, a sighting device kept inside the tower.[^2] She also recalled that an escape from the penitentiary in Marion would alert her father to climb the tower and search for a stolen car or people on foot.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page marks the tower as removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association list marks it as gone.[^1][^3] The article says it came down after Tripp's years there.[^2]

A historian quoted in the article says nearly all of Illinois's towers were removed or sold off in the early 1970s.[^2]

The site lies within the Crab Orchard refuge.[^1] Wikipedia says the refuge's western 24,000 acres offer a wide range of recreation, while its eastern 20,000 acres are a wildlife sanctuary with limited public use.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower at the site today.[^1] The sources do not say which part of the refuge it stood in; the 1963 item's southwest-corner tower may or may not be this one.[^1] Without knowing the location, it is unclear whether the spot is open to visitors.[^4]
