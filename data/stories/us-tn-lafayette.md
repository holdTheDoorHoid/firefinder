The tower at Lafayette, in Macon County, stands at 955 feet (291 m) above sea level.[^1] The Tennessee Landforms list of towers gives it the state forest number 4-22 and marks it standing.[^1] Those numbers are district-tower numbers taken from a 1976 list of state towers.[^1]

We did not find a build date, a builder, or any early history for this tower in the sources we read. The wider picture is clearer. The Tennessee Division of Forestry ran its fire lookout network from 1933 to 1975, and the Civilian Conservation Corps is credited with building 98 fire lookout towers in Tennessee in the 1930s.[^2] Most of the network went up later, between the late 1940s and about 1960.[^2]

A 2014 National Register form lists a Lafayette tower in Macon County among the extant Tennessee fire towers as of March 26, 2014.[^2] At its peak in the late 1960s the state oversaw 208 towers, and 142 of them were still standing in March 2014.[^2]

Today we found no public access information for this tower. The Tennessee Landforms site notes that many towers have been removed and most tower locations are now private property.[^1] It also advises people not to climb the towers, because steps are often gone or rotten.[^1] If you want to see the site, ask the landowner first.
