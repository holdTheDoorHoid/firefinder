The lookout stood on the summit of Blue Mountain, above Deer Park, in Clallam County, Washington.[^1][^2][^3] The summit is about 6,007 feet (1,831 meters) high and now lies inside Olympic National Park.[^1][^2][^3]

A small cabin, 10 feet square, came first.[^1] Sources date it to 1928 or 1929, depending on which one you read.[^1][^2] In 1931 a gable-roofed cab replaced it.[^1][^2][^3] The register and FireLookout.com call that cab an L-4, but the Washington lookouts list calls it an R-1 style cabin on the ground.[^1][^2][^3]

During World War II the lookout was pressed into use as an Aircraft Warning Service post.[^4] The Washington list gives March 19, 1942 as the activation date and ties the lookout to the Port Angeles Filter Center.[^3]

In 1959 a newspaper item, reprinted on the Washington list, named Harry C. Hall as the summer staffer at Deer Park.[^3]

The register says the cab was removed in 1970, and FireLookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2]

A National Park Service article says the building no longer stands.[^4] By 1983, only two park structures used by Aircraft Warning Service staff were still standing: Dodger Point and Pyramid Peak lookouts.[^4]

The register's photo captions, dated September 2, 2004, show concrete along the edge of the site and the spot where the lookout stood.[^1] No source we read says what is on the summit today, how to reach it, or whether it is open. Because it lies inside a national park, ask the park before you go.
