The Taconic State Park Commission put up a 60-foot Aermotor tower on Alander Mountain in 1928, to watch for fires on park land.[^2] The spot sits just inside Massachusetts, about a fifth of a mile from the New York border.[^1][^2]

The two register entries name different agencies.[^1][^2] One says the New York State DEC ran the tower.[^1] The other says operation passed to Forest Fire Control around 1935.[^2]

The first entry dates the start of operation to 1928 and says staffing ended in 1930.[^1] The second includes a 1929 photo caption that names an observer, Mervin Whitbeck.[^2]

In 1930 the area burned, and the landowner asked for the tower to be taken down.[^2] Park staff took it down and put it in storage at the High Falls facility.[^2] In 1933 it was re-erected on Washburn Mountain in Copake Falls, New York.[^1] Both entries put the end of its operation at 1963.[^1][^2] In November 1963 it was carried by helicopter to Beebe Hill and set up again.[^2] A 2026 hiking account dates that move to 1963 or 1964.[^3]

The register reports that the tower no longer stands, though four footings were still at the site as of 2005.[^1] The 2026 hiking account says the four concrete footings remain, and it describes old tower footings at the summit.[^3] The original watchman's cabin still stands and is used as a campsite by Mount Washington State Forest.[^1] A sign on the cabin dates it to about the 1920s.[^3] The hiking account reached the cabin by the Alander Mountain Trail.[^3]
