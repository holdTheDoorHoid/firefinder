Nobscot Hill lies in both Framingham and Sudbury, and its summit holds a fire tower among several radio towers.[^1] The tower standing there now dates to 1966.[^3] The hilltop had a lookout before that, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites register says a 45-foot iron tower at a Framingham site was first operated in 1915 and used until 1965.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register describes the earlier tower on the Sudbury side as a 48-foot iron tower with a ladder. It says the 1966 replacement is a 75-foot steel tower with stairs and a wooden cab.[^3] A Forest Fire Lookout Association post also says the 1966 tower replaced an older Framingham tower, but puts the new one at 68 feet.[^4] The post says the Framingham tower stood 75 feet to the south, was later removed, and left its footings behind the Sudbury tower.[^4]

The two accounts do not match on every point. The register gives the new tower's height as 75 feet and the association gives 68. The older tower is 48 feet in one account and 45 in the other. Neither point can be settled from these sources, so treat both figures as uncertain.[^3][^4]

The register lists the Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control as administrator.[^3] A visit report dated September 27, 2012 answers yes when asked whether the tower is staffed.[^3] None of the sources name a particular fire the tower watched, or say who staffed it in other years.

Wikipedia says the tower can be climbed while it is open, so check before you go.[^1] The hill also has many public hiking trails.[^1] No current opening schedule or closure notice turned up in these sources.
