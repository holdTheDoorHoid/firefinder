The Churchill Mountain lookout stood in Stevens County, Washington, on the Colville National Forest.[^1] firelookout.com places it 12 miles west-northwest of Northport.[^2]

The sources do not agree on the first tower. WillhiteWeb says a tower went up in June 1923,[^4] and a September 1923 item quoted by Forest Lookouts says it was completed by the first of July.[^3] firelookout.com says the site was a camp in 1922 and dates a 40-foot pole tower and log cabin to 1927.[^2] The register also dates the 40-foot pole tower to 1927.[^1] WillhiteWeb says a 7-by-7-foot cab was added to the 40-foot pole tower that year,[^4] and a February 1928 item quoted by Forest Lookouts says a glass-enclosed cage was built on the tower the fall before.[^3]

The names come from newspapers, WillhiteWeb and firelookout.com. Morris Ewell was the lookout in 1928 according to WillhiteWeb,[^4] and a 1928 newspaper item calls him a nephew stationed at the lookout.[^3] firelookout.com puts him there in 1929.[^2] A May 1934 item says Clarence Rhode had been at Churchill the season before,[^3] and WillhiteWeb names him as the lookout in 1933.[^4] Patricia Langdon was the lookout in 1945.[^2][^3][^4] We found no fire record for this lookout in the sources we read.

In 1938 a 53-foot timber tower with an L-4 cab replaced the 40-foot pole tower.[^1][^4] In 1955 that tower was moved to Quartz Mountain, near Republic.[^1][^3][^4] At Quartz the L-4 cab came off in 1959, the tower was trimmed to 30 feet, and a flat cab was put on top.[^5] After the tower was abandoned in the 1970s, vandals damaged it, and arson destroyed it in October 1997.[^5][^6] Quartz Mountain is six miles southeast of Republic.[^6]

The tower no longer stands.[^5][^6] No source we read describes the original Churchill site today. WillhiteWeb includes a photo captioned "Churchill Footings," but its text says nothing about what it shows.[^4] A National Geodetic Survey entry uses the name "Churchill Lookout Tower" for a survey mark, which does not show whether anything stands on the summit.[^3] We found no information about public access to the site, so this story does not suggest a visit.
