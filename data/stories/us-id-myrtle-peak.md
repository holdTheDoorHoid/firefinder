Myrtle Peak lies in Boundary County, Idaho, in the Kaniksu National Forest, at 7,122 feet.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com places it 16 miles west-northwest of Bonners Ferry.[^1]

Two sites date the first structure on the peak to 1925, but one calls it a platform and the other calls it a tower.[^1][^2]

In 1933 two men set up a new Bosworth range-finding board on the observation platform. With it, a detected fire could be located at once, and smoke chasers were sent out on telephone instructions.[^3] Firelookout.com lists a Bosworth firefinder for the same year.[^1]

Only firelookout.com dates a replacement tower, built on a different spot, to 1934.[^1]

Firelookout.com names Evan Watson as a lookout in 1933 and Richard McAteer in 1936.[^1] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page gives the same 1933 name, citing a Bonners Ferry Herald item from September of that year.[^3]

A 1929 Pend Oreille Review item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, reports a half-acre fire above Bonners Ferry.[^4] It says the Myrtle creek lookout reported the fire at 5 a.m., and that a warden and two men had it under control by 6:30.[^4] The item does not say whether that lookout was this tower.

The tower was destroyed in 1955.[^1][^2]

A hiking guide describes a trail to the peak. It says the peak, 50 to 60 yards east of the saddle, holds the remains of the fire lookout tower, and it notes that a high-clearance vehicle is needed for part of the road to the trailhead.[^5] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as a hike-in.[^2] No source we read describes the remains in more detail.
