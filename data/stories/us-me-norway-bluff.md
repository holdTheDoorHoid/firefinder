A 1914 Forest Commissioner's report says a steel lookout tower, 24 feet high, went up on Norway Bluff the previous summer.[^1] The bluff sits close to Munsungan Lake, in township T9 R9 WELS, roughly 35 miles west of Ashland.[^2]

The same report describes a watchman's camp on the mountainside and a telephone line linking the lookout man to Oxbow Plantation.[^1] In a November 1916 letter, chief fire warden J.E. Junkins reported that trees had been cleared off the bluff, so people could see the surrounding land without climbing the tower.[^1] A 1920 inventory still listed a 24-foot steel tower.[^1]

The tower came down in 2012 as part of the state's MSCommNet radio project, a statewide upgrade that affected many fire towers in Maine.[^3] The association's 2012 report gives May 7 as the day it came off the mountain and June 13 as the day it reached the Ashland Logging Museum.[^3] The Maine Forest Service took the tower apart.[^1] Volunteers and Ashland Boy Scout Troop 179 then logged more than 600 hours on the restoration: the cab, the painted metal frame and the reassembly.[^2]

A dedication ceremony for the tower took place at the Ashland Logging Museum on July 7, 2013.[^4] The association's 2013 report calls it the finale of the move and says the tower is now on display at the museum.[^4] The museum also added a display of photos and keepsakes that local families gave, covering life at the various fire towers.[^2]

The association lists the tower as standing at the museum, and the original Norway Bluff site as gone.[^5] Ask the museum whether the tower is open for climbing before you go up it.
