Cabin Creek Peak Lookout sits in the Salt River Range of Wyoming, in the far south of Bridger-Teton National Forest, at about 10,260 feet.[^1][^2] It is a ground cabin rather than a tall tower.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register notes an unusual layout with four window panels on each side, and suggests the building may have been a backup or emergency lookout site.[^1]

The register gives 1941 as the build year and lists its dimensions as approximately 12x12.[^1] A hiking video quoted by KINGFM in 2021 gives a looser account, crediting the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it in the late 1930s or early 1940s.[^2] The same account says the shift to aircraft-based surveillance came in the early 1970s, after which structures like this were no longer needed.[^2]

The register does not know when the cabin was last staffed.[^1] The sources I read describe no fire that affected the lookout. A 2022 Wyoming fire report that appears to mention the cabin came up in a search, but I have not read it. Sources also differ slightly on elevation, since a firelookout.com page gives 10,283 feet.[^3]

KINGFM calls the cabin a faint trace of a long-gone period when wildfire detection began on Wyoming's peaks.[^2] Some hikers recently visited to see what remains, and the article adds that the remains offer no rental or lodging option.[^2] A Wyoming lookout index still lists the cabin as standing.[^4] According to the article, the Forest Service website holds little information about it.[^2]

The route in is long. The hiking video followed a 20-mile round trip up Strawberry Creek to McDougal Pass and on to the lookout.[^2] Nothing in these sources suggests a stay is possible, so a trip means a hike to see the site.
