The U.S. Forest Service built a live-in lookout on Rich Mountain in 1932.[^1] The 14-by-14-foot cab and its catwalk sit on a metal tower. The register gives the tower as 30 feet tall, while a travel guide says 31.[^2][^1] It stands on the North Carolina and Tennessee line inside Pisgah National Forest, just off the Appalachian Trail.[^2]

The tower stayed staffed until the 1990s, though it stopped housing a live-in lookout in the 1970s.[^1] Aerial detection by planes and, later, public reports by cell phone became the more economical and quicker methods of finding fires.[^1] Most of North Carolina's fire towers were dismantled.[^1] Weather and vandalism left this one in disrepair.[^1]

A visit report on the register, dated September 2008, found all the glass gone and graffiti that needed paint over. The stairs, catwalk and cab were in good shape.[^2]

A partnership of the Forest Service, the Appalachian Trail Conservancy, the Forest Fire Lookout Association and the Carolina Mountain Club restored the tower in 2018.[^1][^3] A ribbon-cutting followed on November 19, 2018.[^4] Williams Construction, based in Robbinsville, did the work, and the Carolina Mountain Club built a log staircase up to the base.[^3]

The Forest Service provided the bulk of the money, $101,158. North Carolina gave $3,500 and Tennessee gave $5,000.[^3] A travel guide credits most of the funding to a trail-group license-plate grant instead, so these figures follow the newspaper.[^1]

Visitors reach the tower by a short side trail from the Appalachian Trail.[^2][^3] A gravel road of about six miles also climbs from the Hot Springs side, but it is usually closed in winter.[^1] The register lists the tower as unstaffed.[^2] The newest condition note we found is the 2008 visit report, and we found no account of the tower since its 2018 restoration.[^2]

A North Carolina lookout list on tnlandforms.us also names a Rich Mountain in Madison County, with coordinates close to this one.[^2][^5]
