The Bead Lake Mountain lookout, also called Bead Lake Peak, stood in Pend Oreille County, Washington, on land of the Kaniksu and Colville National Forests.[^1][^2][^3]

The register dates the tower to 1931 and describes a 20-foot pole L-4.[^1] WillhiteWeb gives the same tower and year, with an L-4 cab.[^3] Two patrol points stood nearby: a platform tower half a mile south, and a point two miles north that WillhiteWeb calls Mosquito Point, where it describes footings and anchors.[^1][^3]

In 1941 the Forest Service surveyed a six-mile road from Bead Lake Pass to the lookout. A newspaper that August said it might be built that year.[^3][^4]

The register says the lookout was removed in the 1960s.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page lists it only as removed, with no date.[^4] The firelookout.com page and WillhiteWeb say it was destroyed in that decade instead.[^2][^3] The sources agree it was gone by then, but they differ on how it ended.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

No source describes a standing tower today. WillhiteWeb reports that the old spur road to the summit, a mile long, was still passable in 2023, though overgrown.[^3] Its photo captions name footings, one lying on its side and one upside down, but do not say exactly where they sit.[^3] According to the page, most cars can get up the road, though a higher vehicle helps, and the hike covers a mile each way with roughly 500 feet of gain.[^3] No closure is described.[^3]
