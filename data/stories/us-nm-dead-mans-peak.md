Dead Man's Peak sits in Rio Arriba County, New Mexico, at 8,786 feet.[^1] The register dates a fire tower on the site to at least 1913, and the present lookout sits on the Cuba Ranger District of the Santa Fe National Forest.[^1] It was erected in 1933 as a 30-foot Aermotor MC-24 tower, replacing an earlier platform tower of wood from the 1920s.[^1] The 2020 chapter report says the Civilian Conservation Corps put up the tower with a 12-by-12-foot wooden cab.[^2]

The tower has been reworked several times. The 2020 report, quoting a 2017 account, says a renovation was approved in 1963 under the Accelerated Public Works Program, and the structure was rebuilt during the mid-sixties.[^2] The register notes that the roof was modified and the nine-pane windows replaced in 1969.[^1] In 1988 the lookout was found not eligible for the National Register because its original design had lost integrity.[^2] The 2020 report also says a contractor produced a new steel cab in 2002, modeled on the wooden one, and set it on the tower by crane.[^2] The register still describes a wooden cab, so the two sources disagree. I followed the later 2020 account.

The register listed the lookout on April 24, 2014, on a nomination by Scott Waltemeyer.[^1] The 2020 report says the lookout was staffed at the time of writing and was the only one the Cuba Ranger District had.[^2] I found no fire history for the site in the sources I read.

Today I could not confirm current staffing or whether visitors may climb the tower. Ask the Cuba Ranger District about access before planning any visit.
