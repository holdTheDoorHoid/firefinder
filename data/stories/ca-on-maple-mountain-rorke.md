Maple Mountain is in the Timiskaming District of northeastern Ontario, within Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Provincial Park.[^1] The Temagami First Nation know the mountain as Chee-bay-jing and regard it as sacred.[^1] Dr. Robert Bell of the Geological Survey of Canada gave the mountain its English name in 1888.[^1]

The fire tower on the mountain is steel with a square cupola.[^2] A 1977 topographic map of the area (sheet 41P) shows it.[^2] It is 30 m (100 ft) tall, and Wikipedia says it is still intact.[^1] A photo caption on Firelookout.ca says two people from Bear Island built the tower and left their names in copper on top of the cupola.[^2] The same caption says a repeater station was proposed there in 1985, but the plan was not carried out.[^2]

On the afternoon of 8 July 2018, an aircraft detected a large wildland fire while it was flying to another fire near Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Provincial Park.[^3] A roadside marker in Ignace says that fire turned out to be North Bay Fire #72, a lightning-caused blaze that grew to 27,285 hectares, and that the tower stood tall while the fire burned around it.[^3]

Today, a 3.2 km trail leads to the abandoned tower.[^1][^3] The most popular approach is by canoe or float plane, and roads and trails in the remote area are hard to travel in poor weather.[^1] The sources call the tower abandoned and do not say whether it is safe to climb, so do not climb it.
