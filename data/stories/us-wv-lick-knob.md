A 1916 state report places the Lick Knob station in Clear Fork District, Raleigh County, almost on the line with Fayette County and three miles from Kingston.[^1] The site stood at 3,150 feet, and the report says it could be reached on foot from Kingston.[^1] The station was contracted for in spring 1916 and finished that summer.[^1] Luther Proffit of Kingston was assigned to duty as a temporary watchman and patrolman, and a 1.5-mile telephone line had been built to link with the Solvay Collieries Company's private line.[^1]

The sources do not agree on when the tower opened. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the original tower was built in 1921.[^2] A June 1921 notice in American Forestry, quoted on the Eastern lookout list, says the station was completed on May 10, 1921, with E.N. Wriston in charge under C.W. Harding, the forest fire warden.[^1] Because the 1916 report already describes a station in use, no single opening date can be given with confidence.[^1]

Sometime before April 1926 the tower was chopped down.[^1] A reward of $500 was offered for information about whoever destroyed it, and a district warden found a mass of wreckage where the old tower had stood.[^1] The same item adds that the commission could approve a 60-foot steel tower at its next meeting.[^1]

A 1943 Raleigh Register item describes the observer, Ira Sowder, at the tower, which the item gives as 3,150 feet above sea level.[^1] The observer and his son built a windmill at the tower to power lights and a radio set.[^1] The register says a steel tower replaced the original in 1961.[^2]

A 1985 Bluefield Daily Telegraph item, filed under Lick Knob on the Eastern list, describes a 50-foot tower on a 3,250-foot ridge. Those figures do not match the 3,150 feet given for this site, so the link to this tower is unclear.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks the Lick Knob tower as gone.[^3]

No source describes what, if anything, stands on the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors. The register shows a photo credited to Ronnie Scarbro, but its text does not describe the picture.[^2]
