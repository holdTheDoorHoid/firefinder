Spencer's fire tower stands in Van Buren County, Tennessee, on a site that the Tennessee Landforms list puts at 1,877 feet.[^1] The list numbers the tower 3-17 and marks it standing.[^1] Those numbers are district and tower numbers from a 1976 state list.[^1]

The sources do not say who built this tower, when it went up, or how tall it is. The National Register property document on the Division of Forestry's fire towers says the division built 208 of them between about 1930 and 1970.[^2] That document lists Spencer among the towers still standing as of March 26, 2014.[^2] No newer dated record appears in the sources.

The same document says many of the division's towers have been taken down or are slated for demolition, because they went unused or their land leases ended with private owners.[^2] So the sources do not settle whether the Spencer tower still stands today. The Landforms page also says its lookout history is still being gathered.[^1]

The same document also cautions that climbing surviving towers is dangerous, and that they are frequently in poor shape.[^2] The sources do not say whether this tower can be climbed or who owns the site. Check with the Tennessee Division of Forestry or the landowner before going, and do not climb it.
