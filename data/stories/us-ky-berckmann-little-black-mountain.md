The lookout sits on the highest point of Little Black Mountain in Harlan County, Kentucky.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts files it under the name Berckmann, while the federal survey record calls it the Little Black Lookout Tower.[^1][^2] Its position is given as roughly nine miles east of Harlan and three and a half miles southeast of Evarts.[^2]

A Harlan Daily Enterprise item from April 1936, quoted on Forest Lookouts, proposed a 100-foot (30.5 m) steel lookout on the mountain, south of Evarts.[^3] The item said building the tower would require roughly five miles of new truck trail up the slope.[^3] That notice describes a plan, not a finished tower.[^3] The earliest source we found that describes a standing tower is a 1950 Coast and Geodetic Survey description.[^1][^2]

The 1950 description portrays a grey steel tower on four legs, about 100 feet (30.5 m) tall, topped by a square observation cab.[^1][^2]

Forest Lookouts lists the tower as standing, based on Google Earth imagery dated April 4, 2023.[^1]

The 1950 directions took drivers along a gravel track onto Black Mountain Mine Corp. property, then to a locked gate.[^1] No source says whether that gate is still locked or whether the public may go up the mountain now.[^1]

What remains today is known only from that 2023 imagery note.[^1] No source we read describes the site now or confirms that the tower is open to visitors.[^1]
