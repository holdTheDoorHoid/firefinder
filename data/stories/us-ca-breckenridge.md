Breckenridge Lookout sits on Breckenridge Peak in the Sequoia National Forest's Greenhorn District, about 50 miles east of Bakersfield, and it is the southernmost lookout on the forest.[^2] Fire watching started on the summit in 1912, when the first lookout was a small, partly enclosed cab or a platform set in a tree.[^1][^2]

A Bakersfield Californian report from November 1930, quoted on one lookout-history site, describes a 14-foot-square lookout set on a 24-foot tower.[^3] Accounts of the next version disagree. The National Historic Lookout Register says a wooden tower with an L-4 cab replaced the first one in 1938.[^1] A blog instead dates a wooden tower beside the old tree to 1931, and says it was used until 1944, when a Civilian Conservation Corps structure replaced it.[^4]

The present cab was constructed in 1942.[^1] The register describes an L-4 cab with a catwalk on a 40-foot steel tower, while fire-lookouts.org calls it a C-3 style cab built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1][^2]

Newspaper items from the 1930s name some of the people posted there. In August 1931 Melvin Marshall had hosted 400 visitors since May 11, and in 1935 Ross Shaw was stationed at the ranger lookout station.[^3]

During the Second World War the lookout was used for aircraft warning, scanning for enemy planes.[^1][^4] The register listed it on June 10, 2001.[^1]

A fire-lookouts.org page last updated in 2013 says the lookout has full-time staff in fire season and is generally open to visitors.[^2] A 2009 association report says the forest's fire management officer found the lightning protection at Breckenridge not operationally sound, and that the forest then contracted a grounding inspection and repairs for its remaining lookouts.[^2]

The site is reached by Forest Service Road 28S62 off the Caliente-Bodfish Road, and a blog lists a drive of about 1 hour 30 minutes from Kernville.[^2][^4] Because these descriptions are older, check current conditions with the district before planning a trip.
