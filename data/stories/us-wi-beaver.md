The Beaver Lookout is a 73-foot International Stacy tower in Marinette County, Wisconsin. The National Historic Lookout Register says it was built in November 1933 and has an inside corner ladder.[^1] A Wisconsin lookout directory lists the tower as standing.[^2]

The Register says the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources administered the tower. It was staffed on and off during the spring fire season until 2015, the year the state retired its lookout network.[^1]

A federal survey station description, written in 1934, places the tower in the northeast quarter of Section 10, Township 31 North, Range 19 East. It says the land was owned by a private individual and that the Beaver lookout tower occupied it.[^3]

A Wisconsin Public Radio report says the DNR will stop using the state's 72 fire lookout towers, and that replacing them would probably cost around $25 million.[^4]

A Wisconsin Historical Society record describes a different tower, the Pembine/Paust Tower, built in November 1933 with the International Stacey Company as its architect. That record places its original site in another township and section, and says it was later moved in 1991. The sources read do not link it to the Beaver tower.[^5]

The sources read do not say whether the Beaver tower has been moved, sold or taken down since 2015, or whether the public may climb it. A Wisconsin travel article calls the Mountain Fire Lookout Tower in the Nicolet forest the last Wisconsin fire tower that visitors can still climb.[^6] Do not assume the Beaver tower is open, and check with the Wisconsin DNR before planning any visit.
