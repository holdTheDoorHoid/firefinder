The Texas Forestry Department built the state's first forest fire lookout tower in 1926, on the Kirbyville State Forest in Newton County.[^1][^3] A 1926 report says the work had federal help under the Clarke-McNary Act.[^1] The steel tower was 80 feet tall, stood on a high knoll, and had a cab seven feet square with windows on all sides.[^1][^3]

A lookoutman staffed the tower.[^1] It had a fire finder for plotting fires on a map and a telephone to reach patrolmen nearby.[^1] A newspaper report from late 1927, quoted on a lookout history site, said the lookoutman had found 175 fires that fall, and that fires had been sighted as far away as 35 miles.[^1]

In 1948 a newspaper said the tower would be moved slightly and raised to 120 feet.[^1] I found no later source saying that change happened, and a later Texas A&M Forest Service release still gives the height as 80 feet.[^3]

In 1951 the forest was renamed the E.O. Siecke State Forest, after Eric O. Siecke, the agency's second director and state forester.[^3][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^2] The Texas A&M Forest Service says the tower and the remnants of the first slash pine plantation still exist.[^4]

The forest is open year-round during daylight hours, with limited vehicle access.[^4] The agency lists the tower as "not available for public climbing use," so treat it as something to see from the ground rather than to climb.[^4]
