The Polk County fire patrol association built a lookout on Dorn Peak in the spring of 1929.[^1][^3] A June 1929 newspaper item calls the place Doran's Peak, south of Grand Ronde.[^3] Three large Douglas fir trees served as the uprights. Their tops were cut to 90 feet and fastened together, a small cabin sat on a firm foundation at the top, and steel cables guyed the trees against swaying. A stairway led up to the cabin.[^3]

The sources disagree about the trees and the height. The register describes three 90-foot trees.[^1] firelookout.com says three 50-foot trees.[^2] A 1941 survey note, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, puts the tower at about 80 feet. It describes a triangular plank platform, fixed to three trunks that held it up.[^3]

In 1931 a Mrs. Sawtell had charge of the lookout.[^3] Two papers that year spelled her name differently. The Daily Capital Journal said she met a big cougar on her way down for a pail of water. The Forest Log said a large cougar had taken up guard at the spring she went to.[^3] In March 1937 a fire warden reported that snow had badly damaged the house.[^3]

By June 1942 an inspection report found a married couple, Mr. and Mrs. Walter Isbell, at the station.[^3] The inspector wrote that the tree tower was of little use for spotting airplanes.[^3] The same report said the ground cabin, built about 1928, was old and poorly built.[^3]

The sources agree the tower was gone by 1959, but they differ on how it went.[^1][^2][^3] The register says it was removed that year.[^1] firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^2] A 1960 survey note records a report from the local fire marshal that the station was destroyed.[^3]

We found no source that describes the peak today. A 1941 survey note mentions bronze survey disks set near the tower in 1935, but it does not say whether they survive.[^3] The register lists the site as administered by the Lincoln FPA.[^1] Nothing we read says whether the summit is open to visitors.[^1]
