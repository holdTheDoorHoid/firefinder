In 1938 Alberta's forestry branch put four radio-equipped lookout stations into operation, one of them at Mayberne, northwest of Edson.[^1] A 1938 Calgary Herald report, as quoted on a lookout history site, estimated the four stations at $15,000 and believed the system was the first set up in Canada.[^1] Each unit's cabin gave the lookout man a place to live and held the radio transmitter.[^1] An Alberta Historic Places article dates the first two-way radios in lookouts to the same year.[^4]

Annual reports quoted on the same site record later upkeep at Mayberne.[^1] In 1944 a new ladder was built on the tower, its legs were creosoted, and the radio poles were replaced.[^1] In 1949 the cabin was shingled outside, and its inside was finished and painted.[^1]

The provincial dataset gives 2011 as the year the present steel tower was built. It stands 18 m (59 ft) high, at an elevation of 1,453 m (4,768 ft).[^2] OpenStreetMap also gives the site the alternate name Clarke Lake Tower.[^3]

The dataset lists the tower as active, with an access mode it labels drive.[^2] No source describes the site today, and none says whether the public may visit it.
