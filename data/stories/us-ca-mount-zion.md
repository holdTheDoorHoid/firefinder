Mount Zion rises about 2,969 feet in Amador County, within the Mt. Zion State Forest, and Cal Fire administers the lookout.[^1] A 1928 report placed the station near Pine Grove, though a 1930 item said near Jackson.[^2]

State foresters selected Mount Zion in 1927 as the most desirable site, and a station was in use by June 1928.[^2] A July 1928 report said the man in charge had located fifteen fires from June 9 onward.[^2]

The State erected an Aermotor Company tower in 1930, and Amador County contributed $250 toward the structure.[^1][^2] A December 1929 notice had said the tower would be seventy feet high, and a February 1930 report said it stood 50 feet higher than the old station.[^2]

Newspapers gave different dates for the dedication.[^2] An April 1930 notice expected a Sunday ceremony, while a June 3 report described one held on a Friday afternoon.[^2] A 1930 item reported that the name came from five ministers who had mined the area.[^2]

Winter 1931-32 saw a SERA camp at the site, which later became a CCC camp.[^1] Under the ECW program, a BC-201 lookout residence went up, and an office, water tower and garage may date from 1932.[^1]

A May 2020 visit report said Cal Fire V.I.P. volunteers were to staff the tower from June 1, 2020, and that a locked gate stops vehicles short of it.[^1] The same report said the tower itself was not open to the average visitor, though people could walk up to its fence.[^1]

The Register says the buildings keep a high degree of integrity, and it recorded the site on October 15, 2019 as US 1306, CA 129.[^1] The shutters are gone, and the cabin was being renovated for staffing in 2020.[^1]

The sources I read do not mention a rental, and none says the tower is open to the public now, so check with Cal Fire before planning a visit.[^1][^2]
