Escoheag Hill sits in Kent County, Rhode Island, and the two registers that cover it tell its story differently.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register dates the first tower to 1934, when it stood on an unnamed knoll in Burrillville, inside George Washington State Forest.[^2] A 1934 entry on the Forest Lookouts site quotes the Department of Agriculture and Conservation's first annual report, which says the federal government funded the tower's construction as a work project.[^3] The register says the tower was moved in 1937 and rebuilt on its present spot in 1938.[^2] The former-lookout register instead says the Civilian Conservation Corps built an 80-foot tower here in 1938.[^1]

The register says the tower has been out of service as a lookout since about 1990.[^2] Sometime after 2009, vandals burned the wooden cab.[^2] The blaze wrecked most of the top landings and harmed the upper stairs, and charred boards are strewn across the ground near the lookout.[^2]

The two registers disagree about what is left. The former-lookout register mentions abandoned remains on the site.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Rhode Island list shows an Escoheag Hill tower as standing, with an asterisk meaning its structure remains but its cab is gone.[^4] The two also give different coordinates, so it is not clear that they describe the same structure.[^1][^2]

The Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management administers the site, according to the register.[^2] The register says the tower is currently unmaintained.[^2] We would not encourage anyone to climb it. We found no source saying whether the site is open to the public.
