The Lohmer tower stands in the Mark Twain National Forest in Barry County, just off Missouri Highway 76 between Shell Knob and Cassville.[^1][^2][^3] Local sources spell the name Lohmer, but OpenStreetMap spells it Lahmer.[^1][^2][^5] The community of Lohmer also sits on Route 76.[^3]

In December 1935, enrollees of Camp F-20 at Shell Knob were building a 100-foot (30 m) steel lookout that the Cassville Republican called the first steel tower of that height on the Table Rock Unit of the Gardiner National Forest.[^1] The paper expected work on the cupola at the top to start around December 10, with the whole tower finished by December 14.[^1] A Joplin paper reported on December 15 that it was nearing completion.[^1] The Forest Service's history page also describes a Shell Knob CCC camp that built the Cassville Ranger Station in 1936.[^4]

A visitor guide says the tower is still up but no longer in active use, and that the Forest Service once used it to spot wildfires.[^2] The guide puts it about 1.2 miles west of Missouri Highway 39 on Route 76.[^2]

None of these sources says whether the tower can be climbed today, so this story does not suggest climbing it.
