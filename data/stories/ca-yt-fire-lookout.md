The Dome Lookout, called the Dawson Lookout in a 2024 article, stands near Dawson City, Yukon, on a grassy hilltop that locals call the Second Dome.[^1][^3] From the top, it overlooks the Midnight Dome, and the town of Dawson sits out of sight below.[^1] OpenStreetMap maps the site as a tower used for observation.[^4]

Sources disagree about when it went up. Canada's Fire Lookouts, as quoted by ExploreNorth, gives 1963.[^2] Moyles writes that the tower was built as early as 1965 and describes it as originally built in the mid-to-late 1960s.[^1]

The tower is made of steel and held in place by guy wires.[^1] Its top room is an octagonal domed cupola, painted red and white, with 360-degree views of the forest.[^1] A small log cabin with a porch stands beside it.[^1] The article gives the height two ways: a photo caption gives 70 metres (230 feet), while the text puts his top-of-tower office 21 metres (70 feet) in the air.[^1]

In the 2014 season, six of the Yukon's nine fire towers were staffed whatever the fire risk, and the Dome in Dawson City was one of them.[^2] According to the 2024 article, Markus Lenzin has spent the past 15 summers watching for wildfires from the Dawson Lookout.[^1] Each season runs from May to August, and his days last 12 hours.[^1] On June 26, 2024, he caught two fires, one of them the Moosehide Creek Fire, which started after dry lightning hit a tree less than seven kilometres from his tower.[^1]

The article was published in the Winter 2024 issue of North of Ordinary Magazine and carries an undated post-script from a later visit.[^1] The author has visited every fire season since 2024, so the lookout appears to remain in use.[^1] A steep gravel road branches off Dome Road toward the site.[^1] A visitor listing says the tower was locked partway up.[^5] No official source I could open says whether the public may climb it, so I would treat access as restricted.
