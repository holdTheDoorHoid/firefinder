Girard Ridge Fire Lookout was built in 1931 on what is now the Shasta-Trinity National Forest, above the Sacramento River canyon.[^1][^2] It's an L-4 cab, fourteen feet square with a catwalk, sitting atop an enclosed, tapered 13-foot wooden tower, a design dating to 1917 that the National Historic Lookout Register calls unique to the California region.[^1] The Register also states it's the oldest surviving structure of its type in the state.[^1]

The Forest Service used Girard Ridge for regular fire-watch duty for roughly fifty years, into 1981.[^2] After that, the tower was taken out of active detection service.[^1]

Rather than let it go, the Forest Service and volunteers restored Girard Ridge to its original specifications in 1997.[^1][^2] It went into the agency's lookout rental program the following year, in 1998.[^1]

Today the lookout is open to overnight renters, reached by dirt roads that are steep and narrow in places, running mostly through private timberland where log trucks are still active, so high-clearance vehicles are recommended.[^2] The gate combination has to be requested by phone a week ahead of arrival.[^2] Inside, the cabin has two twin beds with mattresses, storage cabinets, and a table, but no running water, electricity, or cooking facilities; portable gas stoves are allowed with a valid California campfire permit, though wood and charcoal fires are not.[^2] The reward for the primitive conditions is the view: renters look north to Mount Shasta, west across the Castle Crags, and southeast toward the distant silhouette of Lassen Peak.[^2]
