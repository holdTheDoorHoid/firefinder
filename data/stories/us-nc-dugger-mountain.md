Dugger Mountain in Watauga County once carried a steel fire lookout tower, and a fire spotter was still watching from it in 1980.[^1][^2]

In 1928 the state selected a site on Dugger Mountain and ordered a 35-foot steel tower with an inside ladder.[^1] Watauga County was not a cooperating county in fire prevention, so the site could not become a primary lookout station.[^1] The plan was to link the tower by telephone to Penley and on to Darby.[^1] A state report later said a 35-foot steel tower went up near Penley in 1929.[^1] In 1941 a 60-foot tower was erected by the Civilian Conservation Corps and the North Carolina Forest Service.[^1]

By November 1980, Clemmie Watson had kept watch from the tower for 36 years, every spring and fall, watching for fire.[^2] She worked part-time for the N.C. Forest Service and radioed the rangers when she spotted a fire.[^2]

A 1983 survey description of the site records a four-legged silver tower about 75 feet tall.[^1] The heights differ by date: 35 feet in 1929, 60 feet in 1941 and about 75 feet in 1983. The sources do not say whether one tower replaced another.[^1] I found no source after 1983 that says whether the tower still stands, and no source describes the site or its access today.[^1]
