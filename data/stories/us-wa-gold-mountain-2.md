Gold Mountain is Kitsap County's highest summit, and it also tops the hills west of Bremerton.[^4] The first lookout stood at the original site, about seven miles west-southwest of Bremerton.[^2]

A road to the lookout site was cleared in 1947, and a metal tower went up in 1948.[^4] The register and firelookout.com date the first lookout to 1947 instead.[^1][^2] The sources disagree on the tower itself. The Division of Forestry's annual report, as quoted by two sites, lists a 75-foot steel Navy surplus tower, with a ground house of 14 by 18 feet at the end of the road.[^3][^4] Other records give 85 feet.[^1][^2][^4] A 1954 survey party described a wooden cabin on a wooden frame about 90 feet tall.[^4]

In summer 1955, Mary Lou Nelson manned the lookout, helped by Helen Walsh.[^4] Kathy Stewart was the lookout in 1961 and again in 1962.[^4] Gloria and Dick Tyler staffed it in 1965 and 1966.[^4]

In 1962 the state Department of Natural Resources (DNR) began applying for ten acres just west of the original site, for a new live-in lookout.[^4] A 50-foot wooden tower went up on the next knob west in 1965.[^1][^4] It was destroyed in 1972, after only seven years, and the lookout-history website says the reason is unclear.[^1][^2][^4] When a USGS survey party visited in 1974, it found the original tower dismantled, with four leg bases still in place.[^4]

Today, a 2023 visit reported in the same history found both survey benchmarks still in place. Some wood framing is still attached to the footings, and the steps to the old ground house were photographed that year.[^4] Radio facilities now occupy the knob where the 1965 tower stood, and its footings are there.[^4]

The road to the summit runs through the restricted Bremerton Watershed, and the signs marking that restriction have vanished and reappeared.[^4] The sources we read do not settle whether the public may visit, so check the watershed rules before going.
