Cornucopia Peak sits in Baker County, in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest.[^1] In April 1922 the Oregonian reported that a lookout station would be set up on the peak that summer.[^3]

The sources do not agree on when the cab went up. The register dates it to 1920.[^1] Oregon Hikers gives 1922 and says the lookout was later demolished.[^4] Firelookout.com says it was built in 1923.[^2] A February 1923 item quoted by Forest Lookouts described plans for a standard-design lookout house on the summit, at 8,600 feet.[^3] We lean toward 1923, because an April 1923 report describes a newly located station.[^3] The sources do not settle it.

By April 1923 Lookout Magill had been posted to the new station. Soon after he got there, he reported a small fire about seven miles from the lookout. A fireman searched and found no fire.[^3] Firelookout.com names Mr. Coble in 1923 and Edward Burns in 1939.[^2] Forest Lookouts names W.H. Baird for the 1930 season and Wayne Chetwood in charge in July 1946.[^3]

The dates of the end also differ. Firelookout.com says the cab was abandoned in 1945, but the Forest Lookouts site shows a lookout in charge in 1946.[^2][^3] A 2023 trip report dates the lookout's removal to the 1940s.[^5]

The register says the lookout is gone.[^1] Oregon Hikers says a few guy wires and bits of broken glass are still there, and the 2023 report includes photos of the remains.[^4][^5] The summit is reached on foot.[^4][^5] Oregon Hikers puts the gain near 900 feet, starting from the Cornucopia Trail.[^4] The 2023 report says the road past the trailhead is shut to public traffic.[^5]
