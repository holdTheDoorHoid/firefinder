The Red Dirt lookout stood on Kisatchie National Forest land in Natchitoches Parish, Louisiana.[^1] The register says Civilian Conservation Corps Company 1491 built it in 1937 as a 100-foot steel tower with an 11-by-11-foot steel cab and a walkway all around.[^1]

The sources disagree on the height and the build year. A 1941 survey description, copied on the Forest Lookouts site, puts the tower at about 112 feet, with a crow's nest and living quarters on top.[^2] The Longleaf Trail scenic byway guide dates the site to 1936.[^3]

The register says the tower was moved to Fort Sill, Oklahoma in 1983, by which time aerial detection of wildfires had become the usual practice in Louisiana.[^1] The byway guide gives a similar reason: the tower came down in 1983 because it was no longer needed, and a forest-wide aerial detection system replaced it.[^3]

The guide lists the Red Dirt Fire Tower Site as a stop along the trail, which suggests the public can visit the ground where the tower stood.[^3] No source we read describes what remains there, such as footings or a foundation, and we found no visitor rules for the site.
