Taft Peak had an L-4 cab, and a listing dates it to 1937.[^1] The Forest Service describes the L-4 as a 14-by-14-foot pre-cut house, produced between 1929 and 1953.[^3]

The sources do not agree on where the site lies. The firelookout.com listing places it four miles east of Lolo Pass, on the Lolo National Forest in Mineral County.[^1] The association gives coordinates of 47.44855 north and 115.61179 west.[^2] Lolo Pass, on the Idaho–Montana border, lies at about 46.635 north and 114.580 west, more than 100 km from those coordinates, so the two descriptions do not agree.[^4] Until someone checks the location, the exact spot is uncertain.

The association's list calls the site a ground lookout that is gone.[^2] The listing gives 1960 as the year the cab was gone.[^1]

No source describes the Taft Peak site today or says whether the public can reach it.
