Meadow Mountain lies eight miles south of Snoqualmie Pass in King County, on land the U.S. Forest Service administers.[^1][^2] Panoramic photographs dated 1934 were taken from the summit, and the Washington fire lookouts guide at willhiteweb.com says they appear to show a lookout camp.[^1][^3]

The L-4 cab followed in 1935.[^1][^3] It sat on a rock talus outcrop at the summit's southern tip, a spot where trees cannot grow.[^3] In 1958 the U.S. Geological Survey placed a monument at the site.[^3]

The cab stood until 1972.[^3] The register says it was removed that year, while Firelookout.com says it was destroyed, so the sources agree on the year but not on how the cab left the summit.[^1][^2]

Today the site is a hike.[^4] The Mountaineers describe the lookout site on the south summit as abandoned and rarely visited. Getting there means a steep, rocky road, an overgrown clear-cut and an old trail.[^4] The willhiteweb guide says high clearance gets you closest to the start, and that the walk runs from half a mile to two miles depending on where you park.[^3] That guide also records anchor lines still up on the summit and four phone lines running down the mountainside.[^3] No source we read describes a standing cab there today.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
