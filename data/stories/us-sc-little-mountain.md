The register describes the Little Mountain tower in Anderson County as a Bethlehem Steel tower standing 75 feet tall.[^1] The register says the South Carolina Forestry Commission built it around 1938.[^1] The Commission's first tower went up near Camden in August 1930, and the state closed its whole tower system on October 1, 1993.[^2]

I found no source that names the people who staffed the tower or any fire it watched, so those parts of its history are unknown.

The register says the tower was sold to a private owner in 1990, and it places that sale after the state gave up its fire tower system.[^1] The Forestry Commission gives 1993 as the year it closed the system.[^2] These dates do not line up, and the pages I could read do not settle which one is right.

By 2017 the owner was renovating the tower.[^1] The register names Ted Brown as its nominator and Ted D. Brown as the administrator.[^1]

As of the register's 2017 entry, the tower was in private hands.[^1] I found no source that says the public may visit or climb it, so please treat it as private property and do not climb it.

[^1]: Little Mountain Fire Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/sc/little-mountain-fire-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Pictures From Our Past, South Carolina Forestry Commission, https://www.scfc.gov/about-us/history/pictures-from-our-past/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
