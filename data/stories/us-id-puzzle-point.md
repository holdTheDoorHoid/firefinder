Puzzle Point Lookout stood in Idaho County, in the Nez Perce National Forest, about three miles southwest of the Moose Creek Ranger Station.[^1][^3][^4] The site sits at an elevation of 6,055 feet, inside the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness.[^1]

An aviation blog calls the Moose Creek Ranger Station one of the most isolated Forest Service posts in the country. It says no road comes within about 25 miles of the station in any direction.[^5]

The lookout was a two-story log structure topped by a cupola, built in 1929.[^1][^2][^3] An April 2010 Forest Service list, compiled partly from Ray Kresek's book on Northwest lookouts, gives the same year.[^4]

The cabin was destroyed about 1949.[^1][^3][^4] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today.

The Idaho Fire Lookouts page lists the site as hike-in.[^2]
