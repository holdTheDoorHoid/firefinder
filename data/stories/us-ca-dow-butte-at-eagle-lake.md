The Dow Butte cab went up in 1939, built by the Civilian Conservation Corps on the eastern side of the Sierras, in the Lassen National Forest.[^1][^3] The register counts it as one of five cabs of its kind in Region 5.[^1] The lookout was staffed until 1982.[^1]

A 1971-72 engine crew member who worked below the butte remembered the lookout as a retired schoolteacher.[^1] It had a single phone line to the Dow Wells guard station.[^1] The crew could not pick up dispatch on their radios from the low spot where they worked, so the lookout would crank the phone to relay messages from dispatch, though the call did not always get through.[^1] When that relay failed, which was a regular occurrence, she called them on the radio.[^1]

The cab came off the butte in 1994.[^3] The Exploring Lassen County's Past blog says it can be seen at the Eagle Lake Marina.[^3] A December 1994 Feather River Bulletin item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said it had been moved to the Gallatin area and would open the next spring as a visitor's center and interpretive site.[^5] Peakbagging names Gallatin Marina on Eagle Lake as the cab's new home.[^4] The Forest Service puts it on the southern shore of the lake.[^2] The sources do not agree on the exact spot, so the Forest Service location is the one to rely on.[^2][^3][^4][^5]

The register describes the move as a restoration at a museum site.[^1] The Forest Service says the cab has interpretive signage.[^2]

Today it is a day-use spot.[^2] The Forest Service lists it as open from sunrise to sunset, with no fee.[^2] Questions can go to the Eagle Lake Ranger District office at 477-050 Eagle Lake Road in Susanville.[^2] The register listed the cab on October 15, 1997.[^1]
