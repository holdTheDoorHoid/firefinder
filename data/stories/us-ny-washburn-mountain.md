Washburn Mountain, in Columbia County, was the site of a fire lookout tower from 1932 to 1964.[^5] The tower first went up in 1928 on Alander Mountain, just inside Massachusetts, when the Taconic State Park Commission erected it.[^1][^4]

After a 1930 fire burned the area, the landowner requested the tower's removal, and park staff took it down.[^1][^5] Sources disagree on where it was stored.[^1][^5] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says a park facility at High Falls, while Wikipedia says High Valley Farm.[^1][^5] It went back up on Washburn Mountain in 1933.[^1] Wikipedia dates the rebuild to late 1932, with operation starting in 1933.[^5]

Forest Fire Control took over its operation about 1935, and the tower was actively used until 1963.[^1] In 1963 it was dismantled and carried away by helicopter to Beebe Hill.[^1][^2] The dates differ.[^5][^3] Wikipedia gives 1964, and a 1965 newspaper item says the tower came down in Copake Falls the fall before, with construction of a tower atop Beebe Hill underway.[^5][^3]

On Beebe Hill's summit, at 1,726 feet, the tower stood in what is now Beebe Hill State Forest. The tower stayed in service until 1987.[^4] In 1997 local residents, the Forest Fire Lookout Association and the state Department of Environmental Conservation began efforts to restore the tower and open a trail network to it.[^4]

Wikipedia calls the Beebe Hill tower defunct and reports that the state forest's website considered it unsafe as of late 2024.[^4] The Department of Environmental Conservation page for the two state forests describes "a fire tower that is open to the public."[^6] These accounts disagree, so check with the Department before going up, and do not climb a tower that is closed or unsafe.

On Washburn Mountain itself, we found no source that says what, if anything, is left of the 1933 site. Wikipedia says the tower is still on Beebe Hill.[^5]
