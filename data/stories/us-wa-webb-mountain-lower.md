In 1934 the Washington Division of Forestry put a lookout on a sidehill on Webb Mountain, in Jefferson County.[^3] Sources differ on what it looked like.[^1][^3][^4] One says the cab was assembled on the ground and then placed on the tower by way of a ramp of small round poles, and that a carpenter, Ralph McClanahan, built the lookout.[^1] Firelookout.com describes a 10-foot pole tower on a sidehill three-quarters of a mile east of the summit, and says a two-story slant-walled cabin was built on the summit by 1939.[^4] Firetower.org, by contrast, ties the slant-walled cabin itself to the sidehill in 1934.[^3]

Panorama photographs were taken there on June 11, 1935.[^1][^2]

The cabin came down in 1966.[^3][^4] Firetower.org says it was removed, while Firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^3][^4]

In photographs dated 2015 and 2016 on Willhite's page, two timbers from the old lookout still stand upright, and one foundation still has a post on it.[^2] Firelookout.com also says notable ruins could remain at the earlier sidehill site.[^4] Willhite's directions run north of Hoodsport to a parking area at the edge of a clear-cut on the National Forest boundary.[^2] Firetower.org lists the site as administered by the U.S. Forest Service.[^3] None of the pages we read says whether visitors may enter the site, so this account does not say.
