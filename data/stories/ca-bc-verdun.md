The Verdun lookout is a Forest Service lookout in the Bulkley-Nechako area of British Columbia, at an elevation of 1,180 m (3,871 ft).[^1][^2] The sources I could reach do not give a build date or a design for it.[^1][^2]

The only staffing record I found dates from May 1991.[^1] A Lake District News item from that month, as quoted on Canada's Fire Lookouts, reports that Daphne Ewart had come back to the Southside and was again at the Verdun Lookout, where she welcomed visitors.[^1]

A 2015 visitor notice from the Burns Lake Lakes District News places the lookout about 49 km (30 mi) south of a town it does not name.[^2] The route, it says, crosses Francois Lake by ferry from Highway 35, then 11 km (6.8 mi) and another 12 km (7.5 mi) of road, a turn onto the Verdun Lookout Forest Service Road, and about 2 km (1.2 mi) of sometimes rough access road.[^2] From the top, the notice says, the view takes in the Uncha/Binta Valley and Francois Lake, and reaches on to Ootsa Lake and Tweedsmuir Park. It calls the lookout a good outing for all ages.[^2]

No later source I could reach describes what stands at the site today or whether the road is open, so the 2015 notice is the newest word on it.[^2]

[^2]: Verdun Lookout – great place to visit, Burns Lake Lakes District News, https://www.burnslakelakesdistrictnews.com/entertainment/verdun-lookout-great-place-to-visit/ (accessed 2026-10-20).
