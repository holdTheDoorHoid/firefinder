The Twin Sisters lookout stood on a peak above the Cowlitz Valley in Lewis County, Washington.[^1] The Washington Lookouts listing now places it in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1]

In 1922 the Chehalis Bee-Nugget named J. J. Modine as the lookout at Twin Sisters, one of Lewis County's federal fire wardens, and described that season's lookouts as working in the Rainier National Forest.[^1]

In April 1926 the paper reported that new funds for lookout towers and phone lines would bring a fresh approach to fire control in the Rainier forest, and that a tower would go up on the Twin Sisters peak.[^1] The planned tower would pair a ranger's cabin with a lookout, its top a glass-walled cupola holding an Osborne fire finder.[^1]

In July 1927 the paper said Albert Westall and his wife planned a two-month stay at the Twin Sisters station, beginning once the trail was opened to bring in supplies.[^1] By September 1928 the couple had come down from the station, having spent about two months there working for the Forest Service.[^1]

The listing ends with a single word, "Removed," and gives no date.[^1]

A history of lookouts in the Mossyrock area says that by the 1960s most had been abandoned, and that nearly all were later ordered destroyed, most often by fire.[^2] The same history says that only the High Rock and Burley Mountain lookouts remain standing today in the Cowlitz Valley District.[^2]

No source we found describes what, if anything, is left on the Twin Sisters peak today, or whether the peak is open to visitors.
