Twin Knobs stands in the Mark Twain National Forest in Douglas County, Missouri, and the U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^1] The Forest Service acquired the tower site on April 13, 1936.[^2][^3] A December 1936 Howell County Gazette item, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, said the Willow Springs camp had just finished the tower.[^2] A former Civilian Conservation Corps enrollee, however, recalled that it was under construction in 1935 while he was enrolled at the Cabool camp, and the state plan suggests work may have started before 1936.[^3] The two accounts name different camps, so the exact building dates remain unsettled.[^2][^3]

A dwelling complex for the towerman was added some time after the tower went up.[^3] The state plan gives the late 1960s or early 1970s as the likely years those buildings were torn down, and none of the originals stand on the site.[^3]

As of the 2015 plan, the Missouri Department of Conservation used the tower to spot fires under a special-use permit and staffed it during fire season.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the tower on March 14, 2014, as US 999, MO 7.[^1] The 2015 plan says the tower has been found eligible for the National Register of Historic Places.[^3]

The register describes the tower as currently locked behind a tall chain link fence.[^1] The 2015 plan says the site is not open for public use, so it is not a place to visit or climb.[^3]
