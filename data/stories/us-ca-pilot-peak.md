Pilot Peak is a lookout in the Feather River district of the Plumas National Forest, a few miles outside La Porte in Plumas County.[^1][^2] The register gives the summit as 7,412 feet, while Tahoe Quarterly gives 7,457 feet.[^1][^2]

The first cabin, a 4A groundhouse built in 1916, was heavily rebuilt by the Civilian Conservation Corps in 1935.[^1] Vandals burned the lookout in 1971.[^1][^2] The Forest Service then chose the site for a prototype, and the present two-story hexagonal cab was built in 1976.[^1][^2] The overhang is held up by flying buttresses, and Robert Sandusky, a Forest Service architect, designed the structure.[^1][^2] The register also credits it as the Forest Service's first solar-powered lookout.[^1][^2] The register lists it as NHLR listing #600, added in September 2004.[^1]

A 2007 visit report found the stairs unsafe, some of them missing, and the wood badly weathered.[^1] A June 2019 photo caption says the structure is not in use and is in need of some repair.[^3] Tahoe Quarterly calls the lookout inactive and says the second-floor staircase is gone and the roof is missing.[^2] The Tahoe Quarterly piece is dated 26 September and filed under Fall 2025, the same season as the repair work below, so the two accounts may be close in time.[^2]

According to the register, roof work started in fall 2025 under a restoration that the Forest Fire Lookout Association and the Forest Service are running together.[^1]

A dirt road of about 1.5 miles leads from Quincy-La Porte Road in Onion Valley to the tower.[^2] The 2007 visit report says the last half mile is steep and needs a four-wheel-drive vehicle.[^1] Tahoe Quarterly reports the road was recently graded and is in good overall shape.[^2] With the stairs reported unsafe, I would not climb the cab.[^1][^2]

Another lookout, also called Pilot Peak, stands on the Stanislaus National Forest in Tuolumne County.[^4] It was built in 1962-63 and has a separate register record, so it is not covered here.[^4]
