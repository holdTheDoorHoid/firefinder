The tnlandforms.us list of Georgia lookout towers has a "grand bay" entry in Lowndes County, with an elevation of 198. The list prints no unit, so that figure reads as feet.[^1] The same list says names for towers numbered 73 and above are its own, so the label may not match what the tower is called.[^1] The structure at Grand Bay is known as the Kinderlou Tower.[^2]

A 2012 photo essay says the 54-foot tower was used to spot fires in the nearby Kinderlou Forest between 1939 and 1993.[^3] The Georgia Encyclopedia says the structure began as a fire tower above Langdale Forest Products' timber holdings, and has since been altered so visitors can observe the wetland.[^2] The list's note on the grand bay entry reads "Ron GA097-relocated," and its GA097 line is "kinderlou," so the list seems to link the two.[^1]

The essay says Valdosta businessman Harley Langdale, Jr., donated the tower to the state.[^3] The Encyclopedia calls it a gift from a local businessman.[^2] Taken together, the sources suggest the tower was moved from Kinderlou Forest to Grand Bay.[^1][^2][^3]

A Georgia Department of Natural Resources page for the wildlife area carries a notice, posted 10/8/26, that the boardwalk is shut until further notice.[^4] An undated alert on the same page gives gate hours for the boardwalk and the lookout tower, which conflicts with the notice. This story follows the dated notice.[^4] The page also says a federally owned section of the area will be shut to visitors from time to time for military training.[^4]

A Wikipedia photo captioned March 2019 shows the wildlife area as seen from a lookout tower, and the article mentions an observation tower at the education center.[^5] The 2012 essay describes walking up the tower, warns of red wasps in summer, and says the view from the top justifies the climb.[^3] It also says visitors then needed a Georgia Outdoor Recreation Pass to get in.[^3] The boardwalk is the route to the tower, so check the DNR notice before planning a visit.[^2][^4]

Do not confuse this with the Lowndes County Fire Tower, a 100-foot steel tower built in 1937 with CCC labor, which the National Historic Lookout Register lists separately.[^6] The Grand Bay Timber Protective Association backed its construction.[^6]
