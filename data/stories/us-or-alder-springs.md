The Alder Springs lookout stood on Green Ridge in the Deschutes National Forest, about 23 miles north of Sisters.[^2][^3] Sources disagree about the sequence of towers on the site, and the account below notes where they differ.

The first lookout was a tree platform. The register calls it a 70-foot tree platform.[^1] A lookout history page describes a 90-foot pine with a six-foot platform, located southwest of the later site.[^4]

In 1939 the Bend Bulletin reported that a butte called Five Fingered Sentinal had been chosen for a new lookout. By August, Arvo Koski had left Prairie Farm, where he had been stationed while the site was readied, and was in a temporary crow's nest in a tree.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com say an 85-foot treated timber tower with a steel cab, plus a cabin, replaced the tree in 1940.[^1][^2] A history page lists a 76-foot tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1944 a couple miles north of the tree lookout.[^3] We could not tell whether these describe one tower or two.

Charles Lewis was the lookout in July 1940, when lightning struck a green tree about 100 yards from the tower.[^3] In October 1941 forest service employees put out a six-acre hunter fire just west of the lookout.[^3] The station reported one fire during the 1959 season.[^3]

Both the register and firelookout.com say the lookout was gone by 1965.[^1][^2]

The register's photo captions include "Material remains" and "Present lookout site," both dated June 4, 2002, but the captions do not say what was there.[^1] Firelookout.com describes the 70-foot crow's nest as "still there," a claim the register does not repeat.[^2] We found no source that describes the site as it is now or whether the public can reach it.

A separate Green Ridge Lookout, a cabin on a two-story tower on the same ridge, is available for rent.[^5] Recreation.gov says it was built in the 1960s.[^6] The sources do not say whether it stands on the old Alder Springs site, so visitors should not assume it does.
