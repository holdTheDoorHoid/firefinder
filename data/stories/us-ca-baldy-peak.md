Baldy Peak rises in Del Norte County, California, about 25 miles east-southeast of Crescent City and about two and a half miles east of the South Fork of the Smith River.[^1] A 1941 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls the summit building a Forest Service lookout house.[^1]

A May 1940 planning note on the Forest Lookouts page says the point would be needed only in the normal lightning season, since lightning fires had been the usual pattern in recent years.[^1] The page lists the peak under the Siskiyou National Forest, followed by the Six Rivers National Forest, and the Peakbagging table also gives the forest as Six Rivers.[^1][^2]

A 1977 U.S. Geological Survey recovery note says the lookout house was destroyed, leaving only the floor.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks Baldy Peak as gone, with no date.[^3]

The Forest Service's South Kelsey Trail (#4E06) runs for eight miles beside the South Fork of the Smith River, then climbs steeply for five more to reach the peak.[^4] The trail page warns that the section past Harrington Creek has gone unmaintained for several years.[^4] A 2020 trip report describes a lot of burn still visible on the trail over the top of Baldy Peak.[^5] At the summit, the writer found a square of rocks where the fire lookout had stood, with scraps of wood, metal and window glass strewn around it.[^5] The trip report does not say whether that rock square includes the floor the 1977 note describes, and no source we read says whether that floor survives.
