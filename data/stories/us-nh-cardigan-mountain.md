The New Hampshire Forestry Department was erecting new lookout towers in 1924, and one went up on Cardigan Mountain in Grafton County.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says state crews fabricated the 15-foot steel tower themselves rather than buying one, and that it first had a steel ladder, later replaced with stairs.[^1]

In 1954 the summit job was done by a solitary fire watcher. A newspaper reprint from that year says Wyman Larrabee, 66, had held it for the last 12 summers.[^2] In August 1954 he was reported missing, and his body was found 100 feet below his cabin near the peak.[^2] A medical referee said he died of natural causes and that there was no foul play.[^2] Forestry officials had also said he was struck on the head about a month earlier and did not report it, so the press accounts do not fully agree.[^2]

The register lists the tower as registered on March 12, 2002.[^1]

The 2023 Forest Fire Lookout Association report says wet weather delayed the painting and cab replacement at Cardigan and Belknap. It put the Cardigan cost at $436,000 before contingencies and expected both towers to be operating in 2024.[^3] A 2024 Concord Monitor photo caption, however, says the Cardigan cab was replaced in 2023.[^4] Read together, the cab appears to have gone up in 2023 and the tower returned to service in 2024, though no single source says so.[^3][^4]

In June 2024 crews were on the mountain installing fire-locating equipment in the cab.[^4] In August 2024 a forestry aid recorded 19 adults and four children as visitors before ending his shift.[^4] The newspaper says the state runs 15 towers that get weekend staffing from April through October whatever the fire danger, plus weekday staffing when danger is high. At Cardigan one forestry aid shares the part-time duties of staffing the tower.[^4] The 2025 chapter report says an association member partly staffed it that year.[^5]

The register says the trails to the mountain are very well marked, and that the tower is in active service.[^1] During the cab project the state warned that access to the summit and some trails would be limited periodically, and that there would be no access to the tower from July 17 through August 31. It asked hikers not to summit on days the helicopter was in use.[^6] Check the state's notices before going up, since closures are announced there.[^6] We found no rental arrangement for the cab in these sources.
