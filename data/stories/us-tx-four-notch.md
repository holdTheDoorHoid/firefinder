Four Notch was a lookout in Walker County, and the National Geodetic Survey still records the station as the Four Notch Lookout Tower.[^1] The Coast and Geodetic Survey first observed the station in 1952, and NGS codes the mark as a lookout tower.[^1]

For context, a Texas column on East Texas fire lookout towers says spotters first climbed trees, a method it calls seldom effective, and that the Forest Service then began building metal lookout towers at strategic locations in the forests.[^2] The column adds that the Forest Service next turned to airplanes, which could cover more ground and stay in touch with crews on the ground.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list marks the Four Notch tower as gone.[^3]

NGS's record for the station has no written description of the site.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, stands there today, or says whether the public can reach it.[^1][^3]
