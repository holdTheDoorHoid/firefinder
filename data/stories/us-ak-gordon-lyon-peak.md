The Bureau of Land Management's Alaska fire report, dated 1 October 1970, says a lookout at Gordon Lyon Peak was manned that year.[^1] The report names three other lookouts in the same Anchorage-Palmer area: Bald Mountain, Lazy Mountain and Jean Lake.[^1] The report also says the Bureau used lookout towers when they were available in 1970, and that erecting and manning towers in outlying parts of Alaska was almost insurmountably difficult.[^1]

The peak is also home to a Cold War missile site.[^2] Nike Site Summit was activated on Mount Gordon Lyon in May 1959, on the east side of what is now Joint Base Elmendorf-Richardson, beside Chugach State Park.[^2] The site stood guard over Anchorage and nearby bases until 1979, when it closed.[^2]

The National Park Service lists the missile site as not open to the public.[^2] A volunteer group, the Friends of Nike Site Summit, started leading guided summer tours in 2012.[^2] No source describes what remains of the 1970 lookout, or whether anything of it still stands. The only visits the sources describe are the group's guided summer tours.

[^2]: Nike Site Summit, Alaska, U.S. National Park Service, https://www.nps.gov/places/nike-site-summit.htm (accessed 2026-10-20).
