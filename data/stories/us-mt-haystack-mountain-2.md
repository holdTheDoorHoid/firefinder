Haystack Mountain, the highest point in the Boulder Mountains, sits in Jefferson County, Montana.[^1][^3] It carried a Forest Service fire lookout from 1936 until 1977.[^3] In August 1936 a Butte paper reported that a new station would go on the peak in the Deer Lodge National Forest, with windows on all sides. ERA crews were set to carve and blast a trail up the rock to pack in the equipment.[^2]

By November 1936 the station was complete. A report from that month, which calls the peak Lookout Mountain, says the 14-foot cabin was packed three miles up by pack horses.[^2] Rex's listing says a camp was set up on the mountain in 1911, a platform was built in 1920, and an L-4 cab was constructed in 1936.[^1] A photo page dated August 2015 gives the 1920 platform and the 1936 cab as well.[^4]

The station was occupied by July 1937, once its telephone line was connected.[^2] A report from the same month said lightning had struck the station without causing damage.[^2] A June 1944 report planned pack-mule supplies for the Haystack and Highland lookouts, with men moving in after July 4.[^2] In July 1954 the Butte district ranger's office installed a new lookout at the Haystack tower; the lookout was taken up by pack mule.[^2]

Rex's listing says the cab was destroyed in 1977.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page marks the site as removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's list records the site as a Ground type that is gone.[^2][^6]

Today, a Jefferson County write-up says only a piling or two, some weathered boards, a coil of cable and rebar are left.[^3] A 2015 photo page describes only a footing and some scattered wood.[^4]

The summit is on Beaverhead-Deerlodge National Forest land, and it is reached by a trail that was designated a National Recreation Trail in 1979.[^4][^5][^3] The trail is closed year-round to motorized travel, and the trail listing says it is easy to reach from Interstate 15.[^3][^5] The county write-up gives about three miles and just over 2,000 feet of climbing; the trail listing gives 3.6 miles.[^3][^5] Rex's page places the lookout 19 miles northeast of Butte, while the trail listing says 12 miles north of Butte.[^1][^5] We found no source that closes the trail to hikers.[^3][^5]
