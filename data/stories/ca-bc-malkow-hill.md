Malkow Hill is a summit in the Bulkley-Nechako area of British Columbia, listed at 808 m (2,651 ft).[^1] The walk starts about ten minutes northeast of Smithers.[^2] One hike report calls it easy: it runs through aspen woods, crosses meadows, and then climbs to the top.[^2]

In June 1939, The Province reported that ten men in the Prince Rupert district would build a lookout at Malkow, as part of that year's government summer forest programme.[^1] The item describes planned work, not a finished tower.

Trailpeak, a hiking guide, calls the summit an old forestry lookout site close to Smithers.[^3] The last climb reaches a communication tower and a picnic table on top.[^3] A hike report dated August 7, 2026 says buildings and trees on top cut down the views, though far-reaching scenes remain on the summit and along the trail.[^2] That report mentions buildings without saying what they are, and none of these sources says whether a lookout cab stands on the summit today.

Visitors walk in on foot. The trail crosses three gates on private property, and the guide asks walkers to use the foot gates and close the gates behind them.[^2][^3]
