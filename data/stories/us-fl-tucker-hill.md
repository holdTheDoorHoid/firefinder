In September 1936 the Tampa Times reported that John Wallace, project manager of the Withlacoochee Agricultural Demonstration project, had announced a fire tower on Tucker Hill, along the Brooksville-Croom road.[^1] The paper said the public could inspect the 100-foot steel tower around that Wednesday, and that it was the first of five towers in the project.[^1]

The federal government had bought the land that became Withlacoochee State Forest from private owners between 1936 and 1939.[^3] The Soil Conservation Service managed the lands from 1939 to 1954, and the Forest Service held the property until 1958, when a lease-purchase agreement moved it to the Florida Board of Forestry.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts entry quotes only the 1936 report and says nothing about whether any of the tower remains.[^1]

Today the hill is a day-use area inside the state forest, with trailheads, picnic areas, grills and restrooms.[^2] A listing for the area says the day-use fee applies to every day visitor, including people with a pavilion reservation, and that parking has room for both cars and horse trailers.[^4]
