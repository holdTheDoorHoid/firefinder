The steel tower that later stood on Long Mountain was first erected on Spades Mountain in 1941, on the Coeur d'Alene National Forest, as a 54-foot tower.[^1][^2] The Idaho Department of Lands received it and moved it to Long Mountain, south of Sandpoint, in 1963 or 1964. Sources give both years.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

A July 1959 newspaper item reported that a new forestry lookout station on Long Mountain was nearing completion.[^3] The sources do not agree on the tower's size once it reached the mountain. A 1964 listing gives 46 feet for an Aermotor MC-40 steel tower, while a 1998 sale notice describes 54 feet with a 12-by-12 wood cabin.[^3]

The Sandpoint News Bulletin reported in 1960 that the lookout would be manned from July 1 by Larry Eaton and his wife of Sandpoint.[^3] The latest staffing in these sources is 1967, when Joyce Sprinkle of Tucson began work as the fire lookout in the observation tower that the State Department of Forestry ran on Long Mountain.[^3][^4]

In 1998 the Idaho Department of Lands offered the tower for sale, and the buyer had to remove it from the site by September 30, 1998.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts says Dave Kresek bought it that year and moved it to his private property.[^4] Firelookout.com places the new site east of Spokane.[^5]

The tower now stands on 30 acres of private land east of Spokane, Washington.[^1][^5] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 50-foot Aermotor with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and it was registered there in 1999.[^1] Visits are arranged in advance through the Fire Lookout Museum.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Long Mountain site as 'Tower Gone.' No source describes what, if anything, remains there now or whether the site is open to the public.[^6]
