The lookout on Mount Saint Helena was a tri-county project. In May 1934 a Sonoma County paper reported that Sonoma, Lake and Napa counties would share the building and upkeep of a station on the summit.[^1] A newspaper predicted a panorama over much of the land from the Sierra to the Pacific, reaching from San Francisco Bay to the Siskiyous.[^1]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the lookout was constructed and dedicated in 1935, on a 30-foot enclosed tower of a design similar to a Forest Service cabin plan.[^2] Newspapers reported a dedication crowd of 250 to 300 people in July 1935.[^1] That July, Hjalmer Lundell, a former radio operator from St. Helena, was named fire lookout.[^1] A September 1935 report names a Henry Lundell as the state lookout on the summit. The sources do not say whether these are the same man.[^1]

The register says the 1935 lookout was replaced in 1950 by a tower that conformed closely to the 809R plan.[^2] The station also took on other work. In 1942 it was pressed into service on the Sonoma County police radio network as a repeater on the summit.[^1] In 1946 a wind of 85 miles an hour knocked down an aerial and damaged the weather station.[^1]

A 1992 decision followed a statewide review of lookouts and withdrew funding from the Mount St. Helena station.[^1] The register says staffing ended that year and the lookout was taken down some time afterward. It gives no date for the removal.[^2]

A hiking guide says the summit now has several communications towers.[^4] Visitors can reach it on foot from Robert Louis Stevenson State Park.[^3] The Mount St. Helena Trail from the park is a fire road of 5.3 miles that climbs 2,100 feet, according to one guide, while Wikipedia gives the trails as about 6 miles long.[^4][^3] A 1991 note in the register says the summit may be reached only on foot.[^2] We found no source that says whether any part of the lookout remains on the summit, or what the site looks like today.
