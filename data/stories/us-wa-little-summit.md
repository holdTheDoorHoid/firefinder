Little Summit sits near the top of Mount Constitution, inside Moran State Park on Orcas Island.[^4] The Washington Department of Natural Resources put a lookout tower there in 1966.[^2][^3] Officials built it to take pressure off the Mount Constitution lookout, which visitors had overwhelmed.[^1][^4] It was a wooden tower about 50 feet tall, with a cab on top.[^1][^4]

The first observer was Dorothy Allan, in 1966.[^4] The photo captions on the same page spell her name Allen.[^4] Use of the tower stayed low after it went up.[^1]

A private party took the tower down in 1988.[^1][^4] Sources differ in wording: one says the tower was removed, and another says it was sold and moved.[^1][^3]

The tower itself is gone.[^1] WillhiteWeb, an undated page, reports that footings, steps and other foundation work remain on top, inside the picnic area.[^4] From the top, the view to the south takes in the San Juan Islands and, beyond them, the Olympics.[^4]

To reach the site, look for the Little Summit sign on Mount Constitution Road, just after the first views. Parking is nearby, and the walk is about a tenth of a mile.[^4] One guide suggests it may be the park's finest picnic site.[^4]
