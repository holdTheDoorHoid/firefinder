The Rinker Point lookout went up in 1935 as a 30-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, set along the ridge about a mile southeast of the summit it was named for.[^1][^4] Rex's Fire Lookout Page records panorama photos taken on July 21, 1935, by George Clisby.[^3]

During World War II the lookout became an Aircraft Warning Service station.[^4][^3] Rex's page dates that service from March 21, 1942, to October 16, 1943.[^3] Darrington's history says men on these lookouts stayed there year round, and that a shed for sleeping quarters went up in 1943.[^5] WillhiteWeb gives $315 for the sleeping quarters added then.[^4]

The FFLOS register says the tower was removed in 1965.[^1] Firelookout.com and the Darrington history say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^5]

The site sits on private timber land owned by Sierra Pacific Land & Timber.[^4] Logging in the 1980s made the old road hard to follow and changed the site, and on a return visit WillhiteWeb found no evidence of the lookout.[^5][^4] Some people still hike to the old site, following the old road northwest of Texas Pond.[^5] None of the sources consulted describes any remains of the tower.

WillhiteWeb's directions start 5.6 miles north of the Darrington Ranger Station and lead along an unsigned dirt road between private property to a gate at the end of a branch road.[^4] From the gate you park and walk about a mile, gaining roughly 650 feet.[^4] WillhiteWeb's 2019 notes say no signs suggest hiking is off limits, but the land is still private, so check with the owner before you go.[^4]
