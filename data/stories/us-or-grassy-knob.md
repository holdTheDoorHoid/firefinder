Grassy Knob is about six miles east of Port Orford in Curry County, and the Coos Forest Protective Association administered the lookout.[^2][^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 10-by-10-foot cab built in 1934.[^1] Oregon Hikers gives the same 1934 date.[^4] The Oregon Lookouts page instead says a lookout house went up on a 12-foot tower in 1935, so the sources do not agree on the first year.[^3]

The Oregon Lookouts page reproduces records showing the station was staffed for 79 days in 1941, with reports passed to the Gold Beach station by telephone.[^3] It was activated on March 21, 1942, under the Roseburg Filter Center.[^3]

A newspaper account reproduced on the Oregon Lookouts page describes an incident on September 28.[^3] The lookout, Lauren S. Giebner, heard an aircraft engine and saw a flash through the fog, and about two hours later spotted a fire that was brought under control with little trouble.[^3] Oregon Hikers dates to 1942 a sighting, made from the site, of a Japanese aircraft carrying incendiary bombs, while the Oregon Lookouts page dates its newspaper item June 2, 1943, so the year of the incident is not settled.[^4][^3]

The register says the cab was removed in the 1960s, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed then.[^1][^2] Oregon Hikers says it was decommissioned in the 1960s as well.[^4]

Oregon Hikers says only four guy-cable anchors are left of the cabin.[^4] A narrow stone staircase climbs to the former lookout location, and getting there means a short walk up an old lookout road into a Coast Range wilderness.[^4] Shrubs now restrict the view.[^4] None of the four sources mentions a closure, but this check did not consult official access notices, so current access is unconfirmed.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
