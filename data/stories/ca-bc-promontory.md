Promontory Lookout stood on a mountain northwest of Lower Nicola, in the Nicola Valley.[^1][^2] A Merritt Herald item from May 1951 says the lookout was established in 1948, on a mountain it puts at 1,737 m (5,700 ft).[^1]

By 1951 the lookout had a new tower and a new building. The May 1951 item says both were constructed late the previous summer, and that the building was plywood, set on a concrete foundation and cabled to the bedrock.[^1] A July 1951 item likened the building to a Dutch windmill and said it made a comfortable summer home.[^1]

In May 1967 the lookout had a round-the-clock fire watcher. A Merritt Herald notice asked people who spotted fire in the evenings or at weekends to call the watcher by collect call, and said the lookout had a radio-telephone.[^1]

The lookout was dismantled in 2007.[^1] Joy of Exploring says constant vandalism was the reason.[^3] Experience Merritt says the station has long been removed, but the spot is kept for day use.[^2]

Today the site is a recreation site with views over Merritt and Lower Nicola.[^2][^3] Experience Merritt says it is not suited to overnight stays because access is rough and facilities are few.[^2] The access is a forest service road about 13 km long from Highway 8.[^3] Joy of Exploring calls it very rough, narrow and winding, and says it is seasonally deactivated in winter because of snow.[^3] Radio towers stand just past the recreation site, and there is active logging around the 6 or 7 km mark, so drivers should watch for trucks.[^3]

The sources do not agree on height. The 1951 item gives 1,737 m (5,700 ft) for the mountain, while Joy of Exploring gives 1,715 m (5,625 ft) for the site.[^1][^3]
