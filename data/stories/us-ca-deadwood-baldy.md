Deadwood Baldy rises to 5,720 feet in the Klamath National Forest, in Siskiyou County [^1]. According to the register, the site was a secondary lookout from 1924 to 1933, equipped with a canvas fly, an alidade and a telephone and nothing more [^1]. A 1917 item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page mentions trail work leading to the Deadwood lookout [^2].

The lookout house came later [^1]. The register dates it to 1934 and describes a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin on an enclosed timber tower [^1]. The Forest Lookouts page quotes a November 1933 newspaper note saying a new lookout house was then being built with Civilian Conservation Corps labor [^2]. The register gives 1934, but the November 1933 note already describes the house as being built.

In June 1933 a diary entry quoted on the Forest Lookouts page records a trip to Deadwood with a tent and supplies to install someone named Hayes on the lookout. The entry notes that the lookout was then housed in a tent [^2].

The lookout was removed in 1974 [^1][^2]. The register says only the foundation remains [^1].

The register includes a 2008 photo credited to Ron Kemnow that shows the foundation [^1]. It does not say whether the site is open to visitors [^1]. A peak-listing website says a trail leads to the summit [^3], but no Forest Service source here confirms the current route or access rules.
