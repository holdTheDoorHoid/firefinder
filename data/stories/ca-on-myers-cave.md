Myer's Cave Fire Tower was located on Tower Road, just east of Myer's Cave, off North Frontenac Road 506 near Cloyne, Ontario.[^3] Firelookout.ca describes the structure as light steel, with guy wires and an octagonal cupola.[^1]

The Cloyne and District Historical Society says Ontario's fire towers grew out of two major fires, one of them the Great Fire of 1903 in parts of Lennox and Addington.[^2] Its general account says the first towers were wooden, about 11 m (35 ft) high, and built after the First World War, and that steel towers about 24 m (80 ft) tall began to be built in the 1920s.[^2] Those are the newsletter's figures for the period as a whole, not details of this tower.

Four men staffed the tower over the years: Ezra Wheeler, Harvey Spencer, Jim Perry and Oscar Lemke.[^3][^2] Perry is probably the best known. He would invite guests up to take in the view, and he explained how the system worked.[^3] The newsletter puts Oscar Lemke's time in the tower between 1958 and 1972.[^2]

John Bolton, who wrote about the area's towers for the society's 2007 newsletter, reports that a telephone company contact said the phone batteries at this tower had to be changed regularly. He adds that in slow spells Perry would phone friends and visit with them, and the batteries drained quickly as a result.[^3]

The society's museum now holds a topographic map board that was used in the tower to pinpoint fires. Janet and Bill Lemke donated it.[^2]

No source I read says whether the tower still stands. Bolton describes its location with the words "is(was) located," and no source describes the site on Tower Road today, including whether it is open to the public.[^3]
