The cab that was moved to the Missoula Smokejumper Visitor Center came from a lookout on Black Pine Mountain, about nine miles northwest of Philipsburg in Granite County, Montana.[^2] In 1938 Civilian Conservation Corps workers were building the road to the site, and by October 1939 a newspaper said six miles of it had been constructed.[^1] The 50-foot round timber tower with an L-4 cab was finished by the end of 1938 or early 1939.[^1]

An October 1939 newspaper item reported that the tower had been erected. A 14-foot-square house crowned it and served as the lookout man's living quarters.[^1] The sources disagree on the start date. Firelookout.com says the tower was constructed in 1933, which does not match the 1938 and 1939 records.[^2][^1]

The lookout man kept watch each summer.[^1] A 1977 newspaper report said the lookout had served as a fire-spotting post for more than 30 years.[^1] The sources we read do not name the lookout men or any specific fire.

In October 1977 a newspaper reported that the lookout was being taken apart and would be moved to Missoula for display. Its top cabin was to be shown inside the Forest Service visitor center west of Missoula.[^1] Firelookout.com says the cab reached the visitor center in 1978.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the original Blackpine Mountain site as gone.[^4]

The visitor center sits northwest of the Missoula airport, at 5765 West Broadway Street.[^3] The Forest Service page says the center is currently closed for renovations, and that people can ask by email or phone about pre-scheduled tours.[^3] Both the 1977 report and firelookout.com describe the cab going there as a display, not a tower. We did not confirm that it is still on show, so check the current status before you go.[^1][^2]
