Grassy Point, also called Grassy Mountain, is a summit on the Continental Divide south of Anaconda, Montana, at about 7,990 feet.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com puts it about eight miles south-southwest of town, while a 1956 survey description places it south-southeast, so the direction is in dispute.[^1][^2]

Firelookout.com describes a wooden L-4 lookout built in the 1930s.[^1] A 1956 survey description calls the structure wooden and about 25 feet high.[^2] In July 1940 a Sanders County newspaper reported that a new tent lookout would be set up on the mountain that summer.[^2] A later blog memoir says the tower was constructed in 1940.[^3] The sources do not agree on when the tower went up.[^1][^3]

The earliest staffing in these sources dates to 1940, when the station was run as an emergency post and its guards were housed in a tent camp.[^2] In July 1947 a newspaper reported that a family of three from Anaconda would take over the post for the summer.[^2] Firelookout.com says the lookout was last used for emergencies in 1970.[^1]

According to firelookout.com, the cab had collapsed before 2009.[^1] A memoir author who saw the structure from Mill Creek Road describes it collapsing from exposure to the weather, with pieces strewn about the ground nearby.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association list describes a standing tower with ruins.[^4]

No source I read describes what remains at the summit beyond that, or says whether the site is open to visitors. A road guide says the summit road starts at Dry Creek Road, is unpaved, needs a 4x4, and is not plowed in winter.[^5] Do not climb the collapsed structure, and check with the land manager before driving up.
