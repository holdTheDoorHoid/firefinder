Picuris Peak, in the Carson National Forest, holds a ground cabin lookout built in the late 1920s or early 1930s.[^1][^2] Sources give slightly different years. A 2020 chapter report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association says the L-4 type house went up sometime between 1928 and 1932.[^2] The Taos News gives 1932,[^3] and West Lookouts puts it at about 1930, plus or minus a couple of years.[^4]

Descriptions of the size also differ. West Lookouts describes a 12-by-12-foot hip-roof cabin built in a style similar to the L-4,[^4] while the Taos News describes L-4 cabins as 14-by-14-foot frame cabins designed in 1929.[^3]

The Register says the cabin was staffed regularly through 1990 and has seen only occasional use since, when fire danger is extreme.[^1] Eli Romero, a former lookout, told the Taos News that he staffed the tower for about three decades starting in 1958, and was on the peak most days from April through November. Over time, he said, the cabin was fitted with a propane tank and a kitchen.[^3]

In 1988 a thematic National Register review of Southwest Region lookouts recommended that Picuris be found ineligible for the National Register, because it had not kept its original design, construction, workmanship and materials intact.[^2] The author of the 2020 report disagrees and thinks the cabin should be looked at again.[^2]

The Register accepted the lookout on March 20, 2016, as US 1121, NM 38, with Brian Powell as nominator.[^1] It lists the site at 10,752 feet, while the Taos News gives 10,801 feet.[^1][^3]

The Taos News reports the cabin has been unstaffed for most fire seasons since 1990, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list shows it standing.[^3][^5] I found nothing that says whether the public may go up to the cabin, so check with the Carson National Forest before planning any trip to the peak.
