Coal Knob sits in Warren County, Pennsylvania, inside the Allegheny National Forest, near Kinzua.[^1][^2] In June 1924 The Daily Notes reported that a federal appropriation made a tower at Kinzua possible, and by late October that year the tower was finished.[^2]

A Kane Republican item from October 1924, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, called it only 30 feet tall. It said the site's altitude gave the tower a view as broad as that of towers twice its height.[^2] Its top held a plate-glass observatory about 14 feet square, plus quarters for the observer.[^2] A two-mile telephone line ran from it, and a road had been built so that the tower could be reached by automobile.[^2] The article said it was the sole federal lookout in the forest.[^2]

The Warren Tribune reported in August 1924 that 4,000 pounds of steel had arrived for the tower.[^2] Items dated May and August 1927 on the same page do not fit neatly with a finished 1924 tower. One describes officials looking over possible sites on Coal Knob, and the other repeats the steel wording.[^2] The page may have date errors, or the 1927 items may describe a later project. We could not settle which.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register reports just two foundation blocks left from the tower.[^1] It names the U.S. Forest Service as the administering agency and dates its entry to May 6, 2009.[^1]

A trail-guide listing puts Coal Knob just south of the Allegheny Reservoir and describes the area as having multi-use motorized roads, plus hiking paths and designated campsites.[^3] We did not find a source that says what is visible at the site today or whether the foundations are open to visitors.
