The Chickasaw Lookout Tower stands in Chester County, Tennessee, inside Chickasaw State Forest.[^1][^2] It is a 60-foot Aermotor MC-39, a design nearly identical to a 1933 model that International Derrick developed with Aermotor and the U.S. Forest Service.[^1][^3]

The register gives no build year for this tower.[^1] The MC-39 was used by the state forestry division in the 1930s and again after World War II, so the sources leave its date open.[^3]

The MC-39's small landings were judged dangerous, and the design became less popular around the early 1940s.[^3] The LS-40 then became the state division's most popular tower type, and its wood plank platforms were considered safer than the MC-39's landings.[^3] We found no record of who staffed this tower or of any fire it watched.

The register added the tower on August 7, 2023, on the nomination of Jason Johns.[^1] Its visit notes, dated August 5, 2023, report that the first flight of stairs had been removed after an earlier visit on August 28, 2021.[^1] A 2014 list of surviving state towers includes it, and Tennessee Landforms lists it as standing.[^3][^4]

The register gives the elevation as 639 feet (195 meters), while Tennessee Landforms gives 643 feet. We report both rather than pick one.[^1][^4]

Fire towers on state forests are closed to public visits. Photography is allowed, but climbing is not, so please admire this tower from the ground.[^2]
