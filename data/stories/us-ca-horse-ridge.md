Horse Ridge Lookout sits on South Fork Mountain at about 6,070 feet, on the line between the Six Rivers and Shasta-Trinity national forests in Trinity County.[^1][^2] The Register says the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1]

The site has held more than one tower. The Register's visit notes say the earlier structure on Horse Ridge was built in 1933 and destroyed in March 1986.[^1] Peakbagging also has a record of a lookout built here in 1933.[^3] The metal tower standing now was built on Eight Mile in the Mad River District in 1960, and a National Guard helicopter moved it to Horse Ridge in June 1986.[^1] Peakbagging takes a 1986 marking on the tower's base to mean it was likely rebuilt that year, so the sources differ on whether it was moved or rebuilt.[^3]

The Register lists a CL 160 cab, with a catwalk, on a 19-foot tower.[^1] A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1961, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, gives a 14-by-14-foot cabin on eight wooden legs, 50 feet high.[^2] That predates the 1986 move, so it most likely describes the earlier structure rather than the tower standing today.[^2]

The lookout had staff in the 1940s and 1950s. A 1943 item says a family was on leave from the Horse Ridge lookout.[^2] In 1955 George Russell, a veteran lookout man, was watching a doe below his tower when she gave birth to twin fawns.[^2] The Register's visit note from November 2013 says full-time Forest Service staff man it during fire season, usually from mid June to the first week of November.[^1] Peakbagging calls it an active fire lookout that is staffed during the fire season.[^3] The Register lists it as registered on January 2, 2006.[^1]

A hiker's account from June 2023 describes going up Horse Ridge Road (3S12) to a camp area and then walking a little over four miles, partly along a Jeep trail.[^4] The lookout was manned that day, but the gate in the fence and the gate in the observation deck were locked, so the hiker stayed on the ground.[^4] None of the sources read describes a formal closure, and the Register says the tower is in active service.[^1] Because it is still a working lookout, do not climb the tower.
