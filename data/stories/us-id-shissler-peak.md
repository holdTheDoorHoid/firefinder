Shissler Peak overlooks the point where Moose Creek meets the Selway River, within the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness on the Nez Perce National Forest.[^1] The Forest Service built the first lookout there in 1933, a pole tower carrying an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] Twenty years later, in 1953, that structure was replaced with the lookout still in use today: an L-4 cab of the 1936 design, fitted with a catwalk and set on a shorter, 10-foot treated timber tower.[^1][^2]

Because it sits deep in wilderness, Shissler Peak has always been a hike-in post rather than a drive-up one.[^2] It has been kept staffed through fire seasons for decades.[^1] The known staffing record includes Gary Hannon in 1956 and Penny Kummrow in 1967, though Idaho Fire Lookouts notes the full staffing history is incomplete and welcomes more names.[^2][^3]

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2009.[^4] It remains in Forest Service hands and continues to be staffed in the fire season rather than offered as a public rental, so a visit means reaching it on foot through wilderness terrain and respecting that it may be occupied by an active lookout.
