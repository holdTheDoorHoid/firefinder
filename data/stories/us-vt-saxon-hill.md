Saxon Hill, in Chittenden County, is the site of a former fire lookout.[^1][^2] In the 1920s the village of Essex Junction erected a cabin on the hill to watch over its village forest.[^1] The forest had a practical purpose: a water shortage in 1923 was the main reason the village set the land aside for water conservation.[^3]

A fire lookout tower was on the hill by 1932.[^1] A Vermont Forest Service report says the village built a new tower in 1949 or 1950.[^1] A later report in the same series mentions painting the inside of the tower cab in 1953 or 1954.[^1] A 1967 to 1968 biennial report says the state Department of Forests and Parks had manned the station.[^1] In 1968 a couple from Morrisville, Mr. and Mrs. Rod Williams, were manning the tower.[^1]

Lightning hit the lookout cabin in June 1967 and burned it.[^1] While a replacement cabin was built, a house trailer moved to the hill's base served as temporary accommodation.[^1]

In May 1973 the village trustees agreed to demolish the tower, which the state forestry department had stopped using.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association now lists it as gone.[^2]

A December 2017 review says the forest had shrunk by 88 percent from an original 800 acres.[^3] The Town of Essex acquired about 250 acres of the original forest in 2017.[^3] The town aims for public recreation there, but had not yet written a management plan.[^3] Trails on 90 acres held by the Essex-Westford School District total 5.7 miles for hiking and mountain biking.[^3]

No source describes what remains at the tower site today, or whether that spot is open to visitors.[^1]
