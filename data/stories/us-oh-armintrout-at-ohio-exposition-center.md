The Armintrout tower was built by Civilian Conservation Corps crews in 1934, on land in Pike County that the state bought for $1 from the Armintrout family.[^2] A 1934 newspaper item spells the family name Armentrout.[^3] On October 3, 1934, the state announced that it had received a steel fire tower to be put up on Armentrout land, meant to boost forest protection in southeastern Pike and northeastern Scioto counties.[^3] An October 27, 1934 item said H. G. Armintrout of the Shyville community had taken over the just-completed tower.[^3]

The tower stood about 80 feet tall at its original site, though a 2016 article gives 71 feet.[^2][^4] The sources I read name no particular fire it watched.

Ohio's lookout network was phased out after aircraft detection began in the 1940s, and the last tower closed in 1978.[^2] That same year, the Pike County Public Service Trust took ownership of the tower for civil defense work.[^2]

The Ohio Department of Natural Resources later took the tower back so it could be moved.[^2] In 2016 crews dismantled it, shortened it to 60 feet, and set it up again at the ODNR Natural Resources Park on the Ohio State Fairgrounds in Columbus.[^2][^1][^4] Crews sandblasted the metal legs, dipped them in acid and re-galvanized them, and replaced the wooden landings and stairs with wood from state forests.[^2][^4] The cab got a full refinish and new glass.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the tower on September 3, 2014, as US 1021, OH 10, and lists the Ohio Division of Forestry as its administrator.[^1] It sits at the fairgrounds, behind the Smokey Bear statue.[^5][^2]

A 2016 Ohio's Country Journal article says the tower is not open for unassisted visits, and CompassOhio's climbing guide does not list it among towers open to climb.[^4][^5] The sources I could read do not confirm current visiting rules, so check with ODNR before you plan a visit.
