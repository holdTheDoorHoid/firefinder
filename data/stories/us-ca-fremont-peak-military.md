Fremont Peak rises in the Gabilan Range. The state park that covers its summit lies in Monterey and San Benito counties.[^3]

In November 1930 San Benito County supervisors discussed a forest ranger lookout station on the peak, with a telephone line to the courthouse.[^1] Early plans called for the state to provide the phone line and the county to help build the lookout within two years.[^1]

In April 1931 a newspaper reported that a lookout house would soon be erected on the peak, working with the state parks division.[^1] It was to be built of native rock.[^1] Days later, work began on a telephone line from San Juan to the peak for a fire lookout station.[^1] Reports from 1930 and 1931 name the ranger differently: Hiram Wyman in 1930, and H.E. ("Hi") Wyman in 1931. We could not settle which is right.[^1]

In February 1934 work got under way on the peak, ahead of the state's plan to make it a park.[^1] The planned improvements included fire trails, roads and a fire outlook building, and a crew of 30 or more CWA men was expected.[^1] The Oakland Tribune reported that the major portion of the property went to San Benito County, which deeded it to the State Park Commission.[^1] Wikipedia credits the land donation to Rollin Reeves, owner of Rancho Cienega del Gabilán, and says the park was established in 1934.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Fremont Peak tower as gone.[^2] It calls the structure a tower, while the 1931 plan described a rock lookout house and the 1934 plan mentioned a fire outlook building.[^1][^2]

Visitors can come for the day or camp overnight.[^5] The road up is winding and two lanes wide.[^5] The park's observatory opened in 1986, has a 30-inch telescope, and holds public programs on select evenings.[^3][^4] On the summit, BenitoLink describes a flagpole, a historical plaque, microwave repeater stations and cell towers, with a paved access road for the cell towers.[^6] No source we read says whether any part of the 1930s lookout survives on the summit.
