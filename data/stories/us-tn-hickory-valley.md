The Hickory Valley Lookout Tower stands in Hardeman County, Tennessee, on land the state still owns.[^1] The Register added it in August 2023.[^1] We did not find when it was built. The Tennessee Division of Forestry began building its fire tower network in 1933, and the Civilian Conservation Corps supplied labor for the work.[^3] The Aermotor LS-40 later became the forestry division's most popular tower design, and it was widely used until the mid-1970s.[^3]

The Register describes the tower as an 80-foot Aermotor LS-40 and notes that the maker's badge is no longer attached.[^1] It names the state forestry division as administrator.[^1] The Register gives the site's elevation as 640 feet, while the state's list gives 653 feet, so the two sources disagree.[^1][^2]

A 2014 table in the National Register document for the state's towers lists Hickory Valley among the extant towers.[^3] The Register's entry records a site visit on August 5, 2023, and says the site contains the tower.[^1]

We found no account of who staffed the tower or of any fire it watched. The Register does not describe public access.[^1] Because the land belongs to the state, anyone interested should ask the Tennessee Division of Forestry before visiting.
