A tower went up on Mount Carmel around 1914.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts site, quoting a Vermont forestry history, credits a timberland owner, Newton Chaffee, with building it.[^1] The state's forest page gives the same 1914 date.[^3]

A 1915 state forestry report says Chaffee made the tower's reconstruction possible, and notes that the summit still had no telephone line.[^1]

In August 1974, a Rutland Daily Herald hike notice still pointed readers to the Mt. Carmel Fire Tower for its views.[^1]

The state's forest page says the tower was removed in 1974 because it had become unsafe.[^3] Forest Lookouts describes it as toppled over, so the two accounts differ on how it ended.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as gone.[^2]

Mount Carmel is now part of Mount Carmel State Forest, a 263-acre tract given to Vermont in 1956 by Governor Redfield Proctor.[^3] The forest has no deeded access. Visitors reach it on foot by the New Boston Trail, a side trail of the Long Trail, and the Green Mountain Club maintains the trails.[^3] No source read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower on the summit.[^1][^2][^3]
