A tower already stood at the head of Lens Creek on Bee Mountain in Kanawha County by October 1926, when a Charleston Gazette item noted that it was linked by telephone to East Bank.[^1]

In July 1936 the Charleston Gazette reported crowds at a new picnic ground built around an 80-foot steel fire tower on Bee Mountain, one of the state tower picnic sites built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] The site had a fireplace, benches and a roadway to the top, which was finished early that year.[^1] In November 1937 a Bluefield paper reported that the CCC had completed a log cabin for the observer on duty, replacing a corrugated iron cabin.[^1]

The West Virginia Division of Forestry relied on temporary seasonal employees to staff its towers, and newspapers reported observers assigned to Bee Mountain in 1947, 1948 and 1962.[^2][^1]

A Gazette-Mail photo caption dated April 19, 1976 says the observer on Bee Mountain spends 10 hours a day, seven days a week, in a 70-foot tower during fire season.[^3]

The sources disagree on the height: the 1936 article says 80 feet, and the 1976 caption says 70.[^1][^3]

Jeremy Jones, a deputy state fire manager, said the state stopped using its towers because aircraft were more efficient for detecting fires.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the tower as administered by the West Virginia Division of Forestry and says it has been removed. It was added to the register in September 2008.[^4]

No source I read describes what remains on Bee Mountain today, or whether the public can reach the summit.
