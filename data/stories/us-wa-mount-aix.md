Mount Aix stands in the William O. Douglas Wilderness, on the Wenatchee National Forest in Yakima County, Washington.[^1][^3] It lies about 24 miles east of Mount Rainier.[^2]

A D-6 cupola cabin went up on the summit in 1923. That same year, the lookout reported a fire 36 miles away.[^1][^3][^4]

Ernest Beeks was the lookout in 1924.[^3][^4] He suggested improvements to his supervisor, including porch space with concrete floors.[^3][^4] In 1929 Lookout Edwards proposed painting the tops of lookout houses white, with the peak's name on them, as navigation aids for mail planes and other aircraft.[^3][^4] A 1931 report said lightning struck the cabin twelve times in one night.[^3][^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association register says a fire destroyed the cabin in 1961.[^1] Firelookout.com gives 1923 to 1961 as the cabin's dates, without naming a cause.[^2] Willhite reports a USGS benchmark placed next to the west wall that year, and says the lookout was reported destroyed in 1961.[^3]

The sources describe the cabin as gone since 1961, and none describes what, if anything, is left on the summit now.[^1][^2][^3]

The peak is reached on foot. Willhite describes a 6-mile route with a 4,200-foot gain from the signed Mt. Aix trailhead, reached along Bumping Lake Road.[^3] He says the last few miles of road are rocky, but regular cars can make it.[^3] Willhite's page mentions no closure or permit for the trail.[^3]
