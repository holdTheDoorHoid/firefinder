Omak Mountain rises on the Colville Indian Reservation in Okanogan County, about ten miles east-northeast of Omak.[^1][^3] The register lists the land as administered by the reservation, so visitors need to follow tribal rules.[^1][^5]

The first lookout was an L-4 cab, which the Civilian Conservation Corps built in 1935.[^1][^2][^3] A 1960 inspection report describes a wooden cabin on a four-foot concrete foundation wall, credits the CCC with building it in 1935, and calls it in poor condition.[^4] The same report proposed a new cabin fifteen feet higher, and noted that a television reflector station and a Border Patrol building on the site blocked the view in one direction.[^4]

Firelookout.com says the Washington State Division of Forestry first staffed the lookout, and it names two early lookouts: Dorothy Bissell in 1952 and Eleanor Brown in 1953.[^3] In July 1943 a Colville newspaper reported that teachers were working as fire lookouts that summer across the Colville National Forest, and it named one of them on Omak Mountain, Rachel Butte of Chewelah Junior High.[^4]

In 1968 a 20-foot timber tower with an R-6 flat cab took the place of the original cab.[^1][^2][^3] The present 53-foot all-steel live-in tower went up in 1986.[^1][^2][^3] The 1935 and 1986 dates do not conflict. They describe the first cab and the tower standing today.[^1][^2]

The register says the tower is still staffed in summer.[^1] TrailChick, last updated in March 2024, says it is staffed whenever fire danger is high.[^2] Firelookout.com says it is staffed every summer.[^3] Summer is the season, but how often someone is up there is unclear, since TrailChick ties staffing to high fire danger.

Access is by permit. The Colville Tribes' Parks and Recreation sheet says visitors need a Reservation Use Pass. It lists an annual pass at $80 or a $20 daily pass, good for two vehicles.[^5] That sheet is undated, so check current prices before going. TrailChick reports the same $80 annual fee for non-tribal members.[^2]

TrailChick says the road from Omak might be among the rockiest and roughest of any lookout road in the state. A high-clearance all-wheel-drive vehicle can reach the summit, but the road turns very rocky roughly 2.5 miles short of the summit.[^2]

The register listed the lookout on April 13, 2022.[^1] Today it is a staffed, live-in lookout on tribal land, so treat it as a working site and do not climb it.
