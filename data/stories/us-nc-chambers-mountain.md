The North Carolina Forest Service built the Chambers Mountain Lookout in 1936, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1] The register describes a 45-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, in Haywood County.[^1][^3] It gives the site an elevation of 2,466 feet (752 m).[^1]

The tower went up during a busy stretch for North Carolina's fire towers. A description of the Forest Service's photograph collection says the Civilian Conservation Corps assisted in building 52 fire towers from 1933 to 1938, and that two-thirds of the state's forests had organized fire protection by 1936.[^2] The register credits the Forest Service with building this tower and does not mention the Corps, so this account does not assume the Corps was involved.[^1]

An undated register description places it among the few still-active lookouts in western North Carolina.[^1] I found no staffing history, notable fire or named observer for the tower in the sources I could read.

The site was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on April 2, 2008.[^1] The register lists the NC Division of Forest Resources as administrator.[^1]

Today the sources I could read do not say whether the tower is still staffed, whether it can be climbed, or whether visitors are welcome. Check with the Division of Forest Resources before planning a visit, and do not climb a tower that is closed or private.
