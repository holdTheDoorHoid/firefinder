Green Butte rises in the Deschutes National Forest, about 13 miles southeast of La Pine in Lake County.[^2][^1] The sources describe a wooden platform of five or six feet, not a cab.[^1][^2][^3]

The sources do not agree on when it went up.[^1][^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists it as built circa 1998 and removed circa 2000, and describes a five-foot platform.[^1] Firelookout.com dates a six-foot wooden platform to 1990 and says it was used while the nearby Spring Butte lookout was being reconstructed.[^2] The Roaming Civic trip report gives the same 1990 date and purpose, and also gives six feet.[^3] The register's own wording reads as if the platform came after the new Spring Butte tower was ready, which does not fit the other two accounts.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says a new Spring Butte tower replaced an active L-4 pole tower dating from 1932, and that the new tower was erected in 1991; its build field gives 1992.[^4]

In August 2022 lightning started a fire on the slope of Green Butte, and it burned about 23 acres around the top.[^3] The trip writer reported a geocacher's note, found by Garnet, that the structure remained intact after the fire.[^3]

The register says the platform was removed around 2000.[^1] The trip writer disputes that, based on a site visit.[^3]

On May 15, 2023, the writer found the platform still standing among brush but weakened. Garnet, who had also visited the year before, said part of it had fallen.[^3] The summit was overgrown, with no view.[^3] The firelookout.com entry, which has no date, says the platform is still there, but no source is dated after the May 2023 visit, so its condition now is unknown.[^2][^1][^3]

To reach the site, the trip report describes forest roads and a road walk from a signed junction, then a small user trail from the end of the road to the platform.[^3] Fire crews had blocked one spur with downed trees, but the report describes no closure of the site.[^3] The writer warned that heavy snow will probably bring the platform down in one of the coming winters.[^3]
