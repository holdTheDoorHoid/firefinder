Sixteen Butte lies in the Deschutes National Forest, in Lake County, Oregon. The site was a ground-level lookout.[^1][^6]

The register says an L-4 cab went up there in 1931.[^1] A Bend newspaper item from early 1932, quoted by a lookout history site, names Sixteen Butte among the lookouts put up during the 1931 season.[^3] In April 1931 the same paper said the Fox Butte fireman would be moved to Sixteen Butte.[^3] A May 1931 item reported that the road climbing the butte had been finished.[^3]

The cab was moved to Fox Butte in 1948.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register's Fox Butte entry says the cabin standing there, a gable-roofed L-4 on the ground, came from Sixteen Butte that year.[^4]

The Sixteen Butte site itself is listed as ground gone. No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit.[^6]

The cab was still at Fox Butte on a 2021 visit, and by then it had fallen into disrepair.[^5] The road to Fox Butte was locked about three-quarters of the way up, so the visitor walked the last 1.1 miles.[^5] The visitor went up the tower only to its first landing and was not sure how safe the rest was.[^5] That same account says the Forest Service had proposed removing both the tower and the cabin.[^5] We did not check that proposal's later status. No source says the Fox Butte tower or cabin is open to visitors.
