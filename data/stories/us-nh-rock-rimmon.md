The New Hampshire Forestry Department erected a steel tower and a cabin for a watchman on the summit of Rock Rimmon in Kingston in January 1929.[^1] The tower was meant to give fire watch over several towns in south central Rockingham County.[^1] In March 1938 the road to the tower was being repaired as a WPA project.[^1] Dynamite was being used to remove ledge and rock, and the roadbed was being smoothed.[^1]

In April 1939 the Portsmouth Herald reported that Arthur P. Tucker, superintendent of Great Pond State Park, was the lookout at the Rock Rimmon tower.[^1]

firelookout.org lists the tower as gone.[^2] NH Family Hikes says old fire tower footings can still be seen on the summit.[^3] The trail begins on a stretch of Rockrimmon Road that ordinary vehicles can no longer pass, so visitors walk about half a mile to the trailhead.[^3] At the road's end, the left fork is a private driveway.[^3] The hill also appears on NH Family Hikes' list of mountains that once had a fire tower, at 355 feet in Kingston.[^4]
