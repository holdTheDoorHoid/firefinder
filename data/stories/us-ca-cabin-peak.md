Cabin Peak lookout was built on a high, rugged point above the north fork of the Trinity, in Trinity County's Trinity Alps.[^1] Trinity National Forest ran it.[^1]

Preparations began in October 1933. A foreman and two carpenters were sent to start the foundations, and the first materials were due by a twelve-animal pack train, which had to cover a 20-mile pack-in from the end of the East Fork road.[^1] By November work was moving quickly, and a carpenter had been transferred to the site, where a new lookout was being built.[^1]

The cabin was anchored to bedrock with iron rods that ran through the building and were fixed in place with sulphur.[^1] A 1937 item described a 200-foot vertical drop beginning ten feet from the catwalk.[^1]

Joe Strunce was the observer in 1937. The same item said the station picked up Forest Service radio under any conditions and was in heavy demand as a relay point.[^1]

Two elevations appear in the sources.[^1] A 1933 item gives about 6,500 feet for the point, and a 1937 item places the station on a 7,000-foot divide. I could not reconcile the two.[^1]

An October 9, 1937 item reported that during the height of a storm the previous week, a large part of the rocky top broke loose directly beneath the lookout and plunged to the foot of the mountain.[^1] The building was left hovering at the brink of the precipice, and the station was given up for the winter.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks Cabin Peak as gone and as a ground-level site.[^2] The sources I read do not describe what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2]
