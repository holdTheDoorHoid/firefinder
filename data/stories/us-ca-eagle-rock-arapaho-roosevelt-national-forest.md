The Forest Service administered a lookout on Eagle Rock, a ridge that looks down on State Highway 299 about two miles south of Del Loma.[^1][^2] The site lies in the Trinity National Forest, according to the Forest Lookouts page.[^2]

A new fire guard was posted there by August 1933, with a telephone line to the Big Bar ranger station that Civilian Conservation Corps boys had installed.[^2] A 1933 newspaper item said the lookout's view took in 14 miles of the Trinity River and part of Corral bottoms.[^2]

The register gives no build year. In August 1934 Howard Duncan and a crew of four went up to complete the building and beautify the grounds, which suggests the building was finished that year.[^1][^2]

Supplies went up by pack animal.[^2] In 1940 a mule named Tom, shortly after carrying supplies to the lookout, broke his halter rope and headed down the mountain. He was later found stuck on a cliff, according to an Oakland Tribune story.[^2]

A 1975 Caltrans survey description calls the spot the site of an abandoned lookout. Its marker sits in a rock outcrop beside the old foundation.[^2]

The register records rock and mortar remnants of the base, plus a survey marker on the site. It lists the registration date as August 30, 2015.[^1] Check with the Trinity National Forest before going, since the sources do not say whether the site is open to visitors.[^2]
