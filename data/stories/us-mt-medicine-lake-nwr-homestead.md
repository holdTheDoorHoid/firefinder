Medicine Lake National Wildlife Refuge lies in northeastern Montana, on glaciated, undulating plains. Its smaller southern tract holds Homestead Lake, and the refuge was established in 1935.[^1]

The Homestead lookout was built in 1938 and 1939, when the Civilian Conservation Corps put up a 30-foot (9 m) wood-frame tower.[^2] A Montana lookout listing gives the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service as the agency.[^2] A Montana history entry for Homestead Lake also credits the CCC with a lookout tower at the lake, but gives its height as 100 feet (30 m).[^3] The two sources disagree on the height, and neither explains the difference.

Firelookout.com lists a separate record on the refuge: a steel tower used mainly for wildlife observation, which it says is still standing.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Instagram post says a lookout from the 1930s was probably moved to the refuge at a later date, and that people still argue about whether its main purpose was wildlife or wildfire.[^5] It is not clear whether that steel tower is the CCC structure.

The Homestead tower is listed as removed.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the Homestead site today.

Public roads on the refuge total 19 miles, and a 14-mile auto tour is open to visitors for about eight months of the year.[^1]
