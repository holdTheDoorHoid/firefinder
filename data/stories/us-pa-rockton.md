Rockton Fire Tower stands atop Rockton Mountain, at about 2,390 feet, northwest of Clearfield in Pennsylvania's Moshannon State Forest.[^1][^2] The first tower on the summit was a 95-foot Aermotor, built in 1975 to replace the older Old Town and Boone Mountain towers.[^1][^2] The low bid for construction came from Carns Brothers, a Clearfield firm.[^2] The final cost came to $35,285, and a final inspection report from February 1976 survives.[^2]

That original tower did not last. In September 2017, the Department of Conservation and Natural Resources started a $4.6 million project to replace 16 forest fire lookout towers, many of them dating from the 1920s through 1940.[^3] Rockton was on that list.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register added the tower on December 6, 2017, as US 1254, PA 23.[^1]

The replacement is an 80-foot steel tower that the register dates to about 2018, and it lists the 1975 tower as demolished.[^1] A May 2019 article, quoting NPR, reported that the 16 new towers had been built the year before, and that spring marked their first fire season.[^4] An August 2022 photo on the register shows the new tower standing.[^1]

The Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry administers the site.[^1] Staffing and public access did not appear in the sources I checked, so treat the tower as off-limits to climbing until the Bureau confirms it is open.[^1]
