Barton Knob sits on Cheat Mountain in southeastern Randolph County, West Virginia, inside the Monongahela National Forest.[^1][^2] From the summit, the view takes in the headwaters of the Shavers Fork and the Tygart Valley River.[^1]

The State of West Virginia built the tower around 1926, and its cab is a steel box about seven feet on each side.[^1] A newspaper item from October 1926 already mentions a tower on Cheat Mountain, linked by telephone to the Elkins office, which fits that early date.[^3] Wikipedia dates the tower only to "since at least 1939," citing a Forest Service map, so the older date rests on the register and the 1926 item.[^2]

The tower later came under the Monongahela National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service still administers the site.[^1] A 1960 list of projects included road work at the fire tower.[^3] A photo caption on the register shows a watchman's cabin still in place in October 2008.[^1]

In June 2010 the Forest Service approved a repeater project whose work list included removing the existing fire tower.[^3] The plan was for the tower to come down.[^2] By May 2012, a radio repeater had gone in beside the tower, and the tower was not removed.[^2]

By the time of a 2017 visit, the tower had lost parts. That visit recorded the bottom three flights of stairs gone, part of the floor gone, and the cab's roof and windows gone. The same visit found the tower unstaffed.[^1] Wikipedia, as of September 2016, said the tower had been given up and was unsafe to climb.[^2]

Getting there means a hike. The register describes a gate, then a walk of the last three-quarters of a mile, and says the tower is not enclosed or fenced.[^1] Do not climb it. Check with the Monongahela National Forest about trail and gate conditions before you go.
