A May 1931 newspaper item reported that a steel fire tower, 70 feet high, was to be located on the heights at Camp Upton the next spring.[^1] A June 1931 item placed it east of Yaphank.[^1] Two towers already stood on the Island, at Selden and Flanders, but a state ranger found them inadequate for the scrub oak and woodland between them, where on many days clouds obscured fires.[^1]

The state Conservation Department finished the tower in January 1932 on headquarters hill, where the army cantonment offices had stood during the war.[^1] It was the fifth in a series of towers built in Suffolk County, and the report gave its height as 70 feet.[^1] The Long Island list gives 60 feet for the tower at the north site, so the sources do not agree on height.[^1][^3] A man was to be kept on duty in the tower from March 15 to November 15.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register of former lookout sites says the tower was built in 1932, closed in 1953, and removed in 1955 by the state Conservation Department.[^2] The Long Island list agrees on the closing year but says Brookhaven National Laboratory, the new landowner, took the towers down in 1955 or 1956.[^3] The two accounts disagree on who removed the tower and when.[^2][^3]

The site is now on Brookhaven National Laboratory property.[^2][^3] The Long Island list reports that concrete footings are still in place at the northerly site.[^3] No source we read says whether the site is open to the public.[^2]
