Deadwood Lookout, high in Idaho's Boise National Forest, has gone through several lives on the same mountain. The earliest known structure there was a log crib platform put up in 1916.[^1] That gave way in 1924 to a 12-by-12-foot frame cabin with a cupola.[^2] The building that stands today — a ground-mounted L-4 cab built by the Civilian Conservation Corps — replaced the cupola cabin next. Sources disagree on exactly when: the National Historic Lookout Register and Recreation.gov both describe it going into service around 1934–1936, with the Register giving 1936 and Recreation.gov and Firelookout.com both giving 1934.[^3][^4][^2]

The Forest Service staffed the lookout for decades afterward. Among the names on record is Jay Broadie, logged there in 1953.[^5] Recreation.gov states the tower was actively staffed until 1972, after which the Forest Service shifted it to standby, emergency-only status — Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 1973 for that change, which is close enough to likely be the same event described slightly differently.[^4][^5]

Rather than being abandoned, Deadwood was kept up. The National Historic Lookout Register notes a ten-year restoration effort that returned the cab to close to its original 1936 appearance, and the lookout was added to the register in May 2003.[^3][^5]

Today Deadwood Lookout is administered by the Forest Service and sits on the Recreation.gov rental program, where it's become one of Idaho's more sought-after rental cabins for the panoramic forest scenery visible from its perch on the mountain.[^4] Getting up Deadwood Mountain means a twisting, uphill dirt road, so a vehicle with good ground clearance is a good idea; bookings run roughly from the end of June to the middle of October.[^4]
