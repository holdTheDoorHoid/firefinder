The lookout on Peavine Mountain, the peak northwest of Reno, Nevada, was a 1942 joint project of the Mono National Forest and the Grazing Service.[^1] The April plans said the Mono National Forest would provide a standard-size 14-by-14-foot lookout building with a sleeping area, while the Grazing Service would supply the crew, supervision and engineering plans.[^1] A five-mile road from U.S. Highway 395 was needed to reach the summit.[^1] The building was a Forest Service structure that was not in use, and it was moved from the Susanville section.[^1] Grazing Service crews built the station house and the lookout tower.[^1]

The planners pointed to the view over the Plumas, Tahoe and Mono national forests and the grazing land east, north and south of Reno, an area that had seen serious forest and range fires in recent years.[^1] The station opened on July 21, 1942. It had two-way radio, and the Mono forest service's J.P. Ryan was put in charge of it.[^1] In 1961 a Reno newspaper described Mrs. Jean Ferguson of Carson City as the person who ran the Peavine station.[^1]

The Nevada State Journal reported in 1970 that smog had driven the lookout out of service.[^1] A 2012 report by the Forest Fire Lookout Association gives different dates. It says the lookout was abandoned in 1964, was still standing in 1972, and is no longer standing, though its removal date is unknown. The same report says the Tahoe National Forest built it originally.[^2] The sources do not agree on these dates, and none of them settles the question.

Today the summit carries radio towers and transmitters, reached by an access road that climbs the north and east faces of the mountain.[^3] The Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest stewards the mountain and has added many miles of hiking and mountain-bike trails, though access from northwest Reno has become more restricted.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Nevada list shows the lookout as a "Ground" type with the status "Gone".[^4] No source describes what, if anything, of the 1942 station is left on the summit.
