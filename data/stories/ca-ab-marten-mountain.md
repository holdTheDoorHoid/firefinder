Marten Mountain Tower stands at the peak of Marten Mountain, near Slave Lake.[^1][^3] The district calls it a forestry observation tower used for spotting fires in the Slave Lake Forest Area.[^1] A Lakeside Leader profile places it beside the Marten Mountain Lookout and the Lily Lake trailhead in Lesser Slave Lake Provincial Park.[^2]

The district and the profile agree on the total height. The district gives the tower as 61 m (200 ft).[^1] The profile says it is 200 feet in total, with the cupola about 30 m (100 ft) up.[^2]

The cupola is a bit bigger than normal, and the fire-finder takes up the middle of the room.[^2] Douglas Hauer started working in a fire watch tower in 2005, and the profile says he was still in the job as of 2022.[^2] According to the profile, fire-tower workers are posted at a station for three to six months, covering the spring and summer.[^2] Hauer's cabin has electricity, running water and wifi.[^2]

In 2022 the tower took part in the Alberta Wildfire Detection Challenge, which Alberta Wildfire, Alberta Innovates and FPInnovations ran together.[^3] The program chose six commercially available fixed detection systems and set them up on the tower for the 2022 wildfire season.[^3]

The district's page, titled Marten Mountain Viewpoint, directs visitors up a scenic drive to the adjacent lookout and points them to the Lily Lake trailhead.[^1] The page describes the tower as a fire-spotting structure and does not mention public access to it.[^1] The profile counts it among the few towers in the Slave Lake Forest Area that can be reached by road.[^2] Alberta reports 127 wildfire lookout sites, each staffed by an observer who usually works there from April to October.[^4]
