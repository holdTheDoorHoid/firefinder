The New Castle Hill lookout stood in Lawrence County, Ohio, near Ironton and Hanging Rock.[^1][^2]

In late October 1934 the Chillicothe Gazette wrote about a new tower near Hanging Rock, which Corbett Marshall of Pine Grove was to occupy.[^2]

By June 1935 the Amherst News-Times described a small wooden lookout shaped like a box, sitting 40 feet up, atop a large oak. Inside was a telephone in a waterproof box, and any sighting of a fire would be sent on to nearby points for fire crews.[^2] The paper called it the lookout of the Ohio State Forestry Department, where forest rangers kept a round-the-clock watch during the fall months, when fires were common.[^2]

A 2006 article from Columbus, about Ohio's own state forest lookouts, said improved telephone communication and airplanes had made lookout towers obsolete, and that time, weather and vandals had left most of them unsafe for public access.[^6]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Ohio list marks the Newcastle Hill tower as gone.[^3] The land is now part of Wayne National Forest, which the U.S. Forest Service administers.[^1] The forest traces its start to 1935, when it first acquired land.[^4]

Wayne National Forest is public land, but none of the sources we read describes what remains of the lookout site or whether visitors can reach it.[^5][^1]
