The Virginia Department of Forestry administers the Hayters Knob Lookout, a fire tower in Washington County.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the agency began building steel towers in the 1930s, after it was founded in 1914.[^1] This tower went up in 1939.[^1] Civilian Conservation Corps men built it, and the materials reached the site on mules and on the men's backs.[^1]

The register says the tower was in use from 1939 until the spring of 1970.[^1] A photograph on the register page is captioned September 2020, but the page gives no later history of the tower, so its condition after 1970 is unclear.[^1]

The tower sits in The Channels Natural Area Preserve.[^1] The register says the Commonwealth of Virginia created the preserve in 2008 to protect a sandstone formation about 100 feet east of the tower.[^1] A hiking account of the preserve describes walking past what it calls an abandoned fire tower on the way to The Channels.[^2] The account does not name that tower, and it separately describes a fire tower on Middle Knob, so it is not clear whether the abandoned tower it mentions is this lookout.[^2]

Visitors reach the preserve on foot.[^1] The register describes a hike of just over three miles from a parking lot on VA Route 80 at Hayters Gap, along a dirt private road where the easement is for hikers only, and then forest roads.[^1] The register does not say whether the tower itself may be climbed, so this story does not encourage it.[^1] Ask the Department of Forestry before you plan a visit.
