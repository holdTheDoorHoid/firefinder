Trout Creek Butte rises about 6 miles southwest of Sisters in Deschutes County, Oregon, and its steel lookout has stood on the Deschutes National Forest since 1933.[^1][^2]

A Bend Bulletin notice from October 1932 said a 75-foot steel tower was planned for the butte the next spring.[^4] By early August 1933 a 20-man crew from the CCC camp on the Metolius River was raising the steel, and a 14-by-16-foot wood cabin for living quarters followed in 1934.[^4]

The sources give different heights. A 1933 newspaper item called the tower 80 feet high, and the Oregon Lookouts page lists 75 feet, while the Register and firelookout.com give 86 feet.[^4][^1][^2] The Register counts it among the few 7-by-7-foot Aermotor towers left in the central Cascades.[^1]

Seasonal lookouts staffed the tower through the 1930s and into the 1940s. The Bend Bulletin named Paul Tallone as lookout foreman in 1935 and Dick Compton as lookout in 1937.[^4] In July 1935 a blaze on the southeast flank of Black Crater was seen from the butte's lookout at 11 p.m., and a crew went out at 3 a.m.[^4] In September 1940 Gordon Landdon, who had staffed the lookout through the fire season, closed it.[^4]

The Register says the tower has been on standby since 1972, and firelookout.com says emergency use ended in the 1970s.[^1][^2] The 2017 Milli Fire burned across much of the summit, but some trees beside the lookout came through.[^3] Cheryl Hill, visiting in May 2020, listed the status as abandoned, found the cabin floor gone and much of the roof missing, and noted that the lower stairs were gone and that a warning tag on the tower said not to climb it.[^3] The Register's undated comments say the main cab has bullet holes and has nearly lost its paint, while the steel frame is in good condition.[^1]

Cheryl Hill also reported that the road to the butte was in good condition and suitable for any vehicle.[^3] The Register lists the registration date as March 31, 2003, under US 506, and names the U.S. Forest Service's Sisters Ranger District as administrator.[^1] Do not climb the tower. Check with the Sisters Ranger District before planning a trip to the summit.
