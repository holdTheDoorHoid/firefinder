A lookout first stood on Heckletooth Mountain as a tent in 1915.[^1][^2] The frame cab that followed was 10 feet square, with a 22-by-22-foot roof and an open cupola, and it went up in 1917.[^1][^2] A local history of Oregon lookouts calls the 1917 structure a rough cupola shelter.[^3] The mountain sits in the Willamette National Forest, about four miles east of Oakridge.[^1][^2] The register puts the lookout at 3,400 feet, some 280 feet below the 3,682-foot summit.[^1][^5]

Staffing is known from newspaper items of 1920 and 1922 that Oregon Lookouts reprints.[^3] In July 1920 a Mrs. L. E. Flock, the lookout, fell ill and had to go down to Eugene for treatment.[^3] By August she was on the lookout with her young son and daughter.[^3] In December a newspaper item said that during fire season she held a post on top of the mountain and reported sightings of smoke to rangers.[^3] The items spell her name several ways, so her full name is not settled.[^3] A July 1922 item reports that Vida Derflinger was heading back to her lookout job near Oakridge, on a high point there, but the item does not name Heckletooth.[^3]

Firelookout.com gives the site the label "R.I.P. 1917-?" and says the cab was later abandoned.[^2] The register says it was later abandoned and is now gone.[^1] Oregon Lookouts lists the site as "Removed," with no date or reason.[^3] None of the three gives a year for the removal.[^1][^2][^3]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains of the cab or its footings on the summit today.[^1][^4] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator.[^1] The Western Cascades trail guide describes a hike of 2.4 to 3-plus miles round trip, through sloping meadows and a recovering burn to a rocky ridge with a view of Diamond Peak.[^4] The guide sends visitors east out of Oakridge on numbered roads to a trailhead pulloff, then on foot.[^4] A February 2024 trip report from Crest Trailhead #2 describes hiking about five miles and 2,000 feet up to the summit.[^6] Nothing in these sources suggests a lookout is left to visit, and the land is Willamette National Forest.[^1][^4]
