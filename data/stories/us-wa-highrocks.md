The Highrocks Lookout Site was in Snohomish County, Washington, about five miles southeast of Monroe.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives its elevation as 1,463 feet, or 446 meters.[^1]

The register dates the site to 1930, when it held a 10-foot wooden enclosed smokehouse.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says the Washington Division of Forestry managed it at the time.[^3] A 1930 entry in the Washington Forest Fire Association's 23rd annual report says the lookout followed the standard design the association had settled on in 1929, and that its view took in logged-off land in King County.[^4]

The site was discontinued in 1939.[^3][^4] The sources differ on why. The Forest Lookouts page quotes the association's report saying the Blue Mountain Lookout took over its view.[^4] WillhiteWeb says the area was felt to be covered enough by the new Snoqualmie Lookout, 12 miles south, and by Blue Mountain, which had gone up a few years earlier to the north.[^3]

The site was reestablished as a lookout point in 1944.[^3][^4] The building had living quarters at the base and a ten-foot-square glassed room above.[^3][^4]

WillhiteWeb says a USGS visit in 1952 found a house with a cupola, and that the lookout was abandoned again in 1957.[^3] A state report for 1956 to 1958 says a lookout was completed on High Rock in that period.[^4] The two accounts do not fit together neatly, and the sources do not settle which is right.

A 1960 USGS visit found that the lookout house had burned, and its remains lay on the ground.[^3] The register and Firelookout.com both say the site was gone by 1960.[^1][^2]

In 2015, WillhiteWeb reported that all that was left was concrete and one old log, with bolts sticking out of it.[^3] That is the latest description we found. The land is private, owned by the Catholic Church, and is part of a camp that includes Lake Hannan.[^3] WillhiteWeb says visitors need permission.[^3]
