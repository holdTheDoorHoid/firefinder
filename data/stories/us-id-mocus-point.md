Mocus Point sits in the Clearwater National Forest, in Idaho County, 16 miles west-southwest of the Powell Ranger Station, at 5,579 feet.[^1][^2] Its first lookout was a platform in a tree.[^1] Firelookout.com dates it to 1934,[^1] but Louis Hartig's 1981 history, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the first lookout came at the start of the 1935 fire season, in a tent camp near a larch topped at 55 feet.[^3] In 1941 the top was cut down ten feet and the crow's nest set at 45 feet.[^3]

A 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab came later.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com dates it to 1946,[^1] but Hartig's history records a surplus cab moved in during 1947 and construction funding in 1949.[^3]

Hartig names the people who worked the point.[^3] A lookout man, Harry Cummings, built a 14 by 18 foot living cabin by himself, after Ranger Herb Flodberg had material salvaged in 1942 from the Boundary planting camp and an abandoned lookout.[^3] In 1949 George Brocke and his family moved to the point as construction got underway.[^3] The point was last manned by the Lochsa District in 1953.[^3] After it passed to the Powell District in 1963, it was active again and still in use in 1966.[^3]

Firelookout.com, the Idaho Fire Lookouts page and the Clearwater list all say the tower was destroyed in 1973.[^1][^2][^4] The Forest Lookouts page, citing Kresek, says it was removed that year instead.[^3]

None of the sources describes what, if anything, is left at the site today. The Idaho Fire Lookouts page tags the point as a hike-in.[^2]
