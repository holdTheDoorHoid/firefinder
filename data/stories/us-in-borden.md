The Borden fire tower was a Clark County lookout belonging to the Indiana Department of Conservation.[^2] A July 1937 list of fire warden personnel in the state's annual report mentions it, which suggests the tower was already standing by then.[^1]

A 1947 National Geodetic Survey description portrays a steel truss tower on four legs, with a cabin at the top, silver in color and about 85 feet high.[^1] A 1954 newspaper report gives its height as 80 feet, so the two sources do not agree on the height.[^2]

In August 1954 the state moved the tower to a spot about 8 miles north of Salem, in Washington County.[^2] The announcement said the new site would tie together the Skyline Tower in Jackson State Forest and the Clark County State Forest tower, and would add protection for more than 150,000 acres.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the moved tower was renamed the Salem Fire Tower.[^3] The Indiana Fire Towers site, drawing on earlier research, agrees that the Borden tower went to the current Salem site.[^1]

A 1966 federal survey note says the Borden tower had been torn down, with only its footings left.[^1] A 1957 topographic map shows the survey mark at the site but not the tower.[^1]

Today the moved tower is the Salem Fire Tower.[^3] The register describes it as 80 feet tall, four-legged and painted silver, and says it sits on private property, not in a state park or forest.[^3] The Indiana Fire Towers author found it standing but not accessible.[^4] The register gives its registration date as August 2, 2026.[^3] No source I could read describes the original Clark County site today; the Indiana Fire Towers page says only that the tower is not standing there.[^1]
