Moses Mountain Lookout stands on the Colville Indian Reservation, about 21 miles east of Omak in Okanogan County.[^1][^2] Its history begins in 1914, when a crow's-nest platform in a tree and a cabin served as the lookout.[^1][^2]

In 1923 a 30-foot pole tower with a rough cab took the place of the tree.[^1][^2] A 35-foot steel tower followed in 1930.[^1][^2] The current 123-foot Aermotor steel tower went up in 1938, and the sources describe it as the tallest lookout in the Pacific Northwest.[^1][^2][^3] The Aermotor Company, a Chicago firm founded in 1888, made steel towers of this kind.[^1]

The sources read do not name a lookout who worked here or record a fire the site spotted.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout was given up in the mid-1980s.[^1][^2] Both NHLR and firelookout.com suggest the 1930 tower was probably moved to Grizzly Mountain, where it was built in 1941.[^1][^2] That move is inferred from evidence rather than documented, so treat it as likely, not confirmed.

The road is driveable.[^3] From Omak, take State Route 155 east, then BIA-56 for 5.3 miles and BIA-61 for 4 miles to the summit.[^3] TrailChick recommends a high-clearance, all-wheel-drive vehicle because road conditions can vary.[^3] Non-tribal visitors must buy a recreation permit, which TrailChick gives as $80 a year.[^3]

TrailChick visited on November 19, 2018, and found the roads in good shape that day.[^3] The latest visit in the sources read was in 2018, and the TrailChick page was last updated in March 2024, so the tower's current condition is not confirmed here.[^3]
