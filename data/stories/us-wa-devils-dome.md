Devils Dome is a summit in Whatcom County, about nine miles northeast of Ross Dam.[^1][^2]

The land lies in the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administered the lookout.[^1]

An L-5 cab was built on the rocks at the summit in 1933.[^1][^3]

Panoramic photos taken from the ground on August 7, 1935 are credited to the National Archives' Pacific Northwest Region.[^1][^3]

Barney Douglass spent two summers on the summit, according to Willhite, who gives no years.[^3]

The cab was abandoned in 1968.[^1][^2][^3] The register and firelookout.com both list it as gone.[^1][^2]

Willhite places the site a few hundred feet off the trail, at the summit itself.[^3] The sources we read do not describe what remains there today.

Reaching it means a hike, and Willhite describes two trailheads on Highway 20.[^3]

Willhite calls the loop 40 miles, while the Washington Trails Association lists it at 43.0 miles round trip.[^3][^4]

The WTA lists a backcountry camping permit with a fee, plus a wilderness permit that hikers issue themselves at the trailhead at no cost.[^4]
