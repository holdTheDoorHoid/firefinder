The Hamburg tower was set up to guard the City of Frederick's forest, about 6,000 acres on the headwaters of Fishing and Hunting Creeks that the city now owns.[^1] The state's forestry report for fiscal 1930 dates the completion of a 60-foot steel tower to 1926.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1930 as the build year instead. It ties the tower to the city's water-supply work that year, and says the state forestry department helped build the tower as part of a plan to develop the forest commercially.[^2] The two dates disagree.

A 1930 state report says the tower had been fitted out and was in use to protect the city forest and nearby land, and that state foresters were working with the city on the fire problem.[^1] In April 1932 the state announced R.H. Wempe, of Highfield in Washington County, as the tower's towerman.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maryland list marks the tower as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors may go there. The lookout list places the tower at about 39.51 north and 77.48 west.[^3]
