Fox Mountain Lookout stands on Fox Mountain in the Gila National Forest, Catron County, New Mexico, at 9,333 feet (2,845 metres).[^1] The register says it was built in 1959.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page, citing a Forest Service publication on the region's lookouts, describes a steel CL-100 series cab on a 10-foot cinder block base with a storage room.[^2] The 2020 report places the design in the Forest Service's standard-plan CL-100 to CL-106 series.[^3]

The 2020 report says the lookout is staffed seasonally.[^3] I found no source giving staffing for later years, and no source reporting a fire at the site.

A 1988 review for a thematic National Register nomination found Fox Mountain not eligible, because it was only 29 years old at the time.[^3] The 2020 report suggests it be re-evaluated now that it is 61.[^3] The register added the lookout on January 2, 2006, with Dave Lorenz as nominator.[^1]

A federal survey marker description, written by the Coast and Geodetic Survey in 1936, refers to a lookout station on the summit.[^2] The sources do not say what stood there before the 1959 cab.[^2]

Today, I found no official Forest Service page for the lookout. A road guide says the summit is reached by Forest Road 770, starting from NM-32, and that the road is totally unpaved for about 5.2 miles.[^4] The same guide says the road is usually impassable from November through June and recommends a 4x4 vehicle.[^4] No source I found confirms whether the tower itself is open to visitors, so check current conditions before planning a trip.
