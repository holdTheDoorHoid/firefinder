The Mount Wrightson lookout, also called the Old Baldy lookout, stood on the summit of Mount Wrightson, a peak in the Santa Rita range of southern Arizona, at 9,453 feet.[^1][^4] Wikipedia gives the peak as 9,456 feet, a small difference from the figure in the GVRHC and Forest Service accounts.[^5] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a gone, ground-level lookout.[^6]

The first lookout went up around 1909.[^1] Its size was estimated at under six feet square, with no windows, and it was used only while a lookout was on duty.[^1] A telephone line from the Madera Administrative Site reached the lookout in March 1912.[^1] Around 1914 a cabin went up near Baldy Spring, a short way from Baldy Saddle, to give rangers living quarters when they were off duty at the lookout.[^1] About 1920 the Forest Service replaced the first lookout with a slightly larger one, and in 1928 a building of about 12 by 12 feet took its place.[^1][^2] That final version had a work area, a kitchen, a sleeping area and a cupola holding a fire finder, on a platform four feet above the ground.[^1]

Newspapers of the time took notice of the lookout. In May 1911 the Tucson Citizen reported that a watch would be posted on Old Baldy to catch fires early, with phone contact to the district's ranger stations.[^7] In May 1922 the Tombstone Epitaph reported that a fire alarm from the Huachucas proved baseless, and that the Baldy station said it seemed to have been only a small structure or a rubbish fire.[^7] The same station described a fire of fairly large size in Mexico, apparently around 30 miles south of Nogales.[^7]

In 1958 rangers still used the cabin now and then, and it was kept supplied with food and firewood.[^2]

The final lookout was taken down in the late 1950s.[^1] By 1958 only the decking remained, with an open space beneath it, and a sign-in roster was kept in a tin under it.[^1][^2] The Baldy cabin burned in the fall of 1973, and its remnants were cleared away.[^1] A 2011 summary says that only some of the stone foundation was left.[^1]

No source we read describes a standing structure at the summit today.[^1][^3] A visitor who climbed it on February 22, 2022 reported that the summit holds the lookout's foundation, and said a guest register may be in one inner corner of the wall.[^3] Both the Old Baldy Trail (#372) and the Super Trail lead to the summit, and the Old Baldy route starts at the Mount Wrightson Picnic Area.[^4][^3]
