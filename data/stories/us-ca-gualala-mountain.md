Gualala Mountain is a lookout site in Mendocino County, California.[^3] The 1929 newspaper items quoted by Forest Lookouts call the high point Gualala Peak.[^1]

In January 1929, a state forest inspector arranged an agreement between the Division of Forestry and a timber company, which would help pay for better fire protection in the district.[^1] The plan called for a fire lookout station on Gualala Peak that spring.[^1] An April 1929 item said a telephone line was under construction from the peak to Anchor Bay, and that the state would post a lookout man there that season.[^1] In July 1930, the Ukiah Dispatch Democrat reported that a station had recently been put up at the head of the Gualala River and that an observer would go on duty.[^1]

In June 1935, the same paper said a 60-foot-high steel tower would be built. In late July a Civilian Conservation Corps crew was sent to erect it, and in August the Ukiah Republican Press reported that CCC camp workers were building it.[^1] The sources do not say whether the 1930 station was the same structure as the 1935 tower.

A 1956 Coast and Geodetic Survey description places a survey disk at the top of a concrete footing that held one steel leg of the tower.[^2] The disk was marked as set in 1938 by the U.S. Forest Service, and it was recovered in good condition in 1964.[^2] The 1956 directions run from State Highway 1 at Anchor Bay, up Fish Rock Road and Signal Ridge Road, and then along a bladed dirt road to the site.[^2]

Forest Lookouts marks the tower as removed but gives no date.[^1] The firelookout.org index lists the site as gone.[^3] No source describes what remains at the site today, and none says whether it is open to visitors or whether the 1956 road is still passable.
