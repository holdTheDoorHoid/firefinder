Walde Mountain Lookout is one of a handful of 7x7-foot steel fire towers built on the Clearwater National Forest. The 100-foot Aermotor tower went up in 1939, and a log cabin ground house was added two years later, in 1941, to house the staff working the lookout above.[^1][^2][^3]

Unlike many of the region's lookouts, Walde Mountain never stopped working. It's still staffed every summer and fall season, making it an active fire lookout rather than a retired one.[^1][^2][^3] Known staff recorded by Idaho Fire Lookouts include a 1972 stint by Mr. and Mrs. Daniel McGinnis, as reported by the Kellogg Evening News, though a full staffing history hasn't been documented.[^2]

Because the tower is in use through the warmer months, the adjoining cabin is only opened to the public in winter — roughly mid-December through late March — as part of the Forest Service's Recreation Rental program.[^2][^3][^4] The 14x16-foot log cabin has been restored to its original character and sleeps up to four, with two mattresses in a loft and two cots, a wood stove, a propane cook stove and lights, and an outhouse; there's no running water.[^4] Reaching it in winter means a roughly 17-mile trip by snowmobile, skis, or snowshoes, and the route isn't always groomed.[^4]

The cabin sits at about 5,220 feet on the divide between the Lochsa and Clearwater River watersheds, with views toward the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness and the Selway Crags.[^4] Walde Mountain Lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2009.[^1]
