In June 1963 the Poteau News reported that a 100-foot (30 m) steel fire tower was due to be finished that week on top of Cavanal Hill.[^1] Four years earlier, Senator Clem Hamilton of Heavener had helped secure funding for it. He visited it with George Mitchell, the Forestry division's district supervisor from Broken Bow.[^1]

Poteau city and the Kerr estate each donated three lots for the site, and a house was planned nearby for the operator's household.[^1] The footings were 8.5 feet (2.6 m) deep.[^1]

The plan was for a trained fire watcher who would also act as the weather observer.[^1] On hazy days the watcher could see about 15 miles (24 km) out, Mitchell said, and on a clear day up to 100 miles (160 km).[^1]

The later record is thinner. A SummitPost trip report describes walking to "the former lookout site" on Cavanal Mountain, which is the official name for Cavanal Hill.[^2][^3] A RadioReference page listing Oklahoma Forestry systems includes an entry for "LeFlore County -- Cavanal Mountain," which may mean Forestry radio equipment is on the summit, though the entry is undated.[^4] The trip report's wording is the only description of the summit we found, and it does not say whether the 1963 steel tower remains.[^3]

Today a 4.5-mile (7.2 km) paved road climbs to the summit, and visitors there can look out over the Poteau River Valley.[^2] Mountain-bike races and a 5-mile walk are also held on the hill.[^2] A sign at the base gives the elevation as 1,999 feet, but the summit itself is 2,385 feet (727 m) above sea level.[^2] The same trip report describes driving up the mountain, a peak near Poteau.[^3]
