The West Fork Tilton lookout stood in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, in Lewis County, Washington.[^1] The register lists the site as US 1304 and gives a registration date of June 20, 2012.[^1]

The register says the lookout was built in 1934 as a 20-foot wooden L-4 tower.[^1] Willhite thinks something may have stood on the spot before then, but he describes the first structure as built in 1934.[^3] Panorama photographs were taken at the site on July 14, 1934,[^4] and Willhite says those pictures show a nearby shed and an outhouse.[^3]

A washingtonlookouts listing gives an activation from October 1, 1942 to June 21, 1943, under the Seattle Filter Center.[^4]

The register says the tower was removed in the 1950s. Firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed around then, so the sources disagree on how it ended.[^1][^2][^3] The washingtonlookouts list marks it as removed.[^4]

Willhite found the site by comparing 1934 photographs with Google Earth for several hours; it sits at a four-way road intersection.[^3] In 2015 he dug a spot he had picked, and it revealed a foundation. He says not much remains.[^3]

The route runs through West Fork Timber property. Signs say no trespassing just inside the gate, and Willhite advises asking the company for permission before going in.[^3] None of the pages we read describes a standing structure today.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
