In 1930 St. Johns County prepared for a fire observation tower at Mill Creek.[^1] In May, county farm agent E. H. Vance told the St. Augustine Record that the federal government would build and maintain a tower and ranger station about a mile from the local school.[^1] By September, some 90,000 acres had been designated a fire protection unit with the Florida Forest Service, and J. K. Malphurs had been named ranger.[^1]

By mid-November the tower was finished.[^1] The Record placed the 80-foot (24 m) steel observation tower 13 miles (21 km) west of St. Augustine, on the Green Cove Springs road.[^1] During fire season a lookout was to be kept in the tower, with five firefighting crews reachable over 27 miles (43 km) of telephone line.[^1] Landowners, the state and the federal government together contributed four cents an acre toward fire control.[^1] The Record also welcomed visitors to the tower.[^1]

No source I found describes the site today, so it is not known what, if anything, remains there.
