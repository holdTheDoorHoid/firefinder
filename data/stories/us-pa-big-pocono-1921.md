The Big Pocono tower was built in 1921 on Big Pocono Mountain, in what is now Big Pocono State Park near Tannersville, Pennsylvania.[^1][^2] A 2017 Courier article instead says it was first installed in 1919.[^5] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it a typical example of Pennsylvania fire tower work from 1914 to 1939. It also says the tower is thought to be the oldest still operating, though some older structures stand inactive.[^1]

The tower was built of steel, an Aermotor LS 40 model, and its cab measured 7 by 7 feet.[^3][^1] The register gives its height as 21 feet. A 2017 Courier article gives 35 feet instead, and the sources we read do not say why they differ.[^1][^5]

The register's visit notes say the tower was mostly staffed in spring, and that fall staffing depends on weather and need.[^1] We found no named fire or smoke report tied to this tower in the sources we read.

In 2017 the tower was taken down as part of Pennsylvania's plan to upgrade its fire towers, and a 40-foot tower replaced it at the park.[^1][^4] The Courier reported that the old tower was cut into two pieces so it could be moved to Milford, where Grey Towers is.[^5] In 2017 that paper also said Grey Towers, the former home of Gifford Pinchot, was owned and managed by the Forest Service.[^5]

The tower was relocated to Grey Towers to be refurbished and re-erected, and the state donated it as an educational resource.[^3][^6] Restoration began in 2018 with volunteers helping.[^6] The cab was taken apart in the summer of 2018 and reassembled in the summer of 2019.[^3][^6] On September 28, 2022, a local paper reported that the tower was nearly fully restored, with some educational items still to finish.[^6]

The half-mile Forest Discovery Trail winds through the woods behind Grey Towers, and visitors can see the restored tower.[^2][^6] In 2022, the tower could be climbed during the Festival of Wood.[^6] The 2017 plan said groups going up to the top cab would be supervised for safety.[^5] The sources we read do not say what the climbing rules are today, so ask Grey Towers before you plan a climb.

The tower standing at Big Pocono State Park now is the 2017 replacement.[^4] The original 1921 lookout is the one at Grey Towers.[^4]
