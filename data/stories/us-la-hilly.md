The Hilly fire tower stood on a high hill in Lincoln Parish, about nine miles northwest of Ruston and a mile northwest of the small community of Hilly.[^1] Federal survey crews described it in 1952, when its steel framework held up a cabin measuring six feet by six.[^1] The survey put the tower at about 115 feet.[^1] A 1961 newspaper report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, called it a 100-foot tower, so the two figures disagree.[^1][^2]

The Louisiana Forestry Commission operated the tower.[^2] In 1961 a tower man from the commission's Lincoln Parish unit watched from it. The tower was manned every day of the year, for hours that changed with the fire danger, and on the worst days the tower man was expected to stay on duty, or provide a man in the tower, for as much as 24 hours a day.[^2]

A March 1956 report called the tower the commission's unused fire tower, even though a fire had been set at its base that month. Firefighters reached the flames in time to keep the tower from burning.[^2]

In November 1990, the Lincoln Parish sheriff said three men had apparently stolen a pulpwood truck, driven it to the Hilly Fire Tower and destroyed a hut used by on-duty rangers.[^2] Three men were arrested after the incident.[^2]

No source we read says whether the tower still stands. Nothing we read describes what remains at the site, or whether the public can visit it.[^1][^2]
