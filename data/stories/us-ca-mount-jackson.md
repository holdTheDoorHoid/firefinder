Mount Jackson, near Guerneville in Sonoma County, got its first fire lookout in 1928.[^1][^2] An August 1927 newspaper report had put the planned tower at $2,500 and said quarters for lookouts would be provided.[^2] In February 1928 the county set $300 aside to help finish the station.[^2]

The register describes a building of the 809R design, which came out in 1946, and gives 1947 as the oldest construction date.[^1] The register's own built field reads 1948.[^1]

Fred Johnson, a former Petaluma resident, was posted on the summit each summer from 1929 through 1934.[^2] In July 1935 he discovered a fire that threatened Bohemian Grove, and about eight acres burned.[^2] A May 1939 report had Sam Wykoff running the station.[^2]

The register says the lookout was deliberately burned in 1995, after it had been retired from fire detection.[^1]

The register's latest description of the site is a 1991 note (MVT 10/91).[^1] The note says visitors cannot reach the site, and that the lookout sits about a mile beyond a locked gate.[^1] The same note lists communications vaults and towers, microwave installations nearby, and a concrete water tank of 10,000 gallons.[^1] No source we read describes what stands on the summit now.
