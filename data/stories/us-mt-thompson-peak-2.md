Thompson Peak, a mile southwest of Superior, Montana, has had fire detection on it since about 1920, when the Forest Service put up a simple tree platform tower.[^1] In 1934 that gave way to a 30-foot pole tower topped with a standard L-4 cab.[^1] The wooden tower had a rough run of it: bears gnawed at the legs, and vandals went after it with chainsaws.[^1] By 1984 the Forest Service had replaced it again, this time with the structure that stands today — a 20-foot concrete base holding a live-in cab, built on what is now a shared radio and cellular tower.[^1][^2] The lookout cab occupies the third floor of that multi-story structure, with telecommunications equipment on the floors below, which gives it an unusual look, more air-traffic-control tower than classic fire lookout.[^1][^2]

Known staff over the decades include Fern Bonnell in 1950 and 1954, Garry O'Hara in 1953, and Katherine Mercer from 1957 to 1959.[^1] The lookout has not been regularly staffed for fire detection in recent years.[^1]

Today Thompson Peak is part of the Lolo National Forest's recreation rental program, bookable through Recreation.gov.[^1][^2] Because the building still carries active communications equipment on its lower floors, the site notes that repair crews may be working there even during a guest's stay, and the entry gate and lookout stay locked, with an access code sent at booking.[^2] The cabin sleeps up to four and has no water on site; getting there from Missoula means driving west on I-90 to Superior, then following Forest Service roads up to the peak.[^2]
