The Summit Fire Lookout stands at the top of Mount Revelstoke, in Mount Revelstoke National Park, British Columbia.[^3] Parks Canada's heritage statement says the Dominion Forestry Branch built it in 1927.[^3]

It is a two-storey, cupola-style lookout cabin of wood frame, clad in wooden shingles and clapboard.[^1][^3] The lower floor held the fire warden's living quarters, and the upper floor was used only as a viewing area.[^3] The square cupola has windows on all sides.[^1] The register says the design came from the United States Forest Service and reached Canada early in the 20th century.[^1]

By the summer of 1930, a fire ranger, Chas. Kolofsky, was in charge of the lookout station.[^2] A Calgary Herald report, as quoted on Canada Lookouts, says the station began registering visitors on June 19.[^2] By July 18, the same report counted 126 people who had come up the 32-kilometre (20-mile) motor highway to the summit.[^2]

The heritage statement lists the lookout as a Recognized heritage building and calls it a good and rare example of a cupola-style cabin type that originated in the United States.[^3] It also records that the custodial department has since become the Canadian Parks Service.[^3]

The register adds that it is the sole structure on the summit and a prominent, well-known attraction for Revelstoke residents and yearly visitors.[^1] The text I could read gives no date, and no page I could open is dated after the register and heritage statement, so the lookout's present condition and whether visitors may enter the cabin are unconfirmed.
