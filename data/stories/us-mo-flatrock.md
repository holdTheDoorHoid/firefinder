The Flatrock Fire Tower stands in the Flatrock State Wildlife Area in Texas County, Missouri, according to the National Historic Lookout Register, which accepted it on August 2, 2026.[^1] Another lookout history places it in Shannon County, in the Angeline Conservation Area, so the county is in dispute.[^2]

The Register says online resources indicate that the land was obtained in 1942 and the tower erected in 1943.[^1] It describes the tower as a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40.[^1] A Missouri Conservation Commission annual report, as quoted on the lookout history site, records a 50-foot steel tower erected in 1943.[^2] The two sources do not agree on the height, and the annual report entry does not say which structure it covers. A 2022 Missouri Conservationist article says most of the state's towers were 100 feet tall, which does not settle the question.[^3]

Later reports describe steel towers replacing temporary wooden ones.[^2] A fiscal 1957-58 entry describes one such replacement, and a 1962 entry describes another.[^2] Read together, the early record does not clearly say which steel tower stands today. In fiscal 1969-70, a deep well was drilled and a pump installed at the towersite.[^2]

The sources I read do not say who staffed the tower, when the watch ended, or whether it helped catch any particular fire.

The Register calls the tower in decent condition, with light graffiti and some wobbly wooden planks. It says the condition of the cab is unknown.[^1]

The Register says visitors could go only as high as the top of the tower, right under the cab. It adds that the catwalk is not usable and that a locked hatch leads into the cab.[^1] It does not say whether the public may enter the area or the towersite. Check with the Missouri Conservation Department before going, and do not climb it unless it is open to the public.
