The Junction Mountain lookout sits in Kananaskis Country, and Alberta Parks lists a trail to it.[^1] Alberta's wildfire lookout dataset gives 1974 as the year the site was built, codes it as a cabin, and records its tower height as 5 m (16 ft).[^2] Its elevation is recorded as 2,240.5 m (7,351 ft).[^2] Each of the province's 127 lookout sites has an observer on duty, generally from April to October.[^3] To prevent bear conflicts, Alberta says its wildfire staff with Agriculture and Forestry have adopted BearSmart recommendations for many sites, key among them electric fencing.[^3] The dataset still lists the lookout as active.[^2]

The Alberta Parks trail follows a fire road to the lookout from a trailhead 4.4 km up Sheep Trail from Indian Oils Day Use Area.[^1] The route to the summit of Junction Mountain totals 13.7 km.[^1] The trail is on public land and is not regularly maintained.[^1] The dataset codes the access mode as drive. Nothing in these sources says the building itself is open to visitors.[^1][^2] One visitor's 2016 comment on Peakery called the lookout a great jumping-off point for nearby peaks, Junction Mountain included, and said downtown Calgary can be seen on a clear day.[^4]
