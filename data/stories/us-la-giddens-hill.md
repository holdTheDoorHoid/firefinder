Bossier Parish got its third fire tower in 1946, when a lookout went up on Giddens Hill near old Fillmore.[^1][^2] The other two were the Plain Dealing tower, by Rocky Mount, and the Bodcau tower, by old Bellevue.[^1]

A June 1946 report said the planned tower would stand 100 feet and be built entirely of steel.[^1] The same report said the site was reportedly leased for 25 years from the Sandridge-Beauchamp estate.[^1] Giddens Hill stands 525 feet above sea level, and the report called it the best tower location in Louisiana.[^1]

A November 7, 1946 report said the finished tower fell just three inches short of 100 feet, and a November 14 report put it at 97 feet.[^1] A 2021 history post from the Bossier Parish Libraries History Center calls it the 100-foot tower.[^2] A 1969 federal survey put the overall height at about 125 feet and described a steel tower with four legs.[^1] The heights disagree, and the sources we read do not settle which figure is right.[^1][^2]

Early in the Louisiana Forestry Commission's history, lookout towers were positioned so that each district of the state had protection.[^2] In April 1947, towerman Josh Matlock and his wife settled into a new home on Giddens Hill.[^1] On October 1, 1951, Melvin D. McMahon began as crew leader for the commission, based at the Giddens Hill tower.[^1]

By August 1976, the tower had been relocated to the Barksdale AFB reservation, per a Shreveport Journal item quoted on the lookout history site.[^1]

A Facebook group post, with no date in the text we could read, says a Louisiana state forestry fire lookout is located on Barksdale AFB.[^3] No source we read describes the site today, including whether the tower still stands there or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^3]
