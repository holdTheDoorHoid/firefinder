The North Carolina lookout-tower list includes an entry named Long Shoal in Dare County.[^1] The sources we checked describe something else: a light station on a shallow shoal in Pamlico Sound, not a fire lookout.[^2]

Congress set aside money for a light at Long Shoal in 1824, and from 1825 a lightship served as the floating light there.[^2] After the Civil War, the Lighthouse Board replaced the vessel with a screw-pile lighthouse, built on iron foundations set into the muddy bottom of the sound. It was first lit on May 31, 1867.[^2]

Keepers lived in a dwelling on the structure. Thomas H. Baum, the keeper with the longest service there, was head keeper from 1905 to 1927.[^2]

On Christmas Eve 1920, a storm left a string of barges tangled around the lighthouse. Baum cut through the heavy cable that joined them, and the Secretary of Commerce officially commended him.[^2]

Long Shoal Light went automatic on February 17, 1931, and its fog bell was retired.[^2] By 1935 the screw-pile dwelling had been taken down, and a skeletal tower held the automated light.[^2]

Lighthousefriends finds no trace of the lighthouse at the site today.[^2] The list places its Long Shoal point well to the northwest of the position Lighthousefriends gives for the light, so the two records may not describe the same spot.[^1][^2]

[^2]: Long Shoal Lighthouse, North Carolina, Lighthousefriends.com, https://www.lighthousefriends.com/light.asp?ID=1914 (accessed 2026-10-21).
