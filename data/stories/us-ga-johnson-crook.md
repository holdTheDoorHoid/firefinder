A Georgia tower list places a Johnson Crook entry in Dade County, at 34.791667, -85.473889.[^1] Its elevation column reads 2036, and the list does not give a unit. The entry's only note is "topo 1946," which may refer to a 1946 topographic map, though the list does not say so.[^1] The list's legend says some of its towers were discovered on 1:100,000 USGS topo maps.[^1]

Johnson's Crook is a gap on the western slope of Lookout Mountain, south of Trenton.[^2] During the war, it was the lone route that gave wagons and artillery any real chance of crossing the mountain.[^2] A Union division under General James Negley crossed there on September 9, 1863.[^2]

A 2019 Chattanooga Times Free Press report says the Southeastern Cave Conservancy will manage close to 2,400 acres on Lookout Mountain and in Johnson's Crook, now protected as the Charles B. Henson Preserve.[^3] The conservancy's own preserve page marks an off-road vehicle as required.[^4]

No source describes what remains at the Johnson Crook lookout site today, and none says whether the listed spot sits inside the preserve.
