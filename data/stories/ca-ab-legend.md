Our records list a fire lookout tower named Legend in Alberta. The history below describes Alberta's lookout network in general, because the public sources we read do not name this tower.

Alberta's government publishes a map of its wildfire lookout sites. It places 127 of them in the boreal forest and along the Rockies.[^1] According to the province, every site has a lookout observer, usually working from April through October, who keeps watch for wildfire through the day.[^2]

In the boreal forest, steel was introduced for lookout towers in the 1920s and gradually displaced wood.[^3] In 1945 lookouts began using the Osborne, an instrument for pinpointing where a fire lies relative to the tower.[^3] Many of Alberta's operational lookouts are rebuilt on the spots where the first generation stood.[^3]

We found no source that describes the Legend site today, so we cannot say what stands there or whether the public may visit. Do not climb or approach the tower.
