The Blackpine Mountain lookout stood about nine miles northwest of Philipsburg, in Granite County, Montana, on the Deerlodge National Forest.[^1][^2] CCC workers were building the road from Philipsburg to the lookout station in 1938, and by November it was nearly finished.[^2] Forest Lookouts names the design as plan L-11: a 50-foot tower braced with cables, built of round timber, with an L-4 cab on top.[^2]

Dates disagree. Firelookout.com says the tower was built in 1933.[^1] Forest Lookouts places completion at the end of 1938 or early 1939, and quotes an October 1939 newspaper item reporting that a 50-foot tower had been erected, with a 14-foot-square house on top for the lookout man to live in.[^2] That item adds that the lookout man had to stay on watch every summer.[^2]

A 1977 Missoulian item, quoted on the same page, says the lookout served for more than 30 years as a fire-spotting post.[^2]

In October 1977 Richard Venable, the Philipsburg district ranger, said the lookout was being taken apart and would move to Missoula for display.[^2] Firelookout.com dates the move to 1978: the cab was dismantled and carried to the Missoula Smokejumper Visitor Center to serve as a display.[^1] The 1977 report said the top cabin would be exhibited at the Forest Service visitor center west of Missoula.[^2]

No source checked describes what remains of the lookout on the mountain today, or whether the public can reach the site.

The Forest Service says the Missoula Smokejumper Visitor Center is shut for renovation at present.[^3] It says people can ask about pre-scheduled tours by email or phone, and no source checked confirms that the cab is on display there today.[^3]
