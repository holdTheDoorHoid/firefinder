Firelookout.com lists a pole platform tower and cabin at Vaughn Point in 1929, and a two-story house there in 1942.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page gives more detail on the wartime years, when the point became an Aircraft Warning Service post. In July 1942 an inspector recommended moving the post from Iver's Peak to Vaughn Peak. The move was approved on conditions that the State of Oregon acquire the land and handle the slash.[^1] The post was activated on December 21, 1942, under the Roseburg Filter Center.[^1]

A September 1942 letter describes a 14-by-14 Aladdin building at the new location, held up until the slash at the top of the point was cleared.[^1] Firelookout.com instead describes a two-story house that year. The sources do not show whether these were the same building.[^1][^2]

The Coos Bay Times reported in July 1949 that John Alphonse "Jack" Scott had died. He had until recently been in charge of the Vaughn's Point lookout for the Coos Fire Protective association.[^1] Oscar Crites manned the point in May 1950.[^1] In October 1957, The World reported that a Mrs. Allen had come back from the lookout. In September 1958 it said she would take over the lookout for the rest of fire season.[^1]

In 1965 the Forest Lookouts page says the lookout was abandoned, and the land and house were deeded back to the Weyerhaeuser Company.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same abandonment year and calls the site gone.[^2] The Oregon lookout list also marks it as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the point is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
