The Hercules lookout stands in Mark Twain National Forest, in the Pondfork unit of Taney County, Missouri.[^1] Sources differ on its first year. The Forest Lookouts history gives 1935 for a 100-foot steel tower.[^1] The Forest Service says the tower was built in 1936 and is over 100 feet tall.[^2]

A 1954 Coast and Geodetic Survey note describes a four-legged steel tower with a square steel and glass cab, and gives its height as 100 feet (30.5 m).[^1]

In September 1936, recreation facilities were being completed at the tower. A hydraulic ram had been installed to lift water from a spring below it to a picnic ground, and the Forest Service planned its first picnic there for Labor Day.[^1]

The Forest Service still uses the tower for fire spotting and calls it an active fire tower.[^2]

Today the tower is closed to the public and fenced off, according to the Forest Service.[^2] A trailhead on the east side of the wilderness connects with the Tower Trail.[^2]
