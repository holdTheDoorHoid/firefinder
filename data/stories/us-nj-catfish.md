Catfish Fire Tower stands on Kittatinny Mountain in Warren County, New Jersey, on the Appalachian Trail inside the Delaware Water Gap National Recreation Area.[^1][^3] The 1922 papers also called the summit Blue Mountain.[^3]

The state's forest fire service opened a new 60-foot steel tower near Catfish Pond in July 1922.[^3] The NHLR says it replaced an earlier wooden tower.[^1] A telephone line was built to the site as well, which the state's annual report calls eight miles of new line.[^3] A July 1922 newspaper put the tower about five miles west of Blairstown, while the state's annual report said about seven.[^3]

By August 1924, the Easton Free Press reported that 1,023 people had visited the tower since it went up in 1922, and it named Norman Maring as the watchman.[^3]

According to the NHLR, a Forest Fire Observer is posted to the tower when fire danger is elevated, and the full-time observer post is vacant.[^1] Two 2018 visit reports differ: one found no staff present, and another found a man in the tower during the day.[^1]

The cab was rebuilt in 1993 on the same 60-foot Aermotor LS-40 tower.[^1] Wikipedia lists the tower at 60 feet (18 m).[^2] It was repainted in May 2022 for its 100th anniversary, and a 2018 visit noted warped angle iron on two landings but otherwise sound steel.[^1]

The NHLR registered it on December 15, 1997.[^1] Hikers reach it on foot, and a 2018 visitor called the walk about two miles.[^1] A fire road from Millbrook Road joins the route, and a 2018 visit report found no trail signage.[^1]
