Barlow Pass sits in Snohomish County, Washington, in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, at 3,222 feet (982 meters).[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb calls the spot Barlow Point, while the Register and Forest Lookouts use Barlow Pass.[^1][^3][^4]

A lookout cab went up here in 1935.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Panorama photographs were taken that summer, on July 30 according to Forest Lookouts, and WillhiteWeb says the photos were made on the roof.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb says a Forest Service ranger posted at the pass could walk up to staff the lookout during high fire danger or lightning storms.[^4] A June 7, 2017 visit report on the Register says the writer first came in 1956, when the lookout was staffed.[^1]

WillhiteWeb says the AWS used the lookout to spot aircraft in 1942.[^4] Forest Lookouts lists the site as activated at the Seattle Filter Center from March 20, 1942 to October 16, 1943.[^3]

The sources agree the cab ended in 1964 but describe it three ways.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The Register says it was removed that year.[^1] Firelookout.com and Forest Lookouts say it was destroyed,[^2][^3] and WillhiteWeb says it was burned down.[^4]

Today the sources disagree about what remains. The same visit report says the writer found no remains of the lookout, and that trees have grown so much the view of the valley and mountains is blocked.[^1] WillhiteWeb's photos carry 2014 captions, and its page tells visitors to look for four foundation corners of local rock and concrete near the rocky summit, one with a steel bolt attached.[^4] The Register's visit report describes a parking area off the Mountain Loop Highway, near the old guard station, and a one-mile trail with two forks. Take the right-hand fork each time.[^1] WillhiteWeb gives the trail as 1.2 miles and says a short gravel road leads to the lot on the north side of the highway.[^4]
