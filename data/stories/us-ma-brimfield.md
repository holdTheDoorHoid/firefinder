The former Brimfield Fire Tower stands on Steerage Rock, a glacial boulder that crowns West Mountain in Hampden County, Massachusetts.[^1][^4]

Sources disagree about when the first tower went up. The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says 1912, and a state heritage survey says 1911, when a fire tower with a glass-enclosed platform was erected on the mountaintop.[^2][^4] The register describes that first tower as 38 feet tall, with an 8-by-8-foot cab and a ground cabin for the operator.[^2]

A January 1915 newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page places a lookout at Steerage Rock and says he had reported many fires in every season since the station went up. The item gives no name.[^3]

The state bought the tower site in 1923 and put up a 68-foot steel tower in 1924.[^2] The Great New England Hurricane destroyed the lookout in 1938.[^3] Men of the Civilian Conservation Corps built a temporary wooden tower, and a new 68-foot steel tower with two-way radios followed in 1939.[^2]

A new 10-by-10-foot cab went up in 1967, and the tower closed at the end of the 1975 fire season.[^2]

The register says the structure is still up and serves as an antenna mount, though its cab has disappeared.[^2] A state heritage report says the tower no longer has its viewing platform and is seriously deteriorated.[^4]

Access is contested.[^4] A radio tower owned by the Massachusetts Turnpike Authority is protected by a chain-link fence on the rock, and the authority also owns an extra 1.8 acres at the crest, including remains of a fire tower.[^4] The town thinks of the rock as public property.[^4] The owner of the land at the lower end of the access road holds that the road to the top is private, and the report suggests a title search if informal discussion fails.[^4]

We could not confirm whether the public can reach the top today. Check with the Turnpike Authority or the town before going, and do not climb the tower or cross the fence.[^4]
