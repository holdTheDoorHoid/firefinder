North Mountain Lookout is a 41-foot timber tower with an R-6 cab on a summit north of Darrington, in Skagit County.[^1][^2][^3] It was built to watch the North Fork Stillaguamish valley.[^4]

The site started as a fire camp in the early 1960s, and a fire observer lived in a tent there.[^2][^4] The camp was the lookout's home through 1966.[^2] The current tower went up in 1965, according to TrailChick and Discover Darrington, though firelookout.com gives 1966.[^2][^4][^5] Its R-6 cab was a prefab design developed in 1963 for helicopter transport.[^4] The lookout stayed active into the 1980s and was occasionally used for emergencies after that.[^4] A 2006 visit report in the NHLR marks it unstaffed and cites a publication that lists it as emergency use.[^1]

Vandalism plagued the tower over the years.[^2][^4][^5] In 2009 the tower was put forward for removal and formally closed to visitors after vandal damage.[^4] Local residents formed the Friends of North Mountain in 2013.[^4] The NHLR registered the lookout on January 3, 2015.[^1] The restoration ran eight years and relied on over 100 volunteers.[^6] The Herald wrote that the tower would probably have been demolished without that effort.[^6] It reopened at a ceremony on Wednesday, August 10, 2022.[^6]

The lookout now works as a rental.[^3] TrailChick says it was listed for overnight stays in the summer of 2021.[^4] According to the Forest Service, the Friends of North Mountain operate it as a recreation rental under a special use permit issued by the Darrington Ranger District, and bookings go through the group.[^3] To drive there, head north from Darrington on Highway 530 for about a mile and turn left onto North Mountain Road. About five and a half miles on, that road becomes Forest Road 2811; a mile past that, a sharp right keeps you on 2811, and the lookout is another six and a half miles ahead.[^3] The Forest Service lists no potable water at the site.[^3] TrailChick notes that when the gate is closed, the lookout is a short road walk away, as it was on a July 2018 visit during restoration work.[^4]
