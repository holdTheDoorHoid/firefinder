A lookout went up on Beaver Lake Mountain in July 1910, at a cost of $308.39, according to a state annual report quoted by the Forest Lookouts site.[^1] The first tower there was a log structure, put up in 1910.[^2] A steel tower replaced it in 1919.[^2]

The sources disagree on its size. The Forest Lookouts page gives a 60-foot Aermotor tower for 1919,[^1] while a 1942 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, quoted on the same page, calls the all-steel lookout about 30 feet high, with an enclosed cab.[^1]

Other towers nearby made the lookout redundant, and the state closed it in 1945.[^2] After a brief revival of interest following the 1950 windstorm, it was closed for good in the mid-1950s.[^2]

The sources also differ on when it came down. Two accounts give 1977 for the tower's removal, and a 2020 hiking article says the observer's cabin went at the same time.[^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts page gives c.1985 instead.[^1]

No source describes what, if anything, is left on the ridge today. The ridge lies in the Pepperbox Wilderness, a part of the Forest Preserve with few official facilities.[^3] The trailhead is on Moshier Road, near the Beaver River.[^3] The trails are faint, and reaching the tower site means skilled off-trail route-finding.[^3]
