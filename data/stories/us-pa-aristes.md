The Aristes tower stood in Columbia County, Pennsylvania, near the community of Aristes.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists it as a steel Aermotor tower put up in 1919 by the AFPA, and says it came down in 1975.[^1] Newspaper notices from 1920 to 1976, transcribed on a lookout-history site, tell a more tangled story.[^2]

In September 1920 the directors of the Anthracite Forest Protective Association ordered the reconstruction of the Aristes fire tower.[^2] A Mount Carmel paper that month described a planned steel observation tower, 64 feet high on a 12-foot concrete base.[^2] By April 1921 another notice said a tower had been erected nearby, ordered after a serious fire the year before.[^2] The register's 1919 date does not sit neatly with these notices, so the first build year is unsettled.

A 1924 notice says the watchman had until then needed to call Mount Carmel for firefighters when a fire was found, and that a local fire company had been formed at Aristes.[^2] In 1926 state inspectors condemned the tower because its concrete piers were weak and its ladders unsteady.[^2] The Aristes towerman, Mat Smith, was put on patrol duty in the meantime.[^2] Sylvester Beaver was on duty in 1934, and by a 1947 notice he had manned the tower for 18 consecutive years.[^2]

In 1923 the tower was dynamited, and property stolen from it was recovered in Centralia.[^2] The state was prepared to press charges, noting that vandals had now ruined public safeguards for the second time.[^2] A July 1939 notice reports a fire found south of Centralia shortly after telephone connections were made.[^2] A 1947 notice credited Beaver with spotting and reporting many forest fires.[^2]

In 1927 the state announced an 80-foot steel tower at a new site near Aristes, to replace an older tower several miles east that had become unsafe.[^2] A March 1928 notice said the tower had been rebuilt that year and ranked among the state's highest.[^2] In 1938 the tower was still 80 feet tall, and a notice reported Beaver climbing its 108 steps several times a day.[^2]

The register gives 1975 as the year the tower was removed.[^1] A May 1976 state notice still offered the tower for sale to the highest bidder, who would take it down, so the tower appears to have been standing that spring.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, stands on the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors. A map database lists a "Centralia Fire Tower" in Conyngham Township, but its entry does not say whether that is this lookout or another structure.[^3]
