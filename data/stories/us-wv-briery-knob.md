Briery Knob rises in Pocahontas County, inside the Monongahela National Forest.[^1] The earliest record I found is a 1913–14 state forest warden's report, quoted on the Eastern lookouts page.[^2] It says a lumber company built more than twenty-five miles of telephone line to reach the Briery Knob and Job Knob stations.[^2] I found no source giving the year the tower went up or naming who designed it. The register does carry a 1914 photograph taken from a state Fish and Game Commission report.[^1]

By 1916 the station had a watchman, S.A. Sparks, who lived three miles away at Lobelia.[^2] He stayed at the station only at night, and only when fire danger was high.[^2] The station stood at 4,534 feet and was supervised from the Little Levels district.[^2] A 1926 entry says the tower had telephone links to two companies.[^2] In 1938 the forest supervisor said the road to the tower would be improved so visitors could enjoy the view.[^2] Nothing I found confirms that the work was done.

The register says the tower was dismantled and that portions went to the Nettie Volunteer Fire Department.[^1] A separate listing shows a standing tower at the Nettie fire department, coded as relocated from Briery Knob, and marks the original knob as gone.[^3] The minutes of the 2013 Eastern conference describe a Briery Knob tower sitting behind the Nettie fire department.[^4] That places a tower there by 2013, but I found no source that dates the move itself.

Today I found no description of the original summit, so I cannot say what, if anything, remains there or whether the public can reach it. Nothing I found describes access to the Nettie fire department or to any tower parts kept there. The register dates its own entry to January 26, 2017.[^1]
