The New London Lookout Tower stands in Union County, Arkansas, and the Arkansas Forestry Commission administers it.[^1] It shares its name with New London, a community that began with a wagon train in 1839 and was the main commercial center of eastern Union County during the Civil War and the forty years after.[^2] The railroad bypassed the town in 1902, after which the town shrank to houses clustered around a steepled church.[^2]

The sources do not give a build date. The Register lists the year built as unknown.[^1] The only description of the tower's construction is in OpenStreetMap, which tags it as lattice construction and as an observation tower.[^4] The Register gives its elevation as 268 feet (82 meters).[^1]

The sources do not name a watch crew, a staffing period, or any fire the tower reported.[^1]

Statewide background is better documented than the tower's own history. The Arkansas Forestry Commission was created by Act 234 in 1931, and the Encyclopedia of Arkansas says it built many more towers.[^3] The sources disagree on how many survive. The Encyclopedia of Arkansas counts forty-six still standing in 2023, while Preserve Arkansas puts the surviving number at about 40.[^3][^5]

The Register added the tower on April 16, 2023, on a nomination by Jason Johns.[^1] Its visit note, dated 12/24/21, reports that the tower still stands, appears to sit on private land, and had all its access roads locked.[^1] The sources do not say whether the tower is open to visitors or rented out.

Check with the Arkansas Forestry Commission before going anywhere near it, and do not climb a tower that is closed or private.
