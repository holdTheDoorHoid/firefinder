The state built the fire tower on Stratham Hill in 1931, on land that had been given to the town as a park.[^1] Edward Tuck gave about 70 acres to the town in 1905, in memory of his father, Amos Tuck.[^3][^4] The register describes a 53-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10-foot cab and a public observation platform at 45 feet.[^1] A 2026 newspaper article says the original structure was built of wood, so the sources differ on what the tower was made of at the start.[^1][^4]

In 1932 a local paper praised the tower, because the warden stationed there could spot fires in woodland within a few minutes of their start.[^2] In 1946 fire warden Herman Wilbur reopened the tower.[^2] The register says the state staffed it until 1976, and that the town has maintained it since.[^1][^4]

The staffing end date does not agree everywhere. A December 1973 Portsmouth Herald item says state use had been phased out after a longtime watchman retired; it says he had manned the facility for 16 years. A 1977 memorial notice gives his time on the tower as 15 years.[^2]

The tower has burned twice.[^3][^4] The 2026 article says arson fires hit the tower in the 1980s and then in 1997, and that in 1998 the town put up a steel replacement costing $20,000.[^4] The register gives a narrower account: the wooden cab burned in 1997 and a metal cab replaced it.[^1] The town's own page says the tower burned down twice and now stands as steel.[^3]

Today the tower is in town hands and needs work.[^4] A September 2023 engineering report recommended several fixes.[^4] In March 2026 voters signed off on $250,000 to protect the landmark, and work was slated to begin this fall.[^4] The plan covers concrete work, bolt replacement and repainting the steel.[^4]

Visitors can still climb to the observation deck at 45 feet, where the view takes in the Squamscott River and Great Bay.[^1][^4]
