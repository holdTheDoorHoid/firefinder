Cicero Peak lies about six miles south-southeast of Custer, in the Black Hills National Forest.[^2] An earlier wooden tower stood there first.[^2][^3] A 30-foot steel tower with an L-4 cab went up in 1939 to replace it.[^2][^3] The register and Wikipedia give a build span of 1939 to 1940.[^1][^4]

Elmer Jorgenson was the lookout named in the 1939 coverage.[^3] In 1941, Bryon Hazeltine was sent up to finish the season.[^3] June Johnston is the last person the register names as working the site.[^1] In July 1967 the lookout reported two spot fires caused by lightning, and a Custer district crew put both out.[^3]

According to the register, most of the forest's lookout towers were shut down during the 1970s as aircraft spotting took over, and Cicero was marked for closure in 1973.[^1] Forest Lookouts lists 1973 as the final season of staffing.[^3] Wikipedia says the tower was used until 1974.[^4]

After the tower was declared surplus in the 1970s, a Custer resident bought it, according to the register.[^1] The register then says Marine Life in Rapid City took it on as a tourist attraction, and that the exhibit shut in 1997.[^1] Forest Lookouts says the tower was hauled off the mountain on a flatbed trailer in October 1980, and that the attraction closed in 1997.[^3] Wikipedia gives March 19, 1980 as the removal date.[^4] SummitPost tells a different story, with a private buyer in Custer.[^5]

The peak is national forest land.[^5] SummitPost describes a rugged 1.6-mile road to the top, which the Forest Service closes to motor vehicles each year from December 15 to May 15.[^5] None of the sources we read describes what remains of the lookout on the summit, or where the tower stands today.[^1]
