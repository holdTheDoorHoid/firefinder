Dixie Mountain is a fire lookout in the Plumas National Forest in Plumas County, California.[^1] The Register puts its elevation at 8,070 feet (2,460 meters), but a 2017 rehabilitation report and a photo page give 8,327 feet, so the sources disagree.[^1][^3][^4]

The Forest Service started the work in 1928.[^2] Road crews started the access road to Dixie Peak that August, and the station was to go up that fall.[^2] The Register gives 1928 as the build year, but a July 1929 newspaper item reports that the Forest Service had just finished the lookout.[^1][^2]

The Register calls it one of the few surviving examples of a Region 5 design, Plan 4AR, built on a timber tower that is 10 feet tall and enclosed.[^1] A 1929 item said the lumber was carried 1.5 miles up the mountain.[^2]

Newspapers name lookouts from the early 1930s on.[^2] Myron Horn was to be the lookout in 1931, and again in 1932.[^2] A 1936 item said Clyde Robinson would be the lookout that fire season, and in 1938 Victor Johnson moved to the lookout station for the fire season.[^2] In 1940 Clay Bailey fell ill and was taken to Susanville for treatment, and Maynard Pattan served as relief lookout.[^2]

In August 1932 Horn saw a blaze that wiped out the old Burnham residence in Grizzly Valley, and he alerted the dispatcher at Quincy.[^2] A February 1944 Portola Reporter item said that Clay T. Bailey, a former lookout on Dixie Mountain, had been killed in action on the Italian front.[^2]

A contractor, Sustainable Group, Inc., did the on-site rehabilitation of the lookout, and its 2017 report describes the work.[^3] The old roof came off and a new one went on, with fresh sheathing and cedar shingles treated for fire resistance, and the catwalk, stairs and railing were repaired.[^3]

The Register says the lookout is still staffed when fire season is on.[^1] A photo page says it is normally staffed then.[^4]

The sources do not describe public access to the site or whether visitors may climb the tower. Check with the Plumas National Forest before any visit, and stay off any tower that is closed or marked private.
