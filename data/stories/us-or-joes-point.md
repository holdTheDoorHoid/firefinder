Joe's Point was an open platform on a large ponderosa pine in the Mount Hood National Forest, in Wasco County, Oregon.[^1][^3] The register says it likely predated the formal lookouts.[^1] Firelookout.com dates the platform to about 1930, and The Roaming Civic says the 1930s.[^2][^3] The tree's height is reported two ways: 70 feet by firelookout.com, and 100 feet in a June 1934 entry on the Forest Lookouts county page.[^2][^4] A 12-by-12 cabin for the lookout followed in the 1940s.[^2][^3]

In 1935 the site was listed as a supplemental lookout with its own telephone ring: four short and one long.[^4]

Firelookout.com reports that the platform seems to remain.[^2] A 2021 visitor found the cabin gone from the summit, though pieces of the platform and ladder were still visible. Visitors park at a rock quarry and then hike the old road for about a mile.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list marks the site as abandoned.[^5] No source I found says whether the public may visit the site.
