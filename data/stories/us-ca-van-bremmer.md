The Van Bremmer lookout went up in 1934 in Siskiyou County, California.[^1] The register describes a Forest Service cabin on top of a 20-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1] It was built under a cooperative agreement between the Long-Bell Lumber Company and the Forest Service, to detect fires caused by rail hauling of lumber on private forest land.[^1] A photo caption on peakbagging.com puts the building at around 1933, a year earlier than the register.[^3]

The register names the Shasta National Forest as the administering unit, but its location field says Klamath National Forest, and the compilation's entry is headed Klamath National Forest.[^1][^2]

The register says the lookout was staffed from 1934 to 1956.[^1] A Klamath forest chronology, quoted on a lookouts compilation, calls 1956 the last year it was manned.[^2] One story from the staffed years, dated November 20, 1936, says lookout Claire Wright spotted spotlight hunters from his tower room. He followed them and locked two of the hunters in the tower basement until a warden came.[^2]

The register says the lumber company switched to trucks for log hauling, which cut the number of fires its work caused.[^1] Vandals later damaged the lookout, and it was removed by 1970.[^1]

The register does not describe the site today.[^1] A photo dated July 19, 2012 shows one concrete footing of the former lookout beside a modern weather station.[^3] A benchmark placed in 1948 lies about 75 feet north of the old lookout.[^3] A 1948 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description says a good Forest Service road leads to the station from the town of Tennant.[^2] None of the pages we read says whether the public may visit the site now.[^1][^2][^3]
