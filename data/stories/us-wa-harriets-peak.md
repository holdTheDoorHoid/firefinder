Harriets Peak, in Chelan County, Washington, had a wooden lookout tower that the state Department of Natural Resources built in 1972.[^1][^2] The register calls it a 50-foot tower that saw only limited use.[^1] A 2008 chapter report, however, calls it one of two 60-foot DNR lookouts the state built, so the height is disputed.[^3]

The register says the tower partly took over from nearby lookouts on Beehive Mountain and Naneum Ridge.[^1] Ownership later passed to Longview Fibre Company.[^1] The 2008 report says the lookout stood on private land.[^3] We found no source that names who staffed the tower or any fire it watched.

The roof blew off the cab during the 2006/2007 winter, and by spring 2008 the structure was in ruins.[^1] Sources differ on how it came down. The 2008 report says it collapsed.[^3] A Washington lookouts page says it was found toppled over that year, and that loosened leg clamps may have helped.[^4] A visitor page says some people suspect it was brought down on purpose.[^5]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register listed the site on October 20, 2012, as US 1405, WA 305.[^1]

No source we read describes the view from the site today. An undated visitor page says the lookout is still on site, though in ruins on the ground.[^5] The same page describes a rough, rocky road and lists the site as a drive-up.[^5] The 2008 report puts the lookout on private land, and we found no source saying the public may enter, so ask the landowner before going.[^3]
