The Florida Forest Service built the Live Oak tower in 1953, and it took its name from a nearby cemetery.[^1] The register adds that it was among the final towers needed to complete fire-detection coverage across west Florida.[^1]

The tower ran for about thirty years. After that it was moved to standby and finally retired in 1983, a period when the land around it shifted from woods to farms and homes.[^1]

The city later bought the land from the state. A January 2006 News Bulletin item, quoted on a lookout-history site, reports a $67,000 price for five acres, two structures, a warehouse and the tower, and says the sale had closed about a year earlier.[^2] The park now sits within the city's Division of Leisure Services.[^2] According to the register, the city took over the tower and the forestry buildings to make an environmental center.[^1]

The register marks March 14, 2014 as the day the tower was listed, and counts it as the 1,000th fire lookout on the register.[^1] A January 2015 News Bulletin item says the tower now carries registration number 1,000.[^4] The register also describes the site as a loved landmark.[^1]

Today the tower stands beside the McMahon Environmental Center, on Butler Circle off Mapoles Street.[^3][^4] The city adds that the park came from the Florida Division of Forestry, and that several hundred children visit it every school year.[^3] I found nothing saying the tower itself is open to climbers, so the sensible way to see it is from the grounds.
