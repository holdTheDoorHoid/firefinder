Cooper Mountain had a lookout from 1920, when a crow's nest was used.[^1][^2] It sits in the Okanogan/Wenatchee National Forests.[^1] A log cabin went up in 1927, and a forest fire burned it.[^1][^2] The cabin was not replaced until 1932, when a new log cabin with a 45-foot wooden tower and a 7-by-7-foot cab took its place.[^1][^2]

In 1931 the station became the first in the Chelan National Forest with a road built to it.[^2][^3] A July 1932 item says 76 short-wave radio sets were being installed in the Chelan forest, and it names the Cooper Mountain station as one of the places receiving the 11 large sending and receiving sets.[^3]

A 53-foot timber tower with an L-4 cab was built in 1939, and the station was staffed into the 1960s.[^1][^2] In August 1937, a relief man from the 25-Mile Creek station was sent to run Cooper Mountain while its regular lookout was at a fire in the Columbia forests.[^3]

The tower came down in 1968.[^1] The register says it was removed, while FireLookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed. The sources disagree on how it ended but agree on the year.[^1][^4][^2]

Willhite visited in 2020 and describes the site as drive-up.[^2] From the Chelan-Manson Highway, the route runs north on Boyd Road to Cooper Gulch Road, following signs for the Echo Ridge Ski Area.[^2] He says the last hundred yards can be walked if a car has trouble with the ruts.[^2] A 2015 photo caption on his page shows firefighters holding an after-action review on the footings, which suggests the footings were still on the summit then.[^2]
