The Black Mountain tower stood in Pocahontas County, West Virginia.[^1] The state's Division of Forestry completed it in the 1920s.[^1][^3] A Charleston Gazette item from October 1926, reprinted by the Forest Lookouts site, said the tower had telephone links to central offices in both Marlinton and Richwood.[^2]

Nearby, a Forest Service roadside marker says the mountain and its neighbor Big Spruce Mountain took their names from the giant spruce that once grew thick on their slopes.[^4] The same marker recalls a fire in 1930 on the cut-over land, which it says was probably started by train cinders.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the tower as gone.[^5] A Mon Forest Towns guide calls it a former fire tower that sat just above today's Big Spruce Overlook, on the Highland Scenic Highway, bordering the Cranberry Wilderness.[^3]

The overlook has a short, accessible boardwalk that starts at the parking area and offers wide views. The guide describes no remains of the tower itself.[^3]
