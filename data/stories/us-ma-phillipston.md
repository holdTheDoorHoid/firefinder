The Phillipston Fire Tower stands in the town of Phillipston in Worcester County, Massachusetts, and the Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control administers it.[^1] The first tower on the site, a 68-foot iron structure, opened in 1928 on land that the Harvard University forestry program owned.[^1] The 1938 hurricane brought it down.[^1]

The site had a temporary wood tower during construction of the present 68-foot steel tower, which has a wooden cab and dates to 1939.[^1] The register lists 1939 as the build date, which matches the present tower.[^1]

According to the register, the present tower appears among the forest dioramas in Harvard University's Fischer Museum.[^1] Harvard Forest's own site calls the museum the Fisher Museum, and one of its dioramas shows a typical fire-fighting scene from the 1930s.[^2]

We found no record of named lookouts or notable fires at this tower. A visit report dated April 24, 2016 says the tower appears to be fully automated. On red-flag days it is not staffed, and it has substantial monitoring equipment on it.[^1]

The same report notes chipped paint in many spots and wood that looks weathered by the elements. It also describes considerable equipment on and around the tower.[^1] A locked chain-link fence surrounds it, and trail maps for Harvard Forest show the location.[^1] We found no current access information, so do not go past the fence. Ask the Bureau before going any closer.
