The McDade fire tower was the first fire tower built in McKean County, Pennsylvania.[^2] It stood on the McDade farm about two miles from Kane.[^2]

A September 1923 McKean Democrat item reported that the steel was understood to come from the state, and that public subscription covered the foundation and erection.[^2] The item described a 60-foot steel tower set on a concrete foundation.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association register calls it a steel Blaw-Knox tower built in 1923.[^1]

A September 1923 item said a ranger would be kept in the tower constantly during the fire season, with telephone links to other towers and towns.[^2] Newspaper items name the men in charge later on: a forest ranger in 1928, Fire Warden LeRoy Horton in 1932, and I.G. Bailey in 1944.[^2]

In 1928 youths who climbed the tower in the ranger's absence slid horseshoes down the telephone wire, wrecking it, the Kane Republican reported.[^2] In April 1932 the warden on duty spotted a blaze in the Cook Forest area, southwest of Kane.[^2] In May 1932 an item reported that the observer had spotted a fire near Altoona, 125 miles away.[^2]

In November 1944 a blaze of undetermined origin destroyed the cabin and equipment atop the tower. A local paper reported that work to rebuild would start right away.[^2] The register says the tower was removed before 1994.[^1] It lists no coordinates for the site.[^1]

A 1964 U.S. Geological Survey station description places a survey marker centered under the tower, about 500 feet northeast of a barn on the McDade estate.[^2] No source we read describes the site today, who owns the land now, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2]
