The summit of Mount McLoughlin, also called Mount Pitt, in Jackson County, Oregon, once held a fire lookout.[^1][^2] A cupola cabin went up there in 1917.[^1][^4] That June the Forest Service had ordered three standard lookout houses for the Crater National Forest, one of them for this summit.[^2][^3]

Each house was twelve feet square, with a six-foot cupola that held the fire finder and maps.[^2] Pack animals were to haul the lumber up the peak, and no timber in the building ran longer than eight feet.[^3] The house was finished in early October.[^2][^3] A 1916 report by Harold D. Foster had called a lookout on this peak impractical, citing deep snow, fog and cold, yet the house went up the next year.[^2]

Ernest W. Smith took the post first, in September 1917, and could send light signals as far as 20 miles.[^3] From 1922, J.F.G. Cone held the post for about eight years.[^3] In 1925 a Crater National Forest officer wrote that the summit was not satisfactory and suggested other peaks.[^2]

Storms and ice in 1930 weakened the house, so a slightly larger replacement on a cement and stone foundation went up in 1931.[^2][^3] James Radcliffe was named lookout in 1933.[^2] In 1935 the post went unstaffed for the first fire season in several years, and no reason was given.[^2] In 1936 the summit was officially dropped, and lookout duty moved to lower points such as Robinson Butte.[^2][^3]

A 1952 newspaper item warned that the building was in such disrepair it might fall on someone, and said forest officials would probably tear it down.[^2] In 1955 it was destroyed.[^2] Vandalism and misuse were blamed, according to a crew member who was sent up to push it over the side of the peak.[^2] The register says it was removed, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^4] The register also says the replacement was used for about one season, but the Forest Lookouts history names a lookout in 1933 and says the post was empty in 1935.[^1][^2]

Photo captions in the Willhite history show a foundation of the second lookout in 1973 and again in 1987.[^3] A hiking guide says the summit still has the foundation of a stone lookout shelter, and that cables and wires tied to the lookouts can be seen.[^5] The guide attributes the loss of the first wooden cabin to fierce winds, while the Forest Lookouts history gives storms and ice in 1930 as the reason for the replacement.[^5][^2] The summit lies in the Sky Lakes Wilderness and is reached by the five-mile Mt. McLoughlin Trail, which the Forest Service says climbs about 4,000 feet from the trailhead.[^6][^5]
