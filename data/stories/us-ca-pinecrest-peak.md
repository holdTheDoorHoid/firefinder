The Pinecrest Peak lookout stood in the Stanislaus National Forest in Tuolumne County.[^1] The register gives its elevation as 8,134 feet, while the Forest Service note it quotes gives 8,440 feet.[^1]

Newspapers from July 1934 reported that a new lookout station was planned for Pine Crest Peak, and a later July report said it would be finished that week.[^2] The register gives 1934 as the construction date, citing the 1986 Thornton Survey, and calls it a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 ground cabin.[^1]

The Forest Service note says the lookout was first put up as a National Defense Observation Tower during World War II.[^1] The register adds that it may have been used by the Aircraft Warning Service during the war, though it was not constructed for that role.[^1] Because the 1934 reports describe a new station that year, this story follows the 1934 date.[^2]

The Forest Service note says the lookout was staffed from 1939 to 1973.[^1] A trail guide repeats the 1939 to 1973 span, but also says a small fire lookout station stood near the peak "until about the mid 1970's."[^3] The mid-1970s date runs later than the 1973 end of staffing, and no source we read explains the difference.[^1][^3]

The Forest Service says the structure is "no longer standing," and the register lists its removal date as unknown.[^1] The trail guide says some metal set into the rock is still visible where the structure stood.[^3]

The trail guide describes the site as just below the summit of Pinecrest Peak.[^3] The hike is 11.2 miles round trip from Pinecrest and climbs about 2,500 feet.[^3] The guide also mentions a shorter route from Herring Creek Road, which it puts at 4 to 5 miles round trip.[^3] Nothing we read says the site is closed to visitors, and no source describes its condition beyond that metal in the rock.
