A wooden fire tower went up at the Narrow Hills in 1938, at the northern end of a trail that ran north from near the village of Love into the Fishing Lakes country.[^1] The tower was manned every summer until about 1950.[^1][^2] In about 1950 a steel tower replaced the wooden one.[^1][^2][^3] Its height is about 27.4 m (90 ft).[^2] A ranger's cabin followed some years later.[^1][^2] For a while a Torch River resident, Zac (Jackpine) Anderson, kept the tower supplied by way of the trail.[^1]

In July 1959 the tower was dismantled and moved six miles, to the Hanson Lake road, by helicopter.[^3] The Springfield Leader, as quoted on Canada Lookouts, called it the first time a helicopter had moved a lookout tower in Saskatchewan, and possibly in Canada.[^3] The plan was to put it up again on high ground near the north end of Narrow Lake, a site the paper said would be easier to reach.[^3]

A towermen list on the Firetowers of Saskatchewan site gives Ralph Johnson three towers, Big Sandy, Grassy Lake and Narrow Hills, for 1960 to 1987.[^4] It lists Sheryl Fabish at Narrow Hills for 1997 to 2001.[^4] A note on the same list says the last tower was manned after mid-August 2001, and that all towers were condemned and due for rebuilding.[^4] That note names North Cabin, not Narrow Hills.[^4]

An undated travel account describes a visit to the tower, reached by the road from Lower Fishing Lake.[^1] The account mentions a small museum set up in the original ranger's log cabin.[^1] No source describes whether the steel tower still stands today, or whether the public can reach the site now.
