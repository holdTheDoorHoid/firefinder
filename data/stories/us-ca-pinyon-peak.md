Pinyon Peak rises to 5,256 feet in the southeastern end of the Monterey district of the Los Padres National Forest.[^1] The register warns not to confuse it with the Sid Ormsbee lookout, which sits on a different Pinyon Peak in the northern end of the district.[^1]

The register dates the lookout to 1937 and describes an open steel tower on a 30-foot K-brace.[^1] A history of the forest's lookouts gives a 36-foot tower with a 14-by-14 cab, built by Civilian Conservation Corps crews.[^2] The two sources disagree on the height. A Santa Maria newspaper, quoted in the same history, reported in September 1937 that the final loads of steel, timber, cement and glass had been carried up the peak.[^2]

During World War II the Aircraft Warning Service staffed the lookout around the clock, and a cabin was added for living quarters.[^1][^2] The register says it was no longer staffed in the 1960s.[^1]

In 1977 the Marble Cone Fire destroyed the lookout.[^1][^2] The blaze lasted three weeks in August 1977 around Big Sur in Monterey County, and two lightning strikes set it off.[^3]

The register says nothing remains at the site.[^1] A trip report on Bob Burd's site describes four concrete pillars on the east summit, which the author calls the only remains of a lookout.[^4] The two accounts disagree. The same report describes a road that switchbacks up the south side of the peak.[^4]
