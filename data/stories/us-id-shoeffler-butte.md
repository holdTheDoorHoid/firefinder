A trail write-up names Shoeffler Butte as the high point of the northern loop hike through Heyburn State Park.[^2] The park's own description dates it to 1908 and calls it the oldest in the Pacific Northwest.[^3]

The Idaho Fire Lookouts page does not describe a cab on the butte.[^1] It lists a CCC tent and a tree platform in the 1930s, and says the site was abandoned in the 1940s.[^1]

The two sources give slightly different heights.[^1][^2] The lookout page gives 3366 feet, and the trail write-up gives 3,359 feet for the butte.[^1][^2] The lookout page also tags the site as Drive Up, while the trail write-up describes hiking the park's loop trails to reach it.[^1][^2]

A park update for the 2026 camping season says two major windstorms did significant damage and that logging was occurring in the park. It warns that trails still had extensive downfall and that not all of them were expected to reopen until mid to late summer.[^3] According to the park's page, day-use areas in Idaho state parks are open from 7 am to 10 pm.[^3]

No source read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit now.[^1][^2]
