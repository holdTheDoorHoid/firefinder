The Bald Mountain lookout stood on a summit in Clackamas County, about four miles from the west face of Mount Hood.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the U.S. Forest Service built its tower in the 1930s.[^1] A panorama photo of the lookout carries the date June 25, 1934.[^1] The WyEast Blog reads a 1911 USGS map as showing an earlier lookout on the peak, most likely a bare wood platform that a tower later replaced.[^2]

The WyEast Blog also describes the plans for the tower, which it dates to 1944. The plans call for concrete footings and a garage and storage shed at the tower's base.[^2] Most of the materials, including lumber, concrete and windows, were carried up the trails by livestock.[^2] The most recent map to show a lookout on the summit is a 1946 USGS map.[^2]

Later listings call the lookout gone or removed.[^3][^4] One guide describes traces of an old fire lookout on the summit and places it in the 1940s, which does not match the 1930s date in the register.[^1][^5]

Today the concrete footings appear to be all that is left of the structure.[^2] Three are loose and have been moved about by campers, and others remain in the ground, mostly overgrown.[^2] No source describes a tower standing on the summit now.

The summit can be reached on foot.[^5] Most visitors keep to the Timberline Trail and walk past the unmarked old path to the summit.[^2] That path remains open and lightly maintained.[^2] The old path leads to close-range views of Mount Hood.[^2] Both trailheads require a Northwest Forest Pass to park, and the hikes are usually open from mid-June to mid-November.[^2]
