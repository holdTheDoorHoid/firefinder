Burke Mountain Fire Tower stands on the summit of Burke Mountain, in Caledonia County, Vermont.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^2] The register describes a 54-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot steel cab, the fourth tower to stand on the summit.[^1]

The lookout history starts earlier than the current tower.[^3] Forest Lookouts dates the first lookout tower and its camp on the mountain to 1912, when Elmer A. Darling built them.[^3] The register dates the site to 1913 and places it second among Vermont's oldest lookout sites.[^1] The two sources differ by a year.

The early towers did not last. A February 1932 newspaper report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said wind had blown the tower down.[^3] A state report from that year gave a different cause, saying the tower collapsed under snow and ice, so the accounts disagree.[^3] An enclosed 45-foot, 6-inch steel tower went up on the same site in December 1934.[^3] The register says the wooden tower that stood before the current one was lost in the great hurricane of 1937.[^1] The Forest Lookouts timeline also gives 1937, but it quotes a September 1938 newspaper report saying a gale swept the tower from the peaks.[^3] Forest Lookouts dates the present 54-foot Aermotor tower, with its 7-by-7 cab, to 1938.[^3]

People staffed the tower in the 1940s and 1950s.[^3] A lookoutman is recorded on the mountain as early as 1916.[^3] In 1948 John Austin served as watchman and caretaker for the Burke Mountain area, and in 1951 John Dunphy started work at the tower.[^3] The 1957 to 1958 state report says the station had become full time, with a base radio installed in the tower.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page says the tower was discontinued in the fall of 1984, while the register says it was deactivated in the 1970s.[^3][^1]

In 1990 the FAA refreshed the tower's appearance in exchange for a microwave dish hung on it.[^1] The register added the tower on September 1, 1998.[^1] A September 2, 2006 visit report on the register's page answers "No" to whether the tower was staffed.[^1]

In 2023 Vermont Forest, Parks and Recreation staff, with a seasonal employee, replaced the steps, landings and cab floor with white oak on galvanized hardware.[^4] In 2025 the work added a safety railing to the cab floor and metal fencing to the sides, stairs and landings.[^5] A news report posted May 2, 2025 says the Burke tower would close to the public from May 15 to July 31, as part of a restoration project on state fire towers.[^6]

The register says the tower sits in Darling State Forest and is open to the public.[^1] That line is undated, and the 2025 restoration work came with a closure, so check with the state before visiting.
