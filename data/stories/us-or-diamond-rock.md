Diamond Rock stands in the Umpqua National Forest, about eight miles southeast of Tiller in Douglas County, Oregon.[^1][^2] A lookout was already at work there in August 1929, when a fire in a cabin near the station was handled by Eddie Hamlin.[^2]

The sources disagree about the first structure. The register calls the 1930 setup a rag camp.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page says an Aladdin gable-roofed lookout house was built that year.[^2] In 1939 Bruce Ferguson and Ermel Chaney took apart the old station to make room for a new building.[^2] All three sources date the new L-4 cab to 1940.[^1][^2][^3]

The register says the cab stood on a 100-foot rock pillar.[^1] Rex's page describes a 14-by-14-foot cab on five-foot concrete pillars, and it puts the rock at 120 feet high.[^2]

Lookouts worked the rock through the 1930s and 1940s. Ferguson was stationed there in 1935, and John Dean was on duty in 1941.[^2] In 1942 the lookout served as an Air Warning Service observation station, staffed by Alvia Wetherell and Elva Church.[^2]

In July 1944 a small lightning fire started about a quarter mile from the lookout. Mrs. Wetherall waited for daylight to look for it, and by then it had stopped burning.[^2] In August 1962 an earthquake shook the rock while George Hunt was on duty. Visitors felt it on the catwalk, and the News-Review reported no damage to the lookout.[^2]

The register says the site was abandoned in 1941 and restaffed eighteen years later.[^1] Firelookout.com says the same.[^3] Both the register and firelookout.com date the tower's removal to 1968, and firelookout.com calls it destroyed.[^1][^3] Rex's page lists the removal in 1969, so the year is not settled.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the rock today. The register has a photo captioned "Diamond Rock Lookout site 2008," but the caption does not say what it shows.[^1] We found nothing on current access, so check with the Umpqua National Forest before planning a trip.
