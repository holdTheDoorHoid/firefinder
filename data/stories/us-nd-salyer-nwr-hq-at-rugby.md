The Rugby Tower stands on the grounds of the Prairie Village Museum in Rugby, North Dakota, but it began its working life at the J. Clark Salyer National Wildlife Refuge. It is a 110-foot steel tower that started out as one of four at the refuge.[^1]

No source we could reach gives the year the tower went up or names who built it. The refuge headquarters did have a lookout, though. A historic Fish and Wildlife Service photograph of Dam 326 is captioned as taken from the headquarters lookout tower.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association pairs the Rugby tower with the headquarters site and lists the headquarters tower as removed.[^3]

The move is the best-documented part of the tower's history. In 1992 it was taken to the Prairie Village Museum. The move cost $12,000, and it followed a fundraising drive run by local residents.[^1] The West Lookouts directory records the same 1992 move from the headquarters site.[^2] The sources also differ on the refuge's name. The directory writes "Sayler," while the register and the Fish and Wildlife Service use "Salyer."[^1][^2][^4]

Nothing we found says who staffed the tower or which fires it watched during its years at the refuge. Its service history is largely blank, and the relocation is the main event on record.

The register lists the tower as standing at the museum, which also administers it.[^1][^3] It was registered on March 25, 2020, as US 1414, ND 2, with Lawrence Kuracina as nominator.[^1] The museum's visitor page describes its grounds as a village of more than 30 historic structures and exhibit areas, but it does not mention this lookout tower.[^5] No source we checked says whether the tower is open to climb, so visitors should ask the museum before assuming they can go up.
