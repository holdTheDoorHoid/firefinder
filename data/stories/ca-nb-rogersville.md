The Rogersville Fire Tower stood on a hill in the Miramichi River Valley of New Brunswick, at an elevation of 65 m (213 ft).[^1] Its register entry was added on June 30, 2026.[^1]

The register says the tower was built in the late 1950s or early 1960s to spot wildfires for the province.[^1] It was a 24 m (80 ft) tower with a cupola cabin on top. From the hilltop, the entry says, you could see as far as Prince Edward Island, and across to Mount Carlton, which has a lookout of its own.[^1]

The register says the tower was dismantled and its steel sold for beams in the early 1980s.[^1] The entry adds that the other fire towers in the province went the same way, except the Blackville fire tower, which it describes as still standing.[^1]

The entry does not say what, if anything, remains of the Rogersville site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1]

[^1]: Rogersville Fire Tower, Former Fire Lookout Sites Register, firetower.org, http://firetower.org/lookouts/can/new-brunswick/rogersville-fire-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-20).
