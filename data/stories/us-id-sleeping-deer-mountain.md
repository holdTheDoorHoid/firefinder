The Sleeping Deer Mountain lookout is a ground-mounted L-4 cab built in 1935, inside the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com says no other structure was ever raised on the summit.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a hip-roof L-4 groundhouse with a catwalk.[^1]

A Forest Service road, which the climbing guide says the agency built before the area became wilderness, gets a vehicle within two miles of the summit.[^4]

The lookout was last staffed in 1997.[^1][^2][^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Ed Bachel as known staff for 1984–1985.[^3] The register says the cab is now on emergency status.[^1] The climbing guide reports the lookout in poor shape and unstaffed in 2015.[^4] The register added the site on February 12, 2004.[^1]

Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the site as a hike-in with emergency status.[^3] The climbing guide says the trail starts at the end of the road and that a signed trail climbs to the summit in just over a mile.[^4] A separate Forest Service page lists a 0.5-mile trail from the Cabin Creek and Melville Ridge trails to the lookout. It notes that the trail is within the wilderness and that cellphones usually have no service.[^5] The sources do not describe public entry into the cabin, and the guide describes it as in poor shape, so ask the Salmon-Challis National Forest about conditions before you go.[^4][^5]

The sources do not agree on elevation or county. The register gives 9,772 feet (2,979 m) and Custer County, while firelookout.com gives 9,881 feet and Lemhi County.[^1][^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts also uses 9,881 feet.[^3]
