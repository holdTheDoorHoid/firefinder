Deer Peak was a fire lookout on the Lolo National Forest, about seven miles south-southwest of Alberton in Mineral County, Montana.[^1] It sat at an elevation of 6,719 feet.[^1]

The tower was a 30-foot pole structure with a gabled L-4 cab, and it went up in 1933.[^1] On September 22, 1935, a Missoulian item, as quoted on a Montana lookout history page, said most lookout men had come back in from their stations, and it named Jim Castles as coming from Deer Peak.[^2]

Firelookout.com dates the tower's destruction to 1964.[^1] A Montana lookout history page lists the site only as removed, with no date.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the tower as gone.[^3] The sources differ on how it left, but all three agree it no longer stands.[^1][^2][^3]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors. The Association's list gives the site's coordinates as 46.91304 and -114.57139.[^3]
