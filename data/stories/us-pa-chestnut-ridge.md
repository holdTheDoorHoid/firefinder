The Chestnut Ridge tower is a fire lookout in the Moshannon Forest District.[^1]

A StateImpact Pennsylvania report from May 2019 called it brand new. It entered service that spring, together with 15 other towers statewide.[^1]

The report shows district officials standing in the new tower beside an alidade, an instrument used to find where a fire is burning.[^1] The report puts the lookout towers two miles apart. The district manager, John Hecker, said the old tower was just ahead and nodded toward the fork that leads to the new one.[^1]

The same report describes an older tower in the district, the 98-year-old Knobs, as no longer in use. It still stands next to a cabin that spotters used to sleep in between their shifts.[^1]

The sources I found give no build year or current status for the Chestnut Ridge tower. The 2019 report gives its height as 80 feet.[^1] OpenStreetMap lists it as an observation tower with a survey date of 2023-08-18.[^2] Access rules are not stated in any source I found, so please check with the Moshannon Forest District before you go.

[^1]: With new lookout towers, Pennsylvania goes old-school to detect wildfires, StateImpact Pennsylvania, https://stateimpact.npr.org/pennsylvania/2019/05/10/with-new-lookout-towers-pennsylvania-goes-old-school-to-detect-wildfires (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Chestnut Ridge Fire Tower, OpenStreetMap node 11125347370, https://www.openstreetmap.org/node/11125347370 (accessed 2026-10-09).
