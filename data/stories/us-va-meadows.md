Henrico County counts the Meadows Tower, also called the Lower Henrico Tower, as one of two fire towers it put up in the early 1930s.[^3] Chicago's Aeromotor Company produced the galvanized parts as a kit that could be built to several preset heights, and U.S. Forest Service crews, usually five or six men, assembled them on site.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page calls it an Aermotor tower.[^2] The county gives its height as one hundred feet and its cab as seven by seven feet, with eight windows.[^3]

During fire season, watchers, many of them women, went up the tower twice a day to scan the area.[^3] Watchers located blazes with an alidade and reported them by radio to the Forest Service office.[^3] The sources we read name no particular fire this tower helped to fight.

The measurements disagree. The county and the Forest Lookouts page say 100 feet, while the National Historic Lookout Register says about 110 feet.[^3][^2][^1] A 1941 U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey station description, which appears on the Forest Lookouts page, also gives about 110 feet and says the Virginia Department of Conservation owned and ran the tower.[^2]

The county says the tower was in use as a lookout until the 1990s, and that its sister tower in the West End was torn down in the late 1980s.[^3] In August 2015, according to the Forest Lookouts page, the 100-foot tower was offered at a government auction, with a listing that gave its condition as scrap.[^2] The Register lists it as US 1489, VA 43, registered on January 8, 2021, and says it sits just off US 60.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list marks it as standing.[^5]

In 2024 Henrico County put up a historical marker on Elko Road, south of Williamsburg Road (US 60).[^4] The marker places the tower site across Route 60, just east of Meadow Road, and says the lookout served until the 1990s.[^3][^4] We found no source that confirms the tower's condition, owner, or public access after 2021, so its standing today remains unconfirmed. Visitors should check with the Virginia Department of Forestry or Henrico County before going, and should not climb any remaining structure.
