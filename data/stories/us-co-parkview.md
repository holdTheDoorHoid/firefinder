Parkview Mountain sits at the southern end of North Park, close to Willow Creek Pass, and is the high point of the Rabbit Ears Range.[^3][^5] The Forest Service built a lookout on its summit in 1916, a ground cabin with concrete walls and a wood frame.[^1][^2][^3]

The cabin was staffed early on.[^2] A November 1919 newspaper item, quoted on a Colorado lookout history page, says an officer was posted on the peak during the hazardous dry spells of summer.[^2] The register says the building has not been used since 1948.[^1] It also quotes a Forest Service comment that a radio repeater once sat inside.[^1]

Sources disagree about what came next. A 2008 Denver Post article said the building was no longer used as a lookout but still served as a radio repeater station for firefighters.[^3] The register's 2019 report rates the exterior poor to fair and says it has had no maintenance for a long time.[^1] SummitPost says the cabin fell into disrepair and has since been brought back to usable condition, but its text gives no date for that work; a linked page includes a 2007 photo of the restoration.[^4]

The cabin is still standing on the summit.[^1][^5] Visitors hike up to it.[^3][^5] Accounts describe starting near Willow Creek Pass on Colorado 125 and then following Forest Service roads and the Continental Divide Trail.[^3][^4][^5] The 2019 condition rating is a reason to be careful around the cabin.[^1]

The register gives 12,296 feet at the top, but its own description says 12,225 feet.[^1]
