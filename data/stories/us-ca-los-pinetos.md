The Los Pinetos lookout stood on a high point along the Santa Clara Divide Road, near Newhall, in the western San Gabriel Mountains.[^2] It was a cooperative effort between the U.S. Forest Service and the Los Angeles County Forestry Department, and the Forest Service opened it in 1934.[^1] One table in Angeles Adventures gives the dates as 1935 to the 1960s, but the same page's text says 1934.[^2]

The Angeles Adventures page disagrees with itself about the cab.[^2] Its running text describes a round, glass-enclosed tower of only twenty square feet.[^2] Its specification table instead gives a standard Forest Service cab, 14 by 14 feet, on an open timber tower 10 feet high.[^2] The two descriptions do not match.[^2]

The page lists Lee Park among its notable staff in 1938 and Bill Nelson in 1958.[^2] The 1958 name sits awkwardly with the same page's account that firefighters had given up the lookout by the 1940s.[^2]

In January 1937 a Western Air Express Boeing 247D crashed into the north slope of Pinetos Peak near San Fernando, in bad weather.[^2] The victims were taken to the lookout tower, roughly 100 yards away.[^2]

The page says the lookout did not last very long.[^2] Smog grew so severe that spotting fires became impossible, and firefighters had abandoned the lookout by the 1940s.[^2] By 1964 only the concrete slab that had supported it was left.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks the tower as gone.[^4]

The summit today carries radio and satellite towers.[^2] A Nike missile site called Los Pinetos was built in 1955 and 1956, according to the Historic American Engineering Record.[^3] Its launch site is about four miles from the peak and behind a closed gate, and the page says there is no public access to it.[^2] Angeles Adventures says the Santa Clara Divide Road could be driven up to Los Angeles County Fire Camp 9 in 2019, but it does not say whether the summit itself is open to visitors.[^2]
