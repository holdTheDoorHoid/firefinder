Ozark wildfires often raged out of control until the Missouri Conservation Commission was created in 1936.[^1] For decades, lookout towers were the most effective way to detect fires.[^1] Missouri's towers stood between 60 and 120 feet (18 to 37 m) high, and most were steel, though the earliest were wooden.[^1] The state's conservation agency owned 15 towers in 1941 and operated 102 in 1966.[^1]

The Ulman tower stood in Miller County, and the Missouri Conservation Commission was its agency.[^2] The commission acquired a 15.15-acre towersite there in fiscal 1950–51, and a steel tower was erected in 1950.[^2]

In 2013 the tower was sold at auction for $2,500.[^2] On May 21, 2013, it was taken apart and relocated.[^2]

No source describes what remains of the towersite today, or whether the public can visit it.
