The Kabekona tower stands on a ridge off Highway 200 in Hubbard County, Minnesota.[^1][^2] A 2011 newspaper report says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1934, according to historic accounts, and that it sits atop one of the highest ridges in the county.[^2]

A Department of Natural Resources (DNR) assistant area supervisor, Greg Vollhaber, told the paper that DNR records date the tower's acquisition to June 1, 1935.[^2] The article gives 80 steps up to a cramped spot used to watch for fires.[^2] The report does not name any watchers or any particular fire, and we found none in the other sources we read.

Vollhaber said the DNR retired the tower as a fire tower in 1975 or 1976.[^2] A Hubbard County commissioner recalled that it then became a late-night party spot, until its condition got so poor that it was gated off.[^2] The report says it was judged unsafe.[^2]

By 2011 the DNR had handed the tower to the state Department of Transportation, which was to use it for radio.[^2] The county board signed a letter of intent that lets the department proceed with demolition, to clear room for a tower in the state's 800 MHz radio system.[^2] We did not find a newer report saying whether the tower came down.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association still lists it as standing.[^3] That listing and the 2011 report do not agree, so its current condition is unclear.

As of 2011 the site was gated.[^2] We found no current access information. Do not try to climb the tower or pass the gate.
