The Spud Rock lookout point stood in the Rincon Mountains of southern Arizona, inside the Coronado National Forest.[^1] In April 1920 a woman was appointed fire lookout at one of the forest's important mountain stations, and a paper reported that she would move up to Spud Rock early in May.[^1] She was Mrs. Lyle B. Smith, wife of the district ranger in charge of the Rincons.[^1] A May 4, 1920 notice in the Arizona Daily Star described her as the first woman the Coronado forest had appointed as a lookout. It also said she had left for St. Paul for her mother's funeral and would be back within a few days to take up the post.[^1]

That fall, a Tombstone Epitaph item reported that Mrs. Smith staffed the high Spud Rock station for the fire season.[^1] She also spent time clearing miles of trail near her tower.[^1] One late night she made a ten-mile search, with a fire guard and tools, for a reported fire.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list shows Spud Rock as gone, with its type marked unknown.[^2] The same list has a separate Mica Mountain tower, also marked gone.[^2]

A hiker's trip report from February 2015, titled for Mica Mountain and Spud Rock, describes going another half mile from the peak to reach the rock.[^3] From the rock, the writer looked out over Tucson, with Mount Lemon far off.[^3] On the peak before it, the writer found views of the surrounding forest and the foundation of an old lookout tower.[^3]

No source we found describes what, if anything, stands on the Spud Rock summit today, or says whether the rock is open to the public.
