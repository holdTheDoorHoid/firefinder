Split Creek Point Lookout stood in the Payette National Forest, 21 miles northeast of McCall, Idaho.[^1] The lookout and its residence cabin were built in 1933.[^2][^3] The lookout's cab was a 7-by-7 Aermotor design set atop a 72-foot open steel tower with X-brace framing.[^2]

A firelookout.com listing says the lookout was abandoned for several decades before its tower came down.[^1] The Register and Idaho Fire Lookouts both date the tower's disassembly to 2008.[^2][^3]

The residence cabin stayed at the original site.[^2] The lookout cab went to the Central Idaho Historical Museum in McCall, where visitors may tour it.[^2][^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts adds that the tower itself was sold to a private buyer and moved to Lewiston.[^3] The firelookout.com listing describes the museum move as a hope.[^1]

The sources do not fully agree. A McCall visitor listing for the museum names an Aeromotor cab from Sloan Point and does not mention a Split Creek cab.[^4] Ask the museum to confirm that it has this cab before you visit.

Today the Register says the residence cabin remains at the original site.[^2] No source we read says whether that site is open to the public, or what else is there today.
