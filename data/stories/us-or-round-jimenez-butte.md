Round (Jimenez) Butte in Klamath County once carried a 100-foot steel tower with a cab at the top.[^1][^2] The Register says it was built in 1935, with three identical top sections and a middle section turned upside down, apparently to raise the cab.[^1] FireLookout.com also dates it to 1935.[^2]

Oregon Lookouts gives a different order of events: a 12-by-16-foot living cabin in 1935 and a 107-foot Aermotor steel tower in 1936, both built by a CCC-ID crew.[^3] The Klamath County blog gives the tower as 101 feet and dates it to 1936.[^4] Both list the lookout as Round Butte, not Round (Jimenez) Butte, so their link to this site rests on location and dates rather than a stated match.[^3][^4]

Oregon Lookouts lists the site under the Klamath Indian Agency and Winema National Forest, while the Register names the Klamath FPA as administrator.[^3][^1] In 1961, under the termination act, the KFPA took over running and maintaining the lookout.[^3]

The lookout saw some action. In August 1942 a two-acre fire at the Chiloquin Lumber Company's North Marsh unit was discovered from the lookout.[^3] In July 1968 the lookout reported smoke between Highway 97 and the railroad, and a KFPA fighter found a burning stump beside an abandoned campfire.[^3]

The state abandoned the lookout by 1974, when the Oregon Department of Forestry controlled it.[^3] The Register says the tower was removed in 1992, FireLookout.com says it was destroyed that year, and the Klamath County blog gives 1991.[^1][^2][^4]

A post in the Forgotten Oregon Facebook group says only remnants of the lookout are left, but the text we could read does not say what those remnants are.[^5] No source describes the site's access or anything else at the site today.
