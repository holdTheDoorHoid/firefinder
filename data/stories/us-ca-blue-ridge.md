Blue Ridge Lookout stands on the highest part of Blue Ridge in the Klamath National Forest, in Siskiyou County, California, at about 1,800 meters.[^1][^2] A lookout site on the ridge goes back to at least 1910, when a telephone line was being built to a station where a guard was to act as lookout.[^2] The steel tower came later.[^2] A May 1934 diary entry describes scouting an old sled road to see whether trucks could use it to haul steel for the tower, and the Register gives 1934 as the build year.[^2][^1] A road-trip guide repeats 1934.[^3]

The sources disagree on the design. The Register describes one of three 80-foot Aermotor towers with 7-by-7-foot cabs in the state.[^1] A 1952 survey description instead gives a 110-foot, four-legged steel tower topped by a 10-foot square metal house.[^2]

The sources read name no specific fire the tower watched. A July 1935 inspection found the station in fine condition, with its emergency telephone wire still being hooked up.[^2] In June 1943 two women went on duty at the station.[^2] The Register also links the site to aircraft warning work during the Second World War.[^1]

The Register accepted the tower on March 8, 1995, as US 114, CA 5.[^1] Its visit reports from September 2004 and October 2020 both note that the tower needed paint, that no staff were present, and that neither the sign nor directions to the tower were in place.[^1] The 2020 report also marks the wood, stairs and glass as not in good order.[^1] The Register's own description still says the lookout is staffed each year, so the two accounts do not agree on current staffing.[^1]

Today, the road-trip guide says the summit road is unpaved and needs a four-wheel-drive vehicle.[^3] The sources read do not say whether visitors may climb the tower. Anyone planning a trip should check with the Klamath National Forest first.
