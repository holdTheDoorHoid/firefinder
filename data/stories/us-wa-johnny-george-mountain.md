Johnny George Mountain rises to about 4,090 feet in Ferry County, Washington.[^1][^4] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the lookout in 1938.[^1][^2] It is a steel Aermotor tower, and the register calls it the only tower the site has ever had.[^1] The register names the Forest Service's Colville National Forest as administrator, but the trail guide and the fire lookout page place the land on the Colville Indian Reservation.[^1][^2][^4]

A 1937 newspaper item announced a 110-foot steel tower for George mountain that fall.[^3] The register and two other pages give the finished tower as 100 feet.[^1][^2][^4] A 1960 inspection described it as 110 feet, so the height is still an open question.[^3]

The register describes summer staffing at the tower as sporadic.[^1] In 1943 the Colville Examiner reported that many teachers were working as lookouts across the Colville National Forest, including a couple from Malott who staffed Johnny George.[^1][^3] One name tied to the site is Ramona Peone, who spent more than 40 years as a lookout here and at nearby Keller Butte, where a plaque honors her.[^1]

A 1960 inspection called the living quarters a 20-by-15-foot Quonset hut in fair to poor condition.[^3] It found the tower and its shelter generally in good condition, and said the dwelling cabin had outlived its usefulness.[^3]

In 2019 the Williams Flats fire, a 45,000-acre blaze that started in August from lightning, threatened the lookout. The lookout was protected.[^2] A visitor that year was struck by the tower and said the views from its base were still lovely.[^2] The register recorded the lookout on April 12, 2022, as US 1547.[^1]

Today the lookout sits on Colville Reservation land, reached by a long drive on back roads, and it is gated to prevent access.[^2] Non-tribal visitors must buy a recreation permit, which the trail guide gave as $80 a year.[^2]
