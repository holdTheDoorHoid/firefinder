Bogachiel Peak, in Olympic National Park in Clallam County, Washington, was the site of a lookout built in the mid-1930s.[^1][^2] The Forest Service recognized the need for it before the National Park Service took over the area.[^4] In 1933 the Forest Service bought most of the materials for a standard 14-by-14-foot lookout house and hauled them by packhorse to a shelter at Deer Lake, which was boarded up for the winter.[^2]

The Park Service took over construction in 1934.[^2] Its crew began leveling the rocky summit and digging foundations that September, and work paused for the winter.[^2] Construction resumed in July 1935, and the building was finished on September 10, 1935.[^2][^4]

The register calls it an L-5 cab, but firelookout.com and Rex's page both say L-4, and we have used L-4.[^1][^3][^4] On June 26, 1942, the site was put into service as an Aircraft Warning Service station, for summers only.[^2] In 1950 a young couple spent their honeymoon at the lookout, according to a story WillhiteWeb reprints.[^2]

The lookout was abandoned in the 1950s.[^1][^2][^3] The register says the lookout is now gone, and firelookout.com agrees.[^1][^3] WillhiteWeb has summit photos, including one captioned "Lookout site on November 29, 2019," but the captions do not say what is still there.[^2] We found no description of remains, and no source we read says whether the summit is open today.

The peak is reached on foot. The Washington Trails Association calls it a 0.3-mile side trip from the High Divide Trail to a forested summit, reached from the Seven Lakes Basin or Hoh River trailheads, and says camping is not allowed on the summit.[^5]
