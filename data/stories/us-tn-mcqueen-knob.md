McQueen Knob is a summit on Holston Mountain, and the Appalachian Trail runs over the top of it.[^1] Willhite says the trail reaches its final Tennessee lookout here, just before Virginia.[^1] A Forest Service lookout once stood there.[^1] U.S. Geological Survey parties visited in 1934, 1937, 1958 and 1963, and each visit recorded a 40-foot steel tower.[^1] A note from the 1934 visit also mentions a cabin with a fireplace and telephone poles.[^1]

The sources do not agree on every detail. Willhite gives the summit's elevation as 3,885 feet and lists the site in Johnson and Sullivan counties.[^1] Dunigan's list also gives 3,885 feet, but places the tower in Sullivan County alone.[^3] The shelter wiki's figure of 3,840 feet is for the shelter, not the summit.[^2]

A small emergency shelter sits near the old site. It dates from 1934, is nicknamed the Holiday Inn, and is one of the oldest shelters on the trail.[^1][^2]

Willhite lists the site as a former lookout.[^1] The shelter wiki says a fire tower used to stand nearby and was dismantled long ago.[^2] Dunigan's list marks the tower as removed.[^3]

Photo captions on Willhite's page name footings and a foundation, and say the trail runs between the footings.[^1] One caption refers to a directional benchmark in a footing.[^1] Willhite's access note says the trail goes over the summit.[^1] No source I found describes the summit beyond these captions and notes.
