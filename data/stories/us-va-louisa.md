Louisa's fire tower is a 100-foot Aermotor with a 7-by-7-foot steel cab, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1] The Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) built it in 1944 for what was then the Virginia Forest Service, now the Department of Forestry.[^1]

That date sits badly with the CCC's own timeline. Wikipedia says the program ran from 1933 to 1942.[^3] A 1991 state survey ties the growth in fire towers, from thirteen to one hundred, to the CCC period.[^4] We found no second source that settles the builder or the year, so the 1944 date should be treated as disputed.

The Virginia Forest Service was established in 1914, and the agency later built fire watchtowers in key locations across the state.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^5] Its August 2019 visit report said the tower appeared to have fresh paint and intact windows, but the stairs could not be judged from a distance.[^1] The same report marks the tower as not staffed.[^1]

The tower sits beside a Department of Forestry building.[^1] The 2019 report says that office was unmanned and that a sign listed cell phone numbers to call for help.[^1] It also noted a housing development going up next to the Department of Forestry property.[^1] We found no visitor rules, so treat access as restricted and call the Department of Forestry before going near the site. We found no rental listing.
