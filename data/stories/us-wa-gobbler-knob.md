Gobbler Knob is in Grays Harbor County, about six miles west-northwest of Lake Quinault.[^1] Before any cab went up, the spot held a camp from the 1940s.[^2] In 1960 an 8-by-8-foot cab and a trailer were built there, and the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR is listed as the administrator.[^2][^1]

Willhite thinks the cab was likely a mobile trailer lookout.[^3] He names Gladys Bertrand as the lookout in 1962 and 1963, and he calls 1963 the last year for the site.[^3] A 1964 newspaper item quoted on a Washington lookout list, however, said Gobbler Knob was one of seven lookouts to be manned that June.[^4] The sources disagree on the last year.[^3][^4]

The cab and trailer were gone by 1966, according to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com.[^2][^1] The Washington lookout list marks the site as removed.[^4]

Willhite found no sign of the lookout on the summit when he visited.[^3] He reports that the top had been logged, with the north half cut around 2010 and the south half in 2016.[^3] He also says the land belongs to the Quinault Tribe, so visitors now need permission.[^3] The route he describes starts from gravel roads off Highway 101, a walk of half a mile or less with about 150 feet of climb.[^3]
