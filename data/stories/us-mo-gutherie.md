In 1972 the 120-foot (37 m) steel fire tower was taken down near St. Charles, moved by truck and re-erected just south of Gutherie, on Callaway County Route J.[^1][^3]

The tower came from the August A. Busch site, where a towerman's house had been finished by fiscal 1951-52.[^1][^2] The annual report for fiscal 1972-73 says the Busch tower was taken apart and set up again at Gutherie to replace the wooden Reform tower, which was eliminated.[^1]

The Reform tower, built in 1947, was a 60-foot (18 m) wooden lookout that had become rotting and unsafe.[^3] The Missouri Conservation Commission was the agency, and the Reform and Gutherie sites were both in its Daniel Boone Forest Fire Protection District.[^1]

Eugene McCormack, who had been the towerman at Reform since it was built, was assigned to the new Gutherie tower.[^3] The Gutherie tower site received special landscape grading in fiscal 1973-74.[^1]

On July 1, 1974, the tower was set on fire.[^1] By the end of August, an 18-year-old from Fulton had entered a guilty plea to a charge of arson of state property over the incident.[^1] The county prosecutor, Gene Hamilton, said the youths apparently broke in and decided to set the tower alight as a prank.[^1]

The cab was replaced in fiscal 1974-75.[^1]

No source I could read describes the Gutherie site today. It is not known whether the tower still stands there or whether the ground is open to visitors.
