The Hovland lookout was an 80-foot ladder tower, erected in 1931 about three miles inland from the hamlet of Hovland in Cook County.[^1][^2] A visitor's photo log called it a ladder tower with an octagonal cab.[^3]

The spot is on a plateau that drops steeply on either side.[^4] A North Shore guide says that is why this site was chosen for a fire tower.[^4] A county survey lists the owner as the Minnesota Forest Service.[^1]

A visit logged on May 5, 2007 found the tower not in good repair, and the writer was not sure it was original to the location.[^3]

The tower was removed in the summer of 2017.[^4][^1]

Today the site lies inside a scientific and natural area that the Grand Portage State Forest encloses, and the old-growth northern hardwood forest is still there.[^4][^5]

The tower is gone, so there is nothing to climb.[^2][^4]
