Winfall's fire tower was a 99-foot 9-inch (30.4 m) tower in Perquimans County, erected in 1944.[^1] The State Forest Service built it under a cooperative arrangement with the U.S. Forest Service.[^1]

Its job was watching for smoke. A 1959 notice in the Perquimans Weekly said smoke seen from the Winfall tower would be checked by a forest ranger.[^2] A 1999 article in the same paper said that for more than 50 years the tower had served as a lookout post for smoke and blazes.[^3]

Modern life caught up with it. The paper blamed cell phones, CB radios and newer communication methods for making the tower unnecessary.[^3] About five years earlier it had been condemned, because its frame was rusting and its wooden steps had decayed.[^3]

Demolition crews took it down in 1999, beginning with the top section.[^3] A North Carolina tower list still gives the Winfall site as 36.2125, -76.43333.[^4] None of these sources describes what remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
