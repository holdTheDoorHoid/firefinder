The Snoqualmie lookout stood on a hill about 16 miles southeast of Monroe, in King County.[^1][^2] It was on Weyerhaeuser's Snoqualmie Tree Farm.[^1]

Washington's Department of Natural Resources put it up in 1942: a 50-foot pole tower topped by an L-6 cabin.[^1][^2][^3] Rex's Fire Lookout Page dates the first tower earlier.[^4] It quotes a 1939 Washington Forest Fire Association report describing a 60-foot tower erected on Section 9 that year, with the lookout man housed in a tent.[^4] Willhite Web also says a lookout man stayed in a tent in 1939, but it places the tower's construction in 1942.[^3] A ranger's cabin went up at the site in 1940.[^3][^4]

From March 9, 1942, to October 16, 1943, the site served as an Aircraft Warning Service post, with the existing lookout structure as its observation post.[^4] The state kept it for fire detection afterward.[^4]

Former Fire Lookout Sites dates the tower's removal to 1965, shortly after the Cascade Lookout was built at a higher elevation on the same tree farm.[^1][^3] firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2]

No standing structure is described at the site today.[^1][^2][^3] Willhite Web reports that a late-1990s timber harvest removed or buried what remained of the foundations, and that a few logging cables still sit on top.[^3] Former Fire Lookout Sites, whose photos are captioned 2000 and 2001, recorded only a wire in the brush as a remnant.[^1]

Willhite Web traces the land from Weyerhaeuser to Hancock Timber in 2003 and then to Campbell Global in 2015.[^3] Those later owners require a non-motorized day-use permit, which Willhite Web priced at $8 in 2016.[^3] The route is 9 miles one way from North Bend, and its last half mile is overgrown and must be walked.[^3]
