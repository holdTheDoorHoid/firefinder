The Hamma Hamma Guard Station stands in Olympic National Forest beside the Hamma Hamma River, in Mason County, Washington.[^1][^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1936 and 1937.[^1][^3]

A 1936 letter describes plans to build a combined forest guard station and lookout, and a February 1937 letter refers back to those plans.[^4] The letter called the area blind, so the proposed design pushed the living room out toward the brow of the bench to gain visibility through added windows.[^4] The living room still has a hexagonal bay window that looks out over the Hamma Hamma River drainage.[^3]

The same papers say the watershed was restricted during fire season by a gate below the station. Anyone with a permit needed the gate unlocked, and a guard on patrol would have to leave his post and walk down to do it.[^4] We found no record of named guards or of specific fires the station watched.

The Forest Service's own history says the station served as an administrative site for its fire and trail crews.[^3] That page also says the building is being nominated to the National Register of Historic Places. We did not find a listing record in our sources.[^3]

The building still stands, and the Forest Service rents it to guests as a vacation cabin.[^4] Vehicle access may be limited in the winter months.[^3] A short gated road climbs to the building, and guests get through the gate, which may or may not be open.[^4]

A trail from the cabin follows the river.[^3] One guide calls it the Hamma Hamma Living Legacy Trail, and the Forest Service calls it the Living Legend Trail.[^1][^3] The trail is 1.8 miles round trip, with interpretive signs about the work of the CCC.[^1][^3] Because the cabin is a rental, please book a stay or check with the Forest Service before you go, and do not count on being let through the gate.
