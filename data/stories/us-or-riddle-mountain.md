Riddle Mountain Lookout stands in Harney County, Oregon, on land administered by the Bureau of Land Management.[^1][^3] The Register describes a two-story live-in cab of concrete block and wood frame, built in 1964.[^1] Forest Lookouts calls it a BLM-style structure, and firelookout.com calls it a relative latecomer compared with other lookout sites.[^3][^2] The Register registered the site on January 3, 2013, nominated by Howard Verschoor.[^1]

Several listings describe emergency use.[^1][^2] The Register says the cab is staffed during emergencies, and firelookout.com says it is available for emergencies.[^1][^2] When Cheryl Hill visited on May 28, 2022, she listed the lookout as no longer staffed and said the emergency use described elsewhere was clearly no longer the case.[^4]

None of the four sources names a fire that the lookout reported, and none records it being moved, burned or restored.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The 2022 visit found the lookout and its downstairs quarters uninhabitable, one window shutter missing, and no firefinder inside.[^4] A camera had been installed, so the site looks remotely monitored.[^4]

Getting there means gravel and dirt roads over BLM grazing land, with several gates.[^4] The 2022 visitor parked at the fourth gate and walked about 2.8 miles, with 1,600 feet of elevation gain, to reach the lookout.[^4] No source says whether the public may enter the building.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Given the 2022 findings, treat it as a look-from-outside stop and check with the Bureau of Land Management before any trip.[^1][^4]
