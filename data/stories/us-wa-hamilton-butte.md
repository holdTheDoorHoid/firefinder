Hamilton Buttes sit in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest in Lewis County, Washington.[^1][^2][^4] The register puts the lookout site at 5,676 feet, while Firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb give 5,772 feet.[^1][^2][^4]

In June 1929 the Centralia Daily Chronicle reported plans to erect a tower on the buttes that July and August.[^3] The Sunday Oregonian said fire finders would be installed in each planned tower.[^3] WillhiteWeb says a lookout cab was built there in the summer of 1929, and the Forest Service also dates the lookout to 1929.[^4][^5] Firelookout.com instead lists a cab in 1924 and an L-4 cab in 1938, so the early dates do not line up across sources.[^2][^4][^5]

The lookout was abandoned in the 1960s.[^5][^4][^2] The FFLOS listing types the site as Ground and marks it gone.[^6]

No source I read describes the lookout cab as it stands today.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6] The Forest Service says sections of the old phone line are still left along Trail 109, and that the line once linked the lookout with the world below.[^5] WillhiteWeb calls Hamilton Buttes a former lookout site with striking views and gives the one-way hike as 1 to 1.5 miles.[^4] It says most cars should be able to make it, though the road is deteriorating quickly.[^4] The Forest Service says the nearby Trail 109 is open to motorcycles, hikers, horses and bicycles.[^5]
