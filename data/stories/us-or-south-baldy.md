South Baldy is a fire lookout in Wheeler County, Oregon, administered by the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^1] Fire detection at the site goes back to at least 1936. That August a radio set was installed on Baldy Mountain near Waterman, where Earl Huff, the forest guard, could broadcast fire details to any part of four national forests within a few minutes.[^1][^2]

By 1956 the lookout house had been pulled apart by wind, and the district's annual report said a new lookout was needed.[^2] The current tower was constructed in 1958 and 1959.[^1] A 1958 report describes a 40-foot tower with a 14-by-14 cab that was about 40 percent done that season, with about half the job finished by November 1.[^2] The 1959 report says work resumed in April, and the structure was in use by the fire season.[^2] Sources disagree on the height. Firelookout.com lists a 50-foot wooden tower in 1936 and a 54-foot wooden ODF tower in 1959.[^3]

The register says the site was staffed to at least 1964.[^1] The Oregon Lookouts page's 1964 entry counts eleven first discoveries and calls the season busy.[^2]

The register listed the lookout in July 2022 as US 1640, OR 158, and a lookout list shows it as standing.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com says it is still there and adds, "No public access."[^3] The register names the Oregon Department of Forestry as administrator, but the sources do not say whether the public may reach the tower.[^1] Among the sources read, only firelookout.com states that there is no public access.[^3] Check with the Oregon Department of Forestry before planning a visit, and do not try to climb the tower.
