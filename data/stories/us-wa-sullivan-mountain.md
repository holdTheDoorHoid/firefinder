Sullivan Mountain stands in the Colville National Forest, about six miles east-northeast of Metaline Falls, on the edge of the Salmo-Priest Wilderness Area.[^1][^2] The first lookout here was a cupola cabin, built in 1926.[^1][^2][^3] In 1935 a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced it.[^1][^2][^4] The present tower went up in 1959: a treated timber tower topped by an R-6 flat-roofed cab.[^1][^2][^3] Sources disagree on its height. The register and Fire Lookout say 20 feet.[^1][^2] The Washington lookouts history site says 22 feet, and TrailChick gives both figures.[^3][^4] The history site also says final completion came in 1960.[^4] Fire Lookout lists Laurence Davies among its known lookout personnel for 1959.[^2]

The register added Sullivan Mountain on March 24, 2020.[^1] The tower was last used in the 1970s and is now abandoned.[^1][^2][^3] In 2002 a Colville National Forest archaeologist called the Sullivan and Salmo Mountain towers badly deteriorated.[^5] An Associated Press report that year said the Forest Service must also consider removing towers before they become a hazard.[^5]

The upper stairs have been removed to limit access and deter vandalism.[^1][^3] The road is gated about half a mile below the summit, and TrailChick describes the last stretch as an easy short road walk.[^3] TrailChick, which visited in September 2018, calls the road one of the highest in Washington, recommends high-clearance four-wheel drive, and found the summit views worth the trip.[^3]

We found no source describing a rental or a volunteer staffing program for this tower.
