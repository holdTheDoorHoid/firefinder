A fire observation station called the Number Four Fire Tower once stood in Lewis County, New York, southwest of the Number Four area.[^1] The Fisher Forestry and Realty Company erected it in 1928 with help from the state Conservation Department.[^2] It was an Aermotor LX25 whose landings were linked by ladders instead of stairs, and it had a glass-enclosed observatory.[^1][^2]

The sources do not agree on its height. Newspapers from July 1928 said about 80 feet, Wikipedia says 75 feet, and the DEC says the original was 90 feet tall.[^2][^1][^3]

The landowner owned and ran the tower until 1945. That year the Conservation Department took charge of it.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history adds that the duties of the Beaver Lake Mountain lookout moved to Number Four that year.[^2] Accounts differ on the stairs. A 2019 Facebook post quoted in a 2023 newsletter dates the switch to 1945, while Wikipedia says it came around 1960.[^4][^1]

The land with the cabin and tower passed from the landowner to the state in 1958.[^1] Operations ended in 1982.[^2] Wikipedia says the tower shut down in the early 1980s and was afterward taken apart and removed.[^1] The observer's cabin went to Nicks Lake Campground around 1984.[^1] In 1987 the tower was removed, mainly because of extensive vandalism.[^2]

The upper section now stands at the DEC's Lowville Forestry Demonstration Area, where visitors may climb to the observation cabin at the top.[^3] The DEC says the section was displayed near the area's entrance, while Wikipedia says it was used to build a demonstration tower beside the DEC office in Lowville.[^3][^1] A 2018 visit account quoted in the newsletter says the tower was originally 75 feet tall and has since been cut to 25 feet.[^4]

Wikipedia says nothing is left at the former site apart from the tower's footings.[^1] A visit reported in the newsletter found a marked primitive campsite just off the Number 4 road, where the writers suspect the old observer's cabin may have stood.[^4]
