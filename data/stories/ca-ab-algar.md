Alberta's wildfire register dates the present Algar tower to 2008.[^1] It is a steel tower, 30 m (98 ft) tall, standing at 751.3 m (2,465 ft) above sea level.[^1]

Alberta says each of its lookout sites is staffed by a lookout observer, generally from April to October.[^2] A newsletter item from the Lookout Network, dated November 1995, reports that heavy fire activity in June led to the evacuation of three steel lookout towers, which later burned over.[^3] At Algar, the heat was so intense that aluminum bracing pipe melted, and the engine house was destroyed completely.[^3] A photograph of Algar Tower, dated 1995, appears in a Facebook post by the Alberta Wildland Firefighters group.[^4]

The newsletter says the tower was repaired by mid-July and the lookout was back in service.[^3] If the register's 2008 date is right, the tower standing there now went up after that fire.[^1]

The register lists Algar as active, with a weather station on site and access coded "Drive".[^1] No source here describes public visits, so treat it as a working fire-watch tower rather than a place to visit.
