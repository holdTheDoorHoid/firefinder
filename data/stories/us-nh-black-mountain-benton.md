Black Mountain stands in Benton, New Hampshire, and the White Mountain National Forest now covers its summit.[^1][^2] The site was first used as an observation point in 1911.[^2] A new and improved watch tower went up in the 1915-1916 biennium.[^2] In the 1926 season the state forestry department erected a steel tower with a wooden house on top.[^2] It replaced an older wooden tower, the first observation tower built when the state set up its forestry department, which had grown unsafe with age.[^2] The watchman's cabin was enlarged at the same time, with a new room on one end.[^2]

By 1927 Herbert L. Beamis had been the watchman there for four years.[^2] In 1934 a lightning bolt melted about 2,000 feet of the telephone line that ran to the station.[^2] The station was cut off from the outside world until new line was laid.[^2] The tower came through the strike unharmed, but the watchman's wife was stunned.[^2] Maud Bickford returned to a station on the mountain in 1944, a job she had also held the year before.[^2]

The Great New England Hurricane of 1938 damaged the lookout.[^2] In 1939 the White Mountain National Forest took over operation of the tower from the state, according to a report in the Portsmouth Herald.[^2] In 1941 a 28-foot timber tower with a cab of the L-4 type went up.[^2]

The lookout was discontinued and destroyed in 1978.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^3] Wikipedia's article on the state forest describes a Black Mountain lookout station as "no longer present."[^1]

A hiking blog's trip report starts its climb from the trailhead on Howe Hill Road in Benton.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.
