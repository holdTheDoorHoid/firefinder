The Dutchtown lookout stood on a high wooded ridge about seven miles north-northeast of Minden, in Webster Parish.[^1][^2] The Coast and Geodetic Survey first observed the station in 1950 and recorded a steel tower on four legs that carries a cab, standing 100 feet overall.[^2]

A 1954 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, puts the tower on a pine-covered rise a few miles from Minden.[^1] It names Gus Nathanial White as towerman, a job he had held for seven years.[^1] He lived in a state-supplied house directly across the road.[^1]

From the six-foot-square cabin at the top, White could see smoke across nearly every part of Webster Parish and in some areas of Bossier, Caddo, Claiborne and Bienville parishes.[^1]

In the federal survey's NAD 83 position, the mark sits at about 32 degrees 41 minutes 44 seconds north and 93 degrees 14 minutes 21 seconds west.[^2] It lies 100 feet north of the center of Dutchtown Road.[^2]

The federal survey record, retrieved on October 21, 2026, shows no condition report after the 1950 observation.[^2] No source we read says whether the tower still stands, what remains of it, or whether the public can reach the site today.[^1][^2]
