Murphy Dome is a mountain, 2,930 feet (893 m) tall, about 20 miles northwest of Fairbanks.[^2] The Air Force began building a radar station on the mountain in 1950, and the facility was finished by autumn 1951.[^2]

The Bureau of Land Management used a lookout tower on Murphy Dome in 1970.[^1] That year's Alaska fire report lists two lookout towers in service near Fairbanks, one at Murphy Dome and one at Birch Hill.[^1] The same report describes fixed observation towers as a particular difficulty in Alaska.[^1] The terrain often did not give lookouts broad views, and fixed towers were close to useless in severe smoke.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Alaska page is marked as under construction.[^3] It reports that the state's lookouts are poorly documented, but that two may have existed near Fairbanks.[^3]

Murphy Dome Road gives easy access to the mountain.[^2] Hikers and berry pickers visit in summer, and aurora watchers come in winter.[^2] Most of the radar station's buildings were demolished and buried in 1987.[^2] Today a single radar dome, plus its support facilities, is all that remains.[^2] Most of the summit is open to roaming, but the active radar facility lies in a restricted area, and the article's advice is to photograph without trespassing.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, of the fire tower remains on the mountain today.
