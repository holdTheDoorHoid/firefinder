Scott was a fire lookout in Rusk County, Wisconsin, at about 45.535 N, 90.989 W.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wisconsin list marks its tower as gone.[^1]

Wisconsin's lookouts worked as a network.[^2] When a lookout spotted smoke, they used a compass table in the middle of the cab to fix its position, then called the azimuth and distance to a dispatch center.[^2] Other network towers also picked up the same smoke, so the dispatch center could triangulate where it was.[^2] At its peak the network numbered 119 towers, and many surviving towers date from the 1930s, when Civilian Conservation Corps workers built them.[^2]

Statewide, 72 towers still in service were taken out of use in 2015.[^2] An NHLR entry for the Camp Douglas lookout gives 2015 as the year the state's network ended.[^3] Wikipedia gives 2016 as the year the state chose to end operations at its final 72 active towers, so the sources differ on the year.[^4]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the Scott site today, or whether the public may visit it.
