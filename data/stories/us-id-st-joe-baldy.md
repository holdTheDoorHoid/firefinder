St. Joe Baldy has had fire lookouts since the 1920s, when the Forest Service ran a simple camp on the summit.[^2] A wooden L-4 cab went up there in 1935.[^2] In 1954 the agency replaced it with a new structure built on a nearby, slightly lower summit about a mile to the southwest: a Region 1/L-4 cab set on a ten-foot concrete block tower.[^1][^2] A Sandpoint newspaper from that August mentions a Mr. Larson "building a Lookout station for the forestry department" at St. Maries, which lines up with the construction timing.[^4]

Staffing records are thin, but Idaho Fire Lookouts and Firelookout.com agree on at least one name: a Mr. and Mrs. Warren Hollenbeck watched the tower in 1960.[^2][^3] A 1960 Idaho State Journal piece notes that St. Joe Baldy carried a "high lookout" that was expected to bring the state $2,000 a year in added timber revenue, suggesting the tower was still valued for fire detection into that period.[^4]

The lookout was taken out of fire-watch service sometime in the 1980s.[^4] Its catwalk, shutters, and stairs have since been removed, and the cab itself is now used as a communications vault rather than a staffed lookout.[^1]

Today St. Joe Baldy sits on land managed jointly by the Forest Service and the Idaho Department of Lands.[^1] With the structure repurposed for radio equipment and its exterior access features stripped away, it is not set up for public climbing or overnight rental; visitors should treat it as active infrastructure rather than a recreational stop. It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on December 5, 2020.[^1]
