Charlie Bayer, in an interview printed in The Pinedale Roundup, said he used Bayer Mountain as a fire lookout starting in 1910.[^1] He said he made frequent climbs to the summit during fire season.[^1] The words quoted from him describe that climbing and do not mention a building on the summit.[^1]

The peak lies one mile east of the Middle Fork Station, between the Big Popo Agie and Sawmill Creek canyons, in Fremont County on the Shoshone National Forest.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association does not count Bayer Mountain among its lookouts. It places the peak in its unknown or undocumented group.[^2] The association says it will move a site onto its lookout lists if evidence or documentation turns up showing regular use as a wildland fire lookout.[^2] Ron Kemnow's Forest Lookouts site, by contrast, gives the peak its own page among its forest lookouts.[^1]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the site is open to the public.
