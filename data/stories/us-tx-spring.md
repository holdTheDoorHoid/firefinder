The tower standing in Cypress Creek Park, in the Houston suburb of Spring, is not a Texas tower at all by birth. It is an Aermotor MC-39 cab, a model the Aermotor company of Chicago built by the hundreds in the 1930s and shipped out to forests across the country.[^1] This one went to western Montana, where the U.S. Forest Service put it to work spotting smoke.[^1] It stayed on duty there for about sixty years before the Forest Service decommissioned it in 1995.[^1]

That might have been the end of it, but the cab went next to Davanna LLC, a restoration shop in Bloomingdale, New York, which gave it new stairs and a new platform and refitted the cabin.[^1] In January 2023 the restored tower went back up, this time far from any national forest: in Cypress Creek Park in Spring, Texas, a 104-acre park run by the Timber Lane Utility District that also has lakes, fishing piers, and observation decks.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register added it to its rolls on January 2, 2026, as US 1811, TX 5, nominated by Alfred Francis Gessel.[^1]

The register lists the tower's elevation as 113 feet, 34 meters; it doesn't say whether that figure describes the site or the structure itself.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list confirms it still stands, cross-referenced to the same registry number.[^3] Beyond that, little is recorded. Whether visitors can climb it isn't documented in what's available here; the park's own page nods to a "Fire Tower Flyer" without reproducing it.[^2]

Spring Fire Tower is now a piece of Montana's working forest history re-planted as a park feature outside Houston — a lookout with no fire season to watch for anymore, but a clear line back to the woods it came from.
