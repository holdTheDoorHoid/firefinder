The Cedar Bridge Fire Tower has been moved three times since a 60-foot lookout first went up in 1924.[^1] The register places that original tower seven miles south of its present site, while a local history site puts the first lookout on a small knoll near the Cedar Bridge Hotel.[^1][^2]

A July 1938 newspaper item already described an "abandoned fire lookout tower at Cedar Bridge."[^3] Sources then disagree on what became of it.[^2][^3] A 1950 survey note, citing state forestry records, said the tower was torn down in 1938.[^2] A 1951 visit instead found that it had been moved across the county line into Burlington County in 1938.[^2]

The history site, citing an unpublished manuscript by a fire-tower historian, says the Burlington County tower was taken down around 1942 and may have been re-erected at Old Bridge.[^2] A new 110-foot International Derrick tower followed, on a sand road toward the Forked River Mountains.[^2] The history site says it had a flat roof with a railing and no electricity.[^2]

The remote site was vandalized in the 1970s and early 1980s, so it was decided to move the tower again.[^2] Around 1983 a helicopter carried it about a mile to its present spot on County Route 539.[^1][^2]

Accounts differ on the roof.[^1][^2] The register says the tower fell over years ago, damaging its original roof, and that the forest service later put on a more traditional peaked roof.[^1] The history site first describes a flat roof, then says an unusual gable roof was removed during the 1983 move.[^2]

Today the tower stands 100 feet tall and is still in active service.[^2][^1] A 2004 visit report found it fenced in behind a locked gate and said it was usually manned during fire seasons.[^1] We found no newer staffing or public-access information.[^1] Treat it as an active state fire tower and stay behind the fence unless the agency says otherwise.[^1]
