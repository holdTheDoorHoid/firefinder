Black Mountain rises on the eastern shore of Lake George, inside the Lake George Wild Forest, and its summit has long served as a fire-watch post.[^3] The first lookout, a wooden tower, was built by the Conservation Commission in May 1911.[^2] In 1918 the Commission put up a 35-foot steel tower, an Aermotor LS40, and it stayed in use until 1988.[^1][^2]

For 77 years the tower was manned by state fire spotters and forest rangers.[^2] The sources disagree on when the watch ended.[^1][^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower was officially closed in 1988, the same year it was extended.[^1] Wikipedia says lookout operations ended with the 1988 fire season, and the DEC officially closed the tower in early 1989.[^2] The Adirondack Explorer dates the public's exclusion from the tower to 1990.[^3]

In 1988 a 39-foot extension was added for state police communications.[^1] The register gives the total height afterward as 80 feet, more than the 35-foot tower and the 39-foot addition add up to.[^1] Today the tower carries radio equipment rather than fire watchers.[^2][^3] Wikipedia says police agencies use the tower for radio repeater antennas.[^2] The Explorer describes state emergency communications equipment that runs on solar panels and is enclosed by tall fencing with razor wire on top.[^3] A modular building at the base is also enclosed by fencing.[^1] The tower still stands on the summit, and Wikipedia notes that the grounds are open while the tower itself stays shut to visitors.[^2]
