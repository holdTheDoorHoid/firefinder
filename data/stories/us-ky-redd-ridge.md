The Kentucky Division of Forestry built the Redd Ridge tower in Trigg County.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register calls it the first wooden fire tower in Kentucky.[^1] The register says it was built from four 50-foot creosoted poles and other creosoted lumber, and that the poles were sourced in Metropolis, Illinois.[^1]

The register says 25 to 30 people gathered when the build began.[^1] Clarence Gray and Charles Butts, both of the Division of Forestry, added a tin roof, and the tower stayed in use until it rotted.[^1]

According to the register, road work on the Trace in the early to mid 1980s may have cleared the tower away.[^1] The Trace is a parkway that winds through Land Between the Lakes.[^2] The site lies inside that national recreation area, which covers 171,280 acres in Kentucky and Tennessee.[^1][^2] The register does not describe what, if anything, remains on the site today, or whether the spot itself can be visited.[^1]

The site was added to the register on November 2, 2009, on the nomination of Trenton C. Girard.[^1]
