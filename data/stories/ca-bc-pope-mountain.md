Mount Pope stands about 7 km northwest of Fort St. James, on the northeast side of Stuart Lake.[^1] Canada Lookouts files the site under Bulkley-Nechako in British Columbia.[^2]

The Carrier people made the first trails up the peak.[^1] From the summit, the view ran far to the north, and the site gave early notice of hostile groups coming down Stuart Lake.[^1]

The accounts of the name differ in their details.[^1][^3] BC Parks ties it to Major Franklin L. Pope, who was surveying a route for the Overland Telegraph Line in 1865, and who was separated from his party and spent a night alone on the mountain.[^1] A 1928 letter quoted in the provincial name record says instead that Pope had wintered with the Hudson's Bay Company at Takla Lake, and that he wanted to climb the peak as he passed through.[^3] They agree that Pope spent a night on the mountain.[^1][^3]

BC Parks says the peak came back into use as a lookout point in the 1920s. A telegraph line went in at that time, and the summit became a Forest Service fire lookout.[^1]

The lookout was taken down in 1972, and the trail to the summit became a recreational trail in 1983.[^1] Mount Pope Park was established in January 2001.[^1]

BC Parks describes a gazebo at the peak, with a 360-degree view, and does not describe a tower.[^1] A hiking guide, though, describes an old forestry lookout and gazebo at the top.[^4] That conflicts with BC Parks' account of the 1972 removal.[^1][^4]

The park is a day-use area, which BC Parks says is popular with hikers and rock climbers.[^1] The trail to the summit is 6.5 km long.[^1] The trailhead parking lot is 4 km down Stones Bay Road. To get there, drive through Fort St. James on Stuart Drive West, then turn left (west) onto Stones Bay Road.[^1]
