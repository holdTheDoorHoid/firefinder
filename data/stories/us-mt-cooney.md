Cooney Lookout stands on a hillside near Condon, about 700 feet above the Swan River, on the flank of the Swan Range.[^1][^2] The first tower there was a 20-foot L-4 cab built in 1933.[^1][^2] The present 30-foot treated timber tower, with a flat R-6 cab, was built in 1968.[^1][^2]

Summer staffing has come from more than one group, and the sources do not agree on who does it.[^1][^2] The register says the tower is mostly staffed by volunteers in summer.[^1] Firelookout.com says the Montana Department of Natural Resources and Conservation (DNRC) staffs it, and it lists a couple recorded as lookout personnel in 1957 and another couple in 1958.[^2] A 2016 annual report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association's North Idaho/Montana chapter says volunteers provide staffing and maintenance for Cooney and two other Flathead lookouts.[^3]

The register lists the tower as standing and administered by the US Forest Service on the Swan Lake Ranger District of the Flathead National Forest.[^1][^4] It was registered on January 9, 2021.[^1]

I found no Forest Service statement about whether the tower can be visited. A Seeley Lake guide to the Rumble Creek Lakes hike says Rumble Creek Road leads to a parking area a quarter mile past the lookout.[^5] That describes a hiking trailhead, not a way into the tower. Check with the Swan Lake Ranger District before going, and do not climb the tower unless it is open to the public.
