Warren Peak rises in the Bear Lodge Mountains, part of the Black Hills, about six miles northwest of Sundance, Wyoming.[^3][^4] The Black Hills National Forest holds the land, and the Forest Service's Bearlodge Ranger District lists the lookout on its recreation pages.[^1][^2]

The first lookout here went up in 1938, when the Civilian Conservation Corps built a ten-foot tower with a live-in cab.[^2] One site describes that cab as an enclosed L-4 cab from 1938 to 1939.[^4]

Work on the Sundance Air Force Station, a radar station on the peak, started in 1959, and the original tower was demolished during that work.[^2] In 1960 a replacement went up east of the original site.[^2] The Forest Service and Wikipedia both give it as 54 feet tall, while firelookout.com gives 53 feet.[^2][^3][^4]

The register names Mert Ellsbury of Aladdin as the first lookout. It says Maurice Classick worked the original lookout longest and helped erect its replacement.[^1] firelookout.com names Lyman Ellsbury for 1939, so the name of the first lookout is not settled.[^4]

The register says the lookout is still active and manned every fire season.[^1] It recorded the tower on January 2, 2026.[^1]

A short dirt road signed for the lookout runs from Warren Peak Road to the tower, with free parking at its base.[^5] Visitors can climb the stairs almost to the top, but the top floor is locked.[^5] Wikipedia says the peak is easy to reach on an unnamed road because its slope is gradual.[^3] One listing mentions a pit toilet at the base, while the Forest Service page says restroom information is not available, so the sources disagree on that detail.[^5][^2]
