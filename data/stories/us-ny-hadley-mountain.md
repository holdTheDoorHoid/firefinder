Hadley Mountain Fire Tower stands on Hadley Mountain in Saratoga County, New York, west of the Lake George tourist area.[^1] It is a 40-foot Aermotor LL-25 steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, one of ten such towers the state ordered as its first standard steel lookouts.[^1][^3] The register gives the elevation as 2,680 feet, while the Adirondack Explorer gives the ridgeline as 2,653 feet.[^1][^2]

All ten towers were delivered by rail in early 1916, and nine went up on other peaks.[^1] The tenth was meant for Ohmer Mountain, but the state could not reach an agreement with that landowner, so Hadley was chosen instead.[^1] Work began late in 1916 and was finished in early 1917.[^1][^3] The tower went up on a 12,000-acre site that had seen repeated fires in the decade before.[^1]

Sources disagree about the dates.[^1][^4] Wikipedia lists 1917 as the build year, but reports a plaque at the base of the trail that dates the original wood tower to 1916 and the current steel tower to 1920.[^4] The register and Bill Starr's account describe a steel LL-25 finished in early 1917, and this story follows them.[^1][^3]

A 2017 visit report marks the tower as staffed only sometimes, and says the state and a local committee hire a summit steward each season.[^1] The Adirondack Explorer described a steward hired for summer 2017, the tower's centennial year.[^2] Wikipedia says the tower may be staffed in summer months.[^4]

The tower was registered with the National Historic Lookout Register on March 6, 1995, the same year the Hadley Mountain Fire Tower Committee was organized under Jack Freeman of ADK.[^1][^2] Wikipedia reports that it was added to the National Register of Historic Places on September 23, 2001.[^4] A New York listing from the Forest Fire Lookout Association, on firelookout.org, also shows it as standing.[^5]

The tower sits in the Wilcox Lake Wild Forest, on public land.[^2] A 2017 visit report calls it in good condition and says the committee and the state look after it.[^1] That report also describes a clearly marked trail to the summit, and says the town plows the road and trailhead lot in winter.[^1] A 2017 centennial visitor climbed the tower after lunch, and a steward welcomed hikers at the rocky summit.[^2]
