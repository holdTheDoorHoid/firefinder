The Forest Service built Bald (Ingram) Butte Lookout in 1931, on the Fremont National Forest in Lake County, Oregon.[^1][^2] It is a standard L-4 cab, a 14-by-14-foot room with windows on all sides, set directly on the ground rather than up on a tower.[^3][^4]

Like most lookouts of its era, it would have been staffed seasonally by Forest Service lookouts watching for smoke across the Fremont's sage basins and timbered ridges, though the record here doesn't give specific names or fire seasons. What is documented is a harder stretch later on: by the 1990s the cabin had suffered from vandalism.[^2] Rather than let it go, the Forest Service remodeled it and brought it into the agency's recreation rental program.[^2]

Today the lookout still stands and can be rented through Recreation.gov. Guests sleep on two cots, and the small cabin has a propane heater and cook stove for warmth and cooking, plus basic lighting, though there's no water on site and renters need to bring their own drinking, cooking and washing supplies.[^5] Outside, there's a picnic table and an accessible vault toilet.[^5] Entry is by combination lock, available by contacting the Paisley Ranger District ahead of a stay.[^5] The surrounding Fremont National Forest offers hiking on the nearby Bald Butte Trail and access to the Gearhart Mountain Wilderness, and the lookout's remote, high perch makes it a popular stargazing spot.[^5] It's listed on the National Historic Lookout Register, registered in 1998.[^1]
