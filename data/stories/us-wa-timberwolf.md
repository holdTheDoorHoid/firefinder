Timberwolf Mountain sits in the Wenatchee National Forest, in Yakima County, Washington.[^1][^2] A lookout cab went up there in 1931.[^1][^2][^3] The register calls it an L-4 cab, one of many lookouts built along the Cascades' east side to guard against fire.[^1] Willhite's account adds that the cab rested on the ground.[^3]

Panoramic photos were taken from the cab's roof in 1934, and one page dates them to July 17 that year.[^3][^4] At that time vehicles could reach the top, and a good deal of timber had to be cut to open the view north.[^3] The USGS placed a benchmark in 1938 and visited again in 1966 to reference the lookout.[^3] A new benchmark went in 1991.[^3]

The cab came off the mountain in 1975, and the sources do not agree on how.[^1][^2][^3] The register and Willhite say it was removed, while FireLookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^3]

The summit is still reachable by road.[^3] Willhite says a vehicle with enough clearance can drive to the top, and a rough stretch can be walked instead.[^3] Photo captions on that page show an outhouse still there in 2020, a parking area at the summit, and a Timberwolf Lookout sign photographed in 2020.[^3] The register's photos show Mount Rainier in the distance from the site.[^1] None of the four sources describes footings or a foundation for the cab itself.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
