The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 70-foot iron tower that the city asked for and had built on Burrill Hill in Lynn Woods Park in 1926.[^1] The city paid for the tower, its telephone and power lines.[^1] Under an agreement, the state supplied the staff, and the lookout became one piece of its forest fire detection system.[^1]

A 2008 renovation flyer from the Friends of Lynn Woods gives a different early history.[^2] It says the towers on several hills, including Burrill Hill, were wooden and short-lived, until a steel tower went up on Mount Gilead in 1929.[^2] The register's 1926 date and the flyer's account do not match, and the sources do not settle which is right.[^1][^2]

The stone tower on Burrill Hill was built by the WPA.[^2][^3] Construction started in December 1935 and wrapped up the following September.[^2] The flyer says the original tower had a wooden watch house on top, and that the floor is believed to have been destroyed in a hurricane in the 1950s.[^2]

The register says the iron tower was last used in the late 1960s.[^1] By 2008 the stone tower needed repairs: many of its stones were missing from the damaged hip wall, and the rehabilitation was estimated at more than $225,000.[^2] The flyer expected the tower to reopen to visitors after the work, timed to match Dungeon Rock.[^2]

An undated web page describes the stone tower as standing on Burrill Hill, which it calls the highest point in the City of Lynn north of Boston.[^3] The page places the tower in the municipal forest park, where trails link it with Dungeon Rock and other sites.[^3] The sources do not say whether the tower is open to visitors now, or whether the iron tower stood on the same spot as the stone tower.[^1][^2][^3]
