The Black tower was a four-legged steel lookout in Reynolds County, Missouri, on Mark Twain National Forest land.[^1] A 1934 newspaper report, quoted on a lookout-history website, said the Black tower had been located two and a half miles northeast of Black, Missouri, and staked out. It was to have a stairway to the cab instead of a ladder, and the report gave its height as 99 feet, 9 inches to the cab floor.[^1]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1939 places the tower about four miles west-northwest of Centerville, in the Clark National Forest. It calls the tower about 130 feet high, with a glass-enclosed lookout room on top.[^1] The two height figures disagree, and this story reports both.[^1]

The U.S. Geological Survey's 1965 recovery note says the tower had been taken off the site, though its foundation piers remained.[^1] Another note says it was moved to the Oates tower site, but the Oates record is marked removed.[^1][^2]

Today a topographic listing still labels the spot "Black Lookout Tower (historical)."[^3] The 1939 description gives driving directions from Centerville.[^1] No source describes what stands there now or whether visitors may go there, though the site lies within national forest land.[^1]
