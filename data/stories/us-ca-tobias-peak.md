Tobias Peak Lookout stands on Tobias Peak in the Sequoia National Forest, in Tulare County, California.[^1][^4] The register and an older site place it in the Hot Springs district, while a 2025 Forest Service brochure places it on the Western Divide Ranger District.[^1][^3][^4]

The Civilian Conservation Corps and the Forest Service built the current cab in 1935.[^1][^4] It is a C-3 type cab, 14 by 14 feet, with a catwalk, set on a 3-foot foundation.[^1][^3] Supplies came in by mule and horse over a hard trail, and the brochure says all materials and equipment were packed in by mule train.[^3][^4] The peak was probably first used for lookout work around 1912.[^3]

Tobias was chosen to replace Sunday Peak Lookout, which was considered a hazard and was burned down by the government in 1954.[^3]

In June 1937 a newspaper said the lookout would be staffed from June 10 and welcomed visitors. It also said the lookout was just a mile from the road.[^2] A July 1951 newspaper item says Bea Rice and Helen King had kept watch there since 1943.[^2]

The register lists 1947 and 1986 as years the lookout was not in active service. A July 1947 newspaper item, though, names two lookouts on Tobias that summer.[^1][^2] The register says its observer had been in post since 1992.[^1]

Today a Forest Service employee lives in the tower, which is also used as an office.[^4] Visitors may go up when the gate is open, and a sign on the gate may show whether the lookout is open to the public.[^4] The brochure notes there is very little space inside.[^4] Roads to the base are typically open between June and October, depending on weather.[^4] The brochure describes the climb as a moderate one, including the walkway.[^4] An undated fire-lookouts.org page says the lookout is no longer staffed, and in the same paragraph says it is staffed five days a week. The first claim conflicts with the brochure.[^3][^4]
