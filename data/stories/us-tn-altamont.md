The Altamont Lookout stands in Grundy County, Tennessee.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists its elevation as 2,031 feet (619 meters).[^1] The Register dates it to the 1940s and identifies it as a Carnegie steel tower, and a 2006 visit report gives its height as 60 feet, with diagonal steps.[^1]

The sources do not name a builder or a year within that decade. A state study of the Division of Forestry's towers says the majority of them were built between the late 1940s and about 1960. The study mentions Altamont only in a table of extant towers and gives no build date for it, so the exact date is still unknown.[^2] A Forest Lookouts history page files the tower under the Tennessee Division of Forestry's District 3.[^3]

The Register places the tower at a Division of Forestry work center and says it is available for emergency use.[^1] The 2006 visit report says the tower is not staffed, and a nearby caretaker's house did not appear to be occupied.[^1]

The same report rated the tower in fair to good shape. It noted that the cab is open, that several panes of glass were broken out, and that the weather had taken a toll on the floor.[^1] The visitor suggested that the tower would make a good restoration project.[^1] The Register gives a registration date of January 3, 2015, with Jerry Lutes as nominator.[^1]

The tower was still listed as extant in a state list dated March 26, 2014.[^2] A Tennessee Landforms table also marks the Altamont tower as standing.[^4]

Today the sources do not say whether the public may visit or climb it. The 2006 report says a sign stated that climbing was at the climber's own risk, but it also described broken glass and a floor showing weather damage.[^1] The Register notes a sign on the highway points the way to the tower.[^1] Please do not climb it. Our record lists access as unknown, so check with the Tennessee Division of Forestry before any visit.
