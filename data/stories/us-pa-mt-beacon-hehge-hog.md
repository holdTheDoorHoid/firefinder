Mt. Beacon is a fire tower site in McKean County, Pennsylvania.[^1] The page heading also calls it Hehge hog, and a 1935 newspaper item placed the tower under two miles west of Bradford.[^1]

In April 1931 county newspapers reported that the McKean County Protective Fire Association was paying for an eighty-foot tower on Mt. Beacon, the highest point in the northwest of the county.[^1] That part of the county was described as the only area without proper forest fire protection.[^1] The plan also included a new, better access route starting at Bennett Brook Road and running to the tower site, so people could see the county from the top.[^1] An airway beacon was planned for the top at some later date, and the tower was to be named by schoolchildren from Bradford and several nearby townships.[^1]

By October 1935 the tower was open for the fall season as the watch post serving the northern fire district.[^1] Even though the forest was unusually dry, the opening was at least a fortnight later than in many earlier years.[^1] Mr. Coleman said no fires had turned up in the woods so far.[^1] The association was backing telephone service for the tower, which was due to be connected that day.[^1]

No source found describes what remains at the site now, or whether the tower still stands or the hill is open to visitors.
