Flag Mountain stands west of Deerfield, in Pennington County, on Black Hills National Forest land.[^1] The name goes back to 1876, when Joe Reynolds, a former scout for General Custer, put a flag on the peak to warn nearby settlers of a threatened uprising. His sons kept up the custom until a lookout was completed.[^1]

The forest supervisor announced in June 1940 that a 14-by-14-foot lookout on a 10-foot base would be finished by fall.[^3] By November the station was complete, and the Roubaix CCC camp did the work.[^3] Other sources date the build to 1941, so the year is not settled.[^1][^4][^6]

The lookouts named in the sources include Dan Weirs in 1942, Frank Kaump in 1944, Jack Munson in 1946, and Lyle Hamm in 1957.[^2] Claire Marvin is listed for 1963 and is noted as serving several years.[^2] Forest Lookouts says William A. Reman started at the lookout in April 1952, while the firelookout page lists him in 1951.[^3][^2]

The register gives 1975 as the year the lookout was removed.[^1] A Black Hills travel site says it was abandoned in 1951, but the staffing lists in other sources run past that date.[^5][^2] The firelookout page says the cab is gone and parts of the stone base remain.[^2] Forest Lookouts says what is left is the lower rock wall of the base, and a log outhouse sits a short walk away.[^3]

A hiking blog says only a concrete base is left on top, along with a wooden bathroom on the hill's north side.[^4] It says a short set of stairs from the parking area leads up to the tower, and warns that the Flag Mountain Lookout Road does not reach the top.[^4] An onX trail page describes a route that starts near the summit on Forest Service Road 189.2, and says the concrete base is still visible.[^6] The sources do not agree on whether the base is stone or concrete.[^2][^4]
