Sugarloaf Mountain in Clatsop County, Oregon, had a tree crow's nest built on it in 1921, with a patrol point on the rocks nearby.[^1][^2] The register says the Oregon Department of Forestry administered the site.[^1] Both the register and firelookout.com date the end of its use to the 1930s.[^1][^2]

The summit did not make a good watch point.[^3] A letter from the Northwest Oregon F.P.A., reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, says the station was tried out for a while with temporary quarters. Coastal fog hid the view about half the time, so the lookout use was given up.[^3]

In August 1942 the same correspondence considered putting an aircraft warning station on the summit.[^3] The writer said the proposal seemed impractical. Building on the summit rock would be impossible without excessive blasting costs, and timber on a nearby ridge would have to be cut to give the observer visibility.[^3]

The register added the site on October 15, 2019, on the nomination of Tammy McLeod.[^1] It calls the lookout gone.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list uses the word abandoned instead.[^4]

Firelookout.com also gives the location as nine miles north-northeast of Newhalem, which conflicts with the Clatsop County location printed on the same page.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.[^1] A climbing trip report says the mountain sits in the middle of private timberland, and that the approach starts from a gate off Hwy 26.[^5]
