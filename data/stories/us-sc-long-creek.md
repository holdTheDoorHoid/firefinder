Long Creek Tower, also known as Round Mountain Lookout, stands in the Sumter National Forest in Oconee County, South Carolina, at an elevation of 2,117 feet (645 m).[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 54-foot Bethany tower, similar in style to an Aermotor, with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab and a low hip roof.[^1]

Its build date is uncertain. The register says the tower was built around 1935.[^1] A February 1941 newspaper item, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, reports that forest ranger F.W. Wiese had announced plans for a fire watch tower on Long Nose mountain, west of Walhalla.[^2] That may describe this tower or a later replacement. The sources do not say which.

The register says the Forest Service still uses the tower on emergency days of extreme fire danger.[^1] The sources say nothing about who staffed it or which fires it watched.

Do not confuse it with Long Mountain Tower, a separate register entry in the same ranger district.[^3] That tower was also dated to around 1935, and the Forest Service used it until the 1980s.[^3]

The register does not say whether the public may climb this tower. It still has a Forest Service role, so do not climb it. The register names the Andrew Pickens Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] Anyone planning a visit should ask the district first.
