The Hillsboro Peak lookout is in the Black Range of the Gila National Forest, in Sierra County, New Mexico.[^1][^2] Two lookout listings give Grant County instead.[^3][^5] The National Historic Lookout Register gives the lookout an elevation of 9,991 feet, while Wikipedia gives the peak 10,020 feet, so the two sources do not agree.[^1][^2]

The record starts before the steel tower. According to the Register, the first structure was made of wood and went up in the early 1920s.[^1] A May 1915 item in The Santa Fe New Mexican, quoted on a lookout history site, already mentions a fire lookout tower on Hillsboro Peak. That points to an earlier tower than the Register's date, and the sources do not settle the question.[^3]

In 1925 a log observer's cabin was added to the site, and the Register says it is still there.[^1][^4] Wikipedia gives 1925 for one or both of the tower and the cabin, and says a 2007 photo shows a tower that looks different from the 1920s one.[^2]

According to the Register, the steel tower is 45.9 feet tall, an Aermotor MC-40 with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and dates from 1933.[^1] The 2020 New Mexico lookout report gives the height as 45 feet.[^4] The Register also says that in 1965 the Gila National Forest put up modern metal quarters in place of the ground house, yet it says the log house is still there.[^1] The 2020 report says the site also has metal quarters from 1965 alongside the original 1925 log cabin.[^1][^4]

The Register accepted the site on January 2, 2006, as US 651.[^1] The National Register of Historic Places listed the lookout and the log cabin in January 1988.[^4][^2]

In June 2013 the lookout was covered in fire-resistant material to shield it from the Silver Fire, which lightning caused.[^3]

A lookout listing shows the tower as standing today.[^5] Wikipedia says the log cabin can reportedly be used by the public overnight.[^2] No current booking listing turned up, and the sources do not say whether the tower itself is open to visitors, so check with the Gila National Forest before planning a visit.
