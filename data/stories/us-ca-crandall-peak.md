Crandall Peak is in Tuolumne County, on the Mi-Wok Ranger District of the Stanislaus National Forest.[^1][^2] The sources do not agree on its elevation. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 5,458 feet. Its description and a 2024 Stanislaus National Forest post give 5,449 feet, and Peakbagging gives 5,452 feet.[^1][^2][^4]

A Civilian Conservation Corps crew from Camp Jupiter built the lookout in 1934.[^1][^2][^3] It was a three-tiered wood-frame building, 40 feet tall, with a 14-by-14-foot second floor and cab.[^1][^3] The original walls were redwood siding, set horizontally and painted white.[^1]

The lookout stayed in use until the mid-1980s.[^1] It burned in 1989 and was never rebuilt.[^1][^2]

Today only a concrete slab is left.[^3][^4] A Stanislaus National Forest post says most of the handful of old lookout sites across the forest are still fairly easy to reach.[^3] We found no statement about whether the Crandall Peak site itself is open to the public, so check with the forest before you go.
