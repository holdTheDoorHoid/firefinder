Lookout Butte stood in Klamath County, about five miles northeast of Mount Scott.[^1][^3] The Rogue River and Winema National Forests managed the lookout.[^4]

In March 1942 the Rogue River National Forest supervisor sent a memo to the regional forester about buying materials for the Lookout Butte project.[^3] The memo asked for a standard creosoted 40-foot tower, plan CT-3, and said a 14-by-14 house was already in stock.[^3] The Klamath County lookouts blog gives 1942 as the construction year and describes a 40-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14 cab.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both give 1932 instead, so the sources disagree on the build year.[^1][^2]

In 1948 the Forest Service was assigning lookouts to posts across the Rogue River forest, and Glenn Davis was posted at Lookout Butte.[^3] In June 1949 the Herald and News reported that Mr. and Mrs. Ray Antley would run the tower, which could see reservation land and had communication with the Indian agency.[^3]

In July 1949 a fire apparently started by a cigarette burned about half an acre of lodgepole pine before it was brought under control.[^3] Ray Antley, Rex Morehouse and two men from a Lake of the Woods crew fought it.[^3]

In 1952 a newspaper item reported that "porkies" had consumed most of the tires off two cars parked on the butte over two summers.[^3] In 1958 Ben Hunnemuller, a Forest Service employee from Fort Klamath, spent a month staffing the lookout and reporting weather there.[^3]

The sources disagree on how the tower ended. The register says it was removed in 1972, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] A 1953 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description, the last physical description we found, calls it a four-legged wooden tower about 60 feet high.[^3] That height does not match the 40 feet that the register and firelookout.com give.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower or its site today, or whether the public can reach the butte. Anyone planning a trip should ask the forest office before going.
