Meadow Mountain rises in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest in Union County, about 15 miles east of Union.[^1][^2] The register gives the site's elevation as 7,821 feet, or 2,384 meters.[^1]

An August 1916 newspaper report says a fire tower was being built on the mountain, and that once finished it would stand 35 feet tall, with a lookout house on top measuring seven feet by seven.[^3] The report describes work in progress, not a finished tower.[^3] Firelookout.com dates a wooden platform and a camp to the 1920s.[^2] Newspaper items from 1921 and 1924 name Roscoe Cook and Emery Sewall as the lookouts.[^3]

The register page carries a caption for a panorama dated October 3, 1935, credited to the National Archives' Pacific Northwest Region.[^1] An Oregon history page lists panorama photos taken on that date.[^3] The register says a cab was constructed on the site in 1936.[^1]

A December 1937 item recalls a companion named Archibald, who had held that role for eight years. He vanished around the first of August that year, after Leonard Herbert opened the station.[^3] An October 1938 item says a "Teleradio" was being used at Meadow Mountain Lookout.[^3]

The register and Firelookout.com both say the lookout was abandoned in 1942.[^1][^2] The register adds that it is gone.[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
