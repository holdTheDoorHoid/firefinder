Mahan Mountain is a forested summit in Coconino National Forest, a few miles north of Happy Jack.[^2] By 1914 a lookout station there was already reporting fires.[^1] That May, a fire of less than five acres near Lockwood Spring was reported from seven lookout stations, and the farthest of them was on Mahan.[^1]

The Forest Service bought the tower in 1922.[^1] It was a 30-foot tower from the Aermotor Company, costing $240, and the plans called for a cabin and a cement cistern on the summit.[^1]

By 1935 the tower was in fairly good condition, the forest supervisor wrote, but he wanted to replace it on Hutch Mountain.[^1] Hutch stood higher, took in everything Mahan could see, and also covered ground north of it that Mahan could not see.[^1] The Forest Lookouts entry for Hutch Mountain says that in 1936 a 30-foot Aermotor tower with a 12-by-12 cab was built to replace the lookout on Mahan.[^1] The Mountain Field Guide says that during the 1930s the Forest Service concluded Hutch offered better fire-detection coverage, so the Mahan lookout was no longer needed.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Mahan tower as gone.[^3] The Mountain Field Guide reports that traces of the old lookout still survive close to the summit.[^2] The Arizona peak list marks the site as a lookout site, a label it uses where the building has been removed, and it adds that the site may show no evidence of the building.[^4] That key is general rather than a note on Mahan, so this story follows the Field Guide's more specific account.[^2][^4]

Today the summit is a brief, lightly visited hike on national forest land, and the Field Guide lists an old lookout road or forest road as the usual approach.[^2]
