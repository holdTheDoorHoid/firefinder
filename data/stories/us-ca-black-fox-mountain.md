Black Fox Mountain Lookout stands on Black Fox Mountain in the Shasta-Trinity National Forest, in Siskiyou County, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1]

A July 1922 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, reports that a 14-by-14-foot glass house had just been finished on a thirty-foot tower on the peak, and that all of its materials had to be packed in on mules.[^2] Notices from 1923 and 1925 describe a thirty-foot steel tower, and newspapers named lookouts there from the 1920s into the 1950s, including Ed Sanderson in 1923 and George Dunlap in 1929.[^2]

The cab on the mountain now is a later one. The Register says it was built in 1939 as a California Region 4 Plan BC-301 cabin on a 30-foot enclosed timber tower, replacing a Region 5 Plan 4AR cabin on an open steel tower.[^1] Neither account says whether the 4AR cab was the 1922 glass house, so these sources do not show whether they were the same structure.[^1][^2]

Women were stationed there in the 1940s and 1950s. A 1943 item names two of them, and a 1954 item describes one woman's ninth season as lookout.[^2] A 1961 item says a bullet cut the telephone line, and it calls the lookout station the only way to reach the forest area after hours.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page says the lookout was closed for maintenance in 2008 and its duties passed to a station it calls the Siskiyou Bear Lookout. We could not confirm that station anywhere else.[^2] The Register recorded the lookout on October 27, 2019.[^1] The sources read do not say whether it has reopened or what condition it is in.[^1][^2]

Current access is not settled by any source we read. Check with the Shasta-Trinity National Forest before planning a trip, and do not climb the tower.
