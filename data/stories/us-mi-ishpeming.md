The Ishpeming Fire Tower is a steel lookout on Greenstone Ridge, inside Isle Royale National Park in Michigan.[^1][^4]

The tower dates back to a fire.[^1] The 1936 Greenstone Ridge Fire swept across part of the island, not long after Isle Royale became a national park. The National Park Service then built two fire towers.[^1] A ranger's later memoir says that 1936 fire burned over Ishpeming Point.[^2] By the 1950s those towers were in poor shape, and work on this one began in 1961.[^1] The memoir says it was constructed that year.[^2]

The tower is a prefabricated angle-steel frame, bolted together and set on four concrete piers. Its cab has steel panels and metal-framed windows on all four sides.[^1]

Russell Tabbert, an Isle Royale park ranger, wrote about his seasons as a lookout there in 1965, 1966 and 1971.[^2] The living space in the cab measured eleven feet, six inches square, and his wife, Barbara, shared it with him early on.[^2] Across those three seasons they reported no fires and one false alarm, which they thought was most likely fog in a low area.[^2] By the mid-1960s, the Greenstone Ridge had become a popular backpacking challenge.[^2]

The park changed its fire strategy by 1968.[^1] Wikipedia says the tower was eventually abandoned after that, though it gives no date.[^1] The memoir places lookouts in the tower as late as 1971.[^2] Its author returned in the late 1990s and early 2000s and found it empty and abandoned.[^2]

The tower was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on January 5, 2021.[^1]

Today the National Park Service describes the retired tower as sitting in a forest, no longer staffed.[^3] It stands where the Greenstone Ridge Trail meets the Ishpeming Trail, and hikers use it as a place to rest.[^3][^1] Visitors cannot climb it, but it gives shelter from rain and forest views.[^3]
