Nelson Hill is a fire tower site in Saskatchewan [^1]. The Firetowers of Saskatchewan listing places it at 53 degrees 57 minutes 15.8 seconds north and 109 degrees 15 minutes 42.2 seconds west [^1]. In 1965 a new steel tower took over from the old lookout here, according to Canada Lookouts, which cites the Department of Natural Resources annual report for 1965-66 [^2].

Two towermen are named for the site, and the pages spell one of the names differently. The listing gives "Wes Dimion" beside the range 68-9 [^1]. A towermen list on the same website gives "Wesley Dimon" with 1968 [^3]. Thomas Ouellette is listed at Nelson Hill for 1996 to 2002, and his entry ends with the note that he "went to aerial detection" [^3].

A Grassy Lake towerman's biography on the same list, not a Nelson Hill entry, says the Saskatchewan Department of Natural Resources has since been renamed Saskatchewan Environment and Resource Management, or SERM [^3]. The listing also gives 1966 in its notes, but none of the sources I read explains what happened that year [^1].

No source I could read describes what, if anything, stands at the Nelson Hill site today, or whether the public can reach it.
