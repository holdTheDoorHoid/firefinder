The Trevorton fire tower stood near the town of the same name, in Northumberland County, Pennsylvania.[^1][^2] In November 1933 a newspaper reported that Civilian Conservation Corps workers would build an 80-foot steel tower near the town. The paper said it was one of six such towers the U.S. Forest Service had bought as an agent for federal emergency conservation work funds.[^2]

The register gives 1934 as the year the tower was built, and calls it a steel I-S tower.[^1] The 1933 report described a tower still to be built, so the two sources differ slightly on timing.

In April 1935 a forest fire burned for about an hour and a half on North Mountain, two miles west of town, and destroyed three acres of timber. The paper said Isaac Frye, the Trevorton fire tower lookout, spotted it and alerted a local firefighting crew, which put it out.[^2] The paper believed a spark from a passing railroad locomotive started the blaze.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed before 2006.[^1]

A map site still lists a "Trevorton Fire Tower" in Zerbe Township, near the hamlet of Rebuck.[^3] That listing does not say whether any structure stands there. We found no source that describes the site today or whether the public can visit it.
