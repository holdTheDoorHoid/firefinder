The register says a tree crow's nest with a log cabin went up on this summit in 1923, and firelookout.com gives the same starting date.[^1][^2] Rex's page begins with an October 1933 item about work starting on a lookout house on Bald Mountain.[^3] In May 1934 the Eugene Register-Guard announced plans for a cabin and tower at Hell Canyon.[^3] The register dates a cabin with an open platform to 1934, and Rex's page describes a roof-level platform in April of that year, so both place a platform on the site by then.[^1][^3] firelookout.com instead dates a frame cabin with a roof platform to 1936.[^2]

Rex's page puts the lookout on the line between Tillamook and Yamhill counties, near the head of the Nestucca River and Meadow Lake, on the Hebo ranger district.[^3] By April 1934 it was a roof-level platform on a wooden frame, at one end of a cabin with a dirt floor.[^3] In 1935 it reported to the Three Rivers Ranger Station through the Tri County Telephone Company.[^3] A 1941 survey recovery note calls the structure a short tower and says it was also known as Hells Canyon Lookout.[^3]

The register lists the Oregon Department of Forestry as administrator.[^1] Rex's page places the lookout in the Siuslaw National Forest and names the forest supervisor in its 1933 and 1934 items, so the sources disagree about who ran it.[^3]

The register says the lookout was gone in 1951, and firelookout.com says it was abandoned that year and also lists it as destroyed.[^1][^2]

A 1936 Forest Service station description says forest roads reached the station from Willamina or Hebo. It also ties the summit to Sheridan Peak, which may be a useful second name when you search.[^3] We found no source that describes what, if anything, is left on the summit now, or whether the road into it is open to visitors.
