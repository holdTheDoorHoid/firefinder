Woss Fire Lookout stands on the summit of Lookout Mountain, northeast of the community of Woss on northern Vancouver Island. The summit is 640 m (2,080 ft) up and lies within the Nimpkish Provincial Forest.[^1][^2]

The first lookout went up in 1948, built by Canadian Forest Products Ltd. (Canfor), a logging company that was extending its railway south of Beaver Cove that year. Canfor put up a tower of 6.1 to 7.6 m (20 to 25 ft) with a plywood cupola and cut an access trail to it.[^1][^2] At first the site had a timber tower, a cupola and a tent.[^1]

The BC Forest Service took over the site in 1949.[^1] A lookoutman was already stationed there in 1948, and an appointed lookoutman staffed it through each fire season.[^1]

In 1950 the tent and tower were replaced with a permanent cabin, a pre-fabricated hip-roof building with its own cupola.[^2][^1] In 1953 the Kay fire burned the ground east and south of the lookout, but the building was not damaged.[^1] A new metal roof went on in 1970.[^1]

The lookout was deleted from the provincial fire protection system in 1987.[^1] With little to no maintenance it fell into disrepair, and the brochure says it became unsafe and beyond restoring.[^2]

In 2012 the Woss Residents' Association proposed restoring the lookout and building an access trail to the site.[^1] The brochure dates both the removal of the 1950 structure and its replacement with a replica to 2013-2014, while a tourism page gives 2014 for the replacement.[^2][^3]

The Island Coastal Economic Trust page says major water damage and deterioration made the old structure unsafe for public access, and that the decision was made to dismantle it.[^4] The replacement uses modern construction, though some original wall panels and siding were salvaged and reused, and the old wooden cistern and cedar eaves were repaired and incorporated into it.[^4] So that construction could happen in the hard-to-reach area, a helicopter landing pad was built, and the trailhead and parking area were also improved.[^4]

Today the replica is a day-use site reached by a steep trail. Visitors should allow about four hours for the round trip, and campfires are not permitted.[^3] Climbers get views over Woss Lake, the Nimpkish Valley and the Bonanza range.[^3]
