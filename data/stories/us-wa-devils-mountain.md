Devils Mountain sits about four miles southeast of Mount Vernon, in Skagit County.[^1][^2] The register lists the site as administered by the Washington Division of Forestry and DNR.[^1] A tower of treated timber, 84 feet tall, went up there in 1936, with a ground cabin for the lookout's living quarters.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

During the Second World War the summit served as an Aircraft Warning Service station.[^3][^4] It was activated on March 9, 1942, and used buildings the state already owned, plus a new 12-by-20-foot garage.[^3][^4] When the station closed, the state kept the site for fire detection.[^3]

Sources disagree on the year the tower was shortened to 40 feet and given a DNR cab.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register and FireLookout.com say 1962, WillhiteWeb says 1960, and a 1958-60 state report mentions a modernization at Devils Mountain.[^1][^2][^4][^3]

WillhiteWeb names Mary Lutz as the lookout in 1964 and 1965, and Noreen Josephson in 1966.[^4]

According to the register, the lookout was given up completely in the late 1960s, and it was taken down in 1970.[^1] FireLookout.com says it was destroyed that year, and Washington Lookouts also gives 1970 as the removal date.[^2][^3] WillhiteWeb gives the year as removed or destroyed.[^4] The register listed the site in March 2012.[^1]

Radio towers came to the summit after its fire-watch years, and according to the hiking guide a communications site now sits on the way up to the high point.[^4] The guide describes no remains of the lookout.[^4] Visitors reach the high point by walking past that site.[^4] Access is by a gated road with one small parking space beside the gate.[^4] The sources do not say whether the summit is open to the public, or what, if anything, of the lookout remains.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
