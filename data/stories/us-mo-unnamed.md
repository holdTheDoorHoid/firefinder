The Cabool fire tower is on a Missouri Department of Conservation towersite in Texas County.[^1][^4] The steel tower was 120 feet tall and went up in 1960.[^1][^2] A 2015 area plan for three Texas County towersites dates the Cabool and Houston towers to 1960 and gives the Cabool tower the same 120-foot height.[^2] That plan is the most recent physical description we found, so the tower's present condition is not confirmed.

The plan says the sites are kept mainly to detect wildfires, and that the towers probably qualify for the National Register of Historic Places.[^2] It mentions the department's lookout staff but gives no staffing dates. We found no named lookout and no notable fire tied to the tower in the sources we read.

The plan lists repeated vandalism to the towers as a challenge, and describes a locked gate at the entrance.[^2]

In July 2026 the Conservation Commission approved donating about 6.1 acres on the west side of the towersite to Texas County Emergency Response Services.[^3] It also approved disposing of about 9.6 acres on the east side by auction.[^3] The department's site page lists the total at 15.5 acres.[^4] The two parcels add up to about 15.7 acres, so both figures are approximate. The action names the parcels but not the tower, so we cannot say which side holds it or what happens to it.[^3]

The department's towersite page gives directions from Cabool and public-use hours for the area, 4:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. daily.[^4] It says nothing about the tower itself, and we could not confirm whether the 2015 gate is still in use. Treat the tower as off limits unless the department says otherwise.
