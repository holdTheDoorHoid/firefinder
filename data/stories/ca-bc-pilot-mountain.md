Pilot Mountain lies about 17 km (11 mi) northwest of the city limits of Prince George, in the Regional District of Fraser-Fort George.[^2] A 1977 newspaper put it about 19 km (12 mi) northwest of Prince George, without saying what it measured from, so the two figures differ.[^1]

The oldest item I found is a 1913 Forest Branch report, which lists a lookout station and a trail of about 1.6 km (1 mi) on the mountain.[^1] According to a 1946 Forest Branch newsletter, the lookout dates from 1934, and Albert Biederman was the first person to climb it.[^1] The two sources disagree on when the lookout began, and I did not find a source that settles it.

Biederman was the lookoutman there for twelve seasons by the time of a 1946 tribute.[^1] The newsletter credited him with spotting smoke and fixing its position at distances of up to about 113 km (70 mi).[^1] In 1936 the Forest Branch described its new radio work: it had started two years earlier with no radio experience, and was running two stations, one at the Prince George district office and one at the Pilot Mountain lookout.[^1]

In 1977 a Prince George newspaper reported that vandals had broken several windows and forced open a trap door at the B.C. Forest Service tower.[^1] The same report said visitors were allowed into the tower from early May until late September, and that a lookout man, Greg Evans, occupied it from early May.[^1] In 1981 a shotgun blast went through a wall during the off season, and a newspaper item suggested a gate on the access road.[^1]

Wikipedia says the summit is used as a radio communications site, with CKPG-TV channel 2 among the users.[^2] None of the sources I found says whether the lookout building still stands, or whether the public may visit it today.
