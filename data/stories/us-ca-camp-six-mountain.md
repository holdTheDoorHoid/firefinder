Camp Six Mountain, in Del Norte County on the Siskiyou National Forest, had a lookout by 1923.[^1][^2]

A federal survey datasheet, quoted on one lookout history page, describes a wooden tower about 40 feet high and dates it to 1939, which does not match the 1923 date.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says Civilian Conservation Corps crews built a lookout on Upper Coon Mountain in 1934.[^3] A Forest Service page says the corps built one in 1935 on French Hill, at Camp Six.[^4]

A later cabin, built to California Region 5 Plan BC-301, stood on a 20-foot open timber tower in place of the first structure.[^1] Andy Flynn held the lookout post in 1924, and a 1929 item describes Phil Stevens as an experienced hand at Camp 6 Mountain.[^2]

In 1954 three young men shot out the station's windows. They admitted it and were settling the cost in installments.[^2]

In 1997 the cab was removed from the tower and moved to Bear Basin Butte, where it was refurbished and added to the rental program.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says restoration at Camp Six began in 1991, but vandals damaged the lookout further, and the Forest Service then removed, rebuilt and relocated it.[^3]

At the original site, just the tower's piers are left.[^1] A federal survey record also mentions a concrete pier under one leg of the tower.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Camp Six site as gone and the Bear Basin structure as relocated.[^5]

The cab now stands on Bear Basin Butte, which the Forest Service puts at 5,303 feet.[^4] The register gives 5,145 feet and says the Smith River National Recreation Area administers the site.[^3] The Forest Service rents the lookout and a separate cabin, built at the time of the move.[^4] The fee is $75 a night for one to eight people, and July through September bookings go through recreation.gov.[^4] The walk up is a steep, unpaved 250-foot path with 13 steps, and there is no water or power.[^4]

No source we read describes access to the piers at the original Camp Six site.[^1]
