Clayton Mountain lies in the Absaroka Range, in Park County on the Shoshone National Forest.[^2][^3] On August 21, 1937, fifteen firefighters died on its west slopes during the Blackwater Fire.[^2] The peak is named for Forest Service ranger Alfred G. Clayton, who died in the fire along with some of his crew.[^2]

A 1940 Forest Service report, quoted on the register, says the Forest Service had recommended renaming Double Mountain for Ranger Clayton.[^1] The same report says a lookout tower was being built on the peak, one with a wide view over much of the Shoshone National Forest.[^1] The register describes it as an L-4 cabin on a timber tower, built in 1940.[^1][^3] No photograph of it has been located.[^1]

The register gives no removal date.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wyoming list marks the tower as gone.[^4]

The register reports that no part of the tower is left at the site.[^1] The American Trails guide to the Blackwater Fire Memorial says the Forest Service tower there is gone.[^5] The route starts near the Blackwater recreation resort and the Firefighter Memorial on the North Fork of the Shoshone River, and it ends with a steep climb.[^5] The trail is a National Recreation Trail, designated in 1979, and the guide says the vistas repay the effort of the trail's last mile.[^5] No source I found describes the summit in any more detail.
