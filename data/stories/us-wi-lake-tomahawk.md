The Lake Tomahawk Lookout is in Oneida County, Wisconsin, at an elevation of 1,735 feet (529 m).[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 100-foot Wisconsin standard tower with an external protected ladder, built in September 1939.[^1] The register notes that the tower was originally called Tomahawk Lake Lookout.[^1]

According to a WJFW feature, Civilian Conservation Corps workers erected many of the towers that survive in Wisconsin during the 1930s.[^2] The sources I read do not say who built this one.

The register lists the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources as administrator.[^1] The register says the tower never received a cable fall-arrest system like those on other DNR ladder towers, and that it has not seen active service in decades.[^1] Roof damage is visible from the ground: the register describes a large hole, and one cab floorboard is missing.[^1]

In 2015 the 72 towers still in use were decommissioned, according to the WJFW feature.[^2] The sources I read do not say whether this tower was one of them.

Orange paint covers alternating sections of the frame and cab.[^1] The register thinks the paint likely identifies the tower as an aviation hazard, because a private airfield is about half a mile away.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] I found no source saying whether the public may visit or climb it. Given the damage the register describes, do not climb it, and ask the DNR before visiting.[^1]
