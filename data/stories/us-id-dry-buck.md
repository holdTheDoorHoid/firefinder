Dry Buck Lookout was an L-4 cab built in 1933 on Dry Buck Mountain, about four miles north-northwest of Banks in Boise County, Idaho.[^1][^2] The lookout stood at about 6,508 feet, in the Boise National Forest.[^2][^3]

A January 28, 1945 Idaho Statesman item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, names Miss Marylin Carlick of Ola as stationed at Dry Buck in the High Valley district.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts, by contrast, has not yet documented any staffing history for the lookout.[^3]

Forest Lookouts says the lookout was destroyed in 1973.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was gone by then.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a ground site that is gone.[^5] According to a climbing guide, a lookout stood on the summit between 1933 and the early 1970s.[^4]

The same guide reports that the foundation remains on the summit, and that the mountain's true high point is a granite outcrop just west of the foundation.[^4] None of the sources we read describes the foundation in more detail, or what else, if anything, is left.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

The site is on Boise National Forest land.[^2] The climbing guide describes a route from Idaho 55 at Banks up Forest Road 643.[^4] Eleven miles up the road, you reach Dry Buck Summit, a high saddle; from there a 1.1-mile climb gaining 567 feet leads to the top.[^4] The guide does not mention a closure.[^4] Road conditions change, though, so check with the Boise National Forest before you go.
