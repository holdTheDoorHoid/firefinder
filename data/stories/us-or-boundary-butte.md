Boundary Butte rises to 5,738 feet, about 11 miles southeast of Crater Lake in Klamath County, Oregon.[^1][^2] Its records name both the Klamath Indian Agency and the Winema National Forest.[^3]

In 1934 a truck trail was under construction to the top, and a 12-by-16-foot cabin went up as living quarters for the lookout.[^3] A plan from that year called for a 47-foot Aermotor tower, but the tower that followed stood 60 feet tall.[^3][^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the steel tower in 1935 for the Klamath Indian Agency.[^3]

Alice Hamilton was stationed here as a lookout by 1947.[^3] A 1953 newspaper profile said she had spent her summers in the tower since 1942, hauled her own water and chopped her own firewood.[^3]

In January 1961 responsibility for the lookout passed to the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^3] A 1971 survey note still described the tower, but a 1974 lookout summary says it had been removed while the living quarters cabin remained.[^3] The two site listings say both the tower and the cabin are gone.[^1][^2] The sources disagree about the cabin, and only the 1974 summary says it survived.[^1][^2][^3]

No source describes what is on the butte today, so what remains there, and whether visitors can reach it, is unknown.
