Mount Ascutney, in Windsor County, Vermont, got its first fire lookout in 1921.[^1] A cabin and a tower went up that year in the town of West Windsor.[^1] A June 1921 newspaper item said the state forester had arranged for a lookout tower on the summit, and that there had never been one there before.[^1]

A steel tower 54 feet tall went up on the mountain in 1940, erected by a side camp from the Plymouth CCC Camp.[^1] A July 1940 newspaper credited the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it, and described a cabin floor 54 feet above a 25-foot square base.[^1] The same paper said a watchman would be stationed in the tower during periods of greatest fire danger.[^1] A 1942 state report says the light copper roofs blew off and were replaced with galvanized iron.[^1]

The state park gives 1952 as the end of the fire tower's use.[^2] A state report for 1957 and 1958 says the lookout facilities at Ascutney, one of several secondary stations, were continued on an inactive basis.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the original summit tower as gone, and a relocated observation tower as standing.[^3] A hiking guide says a former fire tower at the summit was shortened and turned into an observation platform, but gives no date.[^4] The state park describes a 25-foot observation tower near the summit that gives views of Vermont and New Hampshire.[^2] The sources do not say whether the summit tower was shortened in place or whether the relocated observation tower replaced it.[^3][^4]

Visitors can drive up the paved Mountain Road to the summit, or hike one of four trails that total about 12 miles from the base.[^2]
