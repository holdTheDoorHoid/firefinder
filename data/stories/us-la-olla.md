The oldest dated source on the Olla tower is a federal survey description from 1941.[^1] The Coast and Geodetic Survey first observed the station that year and described it as a lookout tower of the Louisiana Conservation Department, about a mile southwest of Olla.[^1] According to the survey, the structure is entirely steel, roughly 90 feet tall, with a cabin enclosed at its summit, and it stands about a tenth of a mile north of U.S. Highway 165.[^1]

An August 1949 item in the Olla-Tullos Signal said the foundation would be done before that issue went to print.[^2] At that time the tower stood 100 feet high. The paper said the tower would be moved and raised to 130 feet, which would make it the tallest in the state, and that it would carry radio and telephone equipment.[^2] The survey and the newspaper disagree on height, so this history gives both figures rather than choosing one.[^1][^2]

The sources also differ on the site. The Central Louisiana Lookouts page labels the tower as section 35 of township 11 North, range 2 East, while the 1941 survey places the station in section 34.[^2][^1]

A 2011 Louisiana tower list still gives an Olla tower at about 31.897 north and 92.258 west, in LaSalle Parish, and ties it to this survey station.[^3] The station's only history entry is its 1941 first observation, so that record does not say whether the tower still stands.[^1] No source describes the site today.
