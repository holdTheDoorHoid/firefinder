Gold Mountain in Ferry County began as a lookout camp in 1914.[^1][^2] An L-4 cab went up there in 1938, and a 20-foot wooden tower with an R-6 flat-top cab took its place in 1963.[^3][^1] The present 100-foot steel tower was built in 1986.[^1][^2]

Sources describe it as the tallest live-in tower in the state, with a lookout on duty each summer.[^1][^3] Staffing is older than the current tower. NHLR cites a 1943 Colville Examiner report that many teachers were working as fire lookouts across the Colville National Forest, including a Colville couple on Gold Mountain.[^1] A TrailChick page last updated in March 2024 says the tower still has a fire-season lookout.[^2]

The sources disagree about who runs the site. The NHLR lists the Colville National Forest, administered by the US Forest Service, while TrailChick and firelookout.com place the summit on the Colville Indian Reservation.[^1][^2][^3] The main access runs north off Wilmont/Bridge Creek Road, about seven miles to the summit, and TrailChick recommends a high-clearance AWD.[^2] People who are not tribal members need a recreation permit to visit tribal land. TrailChick says it costs $80 a year and can be bought online.[^2]

Another Gold Mountain, in Kitsap County, had lookouts of its own and is a separate site.[^4]
