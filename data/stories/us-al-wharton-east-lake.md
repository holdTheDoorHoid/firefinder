The Wharton Lookout Tower, also known as East Lake Tower, stands in the City of Birmingham's Ruffner Mountain Nature Preserve, a 1,038-acre public park established in 1977 with more than 14 miles of hiking trails.[^3] The tower itself predates the preserve by decades: it is a 100-foot steel Aermotor MC-39, built in 1941 by the Civilian Conservation Corps on Ruffner Mountain near the city's East Lake neighborhood.[^1][^2]

What sets it apart is its roof. It is, in fact, "the only lookout in Alabama with a rooftop observation platform."[^1] That rooftop deck was an option Aermotor offered on its 7-by-7-foot cabs, seen on a handful of towers further north but almost nowhere in the South; here it was added to carry an aircraft beacon.[^1]

The tower has long been run jointly by the City of Birmingham and the Birmingham Board of Education, with the Alabama Forestry Commission and the Ruffner Mountain Nature Center as cooperators.[^1] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on January 18, 2010, as US 870 / AL 49, nominated by Thomas Kaufmann.[^1]

No fire-watch or staffing history survives in the sources available here, and nothing found says when, or whether, the tower stopped being used to spot smoke. By 2016 the structure was still standing but not open to climbers: a local outlet reported that "several years ago, there was talk about refurbishing the tower for public use," and that the preserve's then executive director had given a public talk on the tower's restoration potential.[^2] Whether that restoration ever happened is not documented in what was reviewed for this story.

Today the tower remains on the ridge above the nature preserve's trail network, a surviving piece of the CCC-era fire-watch system inside what is now one of Birmingham's largest urban nature preserves. The preserve's trails are open to the public, but absent any newer word on the tower's condition, it should be treated as closed to climbing rather than assumed safe to ascend.
