Barklow Mountain stands about 17 miles east of Port Orford, in the Siskiyou National Forest.[^1][^2] A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1942 places the lookout at the northwest end of a bald, rocky ridge, and gives the building as 10 by 10 feet and about 10 feet high.[^1] In August 1933 a government packer hauled a new lookout to the mountain.[^1] The Register and Firelookout.com describe that first cab as an L-5 built in 1933.[^2][^3]

The replacement is dated differently by different sources. The Register, Firelookout.com and Oregon Hikers say a steel cab went up in 1955.[^2][^3][^4] Oregon Lookouts instead dates a new all-steel house, described as a prototype for later cabins, to 1957.[^1]

A May 1940 guard-placement plan says that in past years the lookout was not manned before mid-July and stayed open until late September.[^1] In 1941 it was staffed for 65 days, and its reports went through West Coast Telephone to the Powers Ranger Station.[^1] Newspaper items that Oregon Lookouts reprints name a lookout on the mountain in 1967, and a couple who went up to the lookout in 1972.[^1]

The steel cab was moved in 1974 to become the Lake of the Woods Lookout, according to the Register, Firelookout.com and Oregon Hikers.[^2][^3][^4] Oregon Hikers adds that the move was made by helicopter.[^4] Oregon Lookouts dates the helicopter flight to 1973 and says it was brought in to replace an earlier building at Lake of the Woods.[^1]

Today the summit lies in the Copper Salmon Wilderness.[^5] The Forest Service's Barklow Mountain Trail #1258 follows the ridge to the location of the old lookout, and wilderness rules bar motorized and mechanized use.[^5] The trail is open year round, and Forest Road 3353 runs about five miles to the northeast trailhead.[^5] Oregon Hikers says little is left at the site beyond a few concrete blocks and two guy-cable anchors, and that the view runs past Copper Mountain to the south and west, out to the Pacific.[^4]
