The Summit Tower, also called the Sandy Ridge Fire Tower, is in Centre County, Pennsylvania.[^1] A September 1924 newspaper item, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, calls it a steel structure, sixty feet tall, sitting about 2,300 feet above sea level. The writer said the view from the steel structure took in 325,000 acres of forest land in Centre, Cambria and Clearfield counties.[^1]

Our own record gives a 1921 build date, an Aermotor design and an 80-foot height. The 1924 height does not match the 80 feet, and we could not confirm the build date or maker from a page we could read. The two heights may describe different towers, or the record may be wrong; the sources do not settle it.

We found no record of who staffed the tower or of any fire it spotted. The one incident we found is a May 1993 item, also quoted by the same site, which reports six windows broken in the tower and damage set at $300.[^1] The item describes the tower as owned by the state Bureau of Forestry.[^1]

Statewide, the state's Department of Conservation and Natural Resources began a $4.6 million project in September 2017 to replace 16 forest fire lookout towers, and many of the original towers still in use were put up in the 1920s through 1940.[^2] We could not tie the Summit tower to that list. Our record says it was replaced in 2018, but we could not confirm that from a page we could open.

We found no readable recent source on the tower's condition or access, and the record lists it as standing. The 1993 item names the state Bureau of Forestry as owner, so anyone who wants to visit should ask that agency about access rather than climb the tower.[^1]
