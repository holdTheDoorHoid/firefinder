Sunset Park Lookout Site stood on a ridge in Mount Rainier National Park, in Pierce County, Washington.[^1] The Register gives its elevation as 5,530 feet (1,686 meters).[^1]

The lookout was a 14-by-20-foot flat-roofed frame cabin with a second-story cupola, built in 1948.[^1][^2][^3] The Register says it replaced the Colonnade lookout two miles to the southeast. Willhite puts the Colonnade 1,200 feet up the same ridge, and we could not settle the distance.[^1][^3]

A 1930 report of the National Park Service director, quoted on a hobbyist page, mentions a frame lookout station on the ridge above Sunset Park. The sources do not say whether that was this cabin or the Colonnade.[^4]

A June 1930 Chehalis newspaper item says Frank Slagle had a summer forest service job at the Sunset lookout station.[^4] It is the only staffing record we found.

The lookout was abandoned in the 1960s.[^1][^2][^3] Willhite adds that squatters moved in toward the end, and the park service then had the building destroyed.[^3] The Register says it was removed in 1973. firelookout.com and Willhite say destroyed in 1973.[^1][^2][^3]

A photo caption on Willhite's page says "Lone wire remains at the site." It is the only remains described in the sources we read.[^3]

Willhite reaches the site from the Sunset Park Patrol Cabin. He takes the Wonderland Trail south, then an old trail on the left that leads to the lookout site.[^3] He notes that a few abandoned trails also reach the site.[^3] The sources describe no closure, but none gives current rules for visiting the site.
