Dutch Joe was a fire lookout on a ridge about 60 miles south of Winslow, in Coconino County, beside the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest.[^1] It was planned to replace an earlier tree lookout.[^1]

Plans shifted during 1939. In June the Winslow Mail reported a plan for a 110-foot steel tower to take over from the tree lookout.[^1] By September the same paper described a 100-foot wooden tower being built by the CCC camp on Chevelon Canyon, with a two-room caretaker's cabin planned at its base.[^1] A 1940 plan entry sets out a 99-foot timber tower, with a 7-by-7 wood-frame cab, on private land next to the National Forest.[^1] The sources disagree on the material and the height.[^1]

In June 1944 the Winslow Mail named Virdia Haught, a rancher, as the person in charge at the lookout, and said he had been working for the Forest Service for several seasons.[^1]

In June 1956 a blaze threatened the area. The Arizona Daily Sun reported that the lookout, two lumber camps and a ranch had all come through so far as of noon Friday, and that the lumber camps had been evacuated because people there might have been trapped.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list marks Dutch Joe as gone.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. A 1940 plan entry places the tower on private land beside the forest, so confirm who owns the ground before going there.[^1]
