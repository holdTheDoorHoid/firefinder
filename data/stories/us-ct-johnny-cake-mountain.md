Johnnycake Mountain, in Burlington, Connecticut, was the site of a state fire lookout.[^1] In 1924 Harry Ney, who owned the mountain, said he would build and equip a lookout station on it.[^1] In spring 1929 he gave the state a modest parcel at the peak, and a 50-foot steel tower went up there.[^1] Wikipedia gives a different year, saying the tower opened in 1930 and was the tallest steel tower the Connecticut State Park and Forest Commission had built up to that point.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry calls it a steel structure.[^3]

Wikipedia names Paul S. Ney as an owner from the early 1900s, and says he built a small shanty on the summit that was often used in summer as a forest-fire station.[^2] From the tower, views reached Mohawk Mountain to the west, Talcott Mountain to the east and the Hanging Hills to the south.[^2]

Wikipedia says the tower is no longer standing, and the Forest Lookouts page lists it as removed, with no date.[^2][^1]

Today, Wikipedia lists a quail barn and a radio tower among the summit's features, and says one station, WJMJ, transmits from a broadcast tower below the summit on the south side.[^2] In 2016 the Town of Burlington bought Johnnycake Mountain Farm, aiming to make the land a town park.[^2] The blue-blazed Tunxis Trail crosses the mountain.[^2] We found no source that describes what remains of the lookout itself, so we cannot say what stands on the 1929 site now.[^3]
