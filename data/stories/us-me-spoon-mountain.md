The Maine Forest Service built a crow's nest fire lookout on the east point of Spoon Mountain in 1916.[^1][^2] The mountain lies in T8 R7 WELS, Penobscot County, and the crow's nest sat at an elevation of 1,082 feet.[^1][^2]

The two Maine Memory pages we read disagree about the first tower's height. One says the tower stood 30 feet when built in 1916, then gained a 15-foot section in 1920.[^1] The other describes a 15-foot gain in 1920 that brought the tower up to 30 feet.[^2] Both accounts agree that the wood structure gave way to a 50-foot steel tower in 1936.[^1][^2]

A Maine Memory record gives the 1917 watchman as Joseph Ingraham of Patten, and the chief warden as John E. Mitchell, also of Patten.[^2]

The Maine State Archives' Digital Maine collection includes a 1922 360-degree panoramic map centered on the tower. It lists the Maine Forestry Service as the creator.[^3]

The slideshow says the structure was removed in 1978.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list now marks the tower as gone.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether the public can reach it.
