Rabbit Hill is a steel fire tower in Division No. 18, Saskatchewan, at an elevation of 573 m (1,880 ft).[^1] A website devoted to Saskatchewan's fire towers gives 1947 as the year the tower was rebuilt.[^2]

The same website keeps a list of towermen, and several of them are recorded at Rabbit Hill.[^2] Jim Settee's entry gives his years as 1943 to 1963 and lists Rabbit Hill among seven towers.[^2] Scotty Percell's entry places him at Rabbit Hill from 1949 until the mid-1960s.[^2] John Fonos is listed there from 1998 to 2001 and is recorded as taking over after Bernice Reimer.[^2] Johnathan Fonos, whose entry says he took over after his dad, is listed for 2000 and 2001.[^2]

Today Firelookout.ca says the Saskatchewan Public Safety Agency was using the tower for camera-based detection in 2026.[^1] The agency's vice-president told a technical briefing ahead of the 2026 season that the province has 42 wildfire detection towers, all equipped with cameras.[^3] No source describes whether the site is open to the public, so treat it as an active detection site rather than a lookout to visit.
