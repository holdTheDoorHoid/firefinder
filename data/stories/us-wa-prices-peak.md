Prices Peak sits in Grays Harbor County, about seven miles north of Montesano.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as the site's administrator.[^1]

The earliest dated lookout went up in 1940.[^1][^2] Willhite's guide credits Weyerhaeuser, Simpson, the State and the Washington Forest Fire Association with about three miles of road that year, made so a semi-permanent lookout could go up.[^3] The register and FireLookout.com both describe a two-story cabin about ten feet square, which was called a smokehouse.[^1][^2] A 1940 association report, quoted on Forest Lookouts, gives the new road as three and a quarter miles.[^4]

In March 1942 the site was activated for the Aircraft Warning Service under the Olympia Filter Center.[^4] A May 1944 report says the service used existing state facilities and added a 12 by 20 garage, and that the State kept the site for fire detection afterward.[^4]

Newspaper notices from The Daily Chronicle name the people on duty.[^4] Mrs. Ben Harner operated the lookout in 1951.[^4] Leah Warren of Shelton was on duty in June 1955.[^4] Cleo Wing of Montesano was on duty when the new post went into service in August 1957.[^4]

In August 1957 the Department of Natural Resources completed a new live-in tower on the peak, 40 feet tall and built of wood.[^3] Sources say that tower was gone by 1970.[^1][^2][^3]

In 2000, Irene Potter, a lookout enthusiast, went to the site soon after a harvest and found at least one footing and an eyebolt.[^3] Willhite's own visit in 2016 could not find the footings, and a pre-commercial thinning crew had been on the site a few months before.[^3] He adds that the 2000 harvest area has lost all of its views, and that new timber is growing there again.[^3]

Access has been restricted.[^3] As of 2016 the private timber company Weyerhaeuser required a permit for anyone to enter the area.[^3] The guide says the less expensive Aberdeen General Access Permit allows visits during the off season, from February 1 to July 31.[^3]

The tower is gone, and no source describes a standing structure at the site today.[^1][^3]
