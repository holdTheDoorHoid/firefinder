North Baldy is a mountain in Pend Oreille County, about 25 miles north of Newport, in the Kaniksu and Colville National Forests.[^1][^2] In 1909 a camp went in about a quarter mile south of the summit, and a log cabin followed in 1918.[^1] The register dates the first cupola cabin to 1928, while firelookout.com dates it to 1925, so the early date is not settled.[^1][^2] A 40-foot pole L-4 cab followed in 1938.[^1][^2]

Following World War II, the Air Force turned the summit into a radar site.[^1] In 1957 the Air Force acquired 56 and a half acres for an unmanned Gap Filler radar site, which got a concrete block building and a steel radar tower.[^3] In 1964 the summit gained a 41-foot tower of treated timber, topped by an R-6 flat-roof cab.[^3] The only named lookout I found is Susan Wright, listed for 1967.[^2]

The site was declared surplus in 1961, and the property went back to the Forest Service the next year (1962).[^3] The tower was abandoned in the 1970s and torn down in 1989.[^1] Its top 30 feet went to Big Meadow Lake, west of Ione, to become a wildlife viewing tower.[^1][^3]

Big Meadow Lake is a shallow lake with a primitive boat launch, a fishing pier and overnight camping.[^4] The observation tower offers a raised view of the wildlife that lives around the lake.[^4] WillhiteWeb says the platform was once the North Baldy tower. It also reports that access was closed during one visit because of vandalism, a date it does not give.[^5]

Vehicles of every sort can reach the lake from Colville or Ione.[^5] The sources do not say whether the R-6 cab went down with the tower or stayed on the mountain, so treat the platform as a viewing spot and respect any closure.[^1][^3]
