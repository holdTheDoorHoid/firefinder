The state Department of Forestry put up a fire tower on Rarick Hill, near Upper Lambs Creek in Tioga County, Pennsylvania.[^1] In October 1922 a Wellsboro paper reported the work, with the tower going up behind the reservoir of the Mansfield Water Company and near an older monument.[^1] By late December the same paper called it a new tower and gave the tower's elevation as 2,284 feet (696 m) above sea level.[^1] A local paper still described the work as about to start in April 1923.[^1] The state register dates the steel Blaw-Knox tower to 1923.[^1][^2]

A towerman was posted there.[^1] In May 1925 Towerman John Badman saw a fire start at the home of D.M. Clemons at Crooked Creek. He rang the house, but no one was home, and the house burned to the ground while neighbors struggled to save the barn.[^1] A November 1923 item tells of a porcupine on the tower's landing that rolled to the ground and walked off apparently none the worse.[^1] In April 1949 a Wellsboro paper, listing the county's five state towers, named Frank Starkey as the towerman on the Rarick tower.[^1]

The register and the Forest Lookouts page both record the tower as removed in 1973.[^1][^2]

No source I found describes what, if anything, is left on the hill today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
