The first lookout on the St. Nora site went up in 1922. It was a wooden tower about 15 m (50 ft) high, located west of the channel that links St. Nora Lake and Kushog Lake, according to MNR records.[^1][^2] A second wooden tower followed in 1939, on the hill west of Highway 35, across the road from the present Frost Centre campus.[^1]

In 1949 a steel tower of 30.5 m (100 ft) went up where the present tower stands, replacing the earlier structure.[^1][^2] It operated by radio and was tied to headquarters in Dorset.[^1] Staffing stopped in 1970, as aircraft increasingly took over fire detection from lookouts.[^1]

That steel tower was demolished in 1974 as part of a policy of taking down disused towers for safety reasons.[^1] The sources disagree on what came next. Canada Lookouts says a new tower was built that year.[^2] The Algonquin Highlands heritage map says a replacement from Pembroke District was reassembled for demonstrations and was closed to visitors.[^1] The same year the Leslie M. Frost Natural Resources Centre opened on the lake.[^1][^3]

The site had been a training ground for government staff and university students from 1944.[^3] The centre shut on July 13, 2004, despite strong public protest, and the Frost Centre Institute, which leased the property, ceased operating at the end of April 2010.[^3]

In April 2026 Canoe FM and Frequency News reported that the centre had been sold for $3 million.[^4][^5] Frequency News says the new owner intends to run a rehabilitation facility for substance use and mental health there.[^5] Canoe FM said the sale was due to close on June 1.[^4] Canoe FM locates the centre in Algonquin Highlands beside Lake St. Nora, while Firelookout.ca gives the tower's address as Lake of Bays.[^4][^6]

Today the heritage map describes the replacement, a 30.5 m (100 ft) heavy steel tower, as still standing, with its current use as a scenic attraction. It can be seen from Highway 35 at the Frost Centre.[^1] The map records public access as no, and no source says whether the tower is part of the sale.[^1][^4][^5] Please do not climb it.
