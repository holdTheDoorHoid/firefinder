The Surry Fire Tower stands along VA 31 near Elberon, an unincorporated community in Surry County, Virginia.[^1][^3][^4] Elberon sits 5.1 miles south-southwest of Surry, according to Wikipedia.[^3]

The register calls it an Aermotor fire tower and gives its elevation as 114 feet (35 m).[^1] Neither the register nor the Forest Lookouts page for the county gives a year of construction, so when the tower was built is unknown.[^1][^2]

The Forest Lookouts page prints several 1940s items it credits to the Smithfield Times. In December 1942, one item says soldiers from a coast artillery regiment were posted in the Elberon tower to spot aircraft and report fires, since a manpower shortage made it impossible to find a towerman that season. The arrangement was made with the Virginia Forest Service.[^2] In June 1947, an item says Ruby Pierce of Elberon served in the tower, and that 96 percent of the county could be seen from it within twelve miles.[^2] In March 1948, Gilbert Logan was named lookout watchman for that fire season, and the notice gives the tower's height as 100 feet.[^2] I read these only as transcriptions on that site; I did not check the original newspaper pages.

The register gives a registration date of January 2, 2026, as US 1789 and VA 47, and names Evan Stone as nominator.[^1] The register rates the tower's condition as poor. It reports that the first flight of steps seems to be missing, while the rest of the structure survives.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^4]

The sources do not say whether the public may enter the grounds or climb the tower. With the first flight of steps reportedly gone, nobody should try to climb it. Check who manages the land before any visit.
