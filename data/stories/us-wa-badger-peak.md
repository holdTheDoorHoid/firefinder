A plan for a lookout on Badger Peak was reported in April 1926.[^1] A Chehalis newspaper said the first tower would stand on the ridge between the Lewis and Cispus rivers and watch over eastern Lewis and Skamania counties.[^1] Each tower was to have a ranger's cabin topped by a glassed-in cupola, which would hold an Osborne fire finder.[^1]

The sources disagree about the first structure. The register says a cabin was built in 1924.[^2] Willhite notes that the sources give 1924, 1926 or 1928, and that it is unclear what was built.[^3] In the 1930s the first structure was replaced with an L-4 cab.[^2][^3] Panoramic photographs taken in 1929 are credited to the National Archives Pacific Northwest Region.[^2]

No staffing history appears in the sources available for this story.

The L-4 cab came down in 1960.[^2][^3] Willhite says it was removed or destroyed that year, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed in the 1960s.[^3][^4]

Willhite's page describes a USGS marker on the summit, labeled "Badger LO RM 1937." A 2006 trip report on the same page mentions "some old evidence of a lookout" at the summit but gives no detail.[^3] No source here says what, if anything, stands there now. Willhite lists the summit as 3.5 miles away, reached through the Mosquito Meadows trailhead off Forest Road 28 in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^2][^3] No closure is mentioned. Willhite also cautions that this site is different from the former Badger Mountain lookout above Wenatchee.[^3]
