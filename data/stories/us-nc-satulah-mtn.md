Satulah Mountain, in Macon County, sits close to downtown Highlands, inside a radius of several miles.[^1][^3] A list of North Carolina lookout towers includes it as Satulah Mtn.[^1]

In 1909 members of the town of Highlands raised $500 and bought 56 acres on the mountain, to keep a hotel from going up on the summit.[^2] Their aim was to keep the top natural and make it a park for the community.[^2] A local history list gives the 1909 summit item to the Highlands Improvement Society.[^5] A small stone shelter, undated in the sources, was built there for overnight hikers.[^2]

A regional magazine says the summit later housed a U.S. Forest Service fire tower and a cottage.[^2] A Smoky Mountain News column adds that potholes in the rock are taken as a sign of a former fire tower on the summit.[^3]

The Satulah Mountain Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places on October 5, 1995.[^4] The local list counts the summit itself among the district's entries.[^5] The district is residential; log houses are among its homes, and many have Craftsman features.[^4]

Today the Highlands-Cashiers Land Trust lists the Satulah Mountain Preserve among its nine public properties.[^2] Its three-mile hike is rated difficult and aimed at experienced hikers.[^2] At the top, the stone shelter survives only as remnants, and the view spans 270 degrees across North Carolina, South Carolina and Georgia.[^2] The Plateau article's description of the summit today mentions no tower.[^2] None of the sources found describes a tower standing there now.
