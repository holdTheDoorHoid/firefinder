The Point Mountain tower overlooks the Webster Springs area in Webster County, West Virginia, on a summit that is commonly known as Potato Knob.[^1][^2] A 1913 state report records $81 spent on a contract to set up a lookout structure there.[^1] By 1916 a watchman was posted at the station, living twelve miles away and staying there each night, and seven miles of telephone line had been built to it.[^1]

The Register dates the tower to 1925, when the West Virginia Conservation Commission built a 60-foot Aermotor tower, and it says a concrete block observer's cabin was added in 1952.[^2] A 1973 Charleston Gazette article, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says the tower was originally built to 60 feet in 1927, so the build year is disputed.[^1] The 40-foot extension came in 1965, bringing the tower to 100 feet.[^2] The elevations also differ: the Register gives 3,364 feet, and the 1973 article gives 3,500.[^2][^1]

In 1963 a blind observer, E.D. Harrison, relayed fire traffic during deer season, both for other towers in the area and for his own reports.[^1] A helper with no earlier fire-tower experience spotted smoke for him, according to a Post Herald-Register story quoted by Forest Lookouts.[^1]

The Register says the tower is no longer manned on a regular basis but is kept up for emergencies, and it lists the West Virginia Division of Forestry as administrator.[^2] The tower was registered on September 10, 1998, on the nomination of Ralph P. Glover, Jr., Deputy State Forester.[^2] A West Virginia lookout list shows the tower as standing.[^3]

The Register page links to a 2015 video of a climb.[^2] None of the sources read says whether the tower is open to visitors now. A separate Potato Hill tower site in the Former Fire Lookout Sites register has an unknown location within Webster County, so that listing may not describe this tower.[^4] Check with the state Division of Forestry before any visit, and do not climb a tower that is closed.
