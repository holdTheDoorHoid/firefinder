The tower at Lenoir City sits in Loudon County, at about 1,252 feet (382 m) above sea level.[^1] The Tennessee Landforms list gives it the state forest number 2-5 and marks it standing.[^1] The list says those numbers are district-tower numbers from a 1976 list of state towers.[^1]

We found no build date, builder, or early history for this tower in the sources we read. The Division of Forestry ran its fire lookout network from 1933 to 1975, and the Civilian Conservation Corps is credited with building 98 fire lookout towers in Tennessee in the 1930s.[^2] A 2014 National Register form lists a Lenoir City tower in Loudon County among the extant Tennessee fire towers as of March 26, 2014.[^2]

A National Weather Service page for a Division of Forestry weather station in western Loudon County says a state forestry office sits just northeast of the station, next to a fire tower.[^3] The station is at 1,247 feet.[^3] The page does not name the tower, so matching it to this record is our inference. The two elevations are close.[^1][^3]

Today we found no public access information. The Tennessee Landforms site advises people not to climb its towers, because steps are often gone or rotten.[^1] If this is the tower beside the state office, it may stand on a working state site, so ask the Division of Forestry before going near it.
