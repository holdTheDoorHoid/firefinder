The Table Mountain lookout stood on a peak about 12 miles south-southeast of Toledo in Lincoln County.[^2] In 1915 the Forest Service planned to run a telephone line to the summit, where a lookout station would be set up.[^3] The Osbornes Project dates the lookout's first establishment to 1915 and says it was set up through a telephone connection.[^4] By 1917 the line reached a government lookout on top of the mountain.[^3] In 1921 the Siuslaw National Forest sent a fire-finding instrument to the station. Lookouts used it to take angle readings and telephone them to headquarters, where the fire was located.[^3]

Sources disagree about the first structure. The register and firelookout.com describe a crow's nest from the 1930s,[^1][^2] while the Osbornes Project says a cabin and tower were built in 1931.[^4] In September 1931 newspapers reported a new tower going up on the north side of the mountain. Later that month one paper gave its height as 60 feet, but a June 1932 report gave it as 50 feet.[^3]

A man was sent to the peak in 1926 to help locate fires.[^3] In the early 1930s the papers name a lookout man called Kocher, given as Pat, as H. L., and as "Pap" in different items; they appear to mean one man.[^3] A June 1932 report said Kocher had served in the role for 11 years.[^3]

In 1958 the Lincoln FPA's annual report says a 100-foot tower and a 16 by 22 foot ground cabin were built on the summit, for use in the 1959 season.[^3] The same report says that tower had been dismantled at Little Hebo lookout and rebuilt on Table Mountain.[^3] The register dates the replacement to 1959 and describes a 102-foot timber tower.[^1]

Little Hebo's own register describes its 101-foot steel tower, built in 1935, as moved to Table in 1959.[^5] The Table register and the 1958 report both describe timber, so the sources differ on what the tower was made of.[^1][^3][^5]

The tower was taken down in 1973.[^3][^4] The Osbornes Project describes its end as a dismantling, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^4][^2]

Today the register lists the site as administered by the Lincoln FPA and the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^1] No source I found describes what remains on the summit now, or whether visitors can reach it.
