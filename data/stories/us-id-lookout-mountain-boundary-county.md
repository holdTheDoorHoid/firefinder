Lookout Mountain rises above the northern end of Priest Lake, in Idaho.[^4] The register says the mountain was first used for fire detection in 1921, and Idaho Fire Lookouts calls that early role a patrol point.[^1][^3]

The cupola cabin was completed in 1929.[^1][^2] firelookout.com calls it an L-2 design.[^2] In 1939 a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab went up, and the register calls it CCC-built.[^1][^2]

The mountain was abandoned as an active fire detection site in 1947.[^1] The register says the pole tower collapsed around 1965, and firelookout.com puts its fall in the 1960s.[^1][^2]

In 1977 the Idaho Department of Lands moved a steel tower from Packsaddle Lookout and topped it with a wooden cab.[^1] The register gives the cab as 12 by 12 feet, while firelookout.com gives 15 by 15 feet.[^1][^2]

The register says the cupola was restored in 1983 to 1992, and that the site was registered on December 20, 1993.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts places the restoration in the 1980s and says the cupola still stands.[^3]

According to firelookout.com, vandalism in 2017 led to the removal of the wooden cab.[^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts says the 1977 steel tower was dismantled in 2019, and that a restoration was planned as of 2024, with completion estimated for 2026.[^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as hike-in and abandoned.[^3] The same site describes the final six miles of road to the trailhead as rough.[^3] SummitPost says the summit is reached by hiking a trail.[^4] A visit report dated April 11, 2006, in the register noted a large hole in the floor by the door.[^1] Check with the Idaho Department of Lands before you go, and do not enter the structure.
