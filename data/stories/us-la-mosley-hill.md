Mosley Hill Lookout stands in the piney woods of the Kisatchie National Forest, in Louisiana's Grant Parish.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service, through its Catahoula Ranger District, put up an all-steel tower and cab here in 1949.[^1][^2] The cab itself was unusual for the region: a 13-by-13-foot box with a catwalk, carried roughly 110 feet in the air, far taller than the 7-by-7-foot cabs common to Southern lookouts.[^1]

A lookout staffed the tower until 1981.[^1] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2003, nominated by District Archeologist Mark H. Gutzman as US 513 / LA 4.[^1]

In 2006 the tower's story took another turn. A highway-widening project had been surveyed straight through the lookout's original site, and on February 1 of that year crews began taking the cab down from the tower as the first step of a planned move.[^2] The steel tower and cab were slated to go to a hill near the Catahoula Ranger District office, on the site of a former Civilian Conservation Corps camp, for restoration and re-erection.[^1][^2]

Whether that restoration was finished, and whether the tower stands and is open to visitors today, is not recorded in the sources checked for this page.
