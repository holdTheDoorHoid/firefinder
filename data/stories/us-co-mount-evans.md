Mount Evans is the high peak that dominates the skyline west of Denver.[^2] The lookout site lies in Clear Creek County, Colorado, and the register puts it at 14,179 feet (4,322 meters).[^1]

The Forest Service built a temporary lookout building at the summit in 1942.[^1] The register says it may have been the highest lookout in the National Forest System while it stood.[^1] A photo captioned August 14, 1942 gives its purpose: to find out whether observations from the summit were worth more than those from lower stations.[^1]

The lookout did not last long. OuterSpatial says it remained only a couple of years.[^2]

OuterSpatial says Denverites have long made the summit a favorite destination.[^2] OuterSpatial says the trail to the summit starts at a parking area and is never closed to use, but the road to the trailhead is closed to motorized use from Labor Day until Memorial Day.[^2] Do not confuse the lookout with the Crest House, a restaurant at the end of the road that burned in 1979.[^2] OuterSpatial says its stone walls are all that remain.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the 1942 lookout building.[^1][^2]
