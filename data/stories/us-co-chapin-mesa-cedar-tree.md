The Chapin Mesa lookout stands inside Mesa Verde National Park in Montezuma County, Colorado.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register also calls it Cedar Tree Lookout.[^1] That name overlaps with an older site: the park's page for Cedar Tree Tower, near the four-way intersection on Chapin Mesa, says towers at Mesa Verde were mainly built in the Classic period, A.D. 1100 to 1300. It does not mention the lookout.[^3]

The lookout was constructed in 1945.[^1] According to the Register, the tower is 42 feet tall, with an open 6-by-6-foot cab on surplus 8-inch well casing and a steel ladder welded to one side.[^1] The Forest Lookouts summary instead describes a 42-foot pole tower with a covered platform, so the two accounts differ on the top.[^2] The Register also says the roof was taken from a road grader's cab.[^1]

The Register's 2019 review says the lookout is staffed as needed, on a short-term basis. Its role is limited to finding smoke reports that the Park Point lookout cannot see.[^1] The sources read name no particular fire it spotted and no regular crew.

The same review calls the tower in good shape, though its support cables need some maintenance and its platform needs repair.[^1] The Register accepted the lookout on March 16, 2010, as US 875, CO 13, on a nomination by Allen Farnsworth, Fire Management Officer.[^1]

The sources read do not say whether visitors can climb the tower or reach it on foot, and they do not describe any rental. Because the lookout sits in a national park, check with park staff before planning a visit.
