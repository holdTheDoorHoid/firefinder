Henkle Butte is a small butte in Deschutes County, Oregon.[^1][^2] The Oregon Department of Forestry owns the lookout, and firelookout.com says it is staffed every season.[^1][^2] Sources put it five to six miles from Sisters.[^2][^3] A 2014 Bulletin article puts it roughly a mile to the northeast of Sisters, which does not match the others.[^4]

The name comes from a contest. A commander at Camp Polk offered a prize to the soldier who could make the quickest trip to the butte and back, and Jeremiah F. Henkle won it.[^5] The name is often written as Hinkle, a mistake the Roaming Civic flags.[^5]

The first lookout was a live-in cab on a 40-foot tower.[^1] Firelookout.com and the Bulletin date that tower to the 1940s.[^2][^4] The Roaming Civic gives 1943 and a 42-foot tower built from recycled material by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^5] The sources disagree on the height and the date.

The present cab is a three-story enclosed ODF cab built in 1961. It replaced the older tower.[^1][^5]

In 1953 the station opened for the fire season, and its lookout was starting a third year on the butte.[^3] A 2014 Bulletin article says a lookout staffs the cab from about mid-June to October, every day of the week.[^4]

Firelookout.com says the lookout reports many fires.[^2] One documented case is from July 1958.[^3] One of several lightning fires, caused by a severe electrical storm, broke out again about two miles northeast of Three Creeks Butte, on land owned by Brooks-Scanlon, Inc.[^3] The lookout on Henkle Butte found it.[^3]

A 2021 visit recorded the tower as currently standing.[^5] The same visit report says it is not accessible to the public, though it can be viewed from the road. Much of the area is private property, and posted signs should be respected.[^5] Treat it as a view from the road, not a place to climb.
