Timber Knob is a fire lookout site in Ozark County, Missouri.[^1][^3] The Missouri Conservation Commission reported acquiring the towersite in fiscal 1951–52.[^1] A new tower was erected there in fiscal 1952–53.[^1] A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1953 places the survey station on the summit and describes a four-legged steel tower, painted aluminum and about 50 feet (15 m) high.[^1]

According to a Missouri Conservationist article, fire towers were the best means of spotting wildfires for decades.[^4] A 2015 area plan for the Howell and Ozark County towersites describes a new self-supported tower on the Timber Knob site. It carries radio relays for the department, White River Valley Electric Coop and Ozark County mutual aid, and department staff were using it for fire detection at the time.[^2]

The conservation area is open to the public daily from 4 a.m. to 10 p.m.[^3] The department's directions start at Gainesville: west on Highway 160 for 2.5 miles, then south on Highway 5 for 2.25 miles.[^3] The department's page describes the site as completely forested, with towers for radio relay and wildfire detection.[^3] The 2015 plan says the access road is shut to vehicles whenever the tower is unmanned.[^2]
