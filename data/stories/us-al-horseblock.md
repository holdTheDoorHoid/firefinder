The Horseblock Lookout Tower stands on Horseblock Mountain in Cleburne County, Alabama, inside the Talladega National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1][^2] The Register describes a 100-foot Aermotor tower whose staircase runs on a diagonal, but it gives no build year.[^1]

A photo in the Register is dated August 1948, which suggests the tower was standing by then.[^1] The sources read say nothing about who staffed the tower or which fires it watched, so the early years are hard to pin down.

The one serious incident in the sources is from July 1976. A homemade bomb went off on the stairs as a young man carried it up the tower, nearly to the top. A Decatur Daily report, quoted by the Register and by the Forest Lookouts site, says the blast killed him and damaged the tower.[^1][^2] The Register lists the tower as registered on July 24, 2022.[^1]

Wikimedia Commons holds four photos of the tower taken in November 2024. One file title mentions stairs with missing steps, and another looks up through the stairs and tower.[^3] The sources do not describe access or say whether the public may visit or climb it. A file title mentions an old jeep trail approaching the tower, but that is not an access rule.[^3] Do not climb it. Check with the national forest office before you go, since the photo titles point to missing steps.
