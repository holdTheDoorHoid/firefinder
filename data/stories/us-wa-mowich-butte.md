Mowich Butte is a summit in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, in Skamania County, Washington.[^1][^2] Willhite says a first lookout building stood on the summit before 1934, with its living space just down the slope.[^3] He says an L-4 cab later replaced it, possibly in 1937.[^3] The register and FireLookout.com both state 1937 as the cab's build year, without the hedge Willhite uses.[^1][^2]

Panoramic photos were taken on July 6, 1934,[^4] and Willhite says the views show the summit inside the Yacolt Fire burn area, before any of the snags had been felled.[^3] In a 2002 interview, Pete Paladeni said his Mowich stint came when he was new to the CCC.[^3] He spent a week on the lookout while the man on duty, who had a dental problem, wanted to go home to Spokane for treatment.[^3]

A survey team mentioned the lookout in 1965.[^3] A Coast and Geodetic Survey station description dated 1946 uses the Forest Service lookout house as a reference point.[^4] The register and FireLookout.com both say the cab was abandoned in the 1960s and is now gone.[^1][^2]

None of the sources read describes what remains on the summit today. Willhite's photos are captioned "Lookout site in 2017," "Summit in trees," and "View through trees to Mt. Adams."[^3] Willhite's route notes start at the blocked road and follow an overgrown old road up the hillside, which he calls a bushwhack with a hiking distance under half a mile.[^3] The sources do not say whether the site is open to the public.
