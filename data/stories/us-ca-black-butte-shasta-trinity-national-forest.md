A lookout station on the summit of Black Butte, near Mount Shasta, was due for dedication in October 1931, and the clippings report that the trail up to it was dedicated that month.[^2] A Forest Service sheet gives a different start for the trail, saying the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in the late 1930s.[^3]

Forest Service lookouts staffed the station through the 1940s and into the 1960s.[^2] Frank Jackson was on duty in the 1940s, and in May 1939 the lookout spotted a house fire in south Dunsmuir and raised the alarm, though the house was destroyed.[^2] Cecelia Thompson, of Dunsmuir, took the job in 1961 and came back for a second season in 1962.[^2]

The Columbus Day storm of October 1962 wrecked the station.[^2][^3] The sources disagree about what it took. FFLOS says a Plan 4AR ground cabin was blown off the mountain that year.[^1] The newspaper clippings say the original building, which dated from 1931, blew away.[^2]

The Forest Service rebuilt the station in 1963 as a metal cabin, and the materials went up by helicopter.[^2][^3] FFLOS describes that cabin as a C-16 all-steel cabin.[^1] Mildred Weston moved into the new lookout in the summer of 1963.[^2]

The Forest Service says the cabin operated until 1973 and was moved by helicopter to a new location in 1975.[^3] FFLOS dates the move to Hogback Mountain to 1977, and an undated clipping also places the cabin on Hogback Mountain.[^1][^2] The sources do not agree on the year.

Today only a concrete foundation is left at the summit, according to the Forest Service.[^3] The summit is reached on foot. The trail runs about 2.56 miles from a trailhead off a gravel road, climbs about 1,845 feet, and has no water along the way.[^3] The Forest Service keeps the trail up every year, but its surface is rocky, and some stretches are steep.[^3]
