The Aermotor Company prefabricated this steel lookout in 1932, and the Wisconsin Conservation Commission first erected it at a remote spot in the Nicolet National Forest.[^1][^2] The Forest Service, by contrast, dates the tower to 1935 and credits the Forest Service and the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it.[^3]

That first site lay about 2.5 miles (4 km) east-southeast of where the tower stands now.[^1] The southwest pier is inscribed with the date 10/22/32.[^1] Getting to the original spot meant a foot trail, since no road came close, and that isolation is why the tower was moved.[^1]

In 1935 the Civilian Conservation Corps took the tower apart and, the nomination believes, rebuilt it on ground the Forest Service bought from the Holt Lumber Company that January.[^1][^2]

CCC enrollees manned the lookout and fought fires in the area.[^1] The nomination cites a former CCC enrollee who staffed the tower in 1940.[^1] Between May and September, spotters watched from the cab by day, and the last fire reported from the tower was on 25 April 1970.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a gone tower called "Mountain – first," which is probably the first site, though the list does not say so. The standing tower is listed separately as "Mountain."[^4]

Today the moved tower stands about 2.5 miles (4 km) north of the community of Mountain, Wisconsin, off Forest Road 2106 (Old 32).[^3] It is a 100-foot (30 m) steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and visitors may climb to the top.[^3] The Wisconsin Historical Society's register record says it is open to the public.[^2] At the first site, the 2008 nomination found that the four rubble piers on which the tower rested remained.[^1] This research found no source describing that spot after 2008.
