## Dry Hill Land

Dry Hill Land tower stands on State Game Lands #91 in Luzerne County, Pennsylvania, in what was once the Lackawanna forest fire district overseen by the Pennsylvania Department of Forests and Waters.[^2] The Aermotor Windmill Company built the original tower here in 1921.[^1]

For decades the job of watching from it stayed close to one family. In 1922, at age fifteen, a young woman known locally as "Honey" was appointed tower watcher by R. Lynn Emmerick, then district forester for the Lackawanna District — making her, by a 1949 newspaper's account, "the only woman in the State at present holding such a job."[^2] Her post sat 1,921 feet above sea level by one 1949 account and 1,931 feet by a 1948 one, about five and a half miles east of Bear Creek village and three miles from the Lehigh River, and it covered ground in five counties: Luzerne, Lackawanna, Monroe, Delaware and Carbon.[^2]

The tower kept working through the 1940s and 50s. In 1946 it was one of eleven towers across the five-county district being inspected and repaired ahead of fall fire season.[^2] In August 1948 the district announced it would operate the tower starting that October, with Mrs. Jennie Bempkins of Buck Township in charge.[^2] By 1955 the watcher was Peter Dubinsky, working from a site a local paper placed on the "Conlon Estate" near Bear Creek; from there he could spot fires around Bear Creek and Thornhurst that the Penobscot and Tark Hill towers couldn't see, and on a clear day sight as far as "Goulsboro."[^2]

What happened to the tower after that is unclear, and the two sources behind this page disagree. A Pennsylvania fire-tower StoryMap says the original 1921 tower was replaced in 2018, with a tower (now given as 80 feet tall, though the same entry also calls it "the 60 foot tower") still on the site today.[^1] A register of eastern lookout histories, by contrast, marks Dry Land Hill simply "Removed," with no date given.[^2] It isn't clear whether that refers to the 1921 structure coming down ahead of its 2018 replacement, or to the site being cleared altogether — so whether anything currently stands at Dry Hill Land is genuinely unresolved.

There is nothing in either source about current access, staffing, or whether the site can be visited.
