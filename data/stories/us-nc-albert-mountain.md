A log lookout cabin has existed on Albert Mountain since as early as 1942, according to the Forest Service.[^2] In 1951 the U.S. Forest Service built a steel tower on the summit.[^1][^2] It was meant to replace abandoned lookout towers on Big Pinnacle and Standing Indian mountains, and to help detect fires for the Coweeta Hydrologic Laboratory.[^2] The register mentions only a wooden tower on Standing Indian Mountain as the one it replaced.[^1]

Sources disagree on the size and the cab. The register describes a 40-foot steel tower with a 14-by-14-foot live-in cab and catwalk.[^1] The Forest Service gives the tower a height of 55 feet.[^2] Another site describes an L4 cab with a hip metal roof.[^3]

The register says the lookout is kept up and called on in emergencies to back up air patrols.[^1] It recorded the tower in December 1993.[^1]

A June 2010 visit report found the tower unstaffed and apparently abandoned for some time, with nearly all equipment gone and little glass left.[^1] The same report marked the stairs as not in good order, but noted that the open doors allowed splendid views from the top.[^1]

Today the register describes it as a popular stop along the Appalachian Trail.[^1] The Forest Service lists a moderate hike of about half a mile one way to the tower.[^2] Forest Service Road 67 leads to a small gravel parking area, and the trail climbs to the tower from there.[^2] Vehicle access closes for a seasonal period from December 31 to April 1.[^2] A third site says visitors can take in views in three directions from the stairs, though the top cab is usually kept locked.[^3] The 2010 visit report flagged the stairs, so check their condition before climbing, and respect the locked cab.[^1]
