The Hohenwald Lookout is in Lewis County, Tennessee.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it an active Tennessee Department of Forestry site, and Forest Lookouts names the Tennessee Division of Forestry as its agency.[^1][^2] The Register lists it as US 1083, TN 22. The nomination came from Jerry Lutes, and the Register gives the date registered as January 3, 2015.[^1]

The sources read do not say when the tower went up. The Register page carries a 1952 photograph, courtesy of the Tennessee Secretary of State, which suggests the tower was already standing by then.[^1]

A visit report dated March 4, 2006 describes the tower as a 60-foot diagonal stairs-and-steel structure on state property.[^1] Tennessee's forestry agency erected many of its lookout towers in the 1930s, and a National Register study of the state's tower program says towers built between 1933 and 1942 were primarily built and staffed by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^4] The sources read do not connect Hohenwald to that program directly.[^1][^3][^4]

The same visitor found the cab open, with a firefinder table and base still inside. The visit report says the cab showed the tower had not been used for observation for some time, and it marks the tower as not staffed.[^1]

Tennessee Landforms lists the tower as standing.[^3] The visit report says the site sits right along the highway, with directions signed, but it also notes a house that appears lived in and several maintenance sheds. Because it is an active state forestry site, check with the Division of Forestry before going up. The Register page gives no climbing rules.[^1]
