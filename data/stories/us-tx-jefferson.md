The Jefferson lookout tower first stood in Marion County, Texas, about 1.5 miles west-northwest of the town of Jefferson.[^1][^3] A 1958 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description, reproduced on a Central US lookout page, describes a glass-walled cab on a four-legged steel frame, roughly 100 feet tall overall and painted gray.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes the tower as a 100-foot Aermotor MC-39 with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] We did not find when it was built.

In September 1957 a new crew leader joined the Texas Forest Service at the Jefferson lookout tower.[^3] A Marshall News Messenger item dated September 30, 1957, quoted on the Central US page, says his district covers about 150,000 acres.[^3] We found no fire the tower is known to have watched.

In 2003 the Jeffersonian Institute moved the tower to downtown Jefferson, according to the register.[^1] The Texas lookout list shows the original site as gone, and a relocated tower at the new spot as standing.[^2]

Today the tower stands downtown and is owned by Etex Wireless, which uses it as a cell tower.[^1] The register says the Jefferson Office of Tourism Development and the Marion County Chamber of Commerce are offering to help the company repair the steps and open the tower as a site for local history and education.[^1] We found no report that this has happened, and no source says the public can climb it. Please do not climb it, and check with the owner before you visit.
