East Mountain's first lookout was a two-story log house, 18 by 18 feet, built in 1914 by the Southern Idaho Timber Protective Association.[^1] A Civilian Conservation Corps crew later replaced it with a similar but larger 20-by-20-foot log house, built under the direction of two Finnish carpenters.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1929 as the build year, but its own text says the 1930s, and other pages say 1929 or 1930.[^1][^2][^3][^6] A 1997 Associated Press story called the standing lookout three-story, while the Forest Lookouts list describes the 20-by-20-foot replacement as two-story.[^6][^7]

The lookout was staffed through 1995, when it was decommissioned.[^1][^2][^5] A 1997 Associated Press story described the mountaintop as Forest Service land leased to the timber association, which no longer had a use for the site.[^7] That same year the lookout was added to the Register, the first Idaho log house on that list.[^3]

A storm with dry lightning in late June or early July 2002 started a five-acre fire on the lookout's east side.[^1] Wind pushed flaming fragments uphill, and the building caught fire and burned before help arrived.[^1] Another site says the blaze came in late June and destroyed the cabin.[^5] A preservation group had been restoring the lookout when the fire came.[^1] One 2006 chapter report says it burned in 2001, but the other sources give 2002.[^8][^1][^4][^5]

The 1997 Associated Press story said the timber association was willing to sell the lookout for $1. It also said a new East Mountain Lookout Committee would be part of the Central Idaho Cultural Center in McCall.[^7] Another site says the center had already bought it, with plans to restore it.[^5] The two accounts do not match.[^5][^7]

A 2019 climbing guide has a photo captioned as showing the lookout ruins that year.[^4] The guide describes a road to the summit on Boise National Forest land, with a 4WD vehicle recommended and private land at the start of the approach.[^1][^4] We found no source describing the site after 2019. Because no standing lookout remains to visit, check with the Boise National Forest about current access before going up.
