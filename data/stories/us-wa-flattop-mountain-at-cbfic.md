The prototype cab that once sat on Flattop Mountain, in Washington's Gifford Pinchot National Forest, now stands at the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center (CBFIC) in Entiat, as a rebuilt structure.[^1][^2][^5]

The Forest Service cab went up on the summit in 1946, built by the Alladin Company of Portland, Oregon.[^1][^4] It was a two-story frame cab with slanted windows and curved hip roof.[^3][^4] The sources disagree on its size. Two give 14 by 14 feet, while the National Historic Lookout Register gives 12 by 12.[^3][^4][^5] It was intended to replace the region's standard Forest Service lookout, but the simpler R-6 flat-top design became the norm instead.[^5][^4]

WillhiteWeb names Mr. and Mrs. Ernest Fiedler as the summit's lookouts in 1952. Once a local school opened, the couple took up their teaching jobs, and Forest Couch covered the rest of the fire season.[^3]

The summit was abandoned in 1960.[^4][^3] The landowner, the state Department of Natural Resources, threatened for years to tear the cab down.[^4][^2] According to FireLookout.com and WillhiteWeb, the Forest Fire Lookout Association dismantled it in 2005 and moved it to Entiat.[^4][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register instead says the tower was disassembled by 2025.[^6] The register's own description says the lookout had been nearly torn down to clear room for a microwave tower, and a May 2005 visit report found the building neglected, with a hole in the roof.[^5]

The Association later donated the parts to CBFIC, which hoped to rebuild the cab in full.[^2] Most of the original parts were too damaged to reuse.[^2] The work crew kept the surviving beams, window supports and rafters and rebuilt the roof, so the new cab has one story where the original had two.[^2] Construction ran from October 2009 to spring 2010.[^2] WillhiteWeb calls it a replica of the top story, partly built from salvaged wood, particularly in the roof and window frames.[^1] SummitPost reports a split: some people regard it as an original lookout that was moved, while many others, the CBFIC among them, regard it as a replica.[^2]

SummitPost says the center exists to increase public understanding of fire, through interpretive trails and signs, a visitor center and an amphitheater.[^2] The center's own page gives its address as 15212 Highway 97A in Entiat, while WillhiteWeb places the center on US Highway 97.[^7][^1] None of the sources read says whether the rebuilt cab itself can be entered. At the original summit, the register says the tower was disassembled by 2025, and no source read describes what remains there.[^6]
