Blackstone is a lookout in Alberta that the province's wildfire lookout register records as a cabin rather than a tower. The register gives its height as 5 m (16 ft) and its elevation as about 1,576 m (5,169 ft), and it lists 1961 as the year of construction.[^1]

After 1912, Alberta's lookouts took two forms: summit lookouts built on the ground or on short stilts, and towers on high hills in the boreal forest that lifted the observer over any obstacles blocking the view.[^2] The province says its sites are picked for clear sightlines, so that fires can be detected and reported.[^3]

Staffing is described in two ways that do not quite match. Alberta says each lookout site has an observer on watch, generally from April to October.[^4] A 2015 Alberta Wildfire review says most lookouts run through the core fire season, while the rest are crewed only when hazard and risk peak.[^5]

The register still lists Blackstone as active, notes a weather station on the site, and codes its access mode as "drive".[^1] Alberta calls its lookout sites isolated and says staff need to be ready for a varied set of situations, including a run-in with a curious bear.[^4] No source says the public may visit, and none describes what the cabin looks like today.
