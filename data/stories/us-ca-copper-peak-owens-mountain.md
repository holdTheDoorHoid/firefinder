Owens Mountain stands roughly 1,000 feet above the valley floor, overlooking the north side of Clovis and the northeast part of Fresno.[^1] In 1935 the state put a fire lookout on its summit and named it Copper Peak. KVPR says it gave early warning of fires in the foothills and in the expanding Fresno and Clovis area.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site lists the station under the California Department of Forestry.[^2]

In July 1944 a grass fire broke out on the army rifle range near Owens Mountain and ran east over the mountain. The Copper Peak station was the first to report it, shortly before 3 p.m.[^2] A state photograph dated November 22, 1957 shows the garage at the lookout.[^3]

Perry David Corbly, a retired state lookout, had been posted at the Copper Peak station until his 1965 retirement, according to his 1966 obituary in The Fresno Bee.[^2] The lookout closed in 1980.[^1][^4] It burned down in 1985,[^4] and KVPR calls that fire an arson.[^1] The Fire Lookout Association lists the structure as an enclosed tower and marks it gone.[^5]

According to KVPR, the summit now hosts communications masts plus an automated wildfire-watch station.[^1] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains of the original lookout, or whether the summit is open to the public. Check before you visit.
