Red Mountain Lookout sits on the summit of Red Mountain in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, about four miles northwest of Trout Lake in Skamania County, Washington.[^1][^2][^4] The first structure on the summit was an 8-by-8-foot cedar cab.[^2][^3][^4] Sources disagree on its year: firelookout.com and the Washington Trails Association say 1910, while Willhite's history of the site and PCT Washington give 1913.[^2][^3][^4][^5] A cupola cabin replaced it around 1919, and in 1935 an L-4 cab went up beside a garage.[^2][^4] PCT Washington dates that garage to 1938.[^3]

The present tower dates to 1959.[^2][^4] It is a timber tower about ten feet tall with a flat-topped wooden cab, and the register calls it the fourth lookout on the site.[^1]

During World War II the summit served as an Aircraft Warning Station, and the register says it was staffed around the clock in 1942 and 1943.[^1] The garage became living quarters in 1942 while that work went on.[^2][^4] The tower is no longer staffed.[^5] The Washington Trails Association says satellites now handle fire detection, and Willhite's history says the Forest Service no longer uses it to spot fires.[^4][^5]

A windstorm on December 14, 2006, severely damaged the lookout and blew its roof and walls apart, according to PCT Washington.[^3] The same storm damaged radio facilities for the Skamania County Sheriff's Department.[^3] Other accounts place the storm in December 2006 or the winter of 2006.[^2][^5]

In 2007 Matt Haldeman, a custom home builder from the Vancouver area, launched volunteer work on the lookout's restoration.[^3][^4] The accounts differ on the crews: PCT Washington says Passport in Time volunteers donated labor in 2007 and 2008, while Willhite and WTA describe a 13-person Passport in Time crew working in 2010.[^3][^4][^5] The interior walls and flooring were finished in 2010.[^3]

In 2010 a district ranger said the forest was looking at ways for the public to enjoy the lookout, possibly as a rental.[^3] Firelookout.com says plans call for adding it to the Recreation Rental program, and Willhite says it may become a rental lookout in the near future.[^2][^4] These are plans in the sources, not a confirmed rental offer.[^2][^3][^4]

Vehicles cannot reach the top, because the access road is permanently gated down near its junction with Forest Road 60.[^5] The Washington Trails Association describes starting from a trailhead on Forest Road 65, following the Indian Racetrack Trail about 3.4 miles to the gated road, then climbing to the tower base.[^5]
