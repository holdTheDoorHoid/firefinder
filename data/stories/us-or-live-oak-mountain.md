Live Oak Mountain lies in Douglas County, Oregon, at 3,429 feet.[^1] A local item from 1915 reports that a telephone crew had just finished eight miles of line from Camas Valley to Live Oak Mountain.[^3] By September 1934 a station was staffed there, and panoramic photos had been taken from the site in June.[^3] The notes on the prints describe it as a cooperative project of the Umpqua National Forest and the Douglas FPA.[^3]

In August 1934 the Umpqua forest supervisor wrote to the regional forester that the lookout house was in storage at a CCC camp, and reported that two men, Mr. Mercer and Mr. Southwick, favored a 40- to 50-foot treated tower for it.[^3] The house built in 1937 sat on a 10-foot timber tower, put up by men from the Bradford CCC camp.[^3] The register dates the lookout to 1937 and gives it as a 20-foot tower, so the sources disagree on height.[^1][^2]

Staff were named in the local news through the 1930s. Mr. and Mrs. Bob Smith were at the station in 1934, John LeTourneux was the lookout in 1935, and Gordon Harness ran it during the 1938 summer.[^3]

In December 1941 volunteer civilian observers were staffing Live Oak for the air raid warning service.[^3] In September 1942 the Army asked that it run as a year-round observation post, and in October 1943 the Army deactivated the post.[^3]

A 1957 item says Mrs. Brandon had been the lookout for five years, and a 1962 item says the Brandons had spent several summers on the mountain.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed around 1973, and firelookout.com calls it destroyed that year.[^1][^2] The Oregon lookout-history site records only that it was removed in 1973.[^3]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains on Live Oak Mountain today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
