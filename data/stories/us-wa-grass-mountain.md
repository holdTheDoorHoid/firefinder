Grass Mountain sits on a ridge about nine miles east of Enumclaw in King County.[^2][^3] The site's listing names the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as the agency.[^1]

A wooden tower with a cabin went up in 1936, and most sources give it a height of 100 feet.[^1][^2][^4] WillhiteWeb dates the tower to 1937 and puts it at 105 feet.[^3]

Lookouts staffed the site in every season from 1949 through 1960.[^3] Albert Miller was on duty for four of those seasons, 1953 through 1956.[^3] Sue Weidman is listed for 1960 and 1961.[^2] Sue Gunn was the last full-time lookout, in 1975.[^2][^3]

From March 7, 1942, to October 16, 1943, the Army used the site as an Aircraft Warning Service post. The AWS added a portable 14 by 32 cabin to the site.[^3][^4]

In 1958 the tower was cut down, and a DNR live-in cab was added on top.[^1][^2][^3] The sources disagree on the new height: 50 feet in the Former Fire Lookout Sites register and on firelookout.com, 70 feet on WillhiteWeb.[^1][^2][^3] In 1962 the ground house was sold and torn down to make way for an FAA tower.[^3] In 1972 the DNR Lands Division suggested the site would be better used as a communications tower lease.[^3]

The tower was felled in 1980, and WillhiteWeb calls it the last one in the district.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was gone by 1979.[^1] The 1980 date matches firelookout.com and the WillhiteWeb account, so this story uses 1980.[^2][^3]

A WillhiteWeb visit in 2016 was made with the equipment operator who had felled the tower.[^3] The old tower footings are now used for parking.[^3] The ridge's highest point carries communication towers.[^3]

The Muckleshoot tribe acquired the White River Tree Farm around 2013. As of 2016, Hancock Forest Management still ran the pay permit system on the tribe's behalf.[^3] In 2016 a non-motorized daily permit cost $8 for an individual and $15 for a family.[^3] The drive starts on Highway 410 east of Enumclaw, and the route runs on logging roads for about eight miles.[^3] These details date from 2016 or earlier, so check current permit rules before going.
