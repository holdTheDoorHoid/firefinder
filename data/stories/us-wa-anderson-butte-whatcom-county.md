Anderson Butte is a former lookout site in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, in Whatcom County, about 12 miles southeast of Mount Baker.[^2][^1] The site stands at 5,420 feet.[^1]

A camp was set up here in 1920.[^1][^4] In 1928 an account quoted by two sites says the earlier "fireman-lookout" was taken down that year, and that the telephone left on the lookout point was found torn down. The writer blamed a bear.[^3][^4]

Sources disagree on when the L-4 cab that followed was built. The Register and firelookout.com give 1936.[^1][^2] Willhite says it was likely built in 1935, and both Forest Lookouts and Willhite record panoramic photos taken on September 6, 1935. Willhite says the panoramas were taken from the cab's roof.[^3][^4]

The Register and Forest Lookouts say the cab was removed in 1968.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^4]

The Washington Trails Association describes a 2.5-mile round-trip hike to the site of the former lookout tower, climbing about 600 feet in half a mile.[^5] From the top, the views take in Mount Baker and Mount Shuksan.[^5] Parking requires an America the Beautiful Pass or a Northwest Forest Pass.[^5] Willhite notes that old trails once reached the site from the Komo Kulshan and Baker Lake guard stations.[^4] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout today.
