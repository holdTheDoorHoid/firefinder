The Deer Mountain fire tower stands on the summit in Pittsburg, New Hampshire, but only its steel frame is left.[^1][^2][^3] The first tower on the site went up in 1911, built by the New Hampshire Timberland Owners Association.[^1] By 1915 the state had taken over the site and erected a 30-foot wooden tower there. That tower was rebuilt in 1922, and a steel structure replaced it in 1933. That steel structure is the one that remains.[^1]

None of the sources I read names a watchman or describes a particular fire.[^1][^2][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2023 New Hampshire report says the tower ran into the mid-1970s before it closed.[^2] The register gives 1976 as the closing year.[^1]

The register says the cab was removed after the closure.[^1] The Association's report gives a different account. It says the state burned the cab around 1985, and that the stairs and landings burned at the same time.[^2] The trail guide says the cab came off once the tower was no longer in use, and that the wooden steps were removed later to discourage climbing.[^3] The sources agree the cab is gone, but they do not agree on how it went.[^1][^2][^3]

Trees now grow up through the center of the tower, according to the trail guide.[^3] The Cohos Trail Association has reopened an old trail to the tower.[^2] The report says the group is interested in rehabilitating the tower. The likely plan, the report says, is an open observation deck rather than an enclosed cab, and the next step it names is an engineering assessment.[^2]

The stairs and wooden steps are gone.[^2][^3] The trail guide says they were removed to discourage people from climbing what it calls an unsafe structure.[^3] Visitors can still reach the summit on foot along the Deer Mountain Firetower Trail, which the Cohos Trail Association manages.[^3] The trail follows a brook to the remains.[^3] Please stay off the structure.
