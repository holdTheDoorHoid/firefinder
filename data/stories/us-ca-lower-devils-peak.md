Lower Devils Peak is the end of a short ridge above Seiad Valley, in the Klamath National Forest.[^2] A lookout was built there in 1934, and a CCC crew completed the construction, according to a Klamath National Forest chronology quoted on a lookout-history site.[^1]

Getting material to the top was the hard part. A January 1934 entry in the Fox diary records a visit to Seiad to scout the trail the packers would use for lookout supplies.[^1] John Buck, a ranger on the new Siskiyou District, went along on a supply trip in which mules carried 20-foot floor joists up a trail he recalled as having perhaps as many as 25 switchbacks over about three miles. He and a colleague helped the animals negotiate the turns.[^1]

The building had an earlier life of its own. According to Buck, carpenters had already taken the old lookout house down from a short tower, planning to reuse it with alterations.[^1]

In July 1947 Lloyd Schmidt, a Klamath National Forest lookout, spotted a mountain lion about 100 feet from the station and shot it, according to a newspaper item quoted on the same site.[^1] In 1951 the Devils Fire came within 100 feet of the lookout building and forced out its lookout, Louis Meiss, who would not go back after the blaze.[^1]

The cab was later moved to Ukonom Mountain and set on a new base, though the history gives no date for the move.[^1] I found no source saying whether that cab still stands there.

The description I found of the site is a trip report that gives no trip date.[^2] Its writer came in along the Pacific Crest Trail (PCT) from the north, took a trail fork to the end of the ridge, and found an empty cinder-block structure, asking whether it was an old lookout without confirming it.[^2] The same report describes a steep route up from Seiad Valley as one option.[^2] No source confirms whether the cinder-block structure is the lookout.
