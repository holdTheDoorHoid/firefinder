Jackley Mountain Lookout is a ground cabin on a peak of roughly 8,750 feet in Adams County, Idaho, within the Payette National Forest.[^1][^2] The site began as a patrol point in 1928, and a 14-by-14 ground cabin with an L-4 cab went up on it in 1932.[^3][^4][^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register calls it an R1/L-4 ground cabin.[^1] The staff record is thin. Alton Harrington is the only lookout named in the sources I read, listed for 1942.[^2][^3][^5] The Forest Lookouts record says 1948 was the last season the lookout was staffed, and the NHLR says it was no longer staffed after 1948.[^4][^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts and firelookout.com both date its abandonment to 1949.[^2][^3]

Since then the lookout has stood abandoned, and firelookout.com says it is still there.[^2][^3] A June 2019 visit report on the NHLR marks the shutters, wood, stairs and glass as not OK and concludes the lookout appears to be beyond repair.[^1] The NHLR added it on September 29, 2020, with Brad Eells as nominator.[^1]

Getting there is the hard part. Idaho Fire Lookouts tags it as a hike-in, and the NHLR visit report notes there is no trail to the lookout.[^2][^1] A 1962 U.S. Geological Survey station description places it 5 miles north of a road end at Black Lake.[^4]

Today the cabin is still standing on National Forest land.[^1][^3] Given the 2019 report, do not climb it or rely on it for shelter.
