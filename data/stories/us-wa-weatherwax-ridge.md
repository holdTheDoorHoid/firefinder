In the mid-1940s, a tree on Weatherwax Ridge served as a stopgap lookout.[^2][^3] Willhite guesses it stood where the communication towers are now.[^2]

In 1956 the state's forestry agency, listed in the register as the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR, built a 40-foot wooden live-in tower on a logged-off hill in Grays Harbor County.[^1][^2][^3] Its cab measured 14 by 14 feet, was built of plywood and glass, and had an aluminum roof that projects past the walls to shade them.[^2][^3]

Keith Lundy Hoofnagle staffed the lookout from 1960 to 1963. He also drew cartoons, and while posted here he created the character Linda Lookout.[^2] Steve Karr was the lookout in 1964.[^2]

Removal came in 1968.[^1][^2][^3] FireLookout.com instead says it was destroyed that year, so the sources differ on how it ended.[^4]

No lookout tower stands today.[^1][^2] Communication towers now stand at one point on the ridge, while the old lookout was at another, on a lower knob.[^2] Willhite reports that a bronze survey mark stamped WEATHERWAX 1954 is still in place, and that the old road over the knob is still traceable.[^2]

A gate on the 200 road is there to keep people away from the highest ridge, and the sources do not say whether walkers may pass it.[^2] When Willhite returned in 2020, the brush had been cleared and parts of the road had been rocked.[^2] His route is 1.3 miles with 600 feet of gain, and he warns that the view stays closed off until the next timber harvest.[^2]
