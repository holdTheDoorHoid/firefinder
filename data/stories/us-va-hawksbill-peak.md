The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a Hawksbill Peak site in Madison and Page counties, but it files the entry as unconfirmed and gives its status as unknown.[^1] The listed point matches the coordinates Wikipedia gives for Hawksbill Mountain, Shenandoah National Park's highest peak.[^1][^2] The association has not confirmed a fire lookout at this spot.[^1]

Wikipedia credits the National Park Service with building a stone observation platform on the summit.[^2] The Shenandoah National Park Trust says Senator Harry Byrd gave the park its masonry observation platform in 1963.[^3]

For the National Park Service's centennial, the Trust restored the platform's masonry, replaced stolen bronze plaques, and improved the surface of a heavily used trail.[^3]

Visitors reach the platform on foot.[^4] The National Park Service route starts at the Hawksbill Gap parking area (mile 45.5), follows the Lower Hawksbill Trail past the Byrds Nest 2 shelter, and ends at the viewing platform.[^4] The view from the top covers 360 degrees.[^4] The park says closures on Hawksbill are kept to a minimum, to prevent further degradation of important outcrop ecosystems.[^4]
