The Maydelle tower stands in Cherokee County and is listed under the Texas Forest Service.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page quotes a National Geodetic Survey description of it as a standard-type steel lookout tower, painted gray and about 110 feet high, built in 1933.[^1] That survey numbers it Tower No. 8 of the Texas Forest Service, and a June 1939 newspaper item gives the same number.[^1] The 1939 item places it four miles southwest of Maydelle, while the survey says four miles west, so the direction is not settled.[^1]

A Civilian Conservation Corps camp opened near Maydelle in 1933, the same year the survey gives for the tower.[^1][^3] The camp's crews built lookout towers among other projects, but the sources read do not say they built this one.[^3]

Patrolmen worked the tower in 1946.[^1] In February 1946 the Rusk Cherokeean credited Forest Patrolman Ray Sides with reporting and putting out a January fire that an arsonist had started.[^1] An April 1946 item about March gives the patrolman as Roy Sides and credits him with seven of the county's 34 forest fires that month.[^1] The 1946 items spell the name two ways, and the sources read do not settle which is right.[^1] In January 1952 the Alto Herald reported that J.A. Benge, who had managed the Alto tower, was moving to Maydelle to take over the fire tower there.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list has two Maydelle sites in Cherokee County. It shows Loc 2 as standing and Loc 1 as gone.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page gives no coordinates, so the sources read do not show that its tower is the Loc 2 site.[^1]

The sources read do not say whether the public can visit the grounds or climb the tower.[^1] Ask the Texas Forest Service before any trip, and do not climb a tower that is closed or posted off limits.
