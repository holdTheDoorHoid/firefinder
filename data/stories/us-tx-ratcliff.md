In April 1928 the Texas Forest Service reported that it had just finished two steel fire towers, built in cooperation with the Southern Pine Lumber Company and the Houston County Timber Company.[^1] Each was 90 feet tall, with ladders, landing platforms and a glass-enclosed cabin about eight and a half feet square.[^1] One stood at Ratcliff, and the other on Bird Mountain in Anderson County.[^1] The Bird Mountain tower is now listed as gone.[^2]

The two 1928 reports disagree on where Ratcliff sits in Houston County. One places it in the western part of the county, and the other in the eastern part.[^1]

In 1933 and 1934 the Civilian Conservation Corps built a camp at the site, and the Davy Crockett National Forest was set up in the area in 1935.[^3] The marker credits the camp's workers with fire towers, roads and a public lake made from an old sawmill pond. It says the camp closed in 1941.[^3] The sources we read do not say whether the CCC built this particular tower. The Forest Service says the recreation area was built by the CCC in 1936.[^5]

A 1942 National Geodetic Survey description, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, places the tower about 0.3 mile north of Ratcliff, in the northern part of a Forest Service camp. It describes a standard steel tower, painted gray, and gives its height as about 100 feet.[^1] That does not match the 90 feet in the 1928 reports.[^1] We have kept 90 feet, because the 1928 reports describe the finished tower and the survey figure is approximate.

We found no list of the people who staffed the Ratcliff tower, and no record of the fires it watched.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^2] A commercial resort's web page calls it one of the last standing fire towers in East Texas. That is a sales page, not an official record.[^4]

The tower sits within the Ratcliff Lake Recreation Area, which is in the Davy Crockett National Forest and offers camping, picnicking, swimming, fishing and hiking.[^4][^5] The site is reached along State Highway 7.[^4] We found no source that says whether the tower itself may be climbed. Visitors should not climb it unless the Forest Service says it is allowed.
