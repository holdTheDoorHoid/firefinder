The Lacreek fire lookout is about 10 miles southeast of Martin, in Bennett County, South Dakota, on the grounds of Lacreek National Wildlife Refuge.[^1] The site is at an elevation of 3,050 feet.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts site says a 107-foot steel tower was erected at the refuge in 1936.[^2] That is about 32.6 meters, the height in our record.

The refuge was set up in 1935 to serve as a breeding ground for migratory birds and other wildlife.[^3] Part of its history is tied to the work of the Civilian Conservation Corps and Works Progress Administration in the mid-1930s.[^3] A history of the CCC camp there says the crews built levees, roads, a boundary fence and "an observation tower" on the refuge.[^4] It does not say whether that tower is the fire lookout.[^4] The timing fits, but these sources do not prove it.

We found no record of who staffed the lookout or of any fire it watched, so that part of the story is blank for now.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^5] We found no source describing a move, a fire or a closure.

Today the refuge invites visitors to "Enjoy trails, off-trail hiking, kayaking, wildlife viewing, bird watching, fishing and hunting." It also says there are typically no closed areas to foot traffic, though seasonal or temporary closures may occur.[^6] We could not find whether the tower itself can be climbed. Ask the refuge office before walking to it, and do not climb the tower without permission.
