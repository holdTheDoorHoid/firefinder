The Bureau of Land Management lookout on Bennett Mountain, about 15 miles northeast of Mountain Home, began as a 15-by-15-foot cabin in 1937.[^1][^3] A two-story metal and frame cab replaced it in 1952, and a two-story concrete cab followed in 1964.[^1][^2]

Known staff run from Vern Boren in 1939 to David McCoy in 1981. Charles Smead, Ralph Miller and Don and LaVonne Snyders are also named, and Walt and Lola Crawford worked the lookout from 1966 to 1972.[^1][^2] A 1983 obituary for Liz Alverson says she worked there for 25 summers. The Idaho Fire Lookouts site is less certain and says she possibly staffed the tower for 25 years.[^1][^2]

In August 2013 the lookout was cleared out and secured while the lightning-caused Elk Fire Complex had burned more than 90,000 acres.[^3]

The lookout was destroyed in 2016.[^1][^2] One Idaho listing uses the word demolished for the site.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Idaho list marks the cab as gone.[^5]

Today the site is listed as drive-up, but firelookout.com says there is no public access.[^1][^2] A climbing guide says the land beyond the BLM section is privately owned, and that two jeep roads leading into the area were closed to the public, based on a 2019 recon.[^4] Taken together, the sources indicate the site is not open to the public. No source describes what remains at the site today.
