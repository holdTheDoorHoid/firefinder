White Mountain rises to 6,921 feet in Ferry County, Washington, on the Colville National Forest.[^1][^2] Its summit lookout was a cupola cabin, and the sources disagree about when it went up.[^1][^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1919.[^1] A photo caption on the same page reads "White Mountain Lookout- under construction 1922," and firelookout.com dates the cabin to 1922.[^1][^2] A Spokane newspaper reported in April 1923 that rangers were framing lumber for a standard lookout station on the summit.[^3] A September 1923 report said the house was finished by July 1 and first served as a lookout that year.[^3]

Early lookouts included H.P. Hasler in 1923, Ralph Buckley in 1928, S.B. Hayward in 1929, Mr. and Mrs. Chas. Bouk of Republic in 1944, and Homer Therleen in 1945.[^2][^3]

The cupola cabin was removed in 1950.[^1][^3] One list, firelookout.com, says it was destroyed that year instead.[^2]

A Washington Trails Association route description places the old lookout's remains at the south end of the summit.[^4] The association's hike, White Mountain via Kettle Crest Trail South, is a 28-mile round trip and says a spur trail leads to the summit.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the White Mountain site as a ground site that is gone.[^5] Check with the Forest Service about current conditions before you go.
