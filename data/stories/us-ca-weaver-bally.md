Weaver Bally Lookout stands on Weaver Bally Ridge in Trinity County, California, inside the Shasta-Trinity National Forest, at about 6,992 feet.[^1] A lookout building was already on the summit by June 1923, when a newspaper noted the good view from it.[^2] A U.S. Geological Survey station description from 1951, copied onto the Forest Lookouts page, says that earlier lookout was destroyed in the early 1930s and a new one was built about 600 feet to the southwest.[^2] The register dates the cab to 1935.[^1] The dates do not fit together neatly.[^1][^2]

James J. Flagg went to his post on the summit in May 1931, and an October 1932 report still had him there, with Charles McNeill acting as relief in July.[^2] In May 1946 Fred "Pop" Emerson began his sixth season at the station.[^2] George Ballou had been the lookout for three years, and in March 1951 he was not expected back.[^2] A May 31, 1956 item said Del Dewey would be posted there that Friday.[^2]

The fire record is thin. An August 1912 Trinity Journal account of a lightning storm says at least 21 fires were left behind, all found by the lookout system. It also describes a Mr. Reid on Weaver Bally whose telephone receiver was knocked from his hand when lightning struck the wire.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page heads that item with a 1940 date, but the reprint itself names the 1912 paper, so we date it to 1912.[^2] The register notes that nearby Weaverville has been almost burned off the map by wildfire several times in the last two decades.[^1]

The original cab was taken down and moved to the Trinity County Fairgrounds in Hayfork. A 2009 photo caption on the register places it there.[^1] The October 14, 2011 visit report says painting and other maintenance had been deferred for years.[^1] The register still describes the lookout as located atop the ridge, and it was registered on January 3, 2015 as US 1060, CA 105.[^1]

The register says the Weaver Bally Lookout Road requires a sturdy vehicle with at least six inches of clearance.[^1] In 2011, the sign at the end of the pavement, about six miles downhill from the tower, was reported in good condition.[^1] The same visit entry marks the tower as not staffed, yet a comment in it says the tower is staffed seven days a week, eight hours a day, during fire season. We found no current staffing information.[^1] We found nothing on whether the tower itself is open to visitors or on any rental, so check with the Shasta-Trinity National Forest before planning a trip.
