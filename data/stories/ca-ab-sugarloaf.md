Sugarloaf is a fire lookout in southwestern Alberta, at 2,516 m (8,255 ft) above sea level.[^1][^2] A 2007 trip report places it in the Oldman River area.[^3]

A Canada Lookouts history dates the original lookout here, a 12 x 12 cabin, to 1954.[^4] In 1978 that cabin was replaced by a cinder block cabin built on the ground.[^4] The register gives 1978 as the construction year for the present structure, lists the site as a cabin, and gives its height as 2 m (6.6 ft).[^1]

The blog Life is Adventure credits one of the lookout's operators with a stonework mosaic, dating it to about 1980.[^2] The 2007 trip report also photographs a stone design beside the lookout.[^3]

Alberta counts 127 wildfire lookout sites, each with an observer on duty, usually April through October.[^5]

The register still lists the lookout as active.[^1]

The register and a 2007 trip report describe access differently. The register lists the access mode as "fly".[^1] The trip report says a vehicle could cover about two kilometres of rough road, after which the party left the road and walked up a ridge to the lookout.[^3]

No source describes public visits to the site today. Treat it as a working fire lookout, not a public destination.
