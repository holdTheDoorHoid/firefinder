A crow's nest tree platform went up on Trask Mountain in 1916.[^1] The register says a wooden tower replaced it in 1934, with a 7-by-7-foot cab and living quarters in a ground house.[^1] A September 1934 Forest Log item described a 69-foot tower under construction, with a bulldozer hauling materials about a mile to the summit.[^2]

Sources disagree on the height. The register gives 65 feet, and a 1942 inspection report describes a 75-foot tower.[^1][^2]

The register says the tower was cut down to 55 feet and given a 10-by-10 foot cab in 1955.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page dates a new cab, costing $1,616.70, to 1956.[^2] The two dates for the cab do not agree.

Observers were on duty when an inspector visited in June 1942.[^2] A 1982 annual report from the Forest Grove district says Trask was the final staffed lookout in Northwest Oregon, shut when the fire season ended.[^2]

The register and Rex's Fire Lookout Page both say the structures were no longer there by 1996.[^1][^3] Wikipedia, by contrast, says a fire lookout station on the peak was abandoned in the 1970s, which conflicts with the 1982 closure.[^4]

Wikipedia says a seismograph station, installed in 1991, now sits on the mountain.[^4] No source I found describes what remains of the lookout, or whether the summit is open to visitors.
