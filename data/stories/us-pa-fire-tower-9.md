Fire Tower #9 rises above Cook Forest State Park in Clarion County, Pennsylvania. Unlike most fire towers, it was not built to spot smoke first and foremost: it went up in 1928 as a scenic viewing attraction for park visitors.[^1] It became a working fire lookout later, staffed by a paid state forest observer from 1946 to 1966.[^1] Volunteers then took over watch duties, continuing until 1977, when a tornado tore out the tower's phone and electric lines.[^1]

Since then, the tower has gone back to its original purpose. It stands today as a public viewing platform, with the cab itself opened only during scheduled interpretive programs, though the tower is open to visitors year-round.[^1] It is a popular stop in the park: during the summer season it can draw 200 to 400 visitors in a two-hour span.[^1]

The lookout carries National Historic Lookout Register number US 1172 (Pennsylvania US 19), registered in January 2017.[^1] It stands at 1,582 feet elevation.[^1]

If you visit, you can expect to see the steel tower up close and climb to the viewing level when it is open, but the enclosed cab at the top is not generally accessible outside of ranger-led programming — a reminder that this lookout now serves sightseers more than fire watchers.

[^1]: Fire Tower #9, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/pa/fire-tower-9/ (accessed 2026-10-04).
