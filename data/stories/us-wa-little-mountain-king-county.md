Little Mountain lies in the heart of the Cedar River watershed in King County, Washington.[^3][^1] The register says the City of Seattle Water Dept administers the site.[^1]

The lookout went up in 1935, and its builders avoided cutting a road by sending materials to the summit on a logging skyline cable.[^3] A July 1935 article, quoted on Willhite's page, describes how a Seattle forester and a lumber company superintendent hit upon the idea of using one of the logging company's idle machines.[^3] The tower was a 100-foot wooden structure with a 7x7 wooden cab and catwalk, and a ground house stood at its base.[^3][^2] The register describes the cab as an L-6 model instead.[^1]

In 1942 the site was considered for an Aircraft Warning Service post, but the tower was judged too high for continuous use during the winter.[^3]

The sources disagree on how the lookout ended. Firelookout.com and Willhite both say it was destroyed in 1969.[^2][^3] The register says the cab and ground house were removed that year.[^1] Willhite's own photo captions include a 1975 USGS map that shows a lookout on Little Mountain, which conflicts with a 1969 end.[^3]

Photo captions on Willhite's page describe a ground house foundation, a metal tripod, and phone parts and a post still standing at the site.[^3] The view looking up the watershed is now overgrown, and the last section of the access road is growing over.[^3]

Willhite lists a distance of 0.4 miles and a gain of 220 feet, and says access needs permission or a guide from the City of Seattle Watershed.[^3] Anyone who wants to visit should arrange permission or a guide first.[^3]
