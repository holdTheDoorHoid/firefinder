Sargent Mountain lies in Hancock County, Maine, inside Acadia National Park.[^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built a lookout there in 1941: a wooden cabin on a stone base, sited about 1,000 feet north of the summit, with northerly views.[^1][^2]

The National Park Service says the Sargent cabin and the Beech Mountain tower were the two lookouts officially located in the park, and that the cabin was part of the Maine Forestry Service's fire lookout network.[^1] Communications ran by telephone.[^2] Staffing was the hard part. Keeping the lookout manned meant overnight stays at a remote spot, so the NPS calls regular staffing a challenge, and use was sporadic for about a decade.[^1][^2][^3]

According to the NPS, the lookout stood until 1949 and came down soon after.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site gives c.1950 for the removal, and the Memorials of Acadia National Park blog says its operations might have ended the following year.[^3][^2] Unlike the Beech Mountain tower, the cabin was never replaced or modernized, and the NPS says there is no clear indication of why.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a ground lookout that is gone.[^4] A 2014 post on the Memorials of Acadia National Park blog describes scattered remains on the site and concludes that the cabin was built of wood on a stone base.[^2] No source we read says what those remains look like now, or whether visitors can reach them.
