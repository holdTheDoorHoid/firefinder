Flagstaff Butte is in Union County, in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest, about 28 miles northeast of Baker City.[^1][^2] The first lookout here was a 24-foot platform tower, put up in 1931.[^1][^2] In 1933 a D-6 cupola cabin replaced it.[^1][^2] That September a Baker newspaper reported that the Forest Service was building the lookout under the public works program.[^3]

Newspaper items show people working the station in summer.[^3] In July 1936 J.R. Mitchell went to the Flagstaff lookout station to substitute for Claire Fleetwood, a forest service employee who had been injured.[^3] A 1945 La Grande newspaper item reported that a son had spent most of the summer at a camp near the lookout while working for the Forest Service.[^3]

The sources disagree on how the cabin ended. The register says it was "removed in 1959."[^1] Firelookout.com says it was "Destroyed 1959."[^2]

In 1972 the former lookout site, a roughly five-acre plot of undeveloped land on a 4,680-foot mountain, was offered in a sealed-bid sale.[^3] In August of that year it sold to O. Gordon Hudson, of Trail, Oregon, for $789.32.[^3] The August notice calls the site the former Medical Springs Lookout Site, and the register gives 6,511 feet for this peak, so the sources do not clearly tie the sale to Flagstaff Butte.[^1][^3]

No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it now.[^1][^2][^3] A 2014 Bend Bulletin travel column described Medical Springs, the community near the lookout, rather than the lookout itself. It found three buildings there, one a former hotel, and an outdoor pool that looked worn.[^3][^4]
