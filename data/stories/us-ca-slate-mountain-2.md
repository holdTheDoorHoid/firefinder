Slate Mountain stands in the Eldorado National Forest in El Dorado County, at 1,187 meters.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the lookout was constructed in 1935 as a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin on a 20-foot H-brace open steel tower, with a garage built on site.[^1] Newspaper items quoted on the Forest Lookouts site show a lookout post on the mountain before that. In 1914 G.H. Morton was assigned to the post, so the 1935 date seems to mark the cabin rather than the first lookout.[^2]

In 1916 George H. Morton was set to go to the Slate Mountain station, and in 1926 Allen Hughes returned from the lookout station there.[^2] In August 1930 Willard Greenslate became lookout after Dale Rasor was transferred away, but a September 1935 item again calls Dale Rasor the Forest Service lookout at Slate Mountain.[^2]

The sources disagree on when the lookout came down. The register gives the removal date as unknown.[^1] The 2012 annual report of the California-Sierra and Nevada chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Slate Mountain under the Eldorado National Forest as removed in 2003.[^3] The association's California list marks the site as gone.[^4]

Today the register says just the tower piers are left, on ground now used for electronic communications.[^1] We found no source that describes public access to the site.
