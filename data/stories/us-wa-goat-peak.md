Goat Peak Lookout stands in Okanogan County, Washington, and is a prominent landmark of the Methow Valley.[^1][^3] The register says the original cabin was a D-6 cupola cabin, built in 1923 by the old Chelan National Forest.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same 1923 date for that cabin.[^2]

In 1950 the cupola was replaced with the current L-4 cab, which has a catwalk and sits on a 15' timber tower.[^1][^2]

The register says the lookout has been staffed every year since it was built.[^1] Later accounts describe a change. Bill Austin, known as Lightning Bill, staffed it full-time for nineteen straight fire seasons.[^4][^3] When he moved to the Leecher Lookout near Twisp in 2014, Goat Peak became an on-call post, staffed when approaching storms call for extra watching.[^4] OutdoorsNW says it was staffed on a regular basis until 2015 and only now and then during emergencies after that.[^3] Firelookout.com says the post was reduced to emergency-only status, and that a volunteer host has kept watch there in recent years.[^2]

Nearby fires came close enough in 2017 and 2021 that the lookout was covered in protective wrapping.[^3] Washington Trails Association says the lookout still contributes to forest fire detection.[^4]

The hike starts from Early Winters campground.[^1] WTA lists a round trip of 3.7 miles, and OutdoorsNW calls the trip 3.5 miles.[^4][^3] WTA lists the America the Beautiful Pass or the Northwest Forest Pass as the parking pass.[^4] The sources do not say whether visitors may go inside the cab, so do not count on it.
