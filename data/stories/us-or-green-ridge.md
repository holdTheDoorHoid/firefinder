Green Ridge Lookout looks out over the Metolius River drainage toward Mount Jefferson from a "side hill" perch in the Deschutes National Forest in central Oregon — a lookout site set partway down a ridge rather than on a summit.[^1][^3] The first structure there was a Standard L-5 cabin, a modest 10-by-10-foot design, finished around 1933 or 1934 and classified as an emergency station roughly 500 feet below where the current tower stands.[^1][^3]

That first cabin served through the Depression and war years, but by the early 1960s the Forest Service replaced it with something larger and more durable. In 1961, crews built a 15-by-15-foot R-6 flattop cab with a wraparound catwalk atop a 21-foot treated timber tower, a quarter mile northwest of the ridgetop.[^1][^3] That is the structure still in use today.

Green Ridge has continued to serve its original purpose. The Forest Service's Sisters Ranger District cooperates in running it, and in the fire season it's staffed by volunteers who help spot smoke across the Metolius basin.[^1][^2] Green Ridge was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on December 1, 1998, as US 287, nominated by Marilyn Anderson.[^1]

Outside of fire season, the Forest Service rents the cabin to the public through Recreation.gov, typically in two windows — early May through June, and mid-September through mid-November, when volunteer staffing has wound down.[^2] Getting there involves a drive up a series of forest roads from Sisters, and the one-room cabin comes furnished with a futon and a small propane kitchen setup, though guests still need to bring their own water, since none is available on site.[^2] Early and late-season visitors should expect the access roads to need high-clearance or four-wheel-drive vehicles, and an opening can be delayed if snow blocks the route.[^2]

No source consulted recorded any fire, storm damage, or major incident at Green Ridge comparable to what some other lookouts have endured — its history, as documented, is one of steady use rather than destruction and rebuilding.
