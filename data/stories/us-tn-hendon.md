The Hendon lookout is in Bledsoe County, Tennessee, and its site is listed at about 2,319 feet.[^1] The state's tower numbers are pairs of district and tower, and Hendon carries the code 3-10.[^1] A Forest Lookouts index places it in district three of the Tennessee Division of Forestry.[^2]

We could not find when Hendon was built, who put it up, or what its cab looked like. The Civilian Conservation Corps erected 98 lookout towers in Tennessee between 1933 and 1941, but no source we read ties Hendon to that work.[^1] We also found no record of who staffed the tower or of any fire it watched.

A table of towers still standing, dated March 26, 2014, and printed in a National Register of Historic Places document on the state's towers, lists Hendon in Bledsoe County.[^3] Tennessee Landforms also marks it as standing.[^1] That same document says many of the state's towers were removed or face demolition, because they fell out of use or their leases with private owners ran out.[^3]

In April 2024 a 1,256-acre property on Flat Top Mountain in Bledsoe County closed for $2,500,000.[^4] A sales listing calls it Hendon Timberlands, uses the address Hendon Fire-Tower Road, and describes it as a private retreat that is not under a conservation easement.[^4] The listing's description does not mention a tower, so we cannot say whether the tower was part of that sale.

We found no public access route or visitor information. Tennessee Landforms notes that most lookout locations are now private property, and it advises people not to climb the towers because steps are often removed or rotten.[^1] Please keep off the structure, and do not go onto the site without permission from whoever owns it.
