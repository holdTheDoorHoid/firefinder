Stringtown is a lookout tower in White County, Tennessee.[^1] The Tennessee Landforms list gives its elevation as 2,004 feet and marks the tower as standing.[^1]

The sources we checked give no build date, design, or height for Stringtown, and none of them describes its history.[^1][^2][^3]

The sources do not agree about whether the tower still stands.[^1][^2] The 2014 National Register document about the state Division of Forestry's towers lists the towers still standing as of March 26, 2014, and Stringtown is not on that list.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register's Tennessee page does not name Stringtown either.[^3] From these sources we cannot tell whether the tower came down before 2014 or whether the lists simply differ.[^1][^2]

The National Register document says the Division built 208 fire lookout towers between about 1930 and 1970, and that 142 were still standing in March 2014.[^2] As of its 2014 date, the document says aircraft and satellite imagery play a bigger part in spotting fires, and that only a handful of the towers remain in active service.[^2] We found no record that Stringtown was ever staffed.[^1][^2]

Today the sources we checked say nothing about who owns Stringtown, whether it can be visited, or whether anyone maintains it.[^1][^2] The document says many of the old towers have been removed or are awaiting demolition. It adds that surviving towers are often in disrepair, and that visitors are commonly cautioned about the risks of climbing them.[^2] Because the tower's status is unclear, treat it as closed to climbing unless its owner or land manager says otherwise.
