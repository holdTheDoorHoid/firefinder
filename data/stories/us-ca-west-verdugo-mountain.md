In 1934 the Los Angeles County Forestry Department built a steel tower 60 feet tall, topped with a steel cab, on this site.[^2] The register calls the site West Verdugo Mountain, and the Forest Lookouts page calls it Verdugo.[^1][^2] The site's name places it in the Verdugo range, a small range on the southern side of the western San Gabriel Mountains.[^1][^3]

According to the Firetower record, the county both built and operated the tower, starting in 1934 and possibly lasting as late as 1960.[^1] A county annual report for the fiscal year ending June 30, 1934 said towers on Verdugo and West San Rafael Peaks were greatly improving detection in the La Crescenta and La Cañada valley and along the Angeles Forest front.[^2] A county report for the fiscal year ending June 30, 1946 said the tower and its buildings had for a long time been on term leases, and that the land, now tax-delinquent, was being acquired by the county.[^2]

A second register photo shows the tower's foundation legs where they originally stood, now reused to support a communications vault.[^1] The register lists the site as former and does not say when the steel tower came down.[^1]

Today the Verdugo Mountains are largely undeveloped public parkland, used mainly for hiking and mountain biking.[^3] Their highest peaks host communications installations.[^3] The sources I read do not say whether visitors may reach the lookout site itself.[^1][^2][^3]
