Big Bald Mountain is a peak in Northumberland, New Brunswick, known for its bald summit.[^1][^2] Wikipedia gives the summit's elevation as 672 m (2,205 ft).[^2]

Before aerial surveillance was widely used, a hut on the summit served for fire-spotting.[^2] Wikipedia says a very similar hut was maintained on Mount Carleton, the province's highest peak, and that triangulation between these huts and other fire towers let the location of a wildfire be found quickly.[^2]

A 1938 report lists a "Big Bald Cupola Cabin" among the towers painted that year.[^4] In 1940 the cupola windows at the Big Bald Lookout Station received some repairs.[^1] Canada Lookouts' clips call the site a cupola cabin and a lookout station.[^1][^4] Wikipedia and a Commons photograph describe a fire-spotting hut.[^2][^3]

A clip on Canada Lookouts, labelled 1969, says the Big Bald tower and two others went unmanned during the fire season, and that an aircraft based at Dunphy airstrip took over fire detection in the area they had covered.[^1] The report it quotes covers the year ending 31 March 1970.[^1]

A Wikimedia Commons photograph carries a caption calling the fire-spotting hut on Big Bald Mountain abandoned.[^3] Wikipedia lists the easiest route to the summit as a hike.[^2] No source says what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the land is open to visitors.
