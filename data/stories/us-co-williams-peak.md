Forest Service officials said in June 1912 that they meant to set up a lookout station on the summit of Williams Peak, in Grand County, Colorado.[^1] The plan called for a telephone and a watchman, whose main duty would be to spot and report anything that looked like a real or threatened forest fire.[^1]

By December 1912, the annual report of the Leadville National Forest's assistant supervisor listed three lookout stations on the forest, one of them on Williams Peak. A telephone line had been run to that station during the season.[^1] A January 1914 newspaper item said the summit station could be reached by the Williams Peak trail, and that from it one could see almost all of Grand County and parts of Summit and Routt counties.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as abandoned.[^2] We found no source that says what, if anything, still stands on the summit, or whether visitors can reach it.

The peak now lies in the Arapaho National Forest, about 19 miles southeast of Kremmling.[^3]
