Incline Mountain's lookout stood on state land in Wahkiakum County, Washington.[^3] The Division of Forestry's 1938 annual report, quoted on one lookout history page, says a new tower and ground house were built on the mountain, northeast of Cathlamet.[^4] WillhiteWeb describes the tower as a wooden 40-foot structure with a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab on top, and says a ground house was built as well.[^3][^1]

During World War II the Aircraft Warning Service used the lookout.[^3] Its station, called Nan 3-9, was activated on March 9, 1942, and used existing state facilities.[^4] A WillhiteWeb photo caption is dated March 19, 1942, so the activation date is not settled.[^3] A 12-by-20-foot garage was added with AWS money.[^4][^3] When the service ended, the improvements were left with the state.[^4]

The lookout was destroyed about 1967.[^1][^2][^3]

Today the site is on state DNR land. WillhiteWeb says it is under a half mile on foot, and that no gates will stop you.[^3] The same author warns that an active timber sale could affect access, and one photo is captioned "Blocked summit road."[^3]

To get there, take Highway 4 west from Longview, then look for Abernathy Road a few miles past Stella.[^3] The paved county road ends at a major washout, and a work-around road climbs over it and drops back to the main road.[^3]

A photo on the page is captioned "Incline Footing" and is credited to the web. The page does not say what, if anything, remains on the site today.[^3]
