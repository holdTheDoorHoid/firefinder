Gold Ridge sits in Okanogan County, about 11 miles southwest of Twisp.[^2] According to the register, the only structure on the ridge was a crow's nest built in the 1930s, and the U.S. Forest Service is listed as the administrator.[^1]

Willhite, citing the Kresek inventory, says the crow's nest was still there in the 1980s.[^3] The register says the same.[^1]

The firelookout.com listing says the crow's nest was abandoned in the 1930s and is now gone.[^2] That is hard to square with the register and Willhite, who both have it standing into the 1980s.[^1][^3]

Willhite thinks the lookout most likely stood on the summit, which he puts at 4,585 feet.[^3] The register gives the site's elevation as 4,200 feet.[^1] Near the summit he spotted a faint square platform, 5 by 5, outlined with rocks.[^3] A phone line once ran up Oxide Canyon to the ridge saddle and on to the summit, and insulators are still on some trees in the canyon and near the top.[^3] He is unsure when the phone line ran and exactly where the crow's nest stood.[^3]

The route he describes starts on the paved Gold Creek Road, where the Oxide Canyon road leaves just before the National Forest sign.[^3] From there the climb is off trail through mostly open country, and the old road in the canyon fades in places.[^3]
