The Warrior Mountain lookout was a state forestry tower in Allegany County, Maryland.[^2][^3] Its site sits at a base elevation of 2,185 feet above sea level, just beyond the northern edge of what is now Warrior Mountain Wildlife Management Area.[^2]

A 47-foot tower went up there in 1922, and a state forestry report for fiscal 1930 says it was ready for service that year.[^1][^2]

The site's deed, dated March 20, 1923 and recorded in 1929, came from the Warrior Mountain Orchard Company and the Moskwa Land and Development Company.[^2] A 1923 newspaper said the orchard company had allowed a fire tower on its land and had helped install the telephone line for the forest warden.[^1]

In May 1923 a newspaper reported heavy damage on Warrior Mountain and Green Ridge, and an investigation found the fires in that section looked deliberately set.[^1]

For 14 years, Mary Duvall walked three miles a day up to the tower and back over the same rough trail.[^1] A May 1957 newspaper report says she had retired three years earlier.[^1]

The DNR's 2007 resource document says the forestry tower was removed about 20 years before the report was written, and a photo caption dates the removal to the mid-1980s.[^2] A modern communications tower now occupies the site.[^2]

The tract remains part of Warrior Mountain Wildlife Management Area.[^2] The 2007 report gives the access as an unpaved Fire Tower Road from Rocky Road.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^3] No source read says whether the public may visit the tower site itself.
