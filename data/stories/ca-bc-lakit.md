Lakit Lookout, southeast of Cranbrook in British Columbia, is reached by a brief but steep hike.[^1] It began as the Wild Horse Lookout, which the trail page says was established in 1955.[^2] A blog account says the lookout went up that year and was staffed from the start.[^3]

The sources do not agree on how long the lookout worked as a fire lookout. The trail page says it was in active fire-suppression service for about a dozen years, yet it also dates the decommissioning to 1976.[^2] The Cranbrook page says it served as a fire lookout until the mid-1970s.[^1]

The trail page dates the decommissioning to 1976 and says the site was rebuilt the following year as a shelter for outdoor visitors such as hikers and skiers.[^2] The Cranbrook page says a replacement matching the original design was built in 1977.[^1] A blog calls that building a replica of the original.[^3]

The Cranbrook page describes the recreation site as maintained and free to access, with picnic tables and pit toilets, and says there are no campsites.[^1] A blog says the fire lookout is now abandoned, and that the building has a small woodstove (visitors bring their own wood) and a single bed.[^3] The trail page's title names Recreation Sites and Trails BC as the program for the site.[^2]

The hike to the lookout is about 2,300 m (7,550 ft) one way, with about 480 m (1,575 ft) of climb.[^1] The trailhead is at the end of a road of about 7,400 to 7,500 m (24,300 to 24,600 ft), and the trail page recommends a high clearance vehicle for it.[^1][^2]
