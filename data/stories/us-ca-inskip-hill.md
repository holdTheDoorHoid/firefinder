Inskip Hill Lookout stands on the summit of Inskip Hill, a twin-peaked cinder cone west of Lassen Volcanic National Park, near Paynes Creek in Tehama County.[^1][^4][^5] The original lookout was a Civilian Conservation Corps-era station built for the Forest Service in 1934. Its tower follows a Forest Service L-801 design, and CCC crews built the tower and the garage.[^1]

A 1935 newspaper item describes a 40-foot steel tower carrying the lookout, manned by Victor Mentink.[^2] In 1937 Hubert Grissom was the lookout.[^2] The Lassen National Forest staffed the site until 1958.[^1][^5] The tower and its immediate property were quit claimed to the California Department of Forestry (CDF) in 1971, and the stairway was replaced in 1972.[^1]

The original cab was destroyed by fire.[^1] In 1974 the cab then in place was replaced with one in the 732-6A style.[^1] Sources disagree on when the steel tower went up. The 1935 newspaper already describes one, while Peakbagging says the steel tower replaced the 1934 lookout around 1975.[^2][^3]

In August 1973 a 13,000-acre fire near Red Bluff knocked out power lines to the tower, which was then the main radio relay point for local law enforcement.[^2]

In 2024 the Park Fire reached the hill.[^4][^6] The Lookout's satellite view from July 27 shows active fire backing down Inskip Hill.[^6] The Forest Fire Lookout Association reported in August that the lookout survived its brush with the Park Fire.[^5] A CAL FIRE notice received in December 2024 covers the replacement of damaged structures.[^4] The garage, electrical infrastructure and privy were to be removed and rebuilt, with a new 2,500-gallon water tank.[^4] The tower is not listed among the damaged parts.[^4] The notice planned the work for 2025, and the sources read do not confirm that it was finished.[^4]

Staffing is also disputed. The Forest Fire Lookout Association says the lookout is not normally staffed by CAL FIRE.[^5] Peakbagging says a Forest Service employee now staffs it during fire season.[^3] The Register, citing an October 1991 visit note, says the public can reach the station, which lies 4.5 miles from the nearest paved road.[^1] Peakbagging instead says it is on private land.[^3] Given that conflict and the planned rebuilding, check with CAL FIRE before going, and do not climb the tower.
