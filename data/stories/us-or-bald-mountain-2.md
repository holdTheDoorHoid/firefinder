Bald Mountain Lookout stands at about 7,400 feet on Bald Mountain in Klamath County, Oregon, on what was then the Fremont National Forest.[^1][^3] The register says a telephone line was strung from the Oatman Ranger Station to the summit in 1917, and that a steel tower under 30 feet, with a 12-by-12-foot Aermotor cab, went up in 1928.[^1] Newspapers of that year gave other heights: a 60-foot tower announced in April, and a 40-foot steel tower reported in June.[^2]

Firelookout.com dates the site's start to 1932, with a 20-foot wooden L-4 cab.[^3] The early records disagree, but they agree on what came next. In 1941 the Forest Service built the present lookout: an L-4 hip-roof cab with a catwalk, on a treated-timber tower of about 29.5 feet (firelookout.com gives 28).[^1][^3] A May 1941 newspaper said the outdated tower would come down and be replaced by a taller one.[^2]

Observers worked the tower in summer.[^2] The Lake County Tribune reported in 1938 that Guy Martin would be the lookout that season, and in 1939 it described him spotting a lightning-started fire.[^2] In 1988 a newspaper profile called the summit the highest staffed lookout in Central Oregon, and quoted the Walker Range Patrol supervisor praising the observer, Jeff Johnston.[^2] The register says the site has been run by the Walker Range Patrol, and in its early days by the Klamath Forest Protective Association.[^1]

Firelookout.com says the lookout is staffed by the Walker FPA.[^3] A Cheryl Hill index lists it as unmanned, so its current status is unclear from these sources.[^4] The register listed the site on March 31, 2003, as US 480, OR 73.[^1]

The available sources do not say whether visitors may go up into the cab. Check with the Fremont-Winema National Forest, Silver Lake Ranger District, before planning a trip.[^1][^4]
