The Crane Flat Fire Lookout sits on a knoll near Crane Flat in Yosemite National Park, at an elevation of about 6,600 feet.[^3] The National Park Service built it in 1931, and it was the first permanent lookout in the park.[^1] Its stone first story is topped by a wooden cab with a catwalk, in a rustic style meant to blend with the forest rather than stand out like the metal towers the Forest Service used.[^1][^2]

The lookout was also picked for an influential portfolio of park structures, assembled by the Park Service's chief landscape architect, that was meant to serve as prototypes.[^2] It stayed in use into the 1980s.[^2]

A one-story plywood shed was added to the east in 1977.[^3] A 1995 National Register nomination says that addition and a lean-to were to be removed and the lookout fully restored.[^2][^3] I could not confirm from these sources that the restoration was finished.

The lookout was added to the National Register of Historic Places in April 1996 and to the National Historic Lookout Register in June 1998.[^2][^1]

The nomination describes a paved road that leaves Big Oak Flat Road, then a trail from the parking lot near the top of that road, built to give visitors safe access.[^3] Much of the cleared ground beside the lookout is now asphalt, and it serves as a helicopter landing area for the helitack crews based there in fire season.[^3]

A 2024 trip report found the lookout still standing and reachable by road or by a short trail.[^4] The sources do not say whether the cab interior is open to visitors, so check with Yosemite National Park before planning a visit.
