Chair Point Lookout sits in the Nez Perce National Forest about six miles northeast of Riggins, Idaho, and has gone through three towers on the same spot.[^3] The first was a log cabin paired with a pole tower carrying an L-6 cab, which the National Historic Lookout Register and firelookout.com put at 45 feet tall and Idaho Fire Lookouts puts at 65 feet.[^1][^2][^3] Two of those sources date that original structure to 1931, though the National Historic Lookout Register's own description of the site doesn't give a year for it.[^1][^2][^3] The log cabin from that first build is still standing today.[^1]

In 1953 the Forest Service replaced the pole tower with a 41-foot timber tower topped by an L-4 cab.[^1][^2][^3] That one lasted until 1980, when it was swapped for the structure still in use now: a 41-foot timber tower with a flat-roofed R-6 cab and catwalk.[^1][^2]

Known staffing records are thin but span decades: Norm Clark is recorded as lookout from 1956 to 1978, and Tony Cornish staffed the tower in 2012.[^2] The lookout has continued to be staffed every fire season in more recent years.[^2][^3]

The tower had a close call in 2015, when the Teepee Springs Fire advanced on it; crews wrapped the tower and cabin in protective foil, and the fire ultimately did not reach the structure.[^2]

Chair Point was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on April 24, 2009, nominated by Gary Weber.[^1] As of the most recent reporting, it remains an active, staffed Forest Service lookout reachable by road, at an elevation of about 6,951 feet.[^1][^2][^3] Because it's a working fire lookout rather than a public rental, visitors should expect it to be occupied in season and shouldn't plan on climbing it uninvited.
