Church Mountain Lookout stood on a rocky summit in Whatcom County, Washington, inside the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1][^4] The sources disagree about when the cabin went up. The register and firelookout.com both give 1927 for a cupola cabin,[^1][^2] while WillhiteWeb says a D-6 cupola cabin was built in 1928.[^4]

Before the cabin, U.S. Forest rangers built a camp for six people near the summit in 1916.[^3][^4] A July 1916 item from The Lynden Tribune, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says the camp was available to people who wanted to stay the night on the mountain.[^3]

The sources name very few people. WillhiteWeb says the site was used by "A.W.S." observers in 1941 and 1942, and it does not spell out the abbreviation.[^4] Forest Lookouts also lists a Bellingham Filter Center active from March 28, 1942 to October 16, 1943, but the page does not say how that relates to this site.[^3] Visitors came in numbers in the 1930s. A 1936 item counted 125 visitors that season,[^3][^4] and WillhiteWeb says hundreds came each year through that decade.[^4]

The cabin was gone by 1967. The register says it was removed that year,[^1] firelookout.com says it was destroyed,[^2] and WillhiteWeb dates the loss to 1966 or 1967.[^4] The outhouse and storage shed lasted until about the turn of the century, and today they are reduced to rubble.[^4]

We found no source that describes a standing structure today. WillhiteWeb reports little sign of a building at the lookout site, though cables and metal parts hang from the cliffs just below it on several sides.[^4] A survey note quoted by Forest Lookouts places a survey station on a peak a little south of the lookout.[^3]

The hike is popular.[^4] WillhiteWeb describes a trail of 4.2 miles that starts at a parking area at the end of Forest Road 3040, and it warns that the trail runs along a cliff edge near the end.[^4] We found no closure notice for the trail or the summit, so the site appears open to hikers, though no source confirms its legal status.
