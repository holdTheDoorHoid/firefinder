Huckleberry Lookout sits high above the North Fork of the Flathead River in Glacier National Park, near West Glacier, Montana.[^1][^2] The site's first lookout went up in 1924: a simple 10-foot pole platform with a log cabin for living quarters.[^3] That structure didn't last. In 1933 the National Park Service replaced it with the two-story timber lookout that still stands today, built to a modified version of a standard U.S. Forest Service plan and topped with a pyramidal roof.[^1][^2]

Huckleberry was one of a chain of staffed fire lookouts the Park Service built across Glacier to watch for smoke across the park's rugged backcountry.[^2] Beyond its construction history, little has surfaced in the sources reviewed here about the tower's staffing record through the decades, any major fires it watched over, or whether it has needed repair or restoration since 1933.

The lookout was added to the National Register of Historic Places on February 14, 1986, as part of the Glacier National Park Multiple Property Submission, and it was separately registered with the National Historic Lookout Register on September 15, 1991.[^1][^2] It remains standing and, according to a secondhand summary of its history, is staffed by the Park Service in summer months.[^3] Nothing in the sources checked here describes any period of abandonment or disuse, though the gap in the record between 1933 and today means that can't be confirmed either way.

Given Huckleberry's location inside Glacier National Park, any visit, climbing, or staffing arrangement would run through the National Park Service rather than a private or volunteer group. Visitors interested in it should check current park guidance, since backcountry lookouts in Glacier are not generally open for casual public climbing.
