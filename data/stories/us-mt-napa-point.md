Napa Point was a Forest Service lookout in Lake County, in the Flathead National Forest, at an elevation of 6,423 feet.[^1] Firelookout.com dates the tower to 1932 and gives its height as 30 feet. It was a wooden live-in design, with two patrol points, one about a mile and one 1.25 miles away.[^1]

A 1940 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls it the Napa Forest Service lookout tower and says the survey intersected its top.[^2]

Local papers record a few later details. In July 1955 two men rode horseback to the lookout to repair storm damage, and John Dunn was staying there that summer.[^2] A June 1968 Flathead Courier item says the lookout had become reachable by road only the year before.[^2]

Firelookout.com marks the lookout as gone.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list also shows it as Tower Gone.[^3] The sources used here do not describe what remains at the site today.
