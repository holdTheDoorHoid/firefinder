The Beschman lookout on Pine Mountain in Harlan County, better known locally as the Putney Fire Tower, has conflicting build dates.[^1][^2][^3] A May 1929 newspaper report said two officials of the Kentucky Forest Service were engineering a 100-foot steel lookout tower on top of Pine Mountain, opposite Putney.[^2] A 2025 history article says a stone at its base gives 1929, but it also ties the tower to Division of Forestry and Civilian Conservation Corps work in the late 1930s.[^3] We could not settle which date is right, and no source gives a measured height, so the 100-foot figure is a plan rather than a verified height.[^2][^3]

The article says the tower is believed to have begun operating around 1937 or 1938, and that it was staffed daily during the dry seasons.[^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Putney Ranger Station, a log cabin at the foot of the mountain, in the late 1930s.[^3][^4] The article says the tower and station served as Harlan County's firefighting base through the 1940s and 1950s.[^3]

By the 1970s, according to the article, planes and modern communications had started to make the lookout's manual methods obsolete, and state budget cuts and shifts in policy led to its official decommissioning around that time.[^3] To deter trespassers, the lower stair flights were taken out. Rust has eaten away at the metal rails, and the wooden landings have decayed or dropped away.[^3] The tower remains state property inside Kentenia State Forest.[^3]

The Register registered the tower on January 2, 2026.[^1] The Register places the tower on Pine Mountain in Harlan County.[^1] It gives an elevation of 2,662 feet, while the 2025 article puts the tower at roughly 2,726 feet, so the sources differ on that figure too.[^1][^3]

We found no official visitor information and no climbing rule. The lower stairs are gone.[^3] Do not climb it, and check with the Kentucky Division of Forestry before any visit. The ranger station was the subject of a 2019 restoration appeal, which said funding was lacking.[^4]
