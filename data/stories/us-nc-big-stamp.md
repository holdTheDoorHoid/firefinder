Big Stamp Bald lies in Cherokee and Clay Counties, inside the Nantahala National Forest.[^2] Forest Service material, as the Former Fire Lookout Sites register retells it, describes the bald as a gathering place that two Native American tribes used seasonally before European contact.[^1] Later, the bald proved useful for watching for fires, and the Forest Service built a fire tower there.[^1]

Standing 30 feet tall and made of steel, the tower carried a 14 x 14 observation cab.[^2] The register names Glen Beaver as the last fire warden. His final fire call from the tower, in the 1960s, concerned a blaze near Tipton, North Carolina.[^1]

The register lists the tower as removed in the 1960s.[^1]

The register added the site on December 11, 2018.[^1] As of 2018, it says, the site still had concrete footings, a water cistern and an underground storm shelter.[^1] The Forest Service lists a hiking route, the Big Stamp Trail (TR520), on the Tusquitee Ranger District.[^3] That listing does not describe the lookout site.[^3] We found no source describing the site after 2018.
