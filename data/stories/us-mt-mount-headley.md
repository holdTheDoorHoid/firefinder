Mount Headley sits at about 7,400 feet in Sanders County, Montana, on land the Lolo National Forest manages.[^1][^2] The Forest Service set up a camp at the site in 1907. A frame and metal cab followed in 1914, and a cupola cabin was added in 1928.[^1][^2] The register gives 1928 as the build year, which is the year of that cupola cabin.[^1]

Lookouts show up in local papers from the early 1920s. Oscar Moen was on the peak in 1921, and Frank Benson in 1929.[^3] In 1931 Joe Bown cut his foot, and Les Anderson took his place while Bown was treated.[^3] W.A. Kirby was there in 1933.[^3]

In 1935 the forest dropped Headley as a lookout station, along with other peaks in the Cabinet, because of visual observation mapping on the forest.[^3] The post was staffed again in the 1950s. James Law took it in 1954 and George Gibbs in 1955.[^3] John Farris was there in 1956 and Larry Luke in 1959.[^2] In 1957 the ranger's office said VHF radio would be used with Headley and Cougar Peak, for the first time.[^3]

The sources disagree about the end. The register says the site was deserted by 1965.[^1] Firelookout.com says the cabin was destroyed that year.[^2]

The cabin is gone, but a 2010 trip report says some debris from it remains just below the summit sign.[^4] Hikers can reach the top on foot.[^4][^5] A 2015 trip report describes a four-mile trail that climbs about 1,400 feet.[^5] The 2010 report reached the top by trail 528.[^4]
