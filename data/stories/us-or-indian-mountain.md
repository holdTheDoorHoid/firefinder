Indian Mountain rises in Hood River County, Oregon, and the U.S. Forest Service administered its lookout.[^1] The sources disagree about when the first lookout went up. The register says a cupola cabin was built in 1916, and Oregon Hikers gives the same start year.[^1][^4] Forest Lookouts instead records that a fire caused by a burning flue destroyed a D-6 lookout house in 1923, and that a standard D-6 cupola house was built that same year.[^2]

By 1918 the summit had a lookout house in use. A newspaper item that Forest Lookouts quotes calls Nita Vogel the first female Forest Service lookout in an Oregon national forest. It says she lived there until fire season ended, with a friend, Ivy Gray.[^2]

On June 25, 1925, a fire about a mile southwest of Larch Mountain's summit was first reported by Lookout Waldron from the Indian Mountain station, according to a Morning Oregonian item quoted by Forest Lookouts.[^2]

The station burned to the ground in September 1932, according to a Morning Oregonian report quoted by Forest Lookouts.[^2] The replacement is also reported differently. The register says the cupola was replaced with an L-4 cab in 1933, and Oregon Hikers gives 1933 as well.[^1][^4] Forest Lookouts instead describes a 14x14 L-4 early model cabin put up in 1936.[^2]

The station was deactivated in 1964.[^2] The register says it was gone by 1965, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association also gives 1965.[^1][^3] Forest Lookouts puts its removal around 1967.[^2] Oregon Hikers ties the end to the mid-1960s lookout demolition, in which the lookout was decommissioned together with many others.[^4]

Today a few remnants of the old lookout foundation remain on the summit, along with a register for hikers to sign, according to Oregon Hikers.[^4] A moderate loop hike of 11.5 miles, with about 2,640 feet of climbing, reaches the summit.[^4] No source we read describes the remains in more detail.
