The tower on High Spur appears under two names.[^1][^2] The Tennessee Land Forms tower list calls it Bulls Gap, and the Forest Lookouts site calls the peak High Spur.[^1][^2] Both sources give the elevation as 1,928 feet, so they probably describe the same site.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page places it in Hawkins and Greene Counties, near the Persia community, while the Land Forms list gives only Hawkins County.[^1][^2]

The earliest account we found is a Kingsport Times-News item from March 24, 1968, as quoted on the Forest Lookouts page. It described a new lookout at High Spur as half finished and said the tower would be 87 feet high.[^2] The article also says that on a clear day the view reached from Morristown to Kingsport, and that smoke from a hidden valley would reveal a wildfire.[^2] Fire crews had been busy the week before, fighting blazes on Clinch Mountain and at Red Hill, and the article says the new lookout would soon help them.[^2]

We found no source that names a lookout operator for this tower, or a fire it spotted.[^1][^2][^3]

The Land Forms list still shows the tower as standing, though it gives no date for that status.[^1] Its state number, 1-13, comes from a 1976 list of state forest towers.[^1] The state fire-tower nomination also lists Bulls Gap, in Hawkins County, in its table of towers still standing as of March 26, 2014.[^3] The Land Forms page says its author gave unofficial names to some towers that the state list and the USGS maps did not name. We could not tell whether Bulls Gap is one of them.[^1]

We found no recent visit report, photograph or access rule, so we cannot say whether the tower is open to climbers. Anyone interested should ask the landowner or the state forestry office before going near it.[^1][^2][^3]
