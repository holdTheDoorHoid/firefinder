The Shiloh fire tower stood in Dorchester County, Maryland, on the Samuel Webster farm, between East New Market and Shiloh.[^1]

In 1937 the State Department of Forestry had it built with Civilian Conservation Corps crews from the Snow Hill camp, who stayed at the Vienna camp while the work went on.[^1] The International Derrick Company constructed the 120-foot structure, which sat on cement piers, with four foundation blocks weighing 7 to 8 tons each.[^1] A July 12 report said the observation room would have a pointed top and a fire table with a map for sighting fires within a 15-mile radius, while a July 30 report described the fire map as covering a 16-mile area.[^1] That later report called it the second tower built in Dorchester County and said it would be in service from August 1, with a towerman posted there for six months of each year.[^1]

By March 1939 Charles Mosely was the towerman stationed at Shiloh, and he had qualified as a radio operator.[^1] The Daily Times reported that Shiloh was the first tower fitted for short-wave radio, and that it opened as the spring fire season officially started.[^1] The radio went in here because the site is central to Caroline, Dorchester and Talbot counties.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone, at about 38.59 north and 75.88 west.[^2]

No source read describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it. Before going, ask Maryland's forest managers whether the site is open to the public.
