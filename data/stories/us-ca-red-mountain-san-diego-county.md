Red Mountain, near Fallbrook in northern San Diego County, has hosted a lookout since 1921, though no picture of the first one survives.[^2] The Register instead places the first lookout near the Mexican border.[^1] The California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection administers the site, and Cal Fire's San Diego Unit is listed as a cooperator.[^1]

The two sources disagree on the early history. The Register says the state built the first lookout in 1921 and that the CCC replaced it in the 1930s with an L-4 cab on a 10-foot wooden tower.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's San Diego chapter instead dates a new CDF structure to 1935, with a garage and weather station, on a 10-foot enclosed tower.[^2]

The Register dates the cab now on the site to 1973, when CDF built a 15-by-15-foot cab on a 10-foot enclosed tower, and says the lookout was staffed until 1977.[^1] The chapter says the 1935 structure was replaced in 1977 with a very simple wood cab on a concrete-block base, so the sources disagree on whether the present cab dates from 1973 or 1977.[^1][^2]

The lookout closed in 1977. Its catwalk is gone, the cab has been stripped and sealed, and the structure still stands.[^2] The Register lists the site as registered on December 16, 2008.[^1]

The site is now a major communications facility, and it can be seen west of Interstate 15, just north of the Mission Road exit to Fallbrook.[^2] According to the Register, the structure now holds radio equipment.[^1] The sources describe no public visits, so visitors should not expect to go up, and the sealed cab should not be entered.
