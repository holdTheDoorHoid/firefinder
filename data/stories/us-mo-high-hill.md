The High Hill tower is in Shannon County, in the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1] Work on the lookout's cabin had started by July 1937. A Shannon County Democrat item of July 8, 1937, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said the basement was almost complete.[^1] Later that July, the same paper reported that truck trail No. 265 was being surfaced with crushed rock from Highway B up to the tower.[^1]

The tower itself was a 100-foot steel tower, erected in 1938.[^1] The Missouri Department of Conservation says most of the state's fire towers were 100 feet tall, so the height was a common one.[^2]

By January 13, 1938, work on a new telephone line to the tower had reached the digging of the holes. On April 28, 1938, the Democrat reported the line finished and the tower now on the regular Forest Service fire detection network.[^1]

No source found describes the tower or the site today, including whether the tower still stands or whether visitors may go there.[^1]
