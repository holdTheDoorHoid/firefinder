Latta's fire tower was being planned in early 1934. A February notice in a Florence newspaper called a meeting to form a fire protection group in connection with "the fire tower to be constructed near Latta."[^1]

By December 1935 the tower had a keeper, Robert Bethea. A newspaper item reports that he died of heart failure shortly after he helped put out a fire at a dwelling near the tower.[^1]

In March 1936 the Latta Forest Protective Association had around 35,000 acres under forest fire protection. A newspaper report says the steel lookout near Latta, which H.L. Cottingham staffed, had been operating since the association was formed.[^1]

The South Carolina Forestry Commission says that during the 1980s it became clear the tower system served mainly as a communication network, and its towers were gradually phased out. The Commission says its final fire lookout tower closed in 1993. Its post does not name Latta.[^2] The Latta tower was sold at auction in 2010.[^1]

No source we found describes the tower or its site today, including whether it still stands.
