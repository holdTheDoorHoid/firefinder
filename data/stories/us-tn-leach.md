The Leach Fire Lookout Tower stands on Leach Road, near the unincorporated community of Cedar Grove in Carroll County, West Tennessee.[^1][^2]

It is an 80-foot, bolted steel truss tower of the Aermotor LS-40 standard design, topped by a square cab about seven feet on each side.[^1][^2] Windows wrap the cab on every side, so the view runs all the way around.[^1] The National Register nomination dates the tower to 1957 but does not name who built it.[^1]

Until the 1970s, the tower was used to spot fires during high-fire season and was staffed around the clock.[^1] From the mid-1970s, forestry staff leaned more on spotting planes and 911 reports, and the tower stopped being used for lookout watch.[^1] Tennessee's fire lookout towers were largely abandoned around then in favor of other methods.[^1]

The tower has changed little since; the nomination notes only two modern sheds added. About 165 feet north, a modern radio tower stands behind a chain-link fence topped with barbed wire; it is licensed to the state forestry radio division.[^1] Forestry staff occasionally go up the tower for maintenance checks.[^1]

The nomination treats the tower as state property: a deed clause says the structures stay with the state even if the site is abandoned.[^1] The Tennessee Division of Forestry administers the site.[^1][^2] The National Register listed the tower in November 2017 as part of the division's fire lookout group, covering 1933 to 1975.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register followed in February 2023, after a site visit that January.[^2]

The register describes the site as an active state forestry site. It also notes that the original Aermotor badge is gone, though its outline can still be seen.[^2] No source says whether the public may visit. Check with the Tennessee Division of Forestry before going, and do not climb the tower without permission.[^1][^2]
