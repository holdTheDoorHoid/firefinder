Zaca Peak, in Santa Barbara County, was the first permanent fire lookout station on what was then the Santa Barbara National Forest, now the Los Padres.[^1] The register says it was built in 1914 as a crude pole tower about 25 feet tall, with a canvas-roofed platform on top.[^1]

A January 1916 Santa Maria Times item, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page, describes a lookout man at Zaca Mountain and a planned thirteen-mile telephone line from the station to a ranch on the Sisquoc.[^2] With the new connections, he could pass along word of fires seen up the Sisquoc and so cut several hours from the trip to a blaze.[^2]

The station did not stay on the peak.[^1][^2] The register says the Zaca Peak tower was removed in 1917 and that Figueroa Mountain replaced it as the primary site.[^1] A July 1917 item, quoting the Santa Ynez Argus, reported that the station had moved to Figueroa Mountain, where the view was broader, the access easier and the water supply better.[^2] The register and the newspaper agree on the year, so this story treats the move and the removal as one 1917 event.[^1][^2]

We found no source describing what, if anything, remains on Zaca Peak today. The register places the site in the Los Padres National Forest.[^1] A Santa Maria Times column says the forest land around Zaca Lake is open to the public, even though the lake itself is privately owned.[^3] It also calls the Zaca Peak hike via the Grass Mountain Loop Trail a strenuous 8.8-mile hike.[^3]

The same column says the route can be a bit tricky to follow without a good map, and recommends getting maps and details online before starting out.[^3]
