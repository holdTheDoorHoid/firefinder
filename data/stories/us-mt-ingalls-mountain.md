The Ingalls Mountain lookout was a 35-foot steel tower about 19 miles west of Whitefish, in Flathead County, Montana.[^1][^2] A newspaper report from October 1930 says the first steel lookout tower in the Blackfeet forest had been completed on Ingalls Mountain. Its observation room had four large glass windows.[^2] Firelookout.com and SummitPost both give 1934 as the year it was built, so the sources disagree on the date.[^1][^3]

Firelookout.com says the tower was moved in 1978 to a Stoltz Lumber Company site about a mile east of Whitefish Mountain.[^1] SummitPost puts that site a mile east of Big Mountain.[^3] A separate firelookout.com page for Whitefish Mountain describes a 35-foot steel tower put up on Stoltz land in 1978, labeled as moved from Ingalls, and says it was removed in 1993 to private property three miles southeast of Whitefish.[^4]

Lightning started the Brush Creek Fire on June 26, 2007, and it grew to roughly 30,000 acres across the Kootenai and Flathead national forests.[^3] SummitPost says it burned Ingalls Mountain badly.[^3] By then the tower had been gone from the summit for nearly three decades.[^1][^3]

SummitPost says the summit once had a lookout and that there is hardly anything left there.[^3] The site is on Flathead National Forest land.[^1] SummitPost gives driving directions to a trailhead and calls the mountain fairly easy to climb.[^3]

The tower itself may survive.[^5] A 2022 Whitefish Pilot article describes a working vintage lookout in the yard of a rural Whitefish home.[^5] The article says the tower once stood on the south face of the Whitefish Range near Big Mountain, on Forest Service land donated by the Stoltze company.[^5] It calls the lookout 40 feet tall and does not name Ingalls Mountain.[^5] The Whitefish Mountain page describes a tower removed to private property near Whitefish, which fits, but no source ties the two together outright.[^4][^5] The tower sits in a private yard, so it is not a public site.[^5]
