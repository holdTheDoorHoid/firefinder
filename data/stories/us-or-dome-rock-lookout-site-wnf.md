Dome Rock is a rounded rock formation in the Willamette National Forest, about 3 miles northwest of Detroit.[^1][^2][^4] The forest's 1928 improvements included a standard lookout house on the summit, a 14-by-14-foot Supervisor Hall frame cabin.[^1][^3] A 1929 newspaper said the house sat on a huge rock, with a 200-foot drop on one side.[^3]

Otis Dike took up the post in July 1929.[^3] In July 1932, Dome Rock was one of three lookouts manned that Saturday in the Santiam forest.[^3] Paul Ratzeburg was called back to duty there in 1935, and Jimmy Poole spent part of the summer of 1944 on the lookout before a knee injury sent him home.[^3]

Sources disagree on when the cabin came down. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both say it was gone by 1951.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts Oregon page says it was removed around 1965.[^3] Oregon Hikers says a lookout built in 1928 was replaced in 1948, and that the replacement was destroyed in 1965.[^4] A 1947 survey description of the summit, written before that replacement, still describes a low, single-story building with a peaked roof.[^3] The 1951 date could refer to the original 1928 cabin if Oregon Hikers is right, but no source says so.[^1][^4]

The undated Oregon Hikers guide says the remains of the old lookout are there.[^4] Photos in the register, taken on September 19, 2000, show tie-ins, a cable and an anchor, concrete foundation pieces, and a 1920s date etched into a step.[^1]

The Forest Service lists Dome Rock Trail #3381 as a former lookout site with views of Detroit Reservoir and the Cascades.[^5] To reach the trailhead, head west from Detroit across the Breitenbush Bridge, turn onto French Creek Road (Forest Road 2223), and drive seven miles past the Forest Road 2207 junction.[^5] The Forest Service says the Beachie Creek and Lionshead fires affected the trail, so check current conditions before you go.[^5]
