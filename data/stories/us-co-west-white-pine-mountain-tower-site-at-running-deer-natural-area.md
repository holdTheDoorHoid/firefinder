The West White Pine Mountain lookout went up in 1939, when Civilian Conservation Corps crews put up a timber tower carrying an L-4 cab, replacing an older crow's nest.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives the same year and cab type.[^1] A former Fort Collins mayor, Doug Hutchinson, had worked as a lookout there in 1964/65.[^3]

The Forest Service took the lookout out of service in 1978.[^2] That November an Army Reserve helicopter lifted the cab off the mountain and set it down at Lee Martinez City Park in Fort Collins.[^2] The register says the tower itself was taken down in the 1970s.[^1]

The High Park Fire burned over the original foundation on West White Pine Mountain in June 2012.[^3] By then the cab had already been moved.[^2] In August 2011 the cab was trucked to Running Deer Natural Area, a site expected to give the public easier access.[^2] City-led work days, with help from FFLA members, reworked and stained most of the old structure at its new site, and the cab was placed there as an interpretive landmark.[^3] The Forest Lookouts timeline says the repaired cabin opened to the public in 2012.[^2]

Sources disagree on where the cab sits today. The register says it is at CSU's Environmental Learning Center in Fort Collins,[^1] while the chapter report and the Forest Lookouts timeline place it at Running Deer.[^2][^3] The tower has been gone since the 1970s.[^1] Only the cab survives, and none of the sources confirms its current location. Check with the City of Fort Collins before a visit.
