The Grass Valley lookout stood on Grass Valley Ridge, north of Strawberry Flat in the San Bernardino Mountains, and could be reached from the Rim of the World Drive, about four miles west of Lake Arrowhead Lodge.[^2]

Forest Service cooperators finished the tower in 1925.[^1][^2] A news letter item dated August 7, 1925 called it the highest lookout tower in District 5 and said local sawmill men and permittees had helped build it.[^2] The register describes an 80-foot open steel tower with an 8-by-8 cabin that appears to be wood, but the 1925 item says the tower was built of lumber, so the two sources disagree on its construction.[^1][^2]

In August 1931 a second lookout was posted there, so two people watched the Lake Arrowhead district around the clock.[^2]

In January 1933 snow crushed the supporting frame until the tower came down.[^2] The unoccupied cabin tipped over into the snow, and no window was broken.[^2] Officials said the whole structure would have to be rebuilt.[^2] The register says the lookout was destroyed in a storm and heavy snow load that year.[^1] Forest officials then said they would rebuild on a better site, and a new tower at Strawberry Peak, to the south, took its place.[^2][^1]

The register also gives 1935 as the year the site was removed, two years after the collapse.[^1]

The register says the exact location of Grass Valley is unknown.[^1] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the spot is open to visitors.
