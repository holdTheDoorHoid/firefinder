Barren Peak Lookout sits on a heavily forested mountain in the southeastern part of the Cabinet Mountains, in Lincoln County, Montana.[^3][^1] The National Historic Lookout Register places it in the Kootenai National Forest and names the U.S. Forest Service as its administrator.[^1]

The register says the lookout was built in 1940 as a wooden tower with an L-4 cab, replacing an earlier camp.[^1] A firelookout.com page gives the same year and design.[^2]

John M. Richards, who later nominated the lookout for the register, manned it in the summers of 1944 and 1945, during the Second World War.[^1] I found no notable fires recorded at the site in the sources I read.

The sources date its abandonment to the 1970s.[^1][^2] By 2017 its cab had collapsed, according to firelookout.com.[^2] A USGS topographic map from 1966, published in 1971, is described by Pastmaps as marking a lookout tower on the peak.[^4] The register added the site in April 2011 as US 905.[^1]

Getting there is a walk.[^3] A forest road, 231, reaches the trailhead, and SummitPost describes a route that follows Trail 299 for about 2.7 miles to Divide Trail 6, then an unmarked spur to the summit.[^3] Trees have grown up around the summit, and that trip write-up says they block the views.[^3] What remains there, according to SummitPost, is an old abandoned lookout.[^3] The sources I read describe no closure, but the cab that collapsed is not a place to climb. None of them say whether the wooden tower itself still stands.[^2][^3]
