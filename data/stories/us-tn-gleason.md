The Tennessee fire-tower list kept on tnlandforms.us includes a Gleason tower in Weakley County.[^1] Its entry gives the elevation as 555 feet (169 m).[^1] The entry is marked removed, with a note that reads "GD1498 9-2 removed".[^1]

The Tennessee Division of Forestry oversaw the state's lookout network.[^2] At its peak in the late 1960s it oversaw 208 towers, and 142 of them were still surviving as of March 2014.[^2] Credit for building 98 of Tennessee's fire lookout towers goes to the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^2]

A count from the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Tennessee chapter gives 275 towers built in the state, with 101 removed or destroyed.[^3] The totals differ, and the sources do not explain why.[^2][^3]

Many towers in that network have since come down, and others are waiting to be torn down.[^2] The National Register documentation links the removals to either disuse or leases with private owners that lapsed.[^2]

No source describes the Gleason site today, so it is not known whether any part of the tower stands or whether the spot is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
