This tower began on Squaw Mountain, a peak now called Big Moose Mountain just west of Greenville, Maine.[^2][^4] Maine sources describe the site as the first fire tower in the country, and WMTW adds that it was the first continually operated one.[^2][^3][^4] The first tower there was a 22-foot wood and log structure, erected in 1905 by the M.G. Shaw Lumber Company.[^4][^1] It went into service that June, with William Hilton of Greenville as its first watchman.[^3]

A metal tower replaced the wooden one in 1919, according to a state report that WMTW quotes, and Maine Geographic gives the same year.[^2][^4] The register also lists 1919 as the build date, but its description says the present steel tower with a wooden cab was built in 1959. No other source read for this story gives 1959.[^1]

Watchmen lived in a nearby camp and kept watch from the tower.[^2] WMTW says the watchmen maintained a constant presence until 1976.[^2] Maine Memory says the tower was closed for good in 1968 and replaced by aircraft patrols.[^3] The sources do not agree on which date is right, and none of the sources read for this story describes a notable fire on the tower.

The tower came down from the summit in 2011.[^2][^4] WMTW says its cab was removed by helicopter that year and moved to the visitor center in Greenville.[^2] Maine Geographic says the tower itself was removed from the summit in 2011 and reassembled in 2015 beside the Moosehead Lake Region Visitor Center on Route 15.[^4] Maine Memory gives a different move date, October 2012, and a different site, the Natural Resource Education Center in Greenville.[^3] Maine Geographic also reports that a replica of the fire warden's cabin has been added.[^4] Only concrete stanchions and some eye bolts remain on the summit.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list marks the summit tower as gone and the Greenville tower as standing.[^5]

Today the tower stands beside the visitor center on the way into Greenville.[^4] WMTW's 2024 update says the cab still stands there, and Maine Geographic calls it worth a visit.[^2][^4] No source says whether the tower can be climbed, so ask staff at the visitor center before going up.
