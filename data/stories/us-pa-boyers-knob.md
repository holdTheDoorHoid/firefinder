The Boyers Knob tower stood on the mountain south of Shamokin, in Northumberland County.[^1][^2] Newspapers of the time wrote the name as Boyer's Knob.[^2]

In October 1921 the state forestry department began erecting a tower that a Wilkes-Barre paper described as sixty-seven feet tall.[^2] The register describes it as a steel Aermotor tower built in 1921.[^1] A 1927 Shamokin paper gave a different height, describing a steel structure 47 feet tall.[^2] I used that figure, about 14.3 meters, for the tower.[^2]

The fire season ran from about March to June, and a towerman was on duty at the tower to report fires.[^2] In November 1922 the forestry department at Harrisburg put John Deitrich in charge.[^2] By 1927, M.C. Beck of Shamokin had been the towerman for three years and was expected back that spring.[^2]

In August 1930 the paper reported that the situation had become so serious that the tower was reopened to spot further fires.[^2] The paper said it was the first time the tower had been open at that time of year since it was built.[^2] John A. Scott, Jr., was called back from a vacation in Baltimore to take charge.[^2]

The register gives 1975 as the year the tower was removed.[^1] A state reclamation project named the Boyers Knob Lookout project ran from December 31, 2012, to April 18, 2014, and restored 86 acres of abandoned strip-mine ground at the site.[^3] The fact sheet says three hazardous structures were taken away, but it does not say whether the lookout tower was one of them.[^3]

The fact sheet says that, at the time, the reclaimed ground was part of a large RV park, and that Route 125 North near Gowen City gives access to it.[^3] I found no source that describes any tower remains, and nothing I read says whether the land is open to visitors today.
