Fryingpan Mountain Lookout is a 70-foot steel fire tower in the Pisgah National Forest, overlooking the Cradle of Forestry.[^1][^2][^3]

The Forest Service built it in 1941.[^2] The Register says the lookout was established that year.[^1] The cab is a metal live-in cab with a catwalk around it.[^1][^2] The National Park Service dates the tower only to the 1940s.[^3]

Fire watchers lived in the cab for days at a time, sometimes for weeks, with a radio or telephone to reach the Forest Service.[^2] The lookout was staffed for fire detection until the early 1990s.[^2] The Park Service says it was decommissioned in the 1990s.[^3] None of the sources name a specific fire it spotted.

The Forest Service restored the tower in 2010 with federal American Recovery and Reinvestment Act money.[^2] The Register says it now serves as a radio relay point.[^1]

To visit, the Park Service describes a 1.5-mile hike that starts at a dirt pull-off on Forest Service Road 450. Visitors are asked not to block the gate.[^3] The Park Service says the climb is steady for nearly a mile on gravel, and that visitors can still safely visit the historic tower.[^3] The Register says the tower is easy to reach from the Blue Ridge Parkway.[^1] Check conditions before you go.[^3]

The Park Service calls it the Frying Pan Fire Tower.[^3] It also mentions a rumor that herders named the mountain after a shared pan, and it does not present that account as fact.[^3]

The sources disagree on the county. The Register places the lookout in Transylvania County, while a Forest Lookouts listing says Haywood County.[^1][^4]
