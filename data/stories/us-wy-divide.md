Divide Lookout was a steel live-in fire lookout tower in Yellowstone National Park, about five miles southeast of Old Faithful, in Teton County, Wyoming.[^1][^2] It stood close to the Continental Divide, which is how it got its name.[^3]

A 2018 article quotes a Mount Washburn fire lookout who says the park probably had five manned lookouts. He names Divide, along with Mount Sheridan, Mount Holmes and Pelican Cone, with Washburn as the fifth.[^4] Across the park, the towers were not staffed year-round, and a lookout was usually one person.[^5]

The tower was dismantled in 1991, and the register lists its removal that year.[^1] Before then, visitors could climb the metal stairs almost to the top and look south over the trees toward Shoshone Lake and the Tetons.[^3] The same 2018 article says Mount Washburn is the only one of these lookouts still used as a fire lookout.[^4]

According to the register, none of the tower is left at the site.[^1] The Divide Trail starts from a pullout on the Old Faithful to West Thumb road, just west of Craig Pass, and climbs about 710 feet through the forest to the site.[^3] Partway along, the trail offers partly blocked views of Shoshone Lake to the south.[^3] No source I found describes the site in more detail than that.
