Mission Lookout sits above Swan Lake in the Flathead National Forest, watching over the Swan Valley and the Mission Mountains.[^1] The site's lookout history goes back nearly a century: a 25-foot platform tower went up in 1921, then a cabin and a taller 35-foot wooden platform followed in the 1930s.[^1] The structure standing today replaced those earlier ones in 1959 — a 41-foot treated-timber tower topped with an R-6 "flat cab," the standard Forest Service lookout design of its era.[^1][^2]

Lookout personnel recorded on the mountain include Ewing Micken in 1957 and Mr. and Mrs. Richard Beighle in 1958, both staffing the structure that preceded the current tower.[^3] A recreation.gov listing for the site notes that the location served as a fire detection post for about 75 years in total, spanning the platform era and the 1959 tower.[^2]

The tower is still kept in reserve for emergency fire use, but its main role today is as a rental cabin through the Forest Service's recreation program.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 1473, with Mission Lookout added to the register on December 30, 2020.[^1]

Visitors can drive to a parking area at the base and climb the stairway to the 15-by-15-foot cab, which sleeps up to four people on a built-in double bed and two folding cots.[^2] There's a propane stove, a table, and a vault toilet at the base, but no running water or electricity, so guests need to bring their own water and supplies.[^2] Because of the height of the climb, the Forest Service discourages bringing children under 10.[^2] Grizzly bears are common in the area, and guests are asked to store food and garbage accordingly.[^2] Reservations go through recreation.gov, with the combination lock code sent in the booking confirmation.[^2]
