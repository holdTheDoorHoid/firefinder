Fire detection at this site on the Rogue River–Siskiyou National Forest started in the 1920s with nothing more than a crow's nest lashed into a tree.[^1] That gave way in 1933 to a proper L-4 cab, the standard Forest Service lookout design of the era.[^1]

The building that stands today is a different structure altogether. It's a CL-160 flat-topped metal cab, first put up on Barkalow (also spelled Barklow) Mountain in the Powers Ranger District in 1955.[^1][^2] In 1974 the Forest Service had it flown by helicopter to its current site and set on a steel tower — the National Historic Lookout Register gives the tower as 10 feet, while Recreation.gov describes a 12-foot tower and catwalk, so the exact height is unclear.[^1][^2] Once in place, it was staffed through fire seasons from 1974 until 1996.[^2]

After it came out of regular fire-watch service, the cabin stayed in Forest Service hands and was brought into the agency's rental lookout program.[^1][^2] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2003.[^1]

Today the lookout is a one-room rental cabin sleeping up to four, reachable for anyone willing to book it through Recreation.gov. It comes with a bed, a table, solar lighting and a pit toilet nearby, but no running water — guests haul in their own water, bedding and cooking gear.[^2] The payoff is the view: from the tower guests can look out over the Kalmiopsis and Wild Rogue Wilderness areas, the Illinois and Rogue River canyons, and on a clear day the Pacific Ocean.[^2]
