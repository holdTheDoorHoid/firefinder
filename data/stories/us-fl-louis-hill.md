The Louis Hill Lookout Tower is in Bradford County, Florida, and the Forest Lookouts site lists the Florida Forest Service as its agency.[^1]

The Florida Forest Service put up a 100-foot steel tower here in 1939, on land W.H. Edwards, Jr. donated for the purpose.[^1] In May 1939 the Bradford County Telegraph, as quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, reported that the tower would be called the Louis Hill Tower and dedicated to the memory of a young Bradford County man, since deceased, who had stood out in the forestry service.[^1] The same report said the caretaker's home was already built and the tower was complete, but that it had not yet been manned.[^1]

In July 1942 the Telegraph reported that the State Forestry Department had just completed repairs and a fresh coat of paint on the tower and its house.[^1] The same paper named a towerman, Prescott, who was proud of how his grounds looked.[^1] That is the last staffing I found. The sources I read do not say when the lookout was last manned or whether it ever reported a fire.

An OpenStreetMap entry gives the tower's map point an elevation of 64 metres, but that is a map value and does not show whether the tower still stands.[^2]

I found no source that describes the tower's condition, public access, climbing rules, or rental status after 1942. Until the Florida Forest Service or a recent report says otherwise, do not climb it, and check with the agency before any visit.
