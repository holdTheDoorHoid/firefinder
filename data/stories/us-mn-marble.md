The Marble Lake lookout stood in Lake County, Minnesota, in what is now the Superior National Forest.[^1][^2]

A 1988 Forest Service record credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with putting up a 100-foot lookout, plus a cabin and garage made of logs, in the 1930s.[^2][^4] The county list calls the tower a Forest Service stairway tower, and its 1952 survey note puts the height at 110 feet, although the list itself says the tower was probably 100 feet.[^1] The two accounts differ, so we have kept both.

When the railroad bought the land in the early 1900s, the state held back one acre for a lookout tower.[^2] The county list adds that a CCC camp at nearby Spruce Lake used the tower to spot fires.[^1]

The county list marks the tower as removed.[^1] The 1988 record says the tower and buildings were taken down in the 1960s, and only the foundations are left.[^2][^4]

The one-acre tower site is on the Superior National Forest's list of cultural resource sites.[^4] The Forest Service's research page gives 1988 as the year the natural area was established.[^3] Its directions start in Two Harbors and end at a dead-end forest road near the old lookout site.[^2] We found no description of the foundations as they look today, and no notice about public access. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^5]
