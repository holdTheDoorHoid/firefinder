The Sproat Mountain lookout sits above the town of Arrowhead, just south of Revelstoke.[^1] Its first lookout was a tent, put up for a lookout man in the summer of 1940, after a trail to the summit was built in September 1939.[^1]

In 1948 a prefabricated lookout building, 2.4 m by 2.4 m (8 ft by 8 ft), and a cabin were bought for $600 and $400 respectively and taken up by pack horse.[^1] The present building was flown up by helicopter and assembled by a Forest Service carpenter crew in 1956.[^1] Canada Lookouts and a 2014 Revelstoke Review article both date a permanent lookout on the mountain to 1956.[^2][^3]

A road for four-by-four vehicles reached the tower in 1964.[^1] Canada Lookouts also quotes a 1941 Forest Branch News Letter item. It says an assistant ranger at Arrowhead was attacked by a grizzly bear on August 9th while climbing to the lookout with supplies.[^2]

The club's history says the Arrowhead Ranger District managed the lookout until flooding from the High Arrow Dam closed it in 1968.[^1] The same history then says the Revelstoke Ranger District ran it from 1968 until it closed in the 1970s.[^1] The two statements do not agree, and no other source read settles the date. A January 1969 Squamish Times notice, quoted by Canada Lookouts, said there would be a lookout at Sproat Mountain part of the time.[^2]

Tourism Revelstoke says the Revelstoke ATV Club took over management of the lookout in 2003, in agreement with Recreation Sites and Trails BC, and that the tower is no longer a working fire lookout.[^4] The club says it applied in 2002 for permission to reopen the road, and later agreed with Recreation Sites and Trails BC to clear the road and maintain the tower.[^1]

Work followed. New stairs went in during 2019.[^1] In 2023 the club had the tower repainted, laid new plywood on the floor, and installed the fire finder again, along with information displays that Recreation Sites and Trails BC had commissioned.[^1]

Today the tower still stands, and Tourism Revelstoke says the club has restored and maintained it.[^4] A 2014 Revelstoke Review article called the lookout very well maintained and said there was a working stove inside.[^3]

The trail is open to motorbikes, quads and side-by-sides, and a hiking trail runs beside it.[^1] The mountain opens to ATV traffic on July 15 each year.[^4] In 2023 the club noted that the summit was not to be reached before July 15, because the area is grizzly bear spring habitat.[^1] The club asks riders to have at least intermediate skills, and says it welcomes visitors to the tower.[^1]
