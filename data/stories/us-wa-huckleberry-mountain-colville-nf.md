Huckleberry Mountain, in Pend Oreille County, carried a fire lookout on the Colville National Forest.[^1][^2] A 1925 item from the White Bluffs Spokesman, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said a new lookout would be erected there that summer.[^3] The register dates the first pole tower to the 1930s, so the two accounts do not line up cleanly.[^3][^1] In 1938 the lookout was issued two-way radio gear, and in May 1939 a newspaper item reported that lookouts were set up on the mountain that week.[^3][^4]

The first structure was a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab. In 1964 a 20-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 cab replaced it.[^1][^2][^4]

Firelookout.com lists four known lookout staff between 1955 and 1959.[^2] A 1976 visit report describes a seasonal technician who worked the lookout that summer and came down early because fire weather was scarce.[^1] WillhiteWeb says an earlier seasonal worker and his family found the post hard to live at.[^4]

The lookout was taken away in 1979.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

WillhiteWeb's photo captions mention footings and stairs, but the page does not say when those photos were taken.[^4] The same page says the road is in fairly good shape nearly to the top, that a high-clearance SUV should have little trouble reaching the summit, and that the final few hundred feet are partly rocky.[^4] We found no source that says whether the summit is open to visitors today, so check with the Colville National Forest before going.[^2][^4]
