Grimsthorpe is a former township in Hastings County, Ontario.[^1][^2] According to the Heritage PIN site, the township dates from 1822 and its name comes from an English castle, a detail the site attributes to a 1927 Ontario history.[^2] Wikipedia's article on Tweed dates the township's formation to 22 February 1867, during a local gold rush.[^1] Grimsthorpe and Elzevir Township were run together since before 1968, until amalgamation into the Municipality of Tweed, which was incorporated on 1 January 1998.[^1]

The Open Canada Fire Lookout Index lists a Grimsthorpe Fire Tower, with its location given as "Grimsthorpe, ON, Canada."[^3] The index places the tower at 45.71394, -82.32414.[^3] That point lies far to the northwest of Tweed, which sits at about 44.47° N, 77.31° W.[^1] The position looks wrong for a tower in Grimsthorpe, so it needs checking before anyone relies on it.

In 1922 the Department of Lands and Forests founded the Tweed Forest Fire District as one of 17 districts, and it served as the central headquarters for 21 fire lookout towers.[^1] No source I found ties the Grimsthorpe tower to that district.

Today, no source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the Grimsthorpe site, or whether the public can reach it. Confirm the location with the Municipality of Tweed before planning a visit.
