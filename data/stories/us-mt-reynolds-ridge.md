Reynolds Ridge is in Glacier National Park, about three miles southeast of Logan Pass, at an elevation of 6,400 feet.[^1] The Park Service erected a two-story lookout there in June 1931.[^1][^2] It was built from a ready-cut house made by the Superior Buildings Company of Kalispell, and its cab stood at the east end of a spur above Mirror Pond.[^2][^1] The lookout was fitted with an Osborne fire finder, which the account says was the first introduced to this region.[^2]

Because the site lies above timber line and wood was hard to carry up the steep, rocky trail, the lookout had a Coleman gasoline stove and heater.[^2] A telephone line of two and a half miles tied it to the park's fire dispatcher.[^2]

In August 1951, a Hungry Horse News item quoted on the Forest Lookouts site named Mrs. Robert Bloedel and her husband, an Idaho State college student, as the lookouts on Mount Reynolds.[^2] Mrs. Bloedel saw lightning strike a snag a quarter mile away, and Bloedel went over and put the fire out.[^2] The Missoulian dates the opening to 1932 and says the Park Service abandoned the lookout in 1953, when it switched to aerial fire monitoring.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts site says the Park Service burned the lookout on 28 November 1962, once it was no longer needed.[^2] The Missoulian also dates the burning to 1962.[^3] Firelookout.com, a SummitPost guide and a Huckleberry Hiker blog list the destruction as 1963.[^1][^4][^5] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the lookout as gone.[^6]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains on the ridge today. The site lies inside a national park, but none of the sources says whether the spot itself is open to visitors.[^1]
