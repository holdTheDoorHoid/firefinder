Ingram Point stands at 5,714 feet in Crook County, Oregon, in the Ochoco National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administered the site.[^1]

Before 1933 the point was called Tamarack.[^3] A Crook County Journal item from July 1920 says the Forest Service planned a platform there, and an August 1920 item reports a lookout platform being erected on Tamarack.[^3] The register and the Forest Fire Lookout Association date a crow's nest on the point to 1926.[^1][^2]

Between 1920 and 1929, Forest Lookouts names Wayne Adamson, Norman Harrison, Ray Glasgow, Sam Warg and Paul Whiteis among those who held the post.[^3] A Central Oregonian item says a forestry student had occupied the point for several seasons before Whiteis was hired in 1929.[^3]

In 1927 the lookout reported only one fire that season.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Jeanette Searles as lookout personnel in 1952.[^2]

In 1933 the U.S. geographic board chose the name Ingram Point instead of Tamarack, honoring the late Assistant Regional Forester Ingram, according to a Central Oregonian item quoted by Forest Lookouts.[^3]

The register says the name honors Douglas C. Ingram, a victim of the 1929 Camas Fire near Chelan, Washington.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association says the same.[^2] The sources disagree on his role at the site. The register says he was responsible for building the original lookout but never staffed it.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association calls him the site's first lookout,[^2] and the 1933 newspaper says he first used the point as a lookout.[^3]

A 1933 Central Oregonian item reports that a 65-foot tower was being built on Tamarack Point that August.[^3] The register and the Forest Fire Lookout Association instead describe a 1934 replacement: a 50-foot wooden L-6 tower with a cabin.[^1][^2]

Both the register and the Forest Fire Lookout Association say the lookout came down in the 1960s.[^1][^2] The register says it was removed,[^1] and the association says it was destroyed.[^2]

Komoot now lists Ingram Point as a viewpoint and says it sits in a protected area.[^4] The register includes a photo of the site captioned for 2005, but the caption does not describe what remains.[^1] No source we read says what, if anything, still stands at the summit.
