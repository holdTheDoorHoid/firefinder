Jennies Butte rises to 6,410 feet in Yakima County, on the Yakama Nation's land about 16 miles northeast of Mount Adams.[^1][^2][^3] The register lists the administrator as the Yakima Indian Reservation.[^1]

The site was first used in 1920, when a pole tower and cabin were in place.[^1][^3] In 1935 a three-legged wooden tower, 55 feet tall with an 8-by-8-foot cab, went up.[^1][^3] In 1942 a steel Aermotor tower, 60 feet high, took over.[^1][^3]

Willhite says the steel tower came down after 1979.[^3] The register says the tower has since been removed, and the Forest Lookouts page marks the site as removed.[^1][^4] FireLookout's heading runs the dates as "1942-?", which leaves the end of the steel tower's life open.[^2]

The register lists the site as US 1362, WA 262 and says it was registered on July 20, 2012, nominated by Ray Kresek.[^1]

Willhite's photo captions show footings and a living quarters cabin. The captions for those views carry no dates, and no source we read describes what stands there now.[^3]

Willhite says access needs special permission from the Yakama Nation, and that a 4WD vehicle is needed.[^3] A trip blog says non-tribal visitors are barred from most of the reservation.[^5] The same blog names Satus and Signal peaks as the final two lookouts left on Yakama land, which suggests no standing tower remains at Jennies Butte.[^5]
