High Point is a lookout site on the National Bison Range, about five miles west of St. Ignatius in Sanders County, Montana.[^1][^3]

In February 1934 a Missoulian item reported that a crew of 25 men on the range was building roads and erecting three 60-foot steel towers for fire lookouts.[^2] The towers were to stand at High Point, Wild Horse Mesa and Thumb-Nob.[^2]

The High Point lookout, put up in 1934, was a 65-foot Aermotor tower of steel with a frame cabin.[^1][^3] Both sources say it doubled as a wildlife observation point.[^1][^3] The 1934 item gives the planned towers as 60 feet, five feet less than that figure.[^2]

The U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service is named as the agency by firelookout.com and by the Montana page.[^1][^2] The tribal list places High Point among its Flathead Reservation lookouts and names no agency for it.[^3]

The tower and cabin were gone by 1986.[^1][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the tower as gone.[^4]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors may go there. A Coast and Geodetic Survey station, 30 feet west of the high point, was described in 1968, but that description does not say whether it survives.[^2]
