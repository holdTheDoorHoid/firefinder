The Garwood Lookout dates to 1950 and stands on roughly 40 acres of land the Missouri Department of Conservation holds.[^1] Forest Lookouts adds that the state acquired the towersite in fiscal 1947-48 and that a 100-foot steel tower went up in 1950.[^2]

The sources disagree about how the tower was built.[^1][^2] The Register calls it a metal structure, and Forest Lookouts says steel.[^1][^2] A 1962 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, instead describes a wooden structure on four legs, with a closed observing room up top and a height of about 50 feet.[^2] Three sources give 100 feet, so that height is used here.[^1][^2][^3]

The Register records revisions to the tower in 1969-70.[^1] The state's annual report for that year records work that reshaped and resurfaced the tower road.[^2]

The Register describes the tower as fairly sound as of March 2025.[^1] It also says the cab was inaccessible as of 2025, and that video cameras cover the area.[^1]

Signs at the site allow climbing at one's own risk and cap the number of people on the tower at five.[^1][^3] A blogger who posted in March 2025 found no fencing, though earlier online posts had described the tower as fenced off.[^3] The same blogger called the wooden steps aging and said children, and anyone slightly unsteady, should not attempt the climb.[^3]

The Register added the tower on January 2, 2026, as US 1785, MO 11, after a nomination by Rick Brown.[^1]
