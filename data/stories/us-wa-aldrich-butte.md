Aldrich Butte is a prominent point along the Columbia River Gorge, about five miles southwest of Stevenson in Skamania County, Washington.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout inventory dates a first lookout to 1930, a 15-foot wooden live-in cab.[^1][^2] Willhite doubts it, noting that a 1938 survey party mentioned no lookout building.[^3]

In 1942, when a road was built to the summit, a 10-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab went up.[^1][^2][^3] A cabin was added in 1946.[^1][^2]

From October 7, 1942, until October 16, 1943, the Aircraft Warning Service used the lookout.[^3][^4] Willhite says the AWS papers describe a garage built with AWS money.[^3] None of the pages I read names a person who staffed the lookout.

The lookout was removed or destroyed in 1967.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^5] The site still shows some traces of the wartime and later use.[^1][^3] The FFLOS photo of the site is captioned as a garage foundation, and Willhite counts at least a dozen concrete blocks used as parking barriers in the lot below the summit.[^1][^3]

Willhite describes the summit as reachable by hiking or by a 4WD or high-clearance drive.[^3] The official route is the Pacific Crest Trail from the Bonneville Trailhead in North Bonneville, which Willhite puts at 11 miles out and back.[^3] The older road route crosses posted private land, and Willhite reports that a landowner threatened trespass charges.[^3] Use the trail, and leave the posted road route alone.
