The Lyman Fire Tower is listed at Tilden Lake.[^2] Firelookout.ca gives its address as Ellsmere Village, Ontario.[^1] The two names probably refer to the same community. Tilden Lake is an unincorporated community that was once a timber-mill site.[^3] Wikipedia says the mill belonged to the Ellsmere brothers, and the main village road was named for them.[^3]

After a fire destroyed the mill, it closed for good in the early 1950s, for economic reasons.[^3] A Firelookout.ca visitor calls the tower a relic of the days when people did fire watching by hand, and says the view from its base is unremarkable, since trees surround it on all sides.[^1] That visitor also reports that the lowest section of ladder is gone, and warns against climbing the unmaintained structure.[^1]

The list adds that the MNR no longer maintains these towers and asks people to refrain from climbing.[^2] Dated as of 2013, the same list says the MNR had chosen to remove many towers that were still standing, and it still lists this one as standing.[^2]

Today the sources describe only a visitor's walk, over a bridge and then a hike to the tower.[^1] No source says whether the site is open to the public or describes a permitted route.
