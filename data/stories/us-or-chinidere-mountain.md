Chinidere Mountain is a summit in Hood River County, Oregon, within the Mount Hood National Forest.[^1][^2] A fire lookout was erected there in 1929, and it was gone by the 1940s.[^1][^2]

The register describes the first setup as a firefinder platform with a tent and phone.[^1] A July 1930 photo note on the Forest Lookouts page calls the site undeveloped, which sits uneasily with the 1929 date.[^3] An L-4 cab was added in 1934.[^2]

In 1934 Ed Mohr, a lookout, came across an unextinguished campfire on patrol near Wahtum Lake, and the youths responsible were traced back to Eagle Creek camp.[^3] Six Portland boys were fined at Hood River for leaving the fire unextinguished on national forest land.[^3] In June 1935 the Bonneville Dam Chronicle reported that Harold Gustafson, a forestry student, would be the lookout as soon as pack horses could bring his supplies in, since the snow was still too deep for trucks.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page also notes that the site was activated from July 1 to September 14, 1943, under the Portland Filter Center.[^3]

The register says the lookout had disappeared by the 1940s, and firelookout.com gives the same decade.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page only marks it as removed.[^3]

Today the summit has no lookout left.[^4] Oregon Hikers reports that the only traces left are scraps of broken glass and pieces of cable.[^4] Hikers have put up several windbreaks from the shale on the east slope, and a USGS benchmark sits at the summit.[^4]

The guide describes a hike from the Wahtum Lake Trailhead: drive forest roads to the trailhead, then walk about 1.5 miles to the top, where a 360-degree view opens up.[^4]
