Loudoun Heights never had a working fire lookout. While the ridge was in private hands, a campground with an observation tower was planned there, but the plan went nowhere.[^1] Bill Spach, who came across the site in the mid-2000s while following the original AT route, says years of research showed that no tower was ever erected there and that it was never meant to watch for fires.[^1]

The register tells a different story. It names Spach as the nominator, says the tower has been removed, and says the site appears to have once been enclosed by chain-link fencing.[^2] The association's county table lists it as an observation tower that was never built.[^3] Here the sources disagree. The Appalachian Trail lookout page and the county table both say the tower was never built, and the register's removal note does not match either.[^1][^3]

The Appalachian Trail lookout page says the footings and bottom stairs still remain at the site.[^1] The page's 2024 visit found the old side trail mostly overgrown, with a walk of about three minutes from the trail.[^1] The site is less than 0.1 mile off the trail, with a climb of about 20 feet.[^1] The National Park Service's page on Loudoun Heights says Jackson ordered three infantry blockhouses built on the summit in spring 1861, and that their stone foundations remain today.[^4] The trail author, who found the old stonework interesting, wants to know whether it has Civil War connections.[^1] Neither source says whether the summit foundations and this stonework are related.[^1][^4] The sources read mention no closure or permit for the spot.[^1][^4]
