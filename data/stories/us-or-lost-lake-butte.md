Lost Lake Butte is an ancient shield volcano in Hood River County, near Lost Lake.[^2][^3][^5] It sits inside the Mount Hood National Forest.[^2] Oregon Hikers says the butte's outlook made it a good place for a fire lookout.[^3]

A 40-foot wooden tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab went up in the 1920s, with a ground cabin for the people who worked there.[^1][^2][^3] The register says a 41-foot treated-timber tower replaced it in 1949.[^1][^2] A district history quoted on Weebly dates the contract build to February 7, 1950, so the sources do not agree on the year.[^4]

By February 1959, a district history said the lookout was closed except for emergencies, and that high winds the previous October had taken its roof.[^4]

The Columbus Day storm of October 12, 1962 destroyed the tower, according to the register, firelookout.com and Oregon Hikers.[^1][^2][^3][^6] Willamette Heritage Center notes that the storm was an extra tropical cyclone rather than a hurricane, though the register and firelookout.com still call it a hurricane.[^1][^2][^6] A 1963 annual report, quoted on Weebly, says the tower was salvaged and its material and hardware were meant for Postage Stamp Lookout.[^4] That account does not match the storm story, and these sources do not settle what happened to the tower's parts. The original cabin burned in the 1970s.[^1][^2][^3]

Oregon Hikers says the only remains today are the foundation.[^3] The summit is about 1.9 miles from the lake, with 1,330 feet of climb, according to SummitPost.[^5] Oregon Hikers puts the walk at 2.3 miles from Lost Lake Trailhead and calls it a popular hike.[^3] SummitPost says the entry booth is six miles along Road 13.[^5] Neither Oregon Hikers nor SummitPost reports a closure of the summit.[^3][^5]
