The state raised the Laurel tower in 1931 along with the other towers.[^3] Erection began on July 29, and the tower went into service on October 15 of that year.[^3] The tower stood on a 45-foot rise north of the Georgetown-Laurel highway, along the Midnight Thicket-Concord road.[^3] A 1931-32 state report puts it about two and a half miles northeast of Laurel, while a 1934 report says three miles.[^3] Like the Ellendale tower, it was a steel structure with a seven-foot square cabin, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40.[^1][^3]

The site, a one-acre woodlot, was thinned from time to time by the towerman on days when the cabin did not need him.[^3] The towers were staffed.[^4] In 1934 dark green curtains went up in the cabins to cut glare, which a report described as tiring to the towermen's eyes.[^4]

The 1946 state report says the Laurel tower served as an AWS unit during the war.[^4] The cabin windows were replaced with a new design in 1940, and in 1952 the state bought aluminum sliding windows for the tower.[^3] A 1951 report says the power came from the Delaware Power and Light Company.[^4]

The association says the State Police took the tower over for communications, and that it was removed at some point between 2002 and 2006.[^1][^2][^5] The association's page carries a photograph of the tower captioned January 2002.[^1]

No source describes what remains at the site now.
