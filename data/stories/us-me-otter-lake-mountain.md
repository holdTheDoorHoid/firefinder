Otter Lake Mountain sits in Township 3, Range 4, in Aroostook County.[^2] Its first lookout was made of wood and built in 1911 and 1912, according to a Maine Memory photo record.[^1] A steel tower, 48 feet tall, was built in 1918.[^1]

A 1920 photograph is captioned as a Forest Service fire lookout on Otter Lake Mountain.[^1] In 1921 the Maine Forestry Service produced a panoramic map covering 360 degrees, with the tower at its center.[^2] The panoramic maps started as drawings: watchmen sketched the features of their area on drafting paper, and workers in Augusta later converted those drafts into the finished map.[^3]

Maine's fire lookouts trace back to the 1903 fires, which burned more than 200,000 acres.[^5] Within two years, landowners with timber tracts had raised observation towers on their own land, and by 1909 the state had taken over those privately financed towers.[^5] Maine's towers were linked by telephone, and each was staffed by a single observer who watched for smoke and fire.[^5]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Otter Lake Mountain tower as gone.[^4] No source I found describes what remains on the mountain today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
