The North Carolina Forest Service put up the Bearwallow Mountain tower in 1934.[^1] The tower is 47 feet tall, an Aermotor design topped by a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and it sits on the Eastern Continental Divide in Henderson County.[^1] A state report from those years says the steel tower on Bear Wallow Mountain was the only tower bought with state budget money in that period.[^2]

Clyde Curtis began staffing the tower in 1957.[^3] He and his wife made the summit their year-round home, starting in a two-room log cabin and later moving to a small frame house.[^3] Curtis remembered days with as many as 30 or 40 fires, and a top wind reading of 103 mph before the anemometer blew away.[^3]

Curtis lived on the summit until 1992, and the tower was taken out of service a few years after that.[^3] Most fire detection now happens by aircraft and by calls from the public.[^3] The register projected restoration work for 2011 to 2012.[^1] No later source confirms that the work was finished.

Hurricane Helene closed the Bearwallow Mountain Trail in September 2024.[^4] The trail reopened in July 2025, but only from the Edneyville side, and the roads through Gerton stayed closed.[^4] The trail exists because of an agreement with the Lyda family, who own the land.[^4] Conserving Carolina adds that the Hickory Nut Gorge region is still very much in hurricane recovery.[^4]
