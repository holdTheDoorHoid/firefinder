Goat Mountain sits about 11 miles south-southeast of Estacada in Clackamas County, Oregon.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com names the Oregon Department of Forestry as the agency.[^1]

Firelookout.com dates the first tower to the 1930s and describes it as an 84-foot wooden L-6 tower.[^1] In 1949 an agency newsletter reported a new 70-foot tower in use on the mountain.[^2] A district report from that year gives an 82-foot tower topped by a 14-by-14 cab, so the two heights disagree.[^2]

On January 14, 1950, the tower went down under snow, ice and wind.[^2] Newspapers blamed the snow and ice. A district warden also said deep snow had kept crews from reaching the tower.[^2] A March report on the wreckage pointed to ice from a sleet storm as the cause.[^2]

The directors of the Clackamas-Marion Forest protective association decided on February 24, 1950, to rebuild. A redesign for heavy icing was completed with the U.S. Forest Service, and a 1951 report lists a 14-by-14 cab on an 82-foot tower.[^2]

Firelookout.com says a 75-foot steel tower was moved to Goat Mountain in 1967. The Frog Lake Butte record says it was moved there to serve as a radio tower, after the steel tower was built at Frog Lake in 1932.[^1][^3]

In 1970 the cab came off, and a survey note says the floor was left as a platform.[^2] Firelookout.com says the structure was converted to an antenna tower.[^1] An entry dated July 16, 1985 describes an 82-foot treated timber tower without a cab on the site.[^2] That is the latest description we found.

The Oregon list marks the site as gone, which is hard to square with the 1985 entry unless the tower came down later.[^4][^2] A 1953 survey note placed the site on land owned by the Crown Zellerbach Corporation.[^2] We found nothing that says who owns the land now, or whether the public can reach the summit.
