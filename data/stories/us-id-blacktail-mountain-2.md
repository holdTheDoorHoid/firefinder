A camp came first on Blacktail Mountain in 1926, which a local history says may have sat on the high point.[^1] A 10-by-10-foot cabin followed in 1927 at the north end of the ridge.[^1]

That cabin was the scene of a death.[^1] A federal wildland fire lessons-learned record says Glen B. Butler, 23, was on duty as the lookout when lightning killed him on June 21, 1931.[^2] The local history dates the strike to June 23 and gives his age as 19.[^1] It names him James in the June 23 passage but calls him Ben everywhere else.[^1] The sources disagree on the day, his age and his first name.

The same history places the cabin and the later tower on Butler Mountain, which it describes as the north end of Blacktail Mountain.[^1] A 40-foot tower with an L-4 cab went up in 1936, and it was destroyed in 1947.[^3][^1]

Some lists describe a different Blacktail Mountain lookout on the Kaniksu National Forest, six miles north of Nordman, with a 10-foot tower built in 1932 and destroyed in 1941.[^4][^5] Those dates belong to that other site, not to this one.

Today the site is reached on foot. Drive east up Blacktail Road from Highway 95 near Cocolalla Lake and turn onto NF 230, which ends at the Maiden Rock Trailhead.[^1] From the trail, the ridge runs one way to Butler and the other way to the Blacktail summit.[^1] One photo caption says the Butler Mountain lookout site has trees overgrowing its views, but no source describes what remains of the cabin or tower.[^1]
