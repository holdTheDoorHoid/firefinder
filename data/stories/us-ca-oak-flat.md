A Bakersfield newspaper noted in September 1934 that a new steel fire tower was going up for the Forest Service on Oak Flat peak, on Greenhorn Mountain a few miles from the Davis ranger station.[^3] That tower, a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab on a steel tower with a catwalk, is the Oak Flat Lookout that still stands today, on the Greenhorn Ranger District of the Sequoia National Forest east of Bakersfield.[^1][^2]

The lookout did real work over the following decades. In July 1940, lookout staff at Oak Flat spotted the Rattlesnake Grade fire, and a dozen county firefighters plus 20 men from a nearby prison camp had it under control within two hours.[^3] In June 1945, the Oak Flat lookout guard, Mrs. Andre Keown — the Greenhorn district's only woman in that role at the time — radioed for help after falling ill at her post; a fire control assistant drove out to bring her down to Bakersfield, and her four-year-old daughter had been staying up at the lookout with her.[^3] Recreation.gov, which the Forest Service uses to describe its own rental lookouts, says Oak Flat stayed operational into the 1980s.[^2]

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on March 8, 1999.[^1] Storm damage closed it to rental for the 2022 season while repairs were made.[^1]

Oak Flat Lookout is open today through the Forest Service's recreation cabin rental program.[^1][^2] It sits around 4,900 feet with views over the Kern River Canyon and valley, reached by a mix of paved and gravel road that calls for a four-wheel-drive vehicle for the final stretch.[^2] There's no electricity or running water — guests haul supplies up 40 steps or pull them up by a hand-over-hand basket pulley — and the Kern River Ranger District in Kernville handles bookings and current access conditions.[^2]
