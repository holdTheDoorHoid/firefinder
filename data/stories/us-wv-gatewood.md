The Gatewood fire tower stood on ground now occupied by the Gatewood Group Camping Area in the Monongahela National Forest, several miles from Spruce Knob, West Virginia's highest point.[^1] The register lists the tower as built in 1930.[^1] A lookout-history site that credits a Forest Service history of the forest gives the same year and says the Gatewood Fire Tower on the Potomac Ranger District cost $1,495.05 to build.[^2]

The register also holds a 1933 photograph credited to the National Archives.[^1] Two photographs of footings are captioned October 2008, and the register gives a registration date of September 16, 2008.[^1]

The Weebly listing marks the tower as removed.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's West Virginia list shows the site as ground, with the tower gone.[^3]

Across the Monongahela, the forest was winding down tower staffing by the 1970s, and most towers were torn down.[^4] Two regional articles name the surviving towers differently: one lists Red Oak and Olson, and the other adds Bickle. Gatewood is on neither list.[^4][^5]

Today the spot is a small Forest Service campground for groups, and the agency's Potomac district page places it on a ridge where the tower once stood.[^6] Visitors can drive in. Forest Road 131 ends at the campground, which lies roughly two miles east of Spruce Knob Lake Campground.[^6] The Forest Service's list of 2026 opening dates includes the Gatewood Group Campground, which opens April 16.[^7]

No source we read describes what remains of the tower today. The most recent physical record we found is a set of 2008 photos of its footings.[^1]
