Milk Ranch Peak sits inside Sequoia National Park in Tulare County, and the National Park Service administers its lookout.[^1] The first structure on the summit was a wood tower, which the National Historic Lookout Register dates to as early as 1927.[^1][^4] Peakbagging describes it the same way, as a two-storey wood tower put up about that time.[^4]

The steel cabin that stands there now was built in 1964 to replace it.[^1][^4] The register describes an all-steel cab, 13 by 13 feet, on a 20-foot steel tower.[^1][^4]

According to the register, Bob Roberts and Rose Vaughn were long-time lookouts there from the 1930s through the 1950s.[^1] A May 1952 newspaper item, reprinted by the California Fire Lookouts compilation, says Vaughn had spent nine years at Cahoon Rock before moving to a new station at Milk Ranch Peak, north of the Mineral King Road.[^3] A July 1972 item from the Chula Vista Star-News says Willard Layton, of Bonita, was back on fire watch at the station.[^3] The register says the lookout was last staffed in 1996.[^1]

The Paradise Fire began on September 10, 2021.[^1] Early in the fire, the register says, the lookout and its communications gear were damaged in a burnover, and the structure's condition was still being evaluated in October 2021.[^1] The register also says park staff took the Osborne Fire-Finder away and kept it safe.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register takes a harsher view: it says the lookout was reported lost and overrun, with the Fire-Finder rescued just before that.[^2] Peakbagging says the main building was spared, while an aluminum equipment shelter near the tower burned and melted.[^4] The sources do not agree, so I have recorded the status as disputed.

Both registers added the lookout in October 2021, the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register on October 3 and the National Historic Lookout Register on October 7.[^2][^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites record also marks the lookout as removed in 2021.[^2] The sources I checked say nothing about current access, a rebuild, or a rental, so those questions remain open.
