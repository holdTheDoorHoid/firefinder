Honey Hill Lookout is a 100-foot Aermotor tower in the Francis Marion National Forest, topped by a 7-by-7-foot metal cab with a hip roof.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates it only to the 1930s and calls it the oldest surviving tower at a single location on the forest.[^1] It was one of several lookouts the early Forest Service used for fixed-point fire detection.[^1]

The Forest Service used the tower until 1989.[^1] The South Carolina Forestry Commission staffed it until October 1993.[^1]

The Register says the tower is now maintained on standby status, inside a picnic area on the national forest.[^1] The Forest Service says the tower is no longer in use.[^2] The Register lists it in Charleston County, while the eastern lookouts site lists it in Berkeley County.[^1][^3] The Register listed the lookout on December 1, 1998, on a nomination from a district ranger.[^1]

Today the site is the Honey Hill Recreation Area, a primitive camping spot where a short camp loop circles the tower.[^2] It has a vault toilet, picnic tables and grills.[^2] Reach it by driving north on US 17 from Charleston to McClellanville, then turning left at the yellow-light junction onto SC 45 and going eight miles to the camp.[^2]
