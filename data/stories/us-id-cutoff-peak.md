Cutoff Peak Lookout sits at an elevation of 6,844 feet.[^1][^2] It stands on the Kaniksu National Forest in Boundary County, Idaho, about 11 miles southwest of Porthill.[^1][^3]

The lookout combined a log ground cabin with a tower.[^2] Firelookout.com dates the tower to 1937 and gives it an L-6 design, while Idaho Fire Lookouts describes an L-4 cab on the tower.[^1][^2] The two pages disagree on the cab type.

The earliest name listed is William Edwards, in 1932, five years before the 1937 date given for the tower.[^1][^2] The sources do not explain that gap. Later names are John LaFleur (1946), J.D. Hughes (1947), and Charles Littlefield (1949 to 1950).[^1][^2]

Idaho Fire Lookouts says the lookout was abandoned in the 1960s, and that the tower was removed or destroyed while the log ground cabin remains.[^2] Firelookout.com agrees on the basics: the tower is gone and the cabin is still there.[^1] A 2011 regional article still lists towers and log cabins on Cutoff Peak, which conflicts with the later visit report.[^4]

A visit reported in a September 2021 post found the cabin in poor shape. The writers say one side is badly run down and doubt the cabin has many winters left.[^2] Footings, debris and burnt wooden tower legs remain on the site.[^2]

Idaho Fire Lookouts labels the site a hike-in.[^2] No source we read says whether it is open to visitors today, and given the cabin's condition, people should stay out of it.
