Blackhall Lookout sits atop a ridge in the Sierra Madre Range of Wyoming's Medicine Bow National Forest, at just over 10,900 feet.[^1] The Forest Service built the current tower in 1963: a 15-by-15-foot R-6 "flattop" cab with a catwalk, set on a 10-foot concrete block base.[^1] The Recreation.gov listing for the rental cabin agrees with the Register on that build year.[^2]

Newspaper accounts place staffed lookout work on Blackhall Mountain back to at least 1943. That September, a sixteen-year-old forest guard named Clay Apple finished a 68-day solo stint on the peak, the youngest fire watcher in the Medicine Bow forest that season, credited with spotting three fires during his summer there.[^3] Another clipping from the same week describes a lookout being transferred to "Black Hall lookout" from a post at Hahns Peak, suggesting the site was already an established fire-detection post before the present tower went up two decades later.[^3]

According to the NHLR, the 1963 tower was staffed for about 25 years before being placed on standby status, meaning it stopped having a seasonal fire lookout but remained standing.[^1] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on September 1, 1997, nominated by the Medicine Bow-Routt National Forest supervisor.[^1]

Today the lookout is a rentable cabin through Recreation.gov, reached by a high-clearance, four-wheel-drive road for the final three miles.[^2] It sleeps up to four, with a queen bed and a twin bunk, solar LED lighting, and a nearby vault toilet, but no running water or propane, so guests pack in their own.[^2] Recreation.gov notes it remains a functioning fire tower, so the rental can be pulled from the booking system if it's needed for fire duty.[^2]
