Crews put up this tower in 1949 on Sawyer Ridge in the Nez Perce National Forest, a 41-foot timber structure topped with a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab and catwalk.[^1][^2] David Emmons staffed it in 1972, transferring out at the end of that season — one of the few staffing details that survives in the public record.[^2]

By 1987 the Forest Service had declared the tower surplus to its fire-detection needs.[^1] Bruce and Nancy Dreher bought it — Bruce had retired from the Forest Service himself — and had it taken down and rebuilt next to their home overlooking the Slate Creek Ranger Station.[^1][^2] They renamed it the "Eyeful Tower," and the National Historic Lookout Register describes the result as a careful, accurate rebuild. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Idaho list still shows the original Sawyer Ridge site itself as gone, with no tower standing there today.[^1][^3]

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on February 24, 1996, as US 163 / ID 13, nominated by Idaho registrar Gary Weber.[^1] Sources give the elevation as either 7,273 feet (NHLR, at the Dreher property) or 7,303 feet (idahofirelookouts.com, for the original ridge site) — close enough that it may just reflect the two slightly different locations.[^1][^2]

Today the tower stands as a private, drive-up property. The Drehers have offered tours by pre-arrangement, but it is not open to casual visitors, and nothing in the record describes current access or rental status.[^1][^2]
