Wilton Lookout Tower Hill lies about six miles west of Bemidji.[^1] The Beltrami County tower report dates the first Minnesota Forest Service tower there to 1925. It was 34 feet tall, a ladder-type tower with an octagonal cab.[^2] A second tower replaced it in 1928. That one had a four-legged steel frame and a wood cabin on top.[^2][^3]

In 1930 a St. Louis Post-Dispatch story said the Minnesota Forestry Department used the tower as an observation post for forest fires. It also said that until then the only way up the hill was on foot or horseback.[^1] The Bemidji Pioneer cites a December 1940 Pine Needles Press item reporting that a Conservation Corps camp unit of 40 men had set out to develop a recreation area west of Wilton.[^4]

The ski hill also went by Larsmount Lookout, a name taken from the 1928 tower, but the Pioneer says it was simply called Wilton Hill.[^4] One skier from the late 1940s to the mid-1950s recalled that the main slope, with its tow rope, started at the top of the hill by the fire tower.[^4]

Survey notes from 1980 describe a four-legged steel tower with a wood cabin on top.[^3] The A-Z list of Minnesota lookouts now says the 1928 tower is no longer standing,[^3] and the county report marks it as removed.[^2]

Today the hill is Wilton Hill Recreation Area, a 230-acre county day-use site about 1.5 miles southwest of Wilton. It is open from sunrise to sunset, and access is not plowed in winter.[^5] Non-motorized trail access leads to the old site of a fire tower atop a 1,513-foot hill.[^5] No source we read describes what, if anything, is left of the tower at that site.
