Black Mountain stands near the southwest end of Joaquin Ridge, a ridge in the Diablo Range of Fresno County.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page files the site under the California Department of Forestry.[^2] A survey station on the mountain's highest part sits about 11 miles airline north and two miles west of Coalinga.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts summary for the site, under the name Joaquin Murietta, says a primary lookout was established in the 1930s and that it was discontinued in the 1940s.[^3]

A 1944 newspaper item, as quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, reported that forestry crews had begun construction on six miles of new road to the top of Black Mountain, as preparation for a fire lookout on Joaquin Rock.[^2] The item also reports that a permanent building would replace the temporary one once the war ends.[^2] The summary and the 1944 item do not fit neatly. The summary dates a lookout to the 1930s, while the item describes plans to place one, and the sources do not say whether they describe the same lookout.[^2][^3]

A 1958 recovery note ties a U.S. Geological Survey benchmark on the mountain's highest part to a California Division of Forestry building, placing it about 2.5 feet east of that building's northeast corner.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the site as gone.[^4]

No source we read describes what remains on the mountain now, or whether the site is open to visitors.
