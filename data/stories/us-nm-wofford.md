The Wofford Lookout stands in the Lincoln National Forest, near Cloudcroft, New Mexico.[^3][^4] Its 80-foot Aermotor steel tower replaced a worn-out wooden lookout.[^4] A September 1932 newspaper item described the new steel towers as already placed.[^4] A February 1933 notice reported that an 80-foot tower was being shipped to Wofford, and a second February notice said it had arrived.[^4] A June 1933 report says the tower had been erected, with help from Civilian Conservation Corps crews.[^4] The register and the Forest Service both date the tower to 1933.[^1][^3] Newspaper notices from the time gave different distances from town, from two to five miles, so the exact spot is hard to pin down from these sources alone.[^4]

The observer's cabin at the base of the tower also dates to 1933, and a wood-frame storage shed was added in 1937.[^1][^3] Both buildings were restored in 2012.[^3]

The tower was manned for fire control at least by 1943.[^4] A 1938 notice reports that the tower and cabin had been remodeled, though it spells the name "Woolford."[^4] In April 1943 a man named Henry Beebe moved up to the tower as fire-control work began early that season.[^4]

The complex was added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 28, 1988, as part of a group of Forest Service lookouts in the Southwest.[^2] It joined the National Historic Lookout Register on August 30, 2015.[^1]

Today the tower itself is closed to the public, and the site is reached on foot.[^3] The Forest Service page, last updated February 19, 2025, said the restored cabin, to be offered as a rental, was soon to open for public use.[^3]
