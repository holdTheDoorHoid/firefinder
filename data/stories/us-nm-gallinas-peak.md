Gallinas Peak rises in Lincoln County, New Mexico, at 8,615 feet, and the lookout is on the Mountainair Ranger District of the Cibola National Forest.[^1] The cab is 7 by 7 feet and sits on a 46-foot steel tower, an Aermotor MC-39 design.[^1][^3] The complex also has an observer cabin and a shed for tools.[^3]

The lookout was first part of the Lincoln National Forest, which erected it, and was afterward handed over to the Cibola.[^3] A 1922 newspaper item mentions building a telephone line for the Gallinas lookout.[^2] In September 1932 a newspaper reported that new steel towers had replaced worn-out wooden ones, including a 48-foot tower on Gallinas.[^2] The 2020 report gives 1933 as the year the present tower was erected.[^3] Items from February and April 1933 describe a 40-foot tower on its way and a 50-foot tower staked out, and a June 1933 item reports a new tower erected.[^2] The sources disagree on height and date. The register and the 2020 report both give 46 feet, so I used that figure.[^1][^3]

In June 1932 a fire guard, Paul Porter, was hurt in a fall from the ladder to the lookout tower.[^2] Fire swept the slopes in 2004, but the top came through unscathed.[^4]

The register listed the lookout on June 17, 2007, and described it as back in active use.[^1] A 1938 newspaper item says remodeling of several lookout towers and cabins, Gallinas among them, was completed.[^2] The 2020 report says the lookout was not evaluated in the 1988 National Register study. The report puts its age at 87 years and recommends evaluating it for possible nomination.[^3]

Today a road reaches the summit, which has radio towers and the lookout.[^4] I could not confirm whether the cab is open to visitors. For the latest information, contact the Mountainair Ranger District.[^4]
