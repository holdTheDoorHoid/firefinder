In July 1926 the state forester, Col. Page S. Bunker, chose a Bibb County site for a forest-protection lookout tower. A Birmingham Post-Herald report put it at a point six to seven miles east of Centerville.[^1]

By late April 1927 a steel lookout tower had been completed in Bibb County. A Birmingham News report called it the third tower the state had put up, and reported that the state commission of forestry felt the county was entitled to precedence in building the first tower of 1927.[^1] That report placed the site about seven miles west of Randolph, on land belonging to the Kaul Land and Lumber Company, which gave it to the state once it heard of the plan.[^1]

The two reports do not agree on where the tower stands. One puts it six to seven miles east of Centerville; the other, about seven miles west of Randolph in the southeastern part of the county. The sources do not say whether these describe one spot, so the exact location is unsettled.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register also lists a Bibb Lookout Tower. It gives that tower a 1949 build date, later than the April 1927 tower described here, so it is probably a separate structure.[^2]

No source describes the site today, so whether the steel still stands, and whether the land is open to visitors, is not known from these sources.[^1]
