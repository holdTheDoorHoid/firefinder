Sparta Butte sits in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest, about 13 miles west of Halfway, in Baker County.[^1][^2] Someone was already watching from the butte by 1916.[^3] That October the lookout there reported a lightning fire near Sparta to the forest headquarters in Baker.[^3] A 1921 item said a telephone line then under construction would connect with the Sparta Butte station, mainly to help fight fires.[^3]

The cab on the butte was built in 1934 in the L-4 design.[^1][^2] An October 1933 newspaper item had already described "the new cabin" as up to date, with window shutters, anchor cables and a copper lightning rod.[^3] By 1935 the Forest Service had extended the road to within a few hundred feet of the summit.[^3]

Roy Stalker was stationed on the butte as a forest ranger in 1927.[^3] Merle Phelps was set to be the lookout in 1933, and Yale Mitchell held the post in 1935 as a fire lookout and smoke chaser.[^3] Firelookout.com also names Harry Thomas for 1921 to 1923, which predates the 1934 cab, and Earl Strong for 1954.[^2] The latest staffing we found is a 1960 inspection report, which names Steven Perrigan as the lookout in his first year on the station.[^3]

The register says the cab was removed in 1974.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] The Oregon Lookouts page also gives 1974 as the removal year.[^3]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the road still reaches it. Anyone thinking of visiting should check with the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest first.
