The Ulmer Lookout Tower stands in Allendale County, South Carolina, and is listed under US 989 and SC 26.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates it to about 1935.[^1] It describes a 100-foot tower with a steel cab measuring 7 by 7 by 10 feet, and suggests it is a Bethlehem Steel model.[^1] The register does not name a builder. The state Forestry Commission put up its first fire tower in 1930,[^2] and a register entry for another South Carolina tower, at Leesville, describes that tower as one of about 140 the Commission built between the mid-1930s and 1993.[^3] Nothing we read says whether the Commission built this one.

The register names Benny Crim as the administrator.[^1] It names no tower operator and reports no fire that the tower watched.[^1] The Commission's method used two towers. Each took a bearing on a smoke with a sighting device called an alidade, and the fire was placed where the readings crossed on a map.[^2] The sources do not say how this particular tower was used.

According to the register, the tower still stands on its original site, though it looks to be in poor shape.[^1] The sources we read report no move, fire or removal. The register does not say whether or when the tower stopped working as a lookout.

The register accepted the tower on March 27, 2014, on the nomination of Michael T. Finch, Jr.[^1] It gives no access arrangements. Because the tower is in poor condition and not known to be open, do not climb it. Ask the administrator named on the register before going near it.[^1]
