The Tahuya lookout went up in 1944 as a wooden, live-in tower in Mason County, Washington.[^1][^2] The register puts the site at 443 feet, and Willhite places it inside the Tahuya State Forest.[^1][^3] A 1944 Division of Forestry annual report records a new lookout tower at Tahuya, in eastern Mason County.[^4]

In May 1944 an Aircraft Warning Service station was located at the site, which a listing describes as private land at the time.[^4] The service's funding paid for a 14 x 32 cabin, and the State of Washington kept the site once the service ended.[^4]

The register dates abandonment to 1957, with removal coming later.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same 1957 date and marks the tower as gone.[^2] Willhite says it was abandoned by 1957.[^3]

According to the state Department of Natural Resources, Tahuya State Forest draws off-road riders and hikers and has about 84 miles of trails for visitors.[^5] Willhite notes that the lookout site sits where several ATV and motorcycle trails meet.[^3]

Willhite's directions run from Belfair to a pullout and gate on Elfendahl Pass Road.[^3] He says an easy road walk of about half a mile reaches the site.[^3] He warns that the site has little view left, though if the DNR has recently cut, a few peeks at the Olympics may be possible.[^3]

Willhite reports that the lookout's firefinder was kept at a DNR facility and photographed in 2016.[^3]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains of the tower or its cabin on the site now.
