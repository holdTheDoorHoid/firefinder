Saddleback Mountain lies in Sandy River Plantation, Franklin County, Maine, near Rangeley.[^4] In 1914 a thirty-six-foot steel tower was erected on the summit, with a house or cab on top, and a log camp was built for the watchman.[^2][^1] The Lewiston Sun Journal's county list gives 1913 as the tower's date instead, and the same forestry report records a telephone line for the station that year, so the sources disagree by a year.[^3][^2]

In 1917 a newspaper reported that Kenneth Lee was to be in charge of the lookout station, and that he and his bride, Ethel M. Wade, would live in a log cabin as the only residents on the summit.[^2] By 1926 the watchman was Arthur Long of the state forestry department.[^2]

That summer a lightning bolt demolished the tower just after Long climbed down from it.[^2] A replacement was going up on the summit by September, and WillhiteWeb says the new tower was built that year.[^2][^1]

In 1954 the department brought building materials to the summit by tractor and by air. A tractor and roustabouts hauled lumber up, while cement, gravel, water, shingles and hardware were dropped on the site from aircraft, and each plane made two round trips an hour.[^2] The living quarters for Warden James Parker, who manned the tower, were to be built close to it, so the warden could keep better fire and radio watches than from the camp two miles below.[^2][^1]

The tower was shortened and turned into a radio tower in the early 1970s, and it was removed in the 1990s.[^3][^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association now lists the site as gone.[^5]

The Appalachian Trail runs along the ridge and crosses the summit, so the site can be reached on foot.[^4][^1] WillhiteWeb's photographs show an anchor footing from one of the summit's lookout sites, and the page describes two such sites.[^1] No source we read says what else stands on the summit now.
