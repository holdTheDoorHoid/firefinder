Scott Mountain Tower stands in Choctaw County, Alabama, and the Alabama Forestry Commission administers it.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower was completed in 1949 and describes it as a 120-foot Aermotor MC-39.[^1] A 1819 News profile of Thomas Kaufmann's lookout work, which covers Alabama's towers in general, says the MC-39 was the steel tower model most often seen in the state's forests.[^3] The register entry was filed on January 10, 2010, and it lists the site's elevation at 504 feet, or 154 meters.[^1]

According to the register, the tower still sees use when fire danger is very high.[^1] I found no source that describes its staffing, its cab, or its history between 1949 and today. The Forest Lookouts page for the tower carries only a heading that names Choctaw County and the Alabama Forestry Commission.[^2]

No source I read gives public access, so treat the tower as a working fire-watch structure. Ask the Alabama Forestry Commission before going near it rather than climbing it on your own.[^1][^2]
