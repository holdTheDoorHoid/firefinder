Abbot Butte, in the Sisters district of the Deschutes National Forest, got its fire lookout in 1932 or 1933.[^2][^3] In October 1932 the local forest service office said a tower would go up as soon as a road reached the summit, and a crew was already building that road.[^2] A lookout history page says the finished tower was a 40-foot round pole structure with a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab, constructed in 1933.[^2] The register names the U.S. Forest Service as administrator.[^1]

Lookout firemen worked the butte in the summers.[^2] In 1936 the Bend Bulletin reported that Paul Talonen had been placed there, and in 1938 it reported that Robert Hudson would take the post for the summer months.[^2] A 1961 Albany Democrat-Herald item describes a summer lookout job on the butte, and it is the latest staffing record in these sources.[^2]

In 1967 a logging company pulled the tower down for the Forest Service. The first try failed, so one leg had to be cut to weaken the structure.[^2] The register and firelookout.com both say the tower was gone by 1968.[^1][^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the butte today. The register has a photo captioned "Site on June 3, 2002," but the caption does not say what it shows.[^1] We found nothing on current access, so check with the Deschutes National Forest before planning a trip.
