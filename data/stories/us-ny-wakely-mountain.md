Wakely Mountain Fire Tower stands on the mountain of the same name in Hamilton County, in the central Adirondacks.[^1][^3] Its Aermotor LL-25 frame carries a small 6-by-6-foot cab. The tower was one of the first built in the state, in 1916, and it replaced a wooden fire tower from 1911.[^1][^3] The original 64-foot outside ladder was swapped for a factory stairway kit in 1930.[^1]

A new cabin for the observer's living quarters went up in 1922, according to a Conservation Commission report quoted by the Forest Lookouts site.[^2] The tower was staffed for fire watch until 1988.[^1][^3] A 1955 newspaper report, also quoted there, describes Warren Meeker, a ranger who watched for fires on the mountain, dying of a heart attack while driving to a hamlet for supplies.[^2] In 1960 International Paper and Finch, Pruyn gave a 1,200-acre recreation tract to the people of New York. The tower site, which the companies had earlier provided for fire protection, was part of that gift.[^2]

Two researchers gathered field data in 2001 so the tower could be nominated to the National Register of Historic Places, and the National Historic Lookout Register says it was placed on that register in 2003.[^1] The Lookout Register also records its own registration in 1995.[^1] The state says it is listed on both the New York and National registers.[^3]

Trouble began with the footings. A visitor's report from October 2016 said "the footings are not fine."[^1] The Lookout Register says the state closed the tower that December because the footings had deteriorated.[^1] The state's own account blames wind damage in the winter of 2017 and says the trail was closed too. The two accounts disagree on both the cause and the date.[^1][^3] They also disagree on the number of footings. The register says all eight were repaired in June 2018. The state says a crew and contractor replaced four concrete footings over eight days on the summit.[^1][^3]

The state's announcement says the public can again hike to the summit and climb the tower for views of the central Adirondacks.[^3] The tower sits in the Wakely Mountain Primitive Area.[^3] The register says a New York State repeater sits inside the cab, with its antenna on the south side, and a solar panel provides power.[^1] I did not find a later closure notice. The most recent visitor report I could read is from 2022, so check the state's current word before you go.[^1]
