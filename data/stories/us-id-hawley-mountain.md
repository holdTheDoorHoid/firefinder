Hawley Mountain Lookout stands on the summit of Hawley Mountain in Boise County, in the Boise National Forest, at about 7,300 feet.[^1][^2]

The register, Idaho Fire Lookouts and a climbing guide all date the lookout to 1935.[^1][^3][^6] Firelookout.com describes an L-4 cab on a concrete base, and calls it the only structure ever built on the summit.[^2]

The sources disagree on the base and on later changes. The register describes an R1/L-4 cabin on an enclosed 8-foot tower, and Idaho Fire Lookouts gives an 8-foot concrete base, but firelookout.com gives 10 feet.[^1][^3][^2] Forest Lookouts quotes a 1959 Coast and Geodetic Survey description of a wooden building about 14 feet square on a concrete base.[^4] It also lists a 1963 two-story lookout house with an R4-80 cab and a concrete-walled lower level.[^4] None of these sources reconcile the 1963 house with the 1935 cab.

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Mike Rutledge on staff in the 1980s, and labels the tower "Drive-Up Emergency Status," a phrase the page does not explain.[^3]

A November 1972 newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says Idaho's fire lookouts were unmanned except during fire watches. It reports that a youth who broke into a lookout atop Hawley Mountain was caught, and that the Boise National Forest was fitting mountaintop lookouts with recorders that go off when someone breaks in.[^4] The pages read name no specific fire spotted from this lookout.

The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the tower on September 28, 2020, on a nomination by Brad Eells.[^1]

A climbing guide calls the lookout no longer in use and still standing.[^6] It describes a forest road, FS-374, that runs north from Harris Creek Summit about 10.4 miles to the lookout, and says a high-clearance vehicle is needed in a couple of spots.[^6] The Keeping Watch archive places the summit at 43.99239, -116.03041, within about 4 meters of the register's coordinates.[^1][^5] The sources do not say whether the cab is open to visitors. Anyone planning a trip should check with the Boise National Forest first, and treat the cab as off limits unless the forest says it is open.
