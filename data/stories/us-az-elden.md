Elden Lookout stands on Mount Elden, directly above Flagstaff, Arizona, and the National Historic Lookout Register says a trail reaches it from downtown.[^1][^2] In February 1914 the Forest Service completed a new lookout station on the highest point of Elden Mountain, with a one-room house for the ranger.[^2] The site received a 37-foot Aermotor tower in 1926.[^1]

A December 1950 newspaper item reports that the close of the fire season also ended Bill Pradt's 23 years as Mount Elden's lookout.[^2] The same item adds that the lookout's radio carried much of the forest's fire control work.[^2] I did not find a dated record of who staffs the tower today.

The 1977 Radio Fire burned to the crest, and the 1926 tower served until that fire damaged it.[^1][^3] Wikipedia describes that fire as human-caused and says it burned 4,600 acres on the mountain's southern and southeastern slopes.[^3] The replacement, a 54-foot metal tower with a CL-100 live-in cab, was built the same year.[^1][^4]

The National Historic Lookout Register added Elden on April 1, 2001, as US 372, nominated by Dave Lorenz of the AZ/NM Forest Fire Lookout Association.[^1] The register gives the lookout site an elevation of 9,224 feet, while Wikipedia gives the mountain's summit as 9,301 feet, so the two sources measure different spots.[^1][^3]

The register's page describes the tower as in active service on the Peaks Ranger District.[^1] Access is by trail from downtown Flagstaff.[^1] I could not confirm whether the cab itself is open to visitors. Plan on a hike, and check with the Forest Service before you climb.
