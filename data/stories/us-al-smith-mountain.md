Smith Mountain Fire Lookout Tower stands on the highest point along the shore of Lake Martin, just outside Dadeville in Tallapoosa County, Alabama.[^1][^2][^3] The tower grew out of a problem with the reservoir. Alabama Power Company worried that silt could diminish the reservoir's capacity, so it helped plant millions of trees around the shoreline.[^4] The company then pushed hard for fire protection, and this tower was the first of six planned for a detection network.[^4]

Sources disagree on who built it and when. The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1939 as the build year, but its description says the WPA constructed the tower in 1938, and it lists the structure at 82 feet.[^1] The CRATA history credits Alabama Power Company with the construction, says boys from the Dadeville CCC camp built the ranger station and other buildings, puts the overall height of its LS-40 tower at about 90 feet, and gives April 10, 1939 as the official opening.[^4] A Dadeville Record item from April 1939, quoted by a lookout-history site, describes a Kiwanis meeting held at the mountain upon the tower's dedication.[^3]

The mountain also housed the district office of the state's forestry division, and the site stayed busy for about four decades.[^4] Staff were formally called watchmen, a label CRATA says is not really accurate, since at least two women are known to have manned the tower.[^4] Other towers and fire crews were reached over a private phone system that used a hand crank.[^4]

Patrols from the air and better phone service in rural areas reduced the need for towers as the main way to spot fires.[^4] The Alabama Forestry Service shut the tower in 1980, and it was later returned to Alabama Power Company along with a ten-acre site.[^4] A lookout-history site dates the decommissioning to 1981 instead.[^3] The grounds turned into a dump, and the tower decayed into a dangerous state.[^4]

CRATA took ownership of the site and tower in 2010.[^4] The tower was being rebuilt in October 2011, and it opened to the public in June 2012.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register still lists Alabama Power Company as the administrator, which does not match CRATA's account.[^1][^4]

Today the tower and its trails draw visitors.[^2][^4] The site sits just off Youngs Ferry Road outside Dadeville.[^2] Visitors face a steep 0.4-mile walk from the parking lot to the base of the tower.[^2] Whether the cab itself is open to visitors is not settled by these sources, so check with CRATA before planning a climb.
