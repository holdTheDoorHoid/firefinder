Mid-Cottonwood Point Lookout is a former fire lookout on the minor west summit of Cottonwood Point, in the Challis portion of the Salmon-Challis National Forest in Custer County, Idaho.[^1][^2] In 1934 the U.S. Forest Service erected an L-4 cabin of its standard design on the summit, at about 8,975 feet.[^1][^3]

The cabin was abandoned in 1946.[^1][^2][^3] The register says it is no longer in use as a fire tower.[^1][^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts reports no documented staffing history for the site.[^3] Nothing in these sources confirms that the cabin still stands, and none of them describes its present condition.[^1][^2][^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register added the site on June 28, 2026, as US 1828, ID 165, with PJ Smith as the nominator.[^1] The photograph on the register page was taken during a September 1998 Forest Service helicopter flight that documented 19 lookouts or sites on the then Challis National Forest.[^1]

Today the site is a hike-in. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists it that way, and the cabin lies within the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness.[^2][^3] No source describes a road to the summit or any current use, so the old cabin should be treated as an abandoned structure rather than a place to climb.[^2][^3]

Do not confuse this with a different Cottonwood Point site in Bonner County, where a 40-foot pole tower topped by an L-4 cab went up in 1937.[^4]
