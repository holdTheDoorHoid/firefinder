The summit is called Cleveland Rock in the early newspapers and Snow Peak in the lookout records.[^1][^2][^3] A lookout station was on the rock by 1912, when the local association had strung about 40 miles of telephone line to its stations, and Cleveland Rock was one of three.[^2] A lookout house was built in 1923.[^1][^2] The register calls the 1929 addition a cupola cabin. A 1929 newspaper item said a small house being built on top of the rock would have to be bolted down so it would not be blown away.[^1][^2]

Albert Simons served at the station in several summers, as fire warden or lookout, including 1928, 1930 and 1934.[^2] In 1948 John Reuland was the lookout all summer.[^2] On September 16, 1948, his last day, he found the roof on fire after trash had burned in the stove. He ran down to a spring for water and put the fire out, but a large hole had burned in the roof and the heat had broken the windows.[^2][^3]

Winds from the Columbus Day storm damaged the lookout in October 1962.[^2][^3] In 1965 a new 14-by-14-foot building was built to replace it, at a total cost of $3,499.83, according to the district's annual report.[^2][^3] The register calls that 1965 structure an L-4 cab and notes that it may have been altered during construction.[^1]

By 1978 the windows and casings had been destroyed and a hole had been burned in the cabin floor; graffiti marked the remaining walls.[^2] The lookout was dismantled in April 1980 after long neglect and vandalism.[^3][^2] The register says it was removed that year, firelookout.com says it was destroyed, and the Oregon lookout history and Wikipedia say it was dismantled.[^1][^4][^2][^3]

Wikipedia describes the summit as undeveloped, with only primitive road access, and lists a trail as its easiest route.[^3] No source I read describes what remains on the summit now.
