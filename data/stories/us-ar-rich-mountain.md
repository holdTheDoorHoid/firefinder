Rich Mountain Fire Tower stands at the highest point of the Talimena Scenic Highway, in the Ouachita National Forest in Polk County, Arkansas.[^1]

The steel tower and its 14-by-14-foot metal cab were built in 1952. They replaced an unusual wooden cab on a 20-foot log tower.[^1][^2]

The sources do not agree on height. The register gives 42 feet, the Encyclopedia of Arkansas says 58 feet, and a hiking group's page says the tower stood 65 feet.[^1][^2][^3] That page also dates the tower to 1954, not 1952.[^3]

The tower was used for fire detection until 1975.[^1][^2]

Today it works as an observation tower beside the road. A Forest Service placard at the site tells the story of the family that first lived in the tower.[^2]

A hiking group's history describes earlier towers on the summit. It says a wooden fire tower replaced a pine-tree platform in 1910. The register gives no date for the wooden cab it describes, so I cannot check the 1910 date against it and have not used that date in this timeline.[^1][^3]

Nothing I read gives current opening hours or says whether visitors may climb the tower, so check with the Ouachita National Forest before you go.[^1][^2]
