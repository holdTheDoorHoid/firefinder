The Edison fire tower stood in the Sparta Mountain Wildlife Management Area in Sussex County, New Jersey, at 1,368 feet (417 m).[^1] The New Jersey Forest Fire Service administered it.[^1]

The tower went up around 1919 or 1920 on New Jersey Zinc Company property, with the company's help.[^1][^2] A state annual report for 1919-20 says it had been in service since July 15.[^2] A 1923 newspaper item gives 1920 as the year it was erected, and describes what could be seen from it, including the Kittatinny Ridge beyond the Wallkill Valley.[^2]

Sources disagree on its height. The register gives 47 feet for the Aermotor tower, the 1919-20 report gives 45 feet, and the 1923 item says fifty feet.[^1][^2]

The register says the tower was removed in 1935-1936.[^1] In 1937 it was moved and built up with more supports, and it became the Greystone Fire Tower.[^1] The New Jersey Forest Fire Service built Greystone Station in 1937, reusing the Edison tower's top section and metal cab on an extended base, and it now stands 92 feet tall.[^3][^4]

Greystone sits on Union Hill in Morris County, and the National Historic Lookout Register says it is in active service.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says Greystone was still an active tower as of 2019.[^1]

At the Edison site, the register includes a photo captioned "Foundation," which suggests footings remain.[^1] I found no other source that describes the site today.
