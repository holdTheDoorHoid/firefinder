Alberta's provincial lookout dataset gives 1963 as the year the present Limestone lookout was built.[^1] The dataset describes it as a cabin, 5 m (16 ft) high, at an elevation of 2,254.3 m (7,396 ft).[^1] It sits on the highest point of its ridge.[^2] An Alberta Historic Places article notes that early summit lookouts were built on the ground or on short stilts, unlike towers built on high hills in the boreal forest.[^5] OpenStreetMap, the volunteer-built map, tags the site as an observation tower, which disagrees with the provincial label.[^4]

A visitor who reached the summit on 8 July 2010 was greeted by an observer, who went back inside to fetch the visitors' book.[^2] The same account says a sign on the access road called it a private road owned by Shell.[^2] A snowshoe ascent on 5 April 2014 passed communication towers at the normal starting point before reaching the summit lookout.[^3]

The provincial dataset still lists the lookout as active.[^1] No source describes the site today, and none says whether the public may visit it.
