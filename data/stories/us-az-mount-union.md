Mount Union Lookout stands on a peak of nearly 8,000 feet in the Prescott National Forest, in Yavapai County, Arizona.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site says the peak was first set up as a "constant" lookout in 1911.[^2] A February 1924 newspaper item reported that a tower there had been completed, though its smoke-finding equipment was still missing.[^2]

In September 1933 the forest announced that two of its five lookout stations would be modernized with new 30-foot steel towers and 12-by-12-foot cabins, one of them at Mount Union. The announcement said emergency conservation workers would probably erect the Mount Union tower.[^2] The Register describes the present tower as a 30-foot Aermotor Mc-24 with a modified L-4 cab, built in 1933.[^1]

Several people are named in the newspaper items the Forest Lookouts site quotes. An August 1924 item calls the lookout William Pratt, who reported a fire. Items from 1925 and 1927 name William Pradt, who went to the scene of a blaze and reported fires.[^2] Floyd Orr put out two lightning-caused fires in July 1931 after spotting them from his station.[^2] In 1952 the forest announced that a local ranger, Bill Wells, would go on duty at the tower because the forests were extremely dry and hunters posed a threat of fire.[^2] A June 1976 item says a Forest Service ranger and his new bride chose to honeymoon at the lookout.[^2]

The Register lists the tower as US 529, AZ 51, registered on August 5, 2003, and says it remains in active service.[^1]

The sources read do not say whether the public may visit the tower or who staffs it now. The Register lists the Bradshaw Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1]
