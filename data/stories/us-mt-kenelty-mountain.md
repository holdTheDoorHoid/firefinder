Kenelty Mountain sits in the Kootenai National Forest, about 24 miles southeast of Libby, in Lincoln County, Montana.[^1]

The fire lookout site says the mountain started as a camp in 1914.[^1] A 1929 cupola cabin came next. In 1934 the present structure replaced it, a 10-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, so the tower itself dates from 1934.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4] The fire lookout site says the windows are gone, the roof has minimal support, and the tower was close to collapsing in 2017.[^1] The Outbound describes an abandoned lookout that has been decommissioned, and its writer advises against going inside because the floor looks unsteady.[^3]

The hike starts on a National Forest trail, numbered 104, that meets Divide Trail 6 on a ridge.[^2] Visit Montana lists it as 2.5 miles and easy, starting at Road 761 after a drive of 33 miles south from Libby on US Highway 2.[^2] The Outbound describes a moderately difficult walk of about 3 miles from the ridge, and gives a route that turns onto Road 763.[^3] The two sources disagree on length, difficulty and road number, so check a current map before you go.[^2][^3]

I found no staffing dates or record of fires for this tower in the sources I read.
