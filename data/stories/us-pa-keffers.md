Keffers, in Schuylkill County, was the first lookout the state raised in that section for fire prevention.[^1][^2] A 1921 Pottsville newspaper said it was rapidly nearing completion.[^2] The Register says the round wooden cab sits on a metal tower that had first carried a domed military radar.[^1] That radar account does not sit neatly with the 1921 and 1941 dates, so the early history of the metal frame is unclear.[^1][^2]

In October 1926 the state placed Verna Rightler in charge of the tower.[^2] In 1931 it was painted and numbered 913 to help aviators.[^2] In 1941 the state planned to use it as an air interceptor tower, manned by a local American Legion post.[^2] In 1957 James Gilger was given fire-watch duty there.[^2]

The Register lists the tower with the registration numbers US 96, PA 3, dated December 30, 1994.[^1]

The later sources describe the tower's removal.[^2][^3] A 2015 bid notice asked for the tower to be fully dismantled for later reassembly, with work due by November 6, 2015.[^2] A 2017 Forest Fire Lookout Association Pennsylvania report said the top cab was at the District 18 (Weiser) office near Aristes, awaiting refurbishment for educational use.[^3]

No source found says a tower stands at Keffers now, and none describes public access to the site.
