Ojibway Fire Tower stands on Greenstone Ridge in Isle Royale National Park, at the junction of the Greenstone Ridge and Mount Ojibway trails.[^1][^3]

A big fire, the Greenstone Ridge Fire of 1936, burned across part of the island soon after it became a national park.[^1][^2] Wikipedia says the National Park Service responded by building two wooden towers in 1939, one of them on Mount Ojibway.[^1] The nomination disagrees on the cause. It says the fire itself did not prompt the towers, because fire lookouts were already NPS strategy, though the fire did shape where the first ones went.[^2] It also says Civilian Conservation Corps personnel built the earlier tower at this spot, and that it was demolished in 1963.[^2]

The tower that stands today is a steel lookout built to a 1954 U.S. Forest Service standard design, with living quarters.[^2] The nomination says a three-man crew from a Houghton, Michigan contractor assembled it in July 1964.[^2] Wikipedia gives the completion date as 1965.[^1] The nomination measures the tower at 41 feet 3 inches from its concrete piers.[^2]

The tower was a fire watch station.[^3] By 1968 it was no longer staffed, and it was used to house the island's radio repeater.[^1] In 1980 the repeater was switched to solar power, and air-quality monitoring equipment went in during 1987.[^2] The site was added to the National Register of Historic Places in April 2021.[^1]

Today the tower is a popular viewpoint for hikers on the Greenstone Ridge Trail.[^3] Hikers can reach it from Daisy Farm Campground, and boats can dock at Daisy Farm Dock.[^3] The park says the tower room is off limits, though visitors can climb partway up the stairs to look out.[^3]
