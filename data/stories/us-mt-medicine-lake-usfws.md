The Medicine Lake headquarters tower stands at the Medicine Lake National Wildlife Refuge in Sheridan County, Montana.[^2][^3] The refuge was set aside in 1935 by presidential order.[^5] In March of that year the Biological Survey was set to open bids for a headquarters site and buildings, and the list of construction work included a lookout tower.[^1]

The tower went up in 1936.[^1] The Forest Lookouts listing describes it as a 100-foot steel tower with a cab, which was painted red.[^1] The refuge calls it a 99-foot watch tower.[^2] Firelookout.com gives the build date only as "1930s?" and describes the tower as used mainly for wildlife observation.[^3] We found no account of it being used to watch for fires, or of anyone staffing it as a fire lookout.

The tower is still standing. Firelookout.com says it is still there, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's list of Montana towers shows it as standing.[^3][^4] The refuge says the tower has since been renovated.[^2]

The sources disagree on height. The refuge gives 99 feet,[^2] the Forest Lookouts listing gives 100 feet,[^1] and a Montana travel article says the tower rises "100 feet above the office."[^5] Our earlier record gave 9.1 meters, or 30 feet. That figure matches a separate 30-foot wood tower that the Civilian Conservation Corps built at the refuge's homestead site in 1938 and 1939. The Forest Lookouts site lists that tower as removed.[^6]

Today the refuge asks visitors to call before climbing. Its page says the tower can be climbed for much of the year when conditions allow, and gives the refuge office at 223 North Shore Road as the place to stop by.[^2] The travel article says the headquarters lies about two miles east of the highway.[^5]
