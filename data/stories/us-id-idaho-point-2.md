Idaho Point sits in the Clearwater National Forest, about 22 miles east of Kooskia, at roughly 6,027 feet.[^1][^3] A Clearwater forest history's place-name list says the name comes from the shape of the point's top, which resembles the map of Idaho.[^6] That entry gives no location, so linking it to this lookout is an inference.

A 35-foot platform tower went up here in 1930.[^1][^2][^4] In 1932 an L-4 cab on a log crib was installed to provide living space.[^1][^3][^4] The L-4 was a 14-by-14 cabin design, and such cabins were produced until 1952.[^7]

On the morning of August 11, 1934, lightning started the Pete King Fire in a storm that set off many other fires in the area.[^5] Both were lost in the Pete King Fire, and neither was rebuilt.[^1][^2][^3]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as drive-up.[^3] No source says whether it is open to the public.
