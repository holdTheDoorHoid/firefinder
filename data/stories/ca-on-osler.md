The Osler Fire Tower was a lookout in Algonquin Park, in Nipissing District.[^2] The Friends of Algonquin Park call it the Osler Township Fire Lookout Tower.[^1]

Ranger crews built the tower and a cabin for its towerman in 1962.[^1][^2] The cost was $2,800 for the tower and $2,900 for the cabin, and the crews did the work themselves.[^1] The tower was on a hill that let it see out 16 km (10 miles), and it lay within a 24 km (15 miles) zone with many lightning fires.[^1] Builders picked the lake for the cabin because it could be reached by road and aircraft could set down on it.[^1] Pembroke was the hub for 15 fire lookouts, among them the Algonquin Park towers, and Osler is one of those 15.[^3] A 1963 provincial map of the tower detection system also marks the tower.[^2]

Through the 1960s the towers saw steadily less use, as aircraft spotting and fire crews took over that work.[^5] According to the Friends, the towers' detection work ended in the early 1970s.[^1] Since then, Interior maintenance crews and members of the public have both used the site.[^1] In 1994, several Friends directors volunteered to do serious repair work on the cabin.[^1]

A January 2023 forum post passes on a secondhand account, from an email sent to the poster by someone who had visited. That account says the ministry pulled the bolts out of the tower's base and tipped it over, and that remains of the top wooden cabin were found.[^4]

The Birchcliffe cabin can be reached only by backcountry canoe, with no vehicle access.[^1] The old tower site is a long uphill walk of 3.2 km (2 miles) from the cabin.[^1] Visitors need to reserve the cabin, and Ontario Parks charges rental fees for it.[^1] Apart from the forum's secondhand account, no source describes what stands at the tower site today.[^4]
