Higley Peak sits near Lake Quinault, about two miles north of the lake, in Grays Harbor County, Washington.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives its elevation as 3,025 feet, or 922 meters, and lists the Olympic National Forest as its location.[^1] The peak takes its name from Alfred Higley, who arrived in the area with his son and then settled at Lake Quinault.[^3]

The register dates a 14-by-14-foot cabin on the summit to 1934.[^1][^3] WillhiteWeb says a cab of that size was there in 1932 or 1934.[^3] George Clisby took panoramic photos from its roof in 1935.[^3][^4]

During WWII, the Aircraft Warning Service made use of the summit.[^3] The Olympia Filter Center activated it as station "Baker 7-3" on March 10, 1942.[^4] In 1944 observers moved in and added an 8-by-16-foot cabin for year-round living.[^3][^4] When the service ended, the Forest Service retained the site for its original uses.[^4]

The sources disagree about the tower. The register and Firelookout.com say a 40-foot treated timber tower replaced the cab in 1957.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb dates a rebuild on a wooden tower to 1964, and notes that another source gives 1957 for the 40-foot tower.[^3]

The register and Firelookout.com say the tower was gone by 1972. An attempt to remove it by helicopter, they say, ended with it falling into Lake Quinault and sinking.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says it was removed by helicopter in 1973.[^3] It gives two accounts of what happened next: one says the tower and cab fell into Lake Quinault and sank, and the other says the cab and tower found use at the Snohomish County Airport.[^3] After removal the summit carried a radio reflector, and that equipment was taken off in 2002.[^3]

Today WillhiteWeb says the cabin's footings still hint at its spot on the hillside just below the summit.[^3] The summit no longer has its views.[^3] The trail to the top is about half a mile, with an elevation gain of 272 feet.[^3] Part of the drive runs along the boundary with Quinault Tribe land.[^3]
