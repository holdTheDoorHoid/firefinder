Kirkland Butte rises to 5,244 feet in Wallowa County, Oregon, on the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest, about 25 miles northeast of Wallowa.[^1][^2]

The first lookout on the summit was a 90-foot tree platform, built around 1926.[^1][^2] A 100-foot treated timber tower with an L-6 cab followed in 1934.[^1][^2]

A list of known lookout staff runs from D.L. Davis in 1936 to John and Blanche Butler in 1967.[^2] A 1943 newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, names Gerald Bevans and Mrs. Bevans, both members of the school faculty, as in charge of the station that year.[^3]

The 1934 tower fell down in the wind in 1940.[^1][^2] A 101-foot steel Aermotor tower replaced the wooden one in 1941, and it was removed in the 1950s.[^1] The sources do not give an exact year for that removal.

The register says the ground cabin built alongside the tower is still on the summit.[^1] Forest Lookouts says the tower was removed and the living quarters remain on site.[^3] No source we read describes the cabin's condition now. The land is Wallowa-Whitman National Forest land, but no source we read says whether the summit is open to visitors.[^1]
