According to the province's register, Buffalo is a steel lookout tower in Alberta, 30 m (98 ft) tall, at an elevation of about 801 m (2,628 ft), built in 2011.[^1]

The province says its sites are picked for clear sightlines, so that fires can be detected and reported.[^3] Alberta's lookout history predates Buffalo's 2011 construction. After 1912, Alberta had two kinds of lookout: summit lookouts built on the ground or on short stilts, and towers on high hills in the boreal forest that lifted the observer over any obstacles blocking the view.[^2] Steel came into use in the 1920s and gradually took over from wood in tower building.[^2]

Descriptions of staffing differ. Alberta says each lookout site has an observer on watch, generally from April to October.[^4] The 2015 Alberta Wildfire review says most lookouts run through the core fire season, while the rest are crewed only when hazard and risk peak.[^5]

The register lists Buffalo as active, notes a weather station, and codes its access mode as "drive".[^1] Alberta calls its lookout sites isolated and says staff must be ready for a varied set of situations, including a run-in with a curious bear.[^4] No source says the public may visit, and none describes the tower's condition today.
