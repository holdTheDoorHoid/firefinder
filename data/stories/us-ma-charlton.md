Little Mugget Hill in Charlton was first used for fire detection in 1914.[^1] Before a tower stood there, the state watched from the ground, from a grassy pasture with a wide outlook.[^2] The first tower went up in 1917 or 1918, and the sources disagree on its height.[^1][^2][^3] The registry gives 48 feet, while a 1917 state forester report puts it at 50 feet, with stairs.[^1][^3] Neighboring towns helped pay for it, and the station was meant to cover roughly 150,000 acres of forest.[^3]

The September 1938 hurricane destroyed that first tower.[^1][^2][^3] In 1939 Civilian Conservation Corps workers built its replacement: a steel Aermotor tower 60 feet high, with a wood cab measuring 10 by 10 feet.[^1][^3] The FFLA page credits the US Forest Service with supplying the tower, and it lists the structure at 68 feet, so the height is disputed.[^2]

A road to the tower was built in 1951, and a new cab and a chain-link fence went up in 1966.[^2] In 2015 the state's forest fire control maintenance crew inspected the tower to see whether it would need repairs later.[^4] I found no staffing history for it in these sources.

A state listing last updated in May 2016 marks the tower as standing and active.[^5] The FFLA page also says the state still keeps it in service, but I found no current information about visits or rentals.[^2] A state plan for fire observation towers says they are closed to the public and are not meant for recreation.[^6] Please don't climb it without permission. The Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control administers the tower, so that is the office to ask.[^1]
