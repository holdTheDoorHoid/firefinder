Lochsa Peak lookout stood about two miles south of the Lochsa Ranger Station, in Idaho County on the Clearwater National Forest.[^1][^5] The first structure, from 1930, was an observatory built in the cut-off top of a fir tree about 65 feet up.[^3][^5] The L-4 cab that most sources describe went on an 8-foot log crib in 1931, though one account says the whole lookout dates to 1930.[^1][^3][^4][^5]

Earnest Hartman and Harry Chenoweth are the staff named for 1930, the only year Idaho Fire Lookouts lists names.[^2] Staffing continued each season through 1941; the site was abandoned after that.[^3][^5]

Flooring and usable lumber were recovered and taken to the Lochsa Ranger Station; the rest was set on fire.[^5] Louis Hartig's history places the burning in the early 1950s,[^3] while the Forest Service post that quotes his text gives 1953.[^5] Four current sources list the lookout as gone.[^1][^2][^4][^7]

None of the sources we read describes what, if anything, remains on the site today, or whether the public may visit it.[^1][^2] The Lochsa Historical Ranger Station, on U.S. Highway 12, served as the administrative base for the lookouts and was designated an interpretive site in 1976.[^6] Its listed 2026 season ran from May 23 to September 1.[^6]
