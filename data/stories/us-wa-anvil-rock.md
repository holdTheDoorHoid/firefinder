Anvil Rock is a shoulder on the south side of Mount Rainier, at 9,584 feet, inside Mount Rainier National Park.[^1][^2][^4] The first lookout was a stone shelter with a firefinder set outside it. The registry, firelookout.com and Willhite all date it to 1916.[^1][^2][^4] A 1917 newspaper item called the new masonry stations "built recently," so the start date is not entirely settled.[^3]

The Park Service paid for the station, and by 1920 to 1921 the Forest Service kept a fire ranger there.[^3] A telephone line from Paradise Inn reached the station in 1921.[^3] That year a newspaper said the altitude made the post hard to fill, and it named Clement Klingensmith as the lookout in the 1920 season.[^3]

Rice N. Anderson was the primary lookout in 1922. That September, a lightning bolt struck the telephone line just as he picked it up, and it left him unconscious for an hour.[^3] The station also spotted fires. A storm started eighteen fires near Randle in 1922, and the Anvil Rock lookout spotted them.[^3] In August 1927 a thunderstorm led the lookout to report 13 lightning fires.[^3]

A standard D-6 cupola cabin replaced the stone shelter. A 1927 newspaper item said the cabin would take the stone house's place that season.[^3] Willhite places the swap in the summer of 1927, while the registry and firelookout.com say about 1928.[^1][^2][^4] The cabin was abandoned in 1942.[^1][^2][^4]

After that the sources part ways. The registry says the cabin was removed sometime afterward.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed in 1948.[^2] Willhite guesses the Park Service asked for its removal around 1947, and he reports a rumor that the men given the job doused it with gasoline and torched it on a foggy day. He presents the rumor only as a rumor.[^4]

Willhite says little is left on the site today.[^4] None of the sources describe a footing, a foundation or a view from the site now.[^1][^2][^4] The registry names the National Park Service as the administering agency.[^1] The sources do not say whether visitors may go up, so check with the park before you plan a trip.[^1][^4]
