Nannie Peak Lookout Site sits on Nannie Ridge in Lewis County, Washington, inside the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1][^3] The U.S. Forest Service administers the land.[^1] Willhite's trip guide gives the lookout's elevation as 6,106 feet.[^3]

The site had an L-4 cab, which the register and firelookout.com both date to 1934.[^1][^2] Willhite places the work in 1933 and 1934, and says the crew was snowed in during construction.[^3] Willhite also reports panoramic photos from 1936, taken 16 feet above the ground.[^3]

Willhite writes that the lookout was abandoned in the 1940s.[^3] The cab, he reports, met its end in the 1960s, either removed or burned.[^3] The register says the cab was no longer standing by the 1960s,[^1] and the Forest Lookouts listing marks the site as removed.[^4]

No source we read describes a standing structure at the site today. A photo on Willhite's page is captioned as the lookout location with foundations.[^3] A 2017 hiker reported a box's worth of rusty nails spilled at the old lookout, so watch your step.[^5]

The site is reached on foot, up Nannie Ridge Trail 98 from the Walupt Lake area.[^3][^5] The climb covers about 3 miles and roughly 2,100 feet.[^3][^5] Willhite says the old lookout trail turns off to the left at the ridge crossing.[^3] The sources we read describe no closure of the trail. The register recorded the site on July 16, 2012, as US 1334, WA 234.[^1]
