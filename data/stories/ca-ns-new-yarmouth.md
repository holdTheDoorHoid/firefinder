New Yarmouth was a farming and forestry community in Nova Scotia, on a plateau above West Advocate.[^1] Its fire tower stood about 3.2 km (2 mi) north-west of West Advocate.[^2]

Minas Basin Pulp and Paper harvested blueberries and pulpwood on the land, and in the 1940s the company built a fire lookout tower at the site.[^1] A letter dated 8 February 1960 from the Royal Canadian Air Force calls it a forestry tower that the company operated, and gives its height as 21 m (70 ft).[^2]

The letter says a joint-purpose tower was under consideration for the site, with an air defence installation on the roof and a fire lookout cabin just below.[^2] Wikipedia says the Royal Canadian Air Force proposed it to fill a gap in the Pine Tree Line radar system, but the plan was never built.[^1]

The province's Department of Natural Resources later took over the tower. Lloyd E. Bennett of Advocate staffed it for many years, until he retired in 1999.[^1] It remained staffed seasonally until 2012.[^1]

Wikipedia says the tower was closed in 2013, in a province-wide move to replace fire towers with air patrols and citizen reporting, and was demolished in June 2015.[^1] A CBC News report, carried by Yahoo News Canada, says the province's towers were decommissioned in 2015 and later dismantled.[^3] The two accounts differ on the dates, but both leave the tower gone.[^1][^3]

What remains today is mostly open ground, some overgrown foundations and a few hunting cabins.[^1] The land is now part of Cape Chignecto Provincial Park, which acquired some of it in 1989.[^1] The park's largest campsite is at New Yarmouth, and its main backpacking trail runs along the western edge of the old settlement.[^1] Some of the old farmland is now planted with blueberries.[^1] Since the tower was demolished, a visitor finds park land and old foundations, not a lookout.[^1]
