Buster Butte rises to 3,832 feet in the Umpqua National Forest, in Lane County.[^1]

Forest Lookouts dates a tree lookout here to the 1910s, while the register dates a crow's nest and shelter set up on the site to 1929.[^1][^3] A September 1930 Roseburg News-Review item described a six-foot platform built in a fir tree, reached by a pole ladder 71 feet up. It also mentions a little shelter cabin.[^3]

In 1933 a 40-foot round fir pole tower with a 14x14 L-4 cab went up.[^1][^2][^3] A September 1933 Roseburg Chieftain item had said the CCC would build the 14x14 lookout before the snow flew.[^3]

The lookout was manned for 75 days in 1941, reporting through the Steamboat Bridge Ranger Station.[^3] In May 1947 two fliers whose small plane came down about 1,500 feet from the summit reached the unoccupied lookout, broke in and rested for two days before walking out.[^3] In June 1959 a News-Review item said a married couple would both be on the lookout for the summer.[^3]

The register says a 41-foot treated timber tower replaced the 1933 tower in 1955.[^1] The Cinnamon Butte register entry says that tower was first erected on Buster Butte in 1955.[^4] A July 1962 News-Review item gives a different timing: a new tower had been completed about six weeks earlier, replacing the 1933 building, which had become unsafe.[^3] The old building was pulled over with cables and a bulldozer winch, and its debris was piled and burned.[^3]

In 1976 the R-6 tower was dismantled and re-erected on Cinnamon Butte.[^3][^4][^5]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the Buster Butte summit today. The register has a 2007 photo of the site, but its text does not say what it shows.[^1]

The tower itself still stands on Cinnamon Butte in Douglas County, and the Cinnamon Butte register entry says it remains active.[^4] Forest Service directions turn off Oregon 138 onto Forest Road 4793, the road to the Cinnamon Butte lookout.[^5]
