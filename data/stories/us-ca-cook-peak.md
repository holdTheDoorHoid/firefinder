Cook Peak is a former fire lookout in Kern County, California, on the Sequoia National Forest.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists its type as a tower, with the status Gone.[^2]

In February 1936 a Bakersfield paper reported that a telephone line was being built up to Cook Peak, where a new lookout was planned.[^1] By June 1942 the lookout had a 25-foot steel tower and a house.[^1]

The 1942 fire spread quickly, burning 12,000 acres the first night, and the lookout was surrounded.[^1] Cliff Harrington and Robert Crain, who were manning it, stayed at their posts and were not hurt; the tower and house were not damaged.[^1]

Later staff included Claude Stewart, who said in 1947 he had been on the peak for four years, and Mrs. Helen King, the Forest Service lookout in 1952.[^1]

In July 1968 a grass and scrub oak fire near Lake Isabella pushed up the peak, and the lookout was abandoned.[^1] Press reports say a small cabin, a boat and some sheds burned in the same fire.[^1]

A photo set on Peakbagging, taken on June 1, 2005, shows concrete foundation blocks, which the page calls all that is left of the lookout.[^3] The same page mentions a benchmark placed in 1952.[^3] No source describes whether visitors can reach the site.[^1][^2][^3]
