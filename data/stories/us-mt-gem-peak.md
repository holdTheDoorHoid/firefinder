Gem Peak rises in the Kootenai National Forest, about eight miles southwest of Noxon, Montana.[^2] The U.S. Forest Service activated it as a lookout in 1921 with what the register calls a "rag outfit": a canvas shelter set up next to an open platform tower, meant for spotting smoke, not for living in.[^1][^2] For nearly two decades it was the one lookout on the old Noxon Ranger District without a permanent place to sleep.[^1][^2]

In 1939 the Forest Service replaced the platform with an L-4 cab set on a 20-foot pole tower, finally giving the lookout a place to sleep as well as watch from.[^1][^2] That structure lasted twenty-five years. In 1964 crews built the cab that stands today: an R-6 flattop cab with a catwalk, raised on a 30-foot treated timber tower.[^1][^2]

Gem Peak was staffed into the 1970s.[^1][^2] Three of its fire watchers are known by name: Vic Six in 1959, Jan Fisher in 1961 and 1962, and Gary Meyer in 1967.[^2] After the Forest Service stopped staffing it, the tower was nominated to the National Historic Lookout Register by Gary Weber of the Forest Fire Lookout Association and registered on August 20, 1996, as US 180 (Montana 19).[^1]

The lookout still stands on what is now the Cabinet Ranger District, one of three lookouts in the district offered through the Forest Service's overnight rental program.[^1] Reservations run through recreation.gov; check the current listing for road, trail, and seasonal conditions, since winter weather can close off access into the Cabinet Mountains country.
