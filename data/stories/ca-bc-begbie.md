Mount Begbie sits beside Highway 97, south of 100 Mile House in British Columbia.[^1][^2] The lookout stands at 1,276 m (4,186 ft), only a short walk above the car park.[^1][^2]

A lookout has stood on the site since 1923.[^1] The 1923 station was a working lookout that operated in the summer, watching for wildfires.[^2] The current tower was built in the 1970s. When it was in use, an observer staffed it through the fire season.[^1] A directory describes a two-storey wooden lookout, with a deck that wraps around it and views in every direction.[^2]

The lookout takes its name from Sir Matthew Begbie, a judge who arrived in British Columbia in 1858.[^1]

The blog says British Columbia now relies on aerial patrols and cameras, so lookouts like this one are no longer manned.[^1] In 2011 the lookout was formally designated a recreation site.[^1]

Today the site is a short public walk. The hike to the top takes no more than 5 to 10 minutes.[^2] The steep, rocky trail climbs the knob, or an easier service road runs to the base of the building.[^1] Expect a rough trail, with loose rock, exposed tree roots and boulders.[^2] The base has a picnic table, pit toilets and a large gravel parking lot.[^2] The top has one more pit toilet and a picnic table.[^2] The entrance sits just off the highway but is hidden and poorly signed, so look for the sign between 83 Mile House and 93 Mile House.[^2] A veranda wraps the building, and from it you can see across the whole Cariboo region.[^1]
