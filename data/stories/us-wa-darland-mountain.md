Darland Mountain rises in Yakima County, Washington, in the Wenatchee National Forest, about 34 miles west-southwest of the city of Yakima.[^1][^2]

A D-6 cupola cabin was built on the summit in 1925.[^1][^4] The register and firelookout.com both say a 42-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab replaced the cupola in 1935.[^1][^2] Willhite dates that tower to 1934, so the year is in dispute.[^4] The cupola was trucked to the Blue Slide lookout site in 1942.[^1][^4]

Newspaper items that Willhite quotes place people at the lookout in 1958 and 1960.[^4] A July 1958 Goldendale Sentinel item reports that Juanita Ward was headed to Darland Mountain for the summer.[^4] A September 1960 item describes the young woman then attending the lookout as a college student who wintered in Ellensburg.[^4]

The tower was removed in 1966. The register and Willhite both say so.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year instead.[^2] The Washington lookout list marks the site as gone.[^3]

Today, Willhite's photo captions label "tower footings" and an "unknown footing" at the site.[^4] Forest Lookouts lists the lookout as removed.[^5]

Getting there is a drive. Willhite says the summit is drive-up, reached by taking Ahtanum Road from Yakama into Ahtanum State Forest. The road is passable until the last two miles, and hiking books suggest walking the last part from Clover Flats campground, a 2-mile walk.[^4] Willhite also notes the summit sits where National Forest, state forest and Yakama Nation land meet, so check who owns the ground before you go.[^4]
