Grandview is a fire lookout tower in Rhea County, Tennessee, at an elevation of about 1,499 feet.[^1] We could not find its build date, design, or height in the sources we read, so most of its early history is still unknown.

The Tennessee Division of Forestry was responsible for building 208 fire lookout towers between about 1930 and 1970.[^2] A 2014 National Register study describes the surviving towers as examples of the division's earliest organized effort to build a coordinated approach to forest fire management.[^2] It also says most of the towers went up in the late 1940s and around 1960.[^2] The study does not say when Grandview went up, so we cannot place it in that window.

We found no record of who staffed the tower or of any fire it helped watch.

A table of Tennessee fire towers still standing on March 26, 2014 includes Grandview in Rhea County.[^2] The Tennessee Landforms index also lists it as standing, though its entry carries no date.[^1] Across the wider group, the study reports that many of the 208 towers have since come down or face demolition, because they fell out of use or their leases with private landowners ended.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists 76 registered lookouts in Tennessee, and Grandview is not among them.[^3] We found no public access information or rental listing for it. The study also notes that surviving towers are often in poor repair, and that visitors are cautioned about the risks of climbing them.[^2] Treat Grandview as off limits unless you have clear permission from whoever owns the site.
