Crittendon Peak sits on the Idaho-Montana border, on a ridge crest south of Dominion Peak, at 6,416 feet.[^5][^2] The peak is named for an early Forest Service volunteer from the area who did not return from World War I.[^1] An L-4 cab was built on the peak in the 1930s.[^1][^2]

Idaho sources date the destruction of the lookout to 1962.[^1][^2][^4] A Montana list instead marks the site as removed.[^3] The sources also disagree on the forest. The Idaho pages name the St. Joe National Forest, the firelookout.com entry names both the St. Joe and Lolo National Forests, and the Montana list names the Lolo National Forest.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

In a September 2025 post, the Idaho Fire Lookouts team reported that the site had nothing left, not even litter or scraps of metal.[^1] No other source describes remains there.[^1][^2][^3]

The Idaho listing calls the site a hike-in.[^1] A climbing guide says the summit is easy to reach from State Line Road.[^5] No source says whether the site is open to the public.[^1][^5]
