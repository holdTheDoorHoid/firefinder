Burton Peak Lookout sits on Burton Peak in Boundary County, in the Idaho Panhandle, about eight miles northwest of Bonners Ferry.[^1][^2] It stands on Kaniksu National Forest land, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1][^3]

A 1929 newspaper article reported plans to build a lookout on the peak that year.[^5] The log and frame cabin that now stands was built in 1933. It measures 14 by 14 feet and follows a design called the L-5, which the sources also describe as Nez Perce or Nez Pierce.[^1][^2][^5] According to the National Historic Lookout Register, it is the sole surviving L-5 log-frame lookout in North Idaho.[^1]

Firelookout.com lists the staff from 1932 to 1950, starting with E.G. Watson and ending with Lester Stephan.[^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts adds Larry Johnson and Mr. Malcom Smith, and notes that Walter Peel was released from lookout duty in 1935 to lead the Lion's Head Ridge crew.[^3] The 1935 names come from a Bonners Ferry Herald item from August of that year.[^3]

The last staff member named in these pages is from 1950. The register says the lookout was abandoned in 1967, and Idaho Fire Lookouts gives the same year.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com says it has been abandoned since the late 1960s.[^2]

The register says the structure is showing its age, and volunteers have made emergency repairs to it.[^1] The register recorded the lookout on April 6, 2009.[^1] When Idaho Fire Lookouts visited in 2021, the inside still had fold-up cots and two built-in desks, but was otherwise bare. The outhouse had long since been flattened.[^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts calls the site a hike-in and a popular destination hike.[^3] WillhiteWeb gives a hike of about 2.5 miles one way, gaining 1,500 feet, from the end of Forest Road 2411 at the trailhead, and says snow at that trailhead lasts until late spring.[^5] Inland NW Routes lists a 5.2-mile round trip rated moderate.[^4] None of these pages says whether going inside the cabin is allowed, so check with the Kaniksu National Forest before you go.[^3][^5]
