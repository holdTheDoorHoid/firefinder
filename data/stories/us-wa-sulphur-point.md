Sulphur Point is a former lookout site in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, in Whatcom County, Washington, at about 2,262 feet.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page and WillhiteWeb call the same site Sulphur Butte.[^3][^4] The Forest Lookouts page gives the same September 11, 1935 panorama date as the register, and WillhiteWeb gives the same 2,262-foot elevation, so the sources appear to describe one place.[^3][^4]

A 20-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab went up on the site in 1935.[^1][^2][^4] Wernstedt took the panoramas there on September 11, 1935.[^1][^3]

In 1942 the AWS put the lookout to work watching for enemy aircraft, according to WillhiteWeb.[^4] A Forest Lookouts page gives the activation as March 21 to August 24, 1942, under the Bellingham Filter Center.[^3]

The register says the lookout was abandoned in 1959 and is gone.[^1] FireLookout.com gives the same 1959 date.[^2] WillhiteWeb also says it was abandoned in 1959.[^4] A Forest Lookouts page says the site was destroyed but gives no date.[^3]

The 2014 WillhiteWeb visit recorded tower remnants, living quarters, a storage cellar and two firewood piles.[^4] Its photo captions include burned timbers from the tower.[^4] The register's photo captions show remains of living quarters and a storage shed.[^1]

Getting there takes effort. WillhiteWeb's route starts on the North Cascades Highway, runs up Baker Lake Road and FR 12, and parks at the blockade on an abandoned road.[^4] From there it crosses a creek and a lava field, then climbs a steep forested hill, about 1.8 miles round trip.[^4] The 2014 visit is the most recent account we found, so the site's condition today is unknown.[^4]
