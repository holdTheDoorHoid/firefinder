Silver Butte is in Douglas County, and newspapers of the 1920s and 1930s often called the same summit Silver Peak.[^1] The reports disagree about where it lies from Riddle: a 1923 item puts it nine miles west of town, while a 1944 item says southeast.[^1] A different former lookout with the same name stood in Grant County, and the former lookout register lists it separately.[^4]

In July 1923 a Forest Service man and a county fire-patrol man set up a fire finder and a telephone on the summit, and a lookout was to begin work at once while the building went up.[^1] Firelookout.com dates the first station to 1924 and describes a log cupola cabin.[^2]

By 1936 the log house had depreciated so far that the Douglas Forest Protective Association's annual report called it of little value, and the 1937 report asked for a new house on a 25-foot tower.[^1] In 1942 a 14-by-14 standard house went up on a 28-foot tower, mostly built by Civilian Conservation Corps crews.[^1] Firelookout.com instead gives a 20-foot L-4 for that year, so the height is disputed.[^2]

Named lookouts appear through the 1920s and 1930s, among them Neil Tripp in 1928, Quinlan Brown in 1930 and Thomas Lawson in 1933.[^1] In 1934 lookout William Brown reported a fire at 10:30 at night, and the report called it and a midnight fire from the year before incendiary.[^1]

In 1942 the Army asked for a year-round observation post here, and the site ran as an AWS post from October 14, 1942 until October 16, 1943.[^1] In 1944 the Douglas Forest Protective Association had Marge Stephens working as fore lookout.[^1] A new roof went on in 1960 for $428.53.[^1]

Firelookout.com says the station still had summer staff into the late 2000s.[^2] The tower came down on June 4, 2012, because of advanced decay.[^1] A 2013 blog post says the Oregon Department of Forestry took it down the previous June and put cameras in its place.[^3]

Firelookout.com says an automated detection camera took the lookout's place.[^2] No source describes what remains of the tower on the summit, or whether the public can reach the spot.[^1][^2][^3]
