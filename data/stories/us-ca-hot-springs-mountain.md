Hot Springs Mountain Lookout stands on the highest point in San Diego County, on the Los Coyotes Indian Reservation near Warner Springs.[^1][^2][^4] The U.S. Forest Service administers the lookout, but the peak is not public land, and the Los Coyotes Tribe controls who goes up.[^1][^4]

The Cleveland National Forest built a small ground house on the peak in 1912.[^1][^2] In 1928 a 14-by-14-foot cab on a 23-foot timber tower replaced it.[^1][^2] The present cab and tower date from 1942, a Region 5 C-3 design on a new 20-foot tower.[^1][^2]

Early use was hard going. A 1937 report described carpenters packed in to the lookout, which it called the only one on the Cleveland Forest that could not be reached by car.[^3] In 1939 the lookout, William Charlton, had to flee when flames threatened the tower, and he fled down the trail to the Warner Springs hotel.[^3]

The Register added the lookout on December 16, 2008.[^1] Its June 2017 visit report found the structure in an "Advanced state of decay," with pieces of the cab fallen to the ground on the west side and stairs it calls dangerous.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's San Diego chapter says the glass and furnishings are gone and the roof has collapsed.[^2] A lookout directory lists the status as "Collapsing."[^5] The tower still stands, but pieces of the cab have fallen to the ground, the roof has collapsed, and FFLA says its latest photos show the structure about to fall.[^1][^2]

No one staffs it. The 2017 report marks the lookout unstaffed and lists no volunteer openings.[^1]

The Register says the reservation has recently opened again to the public.[^1] The tribe lets visitors hike from its campgrounds to the top, and permits can be bought at its ranger station or prepaid online.[^4] The tribe also says the tower "is not the summit and climbing is prohibited."[^4] The 2017 report says a high-clearance vehicle is required on the 5-mile approach road, where a chain across the road is opened with a key from the visitors' booth.[^1] Given the decay and the tribe's rule, the safe way to see the tower is from the ground.
