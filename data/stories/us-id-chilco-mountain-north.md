A camp went up on the north summit of Chilco Mountain in 1931.[^1][^4] A 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab followed in 1939, according to most sources.[^1][^3][^4] The 1996 Spokesman-Review hike guide instead gives 1948 for a North Chilco cabin and tower, and says the tower was dismantled in the late 1950s.[^5]

A 1940 note from a USGS party says a lookout tower already stood on the peak, with the station benchmark under its steps.[^2] The firelookout.com page lists the site as ruins by 1959.[^1] A 1960 USGS party noted the legs of the tower and recorded that it had been destroyed.[^2] The climbing guide says the tower came down before 1959.[^4]

The Idaho Fire Lookouts site describes what it found on a recent visit: four concrete footings, a set of concrete steps, a stabilizer footing, what may be the ruins of a cabin, a stovepipe, and a good deal of wire and cable.[^2] The climbing guide reports sparse debris on the summit.[^4] Visitors reach it on foot by the Chilco Mountain National Recreation Trail, a class 1 route that climbs 1.7 miles to the bald summit.[^4] The site is in Coeur d'Alene National Forest.[^1]
