The Wilderness fire tower was built for the U.S. Forest Service one-eighth mile east of the junction of Missouri Highways 13 and 86, near Lampe in Stone County, Missouri.[^2] The property was purchased in 1936.[^2]

In November 1936 the Stone County News-Oracle reported that construction had begun on a 100-foot steel tower, to be known as the Wilderness Tower and the fourth of its kind on the Kings River Ranger District.[^1] A 1992 Historic American Buildings Survey report says it is not certain when the tower and other buildings were built. It repeats a former resident's account that Civilian Conservation Corps crews from the Shell Knob camp assembled the tower between 1937 and 1939.[^2]

The tower was observation-only. It was 99.9 feet tall, with a 7-by-7-foot enclosed cab, and the Aermotor Company manufactured it.[^2] The towerman's house was completed by 1940, and a former resident whose father was the first towerman remembered moving in that year.[^2] Missouri's annual reports note major repairs to the residence in fiscal 1956-57 and a reconditioned tower cab in fiscal 1964-65.[^1]

In the early 1960s the lookout cabin was removed and its basement filled in. It now stands on private land and is no longer recognizable as a lookout house.[^2] Another building from the dwelling area was moved from the site in the same period and is now a cow barn on private pasture north of the tower.[^2] The report says that at tower sites generally, dwellings, outbuildings and a number of towers were declared surplus, and removal from Forest lands began.[^2]

By 1992 the site was no longer used as a fire lookout. A microwave repeater station occupied part of it, and the report lists the owner as Mo-Ark Water Company, Inc., which planned a water well and storage tank there.[^2] In 1992 the steel tower itself appeared to be in good condition, though its lower stair treads were missing, its cab windows had no glass, and the cab interior had deteriorated substantially.[^2] The report judged the tower likely to be moved or torn down.[^2]

The 1992 report is the newest source I found. No source I found describes the site after that date, so it is not known whether the tower still stands. The owner in 1992 was a private company.[^2] The sources do not say whether visitors may enter the site.
