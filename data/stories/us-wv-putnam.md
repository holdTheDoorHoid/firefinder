The Liberty tower stood near the community of Liberty in Putnam County.[^3] The register calls the site the Putnam Tower Site, and other lists call it Putnam (Liberty) or simply Liberty.[^2][^3] The register records its entry on January 26, 2017.[^1]

A May 1974 Charleston Gazette item, quoted on a lookout history site, describes the tower as 80 feet (about 24 m) tall.[^3] It tells of Mrs. Beulah Miller, a 67-year-old observer who climbed those steps every day in fire season.[^3] By then she had worked the part-time role for eight years, seven days a week through the eight-week spring and fall seasons.[^3]

The register names the West Virginia Division of Forestry as administrator.[^1] The lookout history site names the West Virginia Department of Natural Resources instead.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^2] Statewide, the state wound down its own towers over time, and the final ones shut at the end of the 1990 fall fire season.[^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.
