Mount Abram, in Franklin County, Maine, got a steel fire tower in 1924.[^1] In April the state forestry department bought steel for a 12-foot tower on the mountain.[^1] By July a 15-foot steel lookout was going up, along with a seven-mile telephone line and a camp for the watchman.[^1] The page spells the name both Abram and Abraham.[^1]

The tower took a beating early. A December 1925 newspaper report says ice overloaded the tower, a bolt on one of its four cables failed, and the structure fell. The news reached officials through a watchman.[^1] A May 1935 item gives the tower's height as 20 feet.[^1]

In May 1949 the Portland Press Herald reported that Cecil Harris, of New Sharon, had found the tower destroyed while preparing for his seasonal fire watch. He found the site in ruins, from a fire thought to have been set off by lightning during a recent shower.[^1]

In 2011 the state was still recommending that the tower be removed, and the watchman's camp was taken down that summer.[^2]

The Association's 2015 chapter survey records one tower lost since its previous survey. Mount Abraham had blown over in high winds and is still lying on the summit.[^3]

A 2019 hiking account says the summit tower and its shelter had been toppled and had collapsed.[^4]

Today the mountain is part of the 6,214-acre Mt. Abraham Public Reserved Land.[^5] The most popular route is the 4.5-mile Fire Warden's Trail to the summit.[^5] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list marks the tower as gone.[^6] No source I read describes the summit after 2019, so what remains there now is unknown.
