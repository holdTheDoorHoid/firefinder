Gold Peak is in Mineral County, Montana, on the Lolo National Forest, 13 miles west of St. Regis.[^1][^2]

The firelookout.com record says the site was developed in 1914 with a 38-foot pole tower.[^1] In 1920 a 30-foot pole with an 8-by-8-foot cab and a log cabin replaced it.[^1] A 1921 photograph in the Montana History Portal shows upright members of the tower being hoisted, with the old tower serving as a "Jim pole."[^3] In August 1922 the Missoulian described a 60-foot tower with the cabin combined.[^2] That height does not match the 30-foot pole in the firelookout.com account, and the sources do not explain the gap.[^1][^2]

The lookouts were to go to the station on July 1 and remain until September 15.[^2] In 1922 Charley Hageland was stationed there for his second season. The paper noted that he was practically alone, and that a pack train brought his supplies every 10 days.[^2]

In 1924 the Missoulian reported that work was under way on two glass-ribbed observatories, one of them on Gold Peak, meant to replace older lookouts.[^2] The firelookout.com record instead dates a D-6 cupola cabin on the peak to 1928, so the two accounts disagree about when the cabin changed.[^1][^2] In October 1928 a 400-gallon water tank was installed at the top of the peak.[^2]

In July 1933 a bear broke down the cabin door and smashed the stove, table and bunk, according to an Independent (Helena) item quoted by the Forest Lookouts site.[^2] In October 1934 the Missoulian reported that an obsolete lookout on Gold Peak had been reconstructed.[^2] In August 1934, Robert Root, stationed at the lookout, was struck by a falling lodgepole pine near the station and taken to Missoula, where he received several stitches.[^2]

The firelookout.com record says the cabin was destroyed in 1955.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a ground lookout, with the status Gone.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the peak, or whether it can be visited.
