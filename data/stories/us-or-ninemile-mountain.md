Ninemile Mountain rises to roughly 3,950 feet about seven miles west of West Fork in Douglas County, Oregon.[^1] A Siskiyou Bulletin item, quoted on a local lookout history page, reported in September 1933 that a Rand district ranger, then at the Mt. Reuben CCC camp inspecting fire equipment, was choosing the lookout site on the best pinnacle of Ninemile Ridge.[^2]

A Roseburg News-Review item dated October 21, 1933, also quoted on that page, describes a 40-foot tower already completed on Nine Mile mountain and equipped against lightning.[^2] Other sources date the tower to 1934.[^2][^1] A 1939 forest history lists an R1 cab for that year, and the firelookout.com history gives a 40-foot pole tower in 1934, after a shake cabin in 1928.[^2][^1] The sources disagree by a year.[^2][^1] The newspaper is closest in time to the building, but we cannot settle the date.[^2]

A May 1940 plan considered the site a supplement to Mt. Reuben for fire detection, and noted that its country was more favorable for fuel moisture.[^2] In 1941 the lookout was staffed for 94 days.[^2] Its communications used a forest line that connected to Pacific Telephone and Telegraph, and from there reached the Rand Ranger Station.[^2]

A slash burn near the lookout got away in December 1933.[^2] By 3 P.M. the wind had risen to a warm, dry gale, and the fire cleared 200 to 300 feet on each side.[^2] The Siskiyou Bulletin account called the fire helpful, since it cleared a serious fire hazard from around the new tower and cabin.[^2]

During the Second World War the site was an Aircraft Warning Service post, station George 9-4.[^2] Its record lists activation on July 16, 1943, deactivation on October 14, and the Roseburg Filter Center.[^2] On October 14 the station was moved to inactive status, and the record recommended keeping it.[^2]

The accounts of its end do not match.[^1][^2] The firelookout.com history says the lookout was abandoned in the 1940s.[^1] The Oregon lookouts page marks it removed, without a year or method.[^2] No source we read gives a demolition date.

The Oregon lookout list on firelookout.org still marks the tower as gone.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the summit, such as footings or a foundation. None says whether the site is open to visitors.
