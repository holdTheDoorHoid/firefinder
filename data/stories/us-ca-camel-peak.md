Camel Peak, in Plumas County on the Plumas National Forest, got a Forest Service fire lookout in 1926.[^1] The register describes it as a California Region 5 Plan 4AR design built on an enclosed timber tower.[^1]

Newspaper items from 1929 to 1936 place Tom Gray at the station during the fire season.[^2] Gray had entered the service in 1918.[^2] When he resigned in March 1939, the Feather River Bulletin said his final post was Camel Peak, a station above the Middle Fork of the Feather River, and that he had held it through the summer of 1938.[^2]

The newspaper items do not agree on where the station stood. A 1929 item puts it 12 miles west of La Porte, and a 1932 item says it was near Cascade.[^2]

In July 1946 the lookout was Joe Miguel.[^2] An army pilot flying over the area spotted a fire less than two miles south of the station, circled to get the lookout's attention, and dropped a message on a streamer.[^2] A logging crew's bulldozers held the fire to about eight acres.[^2]

The register gives 1926 as the build year but lists its removal as unknown.[^1] A photograph credited on that page, dated 1963, shows the lookout in place.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association now lists it as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, is left on the summit today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^3]
