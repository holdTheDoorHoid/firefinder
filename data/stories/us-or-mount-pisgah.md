Mount Pisgah rises about eight miles southwest of Mitchell, in the Ochoco National Forest.[^2][^1] The lookout on top is a 20-foot timber tower with a flat-topped R-6 cab and a catwalk, built in 1964.[^1][^2] Counting this one, the Forest has built five towers on the summit.[^1]

The sources disagree on when the first structure went up. Firelookout.com and a 2011 blog both give 1918 for a 10-foot platform, while the register gives 1919.[^2][^3][^1] The sources agree on what came after. A 35-foot platform with a cabin went up in 1923, a cab was added in 1929, and a 20-foot L-4 tower followed in 1933.[^1][^2][^3] The 1964 tower went up right next to the earlier one, which was then taken down.[^3]

The old 1933 tower served for 31 years before the present lookout replaced it.[^1] Firelookout.com says the tower is staffed every summer, and the register says it is in active service.[^2][^1] The 2011 blog also lists it as staffed.[^3] Firelookout.com notes that a 2008 forest fire burned a patrol point one mile north of the lookout.[^2]

The only detailed access report I found is from 2011. That visitor drove up from the east and described Road 2630 as rough, taking 30 minutes to cover 6.2 miles.[^3] Road conditions may have changed since then. None of these pages says whether visitors may go up into the cab, so ask the Ochoco National Forest before you plan a trip.
