Riley Peak, about 14 miles west of Dallas in Polk County, Oregon, had a lookout station in 1931 that was the property of the Polk County Fire Patrol association.[^1][^2][^3]

In early June 1931, fire swept over about 1,000 acres of cut-over land on the Willamette Valley Lumber company's property on Riley's Peak.[^3] A Daily Capital Journal report said the fire destroyed about three million feet of logs and the lookout station.[^3] The Sunday Oregonian reported the lookout destroyed and about three million feet of logs damaged.[^3] A July item in The Forest Log said the cabin burned on June 6 and put the company's log losses at about two million feet.[^3] The reports agree that the lookout was lost, but they do not agree on how much timber burned.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com describe a later cab. In 1963 a 6-by-9-foot cab with trailer quarters was erected on the peak.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page gives $404.16 as the cost of a ground cabin built at this emergency lookout in 1963, paid from state restoration funds after Fanno Mountain Lookout was lost in the Columbus Day Storm the year before.[^3]

The sources disagree on when the 1963 cab left. The Forest Lookouts page says the lookout was removed in 1968.[^3] The register and firelookout.com say it was gone by 1973.[^1][^2]

The register lists the site as administered by the Oregon Department of Forestry and recorded it on November 15, 2010, on the nomination of Ray Kresek.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the peak today, or whether the site is open to the public.
