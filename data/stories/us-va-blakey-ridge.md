Blakey Ridge sits in Madison County, Virginia, at 2,140 feet (652 m).[^1][^2][^3] The Virginia Department of Forestry put up a fire tower there in the late 1930s.[^1][^2] It was an 80-foot Aermotor tower with a 7 by 7 cab.[^3]

The register says the tower operated for just 20 years.[^1] Later the Rappahannock Electric Cooperative owned it, and the tower served as a radio site.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists the site as registered on November 17, 2008.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register added it on June 5, 2018.[^2] The tower came down that year.[^1][^2] The register says it was removed because of liability concerns.[^1]

A rock-collecting guide says the tower came down in 2018 and that nothing is left to see at the summit beyond an FAA radio site.[^4] The two registers do not mention an FAA site, so that detail is unconfirmed.[^1][^2]

The guide describes reaching the ridge by a steep, rugged jeep trail of about nine miles from Ruth Road (SR 658) outside Madison, with roughly 740 feet of climbing.[^4] It calls the road a public right-of-way, but says the route crosses wildlife-management land and adjoining private hunting land, and that a WMA access permit may be required, depending on activity and season.[^4] With the tower gone, there is no lookout to climb.[^1][^2]
