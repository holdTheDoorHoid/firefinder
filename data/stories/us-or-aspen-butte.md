Aspen Butte is a former lookout site in Klamath County, Oregon, on the Crater National Forest, administered with the Klamath Forest Protective Association co-op.[^1][^3]

The register lists a rag camp on the site in 1910, while the Klamath County lookouts blog describes a compass lookout from about the same time.[^1][^4] In the 1920s a D-6 cupola cabin was built here.[^1][^2] The blog credits the Crater National Forest with building the D-6 cupola.[^4]

Early staffing is documented in a few items. The Oregon Lookouts page reprints a 1910 report by the secretary of the Klamath and Lake Counties Forest Fire Association. It describes a patrolman who, that afternoon, spotted new fires from his post on Aspen Mountain.[^3] The item may refer to a different Aspen summit, since a 1915 item on the page mentions Aspen Lake mountain.[^3] A 1916 report by Harold D. Foster treats the station as worth keeping up with help from the Klamath Patrol Association, and an August 1917 newspaper item names E.P. Miller as a forest-service lookout on Mt. Aspen.[^3] The blog says the Klamath Forest Protective Association later handled the staffing.[^4]

The sources disagree on when the cupola went. The register puts its removal sometime in the 1930s, while firelookout.com gives 1928.[^1][^2] The blog says the cupola may have been moved to Chase Mountain for living quarters in the early 1930s, though it says only that this is believed.[^4]

No source describes what remains on the site today.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The most recent physical record in these sources is a 1933 Coast and Geodetic Survey recovery. It found the station as described, with a bronze tablet set in bedrock at the highest point and its marks in good condition.[^3] The directions from that visit are more than 90 years old, and the sources do not say whether the site is open to visitors now.[^3]
