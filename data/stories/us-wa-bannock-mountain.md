Bannock Mountain sits in Snohomish County, Washington, in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, at 7,484 feet (2,281 meters).[^1] In the 1930s an L-4 cab was built on the site.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The sources give only the decade, so the exact year is not known.[^1][^3][^4]

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed the cab or any fire it watched.

The Register says the cab was eventually abandoned and no longer exists.[^1] Firelookout.com also lists it as abandoned,[^2] while Forest Lookouts says it was removed.[^3] WillhiteWeb found the cab only on a 1955 Forest Service map,[^4] and its author writes that nobody has shown him any remains of it.[^4]

We found no source that describes the site today. WillhiteWeb calls it one of the most remote lookout sites in Washington, says no trail runs from Canyon Lake, and describes the access roads as good gravel.[^4] It also says a search near or on the summit is still needed once the snow melts.[^4] None of the sources says whether the site is open to visitors, so check with the Forest Service before you go.
