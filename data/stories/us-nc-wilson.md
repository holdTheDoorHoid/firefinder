Wilson's fire tower went up in September 1934, when Civilian Conservation Corps workers erected a 99-foot 9-inch (30.4 m) steel tower there, along with a two-room tower house.[^1] The work cost $1,422.87, and the Emergency Conservation Works program paid for it.[^1] The listing cites the state's 1934–36 biennial report for those figures.[^1] It names the North Carolina Division of Forest Resources as the tower's agency.[^1]

Later the lease on the site ran out. A new site was acquired and the 100-foot (30.5 m) tower was moved there, a change the listing dates to 1962–64.[^1] The North Carolina tower list gives a Wilson County entry at 35.72527, -77.99583.[^2] No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
