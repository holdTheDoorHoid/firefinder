Packsaddle Mountain's lookout stood in Lane County, in the Willamette National Forest.[^1][^2] The first cabin's date is uncertain: two lookout-history sites give 1930 for an 8-by-8-foot cab.[^2][^3] An August 1932 Eugene Guard item, as quoted on an Oregon lookout history site, named Pack Saddle mountain as a place for new lookout houses that year.[^1] A September 1932 item said the materials would be packed 12 to 15 miles in by horse.[^1]

In July 1934 the Eugene Register-Guard reported that William G. Fowler was going to serve as lookout fireman on the mountain.[^1] A July 1939 Bend Bulletin item about a fire at Mud Lake said the blaze was at the lake's south shore, by a "Packsaddle mountain lookout station." The item does not say whether that is this lookout.[^1] In 1941 a Eugene Guard item said Felix Sparks had "just completed a lookout station" there.[^1]

In 1942 the 8-by-8 cab was replaced with an L-4 cab, and an Air Warning Service cabin was added.[^2][^3][^1] The sources disagree about the end. Firetower.org and firelookout.com say the lookout was gone by 1960.[^2][^3] The Oregon lookout history site says it was disposed of by burning in 1968.[^1]

The sources describe the lookout as gone, and none we read says what remains at the site today or whether it is open to visitors.[^3]
