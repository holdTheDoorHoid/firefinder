The Slash Ridge lookout was built in 1949 in Carbon County, Wyoming.[^1] Firelookout.com describes a 50-foot, all-steel, live-in tower that replaced the Barrett Ridge lookout.[^2] Forest Lookouts says the 1949 site also replaced Barrett Ridge.[^3] The Register places the site in the Medicine Bow-Routt National Forest, at 9,574 feet, or 2,918 meters.[^1] Firelookout.com gives 9,567 feet, so the two sources differ slightly.[^2]

Our sources do not name anyone who staffed the lookout, and none describes a notable fire it watched. The Register says it was retired in the 1970s, when the Forest Service chose aerial detection and downsized its staffed lookouts.[^1] The Register also notes that Blackhall, Kennaday and Medicine Bow Peaks overlooked the same area, and says Slash Ridge was declared surplus.[^1]

The lookout went to the Grand Encampment Museum, in Encampment, Wyoming, in 1986.[^1][^4] Forest Lookouts describes it as a military surplus tower when it was moved.[^3] The Register says it now serves as a popular tourist attraction.[^1]

Firelookout.org lists the original site as having no tower, and it lists the museum's tower as standing.[^5] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the original site, or whether the public can reach it.

A Live WYld Magazine article describes a guided tour of the museum, where the buildings are unlocked and visitors can go inside.[^4] The article does not say whether the lookout tower itself can be entered, so ask the museum before you go. Please do not climb the tower.
