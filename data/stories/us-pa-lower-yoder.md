Lower Yoder was one of the first towers the state Department of Forestry put up in 1921.[^1] A July newspaper report said the tower would go up on land owned by a Johnstown attorney, F.P. Martin, and that a telephone line would be strung to each tower so the towerman could alert fire wardens quickly.[^1] Forest Leaves named Lower Yoder among the first 18 towers to be put up that year.[^1]

By early August the 60-foot (18 m) tower was finished on the highest point in the Johnstown section. A newspaper said it commanded a view of more than 20 miles, and that it was the first of four planned for the Gallitzin district.[^1]

In October 1921 towerman John Vasil, Jr., reported the season's first call from the tower. The smoke he saw was not a real forest fire, but the newspaper said the response showed that such fires would be attacked within an hour of discovery.[^1]

In June 1939 the tower was raised from 60 feet to 86 feet (26.2 m).[^1]

A 2011 post on the Vintage Johnstown history blog said the tower was still there, off Decker Avenue in Lower Yoder Township, and that generations of children had enjoyed the view from it.[^2] The author said that even the thought of the climb made their legs shake.[^2]

We found no source from after 2011 that says whether the tower still stands, who owns the ground, or whether visitors are allowed. Find out who owns the site before you go, and do not climb a tower that is closed or stands on private ground.
