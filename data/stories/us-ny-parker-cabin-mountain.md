In May 1928 a wooden fire observation tower stood on Parker Cabin Mountain, about four miles northeast of Tuxedo, in Harriman State Park.[^1]

Newspapers that spring described it as one of four observation towers in the park's system for finding woods fires.[^1] The other three were new steel towers of about 80 feet.[^1]

Observers were on watch during dry, windy spells, and a May 1928 report credited them with prompt discovery of fires.[^1]

In August 1928 a large party hiked to the tower during a week when thirty scouts were at Camp Winaki, the Rockland County Boy Scout camp.[^1]

A November 1930 article still described the wooden tower as in place, and gave directions by motor and then on foot.[^1]

A statewide lookout list now marks the tower as gone, with no removal date or detail given.[^2]

No source describes what remains of the tower on the summit today. A 2016 hiker's account describes hiking up Parker Cabin Mountain by the Ramapo-Dunderberg trail to a second view, and it does not mention a tower.[^3]

Do not confuse this peak with Jackie Jones Mountain, which has its own fire tower in Harriman State Park.[^4] That tower was built in 1928 and restored in 2018.[^4] A November 2021 note says it was fenced off and closed, perhaps permanently, and the status may be out of date.[^4]
