The Missouri Conservation Commission acquired 40 acres for a towersite near Goodman, in Newton County, during 1946-1947.[^1] A 1947 newspaper called the site the tower at McElhany.[^1]

A new 60-foot wooden tower was reported erected on June 28, 1947.[^1] A July 1947 newspaper item, quoted on the same page, said the materials were still on the grounds and that the tower probably would not be finished much before fall.[^1] The sources do not settle when the tower was finished.[^1]

That July item also said towerman Mauch and his family had already moved into the McElhany farm at the tower site.[^1] In fiscal 1958-59 the wooden tower was replaced with a 120-foot steel tower, according to a Missouri Conservation Commission annual report quoted on the page.[^1]

The page says the tower was "Removed to private location."[^1] It gives no date and does not say where the structure now stands. A Joplin Globe feature, undated in the copy read, says the towers near Lanagan and Goodman still offer the public a view and that the Missouri Department of Conservation still looks after them.[^2] That does not match the page, and these sources do not settle which account is right.

Today the site is the Goodman Towersite, a 40-acre public area that is mostly wooded. Hunting is allowed, and it is open from 4:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. daily.[^3] A Goodman Fire Department substation sits on its northern boundary.[^3] The Department's page for the site does not mention a fire tower.[^3]
