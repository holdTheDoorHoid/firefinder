Quarles Peak sits next to the Montana state line, about 13 miles east-northeast of Avery in Shoshone County, Idaho, within the St. Joe National Forest.[^1] The name honors a Coeur d'Alene assessor who held a prospect on the peak.[^2]

The earliest building was a log cabin in the northeast saddle below the summit, built in 1919.[^1][^2][^3]

The Idaho Fire Lookouts page's notes, which credit Bert Russell's book, say the station was a patrol headquarters with a crew of fifteen trail workers and seven smoke chasers in 1919-1920.[^2] The same notes say it operated as a ranger station for a dozen years.[^2]

The cupola cabin on the summit went up in 1924.[^1][^2][^3] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page names "Blackie" Kimball among the earliest lookouts at the site, and identifies Charley Scribner as the ranger in 1925.[^2]

The cupola cabin was destroyed in 1957.[^1][^3]

The Idaho Fire Lookouts page includes historic photos and a present-day comparison, but its text does not describe the present-day view.[^2] We found no written description of what remains on the summit now.

The same page lists the site as drive-up.[^2]
