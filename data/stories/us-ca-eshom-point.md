Eshom Point is a former lookout site in Tulare County, California, at 5,084 feet, or 1,550 meters.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives its build date as circa 1935 and credits the Tulare County Fire Department with the original construction.[^1]

The register describes it, tentatively, as a crude wooden platform under a canvas roof, perched on a rock outcrop with room for little more than a fire-finder and one observer.[^1] A tent went with it for the observer.[^1] By the register's account, the lookout was in use from the mid-1930s until at least 1959.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page quotes a November 25, 1959 story from The Oregonian about Mrs. Ira W. Wilson, who worked with her husband as a lookout at Eshom Point.[^2] On every trip down the trail from the tent, she ran into a bear.[^2] Then one day she discovered a bear had taken up residence in the tent, and that ended the job.[^2] The couple quit and returned to their home in Fresno.[^2]

The register names the Cal Fire Tulare Unit as administrator.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page places the site in Sequoia National Forest instead.[^2] We could not settle which description is current, so both are given here.[^1][^2]

The register does not record when the site was removed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a ground site that is gone.[^3] According to the register, a rock foundation lies a short distance south of the current communications site and may still be seen.[^1] No source we read says whether the public can visit.[^1] Because the foundation sits near the communications site, check with Cal Fire's Tulare Unit before you go.[^1]
