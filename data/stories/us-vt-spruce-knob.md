Spruce Knob is a mountain near Middletown Springs in Rutland County, Vermont.[^1][^2] The state chose the lookout site in 1932, and local labor built the timber tower and a watchman's camp that year.[^1] In September 1933 a crew from a state forest camp was assigned to build a telephone line and trail up from the highway and to add a glass-enclosed cab to the tower.[^1]

The tower was in operation by June 1934.[^1] Its watchman, James McIntyre, reported fires in Castleton, Poultney, Wells and Middleton Springs.[^1] All were put out before they caused serious damage.[^1] A 1940-42 state report records new girders and braces for the tower.[^1] Harold Hathaway of North Rupert was stationed at the tower in April 1952.[^1]

The state's 1953 to 1954 biennial report says the wooden tower was taken down.[^1] It had decayed beyond repair, the lease on the site ended, and the telephone line was salvaged.[^1] The same report says a primary air patrol had been set up to cover the area.[^1]

The sources do not agree on every detail. A 1942 survey description calls the structure a steel tower with a glass cage, while a 1933 newspaper and the 1953-54 state report describe a wooden one.[^1] A June 1934 newspaper item places the tower in Poultney, while the 1953-54 state report says Middleton.[^1]

We found no source describing what, if anything, remains on the summit. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Vermont list marks the tower as gone.[^2]
