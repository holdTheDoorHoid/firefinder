In late 1933 the state Department of Forests and Waters chose a site in Union Township, Berks County, for a new fire observation tower.[^1] The land was deeded by Mrs. Louise Brooks of Birdsboro, who gave about ten acres for a consideration of $1, on the understanding that it would return to her or her heirs once the tower was no longer used for fire watch.[^1] A 1934 item names the donor as Mrs. Edward Brooke, so the sources do not agree on her name.[^1]

Federal funds paid for the steel tower, and the state put it into service on May 1, 1935.[^1] The 1935 report called it eighty feet high and said a picnic park would be built around it.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register credits the Civilian Conservation Corps and the French Creek recreation project with building the tower that year.[^2]

Work on the site continued for several years. In August 1936 the Works Progress Administration started work on finishing the road to the tower, which a 1936 item calls the Williams Hill fire tower. In September 1939 about 50 men were clearing brush and constructing a road about a mile and a half long there.[^1] A 1939 item named George H. Wolf as the lookout and described a 90-foot steel tower in the Geigertown area.[^1] That height does not match the 80 feet given elsewhere, so the item may describe a different structure.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association traces its start to a gathering in early 1990 at the District 17 garage in French Creek State Park, after which the group toured the nearby Hopewell tower and its ground cabin.[^3] The 2012 register entry includes photos of the cab interior, which suggests the tower was standing then.[^2]

The register lists the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry as administrator, with the Pennsylvania Forest Fire Wardens Association District 17 as a cooperator.[^2] It places the tower in William Penn Forest, while the 1930s reports put it near Hopewell Furnace in Union Township.[^2][^1] I did not find an official statement on whether visitors can climb the tower, so check with the Bureau of Forestry or French Creek State Park before you plan a visit.
