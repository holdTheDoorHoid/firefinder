Cedarville Lookout sits on the highest point of a hill in Marinette County, Wisconsin, at about 995 feet of elevation.[^1][^2] It was erected in November 1933.[^1][^2] The register describes an 80-foot International Stacy tower with an internal corner-leg ladder.[^1] A U.S. Geological Survey station description from 1934 gives the height as 79.5 feet and describes a square, steel-framed tower with a closed cab.[^2]

The same survey note calls it the Cedarville fire lookout of the Wisconsin Department of Conservation.[^2] I found no staffing records for the tower in these sources, so I cannot say how many seasons it was manned.

Fire towers were once the main way Wisconsin spotted forest fires, but the DNR says the public now reports more than 90 percent of the state's forest fires.[^3] In December 2015 the DNR announced that all 72 of its fire towers would close in the spring, because the towers were judged structurally unsafe for staff and too expensive to replace.[^4] Marinette County became responsible for two unmanned towers in the county, located near Cedarville and Middle Inlet.[^2] In February 2016 the county's forestry and parks committee approved a plan to remove the towers, but the timing was uncertain.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wisconsin list shows the Middle Inlet tower as gone, but still lists Cedarville as standing.[^5] Those listings do not say whether Cedarville has come down.[^5]

The register recorded the tower on June 9, 2017.[^1] The sources I found describe no public access, rental or visitor program for it.[^1][^2] Given that finding, I would view this one from the ground.[^4]
