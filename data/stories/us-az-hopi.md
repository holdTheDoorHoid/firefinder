Hopi Point, about two miles west of Grand Canyon Village on the South Rim, has had fire lookouts since as early as 1909.[^2][^5] In 1909 the Forest Service built a tree-top crow's nest there to scout for forest fires.[^1][^2] The Grand Canyon history site says the wooden lookout may have been Arizona's first fire tower.[^2] In 1927 the National Park Service replaced it with a 40-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10-foot cab.[^1][^2]

Lookouts were stationed there in the early years. A September 1915 report counted the visitors who registered at the tower that season, and a July 1921 item said a fire lookout had accepted a position at Hopi station.[^3]

The 1953 work was a remodel, not a first build. The cab grew to 12 by 12 feet, and the tower was lowered to 24 feet.[^1][^2]

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on April 25, 2009.[^1] A 2009 Park Service notice said that the four lookout towers on the register inside Grand Canyon National Park were all inactive.[^4] The register says it is no longer used for fire detection.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Hopi tower as standing.[^6]

Today the point is a rim viewpoint on the walking trail from Grand Canyon Village.[^2] The sources I read do not say whether the old tower can be entered or climbed.[^1][^5] Stay off the tower unless park staff invite you up, and check with the park before you go.
