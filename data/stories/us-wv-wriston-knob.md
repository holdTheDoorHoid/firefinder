Wriston Knob, in the western part of Raleigh County, West Virginia, was once called Bacon Knob.[^1][^2] In September 1950 a local paper reported that Raleigh County might get a new fire tower there, and that a five-man crew was already working on it.[^2] The next year the tower and an observer cabin were finished, and the paper said the tower would be manned from October 15, when the fall fire season began.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the tower was built in 1951 and names the site for forester Emory "Pop" Wriston.[^1] A 1951 article says the site was named for County Forest Protector E.N. Wriston, of Scarbro.[^2] Neither source says outright that the two are the same man.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on whether the tower replaced an older one. The register says it did.[^1] A 1950 article, though, lists the county's towers as Tams Mountain, Ivy Knob and Round Knob, and does not mention one on Wriston Knob.[^2]

The tower was built under the supervision of F.O. Frazier, the commission's construction superintendent.[^2] In July 1959 the Forestry Division of the Conservation Commission announced that the tower would get a complete overhaul.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed about 1972.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page marks it removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's West Virginia list marks it gone.[^2][^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can get there.
