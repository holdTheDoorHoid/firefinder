Red Top Lookout has been rebuilt at least twice.[^1][^2] A September 1918 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout history site, reports that two standard lookout houses had gone to the Forest Service, and one was bound for "Red Mountain" in the Wenatchee forest.[^3] Willhite Web says one of those houses ended up at Red Top, but the date of the cupola cabin is unclear and may be as late as 1924.[^4]

TrailChick dates the first cabin on the site to 1924 and says a D-6 cupola replaced it in 1928.[^2] The NHLR also dates the D-6 cupola to 1928.[^1] The present tower, a 10-foot timber structure with an L-4 cab, was built in 1952.[^1][^4][^5]

The cab was abandoned in the 1970s and had been battered by decades of weather.[^4][^1] The Cle Elum Ranger District and the Friends of Red Top Mountain spent about two years restoring it.[^1] The NHLR lists its registration date as August 6, 1997, and a summit party on August 26 celebrated the lookout's place on the register.[^1][^2]

Lookouts worked the site in the 1920s. A letter written for the 1924 season and a 1926 item about a lookout named Rosnold both appear on the Washington Lookouts site.[^3] TrailChick reports that in November 1929 lightning struck the summit several times and hit the lookout at least twice.[^2]

Willhite says volunteers staff the lookout during the summer.[^4] A TrailChick trip report from a 2017 visit is inconsistent on the point: it says the lookout used to be staffed in summer, and in the same post says it is now staffed as an interpretive site in summers.[^2]

From Interstate 90 exit 85, drive north on Highway 97 for 18 miles, then take Forest Roads 9738 and 9702 to the trailhead.[^4] The round trip is about 1.5 miles and gains roughly 350 feet. The trail is a short, steep climb through forest to an exposed, rocky ridge.[^5][^2] Visitors can see the inside when it is open, so check before you go.[^5] The U.S. Forest Service administers the site, with the Cle Elum Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1]
