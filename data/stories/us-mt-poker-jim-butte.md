Poker Jim Butte rises in the Custer Gallatin National Forest, about 18 miles south-southwest of Ashland, Montana.[^2][^3] The first lookout on the summit was a 20-foot pole tower with an undersized cab.[^2] The sources do not agree on when it went up.[^1][^2][^3][^5] The Forest Service's National Register nomination says a lookout was present by the spring of 1936, though one passage gives 1935.[^3] A September 1937 newspaper said the tower had been built the previous spring, while the National Historic Lookout Register and FirelookOut both give 1937.[^5][^1][^2]

The present cab replaced it.[^1] The L-4 cab, built in 1955, sits on a 30-foot timber tower.[^1][^4] It is a pre-cut 14-by-14-foot frame under a hip roof, and the nomination says this kind of structure was once common throughout the Forest Service's Northern Region.[^3]

In the summer of 1937 a lookout and his wife were stationed on the peak, and their register recorded 186 tourists from 19 states.[^5] In July 1966 blazes burned in the Ashland and Fort Howes districts, and the Forest Service's Poker Jim tower was evacuated.[^5] In 2008 crews replaced the stairs, rails, catwalk deck and floor joists to fix safety problems caused by deteriorating materials.[^3]

The nomination describes the cab as intact and still in working order as an L-4 house.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register registered it on November 27, 2016,[^1] and the National Register of Historic Places listed it on September 1, 2022.[^4]

The nomination says the Forest Service still staffs the lookout through summer and fall.[^3] FirelookOut describes it as used during emergencies, so the current pattern of use is not settled.[^2] A road runs to the top of the butte, and the lookout sits beside the Poker Jim Picnic Ground.[^5][^3] None of the sources says whether the cab is open to visitors, so check with the Custer Gallatin National Forest before going up.
