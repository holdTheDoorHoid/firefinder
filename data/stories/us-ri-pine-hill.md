Pine Hill Lookout Tower stands in Exeter, in Washington County, Rhode Island.[^1][^2] The first tower on the site was a wooden lookout, built in 1917.[^1] A 1918 state forestry report, quoted on a lookout history site, described it as a 50-foot wood tower with an observer who could see most of the woodland of Washington County.[^2] In 1946 the wooden tower was replaced by the current 65-foot steel tower.[^1]

The tower had observers in its early years.[^2] An August 1939 Kingston Daily Freeman item described Mrs. Hope Andrews, the sixth woman to be fire warden there, on duty from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.[^2] The same item said a woman had always been chosen to man the Exeter fire tower.[^2] The register now lists the tower as unstaffed.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list of Rhode Island towers marks it as standing.[^4] The National Historic Lookout Register registered it on September 3, 2014.[^1] A 2016 visit report there judged the tower to be in reasonable shape, given how little upkeep it has had, but noted that the lower stairs had been removed and that trees and vines had grown up around its legs.[^1]

In 2022 a small group of amateur radio operators and current or former emergency workers started a restoration drive aimed at many of the state's lookout towers.[^3] That year the group reconnected power to Pine Hill in Exeter.[^3] The register names the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management as the tower's administrator.[^1]

The sources do not say whether the public can climb the tower today. The 2016 report found the lower stairs gone, so check with the state before you go.[^1]
