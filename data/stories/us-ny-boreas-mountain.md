Boreas Mountain is a 3,780-foot peak in the Adirondacks, west-northwest of North Hudson in Essex County.[^1] In May 1911 the Conservation Commission put up a wooden fire lookout on the summit.[^1] In 1919 it replaced that with a 47-foot steel tower.[^1]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey station description from 1943 puts the steel tower at about 60 feet, with a glass-enclosed cab on top.[^2] The two sources disagree on its height.[^1][^2]

In September 1919, while the steel tower was being hauled up the mountain in sections, a newspaper reported that Joseph Hanmer of Blue Ridge was the observer stationed there.[^2] A January 1939 newspaper item names Ernest Hamner, a fire tower observer, who spotted fires from the tower in the fall and then spent the winter hunting and trapping.[^2] One of his traps held a 48-pound panther.[^2]

Aerial detection had grown by then, so the tower was shut at the end of the 1971 fire lookout season and taken down later.[^1] Jeff Mans, a chapter member of the Adirondack Mountain Club, told North Country Public Radio that the state removed the tower in the 1970s.[^3] The observer's cabin is also gone, according to a trail write-up.[^4]

Peakery's route description says hikers can still walk to the summit.[^4] Peakery also notes that Elk Lake gave the public an easement for hiking to Boreas Mountain in 2012.[^4] The route starts at The Branch parking area on Blue Ridge Road, follows a logging road along the Branch River to the former cabin site, and then continues on a trail to the summit.[^4][^3] No source we read says whether any footings or other remains of the tower survive.[^1][^2][^4]
