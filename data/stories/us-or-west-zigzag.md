West Zigzag is a lookout site in Clackamas County, Oregon, within the Mount Hood National Forest.[^1] Firelookout.com places it about 10 miles west of Mount Hood.[^2] Firetower.org gives its elevation as 4,468 feet, or 1,362 meters.[^1]

Firetower.org dates a firefinder and camp on the site to 1929, with an L-4 cab taking their place in the 1930s.[^1][^2] A Mount Hood history page says the two Zigzag lookouts were built sometime in the 1930s, and that West Zigzag was an L-4 ground cabin on a ridge with a view to the south.[^3]

Firetower.org names brothers Pat Lynch and Dennis Lynch as lookouts there, without dates.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page credits Frank Deuell, a Trails Club of Oregon member, with staffing the station for 17.5 weeks in 1943, 17 in 1944 and 11 in 1945.[^4] The page calls it an AWS station and does not spell out the abbreviation.[^4]

Firetower.org says the lookout was removed in 1966, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page labels the site "Removed" without a date.[^4] The firelookout.org list classes it as a ground site and marks it gone.[^5] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register recorded the site on September 21, 2010, on the nomination of Ray Kresek.[^1]

A hiking guide describes the site as a destination on the west end of the Zigzag Mountain ridge, with great views over the Zigzag River valley, though it notes there is no view of Mount Hood from there.[^6] A Mount Hood history page says the footings are still there, in a line between its West Zigzag and East Zigzag passages that does not name either site.[^3]

No source we read describes the site's condition in detail. We could not confirm vehicle access, since the guide describes a hike, so check with the Mount Hood National Forest before you go.
