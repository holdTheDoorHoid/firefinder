In September 1934 work had started on a telephone line at Board Camp, and the lookout was hoped to be ready for occupancy within two weeks.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page places the mountain in the Six Rivers National Forest.[^1]

In March 1953 the Eureka Humboldt Times wrote about John S. Williamson, known as "Board Camp Johnny".[^1] He had spent ten seasons at the post, 5,200 feet up and five miles beyond the end of the road.[^1] He was a volunteer lookout, and he had also fitted the station with hot and cold water, a gas stove, a fridge and lights.[^1] The same report said the Six Rivers National Forest no longer treated the mountain as a key lookout point, but his observations were still needed when fire weather turned critical.[^1]

The FFLA list marks the Board Camp Mountain tower as gone.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.
