The Johnston Hill fire tower is a Bremen landmark, on what a commission letter calls the highest point on the Pemaquid Peninsula.[^1] A 2011 newspaper story quotes a commission member saying the Maine Forest Service built the tower in 1948.[^1] The town's conservation commission says the Legislature provided the money for the steel, surrounding towns gave donations, and the finished tower is roughly 40 feet tall, about 350 feet above sea level.[^2]

The Forest Service staffed the tower until 1991, when the agency moved lookout duty into the air.[^1] Maine kept its staffed network going longer than many states, closing it after the budget troubles of 1991.[^3]

In 2011 the Maine Forest Service planned to begin demolition on or after September 15.[^1] The agency said the tower had lost its worth to the state and that it could not afford repairs.[^1] The Bremen commission wanted the tower left on its current site and proposed a "Friends of Johnston Hill" group to look after the tower and trail.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2012 report listed the tower for removal in 2013, at the landowner's request.[^4] Its 2013 report still recommended removal, this time for 2014.[^5] The association's Maine list still shows the tower as standing.[^6] I found no report that it came down, so its present condition is unconfirmed.

Two private owners held the land in 2011, according to the newspaper.[^1] I found no newer ownership information. The commission's letter calls the hill a popular destination for day recreationists.[^1] I found no current word on public access, so ask the owners before you go, and do not climb the tower without their permission.
