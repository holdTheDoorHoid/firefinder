Warner Hill rises to about 605 feet in eastern Derry, New Hampshire.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register gives the elevation as 388 feet (118 m),[^3] while a Derry historic survey gives 604 feet.[^5] The 1938 hurricane caused heavy damage to forests across the state, and many thousands of trees around Derry had been toppled.[^2][^1] The state built a steel tower on the crest with federal emergency money and Civilian Conservation Corps labor.[^3][^2][^1]

Sources differ on the year. The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower was commissioned in 1939,[^3] while the Forest Lookouts site says an International Derrick tower was erected in 1938 and 1939.[^4] A Derry historic survey dates it to about 1935,[^5] which the other sources do not match.

Arthur Picard was hired on April 22, 1939 to watch from the hilltop until the tower was ready, and the tower opened in early July.[^1] Historic Structures gives its original height as 41 feet,[^2] while the Derry News column recalls it as about 55 feet, with a wooden cabin on top.[^1]

During the Second World War the tower served as an aircraft warning station, given how close it was to the coast.[^3][^2] Local volunteers, most often young married couples, kept watch there during the war.[^1] The Army sent five soldiers in March 1944 to take over the watch,[^1] then abandoned the station that year, leaving the site to the Mears family.[^2] The state then bought the two acres from the Mears family for $400 and removed the extra tiers.[^2] After Picard retired, Roscoe Smith took over in the 1950s, and Walter Eaton took the post around 1985.[^1]

Later changes followed. A new cab went up in 1960, and in 1979 a sixth tier was added and the cab raised.[^2] A February 2015 call for bids covered a replacement cab, steel reinforcement and painting the tower,[^4] and the current cab dates from a 2015 restoration.[^2] A May 2017 photo on the register shows the lower cab walls had been removed, and the register says the tower was being remodeled for safety.[^3]

Today the tower is state-owned and still in service.[^2][^3] The register lists it as unstaffed,[^3] but Derry News says the state has closed the tower except on the worst fire days, and that visitors can still climb to the top landing.[^1] NH Family Hikes says it is seldom visited and not well advertised.[^6] The sources do not agree on access, so check with the Division of Forests & Lands before going, and stay off the tower if it is closed.[^2]
