Midway Lookout is set on the Cascade crest, between Mount Adams and the Goat Rocks.[^3] It is about 11 miles north of Mount Adams, in Yakima County and the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^2][^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^1]

The site began with a crow's nest, a platform set high in some trees.[^3] In 1934 a 16-by-16-foot L-4 cab went up, and its lower storage area had one corner set on a 20-foot boulder.[^3] That same year a garage was added, and panoramic photos were taken from the roof.[^3] Firetower.org describes the tower as built in the 1930s.[^1]

The sources disagree on how the lookout ended.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Firetower.org says it was removed in 1952, and Forest Lookouts lists it as removed.[^1][^4] FireLookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^3]

Willhite says the site once had road access, but it is now a trail from the Midway PCT trailhead, with a few overgrown spots.[^3] He lists the hike at 1.8 miles with about 800 feet of gain.[^3] Photo captions on his page show two lower footings and an upper footing on the summit rock.[^3] A 1935 caption places the Midway Guard Station below the lookout, and another says an old garage at Midway Creek Guard Station was still standing in 2018.[^3] No source says whether the summit is open to visitors.
