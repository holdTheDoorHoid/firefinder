The National Historic Lookout Register describes the Brule Lookout as a standard Wisconsin design, 80 feet tall, with a protected ladder on its outside, and dates its construction to 1932.[^1] The tower sits at about 1,362 feet above sea level, and the register lists the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources as its administrator.[^1]

A second listing, from Central US Lookouts, places the site inside the Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest.[^2] The two sources describe the setting differently, and the records do not settle who holds the land.[^1][^2]

The tower was staffed at least as late as 1950.[^3] A Forest History Society photo caption identifies Raymond Thompson as a fire guard at Brule Tower that year, photographed locating a blaze with a firefinder.[^3] Forest Service lookouts widely relied on the Osborne Firefinder through the 20th century.[^3] The instrument let them fix a fire's position from the direction of its smoke.[^3]

In December 2015 the Wisconsin DNR announced plans to stop using its lookout towers.[^4] An assessment earlier that year found that the 72 towers, still in use to varying degrees, cost more to keep up than they were worth for fire detection.[^4] The sources do not say whether Brule was among the towers sold, given away or removed, or whether anyone still staffs it.[^4][^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association still lists the tower as standing.[^5] The register added it on August 16, 2017.[^1] The sources give no public visiting hours, climbing days or rental details, so anyone interested should ask the land manager before planning a trip.[^1]
