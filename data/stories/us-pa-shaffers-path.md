The state register dates the Shaffers Path tower to 1921 and describes it as a steel Aermotor tower [^1]. The land was part of the Tiadaghton State Forest [^2]. Newspaper items quoted on the Forest Lookouts page place the tower on three different mountains: White Deer Mountain in 1924, Bald Eagle Mountain in 1939 and 1940, and Elmsport Mountain in 1944 [^2].

The towermen named in those items were William Yaney in 1924, William Kurtz in 1939, and William Loriner, whom an October 1944 item said manned the tower all year [^2].

In April 1939 the district began installing a short-wave radio system, with the key station at this tower [^2]. That November a pilot who spotted a forest fire at Sylvan Dell dropped a note at the tower, and the towerman used the new radio to pass the warning to headquarters [^2]. CCC men from Elmsport put the fire out after it had burned over only three acres [^2].

In December 1939 a lease with the state Department of Forests and Waters for use of the tower's facilities was signed, and it allowed a beacon light for the municipal airport to be built at the tower [^2]. By July 1940 the city planned to build its own beacon tower on the mountain, near the fire tower [^2].

In 1944 Army security and prisoner-of-war officials asked the state department to keep people away from the site, which overlooked an ordnance reservation, and the district said supervised hikes could still get permits [^2].

The register and the Forest Lookouts page both give 1975 as the year the tower was removed [^1][^2].

No source I found describes what remains at the site, and I could not find whether the public can reach it. A map database still lists a Shaffers Path Lookout Tower in Clinton Township, but that entry does not describe the site [^3].
