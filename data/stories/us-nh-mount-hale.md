Mount Hale, a 4,054-foot peak near Bethlehem in the White Mountain National Forest, once had a fire tower on its summit.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site in Coos County, but NH48 places the peak in Grafton County, so the sources disagree on that point.[^3][^1]

The White Mountain History article says a steel tower on Mount Hale took the place of the Rosebrook lookout in 1929.[^4] The same article says a tractor hauled the tower up the Hale Brook route, a trail that is now abandoned.[^4] NH48 gives the build years as 1928 to 1929, credits the Forest Service, and describes a wooden tower with a warden's cabin.[^1] The two accounts disagree on the material.[^1][^4]

NH48 says the lookout was staffed during the 1930s and 1940s, which it calls big fire years.[^1] The closing date is also disputed. NH48 dates the end of service to 1948.[^1] A 2019 hike report and the Weirs Times, which cites the Forest Fire Lookout Association's website, give 1950.[^6][^5]

The tower was torn down in 1972.[^1][^5][^6]

Today the summit is wooded, with a large rock pile on top left over from the tower.[^1] The Weirs Times says the footings, a concrete step and a few steel pins are the only parts of the tower still there.[^5] NH48 says the rock pile is the base the tower stood on, and that trees now block the sweeping views from the top.[^1] A 2019 hike report says the Fire Warden's trail largely follows the old service route to the lookout.[^6] Hikers reach the summit on foot, and the Weirs Times calls Hale a popular 4,000-footer to hike.[^5]
