Tripoint Lookout is a 60-foot steel tower in Carter County, Montana, and the Register calls it the easternmost lookout in the state.[^1] Firelookout.com places it about 24 miles southeast of Ekalaka.[^2]

The Register and Talking Trail both give 1936 as the build year.[^1][^3] Talking Trail credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building the tower.[^3] A Flickr photo caption dates the lookout's construction to 1935, so the sources are not fully in agreement.[^4]

The Register says the tower at one time had living quarters in a log cabin.[^1] Talking Trail describes a primitive log cabin that served as the lookout's home base.[^3]

The US Forest Service administers the tower.[^1] The forest name differs between sources. The Register places it in the Custer-Gallatin National Forest, while firelookout.com and the Montana Forest Lookouts page use the Custer National Forest.[^1][^2][^5]

The Register says the tower has not been used in several decades.[^1] Talking Trail says it has been out of use for decades.[^3]

The Register lists April 12, 2022 as its registration date, under US 1528, MT 91.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^6]

The Register gives the elevation as 4,125 feet, while firelookout.com gives 4,220 feet.[^1][^2]

The sources read do not say who staffed the tower, which fires it watched, or whether the public can climb it. Check with the Forest Service before planning a visit.
