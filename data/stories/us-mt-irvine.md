Irvine Lookout was a steel tower on the Flathead Indian Reservation, about 15 miles northwest of Polson in Lake County, Montana.[^1][^3] A survey station description written by the U.S. Geological Survey in 1960 places the tower about seven miles southwest of Elmo, on a high east-west ridge.[^2]

In early May 1939, a newspaper item reported that the Indian C.C.C. department had started erecting two 50-foot steel-tower lookouts, one of them on the Irvine Flats section.[^2] Roads to the sites had been built the year before, and telephone lines were to follow once the stations were finished.[^2] Firelookout.com instead dates the lookout to 1935 and describes an 80-foot steel tower with a frame cabin, so the two sources disagree on both the year and the height.[^1][^2]

The cab was an eight-foot-square room sitting atop the steel tower.[^3] When the tower was in use, its operator had to climb down whenever a thunderstorm rolled in.[^3] A 1979 Flathead Courier item, quoted on the Montana lookouts site, found Kathy Felsman of Arlee at the Irvine Lookout, who had spent seven years in the towers.[^2]

Firelookout.com marks the tower as gone in 1993, and the Montana lookouts page lists it as removed.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list, which calls it Irvine Lake, also records it as a gone tower.[^4] The tribes' lookout page says it is no longer in use.[^3]

None of the pages read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. Because the tower stood on reservation land, anyone interested should ask the Confederated Salish and Kootenai Tribes before going there.[^1][^3]
