West Horse Lookout stood on a ridge about a quarter mile south of a summit in Lemhi/Idaho County, Idaho, roughly 45 miles northwest of Salmon.[^1][^2] Its cab was two stories of log-frame construction, and the lookout lists date it to 1934.[^1][^2][^3]

The sources disagree about the forest. Two lookout pages place the site in the Bitterroot National Forest,[^1][^2] while the Forest Lookouts page names the Salmon National Forest.[^3]

Newspaper items quoted on the Forest Lookouts page are the main record of who worked there.[^3] In July 1950 the Recorder-Herald reported that high-frequency radios were being installed at the Stoddard and West Horse lookouts.[^3] In July 1951 a couple from East Lansing, Michigan, came up to man the post.[^3] In September 1954 a bull moose kept Mr. and Mrs. Bill Powell locked inside for two days.[^3] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page has no staffing history for the lookout, so these items are all we found.[^2]

Both lookout pages now list the site as gone.[^1][^2] Neither gives a date or a cause.

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the ridge today. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as a hike-in.[^2] Because the sources disagree about the forest, check with the managing forest before going near the ridge.
