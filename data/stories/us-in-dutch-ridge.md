The Dutch Ridge lookout stood in Monroe County, on Hoosier National Forest land southeast of Bloomington, just off Route 446.[^2] The Forest Service lists it as a tower said to have been 80 feet tall.[^1]

The Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Kurtz is credited with building it.[^5] The Forest Service says the same camp also built the Hickory Ridge and Trinity Springs towers.[^5] A 1947 photo of the tower is on a Hoosier National Forest historical marker seen near Leavenworth.[^2]

A September 4, 1963 item from The Republic, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, lists a new $14,000 tower to replace the Dutch Ridge fire tower, among other projects near Monroe Reservoir.[^3]

The tower was still standing in October 1952.[^4] The Forest Service dates the Dutch Ridge Fire to 1:15 p.m. on Sunday, October 26, 1952, when it started in a broom sedge field near Bartlettsville.[^4] The fire crossed roads and burned houses in northeastern Lawrence and southeastern Monroe counties.[^4] A newspaper reporter, determined to get a picture for his story, drove through the flames to the tower.[^4] He tried to climb it, but the winds nearly blew him off the narrow steel ladder, and he gave up after reaching the third platform.[^4] By October 27 the fire had reportedly covered more than 300 acres and come within half a mile of the Dutch Ridge oil wells, which a farmer saved with a disked fire line.[^4] Scattered rain showers and cooling temperatures ended the fire in early November.[^4]

A visit in 2023 found the tower not standing, with all four base corners still there.[^2] The Forest Service list also says the footers are still visible.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Indiana list marks the tower as gone.[^6]

The site is public land, but the 2023 visitor found no road or parking there.[^2] The author suggests parking briefly east of Route 446, south of Blackwell Church, and walking across the road to the site.[^2] The old forest road is not marked on the current Pleasant Run unit map.[^2] Only the base corners were left in 2023, so there is nothing to climb.[^2]
