Mount Butler, also called Butler Mountain, sits about 12 miles east of Port Orford in Curry County, Oregon, on the Siskiyou National Forest.[^1][^2]

Newspapers from 1930 describe a new lookout built on the summit that year.[^2] A Grants Pass paper said the top had to be widened by blasting before the building could go up.[^2] A July 1931 Siskiyou Bulletin item said the lookout was ready to be occupied.[^2] Firelookout.com dates the cabin to 1929 instead.[^1]

The lookout was staffed in summers from the 1930s into 1941.[^2] In 1933, Mr. and Mrs. Lawrence Brady planned to spend the summer there.[^2] In 1936, Mr. and Mrs. Howard Slonecker were packed in for the summer, shortly after they married.[^2] In 1937, Lyle Nestle was put in charge for the season.[^2] A 1940 item says Donald Slonecker replaced Dean Bundy for the rest of that season.[^2] The last staffing record on the Rex page is from 1941, when it was manned for 65 days.[^2]

A May 1940 Siskiyou National Forest guard placement document says the station sits in a fog belt. It notes that past records show the point was not used until mid-July or later, and that fog probably hobbled its usefulness for detecting fires on a large proportion of days.[^2]

Firelookout.com says the cabin was abandoned in 1962 and is gone.[^1] The Rex page marks the lookout as removed, without a date.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit now, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2]
