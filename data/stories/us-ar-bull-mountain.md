The Bull Mountain lookout is in Clark County, Arkansas, about four and a half miles northeast of Whelen Springs.[^1] No source gives the year it was built.[^1] The earliest description dates from 1941, when the Coast and Geodetic Survey described it as a lookout tower of the Arkansas Forestry Commission.[^1] The entry notes that the station was not visited.[^1] The tower was steel, about 100 feet high, with an enclosed cab at the top.[^1]

Act 234 created the Arkansas Forestry Commission in 1931, and the agency also put up many more towers.[^2] Preserve Arkansas says the state's lookout towers were put up to guard against wildfire.[^3]

A 1957 survey note says the station was probably the same one described in 1941.[^1] It describes a glass-enclosed observation cab on a four-legged steel frame, gives an overall height of 110 feet, and lists the condition as good.[^1] The 100-foot and 110-foot figures do not match, and the sources do not explain why.[^1]

The Encyclopedia of Arkansas lists the state's fourteen lookouts on the National Historic Lookout Register, and Bull Mountain is not among them.[^2] Preserve Arkansas says most of the state's lookouts remain endangered or have been torn down.[^3] That statement covers the whole state and does not name this site.[^3] No source describes the site today.[^1] Whether any of the tower survives, whether a base remains, and whether the public can visit are all unknown.[^1]
