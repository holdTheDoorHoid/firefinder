Beaver Hill is a lookout site 13 miles south-southwest of Coos Bay, in Coos County, Oregon, at 607 feet.[^1][^2]

Road work for the site was under way by November 1933. A field report by Samuel L. Miller from that month put the distance from the road to the planned lookout at about 5,900 feet.[^3] A December 1933 item in The Forest Log said work had started on another lookout tower and road, the Beaver Hill site southwest of Marshfield.[^3]

The 50-foot wooden tower went up in 1934.[^1][^2] Men from the Coquille CCC camp built it with an L-4 cab, according to the Forest Lookouts page.[^3]

In July 1934 a Coos Bay Times item about new hires for the Coos Fire Patrol named Charles Freeland as the person staffing the Beaver Hill station.[^3]

The site gained an AWS cabin in 1942.[^1][^2]

In 1958 a 40-foot wooden live-in tower went up in place of the earlier tower and cabin.[^1][^2] A 1958 district report quoted on the Forest Lookouts page gives the replacement towers as 50 feet, so the sources disagree on the height.[^3] The Columbus Day storm of 1962 destroyed that tower.[^1][^2][^3]

The register lists the Coos FPA as administrator and records the site as registered on November 22, 2010, on the nomination of Ray Kresek.[^1] The register does not describe what remains at the site today, and it says nothing about public access.[^1] Ask the managing agency before planning a visit.
