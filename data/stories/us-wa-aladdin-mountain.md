Aladdin Mountain sits in the Colville National Forest in Pend Oreille County, Washington, at 5,015 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

Around 1939, Aladdin Mountain had a camp with a crude platform tower. No permanent tower was ever built, as far as the sources know.[^1][^2][^3]

Willhite says the site was more of a camp than an established location, so maps do not show exactly where it was placed.[^3] The highest knob seems likely, but the camp could have been lower.[^3]

The sources I read name no one who staffed the platform, and none describes a fire it watched.[^1][^2][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as abandoned.[^4] It registered the site on April 16, 2020.[^1]

I found no source that describes a structure on the summit today. A photo caption in Willhite's account notes some wood at the top, but nothing conclusive.[^3] Willhite says a decent road reaches the summit area, but the last 400 feet of climbing have no trail. A round trip on foot is about half a mile.[^3] The land is national forest, so access appears open to the public.[^1][^3]
