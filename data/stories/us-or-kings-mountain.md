King Mountain was once called Onion Springs Mountain, which sits where Douglas, Jackson and Josephine counties meet.[^3] A cooperative tower and cabin on the summit were reported complete in June 1930.[^3] A December 1930 item says the name was officially changed to King's Butte after Porter S. King, the assistant state forester. A June 1931 item says King Mountain would replace the old name on the maps.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the first lookout on the site to 1931, a 50-foot pole platform with a camp.[^1][^2] A 1931 newspaper item describes a 40-foot tower with a small cabin at its base.[^3] Roy Edwards was in charge in 1931, when he and his wife lived in that cabin, and again in 1932.[^3] A Grants Pass paper reported that in August 1930 lightning started three more fires on the mountain, on top of four earlier ones.[^3]

The Civilian Conservation Corps started a 54-foot tower in July 1936.[^3] The register says a 50-foot live-in tower replaced the first lookout that year.[^1] The records describe a 50-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14 cabin, completed on March 17, 1939.[^3] From October 1942 to October 1943 an Army post on the mountain, called AWS in the records, was linked to the Roseburg Filter Center.[^3] An unmanned radar site was activated here in 1958.[^3] In May 1965 the Air Force declared the site excess, and that September it was transferred to the Bureau of Land Management.[^3]

In January 1953 high winds blew down a 52-foot lookout tower on the mountain. An Oregonian item put the cost of replacing it at $4,000.[^3] The register says a 40-foot live-in tower replaced it in 1953.[^1][^2] The register says the lookout was removed in 1972, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2]

No source I found describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
