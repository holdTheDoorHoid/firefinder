Tecate Peak lies almost on the US-Mexico border, east of San Diego.[^1][^2] Its lookout was an enclosed timber tower about 20 feet tall, with a 14-by-14-foot wood cab on top.[^1][^3] The register considers a National Park Service Plan 7 the most likely design.[^1]

The State Division of Forestry dedicated the new lookout on October 23, 1938, with about 150 people in attendance.[^4] Civilian Conservation Corps workers from the Minnewawa camp had spent about two and a half years building a seven-mile road up from Tecate and the lookout itself.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association dates the construction to 1939 instead.[^3]

At the ceremony, the state forester said the lookout belonged to the public and that many people should feel free to visit it.[^4]

The last staffing we found comes from a January 20, 1966 obituary in the Chula Vista Star-News. It describes Lee O. Wyatt as a forest fire lookout for the state's forestry division for 20 years, with Tecate Peak as his station, and says he retired in the summer of 1965.[^4]

Both the register and the Forest Fire Lookout Association say the lookout operated for at least four decades before it was torn down sometime after 1975.[^1][^3] The Friends of the Descanso Library describes it as a primary lookout that is now gone.[^5] The sources disagree on the exact spot. The 1938 report puts it northwest of Tecate, while the Descanso page places it just west of town, about a quarter mile inside the United States.[^4][^5]

We found no source that describes what is left at the summit today, beyond one hiker's note.[^6] A 2019 post, updated in December 2025, reports no marker the writer could spot, but a metal post set in concrete. The post does not say whether that metal post was part of the lookout.[^6]

Access is unclear.[^6] The same post says a National Defense Area created in December 2025 may affect access to the peak.[^6] An August 2026 report describes bulldozers and dynamite at work on the mountain as the border wall expanded.[^2] Check current access rules before going.
