The Sioux River lookout stood in the western part of what is now the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness, near Ely.[^1] The Superior National Forest is listed as its agency.[^3]

The county list shows a first tower on the site, based on forest guard records, with the years 1912 and 1913 printed beside it. It does not say which year belongs to that tower.[^2] A second lookout, also called Cascade on some maps, was a square-cab tower that probably dates to the mid-1920s.[^2] The author of a 2009 account says the Sioux River is the oldest of the three wilderness towers the author visited, and that the Forest Service had some structure there before the Civilian Conservation Corps years of 1933 to 1942.[^1]

The lookout guard lived in a cabin about a mile from the tower, perched above the canyon of the Devil's Cascade.[^1] A photo in the same account, captioned for August 1932, shows guards at the lookout cabin.[^1] To reach the site, a lookout guard might walk the telephone line trail for many miles, or possibly paddle a canoe and carry it over portages.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the tower as gone.[^4] The 2009 account describes the site differently. It says the forest has reclaimed the spur that leads to it, that finding the towers takes GPS-guided bushwhacking, and that most of the old tower remains on site.[^1] That last point covers the three wilderness lookouts as a group, and the two sources do not agree.[^1][^4]

The 2009 hike is the only description of the site we found, so we cannot say what it looks like today. The author walked about six miles to reach the lookout, on a trail that wilderness hikers also use as the first leg of the 35-mile Sioux Hustler Trail.[^1] Expect to hike rather than drive, and check current wilderness rules with the Superior National Forest before you go.
