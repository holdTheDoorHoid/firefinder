Luera Peak Lookout stood in Catron County, New Mexico.[^1] The register gives 1958 as the year it was built.[^1] It was never a conventional tower.[^1] The structure was a wooden platform set in the top of a pine, with a metal office chair fixed to it.[^1] The register puts the pine at 35 feet, and a 1976 newspaper account puts it at 34.[^1][^2]

The State Forestry Department used the tower for about 20 years, according to the newspaper.[^2] Ray Polasky, a fire control chief with the department, kept his office in the chair for a couple of years.[^2] There was no ladder to the platform, and the sighting device sat on an old tree stump on the ground.[^2] The 20-year figure does not sit neatly with a 1958 build date.[^2][^1]

The newspaper gives lightning danger as the reason for dropping it.[^2] The tree was in an open meadow, and almost every other tree nearby had been struck at some point.[^2] Governor Bruce King asked the Legislature in 1972, 1973 and 1974 for a new 60-foot steel tower, and a $37,000 bill for it died in committee without a hearing.[^1] The chair was staffed for only half of the 1975 season.[^1] The newspaper says the department had stopped using it the year before its story ran.[^2]

The register says the platform was removed from the peak in 1976. Its details block also gives 1975 as the removal year, which does not match its own description, and we could not settle that from the sources.[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the peak today.[^1] The register added the site on January 1, 2026.[^1] A 2016 access project for the wider area is documented, though.[^3] The Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation says it joined forces with the State Land Office and the state Game and Fish Department to fund a two-mile road easement through private land to reach public acreage.[^3] The foundation puts the state's trust land in Catron County at 52,000 acres.[^3] It does not say whether that route reaches the lookout site, so ask the State Land Office before you go.[^3]
