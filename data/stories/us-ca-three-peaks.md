The Three Peaks lookout stood in the Los Padres National Forest in Monterey County, California.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1934 for the U.S. Forest Service, and it had a 14x14 cab on a ten-foot enclosed tower.[^1][^2]

Radio receiving sets were installed at the station by July 1934.[^2] During World War II the Aircraft Warning Service manned the lookout, and the site gained more facilities.[^2] According to the register, the lookout overlooked the Fort Hunter-Liggett practice range, and many fires linked to artillery were reported.[^1]

Lack of money led to the lookout being discontinued in 1958.[^2] The Army and the Forest Service then swapped land.[^2] The Army showed no interest in the lookout. Vandalism and weather wore it down until the structure was torn down.[^2] The register says it was not staffed after 1958, that the land went to the Army, and that the Army removed the structure some time later.[^1]

No source we read says the lookout still stands. A hiker's undated trip report describes the south summit, where the lookout once stood, and says remnants remain: sawed-off telephone poles, wooden posts, some metal scraps and small areas of poured concrete.[^3] The register lists the Forest Service as administrator, but the land was traded to the Army.[^1] We found no current access rule, so check with both agencies before going near the site.
