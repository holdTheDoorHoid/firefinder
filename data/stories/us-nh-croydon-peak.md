Croydon Peak is the highest summit in Corbin Park, a private game preserve in Sullivan County, New Hampshire.[^1][^6] The park's fire tower is the only one in the county, and the state owns and runs it.[^2][^1]

Corbin Park began a fire protection program in 1903, and a watchman's cabin went up on the peak in 1907.[^1] In 1910 the Blue Mountain Forest Association put up a fire tower with help from the Draper Company, which owned timberland nearby.[^1] A 1909 forestry commissioner's report, quoted by a lookout history site, already mentions a station kept on the mountain by the Draper Company and the park association.[^5] The sources do not agree on the year of the first station.[^1][^5]

The 1920 upgrade gave the tower a 17-foot steel frame and a 10-by-10-foot cab.[^1] The Great New England Hurricane destroyed the lookout on September 21, 1938.[^5] The replacement that followed was a steel tower, though Historic Structures gives its height as 24 feet in one place and 34 feet in another.[^1]

The tower got new cabs in 1967 and again in 1989.[^1] In 1974 the state pulled the tower from service to fund a five-year air-patrol trial. Air patrols did not last, and the tower returned to service in 1976.[^1]

A 2008 chapter report had planned a new cab for 2009.[^3] The 2009 report says the state replaced the tower cab that year.[^4] Historic Structures says the cab was substantially rebuilt, with materials flown onto the summit by helicopter.[^1][^5]

Historic Structures counts the tower as one of 15 active fire towers the state owns and runs.[^1]

The summit sits inside the private park. Wikipedia says the mountain is not open to hikers, so the tower is not open to the public either.[^2]

The state's use of the summit rests on a 1950 lease. The Blue Mountain Forest Association rented one acre there to the state for a dollar, and the most recent renewal was in 1984.[^1] Visitors should keep away from the tower.
