The Oxford fire tower stands on Goat Hill in the Oxford State Forest, in Worcester County.[^1][^2] The first tower on the site was a 68-foot iron tower, which the Register says was built in the early 1920s.[^1] The 1938 New England hurricane destroyed it on September 21, 1938.[^1][^2] In 1939 a new 68-foot steel tower with a wooden cab of about 10 by 10 feet replaced it.[^1][^2][^3]

I did not find any account of who staffed the lookout or of any fire it helped fight.

The sources name different managers. The Register lists the Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control as administrator.[^1] A visitor guide says the land is DCR forest and names the DCR State Parks Central Regional Office as manager, but it marks its own details as unconfirmed.[^3]

The Register's March 2019 report describes a fence and No Trespassing signs around the tower.[^1] The visitor guide, dating its information to its most recent published report, says the same about the tower and describes the surrounding forest as public DCR land.[^3] The guide answers no to climbing the tower, and the fence and signs make it off limits, so do not climb it.[^1][^3] The guide says hiking in the surrounding forest is allowed year-round.[^3] I did not find a newer report on the tower's condition.
