A June 1936 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the Texas Forestry Service had just erected a new watch tower slightly north of Mount Enterprise in Rusk County.[^1] The item gives its height as 100 feet and says it was one of 19 watch towers then going up across the state.[^1] The towers were to have telephones linking each one to its neighbor.[^1] No later construction date appears in the sources read, so 1936 is the working date.

The same item said the station would be manned by a patrolman, R. S. Flanagan, and an assistant, Terrell Collier.[^1] In May 1950 the Rusk Cherokeean reported that crewleader Rob Flannagan, stationed at the Mt. Enterprise lookout tower, was replacing his hand-operated Husk fire plow with a Jeep for fighting fires.[^1] A local fire co-op, formed through the work of agriculture teacher B. W. Jackson and Flannagan, largely made the Jeep possible.[^1] The 1936 and 1950 items spell the name two ways and do not say outright that they name the same man.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list shows the tower as standing.[^2] The sources read name no notable fires at this tower and no staffing after 1950.[^1]

The sources read do not say whether the public can visit the grounds or climb the tower.[^1] Check with the Texas Forest Service before any trip, and stay off any tower that is closed or posted off limits.
