The Aimwell tower was built in 1929 in Catahoula Parish, near the La Salle Parish line.[^1] An October 1929 newspaper report, quoted on a lookout history site, said it was being erected on International Paper Company land and described it as the second fire-prevention tower in that section.[^1] It said a state conservation crew would man the tower.[^1]

A 1941 survey description puts the steel tower at 90 feet and gives 1929 as its build year.[^1] That description says the Southern Kraft Corporation owned the tower, while the 1929 report placed it on International Paper land.[^1] The 1929 report and the 1941 survey name different companies, and the sources we read do not explain the difference.[^1]

Newspapers from 1955 and 1957 also reported rainfall totals recorded at the tower, including 57.70 inches for 1955 and 48.32 inches for 1956.[^1]

Wikipedia's list of Louisiana towers marks Aimwell as possibly torn down. It says the tower could not be checked on aerial imagery, and that it was removed from USGS maps in 2015.[^2]

The same list places the tower northeast of Jena.[^2] No source we read describes the site today, so we cannot say what, if anything, still stands there or whether the land is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
