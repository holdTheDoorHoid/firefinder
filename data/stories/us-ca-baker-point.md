Baker Point is a granite summit above the Kern River, north of Kernville, California, in the Sequoia National Forest.[^2][^4] The U.S. Forest Service administers the lookout through the Hot Springs Ranger District.[^1][^2]

In July 1941 the Sequoia National Forest announced a planned lookout on Baker Ridge to replace the one on Sunday Peak. The first step would be a trail to haul in building materials.[^3] A year later a Bakersfield Californian report said the lookout would be manned from August 21, with a temporary structure to be erected first.[^3] Peakbagging.com dates the first structure on the site to 1942, while fire-lookouts.org says maps show a small wooden cab there around 1943, so the early dates disagree.[^5][^2]

The lookout that stands today is a 14-by-14-foot live-in cab on an open timber tower about 20 feet tall.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the cab to 1950, and Peakbagging.com says it was probably built that year.[^1][^5] The NHLR calls the cab a BC-301, while fire-lookouts.org uses the labels C-3-L or L-6.[^1][^2]

All of the materials were packed in by horse and mule.[^2] Fire-lookouts.org says two pack animals died when part of the original trail gave way, while the NHLR says two mules were killed during construction in 1950.[^2][^1] A one-room wooden cabin beside the tower was built in 1943 as living quarters, and the NHLR says it was likely used by the Aircraft Warning Service in World War II.[^2][^5][^1]

The sources say little about notable fires spotted from the tower. Fire-lookouts.org, last updated on March 1, 2013, says the tower has not been used for fire detection for many years and is not staffed.[^2] Peakbagging.com says it has been closed since 1986.[^5] Today the NHLR says the cab is used only in emergencies, but the site still hosts an important radio repeater.[^1][^2]

Fire-lookouts.org says a 1988 Fire Lookout Evaluation Report by Mark Thornton gave Baker Point 24 points for historical significance, which made it eligible for the National Register of Historic Places.[^2] The NHLR registered it on March 10, 2001, as US 376.[^1]

The Baker Point Trail is a relatively easy 2-3 mile round-trip hike from a parking area at about 7,500 feet, according to CalWild.[^4] Fire-lookouts.org calls the trail overgrown and tricky to follow. It puts the distance at two miles from the parking area to the lookout, which would make a round trip of about four miles.[^2] Forest roads 24S15 and 23S16 are often closed, so call the Western Divide Ranger District before setting out.[^4] Fire-lookouts.org says the structure is closed to the public, so keep to the trail and leave it alone.[^2]

The elevations also disagree. The NHLR gives the lookout 7,490 feet, while fire-lookouts.org gives 7,754 feet for the lookout and CalWild gives that figure for the summit.[^1][^2][^4]
