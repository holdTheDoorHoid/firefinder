Jersey Hill is a 2,251-foot mountain in the Southern Tier of New York, in Allegany County.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register places the tower site in Jersey Hill State Forest, which the state's Department of Environmental Conservation administers.[^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps laid footings for a steel lookout tower in 1934, and CCC workers erected the tower in 1935.[^1][^3]

Sources disagree on its height. A 1935 newspaper called it an 80-foot tower, and Wikipedia and the NYSFFLA list both give 79 feet 6 inches.[^3][^1][^4] The Starr list says 76 feet 9 inches, and the register says the tower was about 75 feet tall.[^5][^2]

When the tower went up in 1935, a paper said an observer would soon be posted there and that a cabin for the observer would be put up by April 15.[^3] Wikipedia says the tower was staffed only as needed by the Division of Lands and Forests. The sources do not say when that changed.[^1]

Aerial detection made the lookout less useful, and in August 1987 the tower was shut down, declared surplus and taken down.[^4][^1]

The register says just a fieldstone fireplace is left at the site.[^2] We found no source that describes a route to the spot or any visitor rules, so check with the state forest office before going.
