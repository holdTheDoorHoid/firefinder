Carbon Ridge stands in Pierce County, Washington, about 16 miles northwest of Mount Rainier.[^1] The Washington Division of Forestry, part of the state Department of Natural Resources, ran the lookout.[^2][^3]

The state built it with its own carpentry crew.[^3] A state report covering 1960 to 1962, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says that crew finished nine lookouts in two years, one of them at Carbon Ridge.[^3]

The sources disagree on the year. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and Firelookout both date it to 1961 and describe a 40-foot wooden live-in tower.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page gives 1960 as the year the tower was erected, and says lookout duty moved to it from the South Prairie tower that year.[^3] Willhite says it was built in 1961 to replace the nearby South Prairie lookout, but gives the height as 80 feet.[^4] The heights disagree, and the sources do not settle which is right.[^1][^2][^4]

The Willhite roster marks 1961 as the first posting and 1966 as the last.[^4]

The Forest Lookouts page records the tower as removed in 1969.[^3] Willhite gives June 13, 1969, as the day it was pulled down.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was gone by 1969.[^2]

Willhite also found lookout debris on the ridge in February 1996, after a timber harvest. He believed the lookout had been moved and crushed, but could not recall where he saw it.[^4]

Photo captions on the Willhite page show four lookout footings, a weather station and a large campfire ring.[^4] He says the site is now used as a weather station, and that the summit was open and grassy in 2015.[^4]

Willhite's directions start in Wilkeson and follow Forest Service road 7710 over gravel with many potholes.[^4] He advises parking below the gate.[^4] The sources do not say whether the public may enter the site, so check with the land manager before you go.[^4]
