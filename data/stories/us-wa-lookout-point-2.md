Lookout Point is a former lookout site on a peak in Lewis County, Washington, about twelve miles northwest of Morton.[^2][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places it in the Mount Rainier region and lists the Washington Division of Forestry and DNR as the administrator.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site gives the agency as the State Department of Forestry.[^3]

According to the register, the tower was built in the 1930s.[^1] FireLookout.com gives the same decade.[^2] Willhite also places a tower there in the 1930s, and mentions a claim that the Huckleberry Lookout may have used it.[^4] None of the sources we read gives the tower's design, height or builder.

The name has caused some confusion. Forest Service maps label the peak Lookout Mountain, while the 1960 Metsker map shows the name Lookout Point on it.[^4] The register gives the elevation as 3,415 feet. Willhite says that figure likely comes from a benchmark on the Metsker maps, and that today's USGS data and a 1937 Forest Service map put the summit at 3,520 feet or more.[^1][^4]

The register and FireLookout.com both say the tower is gone.[^1][^2] We found no source that gives a removal date or says why it came down.

Willhite found nothing on top of the peak, though he says he did not search for long. He describes several large, flat areas on the summit, clear of big trees, with a cliff on the east side. An old dozer road climbs toward the top, and he found only old logging landings on the shoulders.[^4]

Access runs through a private gate on Forest Service Road 71. Willhite says the gate is open at some point during the year, and a sign there gives a phone number for access times.[^4] Anyone planning to go should call that number first.
