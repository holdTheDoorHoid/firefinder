Pearygin Peak, about eight miles northeast of Winthrop in the Okanogan National Forest, got a fire lookout in 1935.[^1][^2] The register calls it a 20-foot pole L-4 tower.[^1] Willhite spells the name Pearrygin and describes a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab on top.[^3]

In 1950 the U.S. Geological Survey visited the summit and placed a benchmark about 10 feet north of the lookout.[^3] The survey's description of the structure does not match that height. It describes a wooden building 14 feet square on four pole legs, about 40 feet high.[^3] The sources disagree on the height, and the 20-foot figure comes from the register and Willhite.[^1][^3]

The lookout was destroyed in 1953.[^3] The register says it was removed, while firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts listing simply marks it "Removed."[^4] When the USGS returned in 1956, it recorded that the Forest Service had destroyed the tower.[^3]

A 2022 visitor reached the peak up the Pearrygin Creek approach, a fairly good road that is washed out about half a mile short of the end.[^3] The trail became hard to follow after the first half mile because of wildfire damage, and the last mile and a half was a rough bushwhack.[^3] The visitor's photos show an outhouse still standing and an anchor and directional benchmark.[^3] The visitor says a recent fire skipped the summit and left the relic wood in place.[^3] The visitor reported only partial views from the top.[^3] The hike is three miles one way, with about 2,000 feet of climbing.[^3]

The summit is on Okanogan National Forest land.[^1]
