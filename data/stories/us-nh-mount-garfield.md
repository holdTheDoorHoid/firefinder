Mount Garfield, in Grafton County, is listed by the Forest Fire Lookout Association as a ground site rather than a tower.[^1] Willhite says the lookout cab, set on a concrete foundation, went up in 1940.[^2] The White Mountain History article ties that concrete base to the period after the 1938 hurricane, when the Civilian Conservation Corps also built lookouts, guard stations, trails and roads.[^3]

Willhite gives 1948 as the final year of use, and says the cab was burned in the 1950s.[^2]

Today the concrete foundation remains.[^2] Willhite places Appalachian Trail access about two minutes below the summit, and the last hundred feet or so climbs over slab rock to the summit and the old foundation.[^2] The White Mountain History article also notes that the lookout's outhouse remains standing a short way north of the summit, screened by balsam firs.[^3]
