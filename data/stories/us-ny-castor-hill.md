Castor Hill is a 1,483-foot mountain in the Tug Hill country, east of Greenboro in Oswego County.[^1] The state's Conservation Department built a steel fire lookout tower on the mountain in 1927.[^1] Wikipedia gives its height as 80 feet and its design as an Aermotor LS40.[^1] Local papers gave larger figures: a 1929 article called the tower ninety feet, and a 1936 article said 96.[^2] The sources do not agree on the height.

The tower had a telephone so the warden could report fires.[^2] Nelson Clifford presided over it in 1929 and made visitors welcome.[^2] Melvin Clemons was the observer in the 1930s, and a 1976 obituary for his widow says he died in 1939.[^2] In 1948 George Brennan reported several fires in one week, and the rangers found that many were brush fires at sites where people were cleaning up.[^2] Fred Kastler returned as observer in 1960.[^2] George Yerdon was the fire observer in 1967.[^2] Allen Yerdon retired from the tower in 1971 after working there as an observer.[^2]

The landowner and the Conservation Department could not settle a dispute in the 1950s, so in 1961 the tower was taken apart and rebuilt across the road in the Little John Game Management Area.[^1] Because of aerial detection, the tower stopped operating at the end of the 1970 fire lookout season.[^1] Wikipedia says the tower was taken down in 1981.[^1] A December 1982 newspaper report seems to conflict with that date. It still placed the tower on Little John Drive, said the state had recently offered it for sale, and reported that the Mad River Club had won the bid.[^2] The club planned to reuse part of the tower on its grounds east of Lacona and give the rest to a nonprofit as a birdwatching platform.[^2] A 1983 letter to the editor refers to the tower's disappearance from Little John Drive.[^2]

The state's list marks the tower as gone.[^3] A route-planning guide for the summit says the fire tower is no longer present, and that people now come for the quiet setting.[^4] We found no source describing what, if anything, remains on the summit or at the Little John site, and none saying whether the summit is open to visitors today.

The Little John Wildlife Management Area, where the tower was reassembled in 1961, is open for recreation all year and is free to enter.[^1][^5] The state lists it at 7,918 acres across four towns in two counties.[^5]
