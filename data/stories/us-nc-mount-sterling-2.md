The Mount Sterling tower stands on a high peak in Haywood County, North Carolina, inside Great Smoky Mountains National Park.[^1][^3] The register gives the site an elevation of 5,842 feet.[^1]

The U.S. Geological Survey set a survey mark on the peak in 1928 and described it that year.[^2] The description places the mark 15 feet southeast of a signal and observing tower.[^2] That signal was a tree braced with fallen timber, topped by a viewing platform.[^2]

The register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the present steel Aermotor tower in 1933.[^1] It describes a tower 60 feet tall, with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] Staff lived on site and worked two-week shifts each year, from February 15 to May 15 and from October 15 to December 15.[^1] The register says a watchman's cabin once stood just north of the tower, and he stayed there between watches.[^1]

The register shows the tower was registered on March 19, 2007.[^1] It says Haywood County administers the tower, and that the National Park Service uses it today as a radio repeater.[^1]

The register calls the site a popular backcountry campsite for hikers.[^1] The 1928 survey description says the station is best reached by trail from the Ola post office.[^2]
