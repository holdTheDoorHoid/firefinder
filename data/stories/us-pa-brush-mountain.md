The Brush Mountain tower stood on the highest point of Brush Mountain, northeast of Altoona, in Blair County.[^1][^2] The register calls it a steel Blaw-Knox tower built in 1923.[^1] A March 1927 article said the state erected a 60-foot steel tower there in 1923, with a steel cabin for a ranger at its foot.[^2] Altoona Mirror items from late 1922, though, already described the tower as recently erected and nearly complete, so the build year is disputed.[^2]

The lookout had a ranger on duty during the fire season.[^2] In October 1925 a warden was to man it by October 15, and in April 1939 Jesse D. Brandt was assigned to it.[^2]

The tower also attracted vandals.[^2] In July 1924 a $25 reward was offered for information leading to the arrest of people who broke into the cabin.[^2] A March 1927 article said men or boys, or both, had on a number of occasions damaged the tower and cabin and harmed the trees, with losses too great to estimate.[^2] In July 1927 a man was found guilty of defacing state property at the tower and fined $10. The charge alleged that he cut his name and other marks on it.[^2]

In November 1924 a forest fire started at the base of the mountain and by midnight had burned more than 150 acres.[^2] The Altoona Tribune reported that the blaze was moving toward the steel lookout at the summit.[^2]

The register says the tower was relocated in 1940 and given the name Scalp Level.[^1] A March 1945 newspaper item, however, reported that vandals had badly damaged the tower on Brush Mountain and that it would not be in service that year.[^2] That suggests it was still on the mountain in 1945, so the sources disagree on when the move happened, if it did.[^1][^2]

I found no source that says where the Scalp Level tower stands now, or whether anything of the summit lookout remains. Nothing I read says whether the summit is open to visitors.

[^2]: Brush Mountain, Eastern US Lookouts, quoting Altoona newspapers from 1922 to 1945, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/brush-mountain.html (accessed 2026-10-10).
