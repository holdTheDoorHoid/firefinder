Kempshall Mountain rises 3,350 feet near the hamlet of Long Lake in Hamilton County, New York, and it once carried a fire lookout.[^1] The first structure on the mountain was a wooden tower the Conservation Commission put up in May 1911.[^1] In 1918 a 35-foot Aermotor LS40 steel tower replaced it.[^1][^2] Aerial detection made the lookout unnecessary, and the tower closed after the 1971 season.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on when the steel tower came down. The Adirondack Explorer says it was dismantled in June 1977, after being closed in 1971.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register entry for the museum tower says the Kempshall and West towers were removed in the 1980s.[^3] Wikipedia gives no date for the removal.[^1]

Parts of the Kempshall and West Mountain towers were later combined into a single exhibit tower at the Adirondack History Museum in Elizabethtown.[^1][^3][^4] Wikipedia calls the building the Essex County Historical Museum, while the museum's own events page uses the Adirondack History Museum name.[^1][^6] The register says the museum bought the tower parts in the 1990s and built the exhibit tower. The register recorded that tower on May 22, 2022, as US 1596, NY 48.[^3]

The museum's homepage lists these hours from Memorial Day weekend to Columbus Day weekend: Wednesday through Saturday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., and Sundays, 12 to 4 p.m.[^5] To climb the tower, visitors must go through the museum, and its events page invites people to come for an easy climb.[^3][^6] After the tower came down, little upkeep went into the old trail from Long Lake.[^2] The original mountain site is listed as gone.[^4]
