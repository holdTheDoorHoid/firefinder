Grasshopper Point is a gently sloping prominence on a ridge in Wasco County, Oregon, inside the Mount Hood National Forest.[^4][^1][^3] The site sits at 5,385 feet.[^1]

The first tower went up in 1933. It was a 72-foot timber tower with an L-6 cab, which cost about $475 and took 800 man-hours to build, according to the Forest Lookouts history.[^3] The registry and firelookout.com give the tower a height of 84 feet and a 1930s build date.[^1][^2] The hiking guide also gives 84 feet.[^4]

That winter the tower blew over, and it was re-erected in 1934.[^3] The station was staffed for one day in 1945, by Ed Neubauer of the Trails Club of Oregon.[^3] It was deactivated in 1964.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts page says the unneeded tower was disposed of by burning in 1967, and the hiking guide dates the tower to between 1933 and 1967.[^3][^4] The registry and firelookout.com put its removal or destruction at about 1969.[^1][^2]

The Oregon Hikers field guide has no date on the copy we read, but it is the only account we found of what remains on the ground. It says little is left beyond a couple of concrete pedestals.[^4] There are no views from the point itself.[^4] A walk north along the ridge on an old road track reaches a knoll where trees have been cleared for a view of Mount Hood.[^4]
