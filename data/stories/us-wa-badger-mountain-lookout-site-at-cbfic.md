The Badger Mountain lookout stood on a sagebrush-covered summit about seven miles north-northeast of Wenatchee, Washington, just outside the national forest boundary.[^2][^3]

The tower was 18 feet tall and carried a standard Plan L-4 cab.[^1][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1941 as its build year.[^1] The National Register nomination, written in 1988, says the cab follows a Forest Service plan revised in 1932. It also says no record shows where the cab first stood, and a 1947 Forest Service letter it quotes names Badger Mountain as one of the other lookouts moved on the forest.[^3]

Lookouts staffed it over several decades. Barbara Calder worked it in 1946 and 1947, George Poling from 1948 to 1950, and Oscar Richardson from 1953.[^2] The nomination says Richardson manned it until 1972, while the other list runs to 1974, so the end date is disputed.[^2][^3] In 1988 it was manned during the Dinkelman Fire, and the fire could be monitored from the summit.[^3]

The National Register listed the lookout on December 27, 1990.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the tower served the Wenatchee National Forest until the 1980s, and it was registered there in August 2012.[^1] The cab went to the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center near Entiat in 1999.[^1] The sources do not say whether the 18-foot tower went with it.[^1][^2][^3]

Today the restored cab sits at the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center, just north of Entiat.[^4][^5] The center says its lookout interiors open only during its own events or by special arrangement.[^4] The Washington Trails Association describes three short trails at the center and lists no entry fee.[^5] The original summit is reached only over private land, according to the 1988 nomination, and we found no recent report of its condition.[^3]
