The Oregon Lookouts site has a separate entry called Black Butte (ELO), in the Malheur National Forest of Grant County.[^1][^4] Its listed spot lies about three-quarters of a mile east-southeast of the Black Butte Lookout, in the same section of land.[^1][^2] No source we read describes a structure at the ELO spot itself, so the history below is about the Black Butte Lookout, which the ELO entry uses as its reference point.[^1]

The sources disagree on when the Black Butte Lookout was built. Oregon Lookouts records a 20-foot (6 m) timber tower with an L-4 cab in 1934, and a second one in 1936.[^2] Firelookout.com says the site was developed in 1933 and that a patrol point lay half a mile east.[^3] The register says its first structure dates from 1936.[^4] The patrol point might be the spot the ELO entry marks, but no source we read says so.[^1][^3]

A July 1943 item from the John Day Valley Ranger said Carl Belshaw was stationed at the lookout.[^2] The register says the tower is staffed as needed.[^4] A 1959 Central Oregon district annual report said the lookout should be entirely replaced soon.[^2] The register records that treated timber replaced the original pole legs in 1964, and that the concrete piers were redone in 1994.[^4]

Firelookout.com, which is undated, says the tower is now judged unsafe for anyone to stay in.[^3] A Lookout Network article from spring 2014 said the engineering review was close to condemning the lookout, a warning a 2016 blog repeated.[^5] Treat the tower as off limits. At the ELO spot itself, no source we read describes what, if anything, remains today.[^1]
