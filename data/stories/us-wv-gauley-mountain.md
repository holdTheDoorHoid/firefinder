The Gauley Mountain tower stood in Fayette County, West Virginia.[^1] The Eastern lookouts page names the state Division of Forestry as the agency.[^2] I could not find a construction date. By 1926 the tower had a telephone line to a coal company at Ansted, which could pass messages on to the central office in Fayetteville.[^2]

A 1928 Charleston Daily Mail item, quoted on the Eastern lookouts page, named J.M. Hammond of Ansted as section ranger, based at the tower.[^2] The same item reported a full renovation: a new roof, new windows, a new observation-room floor and fresh paint.[^2] Hammond is the only named staffer I found.

The tower was moved in 2009.[^1] The Eastern lookouts page dates the Blaw-Knox tower's installation at Cass Scenic Railroad State Park to October 28, 2009.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the move to October 2009 and says it added the tower to a forestry display in the park.[^3]

Today the tower stands at Whittaker Station, a stop on the Cass Scenic Railroad line.[^3][^4] According to the register, the tower can be reached by train from Cass.[^3] The minutes of the 2013 Eastern conference record that a train conductor said the landowner did not want the tower seen from far off, so it was painted in camouflage to blend in.[^4] The Eastern lookouts page adds that the tower is meant more for looking at than looking out from.[^2] I found no source that says whether visitors may climb it. The original Fayette County site is listed as gone,[^5] and I found nothing that describes what remains there.
