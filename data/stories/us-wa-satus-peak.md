Satus Peak sits on the Yakama Reservation in Yakima County, Washington.[^3] The Yakama know the area as The Place Where the Wind Lives.[^1] The register says severe wind has blown away or badly damaged structures on the summit, and firelookout.com says more than a dozen have blown away.[^1][^3]

A cabin went up on the summit in 1920, and both TrailChick and the register date the site to that year.[^1][^2] A 10-by-10-foot cab followed in 1933.[^1][^3] Later came cabs in 1957 and 1960, and a steel and wooden cab on concrete pillars in 1967.[^1][^3] A lookout working there in 1967 recalled hiding behind the fire finder during a windstorm that blew out the windows and caved in a wall.[^1]

The present steel structure, a two-story concrete base under a steel cab, dates from 1976.[^1][^2][^3] The 1920 and 1976 dates do not conflict; they describe the first cabin and the building standing today.[^1][^2]

The register says the lookout is staffed every season, though the Yakama fire management team finds lookouts hard to staff.[^1] TrailChick, last updated in March 2024, reports the same.[^2] firelookout.com says it is staffed every summer.[^3]

The register listed the lookout on April 13, 2022.[^1] TrailChick calls it one of two lookouts still standing on the Yakama Reservation.[^2]

Yakama land is closed to the public, and the visits TrailChick describes were arranged with the tribe, as guided tours or work parties.[^2] TrailChick reports that in 2019 the Yakama Nation gave a small group a spring and a fall guided tour, in exchange for volunteer help opening and closing the lookout.[^2] The register lists the Yakima Indian Agency as the administering body.[^1]
