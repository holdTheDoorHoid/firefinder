Quilcene Ranger Station was a Forest Service compound in the town of Quilcene, in Jefferson County, within Olympic National Forest.[^3][^4] Its buildings went up between 1929 and 1934, and a National Register nomination describes the group as a district of eight buildings, six of them historic contributing structures.[^1]

The site also had a water tower. The nomination says it was completed in 1932, along with the fire cache warehouse, the woodshed and the roads and trails warehouse.[^1] It describes the tower as an imposing structure, 50 feet high, with a base of peeled logs and a tank that held 6,000 gallons.[^1] The Forest Service used it mainly, though residents could draw on it during a fire.[^1]

Eric Willhite's page takes a different view. He wrote that the property appears to have had a training lookout, based on an old photo, and that the tower would have been used for wildland firefighting lookout training.[^2] He also thought the dark cylinder on top was a water storage tank, so his page and the nomination may well describe the same structure.[^2] The two accounts still disagree on what the tower was for. The nomination calls it a water tower and says that in July 1935, townspeople climbed it to get a better view of the eclipse.[^1]

The nomination says the water tower was still in place in 1955 and had been demolished by 1964.[^1] Willhite found gravel parking where the tower had stood and says the lookout is gone.[^2]

Ownership of the property passed from the Forest Service to the Quilcene Fire District in 2011.[^3] Willhite says the Quilcene Fire Department owns it now.[^2] Two structures from the old photo still stand.[^2] The closer one is used to train firefighters who become trapped, and it has a small window that serves as the escape.[^2] Willhite also says the house standing today is on the National Register of Historic Places.[^2]

No source says whether the grounds are open to visitors.
