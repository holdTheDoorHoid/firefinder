A fire tower went up in 1934 on the hill west of Mt. Hope Cemetery in Southern Pines.[^1] An August 1934 Pilot item called it the fire tower on Cemetery Hill. A September 1934 item described the site as a plot donated by Paul Barnum.[^1]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built it.[^1] A state biennial report says the CCC erected a 72-foot 9-inch steel tower and a four-room tower house for $991.47, and that the Emergency Conservation Works program paid for it.[^1] In October 1934 the Pilot gave the height as nearly 70 feet, measured up to the roof's coping, and described an enclosed cabin at the top with observation instruments.[^1] The two heights do not match, and the sources do not say why.[^1]

In July 1942 the Pilot said the post at the fire tower just outside Southern Pines needed volunteer airplane observers, or spotters, to man it.[^1] A state report covering 1958 to 1960 notes that a new towerman's residence was built at the Southern Pines tower.[^1]

The records cited here stop at 1960 and do not say what stands on the hill today.[^1] The cemetery is maintained by the Town of Southern Pines, at 405 West Morganton Road.[^2]
