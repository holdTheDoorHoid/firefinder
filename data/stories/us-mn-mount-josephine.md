Mount Josephine stands in the Sawtooth Mountains of the state's northeast and overlooks Grand Portage Bay on Lake Superior.[^1] The mountain takes its name from Josephine Godfrey, a young woman from Detroit who led a day trip of friends from Grand Portage to the summit in 1853.[^1]

A ladder tower once stood on Hat Point near Grand Portage.[^2] Its legs had an unusual lattice design, and a list of Minnesota towers also gives it the name Grand Portage lookout.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^3] Two hiking accounts nonetheless describe a stone base still present at the summit.[^4][^5] One guide describes the stone base and metal pieces from the tower strewn through the woods as you cross the top.[^4] A second account describes exploring a large base of the old fire tower at the summit.[^5]

Hikers reach the site from a small parking area on the north side of County Road 17, about 2.4 km east of the village of Grand Portage, where a small sign marks it.[^4] From there, a two-track road runs about 400 metres before a trail heads into the woods and climbs roughly 200 metres to the former tower site.[^4] One visitor says the trailheads are unmarked, and that the only sign on the route is an unobtrusive one about half a mile in.[^5] Superior Hiking says the mountain is in the Grand Portage Reservation.[^4] We found no source that describes a closure of the site or a permit requirement.
