The Pine Mountain lookout stood in the Angeles National Forest in Los Angeles County, at 4,545 feet, or 1,385 meters.[^1] The site was picked after a survey of the land visible from the peak was completed in 1934.[^1] The register dates the building to 1935.[^1] The register describes a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin on an open timber tower 10 feet high.[^1] An August 1937 newspaper item says work had begun on a garage at the lookout.[^2]

By the late 1940s the cabin had stopped being staffed for fire detection.[^1] Briefly, in the early to mid 1960s, the cabin was used by a cloud seeding project run by Los Angeles County.[^1]

In August 1968 a fire burned through the Fish Canyon area.[^2] A Eugene, Oregon paper reported that the fire covered more than 18,000 acres, wiped out about a dozen cabins, and burned a disused lookout tower on Pine Mountain.[^2] The register files the 1968 event as an arson fire and attributes the burning to "hippies."[^1] A Pasadena paper, quoted on the same page, described the tower as having sat unused for 20 years and estimated the loss at $5,000.[^2] The same item said officers had forced a group, described as hippies, out of the Fish Canyon cabins as the fire approached, but did not link that group to the tower fire.[^2]

The building was not rebuilt.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, and none says whether visitors can reach it. Anyone planning a trip into that part of the forest should check with the Angeles National Forest first.
