Shelton Butte rises on the east side of the Klamath River, about six miles north-northeast of Weitchpec in Humboldt County, at 1,056 meters.[^1][^2] The lookout there was built in 1934 as a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin on a 10-foot enclosed timber tower, and the US Forest Service ran it through the Six Rivers National Forest.[^1] A 1941 survey description says the building was painted white, had a pyramidal roof and stood about 25 feet high, so the two height figures may describe different parts of the structure.[^2]

A diary entry quoted on the Forest Lookouts site says Percy Starritt was moved into the new lookout in July 1935.[^2] During the winter of 1942 to 1943 the station was used by the Aircraft Warning Service during World War II.[^1][^2] Later items name R.F. Lamberger, who replaced Edgar Warrington in 1945, Mrs. Ena Starritt as relief lookout in 1952, the Sonneborn family for the 1958 season, and Julius B. Starr, named to the post in 1959.[^2]

In 1962 the Eureka Humboldt Standard reported that C.W. Taggart died when his car plunged down an embankment while he was traveling to visit his wife, a lookout on Shelton Butte.[^2] In 1965 a temporary tower structure was being erected at the lookout, and Robert Tillitz of Chico was manning it.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives the removal date as unknown.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as gone.[^5] A 2008 blog post, citing a book, said the lookout tower had gone.[^3]

The same 2008 post described a radio tower on the site.[^3] An OpenStreetMap entry tags the site as a fire lookout, but that tag does not say whether anything stands there.[^4] No source we read describes the summit today or whether the public may visit it.
