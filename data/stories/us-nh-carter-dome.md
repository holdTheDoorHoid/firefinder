Carter Dome is a 4,832-foot peak in Coos County, New Hampshire.[^1][^3] Local woodsmen and guides built observation towers there as early as 1876, according to Willhite Web.[^1] By 1907 the mountain was being watched for fires.[^1] In 1915 a watch tower was built, and the U.S. Forest Service took control from the state Forestry Commission.[^2][^1]

A Forestry Commission biennial report for 1921-22, quoted by Forest Lookouts, describes a new trail to the top that opened the way for a telephone line and observation quarters.[^2] The report promised dependable day-and-night service in dry spells, when watching might have to be continuous.[^2]

In 1924 a new steel tower with a cab, described as 30 feet, was built.[^1] The tower was abandoned and destroyed in 1947, according to the same page.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association now lists the tower as gone.[^3]

Today the summit is wooded. The page says a few spots in the trees still offer a photo or two looking north.[^1] Photo captions show footings and an anchor on the summit.[^1] The Appalachian Trail runs over the top, so hikers can reach the site on foot.[^1] No other source I read describes anything at the site today.
