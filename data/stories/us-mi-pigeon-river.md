The Pigeon River fire lookout sat within the Pigeon River Country State Forest, in Otsego County.[^1][^2]

In the state's 1937-38 biennial report, the Department of Conservation described a new cabin at the tower site, built to house the towermen more comfortably. The cabin measured roughly 23 by 21 feet and had bunks, a fireplace and concrete porches.[^1]

A trail guide says the job in the 1930s was among the most isolated in northern Michigan. Towermen spent their days on platforms five feet square, from 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.[^2]

The forest lookouts list calls it the Pigeon River tower.[^1] The trail guide calls it the Pigeon River Middle Fire Tower.[^2] The two names appear to describe the same tower, though no source says so outright.

The trail guide says the Middle tower is no longer standing; the ends of its three galvanized steel anchor legs still poke six inches above the ground.[^2]

Today the site lies along the Towerman's Watch Pathway, a 3.7-mile loop built in 2020.[^2] The loop starts at the Pigeon River Country headquarters, and the tower site is about two miles in.[^2] Hikers may use it in summer and snowshoers in winter, but mountain bikers and equestrians may not.[^2]
