Harrison Lookout stands on Hale Peak, a forested hill on the west shore of Harrison Lake in the Fraser Valley Regional District of British Columbia.[^1][^4][^5] The name Hale Peak is unofficial, and the hiking community knows the summit better as Harrison Lookout.[^4]

Three sources give three different summit heights: 701 m, 717 m and 739 m.[^5][^4][^2]

The lookout dates from the early 1900s, when a network of fire lookouts was being set up across British Columbia.[^1] In the past, it helped detect forest fires.[^1]

A hiking post describes soot and ash on the trees from a human-caused fire in 2015.[^2] It says the old tower somehow came through the flames.[^2]

The sources do not agree on what stands there now. A hiking club post says members of its club helped take the old tower apart and rebuild it as an exact replica.[^2] A directory says the lookout came through a forest wildfire and has been refurbished since then.[^3] A site listing says the original structure has been renovated, yet it keeps its rustic appeal and historical value.[^1]

Today the lookout is a hiking destination. One listing gives the trail as 3.5 km there and back, with about 337 m of climbing.[^1] A hiking post gives 4 km and 415 m of gain.[^2]

The trail begins on a service road a short distance from the main Harrison West Service Road, and you can park on the road shoulder.[^1] One post puts 23.5 km of gravel road on the drive in.[^2] The listing warns that ticks are plentiful here, so check yourself and your pets afterwards.[^1] Inside the lookout, a listing mentions an old stove, some furniture and a book for visitors to sign.[^1]
