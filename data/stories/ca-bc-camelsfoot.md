An old fire lookout stands on the summit of Camelsfoot Peak, sometimes called Camelshoof, just north of Lillooet.[^1][^4] The sources do not fully agree on the spot.[^2][^3] Wikipedia describes an old forestry lookout on an unnamed pinnacle upriver, near North Fountain, reached by a decommissioned side road.[^2] A trip report instead follows the ridge past the first viewpoint toward the old lookout, but turns back before reaching it.[^3]

The peak and the range are named for camels.[^1] The directory credits a Lillooet entrepreneur, John Calbreath, with an 1862 experiment in which 23 camels were bought in San Francisco for $300 each.[^1] A trip report and Wikipedia credit the purchase to a different man, Frank Laumeister, who bought 23 camels from the US military.[^3][^2] The directory calls the camels temperamental and vicious, and says they were set loose.[^1]

The lookout is now abandoned. OpenStreetMap describes it as an abandoned BC Wildfire Service fire lookout tower.[^5] From a turnaround point short of it, a 2015 trip report found the lookout rather derelict and said the writers would not consider using it for shelter.[^3] Another writer said the windows are gone, but the building did not seem too run down.[^4]

Getting there means driving the rugged West Pavilion road.[^1] The summit trail starts across the road from a cattle guard and follows a decommissioned road.[^3] The final stretch is described as loose, slippery and steep.[^4]

A 2015 trip report noticed a few remains of an old TV and radio antenna and building at the first lookout, a treeless knob southwest of Camelshoof Peak.[^3] The directory says the old transmission site has no structures left.[^1]

Trip writers describe the summit as a hike you can take. None of the sources checked mentions a closure or a permit for the lookout.[^4][^1]
