Horse Mountain Lookout was built in 1935 on the Payette National Forest: an R1/L-4 cabin on a 30-foot open timber tower.[^1] It sits at an elevation of 6,892 feet.[^1][^2]

Staffing records are thin, but a couple of names have surfaced: "Scoop" McCoy was reported working the tower in 1939, per a Post Register article, and Leonard Mooney staffed it in 1981, according to the Adams County Record.[^2] Beyond those two mentions, there's little documented about who else kept watch there over the decades.

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on September 29, 2020, as US 1434, nominated by Brad Eells.[^1] It's listed today as staffed and reachable by drive-up access during the season.[^2] Sources don't indicate any period of abandonment, fire damage, or replacement — the 1935 tower appears to be the same structure still in service.

Given the limited documentation available, this is a tower with real gaps in its known history: no record here of exactly how many seasons it ran dark versus staffed, or who else sat watch there between the 1930s and the 1980s. Anyone with more detail on Horse Mountain's staffing history would be filling in a real blank. As with other active Forest Service lookouts, it's a working fire-season facility rather than a public rental, so visitors should check current access and conditions with the Payette National Forest before planning a trip.
