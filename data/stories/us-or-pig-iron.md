Pig Iron Lookout stands in the Umpqua National Forest in Douglas County, about 33 miles south of Oakridge.[^1][^2] The sources disagree on where it sits. The Register says it is a half mile below the summit, while firelookout.com says one mile west of the summit.[^1][^2]

The Weyerhaeuser Sales Company prefabricated the lookout in 1950, and the Register calls it probably one of a kind.[^1] The current structure was built into the fall of that year.[^1]

The cab size also differs. The Register describes a 10-by-10-foot cab on a 10-foot wooden tower, while firelookout.com and the Oregon Lookouts history page give 14 by 14 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1950 Dwight Berryman lived in a tent, and the fire finder stood outdoors under a cover when not in use.[^3] The next year the lookout was mainly staffed in the afternoons, and Gary Wilson, a former lookout, wrote that he manned it that summer.[^3][^1] The sources read do not mention any notable fires.

The Register reports that the lookout was restored and now serves as an emergency detection site.[^1] Firelookout.com says volunteers staff it.[^2] A July 2023 visitor found it marked as staffed, though no one was home that day.[^4] A 2022 visitor met the woman on duty, whom the post describes as a Forest Service employee. She said vandals had cut the gate, and the visitor thought a Forest Service crew had recently fixed it.[^5]

On that 2022 visit a locked gate on the access road blocked vehicles, and visitors walked about a mile from it.[^5] A notice on the same post says the area was closed as of July 20, 2024, due to the active Diamond Complex, and the sources read do not say when it reopened.[^5] A 2023 visitor reached the lookout by a trail from Road 250.[^4] Another report calls Pig Iron Trail #1438 overgrown and steep.[^5] The Register listed the lookout on March 1, 2000.[^1] The sources do not mention rentals, so check with the Diamond Lake Ranger District before you plan a visit.[^1]
