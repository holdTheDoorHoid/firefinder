Cook's Peak sits above the south side of Lake Isabella, east of the town, in Kern County and the Sequoia National Forest.[^1][^3] In February 1936 a notice said a crew of 25 men had been sent from Camp Fulton to lay a telephone line to Cook Peak, where a new lookout was planned.[^1] By June 1942 the lookout had a 25-foot (7.6 m) steel tower and a house.[^1]

That June a fire first spotted by lookouts on Cook Peak burned 12,000 acres on its first night.[^1] The lookout, staffed by Cliff Harrington and Robert Crain, was eventually surrounded and overrun by flames.[^1] Both men stayed at their posts and were not hurt, and the steel tower and house took no damage.[^1]

In July 1947 Claude Stewart of Porterville, who had spent four years as a lookout on Cook's Peak, reported sighting a California condor about 100 feet from the station, which stood at 5,200 feet.[^1] In October 1952 Mrs. Helen King, the Forest Service lookout, sent word by radio about the local crows, and a small legend held that rain always came five weeks after the crows flew away.[^1]

In July 1968 a grass and scrub oak fire burned over 980 acres near Lake Isabella, moved up Cook's Peak, and the lookout station was abandoned.[^1] The two newspaper accounts disagree on what burned.[^1] The Times-Standard reported that a small cabin, a boat and some sheds burned at the station, while a Fresno paper said the flames skirted it.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list shows the tower as gone.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout today. A trip report describes a graded dirt road that goes almost to the top, a small communications tower north of the summit, and a benchmark and register at the highpoint.[^3] The sources do not say whether the summit is open to visitors.
