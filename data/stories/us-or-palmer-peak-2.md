Palmer Peak lies in Jackson County, Oregon.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page places its lookout on the Crater National Forest, the name used in a 1916 report on the forest's lookout system.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the Rogue River National Forest, 19 miles southwest of Medford at 4,736 feet.[^2]

The earliest plan we found dates from 1913.[^1] A history of the Rogue River National Forest, quoted on the page, proposed a trail and telephone line to the peak, with a lookout keeper posted there.[^1] By 1914 the page describes the lookout as a ten foot tree.[^1]

In July 1914 a Medford Mail Tribune item, quoted on the page, reported that Fort McKee had taken up his post on Palmer Creek Knob as the forest fire lookout. An August item placed him on the Palmer Creek lookout.[^1] The newspaper calls the spot Palmer Creek Knob, and the page files these items under Palmer Peak.[^1]

Firelookout.com describes the 1915 set-up as a camp and a crow's nest.[^2] A 1916 report by Harold D. Foster, also quoted on the page, says the observer camped close to the summit, a 10-minute walk from a spring. It adds that the map board stood on a tower at the highest point, with the telephone some distance off.[^1] Foster also wrote that a 120-foot steel tower would be needed for an unobstructed view. The page does not say one was built.[^1]

Firelookout.com and the Association's list both mark the site as gone. Neither gives a date or says how the lookout ended.[^2][^3]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on Palmer Peak today, or whether the summit is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
