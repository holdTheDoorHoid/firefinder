A Washington lookout history lists Blue Grouse Mountain in Stevens County, under the Stevens County Timber Protective Association.[^1] A June 10, 1926 newspaper item places a lookout on Blue Grouse mountain in the Sacheen Lake district and names Richard Smith as its lookout.[^1] A June 19, 1926 item lists three lookouts in Stevens and Pend Oreille counties, one of them on Blue Grouse mountain.[^1]

A second lookout history, on WillhiteWeb.com, reads the June 10 item differently. It argues the item more likely describes Little Blue Grouse Mountain, a peak near Boyer Mountain in Pend Oreille County.[^2] It points to a 1922 item about a lookout station on Little Blue Grouse, west of Sacheen Lake, and notes that the 1926 item also mentions Sacheen Lake.[^2] The same history describes the Stevens County peak, about eight miles to the southwest, as unrelated.[^2]

The name has been confused before. Boyer Mountain was once known locally as Blue Grouse Mountain, and a June 1930 newspaper item said a man would be stationed at a Boyer lookout on Blue Grouse Mountain.[^2] For the Pend Oreille site, a lookout inventory lists a pole platform tower and a cabin in 1923. That lookout was abandoned in the 1930s, the history says, likely because the higher Boyer Mountain got a structure.[^2]

No source we found describes a structure on the Stevens County peak today.[^1] The author of the Pend Oreille page searched the likely spots and found no relics, though that account is about the other peak.[^2]
