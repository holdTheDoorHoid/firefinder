Camel's Hump Lookout stands in Mineral County, Montana, on the Lolo National Forest.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the US Forest Service as its administrator.[^1] A lookout site places it six miles northwest of St. Regis.[^2]

The early dates disagree. A 1928 newspaper report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, named Camel's Hump among new Lolo structures that year, and a 1932 notice says a lookout was employed there.[^3] A Forest Service inspection dated December 1956 gave the tower then standing a build year of 1933, a height of 50 feet to the catwalk and a 14-by-14-foot cab, and it recommended replacement within two years.[^3] The Register describes a first lookout built in 1935: a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^1] The sources do not show whether the 1933 structure and the 1935 pole tower are the same building.

The Register says the present tower, a 41-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 cab, went up in 1960. The lookout site says 1961.[^1][^2] The 1956 inspection recorded the older tower's members as untreated.[^3]

The Register and the lookout site both say the tower is staffed every summer.[^1][^2] The lookout site also lists known lookout workers from 1955 through 1968.[^2] We found no newer staffing record, so that claim is unconfirmed.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4] We found no visit or rental information. Ask the Lolo National Forest about access before planning a trip, and do not climb a tower that is closed.
