Apache Maid Mountain sits about 45 miles south of Flagstaff, and its fire tower went up in 1961, about a year later than the fiscal 1960 plan.[^1][^2] The Forest Service first meant to build it in 1959. In August of that year an Assistant Regional Forester wrote that competing work elsewhere in the region meant the tower would not go up in fiscal 1960. The memo suggested that the cab and tower material be stored safely until fiscal 1961.[^2]

Work stopped and started. The erection could not begin until the access road was finished.[^2] The erection contract went to Martin C. Cline, who began on November 7, 1960. Snow stopped the work on November 17, and it resumed on April 3, 1961. The contract was completed and accepted on April 29, 1961.[^2] The cab is a 14-by-14-foot metal CL-100 series design on a 30-foot metal tower.[^1] A 1959 newspaper item, quoted on Ron Kemnow's page, said the tower would protect the Happy Jack and Beaver Creek watersheds.[^2]

The sources disagree about the height of the peak. The 1959 newspaper called it an 8,000-foot peak, while the 1961 construction report gives 7,300 feet and the register lists 7,301 feet.[^2][^1]

A 2011 visit report on the register marks the lookout as staffed.[^1] I found no fire, move or closure in the sources I read. It was registered on the National Historic Lookout Register on March 15, 2006, and it sits on the Red Rock Ranger District.[^1] The Fire Lookout Association still lists it as a standing tower.[^4]

Today, the sources do not say whether the tower is staffed or open to visitors, so check with the Red Rock Ranger District before going, and do not climb the tower unless staff invite you up. What you can rent is a cabin at the mountain's eastern base, listed on Recreation.gov. It was built in 1909 as a ranch house, later served as a Forest Service ranger station, and now sleeps up to six in 520 square feet. It has a propane stove but no refrigerator, and visitors bring their own drinking water and food. Passenger cars can reach it, though high clearance is recommended.[^3]
