Tick Ridge sits in Cabwaylingo State Forest in Wayne County, a forest the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) developed in the 1930s.[^1][^3] A 1937 newspaper item reported that an 80-foot tower would be erected on the ridge. The steel framework lay on the ground while crews prepared the concrete foundations.[^2] The item called it the farthest west of the state's observation points, overlooking the Twelvepole Creek watershed.[^2]

The dates do not agree. The register gives 1937.[^1] The state park's history says 1935, and a 2026 trail write-up repeats that year.[^3][^4] Because the January 1937 item describes the tower as not yet erected, I have used 1937 in the timeline and flagged the conflict.[^2]

According to the park, Tick Ridge is among fewer than twelve fire towers that are still open yet no longer in service.[^3] The register reports a fence around the tower and says the bottom set of stairs is gone.[^1] I found no record of fires or staffing for this tower.

Today the tower stands uphill from a picnic area, near the campgrounds.[^1][^3] Visitors can hike to it along the forest's trails.[^4] The park's wording suggests it is open,[^3] which sits oddly beside the register's fence and the trail write-up's note that it is not open for climbing. Please leave the tower alone and treat the fence as a boundary.[^1][^4]
