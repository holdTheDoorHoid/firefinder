Courtney Butte sits about three miles southwest of Flora in Wallowa County, Oregon.[^2] The butte and Courtney Creek both take their name from three early stockmen: Lins, Alex and Bent Courtney.[^3]

The Oregon Department of Forestry built a lookout on the butte in 1955.[^2][^4] The 1955 annual report describes a new 14-by-14 combination lookout, garage and crew house, along with a quarter mile of road.[^4] In the 1950s the site was used as a combined fire observation and Ground Observer Corps station.[^4] Another description calls the 1955 building a three-story enclosed cab, so the sources do not fully agree on what was built.[^2]

Known staff included Marilyn White in 1957, and Guy and Mildred Murrill from 1958 to 1970.[^2] In 1969 the roof was painted International Orange to help aircraft identify it.[^4] The lookout was used for emergencies into the early 2000s.[^2]

The department later sold the tower and land at auction to a private buyer, though the sources checked do not date the sale.[^3] The lookout is now private property.[^4] The owners have renovated the cab and host friends and family there as a guest house.[^3]

Today the sources differ on access. The National Historic Lookout Register, which registered the site in January 2017, lists it as available for rental.[^1] Another account says there is no public access.[^2] A 2022 trip report describes it as private land where visits need the owner's permission.[^5] Please do not visit without that permission.

The sources also disagree on elevation. The Register gives 4,387 feet, while the firelookout site gives 4,813.[^1][^2]
