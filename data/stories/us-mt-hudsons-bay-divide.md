The Hudson Bay Divide lookout stood in Glacier County, Montana, on the Blackfeet Indian Reservation, about five miles south of St. Mary.[^1] A register of former lookout sites calls the site Divide Mountain, while the Glacier archives and a lookout history page use Hudson Bay Divide.[^3][^2][^1] The 1934 and 1937 dates and the wind losses line up across these records, so they appear to describe one lookout.[^1][^2][^3]

A lookout house went up on the site in June 1931.[^4] An annual forestry report for the Blackfeet Indian Reservation describes it as a ready-cut 14 by 14 building on the east slope, overlooking timber in the St. Mary and Milk River drainages.[^4]

In November of that year a windstorm completely demolished it, blowing away all but the north wall and taking the roof with it.[^4] An August 1932 report says the agency's only funds covered the Forest Supervisor's salary, so rebuilding was impossible.[^4] That same report set aside savings to pay a watcher at the point from July 25 until about September 10.[^4]

A second lookout went up in 1934.[^2][^3] The register describes it as octagonal, with a design aimed at the site's high winds, while a lookout history page calls it a steel tower.[^3][^1]

The second lookout was lost to wind.[^1][^2] A Glacier National Park Archives photograph dated June 25, 1937 documents wind wreckage, with the damage placed in winter 1936-37.[^2] The lookout history page says it blew over in 1937.[^1]

The register reports that the 1931 foundation was still there in 2022, though wind had destroyed the lookout.[^3] It lists the site as administered by the National Park Service, with the Blackfeet Nation as a cooperator.[^3] No source says whether visitors can reach the site today.[^3]
