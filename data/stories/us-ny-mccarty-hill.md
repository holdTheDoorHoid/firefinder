McCarty Hill lies in Cattaraugus County, mostly in Little Valley and Great Valley.[^4] In May 1940 the Olean Times Herald reported work under way on a new tower there.[^1] Workers from the Hungry Hollow CCC camp had broken ground for its concrete piers.[^1]

Civilian Conservation Corps men from Camp 106, at Camp Seneca, helped build the steel tower.[^2] The 1940 report planned a tower 82 and a half feet high, with a glassed-in cabin at the top.[^1] Wikipedia gives the height of the finished tower as 82 feet 6 inches.[^4] Starr's list gives the design as an International Derrick model 1937.[^3]

The sources disagree on the name. Wikipedia says the tower was first meant for Irish Hill, about six miles northeast.[^4] Starr's list says McCarty Hill is locally called Irish Hill.[^3]

The 1940 report expected a ranger from the state Conservation Department to be assigned to watch for fires in the surrounding forests.[^1] Wikipedia dates the first staffing to 1941, when four fires and 980 visitors were logged at the tower.[^4] Starr's list says it operated three months a year, in April, May and October, and into June and November when fire danger was extreme.[^3]

Wikipedia says lookout work stopped after the 1988 season, and the state formally closed the tower in early 1989.[^4] From 1987 it carried repeater antennas for the county sheriff's office.[^4] In 1993 the state agreed that the county sheriff's office could build a free-standing tower on the same site.[^4] The old lookout came down at the same time the new tower went up.[^4]

Wikipedia says the two ski resorts sit on the north side of the mountain, and most of the rest is state forest.[^4] No source we read describes the 1993 tower's condition today, or whether the public can reach the summit.

Do not confuse McCarty Hill with the Salamanca Fire Tower. That older tower, erected in the fall of 1938 and 40 feet tall to its floor, is in the Town of Little Valley, and a 2022 newsletter says it still stands.[^2]
