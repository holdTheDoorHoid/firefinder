Cooley Hill is a 2,500-foot hill in the northwest part of Easton, in Grafton County, New Hampshire.[^1][^2]

A Portsmouth Herald item dated August 3, 1939, quoted by Forest Lookouts, reported that the Forest Service was building a program of fire observation towers in the White Mountain National Forest.[^1]

The same item said the Cooley Hill tower would be finished within a few weeks, and that it was being built by CCC recruits from Woodsville at Camp Wildwood.[^1]

A later trail guide gives the year as 1939, the builder as the Civilian Conservation Corps, and the tower as a 40-foot wooden structure.[^3] It says the tower was used for about ten years and then removed.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^2]

The Cooley-Jericho Community Forest, owned and managed by the Ammonoosuc Conservation Trust, has trails that reach the hill.[^3] The Yellow Trail starts at a kiosk on Trumpet Round Road and runs 3.1 miles to the summit.[^3] Hikers, mountain bikers, snowshoers and backcountry skiers share the multi-use trails.[^3]

The guide says the tower's remains can be seen on the left as you walk the Jericho Road Trail, which climbs the hill from NH Route 116.[^3] No other source I read describes the site today.
