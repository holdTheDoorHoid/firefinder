Mount Sentinel rises just east of the University of Montana campus in Missoula, and in 1915 the university put a fire lookout on its summit.[^3][^2] In December 1914 the Missoulian reported that students would build a station on the summit, and an August 1915 paper said the university had built it "last spring."[^2] The cab was a two-story hexagonal log and frame building, which the listing calls the first fire lookout on a college campus.[^1]

A watchman was posted there in 1915, and the same paper said the station looked out over three forest reserves.[^2] By 1916 a Ronan paper said rangers used the station through the summer months and that much of the students' fire-protection training took place there.[^2] A photo in the university's archive, dated circa the 1920s, is captioned as showing a man on horseback below the lookout.[^4]

The cab burned. A November 1928 Missoulian report said a fire, thought to have been started by hikers, completely destroyed the building on a Sunday evening.[^2] The same article dates the lookout to 1914 and credits the Forest Service with building it. It also says the cab was used only one season, which conflicts with the 1915 and 1916 papers.[^2] It adds that the Miller Mountain, Sheep Mountain and Petty Creek lookouts were used after the fire.[^2] Firelookout.com dates the burning to 1929 and calls the fire mysterious, which conflicts with the 1928 report.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as gone.[^5]

The concrete "M" built in 1968 still stands on the mountain's western face, about 620 feet up.[^3] Sources do not tie that letter to the lookout site. In the fall of 2010 volunteers helped restore the "M" trail.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, is left of the lookout, or whether its site is open to visitors.
