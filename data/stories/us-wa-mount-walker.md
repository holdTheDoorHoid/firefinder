Mount Walker, about three miles south-southwest of Quilcene in Jefferson County, Washington, carried a fire lookout from 1931 until 1967.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites record describes a gable-roofed L-4 cab, and the Forest Lookouts page calls the ground house a standard R-6 plan, 14 by 14 feet of wood frame, built on the Quilcene Ranger District.[^1][^3]

Panorama photographs were taken in 1935, and a one-car garage was constructed in 1939.[^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps put railings and seats at the lookout by 1937, and it also built the road to the viewpoint.[^5]

In 1942 the site became an Aircraft Warning Service station. The Seattle Filter Center activated it on March 16, and the AWS added a 12-by-18 garage.[^3][^5] When that service ended, the Forest Service kept the site.[^3] A May 1956 news item said the lookout was being manned, and that station crews were on standby with tanker trucks for fires.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites record says the cab was removed in 1967. Firelookout.com and Willhite's page say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^5]

Today the summit is a Forest Service viewpoint site with two viewpoints, north and south. Both have brief paths to the viewing area, and the Forest Service lists parking areas and vault toilets.[^4]

In summer you can drive up.[^5] In winter the road is gated, so you park at the gate and walk the road or the trail, about two miles to the first viewpoint.[^5] Willhite reports a phone insulator still on the ridge between the two viewpoints in 2020.[^5]
