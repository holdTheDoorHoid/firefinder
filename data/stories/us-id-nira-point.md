Nira Point sits at 4,870 feet in the Coeur d’Alene National Forest, in the North Fork of the Coeur d’Alene River drainage.[^1][^3] It was a crow's nest patrol point for Graham Mountain, two miles away.[^1][^2] Idaho Fire Lookouts is nearly certain that no tower ever stood here.[^1]

The site's authors guess that the patrol began around 1922, when Graham Mountain was first established, but they call it only a guess.[^1] They also say some question remains about what stood on the spot.[^1]

On a visit reported in July 2024, the group believes it found the crow's nest tree, whose main trunk it thinks has fallen.[^1] Beside it were a small rock structure, a trunk on the ground with two large spikes in it, and a great deal of #9 wire running to the site.[^1]

Reaching the point takes a hike.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts calls the route somewhat long but mostly downhill, and says the trail out was steep and gave out after a few miles.[^1]

A 2024 Spokesman-Review article says Gary Weber joined the hike to look for the old site.[^3] The group turned up a few signs that they had the right spot, though nothing major.[^3] On the way down, they lost the trail and had to bushwhack.[^3]

No source describes the site beyond these finds, and none gives rules for visiting it.[^1][^3]
