The Entwhistle lookout stood at about 2,676 feet on the Entwistle summit of Lookout Mountain, about 8 miles southeast of Bellingham.[^2][^4] The register lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as the agency.[^1] The first tower was a 50-foot pole platform, built in 1929.[^1][^4]

In 1954 the DNR replaced it with a 40-foot wooden tower and a 14 by 14 foot cab.[^1][^3][^4] Willhite says the cab was prefabricated in the shop over the winter, and that bottle gas for cooking and heating ended the fire hazard of wood stoves and chimneys in the old cab.[^4] The road to the site was hard to build: its last half mile was blasted out of sandstone.[^4]

The lookout was dedicated on January 10, 1955, to William "Uncle Billy" Entwistle, who had been hired in 1908 by the Washington Forest Fire association as its first fire watcher.[^3][^4] Sources spell the name both ways. The register and firelookout.com use Entwhistle, while Willhite uses Entwistle. This story uses the register's spelling for the lookout and Willhite's for the dedicatee.[^1][^2][^4]

Willhite names Junia Buchanan as the first lookout, who staffed it until 1960.[^4]

The register says the tower was removed in 1968.[^1] Firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^4] Willhite adds that the footings can be found tossed over the side at the south end of the site.[^4]

Both summits now hold communication towers, and Willhite says they offer very few views.[^4] His 2016 photo caption says the old lookout was replaced with a metal communications tower.[^4]

The Lookout Mountain Forest Preserve covers 4,430 acres on the west side of Lake Whatcom.[^5] Its trailhead is on Lake Louise Road, across from Sudden Valley Gate No. 9.[^5] From there, Willhite describes about four miles of road to a Y intersection, then about a mile to the left to the Entwistle summit.[^4] He describes the road as gated, for hiking and biking.[^4] The Mountaineers say all new trails are open except the Cougar Ridge Trail.[^5]
