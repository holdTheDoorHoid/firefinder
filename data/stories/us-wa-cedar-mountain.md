Cedar Mountain, in Fairwood (East Renton), carried a tree lookout rather than a tower.[^1][^3] Fred Fraser built it, and the Forest Lookouts index gives the State Division of Forestry as the agency.[^1][^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register listed the site on February 22, 2021, as US 2068, WA 506.[^1]

The register says the lookout was about 50 feet high, with a platform on top of two trees.[^1] Willhite's page describes the build in more detail. Two trees were cut off about 50 feet up, two neighboring trees were placed for use as a ladder, and a platform went on top for the lookout.[^3] A tent at the base gave the lookout somewhere to stay when not up on the platform.[^3] A 1940s photo on the same page is captioned as the "Cedar Mountain Fire Station," a label the text does not explain.[^3]

The lookout was used until about 1950.[^1][^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, of the platform still stands.

Today the summit lies inside Spring Lake/Lake Desire Park.[^3][^4] King County describes the park as having a bald rocky outcrop and three miles of multi-use trails.[^4] Willhite says a hiker-only Summit Trail leads to the top, and that a private home sits just below the summit.[^3] A photo caption on the page shows the word LOOKOUT in the rock at the summit.[^3] Willhite also warns that the view of Mount Rainier from the summit will be lost as the trees grow.[^3]
