High Deck, also called Hurricane Deck, is a summit in Linn County, Oregon, about 14 miles east of Sweet Home.[^1][^2][^3] The Linn County Fire Patrol association administered the lookout.[^1][^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry says the site began in 1907 with a platform tower and a tent, and that cabin living quarters were added in 1920.[^1] Forest Lookouts dates a cupola-style lookout house to 1919, so the two sources disagree on when the first house went up.[^2]

M.J. Nye, agent for the Hill interests in Linn County, organized fire protection in the area in 1904 and set up the lookout three years later.[^2] Forest Lookouts says High Deck was the first lookout manned in that part of Oregon, and that the station was manned each year after that.[^2]

J.A. Duncan spent the summer of 1931 at the lookout and returned for the 1932 season.[^2] In September 1934, Fay Miller, the keeper for the Hill Fire Patrol, fell ill and had to be helped down a three-mile trail to a car bound for a Lebanon hospital.[^2]

In August 1912 two lightning fires were seen from the lookout after dark, and the patrolman there called the valley patrolman by telephone, and the fires were put out.[^2] In August 1931 the Hill Fire Patrol answered a call near Sodaville, reported from the Hurricane Deck and Green Peter lookouts, and put out a fire before it did much damage.[^2] A 1934 field report said the spring had gone dry, that the nearest water was at Cascadia, and that Mr. Nye expected to need a pack horse to carry water up.[^2]

By 1947 the old lookout was no longer usable. That year a replacement 14-by-14-foot standard cab went up, set on a cement foundation with a catwalk, and Forest Service pack mules helped haul the materials.[^2] The Forest Log reported the cabin complete that November.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry dates this L-4 cab to 1947.[^1]

The cab was removed in 1972.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com marks the site "R.I.P. 1947-1972".[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether visitors may go there. A trailhead guide for the Cascadia Day Use Area gives directions that mention High Deck Road, but it says nothing about the old lookout.[^4] Ask the Linn County Fire Patrol association about access before you go.
