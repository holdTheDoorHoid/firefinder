Chelan Butte lies in Chelan County, Washington. Its summit lookout was a timber tower, and that tower was moved away in 1995.[^1][^2] FireLookout.com puts the butte two miles south of Chelan, while a 1963 newspaper item quoted on Washington Lookouts says it is seven miles south of town.[^2][^4]

The register says the first tower on the butte was a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1936, and that it later burned down.[^1] A 28-foot tower of treated timber, with an L-4 cab, replaced it in 1939.[^1][^2] The National Register nomination says a Civilian Conservation Corps crew led by Sim Beeson built the road to the site in 1938, so the tower and cabin could follow.[^3] A 1937 newspaper item quoted on Washington Lookouts names Marvin Kelly at a new station on the butte, where the cabin was still being built.[^4] The sources disagree on the first build year, and we could not decide which is right.

FireLookout.com lists Mac McFadden as the lookout from 1938 to 1940.[^2] The register says the tower had only sporadic staffing into the 1990s.[^1] In October 1963 a fire swept over the butte and burned beneath the tower, yet the Forest Service lookout stayed at his post, according to the same newspaper item.[^4]

Both the register and FireLookout.com date the tower's move to 1995, when it went to the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center, about a mile north of Entiat.[^1][^2] Before that move, the National Register nomination (listed December 27, 1990) placed the lookout on the summit, on state-held ground inside the Chelan Butte Wildlife Recreation Area.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the original tower as gone and the relocated tower as standing at the center.[^6] The center says its lookouts were brought in from other sites, and that people can go inside them only at center events or by prior arrangement.[^5] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.[^3][^6] If you want to see the tower, go to the center and check with them first.
