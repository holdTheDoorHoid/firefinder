The statewide list of North Carolina lookout towers places a tower named Greenville in Pitt County, at 35.55611, -77.37111.[^1] The only Greenville tower our sources describe is a city fire drill tower, a training structure for firefighters rather than a forest lookout, and we could not confirm that it is the structure the list means.[^2]

An ECU Digital Collections photograph record dates the drill tower to 1940, when firemen stood in front of the newly built structure.[^2] The record says the money to build it came from M.O. Minges, who is pictured with the firemen.[^2] A note on the photograph says the Minges drill tower was donated to the fire department and the city in 1940.[^2]

The record describes the tower as 18 by 18 feet and 60 feet tall, of brick, concrete and steel, with a concrete stairway to the roof.[^2] It stood on Chestnut Street and was used for firefighter practice drills.[^2] A sprinkler connection and valves on the first floor allowed a sprinkler system to be demonstrated.[^2]

No source we read describes what remains of the drill tower today, or whether its site is open to the public.

[^1]: North Carolina lookout towers, tnlandforms.us, https://tnlandforms.us/nctowers.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Drill Tower (photograph, Minges Collection), East Carolina University Digital Collections, https://digital.lib.ecu.edu/worksheet/23550 (accessed 2026-10-21).
