The Woodlawn Lookout stands in the Sumter National Forest in McCormick County, South Carolina. The National Historic Lookout Register also calls it the Clarks Hill Lookout.[^1]

Work began in May 1936. An Index-Journal item from that spring, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said the tower was going up on the Daniel McKie tract, one-half mile southeast of Woodlawn, and would help the Forest Service spot and control fires in the lower end of the district.[^2] The Forest Service's history of the district says the Civilian Conservation Corps built firetowers on the forest.[^3] The Register credits the Bradley CCC camp with building this one, the fourth tower in the Long Cane Division.[^1]

The Register describes a 100-foot LS-40 Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot galvanized cab and a hip roof, built in May 1936.[^1] It was one of several main stations the Forest Service used to spot fires in its early years.[^1]

The Register says the Forest Service used it actively until the 1980s.[^1] Forest Lookouts quotes a state forestry report saying that State Forestry Commission personnel began manning it in the 1974 to 1975 season.[^2] Those accounts do not clash outright, but neither explains how the work was divided. The sources we read name no particular fire it watched.

The Register's most recent visit report, dated September 16, 2020, called it the worst of three towers visited that day.[^1] The writer found the bottom staircase removed, a makeshift strap ladder on the first platform, the base fence mostly pulled down, the cab's windows broken and its outside covered in graffiti.[^1] The writer called the tower pretty shabby but said its structure looked ok.[^1] The tower likely needs a check for structural stability, and several wooden steps higher up are missing.[^1] We found no later report.

We also found no official notice about the tower's current status. HikingWNC says the upper deck is not open and describes a roadside stop of about 0.1 miles round trip.[^4] With the lower stairs gone and the upper deck closed to climbers, this is not a tower to climb.
