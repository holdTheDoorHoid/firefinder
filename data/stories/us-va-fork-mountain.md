The Virginia state forest service put up a steel fire tower on Fork Mountain in Madison County in 1929, one of three the state built that year.[^1] The site sits just outside the boundary of Shenandoah National Park, about a mile in a straight line from President Hoover's summer camp.[^1][^2]

According to 1929 newspapers, the tower was built 35 feet high with an inside stairway, and its observation cab was planned at about six by eight feet.[^1] Newspapers said it was built to help protect the proposed park from forest fire.[^1] They also noted that President Hoover and his weekend guests would get a magnificent view from it, and that Marines from the President's camp were to help erect it and act as lookouts when needed.[^1]

In 1940 a watchman, Douglas Seal, was at the site, according to a lookout-history page.[^2] That page describes a metal tower of another model, with five sets of stairs.[^2] It also has an undated park-archive photo of a newer-style tower under construction.[^2] It says the two towers look similar in height, and that it is unclear why a second one would have been built.[^2]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey visit in 1932 found a fire tower at the summit.[^2] By 1954 the tower had been taken down. An Army Corps of Engineers check that year found only concrete footings, and two radio tower installations had been added.[^2]

A 1957 survey called the summit Fort Mountain, not Fork, and noted that the cement foundations of the lookout still remained.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list still marks the tower as gone.[^3] The latest description we found is that 1957 note on the footings.[^2] No source we read says what the summit looks like now or whether visitors may go there.
