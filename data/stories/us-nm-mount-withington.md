The lookout on Mount Withington stands on the summit in the Cibola National Forest, in Socorro County, New Mexico.[^1][^4] The register lists the Magdalena Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1]

The first lookout on the site was a D-6 cupola, built in 1934. That cupola served 18 years before the present station took its place in 1952.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2020 New Mexico report also gives 1952 as the year the lookout was erected.[^2]

The present cab is a special R-3 design with a slightly sloped gable roof, set on a concrete block base.[^1] The register also records a catwalk, added afterward.[^1] The 2020 report contrasts it with the CL-100 series, which has a flat roof; this cab has a slightly sloped gable.[^2]

The register entry, US 669, NM 23, was recorded on April 30, 2006, and nominated by Dave Lorenz.[^1] In 1988 the lookout was evaluated for the National Register of Historic Places under a thematic nomination, but it was not found eligible because of its age.[^2] The 2020 report says it is now 68 years old and should be re-evaluated and nominated if eligible.[^2]

The association's New Mexico list shows the lookout as standing.[^3] The 2020 report marks several Lincoln towers as proposed for the cabin rental program.[^2] Its Cibola list gives Mount Withington no such mark, and I found no rental listing for it.[^2] The sources I read do not say who has staffed the lookout or which fires it watched.

Visitors should know that the summit road is Forest Service Road 138, which a travel guide describes as completely unpaved. The guide says a 4x4 vehicle is required and that the road is closed annually from October 1 to May 15.[^4] The dates come from a travel guide, not the Forest Service, so check the closure with the Forest Service before you drive it.
