Clarke Mountain's first lookout was a D-6 cupola cabin from the 1920s.[^1] The present L-4 cab went up on a 10-foot concrete base in 1952, and the Forest Service's list of Clearwater lookouts gives the same year.[^1][^2][^4]

Idaho Fire Lookouts names people who staffed the tower from the 1930s into the early 1980s. The site credits Fire Lookouts of the Northwest for a note that one of them, Jimmy Clarke, was killed on duty, though the exact year is unknown.[^3]

I could not find a source that dates when the tower was abandoned. The Forest Service list marks it as abandoned, and firelookout.com adds that it is heavily vandalized.[^4][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register added it on June 1, 2004.[^1]

A register visit report dated July 12, 2003 describes a concrete base that was giving way and a catwalk that was unsafe on two sides. Beams had been added beneath the cab for support, and a posted sign asked for help restoring the lookout.[^1]

Today it is a hike-in site.[^3] The 2003 visitor walked an old trail from the Bungalow Ranger Station site, which is open to motorcycles, and another route from Elk Mountain has become an ATV trail.[^1] I did not find a closure notice, but the newest condition report I found is from 2003, and the structure was already in poor shape then. Please do not climb it, and check with the Clearwater National Forest before you go.
