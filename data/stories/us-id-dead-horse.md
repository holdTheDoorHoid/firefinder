Dead Horse Lookout stood on a mountain in Clearwater County, Idaho, about eight miles south of the Canyon Ranger Station.[^1] The earliest structure listed was a log cabin with a crow's nest, built in 1914.[^1][^2] A 1915 entry in the Clearwater Timber Protective Association's ninth annual report lists a Dead Horse Lookout Cabin.[^3]

In 1934 a 40-foot L-4 tower went up on the north point, with the patrol point one mile to the southeast.[^1][^2]

Two people appear in the records in connection with the lookout. A June 1919 item in the Clearwater Republican said Mike Andrews would go to Dead Horse to run the lookout.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists him there again in 1921, and an August 1921 item reports R. M. Crow arriving from Dead Horse.[^2][^3]

Sources agree the tower was destroyed in 1961.[^1][^2] They do not agree on who ran the lookout. Idaho Fire Lookouts names the Clearwater National Forest, while the Forest Lookouts page ties it to the Clearwater Timber Protective Association.[^2][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts describes the site as a hike-in and reports a visit in a post dated October 21, 2022.[^2] The writers spent a long time combing the summit and found nothing but two telephone insulators, lodged in trees beside an open area.[^2] No source we read says whether the site is open to visitors today.
