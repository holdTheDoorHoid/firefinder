The Tom Dick Mountain lookout was a cabin, not a tower.[^1] The Forest Service built it in 1933 in the Mount Hood National Forest in Clackamas County, Oregon, at an elevation the register gives as 5,066 feet, or 1,544 meters.[^1][^2]

The register uses the name Tom Dick Mountain.[^1] Wikipedia says the peak's official name has been Tom Dick and Harry Mountain since the Board on Geographic Names decided in 1969 to include "Harry," and that the older name was used in the past.[^4] Wikipedia gives the highest point as 5,070 feet, close to the register's figure.[^4] Panorama photos from the site are dated October 26, 1933.[^1][^3]

The register says the cabin was removed in 1949.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] The Oregon Lookouts page lists it as removed but gives no date.[^3] The register and firelookout.com agree on 1949 but not on how the cabin ended.[^1][^2]

None of the sources we read describes what, if anything, remains of the cabin. A hiking guide says the trail reaches a ridge where "a large pile of rocks will greet you," but it does not connect that pile to the cabin.[^5]

The route to the top begins at the Mirror Lake trailhead on Highway 26.[^5] The trail passes Mirror Lake, which Wikipedia describes as a well-liked hiking spot, and then climbs steeply over rocky ground in the last part before the summit.[^4][^5] Hikers can reach the mountain on foot.[^5] We found no source that describes the cabin site itself as a place to visit.
