The Millstone Gap tower stands in Blount County on land that Tennessee Landforms lists as the private Blackberry Mountain resort.[^2]

Dry conditions in the 1940s led to repeated spikes in fires on the mountain.[^1] In 1949 the state forestry division drew up a forest fire plan that included building a fire tower and hiring lookouts.[^1] The landowner, Vestal Lumber, deeded the site to the state on April 29, 1949.[^1]

Howard Waters, a state forestry worker, put up the 47-foot steel tower in 1950 from a plan and prefabricated pieces.[^1] A steel frame carries a small square cab at the top, with wall-to-wall windows, and the tower sits on the mountain's highest point at 2,843 feet.[^1][^2]

Lookouts were paid to watch through the fall and spring fire seasons, and a small house with a bed and wood stove near the base gave them somewhere to stay.[^1] Local residents took turns on the job, including one husband and wife.[^1] The article does not name a fire the tower spotted, and I found none in the sources I read.[^1]

The article says the tower was later decommissioned but gives no year.[^1] Passing hunters later stayed in the old lodging and warmed themselves at its fire.[^1] In 2019 the magazine described the tower as the anchor of the Firetower restaurant at Blackberry Mountain, and a restoration expert who visited judged its wear to be light.[^1]

The article puts the Tennessee total at 250 towers, while a 2014 state form says the Division of Forestry built 208.[^1][^3] The two counts may cover different groups, so they are not necessarily in conflict.

Today the site is marked private, so do not climb the tower without the owner's permission.[^2] The 2014 form notes that climbing surviving fire towers carries risks that visitors are often cautioned about.[^3] I could not confirm whether the restaurant is still open or whether the tower looks the same as it did in 2019.
