The Yellowjacket lookout was built in 1917 as a crow's nest atop a 30-foot tree on Yellowjacket Mountain, about 20 miles south-southwest of Medford.[^1][^2] The mountain now lies in the Rogue River National Forest.[^1][^2] Sources from 1916 and 1924 call that forest the Crater National Forest, which was renamed in July 1932.[^2][^3] A cabin stood nearby. One source calls it a log cabin and the other a shake-sided one.[^1][^2] A 1916 report had proposed a 100-foot steel tower, which it said would mean felling 125 trees, but the structure built in 1917 was the crow's nest.[^2]

Personnel tables in the history of the Rogue River National Forest list J. J. Deadmond at the lookout in 1919 and 1920, A. Schichtl in 1921 and 1923, and Alex Schichtl in 1922, 1925 and 1926.[^3] In September 1924 a guard who had been burned on a fire on Red Mountain crawled three or four miles to the station, where A. H. Peachey gave him first aid, according to a Morning Oregonian item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page.[^2]

The lookout was abandoned in 1928, after a cupola cabin was built atop nearby Dutchman's Peak.[^1] That cupola house went up in 1927.[^4] A 1922 report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page for Dutchman's Peak, says Dutchman Peak sees everything Yellowjacket sees, plus places the report lists as hidden from it.[^4]

Today the picture is unclear. An undated page on firelookout.com says the crow's nest was still there when it was written, but the Fire Lookout Association list marks the site as abandoned.[^1][^5] We found no newer source describing what stands on Yellowjacket Mountain or whether visitors can reach it, so check with the Rogue River National Forest before going. The Dutchman's Peak cabin is a separate structure, which the same association list marks as standing.[^5]
