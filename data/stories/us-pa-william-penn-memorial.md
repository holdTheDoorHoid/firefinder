The tower on Mount Penn began as a Depression-era jobs program. In September 1938 the city of Reading, Pennsylvania secured a $15,091 federal grant through the Works Progress Administration, adding $18,445 of its own money for a total build cost of $33,536.[^1] Construction started on December 9, 1938, reusing the stone foundation of an earlier wooden tower that the Mount Penn Gravity Railroad Company had put up in 1889 and that burned down, reportedly by arson, in 1923.[^1] Architect Grover Cleveland Freeman designed the new tower after studying half a dozen other lookouts around the country, including one in Saint Paul, Minnesota.[^1] Local stone was quarried from the mountain itself, and ironwork came from Reading's Bachman Iron Works.[^1] The finished structure is a 120-foot stone tower topped with a round copper-domed cab, named for William Penn after a contest won by a local man named Tony Pietrovito.[^2][^1]

Construction finished in August 1939 and the tower opened that October.[^1] It served both as a tourist attraction and as a real fire-detection post, and the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry staffed it for decades.[^2] Deterioration eventually caught up with the building, and it closed in September 1988, ending its staffed lookout role.[^1]

The tower sat closed through most of the 1990s before a local nonprofit, working with Pagoda-Skyline, Inc., took on restoring it.[^2][^1] A $33,536 original build was followed roughly sixty years later by a renovation, and the tower reopened to the public on October 28, 2004.[^1] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 1999, while the restoration was still underway.[^2][^1]

Today the tower still stands over Reading, about a mile from the city's well-known Pagoda landmark.[^1] The City of Reading owns the structure itself, though not the fenced land immediately around it.[^1] It is a Former Fire Lookout Sites Register entry as well, since the wooden tower that once stood in the same spot before 1939 is the one tracked there, not the current stone tower.[^3]
