Caliente Mountain is San Luis Obispo County's high point.[^1] A 1935 Santa Maria Times report, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, said District Ranger E. M. Lyda planned to look into a lookout on Caliente peak; he believed most of the Cuyama Valley would be visible from it.[^2]

The Aircraft Warning Service, the civilian volunteer arm of the Army Ground Observer Corps, built a station on the peak in 1943.[^3][^5] The register says the design, a cabin on the ground topped by a viewing cupola, was unique to the service.[^3] The register also notes that most of these stations watched for fires as well as planes.[^3]

Volunteers noted each aircraft they spotted in paper records, then telephoned the sightings in.[^5] The service was deactivated on May 29, 1944.[^5]

The register lists the structure as removed and says a collapsed structure remains.[^3] A February 2013 blog post by David Stillman says he found the cabin entirely collapsed.[^4] From summit entries, he judged that it likely fell before early May 2012.[^4] He also wrote that its collapse had been expected for years.[^4]

Access looks restricted. Stillman says ranchers and oil-company security blocked his attempt on the south side of the peak, a climb he called off-limits.[^4] We found no source that describes a public route, so visitors should not plan a trip to the site.

The register's newest photo is from 2013.[^3] No source we read describes what stands on the peak today beyond that collapsed cabin.
