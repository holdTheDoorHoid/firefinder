Hyatt was a lumber town in far southern Tyler County, about three miles south of Warren.[^5] In 1882 the Rice brothers put a sawmill on the Sabine and East Texas Railroad, on the spot that later became Hyatt.[^5]

The lookout tower was in the John Henry Kirby Forest, about 15 miles south of Woodville, and the page that tracks it labels it a Texas Forest Service tower.[^1] A December 1943 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout history site, named Mrs. C.H. Dickens as the lookout there and called her the third member of a trio.[^1]

I could not find when the tower was built. A Tyler County artifact entry titled "Fire Tower" is dated January 1, 1941, but its text describes fire towers in general and does not name Hyatt.[^3] A Find a Grave memorial for "Fire Tower Hyatt" gives 1940 and 1980 as its birth and death dates. A contributor added it in February 2026, and its citation names Hyatt Cemetery without saying which record gives the dates.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Kirby Forest tower as standing.[^2] That listing is the only current status I found. If the 1980 date marks the end of the tower's life, it would conflict with that listing, and the sources do not settle the question.[^2][^4]

I found no recent photo, visit report or access information for the site, so its condition and whether the public may go there are both unknown. Please do not climb any tower until its access has been confirmed with the landowner.
