The Gift Lake lookout is in Alberta, according to our records, but the public sources we read say very little about it.

Alberta's government publishes a map of its wildfire lookout sites. It shows 127 of them, spread through the northern forest and along the Rockies.[^1] The province says lookouts are placed where sightlines are good for spotting and reporting wildfires.[^1]

Lookouts in the province have changed over the years. Early ones were often little more than trees stripped of their limbs, which propped up bush ladders.[^2] Steel came into use in the 1920s and gradually displaced timber in tower building.[^2] In 1945 lookouts began using the Osborne, an instrument that helps pinpoint where a fire is relative to the tower.[^2] Alberta Wildfire's blog says observers at these towers act as watchers for forestry across Alberta's high country, foothills and boreal forest.[^3] Many lookouts still in operation were rebuilt on the spots where the first generation stood.[^2]

We found no source that describes the Gift Lake site today, so we cannot say what stands there or whether the public may visit. Do not climb or visit the site without permission.
