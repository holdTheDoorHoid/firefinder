The Gunflint lookout was a ladder tower with a cab at the top, standing on a hill near the Gunflint Trail in Cook County.[^1][^2] Its purpose was an observation platform for spotting wildfires.[^3]

Sources disagree on when it went up.[^4][^3] A 1934 survey note already calls it the Paulsen Gunflint Lookout Tower,[^4] while a Boundary Waters visitor site dates it to the late 1930s.[^3] The trail that leads to it was created by Civilian Conservation Corps crews in 1937 as part of the Kekekabic Trail, which was built from the Fernberg to the Gunflint lookout.[^3][^5]

An undated local memoir describes a ranger at the tower, and says Gunflint Lodge guests hiked up twice a week to climb it and see that ranger.[^1] In November 1938 a CCC newsletter reported that Jim Dolter and Floyd Denzler had ended their work on the tower.[^6]

The tower stopped operating around 1960.[^3] Around then, the wilderness started to be watched for fires from the air.[^3] A photo of the tower was probably taken in December 1966.[^3]

The tower itself is gone.[^2] Its cab lies on the site, and a stone foundation marks where the ranger's cabin stood.[^1][^3] When a Boundary Waters visitor site last checked the spot on October 27, 2022, it found the cab in ruins and part of the cabin foundation still in place.[^3]

The site sits in what is now the Boundary Waters wilderness, and you reach it on foot.[^7][^3] The junction with the spur trail is about a half-mile from the BWCA Entry Point #56 trailhead, and the sign there says the hike to the tower is about three-quarters of a mile.[^3]
