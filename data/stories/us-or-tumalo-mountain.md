Tumalo Mountain is a shield volcano in the Cascade Range, located just northeast of Mount Bachelor, and it stands in the Deschutes National Forest about 16 miles west-southwest of Bend.[^3][^4] The register says a sighting instrument, an alidade, was set on a stump there in 1913, and that a two-story L-4 cab took its place in the 1930s.[^1][^3]

Rex's lookout page does not mention that cab.[^2] Its entries instead describe a new one-room house, 14 by 14 feet, announced in August 1948 and started that month, and say the lookout's headquarters were then in a tent-house.[^2] The sources do not settle whether the 1930s cab stood before the 1948 house.

A 1948 entry names W.T. Curtis as lookout-fireman, who divided his time between this station and Wanoga Butte.[^2]

In September 1968 the firefinder and anything portable were taken off the lookout, which was no longer needed for the job it was built to do.[^2] The register says the lookout was removed in the 1970s, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed in that decade.[^1][^3] Wikipedia says the tower was left unused in the 1970s and later taken down.[^4] Rex's entry records the equipment leaving in 1968, while the other sources date the structure's end to the 1970s, so these may describe different steps.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

No source we read describes what remains on the summit today. Wikipedia describes a route up the volcano's southwest flank to the top.[^4] We found no source giving a trailhead, a closure or a land-status limit, so check with the Deschutes National Forest before you go.
