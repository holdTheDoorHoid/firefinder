Heybrook Lookout stands on a low ridge above the Skykomish River valley near Index, Washington — one of the lowest-elevation lookout sites in the Washington Cascades.[^2] The Forest Service put up the first structure here in 1925, a simple tent platform.[^2][^3] Seven years later it was replaced by a 45-foot log tower with an L-4 cab and a ground-level room meant as sleeping quarters for the fire rangers; rodents reportedly overran the room and drove the rangers up into the tower itself.[^3]

In 1964 the agency rebuilt the tower again, raising the roughly 63-to-67-foot timber structure that still stands.[^1][^2] Its cab looks like the Forest Service's standard Region 6 "flattop" design, but it isn't one: Region 6 engineers had it designed in 1963 by the Forest Products Laboratory as a lighter, prefabricated cab that could be flown to remote sites by helicopter, much as the older L-4 cabs had once been packed in by mule train.[^1] The design was apparently never repeated elsewhere.[^1]

Because the site has held three structures, sources disagree on a single "built" date — some giving 1925, others 1964 — depending on whether they mean the first lookout or the one standing today.[^2][^1]

The Forest Service abandoned Heybrook as a staffed lookout in 1970.[^2] It sat empty until local volunteers rebuilt the cab in the late 1990s and put it back into usable shape; a volunteer group still helps care for it.[^2][^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register added Heybrook on January 3, 2015, as US 1094, WA 60.[^1]

Heybrook is open to the public today. The hike to it is a roughly three-mile round trip with some rocky footing but is considered only moderately difficult.[^1] The Forest Service rents the cab out overnight through its Recreation Rental program, and the lookout remains in good condition.[^2][^1] On a clear day, visitors can see Mount Index, Mount Persis and the Wild Sky Wilderness from its windows.[^3]
