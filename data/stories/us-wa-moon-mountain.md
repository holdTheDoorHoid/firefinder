Moon Mountain sits on the Colville Indian Reservation in Ferry County, Washington.[^1] The site began in 1922 with a log cabin and a crow's nest, and a tree platform was added in 1931.[^1][^3]

In 1935 a crew under foreman Morris Ladoen finished a 60-foot tower.[^4] A newspaper said the new tower replaced an older crow's nest set in two treetops.[^4] Pete Hall occupied the lookout from August 15 that year.[^4] Three of the lookout-history pages describe a 3-legged, 50-foot wooden tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, built in 1938.[^1][^2][^3] The sources give two heights and do not say whether the 1938 tower replaced the 1935 one.[^1][^4]

Miss Hortense Coleman, a teacher at the Covada school, was the lookout in 1943.[^3][^4]

No source gives a year for the tower's removal.[^1][^3] The register says it is not clear when it was removed, firelookout.com says it is not clear when it was destroyed, and one history page carries a "Removed" tag with no date.[^1][^2][^4]

Willhite's site includes a photo captioned as lookout footings from 2019.[^3] The site is on tribal land, administered by the Colville Indian Reservation, and no source states what rules apply to visitors.[^1] Willhite calls it a drive-up site. He rates the road as fair, with a steep stretch only at the end, and reports no gates. He says high clearance is best.[^3]
