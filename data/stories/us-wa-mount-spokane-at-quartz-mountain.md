The lookout now on Quartz Mountain began in 1979 on the summit of Mount Spokane, where the Washington Department of Natural Resources built it on a 40-foot tower.[^1][^2] The summit's fire-watch history is older still: the Vista House, finished in November 1933, was built with a fire lookout tower added at the Department of Forestry's late request.[^4] TrailChick dates the start of the site's lookout history to the Vista House's construction in 1934, so the two accounts differ by a year.[^2] TrailChick also describes an 85-foot wood tower built in 1948 that collapsed in its first winter, followed by a 45-foot tower built in 1950 and replaced in 1963.[^2]

The 40-foot tower was used for fire spotting until it was permanently decommissioned in 1994.[^1] A TrailChick write-up says that over 60 years of service, the summit lookout held the record for reported wildfires among Washington's 657 fire lookouts.[^2]

The DNR eventually stopped paying for costly repairs, and the tower was headed for demolition until park staff and an advisory committee pushed for it to go to the park.[^1] In June 2001 crews scrapped the tower, and the 9,000-pound cab was lifted by crane onto a truck and taken to a ranger's residence to wait out the permits.[^1][^2] In late August 2004 a parks crew made the old road passable for heavy trucks, poured four concrete pillars, and set the cab atop a new 10-foot tower on Quartz Mountain.[^1][^5]

The cab has been a rental since 2005, and the NHLR says it can be reserved from June through September for about $100 a night.[^1] Washington State Parks describes it as summer-only, a 14-by-14-foot cab that sleeps four and requires a two-night minimum stay.[^3] Visitors hike or bike the 2.7-mile trail in from parking at Selkirk Lodge, since there is no drive-up access.[^3] Reservations open up to nine months ahead, and TrailChick reports strong demand, with the cab usually booked up.[^3][^2] The state page does not quote a price and refers visitors to its fee schedule, so the $100 figure from the other two sources could not be checked against an official rate.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's listing adds that the cab is also available for emergency detection, though none of the sources gathered here describes anyone watching fire from it today.[^5]
