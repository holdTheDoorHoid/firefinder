Castle Butte has carried a lookout since 1916, when the Forest Service put up a simple frame cabin on this rocky point above the North Fork Clearwater drainages.[^1] That cabin lasted twelve years; in 1928 it was replaced with a log cabin topped by a cupola.[^1][^2] The building standing today is the third on the site: a square L-4 cab, fourteen feet to a side with a catwalk, raised on a stone foundation about ten feet tall and finished in 1950.[^1]

The lookout sits on the Clearwater National Forest in Idaho County, at 6,632 feet, within the Lochsa Ranger District.[^1] It was nominated to the National Historic Lookout Register by Virginia Tillotson and registered on March 22, 2004, as US 573 (ID 43).[^1] Getting there means driving the Lolo Motorway, a primitive single-lane road the Civilian Conservation Corps built through this country in the early 1930s, tracing part of the historic Lolo Trail used by the Nez Perce and by Lewis and Clark; the road is not kind to RVs, trailers, or low-clearance vehicles.[^3]

The cabin has since been rehabbed and brought into the Forest Service's recreation rental program.[^1][^2] Today it sleeps up to four, with two twin beds, two cots, a propane stove, propane lights and heat, and a separate pit toilet outside — no electricity or running water, so guests haul in their own.[^3] From the catwalk the view runs across the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness, the Seven Devils, the Great Burn, and the canyons below.[^3] It remains open to the public on a nightly reservation through recreation.gov, reached by a short, steep scramble of about a hundred feet from where the road ends.[^3]
