Jerimoth Hill, in Foster, is the highest point in Rhode Island.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places a wooden tower on the hill, first operated in 1917 and removed in 1926.[^2] It gives the hill's elevation as 812 feet (247 m), while the Town of Foster says 811 feet.[^1][^2]

A January 1917 passage from the state Commissioner of Forestry's annual report, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, says towns had not yet set up lookout stations and that the state would have to establish them.[^3] That passage does not name Jerimoth Hill.[^3]

The register, entered in September 2008, describes the site as private land where the landowner allowed hikes on weekends.[^2] For many years the only path to the summit crossed a private driveway owned by Henry Richardson, who barred entry.[^4] Richardson's son later agreed to allow access four times a year.[^4] After Richardson died, the Mosley family bought his property in 2005, allowed daily access, and eventually gave the land to the state.[^4]

The Foster Preservation Society says Brown University took ownership in 2012, and that since October 2014 the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management has owned the highpoint and its access road.[^5] Highpoint.guide gives the same dates.[^6] Wikipedia says that in 2014 Brown sold the summit itself to the state.[^4] The register's 2008 note of private land came before both changes.[^2]

Today the summit is a prominent rock about 200 yards off Route 101, a walk of under five minutes.[^6] The Town of Foster calls it an easy walk-in and says it is clearly marked with a federal geodetic survey marker.[^1] Highpoint.guide says a short trail was built from the parking area in 2005, and that no fee or permit is needed.[^6]

Sources disagree on hours. The Foster Preservation Society says access is generally from dawn to dusk, while highpoint.guide says the summit is open 24 hours a day.[^5][^6]

The register's coordinates are about 280 meters from the highpoint coordinates the Foster Preservation Society gives, so the tower's exact spot is not clear.[^2][^5] The Forest Lookouts Association lists the tower as gone.[^7] No source I read describes footings, a foundation or any other remains of the tower at the site.[^1][^2][^5][^6]
