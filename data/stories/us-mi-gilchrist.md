Gilchrist was a lumber settlement in Mackinac County, in Michigan's Upper Peninsula.[^2] It had its own post office from 1879 and a train stop, and the post office closed in 1927.[^2]

On May 29, 1937, the towerman at Gilchrist, Frank Fitzpatrick, reported the tanker Boyd on fire.[^1] The Boyd carried gasoline and had gone aground on a reef near Brevort the winter before.[^1] The Gilchrist tower, about 22 miles from the fire, first noted smoke at 7:40 a.m., and flames had broken out by about 10:30.[^1]

A 1965 survey description puts its station near the location of the Gilchrist lookout tower, which suggests the tower was no longer standing on that spot by then.[^1]

The survey notes that the station sits on government-owned property, reached by a narrow sand road to the top of a hill.[^1] The village is now a ghost town along the Hiawatha Trail, with a few residents and a couple of buildings that appear to be original storefronts used as homes.[^2] No source we found says what remains on the hilltop today or whether visitors may go there.
