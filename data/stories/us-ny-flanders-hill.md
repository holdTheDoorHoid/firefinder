Flanders Hill sits near Riverhead on eastern Long Island.[^1] The sources disagree about which side of town it is on. The Forest Lookouts page places it southwest of Riverhead, while the NYSFFLA list puts it three miles southeast.[^1][^2]

A 1918 Conservation Commission report chose the hill as one of two sites for observation towers, and the tower went up that year.[^1][^2] The NYSFFLA list describes a 47-foot Aermotor tower that was operating by 1919.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register describes an Aermotor LS-40 with 47-foot concrete footings, a steel structure, and wood steps and landing platforms.[^3] The NYSFFLA list and the Starr list both give the tower itself as 47 feet, while the register attaches the 47 feet to the footings.[^2][^4][^3]

Observers and wardens worked the tower through the 1930s and 1940s.[^1] In October 1932 Joseph H. Weaver, one of Suffolk County's fire wardens, was found dead in bed at his home, and the paper said he had watched from the Flanders tower.[^1] In the spring of 1933 Addison Jackson was appointed fire warden at the Flanders Hill station.[^1] He was taken ill while working at the tower that June, and he died at home on the evening of June 15.[^1]

By the early 1940s the tower reported fires by two-way radio.[^1] In April 1945 Edward Downs, on duty at the tower, discovered a fire and notified the radio operator at the Riverhead court house.[^1] When the season opened in April 1946, William Snell of Aquebogue was the observer.[^1] The NYSFFLA list captions a photograph of observer Bill Snell operating the radio on the hill.[^2] That May the tower reported what looked like a bad fire, but a check found dust blowing off a potato farm.[^1]

In October 1959 the state abandoned its lookout service to cut costs, and the title passed to Suffolk County.[^1] The tower was closed in 1959 and dismantled in 1960.[^2][^3] The register credits the state Conservation Commission with the removal, while the NYSFFLA list says a local demolition contractor did the work.[^3][^2]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. The NYSFFLA list, the register and the Starr list all record the tower as removed.[^2][^3][^4] We also found nothing about public access, so anyone planning a visit should check with the local town first.
