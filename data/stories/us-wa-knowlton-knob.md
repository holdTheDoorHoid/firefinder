The first lookout on Knowlton Knob was a ground-level L-4 cab from about 1934.[^1][^2] NHLR credits the state's forestry division with it, while WillhiteWeb and TrailChick credit the Forest Service.[^1][^2][^4]

Leslie Clapp was the lookout in the late 1930s and was still there in 1940.[^2][^3][^4] FireLookout.com also lists Eugene Lovejoy in 1947.[^3] Washington State took ownership of the site in 1954.[^2][^4]

In 1966 the state rebuilt the site with a timber tower and a DNR cab.[^1][^2][^3][^4] NHLR and FireLookout.com give its height as 40 feet, while WillhiteWeb says 60.[^1][^2][^3]

A contractor staffed the tower until July 1997, according to FireLookout.com.[^3] NHLR says the tower went to emergency status that year, and it was listed on the register in November 1999.[^1] TrailChick says the contractor has continued to staff the lookout over the years, in partnership with the DNR.[^4]

A wildfire in 1985 moved toward Knowlton Knob near Indian Dan Creek.[^5] WillhiteWeb says it overran the tower, but TrailChick says the lookout was spared.[^2][^4]

In 2014 the Carlton Complex Fire burned through two of the tower's legs, but the tower stayed up.[^2][^3][^4][^6] The fire also burned the toilet building.[^2] The FFLA report says an unknown benefactor repaired the legs quickly, while TrailChick credits DNR crews working with private landowners.[^4][^6]

WillhiteWeb reports only occasional use of the lookout today.[^2] WillhiteWeb describes gravel roads from Brewster, with a gate and No Trespass signs along the way.[^2] TrailChick says the public land ends before the lookout, so going up without landowner permission is trespassing.[^4] Ask the landowners for permission before you go.[^4]
