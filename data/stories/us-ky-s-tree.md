The S-Tree lookout stood in Jackson County, Kentucky, inside the Daniel Boone National Forest, at about 1,464 feet.[^1] The Register dates the tower to around 1937 and credits the Forest Service with building it.[^1] An April 30, 1936 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, already mentions the S-tree tower, so the sources disagree on how early it went up.[^2]

That item puts the tower at roughly 80 feet and says that on clear days a visitor could see about twelve miles around.[^2] It also describes a planned recreation area at Sand Gap, by the tower, with picnic tables and fire sites.[^2]

According to the Register, the tower was torn down in the mid-1970s, though no exact year is recorded.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page also lists it as removed.[^2]

Today the Forest Service's S-Tree Campground occupies a ridge above Horse Lick Creek, near McKee, and its 20 campsites are first-come, first-served.[^3] The Register also notes a Forest Service campground nearby.[^1] A campground guide places the old S-Tree fire tower close to the campground entrance.[^4] No source I read describes any footings or other remains of the tower today, so I cannot say what, if anything, is left.[^1][^3][^4]
