The Pleasant Hill Fire Lookout Tower stands in the Tuskegee National Forest in Macon County, Alabama, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] It is a 100-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree on the build year.[^1][^2][^3] The Register's Built field, the Forest Lookouts page and a local newspaper all give 1936.[^1][^2][^3] The Register's description instead says the USDA Resettlement Administration Land Use Project built it in 1937, and that the project was later to become the Tuskegee National Forest.[^1] The sources do not explain the gap. This story uses 1936 because three of them agree on it.[^1][^2][^3]

Nothing I read says who staffed the tower or what fires it watched. The Register lists the Tuskegee Ranger District as a cooperator, which is the closest related detail I found, but it does not describe any staff.[^1]

The tower appears to be still standing.[^1][^3] A local news story places the tower 100 feet above the Taska Recreation Area site, where restoration is underway.[^3] On a Thursday morning, about 20 young people and adults cleared brush beside the tower as work on Phase One and Phase Two of the restoration began.[^3] The copy I could read does not show a date.[^3] The most recent dated item I found is a March 2024 photo on the Register page, credited to Houlton Moates.[^1]

The sources do not say whether the tower can be climbed or whether the public may go up it. The site is a recreation area inside a national forest, but that alone does not make the tower open to climbers.[^1][^3] Please do not climb it, and check with the Tuskegee National Forest before a visit.
