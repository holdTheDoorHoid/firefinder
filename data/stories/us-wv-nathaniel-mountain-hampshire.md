The Nathaniel Mountain fire tower in Hampshire County was built in 1939, according to a DC News Now report and the Potomac River basin commission's guide to the wildlife area.[^3][^4] The register says only that it was built before 1941.[^1] A 1941 federal station record describes a steel tower on four legs, roughly 90 feet tall, with a cab enclosed in glass at the top.[^2] A 1948 Geological Survey recovery note says the name of the tower was by then Hampshire Tower.[^2]

For a stretch in the late 1970s, a retired wildlife manager spent 90 days each spring and fall at the tower. He lived in a small cabin at its base. From the cab he used a plotting board to fix where smoke was, and passed the position to the nearest Division of Forestry office, which alerted volunteer firefighters.[^3]

Lookout work began to fade once telephones reached West Virginians after World War II.[^3] According to the news report, cellphones doomed more than 100 of the state's fire towers.[^3] The state's own encyclopedia says its forest fire towers were closed in 1990.[^6] The report notes that the oak steps on the first two flights are missing. The regional forester says he had to act because climbers were vandalizing the cab.[^3]

Today the tower stands in the Nathaniel Mountain Wildlife Management Area, which is open to the public.[^1][^4] The hike to the tower is about 4.5 miles round trip from the base of Mill Run Road.[^4] Climbing the tower is not permitted.[^4] The register's only visit report, from September 2021, says the first flight of stairs has been removed and a lot of glass is broken out.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list still shows it as standing.[^5] I found no later condition report, so please stay on the ground.
