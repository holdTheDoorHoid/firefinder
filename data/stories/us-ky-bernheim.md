Isaac W. Bernheim ordered a fire lookout tower the year after he bought the land that became Bernheim Forest.[^3] The arboretum's history dates the purchase to 1928, although another of its pages gives 1929.[^4][^3] At the time the land was worn-out farmland with weeds, brush and few mature trees.[^2] Parts for the tower were shipped by rail from the Aermotor Windmill Company, then hauled up Ledgerock Knob and assembled there.[^2][^3] Construction finished on July 13, 1929. The tower was the first thing built on the property.[^2]

The register and the arboretum credit different parties with the tower, though both date it to 1929.[^1][^2] The register says the tower was constructed that year by the Kentucky Division of Forestry.[^1] The arboretum says Mr. Bernheim directed that it be built, and that the state's help began in the mid-1950s, when a radio was installed in the tower.[^3][^2]

The tower is 48 feet tall.[^2] A phone in the tower was linked to the headquarters building, and one of the employees stood watch in the tower whenever fire danger ran high.[^2] For many years, Bernheim's own workers staffed the tower.[^2] The arboretum says many fires began on neighboring land when farmers burned trash and brush, others came from hot cinders thrown by coal-fired trains on the northern border, and some came from lightning. No complete list of the fires survives.[^2]

The register says the tower was regularly staffed until 1980 and was used to monitor a wildfire in 1986.[^1] After 1980, aerial observers took over its job.[^2] The arboretum describes the tower as one link in a Kentucky system of 165 lookouts, and says a couple dozen of the state's towers survive. Bernheim's is one of two open to visitors, the other being at Cumberland Falls.[^2]

Today the Bernheim Foundation looks after the tower, with help from local volunteers.[^1] Visitors are typically admitted on summer weekends when the weather allows, during spring and fall school breaks, and on extra fall days for the foliage.[^2] The arboretum asks visitors to phone ahead, since schedules can change on short notice.[^2]
