The Civilian Conservation Corps built the High Rocks lookout, a steel tower with a log cabin, in 1935 and 1936.[^1] The site is in Great Smoky Mountains National Park, where the Historic American Buildings Survey recorded the guard house as HABS NC-410.[^2] A lookout index lists the National Park Service as the agency.[^3]

A park document, first drafted on October 9, 1935, says seventeen men were sent to put up the tower and cabin. Six tents sheltered them.[^1] At 46 feet, the steel tower was shorter than the park's usual 60-foot standard.[^1] The cabin was originally roofed with chestnut shakes and had a stone chimney. Facilities once included a 1,000-gallon cistern, a hand-pump sink and a pit toilet.[^1]

The on-duty lookout lived in the cabin and used a two-watt, battery-powered radio to send reports to the dispatch office.[^1] Mountain Press counts the site among the park's most remote and hard-to-reach lookouts.[^1]

Sources disagree about when the tower came down. A 2010 essay in the Smoky Mountain News says the park service took it down by helicopter in the late 1980s.[^5] SummitPost cites a 2016 Asheville Citizen-Times column that suggests the tower stood until the early 1990s.[^4] Mountain Press says the tower has been removed, yet its introduction lists High Rocks among the "four towers and one cabin standing inside the park today."[^1] A Pigeon Forge page counts it among the four original towers still standing.[^7]

Today a crumbling warden's cabin is still there, near the old tower site on remote Welch Ridge.[^6] SummitPost says only concrete pedestals are left where the tower stood.[^4] A photo on that page, taken March 26, 2022, shows the cabin in very poor condition.[^4]

The site lies inside the national park and is reached on foot.[^2][^4] SummitPost gives the most direct approach as starting at Lakeview Drive and passing through a tunnel.[^4] Check conditions with the park before you go.
