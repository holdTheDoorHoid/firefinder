Little Buck Mountain is a site in the Okanogan National Forest, in Okanogan County, Washington, at 5,390 feet (1,643 m).[^1] The register says the Forest Service built an L-5 cab there in 1934.[^1] WillhiteWeb has panoramic views shot from the cab's roof that September.[^3]

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed the cab or any fire it watched.

The register says the cab was removed in 1953.[^1] firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb both say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^3]

WillhiteWeb's author, who visited in April 2020, describes multiple communication towers at the top and found no relic besides an insulator from a phone line, hung on a tree near the peak.[^3] According to the register, the site now has a small ski lift.[^1]

WillhiteWeb describes parking at the summer gate closure and following signs for the summer hiking route, which is the steep communication-tower access road. The hike is 1.5 miles with about 1,300 feet of climbing, and a lift can shorten it in winter.[^3] We found no source describing public access to the tower compound, and we would not encourage climbing the towers.
