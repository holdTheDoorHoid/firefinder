Windy Peak is a former Forest Service lookout site on a summit about two and a quarter miles south of the Oregon border, in Siskiyou County, California.[^1] The register says the lookout was built in 1917 for the Crater National Forest in Oregon.[^1]

The register gives the house as twelve feet square with a six-foot cupola.[^1] A National Geodetic Survey station description, which draws on a 1935 Forest Service description, calls it a wood lookout house on the summit.[^2] A Medford Sun report on the standard design said the cupola held an Osborne fire finder, maps and other lookout equipment.[^2]

In June 1917 the Forest Service ordered ready-cut material for three standard lookout houses from the Mill-Made Construction company, of Portland.[^2] By July 13, 1917, the houses had been shipped from Portland in pieces.[^2] A July 17, 1917 report said the Windy Peak house would be the first assembled, with its parts taken up on pack horses.[^2] An October 1917 report said the station had been completed.[^2]

A Medford Mail Tribune item from August 1916 mentions a guard on the lookout at Windy Peak, a year before the register's build date.[^2] The sources do not explain that gap. In September 1917 a Forest Service telephone engineer reported that the observer was stationed in the new cabin and would have to go a mile for water.[^2]

A 1935 description places the house on the summit at that time.[^2] The register says the removal date is unknown.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page carries a "Removed" label for the site.[^2] The register added the site as a former lookout on April 22, 2020, nominated by Brad Eells.[^1]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the public can reach it. Check with the Forest Service before you go.

Do not confuse this site with another Windy Peak in Lane County, Oregon, where a 50-foot live-in tower built in 1938 was destroyed by a windstorm in 1958.[^3]
