A list of North Carolina lookout towers names a tower called Hatteras Inlet CG, in Dare County.[^1] The National Geodetic Survey has a station with the identifier EX0382, named HATTERAS INLET CG LOOKOUT TWR, at nearly the same coordinates.[^2] The name points to the Coast Guard station at Hatteras Inlet.[^2][^3]

The Life-Saving Service established its first station on Ocracoke Island in 1883, at the north end of the island near the inlet.[^4] In 1917 the old station was torn down, and a new Coast Guard station went up at the inlet, replacing it.[^4]

The Outer Banks Chamber describes the earlier location differently. It places that station on the Hatteras side and says the site is now destroyed.[^3] The two accounts do not agree on where the 1883 station stood.[^3][^4]

By 1951, erosion at the north end of Ocracoke had made the Coast Guard station unsafe in storms.[^4] Its entire crew was evacuated to Hatteras Island on 3 October 1951.[^4]

Today's Coast Guard inlet station lies close to the Hatteras ferry docks.[^3]

The NGS listing gives the survey mark an X code, which its legend defines as a surface mark reported destroyed.[^2] A second row for the same mark carries an N code, which means no geodetic control.[^2] Both codes describe the survey mark, not the tower's own condition.[^2]

No source we read describes a lookout tower at the present station, or whether the public may visit one there.
