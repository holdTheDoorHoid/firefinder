Old Glory Mountain, near Rossland in the Kootenay Boundary area of British Columbia, has a lookout listed at about 2,377 m (7,800 ft).[^1][^3] The B.C. Forest Service kept a fire lookout on the summit from 1926 to 1977.[^2] The original 1926 building was reconstructed in 1930.[^2] In 1963 it was demolished and replaced by the present structure, which sits on more secure foundations.[^2] The Rossland Museum gives about 1969 for the current structure.[^3] The board adds that lookouts of this kind were usually anchored to the mountain with cables, which kept them from being blown off.[^2]

In 1944 the lookoutman was Wilfred Gibbard, who had worked the post for seven seasons.[^1] His wife also acted as lookout during his early-morning supply trips, and their daughter had completed four summers there with her family.[^1] He kept a horse at the lookout, which took the load on his trips to the water hole and fuel supply.[^1] Early staff reached the lookout on foot or on horseback.[^2] The nearest car road was then about five miles away by trail.[^1]

By the mid-1960s the lookout was serviced by helicopter, and the telephone line to Rossland had given way to radio.[^2] The Forest Service retired the lookout in 1977.[^2] The board says that, in the memories of the Forest Service, it was not abandoned.[^2]

A separate weather station, built as a mountain observatory for Trans-Canada Airlines, opened on the summit on 1 October 1944.[^3] The Rossland Museum dates the fire that destroyed the station to 7 January 1968, while the 100 Mile Free Press gives 2 January.[^3][^4] The weather station was a separate structure from the lookout.[^3]

A two-year restoration of the lookout had begun by 19 August 2016.[^1] Volunteers were to do the work, partly financed by a grant, and a helicopter was needed to carry materials to the site.[^1] The Rossland Museum says the Friends of the Rossland Range restored the lookout in 2016/2017.[^3] The museum page also links to an account of repainting the lookout.[^3] I found no source saying whether visitors can reach the summit today.
