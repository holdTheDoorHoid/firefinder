The Hartshorn lookout tower stands on the south side of Highway 394, in the Sunklands Conservation Area of Shannon County, between the communities of Hartshorn and Akers.[^2] The records disagree about when it went up. The Forest Lookouts page says a towersite on the Deer Run State Forest was acquired in fiscal 1947-48 and that a 60-foot steel tower was constructed in 1950.[^1] A survey of the tower, which draws on MDC files, says the site was bought in April 1945 and the tower was constructed in 1947.[^2]

A towerman was on duty by 1949.[^1] That March, district forester Lee Fine said his Hartshorn towerman had spotted a small blaze at 9 p.m. on a Monday.[^1] A report dated March 17, 1949, quoted on the page, said the fire had blackened a thousand acres of upper Current River country. It also said the blaze was apparently set in a line more than two miles long by arsonists on horseback, and that it raged for nearly a day before crews brought it under control.[^1] Two Conservation agents were investigating how it started.[^1]

Later work included an entrance road in fiscal 1950-51, a 2.5-mile access road in fiscal 1971-72, and new anchor bolts in 1974.[^1]

Today the Missouri Department of Conservation owns and maintains the tower.[^2] It is a galvanized steel frame, 60 feet tall, with a steel ladder and a pyramid roof.[^2] The survey calls its condition fair to good, with rust and some window openings missing glass.[^2] The survey judges it eligible for listing on historic and architectural grounds.[^2] The tower now shares its ground with a radio tower, and none of the other buildings once on the site are standing.[^2] The survey, undated in the copy read, says the tower is slated for demolition and recommends documenting it as mitigation.[^2]

The Sunklands Conservation Area is open to the public from 4:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m. daily.[^3]
