The Forest Service says the first lookout on Black Mountain went up in 1926, making it the third lookout built on the San Bernardino National Forest.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes that first structure differently: a California Region 5 Plan 4AR cabin on a 10-foot open timber tower, built in the late 1910s or early 1920s.[^2] Both sources say the original was moved to Barton Peak in the mid-1930s, but the register gives 1934 and the Forest Service gives 1935.[^1][^2]

The cab that stands on the summit now was completed in 1962.[^1][^2] According to the register, the summit cab is a steel CL-100 design with a level roof of corrugated sheet metal, a mesh walkway, and a base resting on solid rock.[^2] The Barton Peak site was discontinued once the new lookout was finished.[^2]

A May 1, 1928 announcement, quoted on a lookout history site, said Rita Morris of Hemet would be on duty at the Black Mountain station during the coming summer, and called her the first woman lookout in Southern California.[^3] A 1929 item says she had again gone on duty there.[^3]

The register says the cab stayed in service through the 1980s, after which the Forest Service and Lookout Host volunteers restored it.[^2]

Today the lookout is staffed by volunteers during fire season, usually open from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., with a season that generally runs from Memorial Day to November.[^1] The register says Lookout Hosts staff it seven days a week, which is a different schedule from the Forest Service's fire-season hours, so check before you go.[^2] From Boulder Basin Campground, the walk is a mile round trip along a dirt road; the Black Mountain Trail is the longer alternative.[^1] The Forest Service page currently reads "Closed for the season," so confirm it is open before setting out.[^1]

The Southern California Mountains Foundation says the San Bernardino National Forest's seven fire lookouts rely on volunteers and welcome visitors from spring through fall.[^4]
