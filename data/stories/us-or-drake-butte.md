Drake Butte sits in the Maury Mountains of Crook County, on the Ochoco National Forest.[^1][^3] Before 1930 it was called Tamarack Point.[^3]

The lookout began as a cabin, not a tower. A July 1929 newspaper item said Ira Swift and the lookout-fireman, George J. Roba, would move a fireman's cabin from the Pine Creek ranger station to the top of the butte, and that a wooden tower would be built as well.[^3] By September the old cabin had been torn down and moved up, and the local paper hoped it would make a fair lookout station, with a tower to go over it the next year.[^3]

The tower came a year later.[^3] A September 1930 paper reported a 40-foot lookout tower under construction, because the lookout had been relying on a tree to get high enough to see its territory.[^3] A November 1930 item said Swift and Roba had completed a 40-foot wood tower built of round timber, with a frame house on top.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both give 1929 and a 40-foot pole tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, so the sources give different years for the tower itself.[^1][^2][^3]

Al Frey was stationed there in the summer of 1931.[^3] A December 1931 item says he completed the tower that season and that its interior, painting and lightning protection were done.[^3] The latest dated entry on the Forest Lookouts page is from July 1942.[^3] It reports that the lookout, Robert Brink, left his station to report to a Marine training base.[^3]

The register says the tower has been removed, and firelookout.com says it was long since removed. Neither gives a date.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page does not say when it came down either.[^3]

No source describes what remains on the butte today, or whether it is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
