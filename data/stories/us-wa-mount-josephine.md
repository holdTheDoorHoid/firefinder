The Washington Forest Fire Association worked with the Civilian Conservation Corps to put up a lookout tower on Mount Josephine in 1933.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb describes a pole tower standing 55 feet high, topped by an 8-by-8-foot cab.[^4] Firetower.org and firelookout.com instead date the first tower to 1938, so the sources disagree on the year.[^1][^2]

In 1942 the tower was used to spot aircraft for the Aircraft Warning Service, which was active there from March 8, 1942 to October 16, 1943.[^3][^4] An Aircraft Warning Service document lists Harry Osborne as warden in 1943.[^4]

In 1956 a woman from Lyman, Maxine Meyers, replaced a man who did not finish the season. She kept watch alone through October.[^5]

WillhiteWeb says the Department of Natural Resources replaced the tower in 1958. Firetower.org and firelookout.com put the replacement in 1959.[^4][^1][^2] Forest Lookouts quotes a DNR biennial report for 1958-60, which says the department's own carpentry crew completed nine lookouts in the past two years, one of them at Mount Josephine.[^3]

The 40-foot replacement tower was destroyed in 1981.[^1][^2][^4]

Getting to the top is the hard part. WillhiteWeb says open roads cross the south side of the mountain, which is Harry Osborne State Forest, but the final road to the summit runs over private timberland on the back side. The guide advises driving as far up as the roads allow and walking from there, because access at the boundary is usually blocked.[^4] It gives a hiking distance of probably 2.5 miles and says the road hike on the back side is part of the Pacific Northwest Trail.[^4] Its maps were good for access in 2020, and roads change often.[^4]

In May 2023 the Washington State Department of Natural Resources said it was temporarily closing the Harry Osborne State Forest to vehicle access, though walk-in dispersed recreation would stay open.[^6] Check current notices before you go, since that announcement dates from 2023.

None of the sources we read describes what, if anything, remains at the summit today.
