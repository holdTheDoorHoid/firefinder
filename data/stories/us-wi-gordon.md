The register describes this tower as a 100-foot Aermotor, likely an MC-39 model, first built in August 1935 at an elevation of about 736 feet, and later moved to its present spot on a date the register does not give.[^1] Other accounts also give 1935 as the year it went up.[^2][^3][^4]

The register notes a Forest Service-style stairway whose treads and landings were later upgraded from wood to expanded steel.[^1] The tower was staffed on and off during spring fire season until 2015.[^1] That year the Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources began decommissioning its towers, and this one was set to be torn down.[^2][^3]

Local citizens and a nonprofit stepped in to buy it instead.[^3] The Friends of the Bird Sanctuary now owns the site, and it held several fundraisers to buy the land.[^3][^2] A Central US Lookouts entry dated September 2020 says a cabin had been moved to the site.[^5] A 2021 news report adds that the cabin was donated and brought in from a nearby lake.[^3]

The tower and cabin opened for rentals through Airbnb in 2021.[^3] The nightly fee started at $67 for a one-room cabin with a composting toilet and no running water.[^3]

The Friends group published open-climb dates for 2022, and one report describes every other Sunday afternoon from May through October, when the public can climb for free.[^2][^4] The sources found do not show whether that schedule still runs. According to volunteers, the cab was left as the last lookout left it, with a phone, compass and stove still inside.[^4] A 2022 visit report on the register says the condition was good and that the tower is sometimes staffed.[^1]
