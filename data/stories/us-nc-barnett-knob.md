Barnett Knob's tower went up in 1932 on the Qualla Boundary, part of the Cherokee Indian Reservation beside the Great Smoky Mountains National Park.[^1] An August 1932 newspaper report said the crew had finished the foundation and set two of six planned eight-foot steel sections, and that a cabin for the keeper was still to be built.[^2] The register gives an 80-foot metal tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, while the hiking guide gives 60 feet, so the sources disagree on the height.[^1][^3]

The 1932 report also expected a Cherokee man to be placed in the lookout, though no one had been appointed yet.[^2] By March 1933 the regular warden, a Cherokee man named Crow, was ill, and another man stood in until he recovered.[^2]

The register calls the tower the last active lookout on the reservation when it was added to the register on October 31, 2006.[^1] The hiking guide says the tower gave views of forest fires until the early 2000s.[^3] The register's word "active" and the guide's early-2000s end date do not clearly match, and I found no firmer date for the end of the fire watch.[^1][^3]

The tower still stands, and the hiking guide says its use for communications has kept it in very good condition.[^3] Nearby antenna towers and tall trees make the views from the stairwell poor.[^3] The cab stays locked, but the stairs still let visitors see past the treeline.[^3] The register lists the Bureau of Indian Affairs as administrator.[^1]

The hike is 1.2 miles round trip from a gate on Barnett Fire Tower Road, also called BIA 406, beside the Blue Ridge Parkway.[^3] This stretch of the parkway is often shut in winter, so look up its closure map before setting out.[^3] The land is tribal, and only the hiking guide describes public access, so confirm the visitor rules before you go.[^1][^3]
