The Green Knob Lookout stands on a ridge in the Pisgah National Forest, not far from Mount Mitchell and a short walk from the Blue Ridge Parkway.[^1][^3][^5] Its 32-foot steel tower carries a wooden cab and catwalk, in a steep pyramid-roof style that the Register says was once typical of lookouts in the South's national forests.[^1] The Register places it in Yancey County, but a North Carolina tower list puts it in McDowell County.[^1][^6]

Sources disagree on the year it went up. The Register credits the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) and the Forest Service with building it in 1932.[^1] The TEHCC trail wiki credits the CCC alone, with the same year.[^2] Romantic Asheville and CNY Hiking say the Forest Service built it in 1931.[^3][^4]

Two blogs say the lookout was staffed through the 1970s, with a person living in the cab to watch for forest fires.[^3][^4] The undated Register text says volunteers staff it occasionally.[^1] We found no record of a notable fire tied to this lookout.

The tower was repaired in 1996, and the Register also records a restoration that year.[^1][^3] Romantic Asheville says the cab was restored in 2013 and that the tower was at risk of being destroyed several times.[^3] CNY Hiking says the tower still needs rehabilitation, but gives no date for the work.[^4]

Today the top of the tower is not open to visitors.[^3] Romantic Asheville says the National Forest Service opens the cab only for special tours, and that the staircase still offers a fine view.[^3] One trail starts at the Green Knob Overlook at Blue Ridge Parkway milepost 350.4 and is about half a mile long.[^2][^3] The Forest Service describes its Green Knob Trail as very steep and rocky, reaching the tower after 2.8 miles, with some minor scrambling.[^5] That Forest Service trail page also shows a "Temporarily closed due to storm damage" notice, with no date and no stated scope.[^5] Please keep off the locked cab.
