The Fern Lake lookout stood in Nacogdoches County, about five miles south-southwest of the city of Nacogdoches.[^1] The Central US Lookouts site reproduces a Coast and Geodetic Survey description dated 1949, which describes a steel tower already standing at the spot.[^1]

That description calls it a four-legged steel structure with a cab on top, and gives its overall height as 110 feet. The surveyors sighted on the apex of the roof.[^1] For background, a Texas column on East Texas fire lookout towers says spotters first climbed trees, a method it calls seldom effective, and that the Forest Service then began building metal lookout towers at strategic locations in the forests.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the Fern Lake tower as gone.[^4]

A National Geodetic Survey listing of control points without published datasheets gives this mark two entries, coded XX and NN. In that listing, X means a surface mark reported destroyed, and N means no geodetic control.[^3]

The Central US Lookouts page says the tower has been removed and its foundation blocks remain.[^1] That is the only description we found of what is at the site today. No source we read says whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
