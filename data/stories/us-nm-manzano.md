Manzano Lookout stands in Bernalillo County, and the National Historic Lookout Register places it on Cibola National Forest land.[^1] The Register says it was built for the Department of Defense, and that Kirtland Air Force Base sits just west of it.[^1] The Register gives no construction date, and the sources read do not supply one.[^1]

The Register's tentative description is a 30-foot open steel tower with K-brace framing and an L-4 cab at the top.[^1] It rates the structure in poor condition.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list shows the tower as standing.[^2]

An archive record for a 2002 cultural survey of Forest Service land withdrawn to Kirtland and the Department of Energy carries the site-type keyword "Manzano Lookout Tower." The keyword ties the tower to that archaeology work, but the record gives no date for the tower.[^3]

The Register listed the lookout on July 7, 2022, as US 1652, NM 51.[^1] The sources read say nothing about who staffed the tower, what fires it watched, or whether it was ever closed.

Today the tower is standing in poor condition, close to a military base.[^1][^2] A 2003 archive record says Forest Service land in the Manzanita Mountains was withdrawn to Kirtland and the Department of Energy.[^3] The sources give no visitor rule for the lookout. Stay clear of military land, and do not climb a tower that is closed or unsafe. Check with the Cibola National Forest and Kirtland Air Force Base before any visit.[^1][^3]
