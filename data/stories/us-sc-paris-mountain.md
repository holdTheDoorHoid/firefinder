The South Carolina Forestry Commission's 1937–38 report lists Paris Mountain among seven new steel fire towers erected that year.[^1] A Greenville News item dated February 20, 1938, which Forest Lookouts quotes, described the tower as still under construction. It was to stand 80 feet high on the mountain's northeast corner, and Civilian Conservation Corps enrollees from the Paris Mountain camp were doing the work.[^1]

A 1936–37 guide to forestry activities in N.C., S.C. and Tenn., quoted on the same page, already places a fire tower at the top of the mountain.[^1] The sources do not say whether an earlier structure stood there before the 1938 tower.

The commission's 1938–39 report says a four-room dwelling, barn, smokehouse, chicken house and latrine were built at six tower sites, Paris Mountain among them.[^1]

The tower went to a state surplus auction in December 2018.[^2][^3] WYFF reported that someone had become its new owner.[^3] Forest Lookouts describes it as shuttered, gives the price as $3,300, and says the buyers had about seven days to remove it.[^1] Fox News gave the deadline as 10 days and said the high bid stood at $525 by early Saturday.[^2] Whoever won also had to load the structure and haul it away.[^2] Fox also calls the steel a 72-year-old structure, which would make it about 80 years old if it went up in 1938.[^1][^2] The two accounts give different removal deadlines, seven days and 10, and neither explains the gap.[^1][^2]

A National Register nomination for the park's historic district describes a Civilian Conservation Corps road to "the old fire tower site."[^4] A trail listing still names a Fire Tower Trail inside the park.[^5] No source we read says whether the steel still stands or where it went, or whether visitors can reach the site today.
