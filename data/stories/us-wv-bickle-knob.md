Bickle Knob sits in Randolph County, West Virginia, at 4,012 feet, inside the Monongahela National Forest.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1933 as the year the tower was built.[^1] Forest Lookouts credits that work to Civilian Conservation Corps labor, citing a USDA Forest Service history of the Monongahela National Forest from 1915 to 1990.[^2]

A 1934 Charleston Gazette item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, placed the tower among a group of similar towers built to guard the forest from fire.[^2] The sources do not describe the early crew or how the tower was run day to day.

A 2017 visit report on the Register marks the tower as not staffed.[^1] The Gazette-Mail says the tower was decommissioned in the 1960s.[^3]

The fire observer's cab has since been removed.[^3] A Gazette-Mail article describes a wooden viewing platform with railings in its place.[^3] The Register describes the same change as an open viewing deck.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, with its cab no longer in place.[^4]

Today the platform is open for public use, with 360-degree views over the Shavers Fork watershed and the Otter Creek Wilderness.[^3][^1] A Forest Service sign along Stuart Memorial Drive marks the approach.[^3] Wikipedia says the tower is not handicapped-accessible, so visitors with mobility needs should check before they visit.[^5] The tower was registered on January 3, 2015, with Brian Powell as nominator.[^1]

The sources do not agree on the elevation. The Gazette-Mail gives 4,004 feet for the observation tower, while the Register gives 4,012 feet.[^3][^1]
