The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 40-foot stone tower on Rams Head in Medford. The register dates its operation to 1916, with use ending after 1918.[^1] It gives the site an elevation of 120 feet.[^1]

The hill lies in Lawrence Woods, an isolated part of the Middlesex Fells.[^2] A visitor guide says the woods are named after a military general and the first mayor of Medford, who owned the land and let the public use it before it joined the park.[^2] The guide credits him with building an observation tower on the hill, which it says was used as a fire lookout before it was dismantled.[^2]

The guide puts the hill at about 215 feet above sea level. The register's 120 feet for the site does not match that, and the sources do not say whether the two figures measure the same thing.[^1][^2]

Today the tower survives only as remains on the hill.[^2] The guide describes the remaining stairs, calls the hike up Rams Head easy, and mentions some views over the surrounding cities and terrain. A 2015 blog post reached what it calls the Ramshead former tower site on a short hike.[^3] Parking is generally limited, and visitors may need to find street parking in neighborhoods to the south.[^2]
