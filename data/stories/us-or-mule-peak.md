Mule Peak Lookout stands in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest, about 18 miles east-southeast of Union, Oregon.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the peak was picked for fire detection in 1916, when it was staffed and used heliograph signals.[^1] A lookout history page that quotes a 1916 newspaper item places the heliograph on Granite Butte instead, so the early set-up is not fully settled.[^2]

The current cab is a D-5 wooden ground house. The register says it may date to as early as 1924, and the Oregon Lookouts history states that a ground cabin was built that year.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com gives the same design and the year 1924, with a question mark.[^3]

Staffing has been seasonal. Firelookout.com says the cab was occasionally staffed in summer and names a lookout, Judy Wing, who worked there for several years in the 1980s.[^3] A September 2025 trip report says the writers found the lookout shut for the season when they arrived, and that an attendant who had been on site only a few days that season was closing it up for winter. The writer heard that the lookout is still actively staffed, not just in emergencies.[^4] Cheryl Hill's standing-lookouts list marks the lookout as unmanned, which conflicts with that report.[^5]

The lookout has been close to fire more than once. A report from July 1926 says a fire on Buck Creek was reported from the lookout station, with no serious damage yet.[^2] In July 2005 two employees were flown out by helicopter as flames threatened, firefighters protected the building with a fire-resistant covering as a precaution, and an aircraft dropped retardant to protect it.[^2] In July 2007 a Forest Service worker was flown out by helicopter from the historic lookout during a fast-moving wildfire.[^2]

The cab is still standing. The register notes that the cab received a cedar shake roof and was repainted in 2021, and the lookout was registered on July 5, 2022, as US 1636.[^1] Published elevations disagree: the register gives 8,395 feet, the trip report 8,554 feet, and firelookout.com 8,679 feet.[^1][^3][^4]

Today visitors reach Mule Peak on foot. The 2025 trip report describes a backpacking route that starts at the Buck Creek trailhead, where a Forest Service campground sits, and it warns that the road in has hazards.[^4] None of the sources consulted says whether visitors may enter the cab, so treat the inside as off-limits unless the Forest Service says otherwise.
