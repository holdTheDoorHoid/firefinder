Bachelor Butte is now called Mount Bachelor, a peak in Deschutes County, Oregon.[^1][^2] The Deschutes National Forest put a lookout on its summit.[^1] The register gives the summit as 9,065 feet, or 2,763 meters.[^1] One firelookout.com page gives 4,273 feet, but a 1932 newspaper report gives 9,065 feet, so we follow the register.[^3][^4]

A new station on the summit was first reported in June 1917, when a paper said it would be set up as soon as men could be employed for the posts.[^4] The Deschutes inventory lists a 1917 telephone and observation post.[^5] Work on the cabin began in June 1922, when lumber started moving up to the summit.[^4] The register dates the cabin to about 1922, and firelookout.com says about 1921.[^1][^3] They describe it as a D-6 cupola cabin.[^1][^3][^5] The 1922 paper said the cabin would have glass on every side, so a seated observer could scan the surrounding country.[^4]

J.B. Gauldin, who had run the Pine Mountain lookout the year before, was to take charge of the new station once it was finished.[^4]

Ice and storms were hard on the cabin.[^4][^5] A 1930 note called for replacing the present building, which had been battered by storms for several years.[^4] The inventory says ice severely damaged the cabin in 1930, and a 14-by-14 ground cabin went up in 1931.[^5] A 1933 newspaper refers to the station as constructed in 1931.[^4] The sources disagree on the new cab's type. The register says it was a Region-5 cab, firelookout.com says an L-4 cab, and the inventory calls it an L-4-31 ground cabin.[^1][^3][^5]

Henry Tiedman, a University of Oregon student, was hired for the summer to protect the forest from fires.[^4] Billy Catlow was the forest service lookout in 1932, when lightning bombarded the exposed summit and a bolt struck close by.[^4] In 1937 Bob Sharp looked out over central Oregon from the station.[^4] In 1938 a plane dropped supplies to the lookout, which the paper called the first time a plane had been used in the district for that.[^4]

Damage and weather took their toll.[^4] A 1933 newspaper blamed climbers and said snow and rain had warped the floor.[^4] In 1939 a Bend paper said the summit was little used because visibility was poor and access was difficult.[^4] The register says the cab was abandoned that year.[^1]

The register and firelookout.com both say the cab has since been removed.[^1][^3] The inventory gives no date, only "Removed: ? Gone".[^5]

Wikipedia lists the easiest route up the mountain as ski lifts.[^2] None of the sources we read describe what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit.
