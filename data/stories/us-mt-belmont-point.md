Belmont Point is a lookout site on a peak in the Lolo National Forest, about nine miles southwest of Seeley Lake.[^1][^2] The firelookout.com listing says a 30-foot pole tower of the L-4 type went up there in 1932.[^1]

In August 1937 a Billings Gazette report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, named Dale White of Missoula as the 24-year-old Forest Service lookout on Belmont Peak.[^2] White reported that a man he had tried to arrest for a game violation cut him across the face with a knife.[^2]

The firelookout.com listing says the tower was destroyed in 1957.[^1] Forest Lookouts marks the site as removed, with no date.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list shows the tower as gone.[^3] The sources do not agree on how the tower ended, but all three agree it is no longer standing.[^1][^2][^3]

We found no source describing what, if anything, remains on the peak today, or whether the public may visit.[^1][^2]
