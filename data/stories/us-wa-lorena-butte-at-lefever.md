Lorena Butte, near Goldendale in Klickitat County, Washington, has carried a lookout since 1937, when the first known structure there was an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] In 1954 a Washington Department of Natural Resources (DNR) cab went up atop an eight-foot rock formation, and the site replaced the nearby Simcoe Butte lookout.[^1] The cab in use today was built in 1974 as a live-in DNR cab, 14x14, on a ten-foot concrete slab at the summit.[^1][^3]

The cab was last staffed in 1993, before it moved.[^1][^2] Since the move, people have sometimes staffed it at the new site when fire danger ran high, most notably in July 2013, when a lightning storm passed over the area.[^1]

The cab was moved in 2006 from the summit down a ridge to the southwest, because of local mining operations. The 1974 concrete slab stayed behind.[^1][^2] Sources disagree on the year. An NHLR photo caption says 2005, and a Forest Lookouts history says 2007.[^1][^3] Firelookout.org marks the original site "Gone" and the relocated cab "Standing".[^5]

Today the cab sits on private land owned by the Lefever Holbrook Ranch, which rents it out nightly.[^1] The NHLR entry registered it on April 12, 2022, as US 1549, WA 79.[^1] TrailChick dates the start of rentals to 2021.[^2] An Airbnb listing describes a rustic stay with access to the tower and grounds, no water or electricity on site, and a dirt road to the top with one steep section.[^4] Anyone curious should arrange a visit through the owners rather than go up the ranch land uninvited.
