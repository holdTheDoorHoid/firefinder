Santa Ynez Peak stands about 4,300 feet high in the Los Padres National Forest in Santa Barbara County.[^1] A lookout went up there in 1934.[^1][^2] Peakbagging dates the tower to 1935, so the sources differ by a year.[^4] The cabin sat on an open steel tower, 30 feet tall and K-braced.[^1][^2] The register calls the cab a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 design, while Carey calls it a classic C3 cab.[^1][^2]

A Forest Service lookout, Ethel Wheeler, was watching over the peak in August 1955, according to a newspaper item.[^3] The Refugio Fire started in the early hours of September 6, 1955, close to the top of Refugio Pass.[^5] The Santa Ynez lookout heard about it through the Cachuma station, which then alerted the Los Prietos ranger station.[^5] The Santa Barbara Independent's history of the fire says the notification delay cost precious minutes.[^5]

The tower is gone.[^1][^2] The register gives no removal date, and Peakbagging's author also does not know when it came down.[^1][^4]

Carey's 2013 post says communications arrays now mostly occupy the site.[^2] Peakbagging includes directions for driving to the site, but none of the sources read says whether visitors may go up today.[^4]
