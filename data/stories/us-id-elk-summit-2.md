Elk Summit Lookout sits on a summit about six miles northwest of Elk City, Idaho, in the Nez Perce National Forest.[^1][^4] The summit has been a lookout site since 1911, when a crow's nest observation point was set up there.[^1][^3] In 1934 a 100-foot steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab went up on the summit, and that is the tower standing there today.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] According to the Idaho Fire Lookouts site, no other steel lookout survives on the Nez Perce.[^3]

Newspaper items quoted on a local history page offer a few glimpses of early staffing.[^5] A Clearwater Republican item dated June 12, 1914, says A. Rogers had set out for his lookout on fire patrol.[^5] A June 25, 1915, item reports that a bear entered the government rangers' cabin at Elk Summit and destroyed the supplies kept there for the rangers.[^5] The Idaho Fire Lookouts site says its records do not yet include a staffing history for the tower.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on August 25, 1997, as US 233.[^1][^3] The register's own entry says the Elk City Ranger District still uses the tower when needed.[^1] A Forest Service page last updated June 4, 2025, reports that the tower remains intact but is not in use by the agency at present.[^2] The two accounts do not match, and nothing gathered here settles which one describes the tower today.[^1][^2]

The Forest Service says the tower is not accessible to the public.[^2] The log cabin at its base may be added to the agency's cabin rental program later, according to the same page.[^2] Snowmobilers frequently stop at the site to rest.[^2]
