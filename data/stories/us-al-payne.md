The register places the Payne Lookout Tower in the Talladega National Forest in Hale County.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] OpenStreetMap records it as a lattice observation tower.[^2]

I could not find when it was built or who built it. The oldest dated reference I found is a 1980 USGS topographic map of Payne Lake.[^3] The listing for that map names the Payne Lookout Tower among the area's landmarks.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register added the tower on January 2, 2026, on the nomination of Brendon Minchew.[^1]

The register's description, current as of August 2025, says the tower is in great condition, with no vandalism seen and the stairs mostly intact. A razor-wire fence around the staircase appears to have helped keep it that way.[^1] The same entry adds that Forest Service volunteers at the Payne Lake Recreation Area did not know whether the tower was still standing, so its present state is less certain than the 2025 report suggests.[^1]

The tower is off an unmarked Forest Service road that cannot be driven, and the register describes a three-quarter-mile hike to reach it.[^1] I found no staffing history or notable fires in the sources I checked.

Please do not climb it. The stair enclosure is fenced with razor wire,[^1] and the stairs' condition is only known from the 2025 report. Check with the Forest Service before you plan a visit.[^1]
