## Double Arrow Lookout

Double Arrow Lookout sits on a low hill near Seeley Lake in the Lolo National Forest, Missoula County, Montana.[^1] The Forest Service built it as an L-4 cab on a 20-foot slanting pole tower; the National Historic Lookout Register dates the construction to 1932, while the current Recreation.gov listing and firelookout.com both give 1933.[^1][^2][^3] It was built before a road reached the site, meaning the materials had to be packed in rather than driven up.[^1]

Forest Service employees staffed the tower for decades. Recreation.gov says it was staffed until the mid-1980s, and the National Historic Lookout Register notes it has been used only on an emergency basis since 1989 — accounts that line up reasonably well, with the gap likely covering a short transition period.[^1][^2] Once regular staffing ended, the lookout found a second life as a recreational rental, now booked through Recreation.gov.[^2][^3]

The tower was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on February 26, 1996.[^1] It remains a popular spot with Seeley Lake valley residents simply as a viewpoint, even apart from its rental use.[^1]

Today the lookout still stands and is open to the public. The cabin is a 14-by-14-foot structure on its 20-foot tower, reached by an exterior stairway and surrounded by a covered catwalk.[^2] Inside there's electric light, heat, a cook stove and a small refrigerator, plus two platforms and two portable cots that sleep up to four people; an outdoor vault toilet sits a short walk away.[^2] The access road is steep dirt and gravel, and Recreation.gov recommends a high-clearance vehicle to reach it.[^2] From the catwalk, renters look out over the Seeley valley and the Swan Mountains beyond, the same view the lookout was built to watch for smoke.[^2]
