A steel fire lookout went up at Liberty Hill, in Kershaw County, South Carolina.[^1] A state forestry report covering October 1931 to September 1932 records a third association set up at Liberty Hill as a branch of the Kershaw County association, and an 80-foot (24 m) steel tower built there with a view over three counties.[^1]

A later report, covering July 1937 to June 1938, lists Liberty Hill as one of five tower sites that received a set of tower unit buildings: a four-room dwelling, a barn, a smokehouse, a chicken house and a latrine.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts site lists the tower under the South Carolina Forestry Commission.[^1] On October 1, 1993, the Commission shut down its fire-tower network, a system-wide closure that no source we found ties to this tower.[^2]

Today, OpenStreetMap, a volunteer map, marks a lattice observation tower at the site, at 173 m (568 ft) above sea level.[^3] No source we found describes the site now, or whether the public can reach it. Do not climb it unless a source says it is open.

Do not confuse this tower with the Liberty Hill Lookout in Edgefield County, a separate tower on the National Historic Lookout Register.[^4]
