Stony Hill is in Suffolk County, near Amagansett on eastern Long Island.[^1] Money to buy the tower came from the Town of East Hampton and from private subscribers, and the Conservation Department erected it in 1930.[^1] It became operational in 1931.[^1]

The NYSFFLA list and the Starr list both describe a 73-foot Aermotor LS-40 tower.[^1][^2] The register attaches the 73 feet to the concrete footings and describes a steel structure with wood steps and landing platforms.[^3]

In 1935 the observer, W.S. Miller, logged 43 fires and 261 visitors.[^4] In April and May 1936 he reported thirty-four fires.[^4] That spring the State reopened the tower and re-appointed Warden S. Miller as observer.[^4]

In October 1959 the State abandoned its lookout service to cut costs, and the title went to Suffolk County.[^4] The tower was closed in 1959.[^1] The NYSFFLA list credits a local demolition contractor with taking it down in 1960.[^1] The register credits the state Conservation Commission with the 1960 removal instead.[^3]

The register says the land is now privately held.[^3] The NYSFFLA list, the Starr list and the register all record the tower as removed.[^1][^2][^3] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site, or whether visitors may enter, so anyone curious about the spot should ask the landowner before going.
