In April 1939 a newspaper reported that a fire tower would be built near Cypress as part of the state's forest fire protection work.[^1] The planned tower was 100 feet high and was meant to watch privately owned timber between two federal forest purchase units in southern Illinois.[^1]

By 1957 a federal survey described the tower as standing on George Bluff, about three miles northwest of Belknap.[^1] It described a steel tower on four legs, with a platform enclosed in glass at the top, standing 100 feet tall on state property.[^1]

Sources disagree on the county. A heading on the Forest Lookouts page says Union County, but the survey record on the same page says Johnson County, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association list agrees with Johnson.[^1][^2]

The Forest Lookouts page marks the tower as removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association list marks it as gone.[^1][^2] A historian quoted in Illinois Country Living says nearly all of the state's towers were removed or sold off in the early 1970s.[^3]

The 1957 record places the site on state property.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower on George Bluff today, or whether the spot is open to visitors.[^1]
