Monkey Creek Ridge is a bald summit in Del Norte County, California.[^1][^2] A 1942 survey note places it about 19 miles northeast of Crescent City, while a 1952 note gives about 24 miles by airline.[^2] The survey station on its highest bald point was set at the apex of the lookout tower.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page lists the site under the Siskiyou National Forest, and then the Six Rivers National Forest.[^1] The page also quotes a history of the Siskiyou National Forest saying that a 10-foot round timber tower with a cab was built here in 1940.[^1]

A 1940 guard-placement plan called the view very good.[^1] It said the view took in several earlier fires and much of the Redwood Highway.[^1]

The sources do not agree on the tower's size. A 1942 station description gives a tower about 14 feet by 14 feet and 20 feet high.[^2] A 1952 recovery note describes a standard Forest Service lookout house about 14 feet square on legs about 15 feet high, with the top of the tower about 30 feet up.[^2] The 10-foot figure from the 1940 history matches neither survey note.[^1][^2] The 1942 description also says the station was about two and a half hours by pack from the Forest Service trail.[^2]

In August 1956 the lookout, Eddie Barnard, could not find water nearby.[^1] The Blue Lake Advocate reported that an airplane made the delivery, sending down four five-gallon cans of water by cargo chute.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as Gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the ridge today, and we found nothing on public access. Ask the national forest's ranger office before planning a visit.
