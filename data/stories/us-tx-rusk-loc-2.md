The Forest Lookouts site reprints a June 1939 newspaper item that calls a Rusk tower Tower No. 9 and places it a mile and a half northwest of town. The page is headed Cherokee County under the Texas Forest Service, which owned the fire lookout towers named in a later sale notice.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists two Rusk towers in Cherokee County, Loc 1 and Loc 2, and marks both as gone.[^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts page describes one Rusk tower with no location tag, so its 1939 and 1946 reports may refer to either site.[^1]

The 1946 reports name a forest patrolman working from the tower. J. Layton Jones handled three of the county's nine forest fires in January, and fourteen of the 34 fires recorded in March.[^1]

A notice dated January 15, 1993 invited sealed bids to buy, take apart and haul away Texas Forest Service lookout towers. It names only a Rusk Fire Lookout Tower in Cherokee County, with no site number, so it does not show which of the two Rusk towers was on the list.[^1]

A map database lists a feature called Rusk Lookout Tower at about the Loc 2 coordinates, but its entry gives only the type, Tower, with no detail about the structure.[^4] No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
