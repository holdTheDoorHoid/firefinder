Quartz Mountain Lookout Site sits in Kittitas County, about ten miles southwest of Cle Elum, in the Wenatchee National Forest.[^1][^2]

The first lookout here was built in 1929 as a platform tower with a tent camp.[^1][^4] In 1933 a steel tower replaced it.[^1][^2] The register and FireLookout.com give that tower's height as 45 feet.[^1][^2] Willhite's guide and a 1937 newspaper give 40 feet.[^3][^4]

In 1937 William Hutchins was the lookout.[^3] A Wenatchee Daily World article from that year, quoted on Forest Lookouts, says he had spent eight summers at the station, and that his family lived in a cabin near the tower.[^3]

In May 1938 plans were approved for a combined garage and woodshed.[^3] That same year the lookout moved to a new 40-foot pole L-4 tower, set east of the 1933 site, on the true summit, among trees.[^1][^4]

The L-4 was removed in 1967.[^1][^4] FireLookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2]

In August 1973 the Forest Service put out a 20-by-20-foot fire at the old lookout, according to an Ellensburg paper quoted on Forest Lookouts.[^3]

Today the summit is a drive-up spot.[^4] Willhite's guide says most vehicles can reach the top, and a campground just below the summit still exists, though it is much smaller.[^4] The forested area at the true summit holds communications towers, and that is where the 1938 lookout stood.[^4] Willhite's photo captions describe the 1933 site with its footings nearly gone.[^4]

Access is by Forest Service roads from Ellensburg (exit 109) or Cle Elum.[^4] The last three miles of gravel have potholes.[^4] No lookout tower has stood on the summit since 1967.[^1][^4]
