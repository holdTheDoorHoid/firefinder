The Brushy Mountain lookout site lies in Monroe County, West Virginia, at about 3,191 feet (973 meters).[^1] The West Virginia Division of Forestry once ran the tower, but the register now lists a private owner in its place.[^1]

Observers were named for the tower in 1957, and one was there in 1960.[^2] A newspaper item from October 1957, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said observers for the district had been chosen to go on duty when hunting season started, and it named L.E. Long for Brushy Mountain.[^2] Three years later another item reported that all District Four towers were now staffed, and it placed Ray Collins of Rupert on Brushy Mountain.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register listed the site on January 26, 2017, on the nomination of Brian M. Powell.[^1] The sources differ slightly on the county. The register says Monroe, while the Forest Lookouts page heads its entry Greenbrier and Monroe counties. The 1957 item places its observer in Greenbrier.[^1][^2]

The statewide lookout list on firelookout.org marks the tower as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today. The register names a private owner, and we found no source saying whether visitors may go onto the land, so ask the owner before going there.[^1]
