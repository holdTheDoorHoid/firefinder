Pharaoh Mountain rises in the Pharaoh Lake Wilderness in Essex County.[^1] Older sources spell it "Pharoah."[^2] In 1909 state fire officials decided to add a lookout there, and in April 1910 a fire observation station opened on the mountain.[^2][^1] With no trees to block the view, no tower was needed at first; the observer used a canvas tarp on a wooden pole frame for shelter from bad weather.[^1]

A 35-foot steel tower went up in 1918, and the sources disagree on the year.[^1][^2] A March 1992 newspaper clipping says it was erected in 1917.[^2] A 1943 Coast and Geodetic Survey description puts its height at about 40 feet.[^2]

Fire watching ended in 1987.[^1] Vandals cut two of the tower's legs in fall 1990, and DEC repaired the damage.[^2]

In 1989 an Adirondack Park Agency plan called for man-made structures to be removed from the wilderness area that year.[^2] Bills by two state legislators to save the tower were not enough, a town supervisor said.[^2]

In September 1992 DEC crews airlifted the tower's debris out by helicopter and took down the observer's cabin and outbuildings, citing hiker safety.[^2] Wikipedia gives vandal damage that could not be repaired as one reason for removal.[^1]

Today the summit lies in a wilderness area that is open year-round at no charge, and the Pharaoh Mountain Trail climbs to it.[^3] The state's lookout list marks the tower as gone.[^4] None of the sources read says what, if anything, is left on the summit.[^1][^2][^3]
