An online list of Georgia lookout towers gives the name Cohutta to a Murray County tower at 2,411 ft (734.9 m) elevation.[^1] The same list's Murray entry sits about 9 km (5.5 mi) west-southwest of that point, and its coordinates match the Murray Fire Tower in the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1][^2]

The register places that tower on top of Cohutta Mountain in Fort Mountain State Park.[^2] It says the metal frame still stands but the cab is gone, and it calls the structure the lost second tower of Fort Mountain.[^2] The register gives an elevation of 2,760 ft (841 m) and names the Georgia Department of Natural Resources as the administering agency.[^2] It records the tower as registered on January 8, 2021.[^2] An Eastern US Lookouts page also lists a Cohutta Mountain entry in the park.[^3]

The Cohutta point and the register's tower are about 9 km apart, so the sources disagree on where the Cohutta name belongs. This story follows the register.[^1][^2]

The park's stone lookout is a separate structure from the frame on Cohutta Mountain.[^2][^4] The Civilian Conservation Corps built a stone fire tower that stands on Fort Mountain's summit.[^4] The Murray County Museum's timeline says the first 150 CCC men reached the Holly Creek camp in 1933 and began work on the park's cabins, roads, dam, two fire towers and lake.[^5] A 2025 Georgia Trend feature says the stone tower gave rangers a view of fires as far as 40 miles (64 km) off.[^6]

The upper wooden portion of the stone tower burned in 1971.[^6] Its condition stayed poor until 2014, when the state's Department of Natural Resources began a restoration.[^6] The article says the $189,000 restoration reopened the tower about a decade before it was written.[^6]

The register does not say whether the metal frame is open to climbing, so visitors should not assume it is.[^2] Fort Mountain State Park also offers a mountain lake, cabins, campsites and other public amenities.[^4] No source we read describes what stands at the listed Cohutta point today.
