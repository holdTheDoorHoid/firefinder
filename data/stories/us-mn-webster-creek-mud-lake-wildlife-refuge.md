The refuge that became Agassiz National Wildlife Refuge was set up in 1937 as the Mud Lake Migratory Waterfowl Refuge, in eastern Marshall County.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association files this lookout under the name Webster Creek (Mud Lake Wildlife Refuge).[^3]

The county survey lists a Mud Lake Wildlife Refuge lookout that was built in 1939 and owned by the United States Fish & Wildlife Service.[^2] Its 1941 survey notes describe a wood tower with an enclosed crow's nest, 110 feet high.[^2] A note in the Agassiz entry of the same survey gives 1938 as the year it went up, calls it about 100 feet tall and of wood, and says other records describe the surviving tower as a 100-foot steel one.[^2]

The county record says this tower was in the refuge when it was called Mud Lake Wildlife Refuge, and it points readers to the present tower at refuge headquarters.[^2] That present tower is a steel structure that the record dates to 1939 and says reopened in 2005.[^2] The index marks both the Mud Lake site and the headquarters tower as gone.[^3] For the headquarters tower, that conflicts with the county record, which says it is standing and open to the public.[^2]

A refuge brochure in the NPS History collection, labelled 2000, describes a 100-foot observation tower and says its use is permitted during the snow-free months and office hours, and that visitors need a key from the office.[^4] No source read describes what remains at the Mud Lake site today.[^3]
