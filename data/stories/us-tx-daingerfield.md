Daingerfield's lookout stood in Morris County on a wooded ridge about 1.5 miles northwest of town.[^1] A federal survey description written in 1958 calls it a four-legged steel tower about 100 feet tall. Its legs were fixed to concrete piers, glass windows ringed the top, and the color was gray.[^1]

In September 1970 a clipping from The Naples Monitor, reprinted on Forest Lookouts, reported that the Texas Forest Service had hired two Morris County natives and assigned them to the Daingerfield tower.[^1] The clipping says their main duty was spotting and putting out fires in Morris County and on adjacent land. They were to keep the suppression gear at home and stay on call around the clock.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas index lists the tower as gone.[^2] A Texas lookout list also places it in Morris County, tagged TFS.[^3]

The 1958 survey placed a survey mark 33 feet southwest of the tower's southwest leg, stamped with the year 1958.[^1] No source we read describes what remains at the site today, including whether that mark survives, or whether the public can visit.[^1][^2][^3]
