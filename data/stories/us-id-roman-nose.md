Roman Nose Lookout stood on a peak about 12 miles west-southwest of Bonners Ferry, in Boundary County, Idaho.[^1] A 2026 trip report places the peak in the Selkirk Mountains.[^2] It was a Kaniksu National Forest lookout.[^1][^3]

The mountain was developed in 1917 with a camp lookout, and it received a D-6 cupola cabin in 1923.[^1] In the 1930s a 30-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab was built.[^1]

The earliest person the sources name as a lookout is Pat Callahan, listed for 1932 and 1933.[^3] In 1947 a Sandpoint paper reported that the previous winter's ice load had badly damaged the tower, so a second pack trip was needed.[^4]

A 29-foot treated timber tower with an L-4 cab arrived in 1955, and the cab was later given a flat roof.[^1]

Newspaper accounts from September 1967 say the Sundance fire destroyed the lookout tower on the peak.[^4] The lookout, Randy Langston, got out of the tower just before it burned, sheltered among rocks, and was flown out by helicopter.[^4]

Other records keep the tower in use into the late 1970s.[^1][^3] The sources do not reconcile those records with the 1967 accounts.[^1][^3][^4]

A windstorm blew the tower over on October 31, 1999.[^1][^3]

A July 2026 trip report includes a photo in which the lookout's ruins are visible.[^2] The same report says the peak is now more often climbed from the lakes to the north than by the old lookout road along the south ridge.[^2] We found no source that says whether the site is closed, and none describes the ruins beyond that photo caption. Before going, check with the Forest Service, which manages this land.[^1]
