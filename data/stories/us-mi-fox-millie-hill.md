The state planned a fire tower for Iron Mountain, Michigan, in 1932, probably at the top of the mountain.[^2] In August that year the state conservation department announced a standard 102-foot tower.[^2] By September, work was underway on an automobile road to the site, a high point east of the old Walpole mine on Pewabic Hill.[^2] An October 1953 newspaper item mentions the fire tower on Pewabic Hill, where antenna tests were under way.[^2]

A survey description from 1954 covers a point called Fox RM 1.[^1] It describes the station as steel, standing on four legs over a concrete base, about 120 feet high, on a wooded hill about 1.3 miles east of Main Street.[^1] The survey page gives no coordinates for the station, so the point cannot be checked against the Millie Hill summit, whose coordinates MountainZone lists.[^1][^4] The 1932 and 1953 newspaper reports place the tower on Pewabic Hill, and none of these sources says whether that is the same hill.[^2] The heights also disagree, with 102 feet in the plan and about 120 feet in the survey.[^1][^2]

Millie Hill's summit is reached from Tower Drive today.[^3] A neighborhood website describes winding trails, scenic lookout points and wide views over Iron Mountain.[^3] No source we found says what, if anything, of the old tower remains.
