The Peach Lookout Tower is near Clanton in Chilton County, Alabama.[^1] The Alabama Forestry Commission built it in 1949 inside a peach orchard.[^1] It is a 100-foot Aermotor MC-39 tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] A 1819 News profile says the MC-39 was the most common steel lookout model in Alabama's forests, followed by the LS-40 version.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists the tower as US 815 and AL 16.[^1] It was registered on June 23, 2009, on the nomination of Thomas Kaufmann, and the site sits at 751 feet, or 229 meters.[^1]

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched. The 1819 News profile notes that when a lookout stopped being staffed, the structure stayed standing, and it gives the most common explanation for the decline: aircraft spotted fires more efficiently.[^2]

The Register does not say when the tower stopped being staffed, whether it still stands, or what condition it is in.[^1] It names the Alabama Forestry Commission as administrator.[^1] We found no statement on public access, so anyone interested should check with the agency before going near the site.

The same profile estimates that probably fewer than 75 historic lookouts still stand in Alabama.[^2]
