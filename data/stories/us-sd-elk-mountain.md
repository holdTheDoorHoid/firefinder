Elk Mountain stands in the Black Hills about half a mile east of the Wyoming border, in the southwest corner of South Dakota.[^1] The steel tower rises 67 feet to a 7-by-7-foot cab, though sources differ on the exact height, and one lookout history site gives 75 feet.[^1][^3][^4]

A July 1939 newspaper report says a steel tower had been placed on the mountain as a primary station and was manned for the first time that season.[^2] The Forest Service dates the lookout to between 1939 and 1941 and says a log cabin was built to go with it.[^1] The register instead credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building the tower in 1941.[^3]

A lookout history site lists named staff from 1941 to 1966, and a 1945 newspaper item mentions the lookout on Elk Mountain.[^4][^2]

In the early 2000s the Elk Mountain Fire burned 13,195 acres, taking the lookout's cabin, outhouse and most of the trees around it.[^1] Another lookout history site says the 2001 fire left only the steel tower standing.[^2] The register dates the cabin's loss to the late 1990s, which conflicts with the other sources.[^3] The Forest Service began replanting ponderosa pine in 2005.[^1] In 2011 the WhoopUp Fire burned across the mountain, and several retardant drops kept the tower standing.[^1][^2]

The register says the lookout still reports an average of eight to twelve fires a year.[^3] The Forest Service says the lookout is manned from late spring to early fall, and 90 steps lead up to the cab for visitors.[^1] The nearby Summit Ridge tower, built as a virtual twin of the original, is now unstaffed.[^1]

The register added the lookout on November 15, 1991, on nomination from the Elk Mountain Ranger District.[^3] Because it is staffed in season, check the Black Hills National Forest's current guidance before you go up.
