The Blue Mountain Fire Tower is in Hamilton County, New York, on Blue Mountain at Indian Lake.[^2][^1] The National Historic Lookout Register suggests it may be the best-known and most-visited fire tower in the Adirondacks.[^1]

The summit served as a land-survey station in 1870, and a wooden tower rose there in 1911.[^1] In 1917 the state built the present tower: a 35-foot Aermotor steel structure with a 7-by-7-foot steel cab.[^1][^2] The tower remained active until 1990, when the state closed it.[^1][^4] A brochure describes it as one of the last operating fire towers in New York at that point.[^4]

After a 1994 rehabilitation, volunteers from the Blue Mountain Fire Tower Committee staffed the tower.[^1] The station was added to the National Register of Historic Places on September 23, 2001.[^2][^3]

In fall 2017 the state cleared communications gear and a building from the tower’s base, and the tower was put back to its historical look.[^1] A 2018 visit report noted plans to repaint and redeck the tower that year.[^1]

Today the tower is listed as restored and open to the public.[^4] Under an agreement between the town and the state, a summit steward is hired each summer to staff the tower, share historical information and teach hikers.[^1] Visitors hike a marked trail to the tower, and a 2018 visit report found the trail well marked and the parking area large.[^1] The Adirondack region listing gives a trail of 4 miles round trip with about 1,560 feet of climbing.[^5]
