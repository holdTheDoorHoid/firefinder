The Verdi Peak Lookout site is on Verdi Peak in Sierra County, at 8,385 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says a lookout has been on the peak since at least 1939, and that the cab was built in 1983 to replace earlier structures.[^1] A 1986 survey called the cab a KEV, with no further detail.[^1]

A 1958 Reno newspaper profiled the staffer that year, Miss Lane Endicott, who worked the lookout by herself in her third season.[^2] She had ten fires to her credit that year[^2] and could see a circled area of 35 to 45 miles.[^2] Dry weather extended her season past the usual close in the first week of October.[^2]

The register says the lookout has not been in service since at least 1998, and its dated photos run from 1998 to 2017.[^1] The sources read do not say when the cab was last used or what condition it is in now.

Today the peak is reached by off-highway vehicle routes.[^3] The Forest Service calls the roads moderately difficult, and most are recommended for 4x4 only.[^3] One route continues up to the junction of the Tahoe and Toiyabe National Forests.[^3] The register names only the Tahoe as administrator.[^1]

Nothing I read says whether the cab is open to visitors, so this story does not suggest climbing or entering it.
