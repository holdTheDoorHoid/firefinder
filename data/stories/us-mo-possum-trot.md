Possum Trot was a fire-detection tower in the Winona Ranger District of Shannon County, Missouri.[^1][^2] Wikidata gives its elevation as 1,004 feet, about 306 meters.[^3] Fire towers were Missouri's chief means of detecting fires between the late 1930s and the 1960s.[^4]

By January 1937 the tower had been refloored, and a ply-board lining had been fitted beneath the windows.[^1] Later that year the Shannon County Democrat reported plans to move the tower to a new location, and the name Tram was expected for the new tower.[^1]

In March 1938 footings were poured on the Possum Trot site for a fifty-foot steel tower, and the paper expected construction to finish that week.[^1] A National Archives photograph dated 14 March 1938 is captioned as looking up at the towers at the Possum Trot site.[^2]

In April 1938 the paper reported that the Possum Trot tower had been taken down and would be set up again on Tram Ridge within days.[^1] A page about the Tram lookout says the structure then at Possum Trot was to be reused at the new site.[^5]

The sources do not settle whether a tower stood on the Possum Trot site after that move. A 1942 item from the same paper reports work on the Possum Trot tower.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site lists Possum Trot as removed.[^1]

No source I read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit it.[^1][^3] The Clark National Forest, named on the Forest Lookouts page,[^1] was combined with Mark Twain National Forest in 1973 and absorbed by it in 1976.[^6] Wikidata places the site inside Mark Twain National Forest.[^3]
