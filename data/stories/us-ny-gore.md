Gore Mountain, in Warren County, New York, got a fire lookout in 1909, when an 18-foot wooden tower was established there.[^1][^2] The tower standing there now is a 60-foot steel Aermotor LS40 tower.[^2] The Register calls it built in 1918. A state Conservation Commission report, quoted on a lookout history site, lists a 60-foot steel tower as purchased and erected under 1918.[^1][^2]

A hurricane blew the steel tower down in October 1919. The state's report for 1920 says it was reconstructed that year, and the Register says it was re-erected in 1920.[^1][^2] A 2020 newsletter from the New York chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association repeats the 1920 rebuild.[^3]

In January 1934 a parcel of 1,861 acres in the town of Johnsburg was acquired, and the purchase included the observation station on the summit.[^1] A newspaper item from that time ranks the station among the more significant ones in the lower Adirondacks and says many climbers come each year for the summit view.[^1] The sources read do not say who staffed the tower or what fires it watched.

The Register gives 1988 as the year the tower was closed. Wikipedia says it ended fire lookout work at the end of the 1988 season and was officially closed in early 1989.[^2][^4] The Register says the tower has since been adapted to hold a large amount of communications gear near the summit of the Gore Mountain Ski Complex.[^2] A Warren County emergency communications document lists the Gore Mountain summit as one of the county's four transmitter and receiver sites.[^5]

Today the tower is closed to visitors. The Register names the state Department of Environmental Conservation as administrator, with the Olympic Regional Development Authority as a cooperator.[^2] A June 2020 photo on the Register shows the tower, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list shows it as a standing tower.[^2][^6] Because it is closed to visitors, it should not be climbed.[^2]
