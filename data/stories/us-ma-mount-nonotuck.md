Mount Nonotuck is an 827-foot peak at the northern end of the Mount Tom Range, inside Mt. Tom State Reservation near Holyoke.[^1][^3] A steel lookout tower stands above the ruins of an old summit hotel, and the National Historic Lookout Register added it in May 2026.[^1]

A summit hotel called the Eyrie House opened on the mountain in 1861.[^3] A Mt. Tom history site lists a lookout tower among the hotel's amenities.[^2] A fire destroyed the complex in 1901, and the state reservation annexed the property in 1905.[^2] The Register says the fire that burned the hotel was started to cremate horses, and it says the present tower was not part of the hotel.[^1]

The Register describes the tower as steel with red paint, with a narrow base about eight feet square and a wide top platform that projects past the frame.[^1] The stairs to the top are steep, almost like a ladder, and a small derelict shed stands at its base.[^1] The Register says the tower is less well kept than its neighbors on Goat Peak and Bray Tower.[^1]

I could not find when the tower was built or who built it. The Register gives no build date, and the Wikipedia article on the mountain does not describe the tower.[^1][^3]

The Register says the tower is reached by a trail off Reservation Road.[^1] A seasonal auto road up the mountain is shut to vehicles for an open-ended period because of its condition, but hikers may still go up.[^3] I did not find a statement on whether the tower itself may be climbed, so ask the state reservation before going up, and do not climb without permission.
