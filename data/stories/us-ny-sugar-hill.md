The Sugar Hill Fire Tower stands in Schuyler County, New York, within the recreation area of Sugar Hill State Forest.[^1][^3] Civilian Conservation Corps crews erected the 67-foot-6-inch steel tower in 1941.[^1][^2] Wikipedia places the tower on Sproul Hill and says the name comes from the reforestation area the tower sits in.[^2]

Volunteers staffed the tower first, and the state took over later.[^1] The register gives 1951 as the end of volunteer staffing and 1979 as the end of state staffing.[^1] A hiking guide says the tower was used until the mid-1980s, when aerial detection had grown cheaper and more effective.[^3] Wikipedia says lookout operations ended after the 1988 season.[^2] New York began retiring its fire towers from spotting duty during the 1980s.[^4]

The tower was officially closed in early 1989.[^2] In 1990 the state shut down the final five towers still in use.[^4] A contributor's note on the NHLR page describes the 1941 observer's cabin beside the tower as in nearly mint condition and notes its original wrought iron door latch.[^1]

The Sugar Hill Recreation Area, off Tower Hill Road, has parking, flush toilets, a picnic area and the trailhead for the Six Nations trail system.[^3] A hiking guide says people may climb to a point just below the cabin, but a state notice on its fire tower page says the tower is currently closed to the public.[^3][^4]
