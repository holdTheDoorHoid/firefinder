Flattop Mountain is a broad summit in Skamania County, in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest. It held two lookout sites, one on the east end and one on the west.[^1][^3]

A 16-by-20-foot frame house went up on the east end around 1921, according to the register, or in 1922, according to WillhiteWeb.[^1][^3] In 1930 the house was moved to the west end. A 7-by-7-foot cab took its place on the east end until 1933, when an L-5 cab replaced it.[^1][^3]

In 1946 a prototype two-story cab measuring 14 by 14 feet, with angled windows and a curved hip roof, went up on the east end.[^1][^3] The register notes that the design was expected to lead the next generation of Pacific Northwest lookouts, but the R-6 flat cab won out instead.[^1] Per the register, no other lookout was built to this design.[^1][^4]

WillhiteWeb names Amos Pearce, of nearby White Salmon, as the first lookout, in 1922.[^3] Darrel and Zoa Couch of Trout Lake were lookouts in 1932 and for a few years after, and Myron Halse was stationed there in 1938.[^3] In 1952 Mr. and Mrs. Ernest Fiedler held the post until a local school started. Forest Couch then took over for the rest of the season.[^3]

The west house was destroyed in 1960, and the site was abandoned that year.[^1][^3] DNR, the landowner, repeatedly threatened to tear down the east cab, but it stood until 2005.[^1][^2] That year the Forest Fire Lookout Association (FFLA) took the cab apart and moved it to the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center in Entiat.[^1][^2][^4]

WillhiteWeb tells the Columbia Breaks part differently. Volunteers and the FFLA built a replica of the top story and salvaged some of the original wood, mainly for the roof and window supports.[^4] The center lies north of Entiat, on Highway 97A.[^5] The center started with the aim of rescuing an old Forest Service lookout and setting it beside a busy highway. The project was seeded in 1990.[^5]

Today the east side has communications towers among the trees, and the west side is overgrown, with little left but a clearing.[^3] The Klickitat County PUD ran power lines up from the Trout Lake area in 1962.[^3] WillhiteWeb calls it a drive-up site, reached by forest roads north or south of the mountain, using FR 8821, with a rocky last mile.[^3] The west side needs a walk, because deadfall blocks the old route.[^3]
