Rising on the high Fishlake Plateau, north of Seven Mile Valley, Mount Terrill is a modest, rounded summit.[^1] It lies in Sevier County, Utah, and a climbing guide also calls it White Knob.[^1][^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a Mount Terrel in Sevier County among its unknown or undocumented Utah sites, not among its lookouts.[^2] The association says only proof of regular fire-lookout use would move such a site onto its lookout list.[^2] The association spells the name Terrel, while the climbing guide and WillhiteWeb's own text spell it Terrill.[^2][^1][^3]

WillhiteWeb lists the name among Utah lookouts its author has not visited. Its entry says "Nothing known" about a lookout, then names Mount Terrill in Sevier County as the probable match, a peak with good views over heavy timber in the Fish Lake National Forest.[^3]

The peak sits inside the Fishlake National Forest, and the climbing guide says a permit matters only to anyone planning to cut firewood.[^1] Few people come, most of them on ATVs, up the worn trail that starts at UM Pass.[^1]

No source I read describes a lookout structure, footings or other remains on the summit today.

Do not confuse the peak with the Mt Terrill Guard Station, a small cabin with a kitchen and bedroom, high on the paved Seven Mile-Gooseberry road in the Fishlake National Forest.[^4]
