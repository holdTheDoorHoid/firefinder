Stark Mountain sits on the Ninemile Divide in the Lolo National Forest.[^1][^2] The first lookout on the summit went into use in 1934, an L-4 cab on a 30-foot pole.[^3][^4] In 1964 the present cab, an R-6 flat-roofed design on a 20-foot treated timber tower, went up to replace it.[^1][^3][^4] By the time the lookout was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on July 14, 2000, Virginia Vincent had staffed it for 30 years.[^1]

That register entry also recalls a busy night. On August 4, 2000, Vincent, a Forest Fire Lookout Association member, photographed the fires, many of which she saw flare up in the middle of the night.[^1] The entry counts 32 fires ignited on the Ninemile Ranger District that day.[^1]

Today the Forest Service reports summer staffing at Stark, and visitors reach it on a four-mile hike along Trail #58 from the trailhead.[^2] Firelookout.com also notes the summer staffing.[^3]
