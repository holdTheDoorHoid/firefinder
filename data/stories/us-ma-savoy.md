The Savoy fire tower stands on Borden Mountain, inside the Savoy Mountain State Forest in Berkshire County, Massachusetts.[^1][^3] The Register lists the Mass. Bureau of Forest Fire Control as its administrator.[^1] The present tower is a 68-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10-foot wooden cab, and it went into service in 1932.[^1][^3] The sources I read do not say why the tower was built when it was.

The site had lookouts before that. The Register says a tree-top station went up there in 1913 to supplement Mount Greylock.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places that 1913 platform on Tower Mountain, where a wooden tower followed in 1914. It says that site was abandoned at the end of the 1915 season, when the main Savoy post moved to Borden Mountain.[^2] The two registers disagree about the tower in between. The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 45-foot steel tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab and gives no date for it.[^1] The other register gives the 1914 wooden tower.[^2]

Observers staffed the tower. A 1949 newspaper item, quoted by the Forest Lookouts history, names Ernest Peabody as the observer, on duty from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.[^3] In May 1954 lightning damaged the tower, the telephone line and the power line, and the observer, Theodore S. Brown, was on forced leave because of it.[^3] In April 1955 the state district fire warden ordered the tower reopened as fire danger rose.[^3] None of the sources I read names a specific fire the tower spotted.

The sources do not describe the tower being taken down or moved. A Wikipedia article on Borden Mountain describes a fire lookout tower at the summit.[^4] A 2024 hiking write-up calls it a fenced-off fire tower.[^5]

Today the mountain sits in a state forest run by the Department of Conservation and Recreation.[^6] The same hiking write-up says the simplest approach is by paved road.[^5] No source I read confirms whether the public may enter the tower or climb it, so check with the state forest before going near it, and do not climb a fenced tower.
