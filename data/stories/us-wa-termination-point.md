A lookout stood on Termination Point, a summit southeast of Swift Reservoir in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, and it was built in the 1930s.[^1][^2][^4] The sources disagree about what was built.[^1][^3] The register calls it a cabin.[^1] A 1937 photo survey took its panoramas from 25 feet above the ground and called the structure a lookout tower.[^3]

Two maps also mark the site. A 1932 Columbia National Forest map labels a lookout there, and a 1937 recreation map marks a structure.[^3]

A USGS party reported a Forest Service lookout named Termination Point in 1946.[^3] A 1953 revisit found that the lookout had been taken down.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both describe the cabin as abandoned and no longer there.[^1][^2]

Some remains survive.[^3] Willhite's photographs document a footing near the summit and some wood on the slope below.[^3] No source describes a standing structure there today. The summit is roughly 20 by 20 feet and drops steeply to the east.[^4] A 2022 trip report praised the view of Mount Adams, Mount Rainier and Mount St. Helens.[^4]

Access is described two ways. Willhite reports good gravel to the last spur road and a walk of under a quarter mile from the end of the roads.[^3] The 2022 trip report describes a rougher route.[^4] The road is rough but passable.[^4] A mud crossing called the Big Muddy is hard to pass when wet, and the reporter advises parking and walking instead.[^4] The trail is a half-mile uphill, hard to make out, and has many game trails crossing it.[^4] No closure appears in the sources read.
