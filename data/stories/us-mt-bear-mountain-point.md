The Bear Mountain Point lookout was a two-story cab, listed by one source as an NPS structure, built in 1935 on a spur about two miles south of Bear Mountain's summit in Glacier National Park.[^1][^2][^4] Sources also call it Bare Mountain or Crossley Ridge Lookout.[^2][^4] The site sits about six miles southwest of Waterton, at roughly 6,200 feet.[^1]

In October 1938 a newspaper item described a pet chipmunk kept by Robert Howell and his wife, who staffed the lookout. The animal leapt onto a hot stove for food and suffered only minor burns to its feet.[^2][^4]

Sources disagree on how the lookout ended. Firelookout.com and the Montana History Portal say it was destroyed in 1965.[^1][^5] Montanalookouts and WillhiteWeb say it was removed in April 1966.[^2][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks it as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the site. A hike guide describes a Bear Mountain Observation Point at the end of the Bear Mountain Trail, which branches off the Stoney Indian Pass Trail roughly 0.5 mile northeast of Cosley Lake.[^6] None of the sources says whether that overlook is the lookout's spot, so the connection should be treated as unconfirmed.
