The McKeetown fire tower stands just west of Atlantic City, in Atlantic County, New Jersey.[^1] Its story begins with the city rather than the state. In June 1916 the State Fire Warden chose a site on the Mays Landing road near McKee City, and Atlantic City agreed to pay for the tower.[^2] The aim was to guard the county forests and, through them, the city's water supply.[^2]

The first structure was a skeleton frame.[^2] Newspapers in January 1917 described it as sixty feet tall, with an enclosed room at the top for a watchman supplied by the state.[^2] A June 1917 article gave its height as sixty-five feet.[^2] The register describes the first tower as wooden with iron bracing, and says the coast resort's water department built it in 1917.[^1] A 1917 state annual report credited Atlantic City with creating South Jersey's first permanent lookout.[^2] A later report said it had been manned throughout the 1917 to 1918 season.[^2]

The register then tells a second story. It says the state built an 80-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab in 1924, and that a 20-foot addition in 1946 raised it to 100 feet.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history page reprints the 1916 to 1919 accounts but mentions neither the 1924 tower nor the 1946 addition.[^2] The sources do not settle how the 1917 tower and the 1924 state tower relate.[^1][^2]

Wikipedia's list gives the tower a height of 100 feet (30 m) and an elevation of 75 feet.[^3] It places the tower in Egg Harbor Township, while the register puts it just west of Atlantic City.[^3][^1] The list also counts it among the towers in active service.[^3] The register records its entry on the National Historic Lookout Register on December 15, 1997.[^1]

No source read for this story says who staffs the tower now or whether the public may visit it. Treat it as a working state lookout, not a place to climb.[^1][^3]
