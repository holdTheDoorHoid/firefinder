Saddle Mound is a summit in Jackson County, Wisconsin, at 1,401 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says a tower has stood on the site since at least 1931.[^1] A hiking guide describes a fire tower built on the west end of the summit in the 1930s, and says an old cabin at the base of the mound housed the fire tower agent for a number of years.[^3]

An August 25, 1931 item from the Wisconsin Rapids Daily Tribune, quoted by Forest Lookouts, pointed to Saddle Mound's strict policy of spotting and stamping out small blazes.[^2] It says many of the cranberry men in Wood County believed their district had poor fire protection, and it sets the Jackson County tower, just across the line, against that.[^2] The sources do not date the agent's cabin or describe how the tower was staffed day to day.

The hiking guide says the tower was decommissioned in 2016 along with all of Wisconsin's remaining fire towers.[^3] The same guide adds that the tower is still standing, though its stairway is closed to the public.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wisconsin county list still shows it as a standing tower.[^4]

The Register lists the tower as administered by Jackson County Forestry and Parks.[^1] It was registered on March 14, 2019, and Brecken Young nominated it.[^1] The guide also says the land is part of the Jackson County Forest and that the public has ready access to the summit.[^3] That access statement sits awkwardly with the stairway closure, so how open the site is remains unclear. The tower itself should not be climbed while its stairway is closed. Anyone planning a visit should check with Jackson County Forestry and Parks first.
