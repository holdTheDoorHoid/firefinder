The firelookout.ca record places the Sherwood Fire Tower on Yantha Mountain, in Ontario's Madawaska Valley, at an elevation of 495 m (1624 ft).[^1]

The record describes a light steel tower with guy wires, built by Goold, Shapley & Muir Co. Ltd., with an octagonal cupola.[^1] It gives a total cost of $680.07 and notes that the towerman lived at home.[^1]

The tower appears on a 1977 Pembroke topographic map, third edition, and on the Department of Lands and Forests Tower Detection System Map of 1963.[^1]

A volunteer history site says most of Ontario's towers went up between the 1920s and the 1950s.[^2] Those towers were administered by the Department of Lands and Forests, which was succeeded by the Ministry of Natural Resources in 1972.[^2] A Sudbury News feature puts the provincial total at 52 wooden and 227 steel towers in 1947.[^3]

No source here describes what stands at the Sherwood site today, or whether the public can reach it.
