Mt. Davis is the highest point in Pennsylvania, at 3,213 feet, and its observation tower stands on top of the mountain in Forbes State Forest, Somerset County.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The date is disputed. The storymap and the WPA page both give 1935, and the storymap names the Blaw-Knox Company as builder.[^4][^1] Willhite instead gives 1934, when the Department of Forests and Waters erected a forest fire observation tower, probably with help from the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^2] The WPA page lists a Negro Mtn. fire tower among the projects of a CCC camp set up in June 1933. It does not say this is the summit tower, though the names suggest it might be.[^1] Willhite also says the summit was called Negro Mountain at that time.[^2]

Most of the state forest land around the mountain was bought by the Commonwealth in 1929.[^1] Mt. Davis was declared a State Forest Monument in 1945, and 581 acres around the high point became a State Forest Natural Area in 1974.[^1]

The storymap gives the height as 50 feet and describes the tower as an observation tower for the public.[^4] The Pennsylvania Parks and Forests Foundation says the 1930s-era tower is getting new interpretive signs for visitors who cannot climb it.[^5] A visitor guide urges readers to climb the tower, and it describes a raised relief map and a plaque at the top.[^6]

Willhite lists the road as paved and good gravel, and says the walk from the parking area is short, past a closed gate.[^2] The storymap and the foundation article both describe the tower in the present tense, as in use or being fitted with signs.[^4][^5]

The sources do not settle whether the tower can be climbed: the visitor guide urges it,[^6] while the foundation article describes signs aimed at visitors who cannot climb.[^5] Ask the state forest office before you go, and do not climb the tower if it is closed.
