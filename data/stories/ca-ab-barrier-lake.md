The Barrier Lake fire lookout sits in Kananaskis Country, Alberta, and is a well-known hike in the area.[^3] Alberta's wildfire database lists a cabin built in 1982, 2 m (7 ft) tall on a site 2,020.5 m (6,629 ft) above sea level.[^1] The Canada Lookouts directory says the 1982 building was put up to take the place of the Pigeon lookout.[^4]

The original Pigeon building has an earlier life. Friends of Kananaskis Country says it began as a guard tower at a Second World War prisoner-of-war camp, on ground that the University of Calgary's Barrier Lake Forestry Station now occupies.[^2]

Explore Kananaskis tells the story differently, crediting the Fire Lookout House with the name Guard Tower #8 from the prison-camp years.[^3] The two sources disagree about which building carried that name, and this story does not settle it.[^2][^3]

Once the camp shut, the Pigeon building was relocated to the lookout site, where it stayed in service until 1982. Its next stop was the university's field station, where the write-up says it still sits, with aspen and poplar gradually closing in on it.[^2]

The house is a private residence, and its lookout manager has held the job for decades.[^2] The write-up recounts a visit in 2016 and mentions a pair of picnic tables near the helipad that are meant for visitors.[^2] Explore Kananaskis describes the house as a historic site that is still a working lookout in the warmer months, keeping watch for fires.[^3]

The database codes the site's access as "fly", but the write-up describes a climb up the official Prairie View Trail, reached from Barrier Dam Day Use Area.[^1][^2] The official trail stops short of the lookout house, and the write-up asks visitors to keep away from the building unless they have been invited.[^2]
