Mount Elizabeth rises over the Stanislaus National Forest in Tuolumne County, within the Mi-Wok Ranger District.[^1] The earliest lookout here was a "windmill type" tower, 60 feet high with a flat, unroofed platform, already in place by 1925.[^1] It was replaced in 1931 by a 40-foot steel tower carrying a small 7-by-7-foot cab, with a wooden residence built alongside for the lookout keeper.[^1]

The structure standing today dates to 1961: a U.S. Forest Service National Plan CL-100 cab set atop a 55-foot K-brace steel tower, built to replace the 1931 tower.[^1] At an elevation of 4,931 feet, it has been staffed daily through fire season by the Forest Service, watching for smoke across the surrounding forest.[^1]

Mount Elizabeth was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2017, nominated by Bill Luedeke, and carries the designations US 1186 and CA 117.[^1] In the Forest Fire Lookout Association's own list of California lookouts, it is likewise recorded as a standing tower.[^2]

Beyond its physical history, little else has surfaced in available sources: no named keepers, no documented fires spotted from its catwalk, no later remodel or closure. What can be said with confidence is the arc of three structures on one summit, from open platform to roofed cab to today's enclosed, live-in lookout. It remains an active U.S. Forest Service facility, and no public access or visiting information is documented here, so check current Stanislaus National Forest guidance before planning any trip.
