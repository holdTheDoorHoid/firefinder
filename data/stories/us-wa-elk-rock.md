Elk Rock sits in Cowlitz County at 4,391 feet, near the Spirit Lake Memorial Highway.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com places it 10 miles northwest of Mount St. Helens.[^3]

By the end of the 1940 fire season, a tractor trail was partly built to the top.[^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps put up the tower in 1941.[^2][^4] The Register describes an 83-foot wooden tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab and a live-in ground house, and firelookout.com gives the same height and cab size.[^1][^3] The 1941 annual report, as quoted by Forest Lookouts and by Willhite, gives 80 feet instead.[^2][^4]

The Forest Lookouts site lists the lookout as active from March 16, 1942 to October 16, 1943, under the Portland Filter Center.[^2] Firelookout.com names Richard Day and Jim Slack as lookout personnel in 1942, and Harold McCorkle in 1953.[^3] In 1954 the thirteen-switchback trail to the top was turned into a jeep road.[^2][^4]

The Columbus Day storm of October 12, 1962 badly damaged the tower.[^2][^1] A new lookout, the Ted Gilbert Lookout, was built about a mile away to replace it.[^4][^2]

Sources differ on how the original ended. The Register says it was destroyed in 1963, when the Ted Gilbert Lookout was chosen to replace it.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same year.[^3] Willhite says it was abandoned that year after the storm damage.[^4] Forest Lookouts says the May 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens completely destroyed the site.[^2] Willhite says the blast hit Elk Rock, but the foundation ruins still remain.[^4]

Willhite describes the way up from the Elk Rock Viewpoint, a highway pullout. He suggests walking east past the road cut and then climbing through the forest, with a gain of about 700 feet and some old roads to cross.[^4] Forest roads come close to the summit, but gates usually block them, and officially a permit is required.[^4] His page does not say when he visited, so his description of the foundations may not match the site today.[^4] The Register lists the site as registered on April 7, 2020, nominated by Tammy McLeod.[^1]
