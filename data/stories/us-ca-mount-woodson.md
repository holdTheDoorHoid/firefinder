Mount Woodson sits about seven miles southwest of Ramona in San Diego County.[^1] A fire lookout stood on its summit for several decades.[^2][^3]

The register describes a first lookout here, an 80-foot battered steel tower with an observation-only cab and a residence cabin on the ground. It says Civilian Conservation Corps crews likely built it.[^2] A hiking guide gives the same 1936 date.[^3] The register's Built field, however, reads 1948.[^2]

In 1950 the California Department of Forestry put up a 30-foot enclosed steel tower with a 15-by-15-foot wood cab built to Standard Plan 809R.[^2][^5] Living quarters took up the second and third floors below the cab.[^2]

In November 1938 a windstorm blew out the windows of Woodson's ranger lookout station, and the same winds sparked brush and grass fires in many parts of northern San Diego County.[^5] On October 29, 1967, a blaze that began near the Ramona Airport swept up the east side of Mt. Woodson and ran toward Poway, where 16 homes burned.[^6]

The register's photo caption says a tower was lost in a 1967 fire, and its text says the 1950 lookout seems to have been lost in a fire.[^2] The same entry notes a lookout still standing in a 1986 survey, so the sources disagree about which tower burned, and when.[^2][^4] The register records 1968 plans for a replacement, but photos and descriptions of it have not yet turned up.[^2] The register dates the final removal to the 1990s and says the last lookout was gone by 1991.[^2][^4] A hiking guide believes it was removed in the 1980s.[^3]

The summit now holds many communications buildings and antennas, and a hiking guide reports that remnants of the old lookout base are also up there.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's current photo caption calls it an antenna farm.[^4] In 1962 the state approved buying a 400-acre hilltop meant for a lookout and a radio repeater.[^5]

Hikers can reach the top from Lake Poway on a 6.5-mile round trip, but the lake area shuts its gates when the sun goes down.[^3] A local history describes it as a primary lookout that is gone.[^1] No source we read describes what the lookout base looks like today, or whether any footings survive.
