The Los Angeles County Forestry Department built the Castro Peak Lookout in 1925, on Castro Peak in the Malibu Hills west of Los Angeles.[^1][^2] It had an 8-by-8-foot metal cab on a 22-foot tower.[^1][^2]

The lookout went out of service in 1971.[^1][^3] Around 1978 the county moved it to the Henninger Flats Forestry Unit, an island of county property in the Angeles National Forest on the south slope of Mount Wilson.[^1] At Henninger Flats the county restored the lookout, filled it with period artifacts from 1925, and set it on a 7-foot support as a historical display inside the Forestry Unit's visitor center and museum.[^1]

The register lists it as US 1289 and CA 127, registered on December 8, 2018, with Scott McClintock as nominator.[^1]

In 2025 the Los Angeles County Fire Department dismantled the lookout at the Henninger site.[^1][^2][^3] The register says the county intended to erect it at the Los Angeles County Fairgrounds, which it identifies as the Fairplex in Pomona.[^1] The sources do not say whether that has happened.

The lookout is no longer at Henninger Flats.[^1] The sources do not show what, if anything, is on view there now, so anyone planning a trip should check with the county fire department before going.
