Cathead Mountain sits north of the hamlet of Benson in Hamilton County, in the Adirondacks.[^4] A lookout was set up on its summit in June 1910, a wooden tower built by the state Forest, Fish and Game Commission.[^1][^4] The Forest Lookouts site quotes the commission's annual report, which describes a 15-foot tower.[^1] Wikipedia gives 18 feet for the same tower, and says a telephone line was run to the summit so the observer could report fires faster.[^4] The two sources disagree on the height, and we could not settle it.

The wooden tower was replaced in 1916 by a steel tower.[^3] Wikipedia says the Conservation Commission put up a 50-foot Aermotor LL25 tower that year.[^4] Interior stairs were added in 1929.[^3]

Observers staffed the tower for decades. The Forest Lookouts site records an observer named in a 1911 announcement.[^1] A 1954 obituary calls Seth Jack Wadsworth the first to serve in the state's tower here, and says he served there for 20 years.[^1] The two accounts do not agree on who came first. We did not find a notable fire tied to the lookout in the sources we read.

The tower was in service through the 1988 season and officially closed in 1989.[^3][^4] The 2020 newsletter of the New York lookout association says the closure followed a dispute between a landowner and the state.[^3] A frame was later added over the old tower to hold communications equipment.[^3] That equipment is reached by helicopter only.[^3]

Today the tower is still standing.[^2] The 2020 newsletter says the public has been kept out for many years.[^3] The summit is privately owned, and state land around it does not allow motor vehicles.[^5] A state constitutional amendment that would have allowed a road to the top was pulled in the Assembly, and the Adirondack Explorer reports it appears back in limbo.[^5] The most recent access report we found is the 2020 newsletter, so current access is unconfirmed. Until that is checked, treat the tower as one to see from a distance, not one to climb.
