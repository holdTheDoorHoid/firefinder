The National Historic Lookout Register says the first tower on Mount Adams was made of wood and went up in 1912 at the Tahawus Club's request.[^1] In 1917 the state erected the present steel tower, which the register describes as a 47-foot Aermotor LS-40 with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] Wikipedia gives the steel frame a height of 56 feet, so the sources disagree on height, and I could not settle which figure is right.[^1][^2]

Wikipedia says the Aermotor Corporation built the tower to guard the Adirondack Park against forest fires.[^2] The observer's cabin dates to 1922.[^2] The register says the ground house went up in 1922 and was improved in 1950.[^1] It also calls the cabin the only surviving Model 1922 in New York State.[^1]

The state staffed the tower until the mid 1970s, according to the register.[^1] The register lists the lookout as registered on February 12, 2004, and Wikipedia says the National Register of Historic Places added the station on April 12, 2006.[^1][^2] The register also says the tower has sparked a public debate about whether lookouts belong in wilderness areas.[^1]

By the time the ADK Highpeaks Foundation was asked to help restore it in 2010, the tower had been closed to the public as a safety hazard.[^4] Volunteers replaced stairs, railings, fencing, and a footer, and they built a new cab roof.[^4] A 2011 flood destroyed the steel cable bridge over the Hudson River, so volunteers had to ford the river with their gear.[^4] Friends of Mt Adams had already started restoring the observer's cabin in September 2007.[^1]

The Department of Environmental Conservation describes the tower as sitting on private property within the High Peaks Wilderness, and calls it restored, with views of the High Peaks.[^3] The Open Space Institute administers the site.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^5] Volunteers still help keep up the trail and tower each year.[^4] ADK says the last half mile climbs 1,000 feet, so expect a steep final approach.[^4]

I could not confirm whether the tower is open to visitors now. Check with the Open Space Institute before you go, and do not climb a tower that is closed.[^1]
