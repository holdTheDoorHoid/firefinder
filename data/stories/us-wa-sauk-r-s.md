A huge cedar on the river bottom near the Sauk Guard Station, in Snohomish County, was turned into a fire lookout in 1918.[^1][^3] A crow's nest was built at its top, and spikes driven into the trunk served as steps for the climb.[^3][^5]

Firelookout.com lists the same site as Sauk R.S., describing a crow's nest in a cedar built in 1918 at an elevation of 1,100 feet.[^1] The names differ, but the dates and details match closely, so this account treats the two as one site. No source says that outright.[^1][^3][^5]

Every day in fire season, someone climbed the tree to keep watch for blazes.[^3] Before 1926, supplies could reach the site only on pack animals.[^3] In 1926 the Sauk River Timber Company brought in a train, and supplies started arriving by rail.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in the 1930s, though the sources give no single year.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com says the tree is still there, and a Washington index lists the site as abandoned rather than gone.[^1][^2]

Willhite says some of the spikes are still visible on the trunk.[^3] WTA describes the Lookout Tree trail as a short section that is no longer maintained, and advises hikers to use caution.[^4] The trailhead lies 11.3 miles south of Darrington on the Mountain Loop Highway.[^3]

The sources disagree on the tree's width: 11 feet at breast height, 15 feet, and 75 feet.[^1][^3][^4] None of the pages we read says whether the crow's nest itself survives.
