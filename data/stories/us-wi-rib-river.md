The Rib River tower stands at the junction of CTH M and C in the Town of Browning, Taylor County.[^1] A November 1959 easement from the then owner, Hans Matheus, gave the state permission to build it, and the Star News says the tower went up in 1960.[^1]

At 120 feet, the Rib River tower is the taller of the two Taylor County towers that the DNR's Medford branch manages. The Perkinstown tower, the other one, is 100 feet.[^1] Dan Schumacher, the DNR's northern region supervisor, said it took a special person to spend hours alone in a 6-by-6-foot cab.[^1]

The Medford branch of the DNR manages both towers, which are staffed 15 to 20 days each spring.[^1] Mike Reigert spent 20 years on watch at the Rib River tower and recalls his first climb there in the spring of 1995.[^1] He said he had expected the closure from the start of his time on the job.[^1]

In December 2015 the DNR announced plans to close all of its remaining fire towers.[^1] Schumacher said the agency has begun notifying private landowners and is laying out options for the towers' future.[^1]

The 2015 article says the land under the Rib River tower is owned by Marvin and Burdella Blumenstein of Rib Lake, so it is private land.[^1] The firelookout.org list still marks the tower as standing, but that list carries no date.[^2] The sources do not say whether the tower is still in use or whether the public may approach it. Do not climb or enter the cab. Ask the DNR's Medford office, or the landowners, before going near the site.
