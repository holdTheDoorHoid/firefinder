Anderson Mountain Lookout stood in Lemhi County, Idaho, on a peak in the northern reaches of the Beaverhead Mountains, a short distance west of the Continental Divide.[^4][^1] The site lies in the Salmon National Forest, about 30 miles north of Salmon.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association gives its elevation as 8,034 feet.[^1]

A 1982 Salmon National Forest history by Bing Young, quoted by a climbing guide, says the peak apparently was picked for a lookout in the winter of 1932–33 and budgeted for that summer.[^4] The tower went up in 1933.[^1][^2][^3]

The sources disagree about its height. Most describe a 50-foot Aermotor LX-24 tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2][^3] The climbing guide, quoting Young's history, puts the tower at 70 feet and places a residence cabin beneath it.[^4] The guide adds that the lookout was used only in emergencies.[^4]

In August 1952, the Recorder Herald reported that Rev. and Mrs. Joe Howard would have charge of the station that fire season.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association and Idaho Fire Lookouts both list the tower as gone in 1962.[^1][^2] A survey station description written by the U.S. Geological Survey in 1966 says the old lookout had been removed.[^3]

According to the climbing guide, the summit now holds only the tower's footings.[^4] The summit sits close to the Continental Divide Trail.[^4] The guide gives a route of 6.4 miles from the highway, reached by FS-081 from Chief Joseph Pass.[^4] It recommends a 4WD and says visitors should check road conditions with the Forest Service.[^4] We found no closure notice in the sources we read.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
