Jumpoff Lookout stands on Jumpoff Ridge in Yakima County, Washington, overlooking the Tieton Basin.[^1][^4] Its current cabin is a flat-topped R-6 design, built in 1961, which the Forest Service administers in the Wenatchee National Forest.[^1][^3][^4] It is also known as "Jumpoff Joe."[^1]

The site was in use before the present cabin. The National Historic Lookout Register dates the first structure to about 1923, a D-6 cupola cabin, and Eric Willhite's history gives the same date.[^1][^2] A 1921 newspaper item reported a new lookout house completed on Jump Off, though it does not clearly name the same building.[^3] Mike Hiler places the earlier cabin in the 1930s through 1958.[^4] Willhite says the R-6 cab replaced it in 1958 or 1961, while the Register and Hiler both give 1961.[^2][^1][^4]

Mike Hiler was the lookout here from 1969 to 1973, and the cabin has been used sporadically since the 1960s on an emergency basis.[^1][^2]

By 2010, Willhite writes, vandalism and neglect were wrecking the cabin. Windows and shutters were broken or missing, snow had collected inside, and copper thieves had stripped the lightning protection.[^2] Hiler and the Friends of Jumpoff began stabilizing it that summer, patching the roof and installing shutters.[^2][^4] A 2013 report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Eastern Washington chapter called the stabilization a success.[^5]

The Register says the roof is now watertight and the shutters have been repaired and replaced.[^1] Its plexiglass openings let visitors look inside without tearing the shutters off.[^1] The Register listed the lookout on February 8, 2022, with Hiler as nominator.[^1]

Access is by foot or by vehicle. The Washington Trails Association describes a 10-mile round trip starting at Lost Lake, and says the route changes character more than once before reaching the lookout.[^6] Willhite notes that a high-clearance vehicle can drive up.[^2] None of these sources reports a closure. Visitors should still treat the cabin as a fragile historic structure and avoid damaging it.
