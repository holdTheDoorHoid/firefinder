The Artic lookout stood on a logged-off hill about six or seven miles south of Aberdeen, in Grays Harbor County, Washington.[^2][^3] The Washington State Division of Forestry built the first tower there in 1948. It was a 50-foot pole tower with a 12-by-12-foot slanted cab.[^1][^2][^4]

In 1952 a 40-foot wooden L-4 tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab replaced it.[^1][^3][^4] The sources do not say who staffed the tower or which fires it spotted.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Most sources say the tower was destroyed in 1973. The registry says it was removed that year instead.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey station on the summit was described in 1953.[^3] When Willhite visited in 2016 he could not find the cement footings that earlier reports described, but he did find a survey reference mark.[^4] The land is private timberland, and as of 2016 the timber company, Weyerhaeuser, required a permit for anyone to enter.[^4] The permit options are the expensive Artic Permit and the cheaper Aberdeen General Access Permit, which covers February 1 to July 31.[^4] Willhite found the summit overgrowing and no view. He says views will stay blocked until the next harvest, around 2045.[^4]
