The Climax Springs lookout tower stands beside Missouri Route 7, roughly 1.5 miles east of Climax Springs.[^3] A state manual for 1947 and 1948 lists the towersite as four acres, owned by the Missouri Conservation Commission.[^1]

A 1946 to 1947 entry records four acres acquired for the towersite.[^1] A Daily Capital News item from June 1947 reports that a new 60-foot wooden tower was erected.[^1] An older Wikipedia text, copied on a mirror site, says the tower was constructed in 1948 and is an Aermotor LS40 model.[^2] The two dates disagree.[^1][^2]

In March 1964, a newspaper report described a man manning a fire tower in the Climax Springs area.[^1] He saw men setting several fires nearby, and he came down from the tower to put them out.[^1] A magistrate fined two men from Climax Springs $400 for setting fires on someone else's land.[^1]

The tower is roughly 50 feet off Route 7, and parking is available alongside it.[^3] Visitors may approach the tower on foot, but climbing is not allowed.[^3] TopoZone describes its current use as uncertain.[^3] An older Wikipedia text says the tower is in good condition and open to the public, but the cab is closed.[^2]
