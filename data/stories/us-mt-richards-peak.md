Richards Peak Lookout sits at 5,771 feet in the Lolo National Forest, in Sanders County, about 21 miles northeast of Thompson Falls.[^1][^2]

The Register and firelookout.com both date the first lookout on the peak to 1925, when a cupola cabin was set up there.[^1][^2] Newspaper items quoted on the Forest Lookouts site put a lookout here earlier.[^3] In 1918 Denver Laughlin spent the summer as lookout on Richards Peak, and the 1919 and 1920 items show him back in the same post.[^3] A 1931 item says he had been there nearly 15 years.[^3] The Register also says the peak was named for a man called Richards, its first lookout, who was killed in action in France in World War I.[^1] The newspaper items I read do not name him, so the sources disagree about who staffed the peak first.

The present cab, an R-6 flattop with a catwalk on a concrete block base, dates from 1960.[^1][^2] The Register accepted the lookout on April 24, 2009, on the nomination of Gary Weber.[^1]

Both sources say the state staffs it now. The Register says it is staffed by the state, and firelookout.com names the Montana DNRC.[^1][^2] Neither source dates that statement.[^1][^2] The sources I read do not say whether visitors may climb or drive to the lookout, so check with the Lolo National Forest or the Montana DNRC before planning a visit.
