In October 1933 State Forester J. S. Holmes said that eight steel fire lookout towers would go up in North Carolina, supplied by the federal government through its Emergency Conservation Work program.[^1] One was to stand in Halifax County, four miles west of Scotland Neck.[^1] The towers were meant to tie in with the state's existing lookout stations, and crews from nearby Civilian Conservation Corps camps were to erect them.[^1]

The statewide list of North Carolina lookout towers places the Halifax County tower at 36.17138 north and 77.49944 west.[^2] A second list of the state's forest lookouts gives it the name Scotland Neck (Moonlight), and dates the tower to 1934, when the CCC erected a 99-foot 9-inch (30.4 m) steel tower there.[^3]

The 1933 notice describes a plan, and the list gives 1934 as the year the tower was built, so the two sources fit together.[^1][^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the site is open to the public.
