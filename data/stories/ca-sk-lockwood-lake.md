Lockwood Lake is on the websiteservice.ca list of Saskatchewan fire towers, which places the site at 55°37.8125′ N, 109°13.125′ W.[^1]

In northern Saskatchewan, the Department of Natural Resources set up fire control towers roughly fifty miles apart. The gap was chosen so that tower men could reach one another by radio.[^2] Heights ran from 11 m (35 ft) to 30 m (100 ft), and the towers were built of wood or steel.[^2] The same equipment was kept in every tower: a fire finder, a two-way radio and a pair of field glasses.[^2]

These notes come from a Big River history submitted by Arnold Fonos, and they cover the Big River district's towers and the network in general, not this site.[^2]

The two sources read for this record do not describe the Lockwood Lake site today, so it is not known what, if anything, remains there or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2]
