A Saskatchewan tower list places a Porcupine tower at 52-37.2000 X 101-49.6800, or 52.62°N, 101.83°W.[^1] Canada Lookouts' Saskatchewan index also lists a Porcupine tower.[^2]

The tower shares its name with the Porcupine Forest Reserve. In 1906 the Canadian government officially established that reserve as a national forest, and it built a headquarters at Ushta, Saskatchewan.[^3] The Porcupine Provincial Forest article also lists four fire towers, plus four ranger cabins and five stables, put up for the rangers alongside the headquarters.[^3]

A 1915 to 1916 entry on Canada Lookouts' Saskatchewan page describes four wooden lookout towers built in the Porcupine and Pasquia Reserve, with three more under construction. Most were 12 to 18 m (40 to 60 ft) tall and stood near the ranger stations.[^2]

The sources do not say whether the Porcupine tower on this record is one of these towers.

No source we found describes the tower site today, or says whether it is open to visitors.
