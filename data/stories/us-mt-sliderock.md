Sliderock was a fire lookout on Sliderock Mountain, about 21 miles north-northwest of Philipsburg in Granite County, Montana.[^1] Ron Kemnow lists the agency as the Lolo/Admin. Deer Lodge National Forest,[^4] while firelookout.com names only the Lolo National Forest.[^1]

In 1919 the site was developed with a 35-foot pole platform and a model log cabin.[^1] Firelookout.com says a 40-foot pole L-4 tower followed in 1935.[^1] The Fort Missoula museum dates the lookout to about 1930, and a state marker says it was made in Columbia Falls, Montana, that year, along with other prefabricated lookouts shipped across a four-state region.[^2][^3] The museum says L-4 lookouts were portable enough for pack animals to haul to a site, where they were set up on timbers cut there.[^2] The museum says the 1910 fires pushed the Forest Service to rethink its practices, and that is when it began building lookouts.[^2]

In 1983 the lookout was taken apart on its mountain and moved to the Fort Missoula museum, with help from the Friends of the Historical Museum and the Missoula Chapter of the Society of American Foresters.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com instead says the cab and top two-thirds of the tower went there in 1984.[^1] Kemnow's page says only that it was moved to Fort Missoula to be used as a display.[^4] The sources disagree on the year and on how much of the lookout moved.[^1][^2][^4]

The tower on the mountain is listed as gone.[^5] No source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the original site today.

The lookout now stands at the museum, on what the marker calls its own “mini-Sliderock” mountain.[^2][^3] The first section of stairs was removed, so it sits lower than it would have on the original site.[^2][^3] The museum's page describes visitors standing on the landing between two flights of stairs, so check with the museum for hours before you go.[^2]
