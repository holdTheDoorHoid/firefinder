Canada Lookouts transcribes entries from annual reports of the Department of Lands and Mines, which trace a Heart Lake tower from 1943 to 1947.[^1] The entries file it under the Lac La Biche Division.[^1]

The entry headed 1943 (the report for the year ending 31 March 1944) gives 9.7 km (6 mi) of road, with some bridges and corduroy, from Touchwood Lake to the tower site in Township 68, Range 9, West 4th Meridian.[^1] The site also gained a log cabin of standard forestry pattern and barn walls, and building material for the tower was hauled in over the winter.[^1]

The tower was finished in summer 1946, in time for the spring 1947 danger period.[^1] A report for the year ending 31 March 1947 describes it as a pole structure, 23 m (76 ft) high, with a lumber cupola, on Section 29-68-9-W4.[^1] The report for the year ending 31 March 1948 records four 15 m (50 ft) radio masts, and says guy wires and dead-men were installed for the tower.[^1]

The Alberta lookout layer dates the steel tower now listed at the site to 2012.[^2] The layer gives the tower a height of 30 m (98 ft) and an elevation of 866 m (2,841 ft).[^2] OpenStreetMap, a volunteer-edited map, tags the Heart Lake Fire Tower at 50 m (164 ft) with lattice construction, which does not match the layer's 30 m.[^3]

The layer lists the tower as active, reached by road (its access mode is drive), and without a weather station.[^2] No source read describes its condition today, or states a rule on public visits.
