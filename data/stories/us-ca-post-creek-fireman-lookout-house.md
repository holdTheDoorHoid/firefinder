In 1934 the Civilian Conservation Corps built a cabin on a ridgeline above Beegum Gorge, in the Hayfork area of the Shasta-Trinity National Forest in Tehama County, California.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register classes it as a Forest Service Plan BC-201 fireman-lookout cabin and calls it the Fireman-Lookout House.[^1] Its back room, with a 270-degree bank of windows, is the spot where a fire finder could be posted to watch the forest.[^1][^3]

Sources disagree about its purpose. The register says BC-201 cabins were rarely used for real lookout duty and more often served as living quarters for people at observation-only towers.[^1] Recreation.gov and a California camping guide both say it was built to watch for wildfires.[^2][^4]

The register listed it on October 28, 2019, as US 1363, CA 186.[^1] Recreation.gov describes it as eligible for the National Register of Historic Places.[^2] Today it is open for recreational use.[^2] The Forest Service rents it through Recreation.gov for $75 a night.[^3][^1]

Recreation.gov says it sleeps four comfortably. The Forest Service page and the register give six, and one camping guide's fee line says up to twelve.[^2][^3][^1][^4]

Plumbing is described two ways too. The register mentions an outdoor portable toilet and no running water.[^1] Recreation.gov and the camping guide describe an indoor flushing toilet and water for dishes.[^2][^4]

Getting there means mostly gravel Forest roads, and there is no cell phone service.[^2] There is no electricity, heat, stove or refrigerator, and guests are told to bring drinking water, bedding and a camp stove with fuel.[^2] Reservations are required, through Recreation.gov or by phone.[^3]
