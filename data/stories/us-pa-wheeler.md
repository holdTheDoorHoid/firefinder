The Wheeler Fire Tower stands in the Hearts Content Recreation Area of the Allegheny National Forest, in Warren County, Pennsylvania.[^1] Its story starts a little before the forest was formed. In 1921 foresters began planning a lookout on land owned by the Wheeler & Dusenbury firm, to be erected and staffed under a joint arrangement with the state forestry department.[^2] The first plans called for a wooden tower, but by the autumn of 1922 the papers were reporting a finished concrete base and steel on the way.[^2]

Newspapers gave the planned height as 80 feet, called it the tallest in the state, and said a ranger would staff a hut at the summit through the fire season.[^2] The register gives the year built as 1923 and calls its Blaw-Knox design unusual, with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab and legs that slope until the final four sections, which are vertical.[^1] Newspapers had announced construction for September 1922, so the register's 1923 date is later than that start.[^2] A May 1923 fire report suggests the tower was already in service by then.[^3]

The tower was soon in use. A Forest Service history says the Farnsworth Fire, put at more than 1,000 acres, began on May 20, 1923 and was called in by the Wheeler lookout.[^3] In March 1924 a Cherry Grove man, Andrew Marfink, was named as the future lookout.[^2] I found no staffing records after that.

The register describes the tower in the present tense and records no later fire, move or removal.[^1] The company that owned the land gave 20 acres of virgin forest to the Forest Service; that stand is now the Hearts Content Scenic Area, a natural landmark.[^3]

I could not find a source saying whether the cab is open to visitors or safe to climb. Check current Forest Service guidance before visiting, and do not assume the cab can be climbed.
