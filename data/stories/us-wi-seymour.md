In July 1952 the Conservation Department put up a fire tower in the Town of Seymour, Eau Claire County, at a cost of about $3,500.[^1] It was built next to the town shale pit.[^1]

Fire spotters were to man it once the fire season came around.[^1] In August 1952 a newspaper item said fires in an area recently added to fire protection district 10 would be spotted from the tower, and Vernon Jacobson was its towerman.[^1]

In late March 1958 vandals broke into the 100-foot tower and wrecked the place.[^1] They broke 12 windows, smashed a fire-spotting protractor and took a radio and an electric heater.[^1] Conservation wardens and the sheriff's department investigated.[^1]

The statewide lookout list now marks the tower as gone.[^2] No source describes the site today, so what remains there and whether it is open to visitors are unknown.
