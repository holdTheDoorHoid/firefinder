The Bates Fire Tower stands in Iron County, Michigan, on land that the Ottawa National Forest manages.[^1][^2] We did not find a build date or a builder in the sources we read, so its early history is largely unknown to us.

The Michigan fire tower listing says the tower has not been removed and is standing.[^1] The map database tags it as a lattice observation tower and gives the site an elevation of 542 meters.[^3] The same listing has a field called "T. Cabin" marked "no," but the site does not explain the abbreviation, so we cannot say what sits at the top.[^1]

Staffing is the part of the history we can date. A June 1943 report in the Escanaba Daily Press, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, said Mary Scalcucci was one of four women the conservation department had hired to watch for forest fires from towers.[^2] She had taken over the Bates job from her husband, who had manned the tower for eight years, so that he could serve as fire warden in Crystal Falls.[^2] We found no record of any fire the tower watched.

Today, the sources we read say nothing about public access, climbing, or rental, so we cannot say whether visitors may go up.[^1][^2] Check with the Ottawa National Forest before going near it, and do not climb the tower unless it is open to the public.
