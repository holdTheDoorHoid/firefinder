Monument Ridge Lookout is a wooden cab in Sublette County, Wyoming, about three miles southwest of Bondurant, inside the Bridger-Teton National Forest.[^1][^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1941.[^4] The Forest Service survey describes it as the only remaining lookout on the forest that appears to have been built by precut methods, and the only one built of logs.[^4] The Forest Service picked the site in the 1930s, judging it the best location for a full view of the Hoback Ranger District.[^4] The survey adds that a road to the summit would be easy to build.[^4]

Newspaper items reprinted on a lookout history site show the lookout in use. A 1945 item assigned Anne White of Dubois to it for the summer, and a 1954 item says the Teton Forest found it advisable to staff it because of hot, dry weather.[^3] The Register says the tower plays an important part in detecting fires across western Wyoming.[^2] The sources read do not name any particular fire it watched.

Firelookout.com says it is still there.[^1] The Forest Service survey rates its integrity high in every aspect. It notes that some windows are boarded up, but says this is easily reversible and does not alter the building's integrity.[^4]

The Register lists it as registered on September 3, 2014, nominated by Tom Izzo, with an elevation of 8,252 feet.[^2] Firelookout.com gives 8,261 feet.[^1]

The survey notes that a visitor needs neither a long hike nor a horseback ride, which makes it one of just two lookouts on the Bridger-Teton where that is true.[^4] None of the sources read say what current access rules apply, so check with the Bridger-Teton National Forest before going.
