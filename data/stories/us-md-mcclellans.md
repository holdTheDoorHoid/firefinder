McClellan's Lookout stood on Red Hill, a ridge above the Antietam battlefield in Washington County.[^2][^1] A November 1938 newspaper said Red Hill had been General McClellan's observation post during the Civil War battle of Antietam.[^2]

That month an assistant state forester announced a 60-foot steel fire lookout tower for the ridge, to be built by Civilian Conservation Corps enrollees at a cost of $2,500.[^2] A newspaper item also said forest guard Clayton Phillips would be posted at the tower.[^2] The state acquired the 8.5-acre parcel from John Miller under a deed dated December 12, 1939.[^1]

Newspapers gave the site's elevation as 1,040 feet and 1,020 feet, and the DNR lists the property's base elevation as 1,000 feet.[^2][^1]

By 2007, when the DNR reviewed its forestry towers, the tower was gone and only concrete footings remained.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association also lists the tower as gone.[^3]

Today the state parcel fronts Red Hill Road just south of Keedysville. It had no current use in 2007 and was regrowing as forest.[^1] The DNR suggested it as a possible communications site or scenic overlook.[^1] The report also says the USGS map places the actual lookout farther south along the same ridge, so the parcel may not include the exact site.[^1] The report does not say whether the public may visit.
