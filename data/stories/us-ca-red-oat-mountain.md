In 1967 residents of Cazadero built a fire lookout on Red Oat Mountain in Sonoma County.[^1][^2] They also staffed the lookout themselves.[^1][^2] According to the register, Sonoma County had just two state towers, and Cazadero's residents judged that more detection was needed.[^1] A California lookout list describes the Red Oat tower as an enclosed tower.[^3]

The tower moved in 1981 to a better site on Pole Mountain, according to the register.[^1][^2] The Sonoma Land Trust places that move after the Creighton Ridge Fire of 1978.[^4] The Pole Mountain Lookout group's About page, however, says the move happened in the late 1960s, so the sources disagree on the date.[^5]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Red Oat Mountain site today.[^2]

The structure now stands on the summit of Pole Mountain, between Cazadero and Jenner, and a volunteer-led group runs it.[^6][^1] The register says seasonal staffing is funded and managed by the association, with CAL Fire assistance.[^1] The 238-acre site was acquired by the Sonoma Land Trust in 2014.[^1]

Hikers can reach the summit from the Jenner Headlands by the Sea to Sky Trail, and both preserves opened to hikers in September 2018.[^6][^4] An October 2009 visit report, which predates that opening, says the tower sits on private property and that general visits must be arranged through CAL FIRE or the association.[^1] Check with the association before going up to the tower itself.
