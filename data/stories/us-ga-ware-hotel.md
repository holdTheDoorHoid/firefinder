The Georgia Forestry Commission's own history credits the Ware Hotel in Waycross as the site of the first fire observation tower.[^1] It links that, perhaps, to a 1928 forestry fair held in Waycross, and also notes a follow-up fair planned for Valdosta, which went ahead in 1929.[^1] A Georgia tower list also gives its "ware hotel" entry the note "first tower".[^2]

The Ware Hotel stands seven stories tall in downtown Waycross.[^5] It opened on 21 February 1929 with 110 guest rooms, each with a private bath.[^3] Its architect was Henrik Wallin.[^4] Early guests included railway crews and travelers who lodged overnight in Waycross before heading out by rail.[^5]

Before its renovation, the building housed an assisted-care facility.[^4] W.H. Gross Construction, based in Kingsland, was overseeing the rebuild, and a photo caption dates a restoration view to 2011.[^5][^3] Since then it has been refurbished and put to use as apartment housing.[^4]

Today the building is the Ware Hotel apartment community at 604 Elizabeth Street.[^6] It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and sits within the Downtown Waycross Historic District.[^4] No source describes a lookout tower on the building today.
