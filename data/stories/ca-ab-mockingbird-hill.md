The lookout sits on a foothill that goes by the unofficial name Mockingbird Hill, in the Ghost Wilderness.[^1][^2] The trip report says the name is believed to come from surveyors who scouted the hill while listening to a song called Mockin' Bird Hill on a transistor radio.[^1] The register lists the cabin at 1,906.6 m (6,255 ft).[^3]

According to the 2020 trip report, the lookout first went up in 1952 as the successor to the one on Black Rock Mountain, a site the report says was often hit by wind and cloud and was hard to supply.[^1] Mountain Legacy credits Harry Fisher of the Alberta Forest Service with building Black Rock in 1928, and says it was decommissioned in 1950.[^4] The dates do not line up neatly: the trip report gives 1952 for the new lookout, while Mountain Legacy says Black Rock was closed in 1950.[^1][^4]

The register gives 1972 as the construction year for the cabin now listed on the site.[^3] That is later than the 1952 date for the original, which suggests the cabin standing today is not the first one.[^1][^3]

In a November 2020 post, Mountain Legacy called Mockingbird Hill the staffed successor to Black Rock, lower down and with milder weather.[^4] The 2020 trip report also says the lookout is still active.[^1] In June 2023, a group from the Alberta Wilderness Association walked up the fire road and was given a tour of the lookout, where its members learned how observers spot and report fires.[^2] One member said he had lived in the lookout during the 1970s.[^2]

Alberta bars the public from climbing lookout towers, treats lookout dwellings as private, and asks visitors not to count on a tour of the cabin.[^5] A gate near the trailhead keeps off-road vehicles out.[^1] The trip reports disagree on the climb: 350 m (1,148 ft) in 2020 and 300 m (984 ft) in 2023.[^1][^2]
