Bad Luck Point is a former Forest Service lookout in the Bitterroot National Forest. It sits on the edge of the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness, about 13 miles north of the Magruder Ranger Station.[^1]

The lookout was an L-4 cab on a 20-foot pole tower, built in 1939.[^1][^2] One lookout history gives 1938 instead.[^3]

Staff lists name observers in 1955 and in many years from 1959 to 1976.[^1][^2] The first name is William Moen, who worked part of the 1955 season and was also on Mt. George that year.[^1] Linda Ambelang was listed in 1974 and 1975, and a 1975 newspaper item quoted by one history says this was her second season there.[^1][^3] Alexia Cochrane is the last name listed, for 1976.[^2]

A 1971 newspaper item, also quoted by one history, says the Bitterroot forest had closed the lookout that year.[^3] The staff list shows it unstaffed in 1971 and names observers again from 1974.[^1]

The lookout was gone by about 1980.[^1][^2][^4] One history gives a later date, c.1983, for its removal.[^3]

A 2018 amateur-radio activation report says the summit is now largely wooded, with only a handful of concrete blocks left.[^4] The road runs out at Paradise Work Center, deep in the wilderness, and the trailhead sits across from the Work Center's upper access road.[^4] It is a long climb of about 2.5 miles to the summit, with no water on the way.[^4] Nothing stands at the site to climb.[^4] We found no other description of the site today.
