Huff's Knob, on Flat Top Mountain, was the site of a Civilian Conservation Corps project in 1936 and 1937.[^2] A February 1936 notice planned an 80-foot forest fire lookout on the knob, sitting on the Raleigh-Mercer county boundary, with a glassed room at the top of the mast.[^2] The work was undertaken under the state conservation department, and Camp Raleigh crews did the road and the tower erection.[^2] In December 1936 the crew dug the footings for the four legs and was nearly finished with a log cabin for the observer at the foot of the tower.[^2] A December report gave the steel as being for a 79-foot structure, which does not match the 80-foot plan.[^2] By May 1937, E.N. Wriston and ten CCC men were putting up the 80-foot steel structure.[^2]

The tower was staffed by the state until 1990.[^1] The Register describes it as the final lookout in the state to keep a regular watch.[^1] The state's encyclopedia says West Virginia's forest fire towers were closed in 1990, and that fewer than a dozen remain standing, none of them in service.[^3]

The Register places the tower on Flat Top Mountain, where it is now surrounded by a cluster of antennas.[^1] The Register lists the West Virginia Division of Forestry as its administrator.[^1] A 2024 state records request asked about the ownership and use of Tower Road and the tower.[^4] The state marked the request granted, but the page does not show what was found.[^4] The sources do not agree on the county. The Register lists Raleigh County, the 1936 reports describe the Raleigh-Mercer line, and the 2024 state entry places the tower on the Mercer/Raleigh line.[^1][^2][^4] The Register does not describe public access, so this story does not recommend climbing the tower.[^1]
