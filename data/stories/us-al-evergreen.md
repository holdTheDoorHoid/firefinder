The Alabama Forestry Commission finished building the Evergreen Lookout Tower in 1949.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 100-foot Aermotor MC-39 with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] Thomas Kaufmann, in a 1819 News column on Alabama's towers generally, says the MC-39 was the most widespread steel lookout model in the state's forests.[^3]

The Register places the tower at Evergreen in Autauga County, and it gives the elevation as 604 feet, or 184 metres.[^1] The Register entry was added on June 23, 2009, with Thomas Kaufmann as nominator.[^1] An OpenStreetMap entry for the site calls it a lattice observation tower, but it lists the elevation as 174 metres, which does not match the Register.[^2]

Kaufmann's column also reports that the state holds on to roughly a third of the lookouts it once had, and that many of those left are unsafe and climbing them is illegal.[^3] Those points are about the state as a whole, not this tower specifically.

The sources read say nothing about this tower's fire history, the people who staffed it, or its condition since the Register's entry. No visit report appears on the Register page, so whether the tower still stands is not confirmed here. Anyone curious about it should contact the Alabama Forestry Commission before going to look.
