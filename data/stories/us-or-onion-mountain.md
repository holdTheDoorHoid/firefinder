Onion Mountain's lookout history goes back further than its current building. Around 1915 or 1916, the Forest Service put up a 20-foot platform tower on the summit; a D-6 cupola replaced it in 1924.[^1][^2] During World War II, in 1942, an Aircraft Warning Service cabin was added to the site.[^1][^2][^3] The present structure — a 10-to-12-foot treated-timber L-4 tower — went up in 1952 and is the building that still stands on the mountain today.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout stayed staffed through the 1990s.[^1][^2] Accounts differ on exactly what happened next: Firelookout.com reports that it went onto the Forest Service's rental program, came back into staffed use briefly in 2007, saw continued use into the early 2010s, and has sat unused since.[^2] The Forest Service's own Recreation.gov page lists it as a rental cabin in the Rogue River-Siskiyou National Forest, but opens with a notice that the facility is closed to reservations until further notice — consistent with Firelookout.com's account of the site currently sitting idle.[^2][^3]

Getting there is not casual. Reaching the lookout requires roughly an eight-mile drive on gravel Forest Service roads off Riverbanks Road south of Grants Pass, and when it is open for rental, guests must pick up a key in person from the Wild Rivers Ranger District office in Grants Pass several days ahead of arrival.[^3] The Forest Service warns that the tower makes the cabin unsuitable for families with young children, though pets are welcome.[^3] From the catwalk, visitors get wide views across southwestern Oregon, with the Oregon Caves within about an hour's drive and Crater Lake National Park also reachable nearby.[^3]
