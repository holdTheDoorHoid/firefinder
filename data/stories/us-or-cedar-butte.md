Cedar Butte rises about 12 miles northeast of Tillamook, in Tillamook County.[^2][^1]

Sources disagree on the first structure. The Forest Fire Lookout Association says a rag camp and a pole platform went up in 1917, firelookout.com lists a crow's nest and cabin, and a weebly page calls it an observation point that year.[^1][^2][^3]

After the 1933 Tillamook burn, a 1936 newspaper report said a new lookout was planned for Cedar Butte.[^3] The first tower came in 1937, a 40-foot structure with a cab.[^1][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association says a forest fire destroyed that tower in 1938.[^1] Oregon Hikers instead dates a fire that hit the lookout to 1939, without saying which tower it meant.[^4] A new 40-foot tower with an L-4 cab went up in 1939.[^1][^3]

In August 1939, during the Trask fire, a newspaper reported that Gene Scott, the Cedar Butte lookout, had broken through a ring of fire around his station and then traveled four miles along the Wilson River, swimming and wading.[^3] The lookout was activated for the Portland Filter Center on March 18, 1942.[^3] Lookout duties moved to a new lookout at Triangulation Point in 1955.[^3] A weebly list marks the Cedar Butte lookout as removed, with no date given.[^3]

The 1939 tower is now gone.[^1] Oregon Hikers describes two benches on the former site, with views west down the South Fork Kilchis valley to the ocean. It says broken glass from the torn-down cabin is scattered around the site.[^4] The hike is short but steep, about 1.6 miles with 780 feet of climb.[^4]
