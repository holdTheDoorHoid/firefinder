Signal Peak rises to 3,286 feet on state land between the North and South Forks of the Toutle River, in Cowlitz County.[^1][^2]

The Washington Department of Natural Resources built a 40-foot wooden tower here in 1959.[^1][^3] It was constructed by the department's own carpentry crew.[^2][^4] A 1958–60 DNR biennial report, quoted on the Washington Lookouts site, said that crew had finished nine lookouts in two years at an average cost of $8,000 each, one of them at Signal Peak.[^4]

The lookout was staffed until 1967.[^1][^2][^3] All three sites say it was gone by 1970.[^1][^2][^3] No source we read says how it came down.

The route to the summit is a drive-up on good gravel roads, according to WillhiteWeb.[^2] Getting there means passing through Weyerhaeuser land, with many gates along the way.[^2] WillhiteWeb's page, which lists a 2017 site visit, includes a photo captioned as one of the lookout footings.[^2] The same notes expect the summit to have no view for some time, likely until a harvest around 2045.[^2]
