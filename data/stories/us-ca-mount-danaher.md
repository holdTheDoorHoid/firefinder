Mount Danaher is a 100-foot steel lookout on the grounds of a California forestry station near Camino, in El Dorado County.[^1][^3] A 1933 trade report describes a Civilian Conservation Corps camp near Camino, on property given by the Michigan-California Lumber Co., and says the crew also built an 80-foot lookout tower.[^2]

The register describes the tower standing today as a 100-foot steel tower with a metal cab, built in 1949 on land given in 1930.[^1] A Division of Forestry photograph from August 1946 already shows a ranger station and lookout on the site.[^5] The records I found do not explain how the 1933, 1946 and 1949 lookouts relate to one another.

In August 1938 the old Six-Mile House, a roadside stop on the route to Lake Tahoe, burned. A lookout at the Mt. Danaher station had noticed smoke from its roof, and by the time the crew arrived the roof was fully ablaze.[^2] In May 1955 state foresters announced a three-day test, planned for June, to study whether TV cameras could replace or supplement human lookouts. The test was to run from a lookout tower atop Mt. Danaher.[^2]

The lookout was closed in 1987, according to a compilation of these newspaper items.[^2] The register recorded the tower in January 2002.[^1] In 2006 the Forest Fire Lookout Association's restoration grant program funded new shutters and stair treads for the 100-foot tower.[^3]

Today the register places the tower on a secure site, kept up for emergency use and for its historic value.[^1] A PeakBagging photo page reports that Cal Fire looks after it and brings in staff as needed during fire season.[^4]
