Lonny Gadberry built the Juniper Point lookout for the Chelan National Forest in the 1930s.[^1][^2][^3] The sources do not agree on the year. The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says he developed it in the late 1930s, while WillhiteWeb says the mid-1930s.[^1][^3]

A 2015 news report quotes a photographer who visited the site as saying there was never a tower there, only a platform in a tree.[^5] WillhiteWeb describes a sawed-off fir with a small platform of thin sticks and a 15-foot ladder, with an Osborne firefinder resting on one fork of the stump.[^3] Forest Lookouts calls it a crow's nest platform in a topped-out tree, with a tent camp.[^4]

Panorama photos were taken from the crow's nest in 1938.[^3]

The site was abandoned in the early 1940s.[^1][^3] Recycled telephone wire helped the tree stand until it collapsed, around 2007.[^1][^3]

A 2015 news report says the fire-scarred tree and the firewood were still there.[^5] That does not match the collapse date given above, and the report does not explain the difference.[^5]

The Register lists remains at the site: the tent floor, flagpole, woodpile and food cooler.[^1] WillhiteWeb also lists the crow's nest remains.[^3] Forest Lookouts says some decaying artifacts remain.[^4]

The Register lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator, but it also places the site within the Loomis State Forest.[^1] These sources do not settle which agency manages the land now.[^1][^5]

We found no source that says whether the public may enter the site today.[^1][^3] WillhiteWeb describes a walk from a parking area: cross a small creek, follow an old road, then continue on a slight uphill walk above the cliffs to reach the point.[^3] A 2015 news report calls the site remote and long-abandoned.[^5] Plan the walk carefully, and check with the land manager before you go.
