Border Peak sits about 23 miles north-northeast of Thompson Falls, at 5,422 feet, in Sanders County.[^1] Listings place the lookout under the Lolo National Forest.[^1][^2]

Work on the lookout was under way by October 1931.[^2] A newspaper item of October 7, 1931, reported that Bob Paulin and Jim Carter were building a lookout house on Border Peak, as quoted on a Montana lookout history page.[^2] The design was an L-4 cab.[^1] In general terms, a local history column says the L-4 was far and away the favorite live-in design. It describes the cab as 14 by 14 feet, of wood frame, with windows on every side.[^4]

Sources disagree on how the cabin ended. One listing says it was destroyed in 1962.[^1] Another lists the site only as removed, with no date.[^2] A state lookout list marks it as gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3] Anyone planning a trip should check with the Lolo National Forest first.
