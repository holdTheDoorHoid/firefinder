In spring 1930 the Los Angeles County Forestry Department put up a tower on Pacoima Peak, just northwest of its warehouse in Pacoima.[^1] Forest Lookouts quotes a county annual report for the year ending June 1931, which says the tower was meant to overcome haze over the foothills between Pacoima and Big Tujunga canyons, haze that impaired views from the Oat Mountain and Mt. Lukens towers, and that the peak was county property.[^2] A November 1931 item in The Forest Worker, also quoted there, describes the tower as built on county property.[^2]

By the early 1950s most of the county's foothill towers had closed, Pacoima Peak among them, according to Angeles Adventures, which also says smog had reduced visibility.[^1] That page lists the tower as demolished, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks it gone.[^1][^3]

Today the lookout site is part of the hilly background around the airport, the county fire warehouse and Jessup Park, according to Angeles Adventures.[^1] No source describes any remains on the peak, and none says whether the hilltop is open to visitors.
