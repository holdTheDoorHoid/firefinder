Stillman Point is a lookout site in the Nez Perce National Forest, in Idaho County, at 5,070 feet.[^1][^2][^5] Most sources say a 30-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab went up there in 1933.[^1][^2][^3]

A 1981 history of the Lochsa Ranger District tells a different story. Louis Hartig's report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, describes a 100-foot steel tower with an observatory enclosed in glass at the top, built in 1939, and a log cabin added in 1941.[^4] The report says the lookout was last staffed in 1979 and then abandoned.[^4] It also says the cabin was condemned, then burned, that year, though the steel tower was still standing.[^4]

The other sources describe the pole tower as destroyed. Firelookout.com says it was destroyed about 1954, and the 2010 Forest Service list also gives 1954, with a margin of error.[^1][^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts marks the site as destroyed and hike-in.[^2]

The sources do not agree. The 1981 history describes a steel tower standing that year, while the others say the pole tower was gone by about 1954. None of them explains whether these describe the same structure.[^1][^3][^4]

No source I read describes the site today, or says whether the ground is open to the public.[^1][^2][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^5] In the sources I read, the steel tower appears only in the 1981 report, which is more than 40 years old, so treat any claim that it still stands with caution.[^4] Do not climb any structure there without checking that it is safe and that the land is open to you.
