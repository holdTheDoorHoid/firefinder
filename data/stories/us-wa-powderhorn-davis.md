Powder Horn Peak, roughly nine to ten miles northeast of Woodland, first carried an L-4 cab.[^1][^3] An August 3, 1930 newspaper reported that the Washington Forest Fire Association had opened its new lookout, with Edward McGrill as lookout.[^3] It had a telephone link to the other ranger stations. From it, the lookout could watch all of eastern Cowlitz County and the neighboring land in Lewis and Skamania counties.[^3] Sources disagree on the year: firetower.org and firelookout.com say 1931, while WillhiteWeb and the 1930 newspaper point to 1930.[^2][^1][^4][^3]

In the 1940s a two-story, 10-by-18-foot "smokehouse" tower replaced the cab.[^2] A 1947 state report describes that tower, with its living quarters enlarged.[^3]

I.G. Driver manned the station in 1953 and again in 1954, with only his dog for company, and was due to be a senior at Columbia that fall.[^3]

In 1960 a 20-foot wooden live-in tower from the state Department of Natural Resources replaced the smokehouse.[^2][^4] A state report covering 1958 to 1960 says the department's own carpentry crew finished nine lookouts in those two years, one of them at Powderhorn, at an average cost of $8,000 apiece.[^3]

In August 1969 The Oregonian reported that the lookout had been discontinued because of air patrols, and that it would most likely be burned.[^3] That was a forecast, not a report of a fire.[^3] A plan to relocate the tower to Clark College in Vancouver for display, scheduled for 1970, never went ahead.[^2][^4] Firelookout.com lists the tower's years as 1960 to 1970, which conflicts with the 1969 discontinuation.[^1][^3]

Firetower.org says the tower is now gone, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as gone.[^2][^5] The latest dated on-site detail is a firetower.org photo captioned "Equipment on site" and dated July 24, 2000.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the summit today.

WillhiteWeb describes a low mountain with a rocky summit that should offer views, reached from Highway 503 not far from I-5, and it describes no structure on the summit today.[^4] No source says who owns the land or whether the summit is open to the public. WillhiteWeb's page title also says Davis Peak, but the association lists a separate ground site by that name near Cle Elum.[^4][^5][^6]
