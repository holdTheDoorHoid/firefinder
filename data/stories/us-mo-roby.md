The Roby fire tower is just outside the community of Roby, in Texas County, on Mark Twain National Forest land.[^1][^2] It is a 100-foot steel tower, erected in 1934.[^2] The Register calls it allegedly the first fire tower in Missouri.[^1] A Missouri Conservationist history of the towers notes that most of the state's towers were about 100 feet tall, and that fire towers sat on high ground for a wide view of the country.[^3]

The first lookout named in our sources is Kimrey, a Forest Service lookout who was in the tower in February 1937 when he saw smoke over a ridge at about six in the evening of February 11.[^2] The Houston Herald printed the account on February 25, 1937. It said reaching the fire took two hours and ten minutes, because of muddy roads and a half-mile walk in the dark.[^2]

In November 1938 The Oak Tree, a CCC camp newspaper, reported that Palmer Russell had been sent to Roby Tower to work with the local towerman. The reason given was worsening fire weather, which meant towers had to be manned for longer hours.[^2] The same paper also calls Roby Missouri's earliest tower.[^2] A Rolla Herald item from November 24, 1938, says a night guard and a towerman had been assigned to the tower.[^2]

The Register added the tower on August 13, 2026. Its photo credit is dated September 28, 2018.[^1] A 2014 Houston Herald headline said the tower complex "remains intact", but the article is behind a subscription, so we read only the headline.[^4] We found no visit report from 2024 to 2026, so we cannot say whether the tower is open to climbing or to visitors. The Register lists the Houston, Rolla and Cedar Creek ranger district as a cooperator, so ask there before you go.[^1]
