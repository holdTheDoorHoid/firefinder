The Eastover lookout was built around 1936 as a 120-foot (37 m) Aermotor steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts lists it in Richland County under the South Carolina Forestry Commission, and the register says the Commission named it Eastover Tower.[^1][^2]

On Commission towers, an operator's hours in the cab depended on fire danger.[^3] On high-danger days, an operator could spend up to ten hours in the cab each day.[^3]

The Commission shut down its fire tower system on October 1, 1993.[^3] Forest Lookouts says the tower changed hands and moved to private property in 1995.[^2] The register says Dr. Ken Huggins bought it that year and moved it to family property.[^1] He sold it to Ray Littlejohn in 2007; the register adds that it had yet to be moved after that sale.[^1]

A magazine feature recalls a visit to Huggins's property, where a decommissioned fire tower rose about 120 feet from the forest floor. The visit took place about fifteen years before the article was written.[^4]

Today the register lists Ray Littlejohn as the owner.[^1] The register's note that the tower had yet to be moved after 2007, together with the magazine's visit, suggests it may still stand on Huggins's family property, but neither source confirms where it stands now.[^1][^4] Because it is privately owned, ask the owner before going near it.[^1] Forest Lookouts does not say what remains at the original Eastover site today.[^2]
