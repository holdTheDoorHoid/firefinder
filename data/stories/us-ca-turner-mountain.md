Turner Mountain is a lookout in Tehama County, on the Lassen National Forest.[^2] The peak stands about 6,900 feet high, and the Forest Service administers the site.[^1][^2]

The first structure on the summit was a 10-by-10 lookout house.[^1][^2] In 1913 three of these were built on the Lassen forest.[^2] They were first put together to plan, then taken down, bundled and marked, and hauled toward the site.[^2] The one for Turner or Bald Mountain came with a stove, a fold-up bed, a table and a telephone.[^2] In 1934 an Aermotor observation cab, 7 by 7, was built atop a steel tower of about 60 feet.[^1] A second, ground-level cabin of a standard Region 5 plan is also listed, but the register gives no date for it.[^1]

The lookout was in use early.[^2] In late September 1914 a lookout there counted seventeen balls of fire when Lassen Peak began to erupt, according to newspapers of the day.[^2] The register prints that date with the wrong year.[^1] By late September 1915, A.E. McDonnell had taken over from J.M. Stark as lookout.[^2]

Water was a steady problem.[^2] In October 1915 a supervisor began installing a summit water tank, to be filled with melting snow.[^2] Heavy snow later crushed it. By 1930 the crew had found a spring under the rock that gave about 20 gallons a day.[^2]

On July 3, 1937, lookout Oscar Edwall spotted smoke at 1:14 in the morning, and a crew from Mineral was on the way within a minute.[^2] That August the lookout and an ERA foreman remodeled and painted the tower and put in new windows, and the Susanville paper gave its height as 65 feet.[^2] The register says 60 feet, so the height is not settled.[^1][^2]

A 2002 photo caption calls one view the new and active lookout and labels another photo as the old tower, but the captions do not say when the change came.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list still marks the tower and its ground cabin as standing.[^4]

Today the register lists two lookout structures on the site.[^1] The sources read do not say whether the public may visit or climb the tower. Check with the Lassen National Forest before going, and do not climb a tower that is closed.
