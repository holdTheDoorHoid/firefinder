Josephine Peak rises to 5,558 feet in Los Angeles County, inside the Angeles National Forest.[^1] The register dates the lookout to 1935 and describes a Region 5 Plan BC-301 design on a short timber tower.[^1] It also notes that some accounts say the structure came from Mount Lukens, though photos of that lookout suggest an earlier design.[^1]

Another history gives a different date for the move. It says the Mount Lukens tower, standing there since 1923, was relocated to Josephine Peak in 1936 because urban smog had disrupted visibility for fire detection.[^3] The sources disagree on the year and the tower's origin, so 1935 should be read as the register's figure rather than a settled one.

Byrd K. Walsh and family were the first lookout staff to live in the glassed-in tower.[^3] A 1939 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout history site, named Vaughn Turner as the lookout on Mt. Josephine.[^2] In 1960 a jet crashed on the mountain's southern face and started a brush fire, killing a Marine captain.[^3][^2] Peggy Hotchkiss, the lookout that year, was evacuated by truck due to the fire danger.[^2] The sources disagree on the fire's size, one giving 15 acres and the other about 20.[^2][^3]

By 1972 smog had greatly reduced visibility, and the forest staffed the peak only when fire danger was extreme. Barney Donaldson was among the last attendants.[^3]

The lookout burned in the 1975 Big Tujunga Fire, also called the Mill Fire, and was not replaced.[^1][^3] All personnel got out safely.[^2] In 2019 the Angeles National Forest Fire Lookout Association used the summit for fire detection while the Vetter Mountain lookout was rebuilt.[^1]

Today only the concrete footings of the tower remain, next to a small shed that carries communications masts.[^3] California's transportation division installed a summit benchmark on a concrete slab a few years after the fire.[^3] That page says visitors hike up to take in wide views of the Big Tujunga watershed and the Los Angeles basin.[^3]
