The Shoestring Butte Lookout Site sits at 7,280 feet (2,219 meters) in Lake County, Oregon, on the Fremont National Forest.[^1][^2] In May 1931 the summit was chosen as an auxiliary lookout site, with plans for a fifty-foot wooden tower.[^3] The Aermotor steel tower that went up that year is listed at 73 feet, with a 7-by-7 cab and a one-room, 12-by-16 living quarters.[^2][^3]

Sources disagree on the height. The register gives 100 feet for the steel tower.[^1] A 1931 entry on the Forest Lookouts page describes a seventy-five-foot tower nearing completion.[^3]

In early August 1931 lookouts could not pin down a fire that started on Cox Creek. Though only days from completion and staffing, the tower covered the area where that fire burned.[^3] It was equipped with an Osborne No. 4 firefinder.[^3]

Later records describe upkeep. A 1948 entry notes major work on the living quarters, including new cabinets, linoleum and a new roof.[^3] A September 1959 regional inspection judged the grounds clean and in good order.[^3] By October 1961 the tower had no living quarters, and the Forest Supervisor asked the Regional Forester for funds to buy a trailer house as living quarters if funds for that replacement could not be found.[^3] Work completed in June 1962 included checking the screens and adding safety devices to the trap door.[^3]

In the fall of 1967 the forest offered two steel lookout towers, this one included, to the Army, which did not take them. The forest then planned to try selling the scrap metal.[^3] In October 1968 the Forest Engineer reported that both towers had been sold for scrap but not yet removed.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both give 1968 as the year the site was gone.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes what remains of the tower, cabin or summit today, or whether the public may reach the site.
