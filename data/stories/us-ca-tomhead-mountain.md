Tomhead Mountain Lookout stands on Tomhead Mountain in Tehama County, California, at 2,061 meters, in land the Shasta-Trinity National Forest administers.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the present lookout to 1939 and describes a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin built on a steel tower 20 feet tall.[^1]

Lookouts were on this peak before that. A 1916 newspaper item says Wilson Slater spent the summer there as the Forest Service lookout.[^2] Two 1920 articles describe Abbie Brownlee, whose name is spelled two ways, at the lookout and near her cabin. One puts the lookout on a commanding spot near the Sacramento valley's edge.[^2] So 1939 most likely dates the building people see today, not the first lookout, though the sources do not say whether it replaced an earlier structure.

Staffing ran through the 1930s and 1940s. G. H. McCausland went on duty in June 1933.[^2] Lee Braden served from 1932 until 1942, and his wife Dolly took over the duties in 1942 and stayed on through the wartime staffing shortage.[^2] In 1960 a missing man was traced to a canyon bottom after the lookout spotted smoke from his campfire.[^2]

The register also holds a 1973 visitor's note from a man who says he was the lookout that summer and that the post was shut down after he left.[^1] That is one person's recollection, not an official record.

The lookout came through the 2019 South Fire. The register says it survived that September, and that the building was wrapped to prevent damage.[^1] The FFLA California list still shows the lookout as standing.[^3] I did not find a current access or rental notice in the sources I checked, so this history is not a guide to visiting.
