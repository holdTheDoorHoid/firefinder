The Forest Lookouts page for Lufkin quotes a March 1974 item from the Mexia Daily News. The item said a 100-foot lookout tower was planned for the grounds of the proposed Texas Forestry Museum in Lufkin, and that the Texas Forest Service would keep ownership of it.[^1]

Texas Highways, writing in March 2026, puts the tower's construction in 1936 and says it came to the museum from near Conroe.[^3] The 1974 item describes a tower still to be built, so it does not match the 1936 date, and the sources we read do not settle the question.[^1][^3]

Bob Bowman's TexasEscapes.com column says the tower left its site near Conroe in 1976.[^2] The museum opened that same year to hold relics of the state's timber era.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas index lists a Lufkin tower as gone.[^4] That entry conflicts with Texas Highways, which describes a standing tower at the museum, and the index does not say which location it means.[^4][^3]

Texas Highways says the tower overlooks the museum grounds, and gives the museum's address as 1905 Atkinson Dr. in Lufkin.[^3] Bowman also reports that the tower was modified to block would-be climbers.[^2] Entry to the indoor displays costs nothing, thanks to donors.[^3] No source we read describes the tower's base or whether visitors can walk up to it.[^2][^3]
