Red Plume is in the Great Bear Wilderness of the Flathead National Forest, about 17 miles southeast of Essex.[^1] Firelookout.com dates the original lookout there to 1928, a D-6 cupola cabin.[^1] An April 1954 newspaper account says it was built on the ground, partly because hauling and moving timbers was impractical.[^2] Charles Shaw, then a smokechaser, was in charge of building it.[^2]

In late March 1954, treated timbers were air-dropped from a Johnson Flying Service DC-3.[^2] The April 1954 account says the new lookout would stand on a 15-foot tower.[^2] A September 1954 item gave the height as 10 feet.[^2] Firelookout.com also gives 15 feet, and says the replacement went up in 1957.[^1] The Montana Lookouts page says the new tower was completed in 1955.[^2] The sources disagree on the completion year.[^1][^2]

Elevation is listed two ways too: 7,947 feet on firelookout.com, and 7,500 feet in the 1954 account.[^1][^2]

Firelookout.com lists Leif Holgerson as a lookout there in 1957 and Kenneth Thompson in 1958.[^1] An oral history interview records Britt Franklin working there as a lookout in 1967.[^3]

The April 1954 account says the old lookout had been declared unsafe for further occupancy.[^2] Firelookout.com says the replacement was destroyed in 1991.[^1]

No source describes what remains at the site today.[^1][^2] The Forest Service's Great Bear Wilderness page says rules on food storage, camping and trail conditions are posted on its separate wilderness page.[^4]
