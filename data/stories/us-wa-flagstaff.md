Flagstaff Mountain sits four miles west of Northport in Stevens County, Washington.[^1][^4] Fire detection began on the summit in 1935, when a 40-foot L-4 tower was built.[^2][^4] WillhiteWeb says that date is only reported, and that the tower probably came a few years later.[^6]

In 1931 the Colville Examiner reported that spur lines would be built that summer to Flagstaff Mountain and other peaks, to cut the time it took to find fires.[^5][^6]

In 1952 a pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced the first one.[^2][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com give its height as 40 feet, but WillhiteWeb says 50.[^2][^4][^6]

The register says the site passed to the Washington Department of Natural Resources in the late 1940s.[^1] In 1971 the DNR built a 40-foot timber tower with a live-in cab.[^2][^5] Firelookout.com says summer staffing ran into the 2000s.[^4] The register listed the lookout on November 21, 1999, and called it one of the few DNR lookouts still active in the state.[^1] Firelookout.com names Frank Kjolseth and Leon Hammer as lookout staff in 1957 and Monita Engvall in 1958.[^4]

In 1958 the Air Force acquired more than 50 acres by lease for a gap-filler radar station.[^5][^6] WillhiteWeb says the station was completed in 1959, deactivated in September 1960, and that removal of its improvements was contracted in September 1961.[^6]

In August 2013 the lookout was removed to make way for a communication site.[^6] The tower was taken apart and the cab moved to Northport in 2013.[^2][^6] The 2013 chapter report says the lookout came down so that more cell towers could go up, and that Northport adopted it.[^3]

The cab now sits in Northport's town park.[^3][^2] WillhiteWeb reports a gate and says a walk is needed to reach the site.[^6] None of the sources I read describe what remains of the older towers on the summit, and I did not find a description of the summit as it is today. The Washington Department of Natural Resources administers the site, so check with the DNR before going up the mountain.[^2]
