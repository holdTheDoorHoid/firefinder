Coyote Point is a former lookout site in the Umpqua National Forest, in Douglas County, Oregon, at about 3,093 feet (943 meters).[^1] The register and firelookout.com both date the tower to 1935.[^1][^3] Rex's Fire Lookout Page describes it as a 14-by-14 L-4 lookout house on a 30-foot round timber tower.[^2]

A Roseburg News-Review item dated April 17, 1936, quoted on Rex's page, says a new lookout house went up on Coyote point, in the southeastern part of the district.[^2] That item gives no construction year, so it does not settle the 1935 date.[^2]

A second News-Review item, dated September 10, 1937, says Joe Chamberlain was then responsible for the lookout station at Point Coyote.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed in 1960 and destroyed that year.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was abandoned in the 1950s, so the two sources give different dates for the end of its use.[^3] Firelookout.com and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list both mark the tower as gone.[^3][^4]

The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as the administrator of the site.[^1] None of the four sources describes what, if anything, is left at the site now.[^1][^2][^3][^4] We do not know whether any part of the lookout is still on the ground, and we found nothing on whether the public can reach the spot.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Anyone planning a visit should check with the Umpqua National Forest before setting out.
