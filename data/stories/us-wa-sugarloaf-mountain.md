Sugarloaf Mountain Lookout stands on Sugarloaf Peak in the Wenatchee National Forest, about 12 miles north-northeast of Leavenworth in Chelan County, Washington.[^1][^2] The site is also known as Sugarloaf Peak Lookout.[^4] Fire detection began on the summit in 1914, when a heliograph station was set up, and that first station was little more than a camp.[^4][^1] A D-6 cupola cabin followed in 1924.[^2][^4] WTA dates the start of lookout activity to 1924 instead.[^3]

In 1949 the present L-4 cab was moved up from Lion Rock, where it had been built in 1932, and it replaced the cupola.[^4][^2] The register gives the cab's vintage as 1933, so the build year differs between sources.[^1] Willhite's history says the building was sawed into sections for the move.[^4]

The register says the lookout has been staffed every summer since it was established in 1914.[^1] Firelookout.com lists known lookout staff by year, including Ed Kassell in 1920 and Gladys West in 1922.[^2]

The Entiat Fire of 1970, which burned 120,000 acres, threatened the lookout.[^1] In 1994 the Tyee-Hatchery Fire threatened it again. A Fort Lewis fire crew saved the cab, but the outhouse burned.[^1][^4] The register also says the garage burned, while Willhite mentions only the outhouse.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com says the cab survived and the outhouse did not.[^2] In 2025 the Lower Sugarloaf fire threatened the lookout again.[^2]

The lookout is still in use, and Willhite notes that this makes it one of the longest continuously used lookouts in Washington.[^4] It is listed on the National Register of Historic Places under number 90001914.[^5]

Visitors drive gravel roads to the spur road, which has a gate, so the last part is a short walk.[^4] WTA calls it a drive-up lookout and says the destination is only about a quarter mile from where you park.[^3]
