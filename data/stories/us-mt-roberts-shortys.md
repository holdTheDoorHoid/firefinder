Roberts (Shorty's) began as a crow's nest about 1920.[^1] In 1924 it became a 40-foot steel tower with a 6-by-6-foot wooden cab, two miles southwest of Fortine, Montana, in the Kootenai National Forest, at an elevation of 3,790 feet.[^1] Permanent cabin quarters came in the 1940s.[^1]

The tower was moved to the Eureka city park in 1979.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list now marks the original site as gone.[^4]

Today the tower stands at the Tobacco Valley Historical Village in Eureka.[^2] A 2024 newsletter from the Northwest Montana Lookout Association said the group had helped complete a project telling the story of Roberts Lookout, which now sits there.[^2] The village lists a fire tower among its buildings and artifacts, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's list shows the relocated tower as standing.[^3][^4] The village grounds are open year-round.[^3] The museum and the other buildings are open from Memorial Day through Labor Day, 1 to 5 p.m.[^3] We found no source saying whether the tower itself can be climbed, so check with the village before you plan a visit.
