The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Towerview Fire Tower in 1935 and 1936 for the Tennessee Division of Forestry, at Stoney Point in Dickson County.[^1] It is a 70-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab. The towerman's home was a 14-by-24-foot chestnut log cabin.[^1]

The sources we read do not say who staffed the tower during its working years or whether it watched any notable fires.

In 1997 Joel and Jason Clark bought the tower and moved it intact by truck, along with the cabin, about 12 miles to their Towerview Farm.[^1] A 2018 WKRN report says instead that the tower came from an adjacent property.[^2] The two accounts disagree about where it came from. We follow the Register here, because it gives the distance.[^1]

The Clarks also acquired a forestry fire truck. The Register says it remains active as the Towerview Farm Independent Volunteer Fire Service.[^1] The Register added the tower on March 1, 1998, as US 252, TN 4.[^1]

A table of extant towers in a National Register study, as of March 26, 2014, lists Towerview among them.[^3] Tennessee Landforms lists the Stony Point tower in Dickson County as moved, which fits this history.[^4]

A 2018 WKRN visit described a working farm that had been in the Clark family for generations, and reported that visitors could climb the tower to see the view.[^2] Tennessee Landforms lists Towerview as standing, though it gives no date.[^4] That was one farm visit eight years ago, and we found nothing newer about access. Ask the owners before you go, and do not climb a tower on private land without permission.
