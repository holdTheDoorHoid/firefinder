The U.S. Forest Service put up the Rich Mountain tower in 1932. A 14-by-14-foot cab on top was built so a lookout could live there.[^2] The metal tower is about 30 feet tall, though one guide says 31.[^1][^2] The tower sits on the North Carolina and Tennessee line inside Pisgah National Forest, a short distance from the Appalachian Trail.[^1]

Lookouts staffed the tower into the 1990s, though it stopped housing a resident lookout in the 1970s.[^2] Aerial detection, and later public reports by cell phone, proved more cost-effective and quicker. Most of North Carolina's fire towers were taken down.[^2] Weather and vandalism then took a toll on Rich Mountain.[^2]

A partnership of the Forest Service, the Appalachian Trail Conservancy, the Forest Fire Lookout Association and the Carolina Mountain Club restored the tower in 2018.[^2][^3] Robbinsville-based Williams Construction completed the work, and the Carolina Mountain Club built a log staircase to the base.[^3] A Forest Service fire officer who once acted as lookout in that tower spoke at the ribbon cutting in November 2018.[^4][^5]

The two states' trail license-plate programs gave $3,500 from North Carolina and $5,000 from Tennessee, and the Forest Service provided the bulk of the money, $101,158.[^3] A travel guide credits much of the funding to the trail group's plate program instead, so this account follows the newspaper's figures.[^2]

Visitors can reach the tower by a short side trail.[^3] A gravel road of about six miles also climbs to the top from the Hot Springs side. It is usually shut during winter.[^2] The register lists the tower as unstaffed.[^1]
