Butler Peak Lookout overlooks Big Bear Lake from a rock outcrop in the San Bernardino National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The peak is named for George E. Butler, a local politician and Bear Valley property owner.[^3]

Local papers followed its construction in 1934 and 1935. A March 1934 report said workers from the City Creek camp would build lookouts on Butler and Morton peaks.[^2] A February 1935 report said a forest crew would begin building the lookout on top of the peak, and an October 1935 report said construction was ending.[^2] The cab is a standard 14-by-14-foot wooden house set on a rock that was blasted flat. Because of the peak's elevation, no tower was needed.[^1][^2] A telephone line and a three-mile road connected the station to the Green Valley-Fawnskin truck trail.[^2]

The sources disagree on the start date. The Register gives 1935 as the build year, but its description says the CCC built it in 1933. This story uses 1935, which the Register's build field and the newspaper reports support.[^1][^2] The sources also give three elevations: 8,419 feet in the Register, 8,502 feet in the 1935 report, and 8,535 feet on the Foundation's page.[^1][^2][^3]

The earliest staffing in these sources is a June 1938 report that the lookout had opened with Forest Lookout Walter Fieg in charge.[^2] Through the 1950s, The Grizzly named the lookouts season by season, often married couples, and by mid-August 1952 it had counted the 465th visitor of that year.[^2]

In 1970, lookout staff member Eileen McMahon spotted the Bear Fire, which destroyed 49 homes and burned 53,000 acres.[^3]

Bruce D. Risher was the last paid lookout operator, in 1981.[^1] The tower then went out of service, and volunteers took over its staffing when it reopened in 1986.[^1] Storm damage to the access road led to a three-year closure, and Southern California Mountain Foundation volunteers were staffing the lookout again as of August 2022.[^1]

The Foundation says visitors can go up from Memorial Day to November, and that the lookout was open for the 2025 season.[^3] The Register lists normal hours of 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. daily, in a season from Memorial Day weekend to Labor Day weekend, sometimes longer.[^1] The Foundation warns that the dirt road and trail have had up to five feet of snow as late as July 4, and it recommends a high-clearance vehicle.[^3] The directions run from Highway 38 on the north shore of Big Bear Lake to Fawnskin, then along Forest Roads 3N14, 2N13 and 2N13C.[^3] The Register says the lookout will celebrate its 90th anniversary in 2026.[^1] The sources do not say whether the 2026 season is set, so check with the Foundation before you go.
