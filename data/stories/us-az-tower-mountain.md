Tower Mountain Lookout sits at about 7,590 feet in the Prescott National Forest, in Yavapai County, and drivers on Interstate 17 can see it.[^1] Daily patrol work from a lookout here began in 1911.[^2] A tower there was reported complete in February 1924, although the paper said the smoke-finding apparatus and fire-fighting gear were not yet installed.[^2]

The register dates the present tower to 1933.[^1] It describes a 30-foot D-2 style tower from Pacific Coast Steel, with a modified L-4 cab.[^1] In September 1933 a Prescott paper reported that two of the forest's five lookouts would get new 30-foot steel towers, and that one would go up near Crown King on Tower Mountain.[^2] The cabins were to be 12 feet by 12 feet, with glassed sides and insulation against lightning.[^2] The newspaper announced the plan but did not confirm the finished work, so the 1933 date rests mainly on the register.[^1][^2]

The lookout had at least one known occupant in the 1930s.[^2] In May 1938 a ranger placed John F. Alpine on the lookout in the Crown King district.[^2] I found no notable fires tied to this tower in these sources.

The register recorded the lookout on August 5, 2003, and said it continued in active service.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] The register gives 7,590 feet for the lookout, while a trail guide gives 7,628 feet for the top of Towers Mountain, which may be a different point on the summit.[^1][^4]

A trail guide describes a forest road from Crown King up Towers Mountain, past communications towers, to the lookout area.[^4] I could not find a current opening notice or staffing report. The register names the Bradshaw Ranger District as a cooperator, so ask there before you go, and do not climb the tower.[^1]
