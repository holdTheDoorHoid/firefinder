The James T. Saban Lookout was first named the High Park Lookout. It is a stone and natural wood structure, 14 feet square according to the register and Wikipedia, though the nomination gives a 20-foot exterior footprint.[^1][^2][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps completed it in 1942, following standard Forest Service plans.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com describes it as a two-story L-4 cab.[^5]

The work ran past the summer of 1941, when the supervisor had planned to finish the lookout. A 1941 letter says the rock work would need a stonemason.[^3][^4]

The Chief of the Forest Service renamed the lookout for James T. Saban on April 7, 2015.[^1] Saban had been a CCC foreman and had also served as a Forest Service ranger. He lost his life fighting the Blackwater Fire on August 21, 1937.[^1] That fire killed 15 men and burned more than 1,200 acres of Shoshone National Forest land.[^1]

Paid lookouts staffed the tower until the late 1970s, and volunteers took over until the mid-1980s.[^3] A 1956 memo reported 496 visitors that season.[^4] The Forest Service says three CCC-built lookouts in the Bighorn Mountains remain, and this one is staffed only in the seasons of highest fire danger.[^6]

The register entry says the lookout was added to the National Register in December 2016. Wikipedia gives November 15, 2016.[^1][^2] The nomination calls its present condition very good and faithful to its original form.[^3] Repairs included refinishing the wood floor and replacing the top-story entry door.[^3]

The Forest Service lists it as a recreation site, with seasons of use that depend on the weather and a rule to pack out everything brought in.[^6] The page does not mention overnight rental, so check with the Powder River Ranger District office in Buffalo before you go.[^6] From Ten Sleep, head east on Highway 16 for roughly 23 miles, then turn right onto Forest System Road 429 and follow the signs.[^6]
