Schoodic Mountain, a 1,069-foot peak in Hancock County, got its first fire lookout in 1914, when a wooden tower went up as part of the state's system of watchtowers.[^1] Six miles of telephone line ran from Franklin Station to the lookout, and a camp was put up for the watchman.[^2]

A state photo caption names Wendall Harrison and Howard Webb of North Sullivan as watchmen from 1917 through 1919.[^3] A gale in October stripped the roof from the lookout and sent it roughly 100 rods, and the lookout still needed a new map and glass.[^2]

The Maine Forestry District swapped the wooden tower in 1920 for a 24-foot steel tower that came with a cab.[^1][^2] Maine's staffed fire-tower network ended after a budget crisis in 1991.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association still lists the Schoodic tower as standing, though its cab is missing.[^5] The Forest Lookouts record also notes that a "lookout structure" was removed in 2005. It is unclear whether that meant only the cab, so the sources do not agree on what remains.[^2][^5]

The most direct way up begins at the Schoodic Beach parking area and climbs about 1.3 miles to the summit.[^6] The mountain lies within Donnell Pond public land.[^7] One hiking site notes a communications tower on the summit, so do not mistake it for the old lookout, and do not assume the old tower is open to climbers.[^7]
