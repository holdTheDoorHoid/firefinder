The Margarita Lookout site sits in the Santa Margarita Mountains, part of the larger Santa Ana Mountains in San Diego County, California.[^1] It lies about a mile north of Margarita Peak, inside the Cleveland National Forest.[^1] The lookout was also known as Santa Margarita Lookout and as Margarita Peak Lookout.[^1][^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the first tower there in 1935.[^1][^2] It was a steel K-brace tower with a wood cab, built to a Region 5 design.[^2]

In October 1938, a California Ranger item mentioned a lookout named Charlton on Santa Margarita Peak.[^3] The Forest Service replaced the tower in 1965 with its standard CL-104 design, a steel K-brace tower with a steel cab.[^2] On August 23, 1969, a fire in Devil's Canyon, in the northeast corner of Camp Pendleton, spread into the forest and surrounded the tower. The garage burned, but the tower did not.[^3] A wildfire in 1986 burned over the 1965 tower and cabin.[^1][^2]

On March 2, 1988, a Marine Corps CH-46E helicopter on a training exercise collided with the tower.[^3] The four crew members suffered only minor injuries.[^3] The collision badly damaged the lookout, and it was then taken down.[^1][^3] Wikipedia and the register both describe it as destroyed in the collision.[^1][^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register listed the site on February 17, 2009, as US 517 and CA 3, with Brad Eells as nominator.[^2]

Wikipedia says that only a concrete foundation is left at the site today.[^1] The spot can still be reached by trail, via Margarita Road/Truck Trail, but no tower stands there to climb.[^1]

Sources disagree on elevation.[^1][^2] Wikipedia gives 3,087 feet (941 m) for the highest point, while the register gives 2,502 feet (763 m) for the tower site.[^1][^2]
