A lookout went up in 1940 on a high peak about four miles east of Markleeville, and the register places the site atop Mogul Peak.[^1][^2] The register calls the design unique and says the lookout was built for the Mono National Forest, which in 1945 was split into two forests, Inyo and Toiyabe.[^1]

Work had started the previous fall but stopped for the weather.[^2] That spring a spike camp near the Markleeville ranger station was set up to finish the building, the road and a telephone line, and a crew from the Galena Creek camp was still completing the building that summer.[^2] The station was meant to cover the Wolf Creek country, where lightning-caused fires had been a frequent problem.[^2]

By late July 1940 a fire lookout was on duty.[^2] By August the Nevada State Journal reported the building complete, with a man posted there during thunderstorm spells.[^2] The paper also said the building was being fitted with copper wire as lightning protection.[^2]

The register does not say when the lookout was removed, and notes that only the foundation is left.[^1]

Today the register lists the location as Toiyabe National Forest.[^1] A trail guide lists a 15.5-mile out-and-back from the Barney Riley Trail trailhead that reaches Mogul Peak.[^3] None of the pages read says how easy the foundation is to find from that route.
