Reyes Peak rises to about 7,500 feet on Pine Mountain in the Los Padres National Forest.[^1][^2][^4] A lookout went up on its summit in 1927.[^1][^2][^4] Newspapers had discussed a station there as early as 1922, when they called the peak the highest point in its district.[^3] The cabin was a California Region 5 design with a 14-foot-square cab on an open timber frame.[^1][^2] Sources disagree on the frame's height: the Forest Service register gives 20 feet, while Craig Carey describes a 14-foot wooden frame.[^1][^2]

In September 1932 the lookout was William Green.[^3] Newspapers reported that he got out with most of his instruments as flames reached his post.[^3] The Matilija Fire started in early September 1932 and burned close to 220,000 acres of the forest.[^5] The fire destroyed the lookout that month, and the Forest Service register says it was never rebuilt.[^3][^1]

No tower stands on the summit today.[^4] A 2011 visit found nothing left but footing pipes, plus scattered debris that included a bed frame and part of a roof.[^4][^2]

Getting there means walking about a mile from the Reyes Peak trailhead, with some steep stretches.[^2] None of the sources read describes a closure. The newest visit among them is from 2011, so check current conditions with the national forest before you go.[^4][^2]
