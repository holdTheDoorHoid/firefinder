Horseshoe Mountain was a patrol point in Ferry County, Washington, on a small, isolated parcel of Bureau of Land Management land northwest of Republic.[^1][^2] The lookout was a 20-foot pole tower with an open observation platform.[^2] The register calls it a patrol lookout point tied to the Bodie Mountain lookout to the north, and says it had a ground return telephone.[^1]

A 20-foot tower was built on Bodie Mountain in 1930, and the last lookout there was removed or destroyed in 1975.[^4]

A Curlew Job Corps forestry class came across the site while studying forestry nearby.[^1] Jim Beckwith, the class's instructor, eventually reached a person who wanted to look into the remains, and Eastern Washington director Rod Fosback went out with the students in mid-October.[^2]

The remains showed the tower had been guy-wired to the rocks, and decayed stumps marked trees cleared from the sight line toward Bodie.[^2] The searchers found no sign of a cabin or tent site.[^2]

The write-up says a phone line with white insulators ran along the stock trail to the lookout.[^2] Trail signs carried dates back to 1932, but the write-up does not connect those dates to the tower.[^2]

A photo on the register is captioned "Platform Tower Ruins."[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Washington list marks the site as gone.[^3]

No source describes current access or what can be seen there today.
