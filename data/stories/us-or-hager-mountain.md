Hager Mountain has been watched for smoke since 1915, in the Fremont National Forest about 13 miles northwest of Summer Lake.[^1][^2] The first structure here was an octagonal cab built in 1912.[^2] It was replaced in 1936 by an L-4 cab, a style used widely across the Forest Service system in that era.[^2] The lookout standing today, an R-6 "flat top" ground cabin, went up in 1967; its flat roof was swapped for a shallow hip roof in 1992, the shape it still has.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register gives the mountain's elevation as 7,188 feet; Recreation.gov gives 7,195 feet.[^1][^3] Recreation.gov reports views reaching Mount Hood and Mount Shasta on a clear day.[^3] The lookout was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register in 2022, nominated by Brad Eells.[^1]

Hager Mountain is still a working lookout. Forest Service staff keep watch from it during fire season, and from November 15 through May 15 the Forest Service rents it out through Recreation.gov as a winter cabin, reached only on foot, snowshoes, or skis.[^3] The 14-by-14-foot room holds three beds, a woodstove and propane stove, and sleeps up to four; there is no water on site.[^3] For the rest of the year it returns to its working job, watching the flat country around Summer Lake for smoke.[^1][^3]
