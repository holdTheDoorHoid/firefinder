High Rocks is a summit of about 5,190 feet in Swain County, North Carolina, inside Great Smoky Mountains National Park.[^1][^2][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built a fire tower and a cabin on the summit between 1935 and 1936.[^3] The steel tower stood 46 feet tall, lower than the standard 60-foot towers elsewhere in the park.[^3] A park document written in October 1935 records that seventeen men were assigned to build the tower and cabin.[^3]

The cabin housed the lookout on duty. From there the lookout passed findings to the dispatch office over a two-watt radio that ran on batteries.[^3]

The tower came down later, and the sources give different dates. A 2010 personal essay says the park used a helicopter to take the tower down in the late 1980s.[^6] SummitPost, which cites a 2016 Asheville Citizen-Times article, says the tower stood until the early 1990s.[^4] The Mountain Press says only that the tower was removed, and gives no date.[^3]

Today the cabin still stands, and a SummitPost photograph from March 2022 shows it in terrible condition.[^3][^4] The same site says just concrete pedestals are left where the tower stood.[^4] A federal survey team photographed the tower's pilings and the guard house in May 2009.[^5] No tower is left to climb.[^3][^4]

The site lies inside the national park.[^1][^3] SummitPost describes a route whose main trail is Bear Creek Trail, which you follow for about 4.8 miles.[^4]
