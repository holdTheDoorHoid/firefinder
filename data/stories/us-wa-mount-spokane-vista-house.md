The Vista House on Mount Spokane is a two-story stone building at the summit of Mount Spokane State Park.[^1][^2] It was built in 1933 as an emergency relief project, using native stone from the summit.[^3][^1] Work was completed on November 21, 1933.[^3] Contractor Einar Ole Fieldstad built it from a design by Spokane architect Henry Bertelsen.[^3][^6] Some accounts credit the Civilian Conservation Corps, but TrailChick says a local contractor won the job.[^4]

The Department of Forestry made a late request to include a fire lookout in the building, and it became the second-floor lookout room.[^3][^5] A 2026 SpokaneTalk article reports that by 1950 the building had shifted from lookout to gathering place.[^6]

The summit towers had a rougher history. A wooden tower built in 1948 collapsed under ice within about a year.[^1][^4] NHLR dates a shorter tower to 1959, with its successor about 13 years later, while TrailChick dates the shorter tower to 1950 and its successor to 1963.[^1][^4] The 1979 tower stayed in service until 1994, was dismantled in 2001, and moved to Quartz Mountain in 2004, where it still stands and can be rented overnight.[^4] NHLR, which registered the site in 1993, describes that tower as still in service at the time.[^1]

The stone building belongs to the State Parks Commission, and the summit towers are held by the Department of Natural Resources.[^1] The Vista House was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on December 7, 2018.[^2] Its Spokane Register listing is dated April 16, 2019 in one table and June 3, 2019 in the text.[^2]

Today the building serves the visiting public, with a public gathering room, and TrailChick says you can drive very near the summit except in spring.[^2][^4] The park covers nearly 14,000 acres and is the largest in Washington's state park system.[^2]
