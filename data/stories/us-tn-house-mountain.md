House Mountain is in Corryton, Knox County, Tennessee, about eight miles (13 km) northeast of Knoxville.[^3] The state's list gives the site an elevation of 2,103 feet (641 m) and the tower number 2-3.[^1]

A WVLT feature on East Tennessee's fire towers says the House Mountain tower was built in the 1950s.[^2] The same feature notes that most of the state's fire towers are no longer standing.[^2]

The sources disagree about whether this tower survives. The state's list marks it as removed, and was last revised in January 2026.[^1] The WVLT feature describes the House Mountain tower as still used for teaching.[^2] I could not settle which is right from the sources I checked.

Do not confuse the lookout with the broadcasting tower on nearby Zachary Ridge.[^3] That tower is 1,751 feet (534 m) high, stands about half a mile east of the mountain, and was built in 1963.[^3]

Part of the mountain is a state natural area, designated in 1987, and it is known for the views from its two peaks.[^3] No source I read says whether the old tower site is open to visitors.
