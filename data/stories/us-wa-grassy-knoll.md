Grassy Knoll is a meadow in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, in Skamania County.[^3][^4] A 10-foot wooden L-4 lookout cab went up there in 1934, about 2.5 miles southeast of the Big Huckleberry lookout.[^1][^2][^3]

All three web sources give that year and say the cab was replaced in 1952.[^1][^2][^3] They disagree on the new cab's size. Former Fire Lookout Sites (FFLOS) says 15 feet, while firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say 20 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

June 1953 newspaper accounts say high winds blew the station down and demolished it.[^4] One places that in the year before, and another says January 1953.[^4] By late June the Forest Service had dropped about half of the 20 tons of materials for a 14-foot-square building by parachute. It planned to pack in the rest by mule over about a month, to have the lookout up before the fire season.[^4]

The sources give no date for when the rebuilt cab came down. FFLOS and firelookout.com call the date unclear, and WillhiteWeb has no word on it.[^1][^2][^3]

WillhiteWeb's undated trail description says the knoll is a meadow reached by a dirt road with potholes and then a hiking trail.[^3] That route was formerly the Pacific Crest Trail.[^3] WillhiteWeb describes meadow flowers, long views and what is left of the lookout, and says all four anchors are still there.[^3]
