Deep Creek is in Grays Harbor County, about ten miles north-northwest of Hoquiam.[^1][^2]

The first lookout was a two-story cabin, 10 by 10 feet, of the Cowen type, built in 1942.[^1][^3] The Aircraft Warning Service activated a station at the site on March 28, 1942.[^3][^4] When the service ended in 1944, the state kept the site for its original purpose.[^3]

The forestry division staffed the lookout in the 1950s.[^4][^3] It closed for the season on September 13, 1952, because fog made lookouts useless at that time of year, and Frances Getty of Copalis Beach had tended it.[^4][^3] A May 30, 1957 report said it was now in operation, with Dorothy Smith of Porter on duty.[^4][^3]

In 1954 a 40-foot wooden DNR live-in tower replaced the cabin.[^1][^2][^3] It came down in 1968, and sources differ on whether it was removed or destroyed.[^1][^2][^3][^4] A 1970 USGS note says the house had been torn down and would not be replaced.[^3]

Willhite Web says most of the summit was removed and has become a deep gravel pit.[^3] On a 2020 visit, its author found a battery, flat glass and some metal pieces at the edges of the former summit.[^3] The sources describe no standing structure there today.[^3]

Reaching the site means walking.[^3] Willhite Web gives the hike as 1.6 miles one way from Failor Lake, and says a private timberlands permit is officially required, though people are rarely met on the route.[^3] Access through the watershed gate on Highway 101 north of Hoquiam runs from late April to October or Labor Day.[^3]
