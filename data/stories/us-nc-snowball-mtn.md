The state's list of North Carolina lookout towers gives a Snowball Mtn in Buncombe County.[^1] No source read confirms that the list's entry is Little Snowball, so that match is an inference. This story follows the lookout known as Little Snowball, which the Big Ivy Civilian Conservation Corps camp built in 1934.[^2] Its cab was 14 by 14 feet, set on a 21-foot steel frame, and it watched over a fire-prone stretch of the Great Craggy Mountains in the Pisgah National Forest.[^2][^3]

Mote Allen, who had helped build the tower as a Corps member, was its first lookout.[^4] Only two people ever worked the lookout.[^2] Cling Webb took the tower over in 1944.[^4]

In 1980 the Forest Service was done with the tower, which was scheduled for demolition.[^2] Lloyd Allen, who had grown up in the Big Ivy community, bid $300 for it and spent the next year taking it apart by hand.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says volunteers dismantled it in the 1980s, while the Big Ivy blog says Allen removed it around 1985, so the sources differ on who took it down.[^3][^4] Get Up and Go Camping says the pieces then sat in storage for more than a quarter century, while the Big Ivy blog says twenty years.[^2][^4]

It was re-erected at Big Ivy Historical Park in 2006, though the Big Ivy blog dates the dedication to 2007.[^2][^4] Today the restored tower stands at Big Ivy Historical Park, 550 Dillingham Road, in Barnardsville.[^2][^4]

The original site, in national forest land north of the Parkway near Craggy Gardens, has no tower now.[^2] The original spot still has the view the tower was built to watch.[^2] Reaching it on foot takes a hike, not a drive, and trail conditions can change, so check before you go.[^2]
