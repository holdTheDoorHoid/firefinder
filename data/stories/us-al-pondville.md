The Pondville Lookout Tower stands on the Talladega National Forest in Bibb County, Alabama. The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower went up in 1937 to guard 82,000 acres that the Resettlement Administration had recently obtained.[^1]

The Talladega National Forest dates from a July 17, 1936 proclamation by President Roosevelt, which drew it from the Talladega and Oakmulgee purchase units.[^2] That was a year before the tower went up.[^1]

The U.S. Forest Service administers the tower, and the Register lists the Oakmulgee Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] In March 2012 the agency signed a decision to replace the metal building at the tower's base with a pre-cast cement building.[^3] According to the project notice, the new building would protect communication equipment that fails in summer because of poor insulation.[^3]

The Register accepted the tower on December 8, 2011, as US 939, AL 58, with Bob Pasquill as nominator. It gives the elevation as 569 feet (173 meters).[^1]

We found no sources on who staffed the tower, which fires it watched, or whether the public can climb it today. The most recent source we read is the 2012 decision, so the tower's present condition is unconfirmed. Check with the Forest Service before planning a visit.
