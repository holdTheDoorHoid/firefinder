The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list includes a tower called Stony Man, listed for Madison and Page counties and marked gone.[^1] The peak it is named for lies in Shenandoah National Park.[^2] A park marker ranks Stony Man as the park's second-tallest peak, at 4,011 feet (about 1,222 m), and explains the name: someone thought a rock outcrop on the peak looked like a man's stony face.[^2]

The marker stood near Luray, in Page County, and the HMDB entry says the marker itself is gone.[^2] A WillhiteWeb page on the park's lookouts says the park had six lookouts inside its boundaries. It names six: Hightop, Millers Head, Hogback Mountain, Trayfoot Mountain, Old Rag Mountain and Lewis Peak. It also names Fork Mountain, which it places just outside the boundary. Stony Man is not on that list.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association list names a Stony Man tower, but the park list does not include one, and neither source says a tower stood on the summit.[^1][^3]

The same WillhiteWeb page says the park had stopped staffing its five towers on a regular basis by the late 1950s, and that by the early 1960s it had begun to dismantle, scrap or sell them.[^3]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit. The marker points visitors to the Stony Man Nature Trail, which starts 3.1 miles south of the marker, at the Nature Trail lot by Skyland's north entrance, for the views from the top.[^2]
