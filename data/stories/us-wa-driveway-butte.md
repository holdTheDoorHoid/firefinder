Driveway Butte rises in the Okanogan National Forest, about 20 miles northwest of Winthrop.[^2] Willhite says the name comes from an old cattle or sheep drive.[^4]

A slant-walled cupola cabin went up on the butte in 1931.[^1][^2][^5] Firelookout.com says it was first set atop a 15-foot pole tower.[^2] That July, a Wenatchee paper reported that Ed Allen and Raymond Johnson were building another lookout tower and house there.[^3]

In 1938 a 30-foot treated timber L-4 tower came after the cabin.[^2] WTA says the 1938 tower was built for wider sightlines.[^5]

Bob Watkins was the lookout in 1932.[^4] In 1933 a Wenatchee paper described Don Watkins as ranger at the lookout, busy decorating the inside of his tower house.[^3]

Both structures were destroyed in 1953.[^4][^2][^5] The Register instead says the cupola cabin was removed that year.[^1]

Today, WTA describes what remains as mostly tower foundations and rusty nails, along with a reference point marked by an arrow and a benchmark.[^5] Willhite's photo captions point to two footings and part of the cupola still at the site.[^4]

The route starts at a signed trailhead off Highway 20, near the Klipchuck Campground.[^4] The site is a hike, not a drive-up.[^5] WTA gives 8.6 miles round trip, with the lookout site 4.3 miles from the trailhead, and says all or part of the trip can be done from late March through November.[^5] Willhite lists the hike as 4 miles, a little shorter than WTA's 4.3-mile figure.[^4] The trail passes through the aftermath of the 2003 Needle Creek Fire.[^5]
