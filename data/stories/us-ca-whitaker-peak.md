The Angeles National Forest built a lookout on Whitaker Peak in 1934.[^1] The cabin followed California Region 5 Plan BC-301 and sat on a 10-foot tower of open timber.[^1]

In October 1959 officials said, in an Arizona Republic report quoted on a lookout history page, that a lookout at the Whitaker Peak station had seen a man set a fire and drive away.[^2] A third blaze on Whitaker Peak itself burned about 100 acres and led to the lookout tower being cleared.[^2]

The register gives no removal date for the tower.[^1] A 2012 hiker's blog found rumors of a lookout at the end of the road, and says the 1988 Whitaker Peak map labels one, but that the lookout has been completely removed.[^3]

A 2022 trip report on Peakery describes a long, unshaded fire-road walk to Whitaker Lookout. The writer then visited the lookout point and a point with towers.[^4] The report does not say what the towers are, and no source we read describes a lookout structure standing there today.[^4] Hikers reach the area on foot.[^3][^4] A 2012 post says the writer parked by a gate after following a faded sign along the old Golden State Highway.[^3] We found no official notice about access, so check Angeles National Forest notices before you go.
