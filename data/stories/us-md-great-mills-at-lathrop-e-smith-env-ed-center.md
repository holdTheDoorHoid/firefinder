In the summer of 1933 Maryland put up a 120-foot steel lookout tower at Great Mills, in St. Mary's County.[^1] The state forester said it should improve the fire record in southern St. Mary's County, which has high-value pine land on the bay's western shore.[^1]

In November 1933 the Evening Star reported that the tower had just been completed on a hilltop.[^1] The paper described it as the tallest fire tower on Maryland's western side of the bay, built of steel throughout and overlooking the St. Marys River.[^1] The Midland Journal described the tower as galvanized steel.[^1]

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched.

The state's draft fire tower report says the state had given the entire property to St. Mary's County by 2006.[^2] The tower structure was then removed and reassembled for new use at the Lathrop E. Smith Environmental Education Center.[^2] The report places that center in Montgomery County and gives no date for the move.[^2]

The Lathrop E. Smith center is owned and run by Montgomery County Public Schools, inside Rock Creek Regional Park, at 5110 Meadowside Lane.[^3] It hosts residential programs for sixth graders as well as day programs.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing with its cab gone.[^4]

We found no account of public visits to the tower. Because it sits on a school system site, check with the center before going, and do not climb it without permission.[^3]
