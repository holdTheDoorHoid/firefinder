Buffington is listed as a fire tower site in Cherokee County, Georgia, at 34.2382, -84.43521. The list gives its elevation as 1,236 with no unit; read as feet, that is about 377 m above sea level.[^1] The list ties the site to two Georgia Forestry Commission codes, BLL-41140 and gfc-727.[^1] It says the commission supplied coordinates under its BLL numbers, and that an older commission list used the gfc numbers.[^1]

The list's name, "buffington," is also the name of an unincorporated Cherokee County community.[^2] That community began at Fort Buffington, a collection point in the Cherokee removal.[^2] The same list says names for towers numbered 73 and above are the compiler's own creation, so "buffington" is a label from that list.[^1]

A Forestry Commission district supervisor told a newspaper in 2006 that the state's fire tower count had reached a high of over 200 in the 1960s.[^3] A commission spreadsheet from that year counted 137 of the state's 144 surviving towers as unstaffed.[^3] At that point the towers were being auctioned, with some going for scrap and others to hunting clubs or similar groups.[^3]

No source I found describes what remains at the site today, or whether it is open to the public.
