Curly Bear Mountain sits inside Glacier National Park, about five miles south of Saint Mary, in Glacier County.[^1] The lookout was a two-story frame cab on a mid-slope hillside half a mile north of the summit, at about 6,500 feet (1,981 m).[^1][^3]

The sources disagree on the year it was built. Firelookout.com, the Forest Lookouts page and the Montana History Portal all give 1934.[^1][^2][^4] WillhiteWeb says the cab went up in 1935, and that panoramic photographs were taken from the site that year.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page dates a set of panorama photographs to July 26, 1935.[^2]

A 1949 guidebook briefly mentions the trail to the lookout, which was then in use.[^3]

Heavy spring rains washed out the trail in 1948.[^2][^3] A Hungry Horse News item dated September 17, 1948, reported that a trail camp was being set up that week to rebuild it.[^2]

The National Park Service burned the cab in April 1963.[^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts page says the cab was burned because it was no longer useful.[^2] The Montana History Portal and WillhiteWeb both record the loss in 1963.[^3][^4]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today. A December 2009 post on the Glacier National Park Chat forum said a climbing guide by Dr. Edwards indicated the old trail could still be followed. The poster wondered whether that still held, given the years since the book came out.[^5]

The site lies inside Glacier National Park.[^1] Anyone planning a trip should check current park trail information before setting out.
