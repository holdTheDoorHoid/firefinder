The Cajon Summit lookout stood at the top of Cajon Pass in San Bernardino County.[^1] The state's forestry agency sited it to watch an area where trains often started fires.[^1] In February 1932 a San Bernardino County Sun item said the summit site had beaten several other locations in surveys, and that a tower costing about $1,200 was planned.[^2] By late May 1932 a Healdsburg Tribune item said construction was under way.[^2] The register gives 1932 as the year it was built.[^1]

The register calls it an unusual build: a small lookout tower with a walkway set over the living space.[^1]

In August 1939 a San Bernardino County Sun item said Don Wadel, a member of the state fire crew in Colton, had been transferred to the lookout station at the pass summit.[^2]

The old National Trails Highway, also known as Route 66, passed the site too.[^1] The register notes that Interstate 15 has since replaced it.[^1]

The register says the building deteriorated and was taken down, probably in the late 1980s.[^1] It gives the removal only as "1980s".[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the summit today, or whether visitors can reach it.
