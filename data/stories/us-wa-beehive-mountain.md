Beehive Mountain sits about six miles southwest of Wenatchee, Washington, in the Wenatchee National Forest.[^1][^2] A fire lookout stood on the peak from 1931 until 1973.[^2][^5]

The first lookout went up in 1931.[^1] The first cab was an L-5 model sitting at ground level.[^3] In 1940 an 18-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced it.[^1][^3]

The Wenatchee Daily World reported in June 1935 that two Civilian Conservation Corps workers from Camp Icicle had been assigned to Beehive's emergency lookout.[^4] A lookout-history site lists observers from 1947 through 1963, starting with Don Wehmeyer.[^2] In August 1935 the lookouts passed a fire call from a Squilichuck rancher to the district fireman, but they could not see smoke where the caller pointed.[^4]

The register says the lookout was removed in 1973.[^1] The state's Department of Natural Resources had built its Harriets Peak lookout about five miles away the year before.[^1] Firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb both say it was destroyed that year, so the sources differ on the wording.[^2][^3] A photo caption on WillhiteWeb calls a 1963 structure the second Beehive lookout, which does not match the 1940 date the other pages give.[^1][^2][^3]

Today, WillhiteWeb reports that only a concrete footing, a few eyebolts and the base of a storage building remain.[^3] No tower stands there now.[^3] A SummitPost writer said the summit offers wide views over the east side of Mission Ridge.[^5]

Forest Road 9712 branches off Mission Ridge Road and goes much of the way up to Beehive Reservoir.[^5] A locked gate sits on the road about half a mile past the reservoir, and the last stretch is a walk up a gated logging road.[^3] SummitPost says the road is closed in winter, so check its status before you go.[^5]
