In 1941 the Missouri Conservation Commission bought ten acres near Mineola, in Montgomery County, to build a fire tower.[^1] A Warrenton newspaper reported that the commission planned a 68-foot (21 m) steel lookout tower on the site, for spotting forest fires.[^1]

The commission's report records the steel tower as erected in 1942.[^1] In February 1942 a woods fire of about four acres, southeast of Danville, was put out. The newspaper placed it about four miles from the Mineola hill site, where the commission was then constructing the tower.[^1]

A state manual for 1945–46 lists the towersite as owned by the commission, at 10.2 acres, close to the ten acres bought in 1941.[^1]

In 1959 a new quarter-ton jeep was allotted to Harry Sowers, the towerman at Mineola, to replace the 1952 jeep he had been given.[^1]

In 1960 vandals overturned picnic tables, broke benches and tore down signs at the tower site. A conservation agent put the damage at about $250, and one 17-year-old boy was sentenced to 90 days in jail.[^1]

The last dated entry on the Forest Lookouts page is from 1960. I found no other source that says whether the tower still stands or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1]
