Signal Peak Lookout stands on a summit in Mariposa County, on Sierra National Forest land southwest of Yosemite.[^1][^3] The peak is also known as Devil's Peak.[^4][^5]

A lookout has stood here since 1900, when a compass lookout was built on the site.[^2][^4]

The early history is disputed. The register describes a 12-by-16-foot ground house with an octagonal second story added in 1911. The volunteer history puts the 12-by-16 cabin in 1911 and the second story in 1916.[^1][^2] The present 14-by-14-foot cab on a C-3L tower was built in its current form in 1951.[^1][^2][^4] The register listed the lookout on March 31, 2005, as number 639.[^1][^2]

A 2010 Forest Fire Lookout Association report on the volunteer history page says the lookout remained staffed for fire detection, and the Forest Service made repairs that year.[^2] A 2012 report describes a new catwalk and railings, plus a privately funded microwave internet link to Miami Mountain.[^2] A caption on Peakbagging.com, attached to an October 2002 photo, says Sierra National Forest employees staffed the lookout during fire season.[^5]

Today is unclear. A volunteer history page last updated March 1, 2013, says the tower has been shut for several years because of Forest Service funding cuts, and that the public can reach it only on foot.[^2] That claim sits awkwardly beside the 2012 renovation report on the same page.[^2] An undated tourism listing counts the tower among the few fire lookout towers still in active use, and describes a dirt road that a high-clearance vehicle can brave, with several places to park along the way.[^4]

The register gives an elevation of 6,867 feet, while other sources give 6,965, 6,992 and 7,079 feet.[^1][^3][^5][^2]

We could not confirm current staffing or public access, so check with the Sierra National Forest before you go.
