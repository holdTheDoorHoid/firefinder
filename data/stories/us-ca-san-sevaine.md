The San Sevaine lookout stood in San Bernardino County, at about 5,230 feet, in the San Bernardino National Forest.[^1][^2] In March 1934 a San Bernardino paper reported planned work for a lookout tower on San Sevaine ridge.[^2] The register gives 1934/37 as the build date and says the original structure was lost in the 1936 Etiwanda Fire.[^1]

A newspaper report dated October 2, 1936 said the San Sevaine Mountain lookout had been lost. Its two occupants escaped down a firebreak; they were Lookout Oliver O. Mittman and Roy Moore, assistant ranger.[^2]

A replacement went up in 1937.[^1] A June 1937 report said Warren A. Divoll, building engineer for the San Bernardino forest, would oversee the work, and that the new lookout would have a 10-foot steel tower and a 14-foot square base.[^2] The register also describes a new 10-foot steel tower built that year.[^1] A summit page disagrees, describing a 10-foot timber tower with a 1935 date, which does not match the 1937 steel-tower accounts.[^3]

Glenn E. Downer was lookout at the station until June 1938, when he resigned to take a state lookout job on Gilman Peak in Orange County. The paper said Raymond R. Higgins would replace him.[^2]

The register does not give a removal date for the 1937 tower.[^1]

Foundations and melted glass are what remain. A summit page reports numerous foundations and considerable melted glass on the site.[^3] A hiker's trip report from February 2025 points to the knob where the lookout once stood, and says the two summit registers, kept in a PVC pipe, were drenched and largely ruined.[^4] That report gives free parking on Forest Road 1N34, which it describes as gated and open only seasonally, then a two-mile round trip on an overgrown use trail.[^4] The same report says the road was about to close for the summer and would reopen in September, so vehicle access is seasonal.[^4] We found no source giving the site's own access rules. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^5]
