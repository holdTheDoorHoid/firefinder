Mount Thompson-Seton stands at 7,820 feet in the Flathead National Forest, about 18 miles northwest of Polebridge.[^1] The site dates its cabin to 1931 and calls it an L-4 cab.[^1] The Forest Association's list classes the site as a ground structure rather than a tower.[^2]

After the 1910 fire season, the Forest Service began placing fire-watch stations on high summits.[^3] The Forest kept putting up more lookouts, the 1930s especially, and by 1939 it counted 147 lookouts and towers.[^3]

The site says the cabin was abandoned in the 1950s and that ruins were reported there in 1984.[^1] The Forest Association's list marks the site simply as gone, and neither source says when the ruins disappeared.[^1][^2]

A travel listing for the Mount Thompson-Seton Trail describes a 12.2-mile National Forest trail of high difficulty.[^4] It names remnants of an old fire lookout among the trail's special attractions.[^4] The trail begins at Whale Creek roads 318 and 1674.[^4] The listing does not say whether those remnants are the cabin site, and no source we read describes the cabin site now or whether it is open to visitors.
