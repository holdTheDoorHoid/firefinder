Mount Wright is a peak in Teton County, about 33 miles west-northwest of Choteau, in the Lewis and Clark National Forest.[^2][^1] A 1940 Coast and Geodetic Survey description places the lookout on the northern and highest peak of the mountain.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the elevation as 8,855 feet, while an October 1933 Choteau paper gives 8,865 feet above sea level.[^2][^1]

Crews were sent to the Teton in September 1933 to build a glass lookout on top of the mountain.[^1] By October 19, 1933, the first project to be completed was that lookout, and a December notice called it a new lookout house.[^1] Firelookout.com instead calls the 1930s structure an L-4 tower.[^2] The sources disagree about the type of structure, and this story reports both accounts.[^1][^2]

Firelookout.com dates a camp on the site to the 1920s.[^2] A September 1931 lightning fire was spotted by guards posted at the Wrong Creek and Mt. Wright lookouts, so a watch post was in use on the mountain before the 1933 work.[^1]

Newspaper notices quoted on the Montana lookouts page name the men assigned to the summit through the 1930s and 1940s.[^1] Elmer Allum was assigned in 1935, Houston Bosseler in 1938, Kenneth Baldwin in 1939, and Lorman Bruno in 1940.[^1] In 1943 Bill Breen, a Teton high school youth working as a lookout, put out a lightning fire in the timber on the mountain.[^1] In 1945 William C. Grater, a Forest School student from Montana State University, was stationed there.[^1]

Firelookout.com says the lookout was abandoned in the 1950s and is now gone.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list also marks the site Tower Gone.[^3]

A hiking guide describes Mount Wright Hiker Trail #160 as a route from the North Fork Teton Canyon road to the top of the mountain.[^4] The same guide notes free parking at the trailhead.[^4] It also describes a radio tower and a small building at the top, but it does not say whether either is the old lookout.[^4]
