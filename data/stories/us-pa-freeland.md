The Freeland tower stood on Freeland Heights, a tract beside the road to Butler Valley in southern Luzerne County.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says a steel P tower was in use there from 1925 to 1927, with a replacement in 1928.[^1] The 1927 newspapers describe something different.[^2] A lightning strike had destroyed an older wooden structure, and the State Forestry Department began putting up a new steel tower that November on the highest ground on the North Side.[^2]

The newspapers also disagree on the height: one notice gave the new tower as 80 feet, while another gave 85.[^2] The register gives the site's elevation as 613 metres.[^1]

In April 1930 a crew of fire fighters was stationed at the tower and was to handle forest fires in that section.[^2] In July 1939 a newspaper named Louis Corazza as the fire tower man at Freeland, and he expected the dry-spell fires to break every state record.[^2] In April 1955 Frank Swankoski was listed as the operator, and its coverage reached lower Luzerne County, overlapping the Penobscot tower's area.[^2]

The register now lists the site among former fire lookouts.[^1] The latest dated entry on the history page is the 1955 listing.[^2]

A City-Data listing of FCC records shows a "Freeland Fire Tower" radio site a short distance north of the register's point. Its license was granted to Pennsylvania's Department of Conservation and Natural Resources in September 2011 and cancelled in February 2022.[^1][^3]

No source describes what remains at the site now, or whether visitors can reach it.
