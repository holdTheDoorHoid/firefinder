Togo Mountain sits on the Colville National Forest in Ferry County, Washington, at 6,043 feet (1,842 meters).[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register lists the site as US 1474 and WA 374.[^1]

The lookout was built in 1933.[^1] A July 1933 item from the Republic News-Miner, quoted on one of the sites we read, reported that "Tux" Salisbury and twelve men were building a lookout tower and cabin on the mountain.[^3] Willhite's page names the crew as the Midget Creek Emergency Conservation Camp, F-6.[^4]

The register describes a 60-foot pole tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, along with a ground cabin used as living space.[^1] The Willhite page gives the same 60 feet.[^4] A September 1933 newspaper report called the finished tower about 70 feet high.[^3] The sources do not agree on the height, and we could not settle it.

In August 1935, The Nespelem Tribune named Vernon Cotton as the lookout on Togo Mountain.[^3] A 1935 item from Six Twenty-Six, which both sites quote, said he had shot two coyotes and a lynx from the tower. It also said that, so far, every fire had been found and put out while small.[^3][^4] We found no other staffing records and no named fires.

The register says the tower and cabin "were removed in 1952."[^1] Firelookout.com says they were destroyed that year.[^2] Willhite's page says the tower "was removed or destroyed" in 1952.[^4] The sources agree on the year but not on how the lookout left the mountain.

In a 2009 chapter report, the Forest Fire Lookout Association recorded former lookouts on the Colville and noted a "C.C.C. 33" mark in the concrete of one footing at Togo Mountain.[^5] We found no later report on that footing. A photo caption on the Willhite page reads "Lookout footings," but the page gives no date for it.[^4] We found no source that describes the site as it is today.

We found no current Forest Service statement on public access. The Willhite page gives driving directions over forest roads and says, "If the gate is open, it is a 4WD/high clearance road to the top, or a half mile hike."[^4] That account may be out of date. The tower is gone, so there is nothing to climb.[^1] Anyone going should check with the Colville National Forest first and respect any closure.
