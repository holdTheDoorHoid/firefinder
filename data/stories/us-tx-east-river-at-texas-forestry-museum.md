The 100-foot fire tower at the Texas Forestry Museum in Lufkin, Angelina County, overlooks the museum grounds.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing and as relocated from East River, a site in Harris County that the association lists as gone.[^1] Two other accounts place its earlier site near Conroe instead.[^2][^3] Texas Highways dates the tower to 1936, and Bowman's column gives the move as 1976.[^2][^3] We could not settle the origin from these sources, so the East River link should be treated as unconfirmed.

A 1952 survey station description, filed under the East River name, places a lookout tower just northeast of the station, near the edge of a clearing.[^5] That shows a tower stood near the station in 1952. It does not say the tower was later moved to Lufkin.[^5]

Bowman says the Civilian Conservation Corps built towers like the Lufkin one in 1936, and that such towers were in service from the 1930s into the 1970s.[^3] A March 1974 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page for Lufkin, reported plans for a 100-foot tower on the proposed museum grounds, with the Texas Forest Service keeping ownership.[^4] That item describes a tower to be built, not one moved, so it does not settle the question of origin.[^4]

The museum opened in 1976 to keep relics of Texas's lumber era, and its indoor exhibits cost visitors nothing.[^2]

Bowman's column, dated February 28, 2010 and updated December 6, 2012, says the tower has been changed to keep would-be climbers off, so please do not try to climb it.[^3] The association lists it as standing.[^1]
