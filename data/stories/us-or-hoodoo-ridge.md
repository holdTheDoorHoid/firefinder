Hoodoo Ridge began with a crow's nest in a tree.[^2] In 1925 the Umatilla National Forest built a wooden platform up a ponderosa pine, roughly 110 feet tall, and until 1933 it was the only structure on the site.[^2] In 1933 the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) added a steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, along with a cabin, a garage and an outhouse.[^2][^5] The register gives the crow's nest tree as 65 feet tall, while the nomination measures the 1925 tree at 110 feet.[^1][^2] The register lists a 1935 build year but also says the lookout was built around 1933.[^1]

The tower stands about 100 feet tall, though the nomination measures it at 101 feet.[^1][^2][^4] The site served as a fire lookout and, from 1933, as crew quarters until the 1970s.[^2] A lookout history site lists Doug Flaiz as lookout personnel in 1968.[^4] A 2023 visit report says the lookout stopped being staffed in the late 1960s or early 1970s.[^5]

The site was added to the National Register of Historic Places as a historic district on May 26, 2015.[^3] Earlier, the register had listed it on September 4, 2011.[^1]

Passport in Time volunteers worked on the buildings in the 2000s.[^2][^5] The nomination describes their 2001 work on the cabin as cleaning it and covering the openings to keep out rodents.[^2] A 2023 visit report calls the same work a restoration that was meant to let the cabin join a rental program.[^5] That rental never happened.[^5] Volunteers returned in summer 2002 to clean the garage and paint its outside white with green trim.[^2]

A 2023 visit found the site abandoned.[^5] Road 090 is gated, so visitors park on Road 62 without blocking the gate, walk about 0.6 miles up the road and then take a spur to the tower.[^5] The lower tower stairs have been removed to keep people from climbing.[^5] The cabin has fallen into disrepair, and the garage is in worse shape still.[^5] The National Register nomination described the tower and cab as in good condition.[^2]
