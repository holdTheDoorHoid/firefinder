Kennedy Peak sits in the Massanutten Mountain Range, near Luray, Virginia.[^5][^6] A hiking listing files the trail under the George Washington National Forest.[^5] A 2014 blog post says the Depression-era Civilian Conservation Corps first built the tower.[^2] State forestry records summarized in a 1991 survey credit that corps program with 88 lookout towers in Virginia through 1941.[^3] We did not find the year the Kennedy tower went up, what it first looked like, or who staffed it.

The structure has changed. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as two-story and still standing, with its cab no longer in place.[^1] The same 2014 post says the Potomac Appalachian Trail Club rebuilt the lookout in July 2014, with help from Fort Valley Ranch, and that the overlook rests on an old stone base from a former fire tower.[^2] A hiking guide describes an old shelter from the Depression era at the summit, without settling whether it was a lookout.[^6]

According to one hiking guide, the overlook is about 2.6 miles up the trail, at an elevation of 2,560 feet.[^4] The same guide says visitors can climb the steps for the views, and that an overnight shelter sits just below the top deck.[^4] The hiking listing puts the round trip at 6.8 miles and calls it relatively easy.[^5] It also says parking on VA 675 is no longer allowed, so drivers should use the Stephens Trailhead lot.[^5]

We saw no closure notice in these pages. Check current notices with the national forest before you go, and do not climb the tower if it is posted closed.
