Devil's Head Lookout is a Forest Service tower on a large pinnacle of Pikes Peak granite in the Pike National Forest, in Douglas County, Colorado, near Sedalia.[^2][^6] The National Register nomination says the first lookout station was set up on the summit in 1912, with a table holding a fire finder.[^4] It also says a tower built in 1919 was replaced in 1951 by the present lookout.[^4][^2] The register's own page gives 1912 for its 14-by-14-foot ground house, which does not match the nomination's 1951 date for the present cabin.[^1][^4] The 1912 date most likely marks the first station.[^4] The nomination and Wikipedia both date the present lookout to 1951, so I follow them.[^4][^2]

Staffing has been part of the lookout's story since at least 1919. The register credits Helen Dowe, a Forest Service employee, with staffing the lookout from 1919 to 1921, the first Colorado lookout staffed by a woman.[^1] In 2017 the register noted that the lookout was still staffed each summer.[^1] A 2019 note in the register described the stair treads as about 70 years old. It also said the post may go unfilled, with cameras used instead, if the current host retires.[^1] A Forest Fire Lookout Association chapter report says the lookout was not staffed in 2025 and is not expected to be in 2026.[^5] It says the lookout was considered for removal from service after the host retired, and that staffing cuts in fall 2024 appeared to be the final blow.[^5] I found no record of a fire at this lookout in the sources I read.

The register says the lookout was renovated in 2013 in a partnership between the Forest Service and HistoriCorps, with wood repaired and replaced, new roof shingles installed, and the lookout painted.[^1] A 2022 chapter report says the writer understood that Great American Outdoors Act funding and HistoriCorps volunteers had improved the stairs and replaced the aging treads. The writer had not confirmed this with the district.[^3] The register lists its own registration date as October 13, 1990.[^1] Wikipedia gives June 13, 2003 as the date the lookout was added to the National Register, and the NPGallery record for reference 03000518 is dated the same day.[^2][^6] Wikipedia also says the lookout was added to the register in 1991, which conflicts with its own date.[^2]

The 2025 report says the writer helped close the lookout up in fall 2024 and worries about vandalism and theft.[^5] The sources do not say whether visitors may enter the cab, so check with the Pike National Forest first.[^5][^2] The trail is about 2.8 miles round trip, with an elevation gain of 951 feet and a final climb of 143 stairs.[^2] The 1951 nomination counts 243 steps up the whole stairway.[^4] The trailhead road is closed to cars in winter and generally reopens in April or May.[^2]
