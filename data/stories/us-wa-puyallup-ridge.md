Puyallup Ridge stands in Pierce County, Washington, on a high knob inside the Kapowsin Tree Farm, about 12 miles west of Mount Rainier.[^1][^2][^3]

The Washington Department of Natural Resources built the lookout in 1964.[^1][^4] A Forest Lookouts page cites a Department report of a replacement tower erected on the ridge in 1962-64.[^5] The Register says the tower may be the most recent new detection site the state set up, and that its service was short-lived, phased out in the 1970s.[^1] The Register describes a 30-foot wooden tower with a 14-by-14-foot live-in flattop cab and catwalk.[^1] Firelookout.com, Will Hite and TrailChick all give the tower's height as 20 feet.[^2][^3][^4]

The Department planned to move the tower to the Pierce County Museum. The museum received the Salmon River Lookout instead, so this one stayed on the ridge.[^2][^3][^4] Sources describe only limited use over its life, and none of the pages we read name the people who staffed it.[^2][^4]

In 2017 members of the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Eastern Washington chapter helped restore the tower. Project coordinator Rick Eades obtained a grant from the Chuck Butler Memorial Fund, and the report gives no amount. The same report says access to the former DNR lookout was gained through restricted property.[^6] TrailChick says landowner Manke Lumber Company gave permission for the association's work parties, which Eades directed.[^4] The 2017 report does not say when the work was finished.[^6]

The sources disagree on who owns the land.[^1][^4] The Register says access to the lookout, which needs restoration, is by special road permit through private timberlands, and names Plum Creek Timber Co. and Champion International.[^1] Will Hite and TrailChick place the ridge in the Kapowsin Tree Farm, and TrailChick says permits come through Hancock Forest Management.[^3][^4]

The ridge is private timberland, and access is restricted.[^3][^4] Firelookout.com says it is not generally open to the public, and Will Hite says vehicle access requires an annual permit.[^2][^3] TrailChick says permits are expensive and do not allow motorized access to the summit. It describes a hike-in route of 21 miles round trip with about 2,500 feet of gain.[^4] Will Hite counts the lookout as part of the Mount Tahoma ski trail system in winter, and TrailChick says the Copper Creek Hut is about 6 miles from Puyallup Ridge.[^3][^4] Get the landowner's permission before any trip, and do not climb the tower without it.
