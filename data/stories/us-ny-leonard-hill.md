The Leonard Hill tower did not start on Leonard Hill. The National Historic Lookout Register says an 80-foot Aeromotor LS40 tower was first erected in 1934 at Gilbert Lake State Park, while Wikipedia says the tower stood there from 1932.[^1][^2] The register dates its move to Leonard Hill to 1948, while Wikipedia says the Conservation Commission built an 80-foot steel tower on the mountain that year.[^1][^2] The register's own Built field also gives 1948, which does not match its 1934 date.[^1]

Its working life on the mountain ended in 1988. Wikipedia says fire watching stopped at the end of the 1988 season, and the register says the tower has not been regularly staffed since then.[^1][^2] The New York State Department of Environmental Conservation officially closed it in early 1989.[^2]

A visit recorded in April 2017 found the bottom two sets of stairs removed to prevent climbing, and the cab floor gone after a fire set by vandals.[^1] The register lists the tower as closed to the public.[^1]

A local article from Gilboa says the tower has been closed to the public for many years.[^3] It says progress has been at a standstill for the last few years, because the state must first certify the condition of the steel.[^3] It also calls the tower the tallest in New York State, a claim that could not be verified.[^3]

Because the tower is closed and its lower stairs are gone, there is no public climb.[^1][^2] The Gilboa article adds that the state owns both the tower and the land it stands on.[^3]
