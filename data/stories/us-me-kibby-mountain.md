Kibby Mountain rises to 3,656 feet in Skinner Township, about six miles from the Quebec border.[^1] The tower is in Franklin County, and the Maine Memory Network files it under the spelling Kibbie.[^2]

Records disagree about when the steel tower went up.[^2][^3][^1] A Maine Memory Network photo record says a wooden tower from 1906 gave way to a steel tower in 1914, and that a new 14-foot steel tower replaced that one in 1926.[^2] A 2010 trip report describes a 48-foot tower erected in 1914. Heavy ice collapsed its upper sections in 1925, and they were removed, leaving a tower about 15 feet tall.[^3] A 2025 article gives 1926 as the erection date for the tower now on the summit.[^1] I could not settle which account is right, so treat the dates as uncertain.

A photo dated to about 1920 pictures a watchman and camp beside the lookout.[^2] Maine's staffed tower network ended in 1991, when the state was in budget trouble, and I found no source naming the last year a watchman worked here.[^4]

The cab is gone.[^1] The 2025 article says the cab came off and an observation platform took its place.[^1] The 2010 report dates that change to the early 1990s, and calls the tower wobbly and its platform rotting.[^3] A hiking guide instead says the observation deck was built in 1926.[^5]

Today the summit is reached on foot.[^5] The trail begins on an old logging track and climbs to the tower, a hike of roughly 2.3 to 2.6 miles depending on the source.[^1][^5] Reaching the trailhead means nearly 10 miles of gravel logging road, crossing Weyerhaeuser Timberlands land.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list shows the tower as standing, with its cab gone.[^6] A 2024 visitor found holes in the wooden platform, so stay off it.[^5]
