The Missouri Conservation Commission's annual report for fiscal 1948-49 records that a 48-acre towersite was acquired for the Dixon site.[^1] The towersite lies about three miles west of Dixon, on Highway 133 in Pulaski County.[^2]

By November 1950 the tower was in use.[^1] The Bland Courier reported that the Dixon fire tower had radioed in a fire southeast of Crocker, and that a cross-shot from the Fort Leonard Wood tower had located it.[^1] The Fort Wood towerman found the blaze only 100 yards from a barn of cattle and hay.[^1] He drove the cattle out and fought the fire until a crew from Dixon arrived.[^1]

Accounts of the tower itself disagree. The annual report for fiscal 1956-57 mentions major repairs to the Dixon tower, which suggests a tower already stood by then.[^1] The 2015 management plan dates a fire tower to 1956 and gives it a height of 90 feet.[^2] The annual report for fiscal 1964-65 instead describes a 100-foot steel tower erected at the towersite.[^1] A pond was built on the towersite in fiscal 1972-73.[^1]

Department-wide, the Missouri Department of Conservation counts roughly 50 fire towers on its property, and the towers no longer need full-time staff during fire season.[^3]

The 2015 plan lists the fire tower among the site's existing structures, and says a locked gate guards the towersite entrance.[^2] The rest of the area is open to suitable public use, and state rules apply.[^2] The plan calls these towers historically significant, and says the Dixon site may be eligible for the National Register of Historic Places.[^2] No source found describes the tower's condition since 2015. Because the gate is locked, the tower should not be treated as open to visitors, and this page does not suggest climbing it.
