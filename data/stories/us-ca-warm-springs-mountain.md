Warm Springs Mountain stands in the Angeles National Forest, in Los Angeles County, at about 4,022 feet.[^1] The lookout was constructed in 1934 to a California Region 5 plan, BC-301.[^1] The cab sat on a steel H-brace tower 20 feet high, and a trail report measures the wood cab at 14 by 14 feet.[^1][^4]

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed it. A newspaper report from August 1969, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says the tower first spotted smoke near a Los Angeles water reservoir.[^3] That same blaze shut off a power transmission line serving most of the San Fernando Valley.[^3]

The cab was lost in 1987.[^1][^2] The registry says the lookout was overrun by the Ruby Fire and burned, and that the cabin had no staff and was never rebuilt.[^1] The sources disagree on the fire's name.[^1][^2] The Angeles National Forest Fire Lookout Association calls it the Ruby Ridge Fire.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page describes a September 1987 fire northeast of Castaic Lake that burned the lookout.[^3] One trail report lists the status as demolished, even though its own text says the steel tower remains.[^4]

The steel tower still stands.[^1] The Angeles National Forest Fire Lookout Association has abandoned its plan to restore it, though it says a future project might bring the tower back.[^2][^4] A trail report describes the structure as very unstable, with planks missing, and a Do Not Climb sign is posted on it.[^4] The route is a fire road, 6.5 miles one way, from Lake Hughes Road.[^4] Please keep off the tower.
