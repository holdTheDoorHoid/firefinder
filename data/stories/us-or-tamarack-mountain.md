Tamarack Mountain, in Grant County, Oregon, got its first lookout in 1925, when a platform was built in a tree on the site.[^4][^1] A steel tower replaced it in 1933.[^1][^2] The sources agree that it is a 96-foot Aermotor, but a June 1933 newspaper called it a hundred feet high, and a 1935 item gave the top as 102 feet.[^1][^2][^3][^4] A cabin for Forest Service staff was built alongside.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates that cabin to 1934, but a January 1931 newspaper item already mentions a new cabin erected for the lookout.[^1][^3]

In 1921 Frank Bloss, an army vocational trainee, took the post, and one Tuesday that August he reported thirteen fires.[^3] In 1935 George Gillis, a member of the Lexington school faculty, had the summer lookout job.[^3] In the summer of 1938 a bolt of lightning knocked the keeper, Max Buschke, unconscious for an hour.[^3] His wife carried on his work and reported five fires set off by the storm.[^3]

In 1966 an accidental fire destroyed the cabin.[^1][^2] The garage was converted into living quarters, and that converted shed is the cabin people rent today.[^1][^2][^3] The tower was not lost and still stands.[^2] The Friends of Blue Mountain Lookouts replaced the stairs in September 2021.[^1]

Recreation.gov rents the shed as Tamarack Cabin.[^2] It sleeps four, has no water on site, and is not open in winter.[^2] Visitors may climb the tower when it is staffed, and the NHLR describes that staffing as irregular.[^1][^2] The NHLR registered the lookout in October 2021.[^1]
