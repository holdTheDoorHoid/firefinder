Dix Peak is a summit near Dixville Notch in Coos County, New Hampshire.[^1] A 2006 newspaper column calls a summit near the notch Dixville Peak.[^3] No source we read says outright that the two names belong to one summit.

The New Hampshire Forestry Commission's biennial report for 1921 to 1922 counts 29 lookout stations maintained for most or all of the 1921 fire season, a year of dry conditions.[^1] Among them was a new station on Dix's Peak, which the report calls an emergency station.[^1] In 1922 the station was temporarily shut down, except in emergencies.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Hampshire list marks the tower as gone.[^2]

That 2006 column, by Kim Robert Nilsen in the Littleton Courier and reposted on a hiking forum, describes anchor marks on Dixville Peak, a few miles south of Route 26 in Dixville Notch and 1,500 vertical feet above it.[^3] Those anchors are the only remains that any source we read describes. None says whether the summit is open to visitors, so check before you go.
