F. Forrest Pease started Opportunity Farm for Boys in New Gloucester, in Cumberland County, in 1910, with considerable support from Ida Nowell.[^4][^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a fire tower under the farm's name.[^1]

Newspaper notes printed on the Eastern U.S. Lookouts page name Harry Chadbourne as the attendant of the "New Gloucester fire tower" in 1950, and say Charles Bragdon was appointed its watchman for the 1951 season.[^2] Those notes never mention the farm, so tying them to the farm's tower is an inference.[^2]

The tower's fate is murky. The Forest Fire Lookout Association marks the Opportunity Farm tower as gone.[^1] The same list shows a standing tower at the Fryeburg Fairgrounds, in Oxford County, labeled as relocated from the farm.[^1] A Fryeburg Fair profile of Howard Hatch, who supervised that tower, calls it a replica of the Kearsarge (North) fire tower, built by the fair in 2009 and refreshed in 2012.[^3] The profile does not mention New Gloucester, so the move stays unconfirmed.[^3]

No source I read describes what, if anything, stands on the New Gloucester hill today. A Daughters of the American Revolution chapter history says the Community School of Camden merged with Opportunity Farm in 2011, and that its alternative high school program then served students at both Camden and New Gloucester.[^5] At Fryeburg, the fair's profile describes a visitor climbing a steep staircase to a second-level platform.[^3] It does not say whether that tower is open to visitors outside such a visit.
