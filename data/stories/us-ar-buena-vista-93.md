The Arkansas Forestry Commission put a fire lookout on high ground southwest of the community of Buena Vista, in Ouachita County.[^2] The Encyclopedia of Arkansas dates it to 1936 and describes a 100-foot steel tower with an enclosed cab on top.[^2] The county historical society agrees on the year and height, and puts the lookout roughly half a mile south of town, beside U.S. Highway 79.[^3] A federal survey description from 1941 calls it an all-steel tower with an enclosed cabin, placed on the north side of the highway about half a mile southwest of town.[^1] The survey and the historical society differ slightly on the direction from town, but both put the tower beside the highway.[^1][^3]

The tower was used alongside nearby towers, such as the Poison Springs Tower, to pinpoint forest fires.[^2]

The tower is gone.[^2][^3] The Encyclopedia of Arkansas says it no longer stands, and the historical society says the structure no longer exists.[^2][^3] The Forest Lookouts listing marks it as removed.[^1]

The historical society adds that the site is the county's highest ground.[^3] No source here describes what, if anything, remains there now, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
