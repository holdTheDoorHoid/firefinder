The McGowan tower stood on McGowan Mountain in Tucker County.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as gone, with the tower type unknown.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register places the tower on the Parsons topographic quadrangle and names the State of West Virginia as administering agency.[^2]

A transcription of West Virginia's forest, game and fish reports, on the Eastern US Lookouts site, lists a station under the name McGowan in Tucker County in 1913 and 1916.[^3] I found no source saying whether it was the same spot as the register's tower. The transcription records $142.50 spent on trail and telephone line construction in May 1913.[^3] Its 1916 entry places the station in Black Fork District, three miles from Hambleton, and says F.M. Glenn supervised it. It puts the elevation at 3,354 feet, with a view of about 600 square miles.[^3]

The 1916 report names Clyde Westfall as the watchman. He lived in Hambleton and stayed at the station overnight only in very dry weather.[^3] A 2.75-mile phone line, known as the McGowan Line, has been built and links to the Mutual Lines at Hendricks.[^3]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
