Shellrock Peak sits on the boundary of the Payette and Salmon national forests in Valley County, Idaho.[^1][^5] The lookout there was built in 1935 as an R1/L-4 ground cabin, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page says it replaced an earlier lookout on Two Point Peak.[^4] The climbing guide quotes a 1982 history that puts that move in the early 1930s and says it was made for the view into the Payette National Forest.[^5] Those dates do not line up with 1935, and the sources do not explain the gap.[^1][^5]

According to the same 1982 history, the Payette National Forest usually staffed the lookout, since the country that mattered most from it lay inside that forest.[^5] The register says staffing stopped after 1950.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts has no documented staffing history for the site.[^3]

Abandonment came in 1950 by several accounts.[^2][^3][^4] A 2019 note on the Forest Lookouts page says the structure is still up and can be seen on Google Earth.[^4] The register, which added the site in October 2020, also reports that it stands today.[^1]

A second firelookout.com page, titled Shell Rock Peak, gives a 1936 L-4 cab abandoned in 1967, at 8,565 feet and 11 miles south-southwest of Yellow Pine.[^6] Three sources put Shellrock at 9,435 to 9,442 feet, and the first firelookout.com page places it 28 miles east of Yellow Pine.[^1][^2][^3] That mismatch suggests the second page probably describes a different site, so this story does not use it.

Today the site is reached on foot. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists it as a hike-in and abandoned site.[^3] The climbing guide describes the spot as remote, a trip of several hard hiking days, with better access from the Payette side.[^5] The sources do not describe public entry into the cabin, so check with the Payette National Forest about its condition before going.[^3][^5]
