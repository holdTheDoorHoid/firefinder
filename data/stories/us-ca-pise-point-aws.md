In August 1942 the San Mateo County defense council's executive committee authorized $1,700 for a tower on Kings Mountain, a peak that looks over Half Moon Bay.[^1] The San Mateo Times reported the decision and said the tower would go up at once on a rise it called Pise Point.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page lists the site as AWS and fire, under the name Pise Point (Kings Mtn).[^1] That 1942 item reports a plan, not a finished tower.[^1]

The Aircraft Warning Service was a civilian unit under the Army's Ground Observer Corps, set up during the Second World War to watch for enemy planes.[^2] The paper said the Army would supervise the post, with most of the staffing drawn from county civil defense.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list now marks the Pise Point AWS as gone.[^3]

A map directory calls Pise Lookout a scenic viewpoint, placed near the Phleger Estate nature reserve.[^4] No source I read describes a tower, footings or other remains at the spot now, or says whether it is open to visitors.
