Grass Mountain Lookout stood on a peak in San Miguel County, New Mexico.[^1][^2] By 1910 a telephone line had reached it on what was then the Pecos National Forest, and in 1915 the Pecos and Jemez forests were combined into the Santa Fe National Forest.[^1]

The lookout was a cabin, not a tower. A 1921 photograph caption says it needed no tower, since no trees blocked the view.[^1] By 1916 a small wood-framed cabin with a shingle roof stood on the summit, and the history describes it as the first cabin built on a summit. The history gives no construction date.[^1]

The lookout was never manned on any set schedule. A local rancher and outfitter, George "Skipper" Viles, rode lookout there during a very dry June in 1914.[^1] In June 1920 Ranger Johnson had a telephone installed in the cabin and staffed it, and Karl Valentine spent 18 days on watch there in July 1921.[^1] Johnson placed watchers there again in 1922 and 1928.[^1]

By 1925 the Forest listed Grass Mountain as an emergency lookout, with a cabin it judged "not worth maintaining."[^1] By 1931 the Forest had given up on it, though it still served as a patrol stop when fire danger was critical.[^1] That year Ranger Johnson helped set benchmarks there, ran a flagpole up through the old cabin's roof for orientation, and cut down the telephone poles along the line.[^1]

Johnson may have closed the lookout after 1927, when he arranged for linemen on the Terrero Tramway to spot fires.[^1] A local story that the history does not confirm says lightning damaged the cabin at an unknown date and ended its use.[^1] Maps show it as a lookout from 1914 to 1936, and a 1958 photo taken from far off appears to show it still standing.[^1]

The most recent description of the site we found is from May 2013, when old lumber, wire and green glass still lay on the ground there.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as gone.[^2] No source we read describes the site since then, or says whether the public may visit it.

[^2]: New Mexico Lookouts, Forest Fire Lookout Association, https://firelookout.org/lookouts/us/nm/ (accessed 2026-10-10).
