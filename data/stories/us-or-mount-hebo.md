Mount Hebo sits in the Siuslaw National Forest in Yamhill County, about 20 miles south-southeast of Tillamook.[^1][^2] A lookout man was stationed there by 1913.[^3] That July a Eugene paper reported a newly finished phone line to the peak and a permanently assigned observer posted there.[^3] The register lists three observation spots on the summit that year, with tents as living quarters.[^1] In 1922 a forest inspector went up to instruct the Hebo lookout in fire finding.[^3]

In June 1923 work was under way on a lookout house atop the mountain.[^3] The register says a cupola cabin went up on the northeast summit in 1924, with a 30-foot platform on the southwest summit.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history calls that southwest structure a tower.[^3]

A December 1936 item in a Forest newsletter, as quoted by the Forest Lookouts history, reported that a new tower and house had just been finished on the mountain.[^3] The register and the Forest Lookouts history both date a 53-foot tower on the east summit to 1937.[^1][^3] In 1950 a 67-foot tower of treated timber, topped by an L-4 cab, went up on the northwest summit.[^1][^3] The last tower was removed in 1978.[^1][^2][^3]

A 2013 Pew Trusts fact sheet calls the lookout a popular day hike and says hikers still use the Pioneer-Indian Trail.[^4] The other sources date the last tower's removal to 1978,[^1][^2][^3] so it is unclear what the fact sheet means by the lookout. We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout site today.
