Iron River Lookout is a steel fire tower on Forest Road 244 in Bayfield County, inside the Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest.[^1][^2][^3] The register gives its elevation as 1,295 feet.[^1]

The tower was built in 1934.[^1][^2][^3] The register dates the work to February of that year. It presents the tower as evidence of a joint effort by the Forest Service and the Wisconsin Conservation Commission to safeguard the state's forest resources.[^1] Central US Lookouts also dates it to 1934 and credits the CCC with building the 100-foot steel tower.[^2]

The builder is not settled. The register credits the Conservation Commission, while Central US Lookouts and the state's property file both say CCC without saying which body they mean.[^1][^2][^3]

The sources also disagree on height. The register says the tower was made by the International Stacey company, has an internal corner leg ladder, and stands 79 feet 6 inches tall.[^1] The state's property file calls it a 100-foot fire tower, and Central US Lookouts gives the same 100 feet.[^3][^2]

The register says the state ran the lookout for 81 years, until 2015, when the DNR decommissioned its lookout network.[^1] A Milwaukee Journal Sentinel article from December 2015 gives a different year. It says the DNR would take all 72 of its fire towers out of service in 2016, after judging them unsafe and too costly to replace.[^4]

I found no fire or smoke record tied to this tower in the sources I read. The state's property file says the tower is not on the National or State Register of Historic Places.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^5] The register entry is dated April 3, 2018.[^1]

I could not find a current access note for Iron River. According to the Forest Service, the Mountain Fire Lookout Tower is the only one of the forest's 14 original towers that visitors may ascend.[^6] I could not confirm whether Iron River is one of those 14, so do not assume it can be climbed.
