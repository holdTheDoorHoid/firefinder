The Panther Creek lookout stood in Yakima County, Washington, on land the register calls the Yakima Indian Reservation.[^1][^2][^4] It sat at about 4,972 feet.[^1][^3]

It was built in 1935 as a 68-foot steel tower with a ground house.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1964 the tower and its house were relocated to Signal Peak, within the reservation.[^1][^2][^4][^5] WillhiteWeb places Signal Peak a few miles south of the original site.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register describes the current tower as a 68-foot Aermotor that reached Signal Peak in 1964, having stood at nearby Panther Creek.[^5] The most recent account we found dates from 2019.[^6]

A 2019 trip report says most of the reservation is off limits to non-tribal members.[^6] The writer's group helped the Yakama Nation fire crew get Satus Peak ready for the season, then toured Signal Peak in return.[^6] By the writer's account, Signal Peak is one of two fire lookouts left on Yakama land.[^6]

At the original site, WillhiteWeb says the footings can be seen from the air.[^3] The same page says access needs permission from the Yakama Nation.[^3] No source we read describes the original site on the ground today.[^3]
