The Redding fire tower stood on Umpawaug Hill in West Redding, in Fairfield County.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it, and a 1942 newspaper item quoted there says the steel structure was finished in 1936 and had been used every season since.[^1]

According to that 1942 item, the cabin floor was 62 feet (19 m) off the ground at the tower's top.[^1] A 1966 item quoted on the same page gives a 52-foot perch instead. The sources do not explain the difference.[^1]

In the fire season an observer kept watch from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day.[^1] In April 1942 the observer, Mrs. Russell Hoddinott, saw and called in two of the three grass fires in Ridgefield that Sunday, and the tower also flagged a grass fire on a property in the Florida district.[^1] By 1966 Mrs. Margaret M. Wixted was spending 45 hours a week on watch, starting in March. The item identifies her as working for the Park and Forest commission and says a short-wave radio let her stay in contact with the state's 14 fire towers.[^1]

The 1942 item also said people were always invited to visit the tower.[^1] No source we found describes the site now, so it is not known what remains on the hilltop or whether the public can visit.

[^1]: Umpawaug - FOREST LOOKOUTS, Forest Lookouts (eastern US), https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/umpawaug.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
