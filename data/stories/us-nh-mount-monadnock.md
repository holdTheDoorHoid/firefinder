Mount Monadnock is a mountain in Jaffrey, New Hampshire.[^3] The New Hampshire Forestry Commission built the first lookout in 1912, a wooden structure 6 by 6 feet.[^1] A 1972 newspaper account places that first tower on the summit.[^2]

In 1928 the Boston engineer Frederic H. Hay designed a stone base to replace it.[^1] Summer residents of Dublin paid for it.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site quotes a 1972 Telegraph article saying residents found the site ugly, so in 1928 the new foundation was set in a dip just off the summit.[^2] The register says the lookout was used from 1928 to 1948.[^1]

By 1948 the building had weathered badly and was being vandalized by climbers.[^2] The Park Department remodeled it in 1952 as a concession stand, which lasted five years.[^2] In 1957 it was dismantled and rebuilt.[^2] Vandals repeatedly broke into the stand.[^2] In 1969 it was decided to abandon the idea of selling snacks and cold drinks at the summit, because of damage and litter.[^2]

The register gives 1972 as the year the lookout was removed.[^1] The 1972 article says state park crews, working with the Society for the Protection of New Hampshire Forests, carried the building off the summit and were breaking the cement and granite foundation apart, stone by stone.[^2]

The sources disagree about the start date.[^1][^2] The 1972 article says the first tower went up "almost 60 years" earlier, and that the summit would reclaim its natural beauty "for the first time since 1914." We have used the register's 1912 date.[^1][^2]

The mountain has long been cited as one of the most frequently climbed mountains in the world, and it bears a number of hiking trails.[^3] None of the sources we read describes what, if anything, is left of the foundation on the summit.
