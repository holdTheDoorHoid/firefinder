Jodrie Hill is a summit in Milan, New Hampshire.[^1] A 2014 hiking report says a wooden fire tower went up there in 1925, intended as a replacement for one that had stood on Cambridge Black.[^1] The report says a later tower on Milan Hill, a short distance away and still standing, took its place.[^1] That Milan Hill tower, a 45-foot steel structure with a standard New Hampshire wooden cab, was built in 1932, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^2]

A New Hampshire family hiking list places the hill among mountains that once had a fire tower.[^3] The Eastern US Lookouts page lists the tower as removed.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Hampshire list shows the site with its tower gone.[^5]

The two hiking reports disagree about what is left. In 2014 the writer reported four drill holes for foundation poles on the summit and nothing else left behind.[^1] A hiker who visited the wooded hill in 2020 saw nothing of note and found no relics of an earlier tower.[^6] That same 2020 report says private homes surround the hill.[^6]

I found no source that says whether the summit is open to the public. Do not assume you may go up, and ask the landowners before you visit.
