The state obtained land for the Tecumseh towersite, in Ozark County, in fiscal 1952-53, and put up a new tower there the next year.[^1] The Department of Conservation's 2015 plan for the Howell and Ozark County towersites gives this site as 40 acres, acquired in 1953, about 10 miles east of Gainesville on Highway 160.[^3]

Department staff use the tower to watch for smoke.[^2] The 2015 plan says it is currently used for fire detection, and the Department's page says staff use it to respond more quickly to wildfires in that part of the county.[^2][^3]

The plan judges the site probably eligible for the National Register of Historic Places.[^3]

According to the plan, the public may visit the towersite, but vehicles may not use the access road while the tower is unmanned. The plan's infrastructure table lists no parking area for the site.[^3] The Department's page describes the 40-acre site as mostly wooded and lists its hours as 4 a.m. to 10 p.m. every day.[^2] The plan also says hunting is permitted there.[^3]

None of the sources I could read says whether visitors may climb the tower, so this story does not say they can.
