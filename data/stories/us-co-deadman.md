Deadman Lookout sits at 10,673 feet on Deadman Mountain in the Canyon Lakes Ranger District of the Roosevelt-Arapaho National Forest, in Larimer County, Colorado.[^1] The first fire tower here was a wooden structure built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in 1938.[^1] That CCC tower was replaced in 1960 by the 55-foot tower standing today, topped with a 14-by-14-foot CL-style cab and catwalk.[^1]

The Forest Service nominated the lookout to the National Historic Lookout Register in 1990, through District Ranger Donna L. Hepp and retired USFS volunteer coordinator Robert D. Sullivan; it was registered on December 17, 1990, as US 10 / CO 2.[^1] The Colorado/Utah chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it under the name Deadman Mountain, standing, in Larimer County.[^2]

The tower has continued to be staffed by volunteers rather than a paid lookout.[^1] A visitor who climbed it in September 2004 found it freshly painted with no rust, "spotless" inside down to the windows, and a volunteer on duty who happily answered questions; the visitor noted the Forest Service had also converted a nearby garage into a visitor center and built a new composting bathroom on site.[^1] As of a 2019 assessment, the tower, roof and cab were in good condition.[^1] The same visit report flagged that a Sprint cell site had been attached to the tower itself, with solar panels on a building below it, which the visitor felt marred the site's appearance.[^1]

No source consulted records the lookout being closed, damaged, or taken out of volunteer service since 1960.
