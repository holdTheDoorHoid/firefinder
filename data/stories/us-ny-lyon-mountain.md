Lyon Mountain's lookout began in April 1910 as a station with no tower, because the summit was bare at the time.[^2] In 1917 the New York State Conservation Commission put a 35-foot Aermotor LS40 steel tower on the summit.[^2][^3]

The tower was in use until the end of the 1988 fire season and was officially closed in early 1989. The state concluded that airplanes did a better job of spotting fires than towers did.[^1][^2]

Ownership changed hands in the years that followed.[^2] Domtar owned the tower and the land around it until January 2005, when it sold about 20,000 acres to The Nature Conservancy. The land later went to New York State.[^2] That same year, the DEC and the Student Conservation Corps replaced the footings, landing boards, stairs, cab floor and safety netting, and materials were flown in by a state police helicopter.[^1] The cab's interior was repainted in 2017 to cover graffiti.[^1] The NHLR added the tower to its register in January 2017.[^1]

The NHLR calls the tower's overall condition excellent, and a radio repeater with solar panels and an antenna sits on the tower.[^1] Each summer the Town of Saranac and the NYSDEC employ a summit steward to welcome visitors and explain the tower's past.[^1] In the fall of 2016 the New York chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association signed a deal to keep up the trail and the tower.[^1]

Wikipedia says hikers can still reach the tower.[^2] A 2022 article describes a 6.8-mile round trip from a parking area about a mile up a seasonal dirt road off Chazy Lake Road, on a trail the Adirondack Mountain Club built about a decade earlier, and says the tower can be climbed.[^3] Check current conditions before you go.
