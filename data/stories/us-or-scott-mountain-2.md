Scott Mountain, near Roseburg in Douglas County, has been used for fire detection continuously since 1912, according to the register.[^1] The sources disagree on the first lookout. The register and firelookout.com date a log cabin to 1911,[^1][^2] while a 1914 note on Rex's page says the site was the first lookout put in service by the Douglas County Fire Patrol Association.[^3] A 1914 Roseburg paper put the summit 25 miles east of Roseburg,[^3] and firelookout.com says 18 miles northeast.[^2]

firelookout.com dates a 50-foot pole tower with an observation cab to 1928.[^2] A 1931 state forestry report says a new 50-foot tower was built on the mountain,[^3] and a 1933 Corvallis paper reported that C.C.C. workers had finished a 50-foot tower with a 14-by-16-foot log cabin.[^3] The register instead describes a 100-foot wooden tower in 1933.[^1]

The register says the 100-foot tower was replaced in 1957 by a 30-foot flat-roofed cab with a catwalk.[^1] firelookout.com instead dates a 35-foot timber tower with a live-in cab to 1947.[^2] The Rex page records a 1947 replacement costing $1,192.36 and says a tower built mostly from two salvaged lookouts was finished in 1977.[^3] The sources do not agree on what stood on the summit in 2020.

An assistant was stationed at Point Lookout on the summit in 1914.[^3] In 1935 a ranger at the lookout telephoned Roseburg that a missing 13-year-old boy had reached safety there.[^3] In 1944 two women set off to take charge of the lookout for the summer.[^3] In 1946 lookout Marcelene Moore noticed a fire in the distance shortly after midnight, and a crew had it under control before the heat of the day.[^3] firelookout.com says staffing ran every summer into the late 2000s, after which an automated detection camera took over spotting.[^2]

The station was badly wrecked in 1942, apparently by a bolt of lightning.[^3] In late 1965 a radio tower fell onto the lookout house and damaged it.[^3]

The Archie Creek Fire destroyed the lookout and its detection camera in September 2020.[^1][^2][^3]

The register lists the Douglas Forest Protective Association as administrator.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors. Ask the association before planning a visit.
