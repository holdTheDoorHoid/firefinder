Benndale was a lookout tower in George County, Mississippi, built in 1929 by the Mississippi Forest Service.[^1] Newspaper reports from that year, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, spell the place "Bendale."[^1] A September report from the Sun Herald said the material for the tower had been shipped and would be put up within two weeks, according to district forester K. E. Kimball.[^1]

The tower was completed in the week ending September 30, 1929, two and a half miles west of Bendale.[^1] It was a 100-foot (30.5 m) galvanized steel tower of the ladder type, made by the Bessemer Galvanizing Works. A five-man crew took about four and a half days to erect it.[^1] The report called it the fifth tower the service had built in two years, which made six for the coming fire season.[^1]

The valleys of the Pascagoula River and Red and Black creeks lie within the tower's protective radius.[^1] The report said smoke from manufacturing plants at Lucedale, Moss Point, Wiggins, Maxie and Hattiesburg could be seen on clear days.[^1] The tower was to be joined by telephone to the other Pascagoula-area towers and to the wardens, rangers and smoke-chasers of the service.[^1] The Mississippi Forestry Commission, the state's forestry agency today, was founded on March 6, 1926, in response to widespread forest fires.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts site now marks the tower as removed, but gives no date.[^1] A survey mark listed as the Benndale State Forest lot has no written description on that page.[^1] Research found no source describing what remains at the site today or whether it is open to visitors.
