O'Farrell Lookout, also called South Prairie, stood in Pierce County, Washington, at 3,236 feet (986 meters).[^1][^2] One site says it was likely named for Thomas E. O'Farrell, a former chief park ranger at Mount Rainier National Park.[^4]

The tower was built in 1934.[^1][^2][^4] Two sources give its height as 20 feet,[^1][^4] and one gives 15 feet.[^2] Records list it as active from August 31, 1942, to October 16, 1943, under the Seattle Filter Center.[^3]

Staffing records are unclear. One site lists Jane Stanton as the lookout here in 1958.[^2] Another page lists her 1958 season, and Helen Jones's 1959 season, under its heading for Carbon Ridge.[^4]

In July 1959 the state Department of Natural Resources abandoned the lookout, citing its poor shape. A letter says a forest engineer judged the building not worth fixing.[^4] A photo caption dated November 1959 calls the tower "just before being burned,"[^4] and a 1960 entry on another site says the Forest Service burned it.[^3] A November 1960 letter from the DNR to the Forest Service describes a break-in in which a state radio was stolen.[^4]

Two sites say the Carbon Ridge Lookout took over the duties in 1960.[^2][^3] WillhiteWeb places that lookout further up the ridge and says it replaced the tower in 1961.[^4]

The most recent visit we found, in 2015, turned up three of four foundation footings. Two sat firmly in the ground, while a third lay sideways roughly 200 feet away, down the ridge.[^4]

To get there, take FS 7710 from Wilkeson for 4.3 miles to a Y, then 0.2 miles to a gate, and park below it.[^4] The hike is listed at 4.5 miles, with 1,300 feet of climbing from the gate, and includes some off-trail searching for the footings.[^4] The route crosses a gate on a tree-farm road. No source we read describes the rules for visiting.[^4]
