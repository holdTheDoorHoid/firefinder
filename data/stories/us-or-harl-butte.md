Harl Butte is a Wallowa-Whitman National Forest lookout that sits above the lower end of the Hells Canyon National Recreation Area.[^1] The register says the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The butte is named for the homesteader John Harl.[^2]

A Forest Service lookout station was set up here by 1925.[^3] A newspaper item from that year, quoted on the Oregon Lookouts page, says a house was being built on the prominence.[^3] The register, firelookout.com and cherylhill.net date the earlier L-4 cabin to 1934.[^1][^2][^4] The sources do not reconcile the 1925 item with the 1934 date.

The current cab went up in 1954 to replace the 1934 cabin.[^1][^2][^4] The register describes it as an L-4 ground house with an outrigger hip roof, and cherylhill.net notes that it has no catwalk around it.[^1][^4]

A 1944 newspaper item, quoted on the Oregon Lookouts page, says a lookout had gone to Harl Butte for the rest of that season.[^3] A 1952 item says a dry season kept a lookout on longer than usual. It is the only fire-season detail I found for this site.[^3]

A visit on June 22, 2023 found the cab staffed, and the staffer said it was his 23rd season in the region.[^4] The register says the site is in active service.[^1]

The route runs from Road 39 onto Road 3930 for 15.2 miles, then forks left onto Road 280 and follows it for 2.5 miles to the road's end.[^4] A wire gate below the lookout is there to keep cattle out.[^4] The sources do not say whether vehicles can go past that gate, so check road conditions and access with the Forest Service before you go.
