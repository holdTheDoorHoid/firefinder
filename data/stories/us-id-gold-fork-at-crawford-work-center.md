The Gold Fork lookout began as an open platform on a rock ledge, reached by a long staircase, around 1918 to 1920.[^1][^3] In 1933 an L-4 cabin on a concrete base went up about two miles from that first site.[^1][^4]

The Forest Lookouts page quotes two old newspaper notes about the station. One from 1935 says a couple spent the summer there, and one from 1945 names a Boise woman who served there out of Cascade.[^4] I could not find a full staffing list, a date for the last year it was staffed,[^3] or any notable fires.

According to the register, the lookout eventually stopped operating, and in 1988 the cabin went to the Crawford Forest Work Center northeast of Cascade.[^1] The sources do not agree on the destination. Firelookout.com says it was moved to two miles east of Cascade "for private use",[^2] and the Forest Lookouts page says it went to the Crawford Ranger Station.[^4] The register's photo caption, dated 2012, places the cabin at the Crawford Forest Work Center.[^1]

Today a statewide list marks the relocated Gold Fork entry as standing.[^5] The register added the lookout on October 2, 2020.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts labels the relocated cabin as drive-up.[^3] The sources give two elevations, 4,889 feet on the register and 7,790 feet on the other two pages, and I could not reconcile them.[^1][^2][^3]

The register says the Boise National Forest administers the lookout.[^1] None of the sources I reached say whether the public may visit the work center, so anyone interested should ask the forest before going.
