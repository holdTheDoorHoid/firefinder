Moosehead Mountain, northwest of Piercefield in St. Lawrence County, got a fire lookout in 1910.[^1] In October 1909 the state's fire superintendents had decided to build one there, to back up six stations in the Adirondacks and three in the Catskills.[^1] The first tower was 20 feet tall and cost $442.26.[^1]

A 40-foot Aermotor LS-25 steel tower with external ladder access went up in 1916.[^1] A 2016 state newsletter called the tower 100 years old, which fits that later structure.[^3]

Forest observers worked the mountain during the fire season.[^1] In July 1911 W.D. Pond was appointed to it at $60 a month.[^1] In 1950 a newspaper reported that Mrs. Pearl Eakins would again be on the mountain, and named her the sole woman observer in Northern New York.[^1]

In 1978 a private party dismantled the tower and stored it for later re-assembly.[^1] In October 2016 Dave Vana of Davana LLC refurbished it and re-erected it in Brooklyn.[^2][^3]

Today the tower stands in a courtyard between warehouses on the Industry City campus.[^3] The Todd Group, a landscape contractor that installed one of Industry City's courtyards, says the campus has three public courtyards.[^5] A May 2021 blog post says the tower cannot be climbed, because its lowest flight of stairs has been taken out.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Moosehead Mountain site as gone, and no source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain.[^6]
