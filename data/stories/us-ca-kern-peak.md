Kern Peak rises to 11,515 feet in Tulare County, on the Inyo National Forest, and the register counts it among the state's highest-elevation fire-detection sites.[^1] Its lookout was a Region 5 Plan 4A ground cabin, dated on the register to about 1917.[^1]

One named lookout appears in the sources: Lester E. Creasey, identified as the government lookout at Kern Peak station in a 1917 report reprinted in a newspaper.[^2] During a storm on October 1, 1917, lightning struck the cabin nine times. Creasey wrote that the cabin still looked fine, with black spots where it was hit, most of them on the flagpole.[^2]

The register gives no removal date and says no trace of the lookout is left at the site.[^1] A 2008 trip report disagrees. It describes the summit as a largely collapsed lookout tower, with one platform still usable.[^3] A reader's email added to that report in 2017 suggests that an object the writer had thought might be an outhouse was more likely the remains of a stand for an Osborne Fire Finder inside the lookout.[^3]

The peak lies on national forest land, and the 2008 hikers reached the summit.[^1][^3] Because the register and the trip report disagree, no source settles what stands on the summit now.
