Pogue Point lies about 12 miles north-northwest of Unity, in the Malheur and Wallowa-Whitman national forests.[^2][^1]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com describe the first lookout as a 43-foot pole platform set up in the 1920s. They say it was replaced in 1934 by a 22-foot wooden L-4 tower.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts describes the platform as 45 feet tall, and its 1937 notes describe a lookout station still to be built at the point, which is hard to square with a 1934 tower.[^3]

In July 1948 the Forest Service awarded a contract to Dick Hindman of Baker to build a new tower and house there.[^3] In 1949 a 41-foot CT-2 tower with a 14-by-14-foot lookout house replaced the old platform, which was torn down.[^3] The register calls the 1949 structure a 41-foot treated timber L-4 tower instead.[^1]

In 1952 Mr. and Mrs. Zelador came from Texas for the summer, and Mr. Zelador, who worked on a Forest Service crew, was to be stationed at the lookout.[^3] In September 1962 Bob Fiore left Pogue Point and was replaced by Mr. and Mrs. John Hauk, although the same notice said the Unity-area lookouts were no longer manned by summer personnel.[^3] In October 1963 Roy Howard was stationed there during the fire danger season.[^3]

The register says the tower was used for emergencies in the 1960s and is now gone.[^1] firelookout.com's summary reads "Emergency 1960s. Gone."[^2] Forest Lookouts says the tower was demolished during the winter of 1986.[^3]

We found no source that describes what remains at the Pogue Point site today, or whether the public can reach it.
