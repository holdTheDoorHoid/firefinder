The Washington Department of Natural Resources built the K.O. Point lookout in 1962.[^1][^2][^3] The DNR's third biennial report says its own carpentry crew finished nine lookouts in the two years before that, at an average of $8,000 each, and K.O. Point was one of them.[^4] The tower was a 20-foot wooden live-in lookout on the Willapa and Columbia River divide.[^3][^4] It was named for Karl O. Lawrence, a DNR employee of 32 years, and dedicated in early September 1962.[^4]

It replaced an older Hull lookout that stood lower on a nearby ridge.[^2][^4] The sources disagree about which direction that ridge lay: Forest Lookouts says north, and WillhiteWeb says south.[^3][^4] A 1962 newspaper quotes a DNR supervisor saying the new site gave a sweeping view in every direction and cut out some blind spots that had made the old lookout less effective.[^4] A cabin stood at the spot in the 1940s, before the tower.[^1][^3]

Firelookout lists Helen McGarity as known lookout personnel in 1962.[^2] In June 1968, a newspaper reported that Karin Johnson, of Willapa, would be on duty at the lookout, and that only one lookout in the county would be in service that year.[^4]

WillhiteWeb says the lookout served until it closed in 1975.[^3] A June 1977 newspaper said the last lookout in the central area, at K O Point south of Menlo, had closed two years earlier.[^4]

The Register says the tower is now gone.[^1] WillhiteWeb's photo captions show two footings at the summit and one footing tossed down the hillside.[^3] A caption dated February 2021 shows a communication building at the summit.[^3] No source we read describes the summit after that photo.[^1][^3]

WillhiteWeb lists the access as good gravel to a gate, and says an access permit is needed.[^3] Without a vehicle permit, the trip up the road is about 8.5 miles each way by bike.[^3] The page also notes many road splits, and says active logging can be a problem on them.[^3] There is no tower left to climb.[^1] Check who issues the permit before you go, and do not pass the gate without one.
