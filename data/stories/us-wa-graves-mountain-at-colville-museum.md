The first lookout on Graves Mountain stood in the Colville National Forest in Ferry County, Washington.[^3] It was a pole tower, with a small log cabin for living quarters on the ground below.[^2] Civilian Conservation Corps crews built it in the early 1930s, and the register dates the tower to 1934.[^2][^4][^1]

Sources disagree on its height. The register and a former-lookout site record give 72 feet, while the Stevens County Historical Society says 75.[^1][^3][^2]

A couple manned the lookout in 1944.[^4] In 1955 the Forest Service replaced that tower with a 30-foot timber tower and an L-4 cab, which the historical society says measured 14 by 14 feet.[^3][^2] The historical society says the station worked for almost 50 years, while the register says the L-4 cab stood for 30 years.[^2][^1]

In 1985 the Stevens County Historical Society moved the tower and cab, piece by piece, to a museum in Colville.[^1] The register calls the new home the Stevens County Park and Museum.[^1] A former-lookout site record and another lookout history site place it next to the Keller Historical Museum, on a small hill overlooking the city.[^3][^4] The site record says the tower was removed from the mountain that year.[^3]

Today the register describes the restored lookout as open to the public daily, and it lists the historical society as the administrator.[^1] The sources we read do not give hours or say whether visitors may go up, so check with the museum before you go.
