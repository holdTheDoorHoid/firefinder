The Pine Mountain Lookout is a 100-foot steel tower in Tuscaloosa County, Alabama, and the Alabama Forestry Commission administers it.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register accepted the tower on May 27, 2016, as US 1129, AL 62, on a nomination by Andrew Zerbe.[^1]

The build date is missing from every source I could read.[^1][^2] The Register page gives no build year, and the Forest Lookouts page for the tower has a county and agency header but no history text.[^1][^2] For context, a column by Thomas Kaufmann says that at one time over 200 of these structures dotted Alabama.[^3]

Nothing I read says who staffed Pine Mountain or what fires it watched.[^1][^2]

The Register's entry is the only description of this tower I found. It says the tower currently holds communications equipment, and that its cab is rusting and its floor and windows are gone.[^1] Kaufmann says that after a lookout was no longer manned, the structure stayed up and was later fitted with antennas used for communications.[^3]

Today's condition is the open question. The Register entry dates from 2016, and the sources do not say whether the public can visit or climb the tower.[^1][^2] Kaufmann also warns that many of the state's surviving towers are dangerous and illegal to climb.[^3] Please do not climb Pine Mountain, and check with the Alabama Forestry Commission before planning any visit.[^1]
