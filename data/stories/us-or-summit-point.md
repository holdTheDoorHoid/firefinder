Summit Point sits on a ridge in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest, about 12 miles northwest of Halfway in Baker County.[^2] Firelookout.com says the site was first used in the 1930s with a 20-foot platform tower.[^2]

The present tower dates from 1949 and took the place of that earlier open platform tower.[^1][^2] The Record-Courier reported on September 29, 1949, that the tower was complete except for painting.[^3] Oregon Lookouts also dates a 20-foot treated timber tower with an L-4 model 1936 cab to 1951, which conflicts with the 1949 date.[^3] The Register gives the setup as a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab on an open timber tower 20 feet tall.[^1]

In 1964 the Record-Courier said Galen West was due up at the station for the summer, and on August 27 it reported that Terry Speelman of Richland would replace him.[^3] Firelookout.com lists Ronalee Leckenby as the recorded lookout for 1988 to 1989 and 1993 to 1995, and marks 1996 and 1999 as not staffed.[^2] The sources read name no fires this lookout spotted.

Cheryl Hill visited on August 17, 2013, when the lookout was listed as staffed in summer.[^4] A woman in the lookout told her that the spur road had been gated that year because people were driving up at night.[^4] She found the road closed to traffic and the walk from the trailhead about a mile.[^4] A 2023 report from The Roaming Civic lists the lookout as active and currently standing, and describes the trailhead as reached over forest roads NF-77 and NF-7715.[^5]

The Register lists its registration date as July 6, 2022.[^1] The sources read do not say whether the public may enter the cab, and the 2013 gate may no longer be in place. Check with the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest before planning a visit, and do not climb the tower without permission.
