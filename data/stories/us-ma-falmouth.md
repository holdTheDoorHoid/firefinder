The Falmouth Fire Tower stands on Howlands Park Hill at 181 Fire Tower Road, on town-owned land.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 68-foot tower with a 10-by-10-foot cab, constructed in 1947 to replace a 40-foot steel tower built in 1914 at the request of local officials.[^2]

The origin story is less tidy. A New England Defenses page says the 1914 tower served as an observation post for the Anti Aircraft Intelligence Service from 1942 to 1945.[^3] It adds that in 1946 the 1914 tower was taken down and swapped for a 1930 tower from Loc 59A, and that the current tower went up in 1947.[^3] The Register describes no move.[^2] The two accounts conflict. The New England Defenses page relies on a November 1989 edition of the CDSG News.[^3] None of the sources read settles which account is right.

A visit report filed with the Register in March 2014 rated the tower in good condition, though it needed paint, and recorded that it was not staffed.[^2] By 2017 a public radio report said observers spend eight hours a day in the towers from March to October.[^4]

In late October 1947 a forest fire in Beebe Woods burned for three days on the wooded rise above Falmouth village.[^5] None of the pages I read say whether the tower spotted that fire or helped fight it.

The Register lists the tower as US 608, MA 19, registered in December 2004.[^2] In 2015 a state maintenance crew repaired nine Massachusetts towers, and Falmouth received a new antenna.[^6] A Massachusetts state document lists the tower among seven active fire observation towers in the Cape and Islands region.[^1]

The sources do not say whether the public may climb the tower or go into the cab. The 2014 visit report recorded no sign posted for the tower and no signed directions to it.[^2] It is a working fire lookout on town property, so check with the Town of Falmouth before any visit, and do not climb it without permission.
