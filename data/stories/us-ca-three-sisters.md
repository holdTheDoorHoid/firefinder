Three Sisters Peak sits west of Williams in Colusa County, and in late 1930 the state agreed to put a fire lookout station there.[^1]

State Forester M. B. Pratt accepted the county's proposal in December 1930, and by the end of that month a site had been picked and a roadway arranged for the building.[^1] The state agreed to furnish the lookout man.[^1] A lookout house was going up on the peak by April 1931, built with help from local stockmen.[^1] Travel to and from the station had been difficult, with nothing but a trail, and a road to it was being built by November 1931.[^1]

O. W. Friedrick was in charge of the lookout in 1931.[^1] In late May he phoned the fire house in Colusa to report smoke, which turned out to be weeds burning in town, and the local paper said the episode showed the lookout was worth having.[^1] The station was tied by telephone to the state ranger's office in Williams.[^1] In May 1932 the fire warden announced that Friedrick would begin his summer's work as lookout, barring anything unforeseen, and that the road was open and the public was invited to inspect the station.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page files the station under the California Department of Forestry, and the December 1930 notices describe a state station.[^1] A May 1931 item in the Colusa Herald calls it a forest service lookout instead, so these sources do not settle which agency ran it.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks Three Sisters as gone and gives its type as ground.[^2] The clippings on the Forest Lookouts page end in May 1932.[^1]

No source describes the site today, so we cannot say what remains on the peak or whether the public may go there.[^1][^2]
