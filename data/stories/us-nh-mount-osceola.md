Mount Osceola rises to 4,340 feet in the White Mountains of New Hampshire, and a fire lookout tower once stood at its top.[^1]

The New Hampshire Timberland Owners Association put up the lookout in 1910, one of ten new towers that year.[^2][^3] The White Mountain History account classifies its lookouts into types. Osceola is one of the examples it gives of a secondary lookout: a less prominent peak with shorter views, staffed only in high-risk periods.[^2]

The tower came down in the late 1970s.[^1][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Hampshire list marks the Osceola tower as gone.[^5]

Hikers reach the summit by trail in the White Mountain National Forest, from Tripoli Road to the south or from the Greeley Pond Trail to the northeast.[^1][^4] From the top, the panorama takes in Mount Washington to the northeast and Waterville Valley to the south.[^4]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower on the summit today.
