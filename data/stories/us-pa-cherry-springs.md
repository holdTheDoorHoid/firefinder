Cherry Springs Fire Tower stands along PA 44 in Potter County, in the Susquehannock State Forest, where it looks over the headwaters of East Fork First Fork Sinnemahoning Creek and Pine Creek.[^1] In August and September of 1917 the state forestry department erected a new steel tower near Cherry Springs, bought from the Aermotor Company of Chicago.[^2] Accounts disagree on its height. The 1917 forest warden's report gives sixty feet, while a Wellsboro paper of the same year had described a seventy-foot tower.[^2] Iron steps were added in June 1918, and a telephone line about thirteen miles long was run to the tower from Carter Camp.[^2]

The tower was rebuilt in 1938 by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^3] Enrollees from Camp S-136 put up a larger steel frame tower there.[^3][^4] The Lumber Museum gives that tower a height of 80 feet and says it replaced an earlier 65-foot tower from 1917.[^3] Those figures do not match the 1917 report, so the height of the 1917 tower remains uncertain.

The tower had a watchman on duty in the 1920s.[^2] A December 1924 item in the Wellsboro Gazette names Jap Knecht as the watchman at the tower.[^2] The sources I could reach do not say when the watch ended, and they do not name a notable fire that the tower helped to spot.

The tower still stands, but it is no longer used to detect fires.[^3] The forest around it has grown tall enough to block the view.[^3] The stone cabin the same CCC camp built nearby is still there, and local scout troops and the Susquehannock Trail Club use it.[^3]

You reach the tower complex from Route 44, roughly two miles south of Cherry Springs State Park.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the tower as administered by the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry.[^1] It was registered on April 28, 2017, as US 1193, PA 20.[^1] I did not find a source that says whether the steel tower can be climbed. Until the Bureau of Forestry confirms otherwise, do not climb it, and check with them before you visit.
