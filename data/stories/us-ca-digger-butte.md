The Digger Butte lookout went up in 1936 on Lassen National Forest land in Tehama County.[^1] A February 1936 newspaper item said camp superintendent Walter Flagg was overseeing work on a tower, house and garage, plus a bridge over Digger Creek.[^2] The Digger Butte CCC camp finished the station that spring.[^2]

The Register describes a 20-foot L-401 tower, one of only two surviving in the state system, topped by a BC-301 wood-frame cab.[^1] A November 1936 item, though, put the station's quarters on a thirty-foot steel tower, so the sources disagree on the height.[^2]

Mr. O.E. Kirkpatrick and his wife occupied the station for the 1936 fire season, from June 1 to November 1.[^2] That autumn, between September 18 and October 11, Kirkpatrick reportedly killed 85 rattlesnakes from the tower.[^2]

In July 1944, weather reports were held up while workers searched for a fuel-moisture stick that a porcupine had carried off.[^2] The stick turned up after an all-day hunt.[^2] The station had been closed since 1991, a 2009 state notice says, and its buildings were deteriorating from weather and vandalism.[^3]

According to the Register, the lookout came down in 2009 and its cab went into storage.[^1] A 2009 state notice planned to dismantle the tower and cab, rebuild them at CAL FIRE's Davis Mobile Equipment Facility, with the move likely in 2009 or 2010.[^3] An earlier plan to hand the isolated 40-acre parcel back to Lassen National Forest fell through when the forest refused the transfer.[^3]

A 2012 update to the Register said the tower and cab would be refurbished in Davis and opened to visitors, with completion targeted for January 1, 2015.[^1] It warned that state budget problems meant no firm schedule could be set.[^1] In April 2014, the lookout site and about forty acres of timber land were listed among California excess properties being reviewed for possible state use or sale.[^2]

No source we read confirms that the tower was rebuilt in Davis. The Register's 2012 update said CAL FIRE was storing the fire-finder plotting device, a logbook and an old weather station at its Tehama-Glenn Unit headquarters.[^1] The logbook covers the 1970s to 1988 and holds many visitors' signatures.[^1] No source describes what remains on the Digger Butte site now, or whether the public can reach it. Anyone curious about the tower should ask CAL FIRE before planning a visit.
