The tower sits on Mount Dunlap, a knob near Blue Ridge Summit in Washington Township, Franklin County.[^2] The state forest service took up the knob as a fire-watch site in 1926, and early observers worked from birch trees at the summit.[^3] A troop of Boy Scouts constructed a log cabin for the fire warden of the state forest in 1930.[^3]

The steel tower followed in 1936. CCC crews from a camp in Old Forge, outside Waynesboro, built it, and the U.S. Forest Service bought it with emergency conservation work funds.[^1][^2][^3] Newspapers that year called it 80 feet tall, and the register gives the same height for its Aermotor MC-39 design.[^1][^2] The sources do not agree on the height. A Penn State mapping page says 90 feet, and a Coast and Geodetic Survey team reportedly estimated 110 feet.[^3][^4]

Jesse Thompson, a state fire warden based at Blue Ridge Summit, was named to run the new tower.[^2] He stayed on for many years.[^3] In 1937 CCC crews built roads to the site and a stone observer's cabin roughly 100 feet from the tower.[^3]

A Waynesboro paper reported more than seventy-five people at the tower on one Sunday in October 1939.[^2] Later lookouts included Harlan Benchoff in 1944 and Harold Naugle in 1963.[^3]

In November 1945 a towerman spotted a forest fire, which The Morning Herald said had burned roughly 75 acres of woodland.[^2] In 1966 the state offered the two-story stone cabin at the site for sale and asked that it be removed.[^3]

In 1973 a 19-year-old from Waynesboro died after a fall from the tower.[^3] That fall the tower went unstaffed, because the Snowy Mountain lookout to the north was back in service.[^3]

In 1993 the tower carried communications gear for Washington Township.[^3] In 2004 the township bought the tower and an acre around it from a church that had used the tower as an antenna site.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the tower with a registration date of January 7, 2014.[^1]

Today the register says the tower carries communications equipment and is fenced off.[^1] The Appalachian Trail runs just below the summit.[^3] Please do not climb the fenced tower.
