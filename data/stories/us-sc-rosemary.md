The Rosemary Fire Tower stands in Sumter County, South Carolina, and the state forestry agency once listed it among its own lookouts.[^1] It sits beside the Rosemary House on Highway 261 South, near Wedgefield.[^2]

The Commission's first tower went up near Camden in August 1930.[^3] Most of its sites ran about ten acres and included a home for the operator, occupied all year.[^3]

A tDAR catalog record for the tower complex's 1995 registration form lists the Civilian Conservation Corps as a keyword and gives a date range of 1934 to 1946.[^4]

The Commission closed its last lookout towers on October 1, 1993.[^5] In 1994 Shaw Air Force Base took over the idle Rosemary site, which lay within the former Manchester State Forest.[^6] The South Carolina Department of Archives and History found the site eligible for the National Register of Historic Places in April 1995.[^6] A 2005 Section 106 review covered a rehabilitation and treatment plan for the Rosemary Center for Natural and Cultural Resources.[^6]

The 2026 memo describes a galvanized steel Aermotor, about 80 feet to the top of its cab, with an inside stairway.[^2] An Air Force engineer inspected the tower on 22 January 2026 and found significant cracking in the concrete foundations and extensive rot in the wooden stairs and cab floor.[^2] The memo, dated 23 March 2026, recommends demolition, judging that saving the tower is neither feasible nor prudent.[^2] Whether that demolition has happened since is not in the sources we read.

A tDAR record says the area is part of the natural area around the Poinsett Electronic Combat Range, and no source we read describes public access.[^6] The memo warns that any attempt to climb the tower would be extremely hazardous.[^2] Please stay off it.
