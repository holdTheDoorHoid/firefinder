The first building on Werner Peak was a log cabin, raised in 1911 about a mile east of the summit.[^6] Another log cabin went up on the summit in 1914, and a 10-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab was built by 1941.[^1][^6] The present two-story concrete and frame tower replaced that pole tower in 1983.[^1][^6] A 2010 newspaper gives 1984 for the finished 15-by-15-foot building, so the exact year is a little unsettled.[^5] The old L-4 cab went to a museum south of Kalispell in 1987.[^1]

Stillwater State Forest staffs the lookout in an extreme fire season, and the state keeps August for fire watch.[^4][^3] Known keepers include Allen Overton in 1952 and Wendy Boggess from 1975 to 1977.[^2] In mid-August 2001, a lightning-started fire burned 860 acres east of the peak, and firefighters contained it within two weeks.[^4]

The National Historic Lookout Register dated its listing to January 9, 2021.[^1] In 2021 a volunteer crew from the Northwest Montana Lookout Association spent a weekend replacing decking and repainting the building.[^6] Under a yearly agreement with the state, the crew comes back to keep the lookout in shape, and a local report counts it among the northwest Montana lookouts still standing.[^6]

The Montana DNRC administers the lookout, and it can be rented in summer.[^1] The Stillwater State Forest began renting it out as a pilot project in 2010, at $35 a night.[^5] The 2026 listing charges $60 a night plus bed tax, takes phone reservations only, and opens July and September for booking, with August kept for fire duty.[^3] Visitors reach it by vehicle along a narrow, bumpy road, bring their own water, and find the Ralph Thayer Memorial Trail next to the building.[^4][^5][^3]
