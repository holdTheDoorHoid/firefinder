A structure that may be a fire tower sits above the old Murray Mine site in Greater Sudbury, Ontario, not far from Copper Cliff.[^1] A user-written listing says that "what appears to be a firetower" watches over the site.[^1] The Ontario record places the mine in the McKim area of the Sudbury district.[^2]

The listing dates the earliest claim here to February 1884, when brothers Thomas and William Murray, merchants from Pembroke, staked it. It says a mine was opened that same year.[^1] The Ontario record gives the mine's closing date as 30 December 1971. It says the mine was then placed on standby and its underground workings were allowed to flood.[^2]

The listing calls the site abandoned: "Today it is abandoned, containing a handful of old buildings as well as many mining relics."[^1] The Ontario record says something different. It says the site is part of the active North Mine complex and was not inspected.[^2] The two sources do not agree on the site's current state.

The listing also says the mine is on private property belonging to INCO and gives no directions.[^1] The site is private, so it is not a place to visit or to try to reach the tower.

One reader comment on the listing calls the tower one of the strangest in the provincial Lands and Forests network of about 320 towers, and says it overlooked bare rock in the 1960s after acid rain had killed the trees. The comment cites no source, so treat it as unverified.[^1]

[^2]: Abandoned Mine Inventory Record 05322: Murray Mine, Geology Ontario, Ontario Ministry of Northern Development, Mines, Natural Resources and Forestry, https://www.geologyontario.mndm.gov.on.ca/mndmfiles/amis/data/records/05322.html (accessed 2026-10-20).
