Spring Butte, in the Deschutes National Forest about 13 miles southeast of LaPine, got its first lookout in 1932.[^2] A Bend Bulletin item from that August, quoted by Oregon Lookouts, describes a 30-foot wooden tower with a lookout house, but the same page gives 28 feet in one place and 32 feet in another.[^4] Firelookout.com and Cheryl Hill both describe that first tower as a pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^2][^3]

In 1940 the Walker Range Patrol operated the lookout, which was staffed for 180 days and reported to LaPine by telephone.[^4] On May 27, 1936, a bolt hit close to the lookout and started a timber fire, which C. E. Hein, temporarily on duty there, controlled without much trouble.[^4] In July 1950 a lightning-caused spot fire west of Paulina Peak was put out by lookout Gene Roberts and Dave Adrian, who ran a sporting goods shop at Lapine.[^4]

In 1955 the tower was rebuilt with tamarack-log legs, and in 1974 its catwalk was replaced.[^4] In 1990 the lookout was judged unsafe and shut until further notice.[^4] In September 1991 it was taken apart, and its parts were listed and kept in storage.[^4]

The present tower is a 41-foot pole tower with a 16-by-16-foot octagonal cab.[^1][^3] Sources disagree on when it went up.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The Register gives 1992 as the build year, yet its own text says the tower was erected in 1991.[^1] Under its 1991 entry, Oregon Lookouts quotes a Sun item saying the cab went up on December 18th.[^4] Firelookout.com and Cheryl Hill both give 1992.[^2][^3] The Register lists its registration date as May 30, 1996, and gives the site elevation as 5,449 feet.[^1]

Firelookout.com says the tower is staffed every summer and that it is on the Register.[^2] Cheryl Hill visited on June 19, 2020, found the lookout listed as staffed, and reported that the gate was open and visitors could drive to the top.[^3] Her page also carries a 2025 visitor's account that links the end of staffing to a failed inspection in September 2024.[^3] The sources read do not confirm that report, so current staffing is unsettled. Anyone planning a visit should check with the Deschutes National Forest first, and should not climb a tower that is closed.
