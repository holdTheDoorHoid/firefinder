A 125-foot lookout tower went up at the headquarters of the Fort Niobrara National Wildlife Refuge in 1936.[^1] Forest Lookouts says it was built and painted that year, with the top twenty feet orange, then a ten-foot white band, then alternating orange and white bands.[^1]

A December 1936 description, quoted on that page from a Region 6 historical assessment by Lou Ann Speulda, calls the tower very spindly and says it swayed whenever someone stepped onto the platform.[^1]

Forest Lookouts lists the tower as removed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Nebraska list marks the headquarters tower as gone.[^2]

No source we found describes what, if anything, stands at the headquarters site today. The refuge's own page gives its address as 39983 Refuge Road, about five miles east of Valentine on Highway 12, and says the grounds are open every day from sunrise to sunset.[^3] That page also gives weekday hours for the visitor center.[^3] The National Park Service page says the visitor center is open from Memorial Day to Labor Day, which does not match the refuge's hours.[^4]
