The Ocoee lookout is not a working tower.[^1][^3] What remains is a segment of the Oswald Dome fire tower, with its cab, set up as an exhibit beside U.S. 64 near the Ocoee Whitewater Center in Polk County, Tennessee.[^1][^3]

The original tower was built in 1935 on Bean Mountain, which is also called Oswald Dome.[^1][^2] Fire towers in the Cherokee National Forest peaked in the 1960s, and Oswald Dome was one of seventeen towers the forest used to watch for fires.[^1] Lookouts watched with binoculars and radios.[^1] Two or three lookouts at neighboring posts could triangulate on the same smoke, pinning its location on a map.[^1]

Regular staffing of the forest's towers ended by the late 1980s, around the time spotter planes began doing more of the detection work.[^1]

The tower was taken off its mountain in 2003. In 2004 a segment of the structure and its cab were restored and set up at the present site.[^1] Wikipedia gives only the 2004 move.[^2] Tennessee Landforms calls the exhibit a partial reconstruction.[^3]

Oswald Dome Road, a Forest Service road off U.S. 64, gives access to the mountain, where radio and television transmitters now sit.[^2] We found no source describing climbing at the exhibit, so treat it as a display, not a tower to climb.
