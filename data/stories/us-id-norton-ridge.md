Norton Ridge Lookout is a ground-mounted cab rather than a tower, set inside the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness in Valley County, Idaho.[^1][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1933 to the L-4 standard design of the 1930s.[^1][^5] Forest Lookouts and firelookout.com both give 1934 instead, so the year is disputed.[^2][^3]

A Forest Lookouts page, quoting The Salt Lake Tribune, describes the station in August 1936. Fire guards were in the building when a supply drop from an airplane landed on the station stove.[^2] Both the window and the stove were damaged by the drop.[^2]

Later pages describe the cabin as abandoned for years.[^3][^4] In 2014 Arnold Aviation airlifted shingles to the Middle Fork of the Salmon River, and Treasure Valley Back Country Horsemen packed them in for storage.[^2] Grants from the South West Idaho RAC and the Idaho Heritage Trust were meant to fund the restoration.[^2][^5]

Forest Lookouts says the re-roof was finished in August 2015. The NHLR and Idaho Fire Lookouts both say the roof was replaced in 2014, so the completion date is in dispute.[^2][^1][^4]

The NHLR registered the lookout on October 3, 2020, and calls it eligible for the National Register of Historic Places.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists it as a hike-in site, and no staffing history is documented.[^4]
