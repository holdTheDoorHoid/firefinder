The register places Lilly Lookout in Brown County, Indiana, on land that the Lilly family donated to Indiana University.[^1] The register describes it as a 75-foot tower with a 7-by-7 steel cab and wooden steps.[^1]

The tower was erected in 1931 for J.K. Lilly, an Indianapolis business executive, on his 500-acre forest northeast of Nashville.[^2][^3] A 1931 newspaper said the tower would be 80 feet tall, with a closed cab and telephone links to the state's towers.[^2] A state conservation report from the same year says Lilly paid all the expenses and dedicated the equipment to the division protecting northern Brown County's forests.[^3] The register gives a different account: it says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower in the 1930s, and it lists 1930 as the build year.[^1]

The tower was to rise on Pinnacle Hill, seven miles from a tower on Weed Patch Hill.[^2] The register says the land was donated to the university in 1942.[^1] The Outbound says the tower was donated that year and later moved to Brown County State Park, where it serves as a radio tower and as a public lookout that visitors can mostly ascend.[^4] Indiana Fire Towers, a guide to the state's towers, places the tower on the university's Lilly-Dickey Woods and says it is not publicly accessible.[^3]

The sources disagree on whether the tower was moved, so where it stands today is not settled. The register lists a September 9, 2012 registration, nominated by Michael Chitwood, the preserve manager, and it says access is by permission only.[^1] I found no source that names who staffed Lilly or when it last watched for fires. Do not climb the tower without the preserve's permission.
