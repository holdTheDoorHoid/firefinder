A 1936 Forest Service description places Snowboard Ridge on a ridge about six miles southeast of Kinzua, in Wheeler County.[^1] Firelookout.com instead places the lookout six miles northwest of Winlock.[^2]

A tower of logs and lumber went up in 1935 for $300.[^1] In 1936 the Forest Service was building a 10-by-10-foot cabin on top, and a newspaper called it the only cabin of that size ever placed on a high tower in the Umatilla forest.[^1] By 1951 the station was a 50-foot tower with a 14-by-14 cab, costing $4,615, according to a district annual report.[^1] Firelookout.com lists a 60-foot wooden tower in 1935 and a 50-foot tower it calls an ODF tower in 1952.[^2]

In 1939 lookout R.P. Parrish was off work with a serious case of flu, and Owen Leathers was to take over from him.[^1] A seven-mile telephone line from Kinzua reached the station in 1953, serving phone and remote radio links.[^1] In 1964 the station was made a radio relay point for the switch to FM, and in 1965 an FM antenna went up on a 90-foot round timber pole.[^1]

Fire reports from the station ran from none to nine a season between 1960 and 1964, with nine discoveries in 1964.[^1] In 1962 someone got onto the catwalk and fired through the building's shutters.[^1]

Firelookout.com lists the station as abandoned and says the tower collapsed in a windstorm in June 2021.[^2] Only firelookout.com reports that collapse; the Oregon Lookouts page does not mention it.[^1][^2] The site has no public access, according to the same page.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains on the ridge.[^1][^2]
