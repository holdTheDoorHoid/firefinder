Miami Mountain Lookout stands on Miami Mountain in the Sierra National Forest, just north of Nipinnawasee and about a mile west of California Highway 49.[^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1934, and the Forest Service put it into service that same year.[^1][^2] A 1933 visibility study had identified the site as the only permanent lookout location on the forest that had not been used as one before.[^2] The cab is 14 feet square and sits on a 20-foot steel tower with a catwalk.[^1]

Paid staff worked the tower until budget cuts ended paid staffing in 1995.[^2] Local volunteers have staffed it since, during a fire season that begins around May 15.[^2]

Volunteer Joe Knowles Jr. died in the evening of October 26, 2006, while working at the tower.[^2] In 2009 the Sierra National Forest named the tower in his honor.[^2]

Volunteer reports describe interior and exterior renovation in 2011 and a weather station installed during fiscal 2012. The live cameras were never swapped out or fixed, so the public can no longer watch live from the tower.[^2]

According to the register, the lookout is still in active use, and volunteers now staff it.[^1] The private volunteer site's page was last updated in February 2017 and has no updates on tower work after 2012, so its present condition is unconfirmed.[^2]

Visitors should avoid parking near the locked gate, since the site warns that a parked car may be towed. The walk from there to the tower is about two and a half miles.[^2]

Sources differ on a few details. The register places the tower on the Mariposa Ranger District and gives an elevation of 4,285 feet, while the volunteer page places it on the Bass Lake Ranger District at 4,327 feet.[^1][^2] The volunteer page also gives two distances from Highway 49, one mile and about two miles.[^2]
