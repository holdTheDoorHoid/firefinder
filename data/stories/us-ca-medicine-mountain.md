Medicine Mountain sits in Siskiyou County, inside what is now the Marble Mountain Wilderness.[^1] The register says the site was chosen in 1932 to replace Marble Mountain, and that a ground cabin built to California Region 5 Plan BC-301 went up in 1934.[^1]

The Siskiyou News reported in June 1934 that a lookout house would be started the next week, with materials brought in by pack train on a two-day trip.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page also quotes a Klamath National Forest chronology saying a CCC crew from Orleans Camp built it in 1935, so the 1934 date and that account differ.[^1][^2]

A 1952 survey description, quoted on the same page, calls the house a standard Forest Service single-story type, about 14 feet on a side and about 15 feet tall.[^2]

The register says the lookout was manned as required, apart from 1955 to 1960, when it had full-time staff.[^1] In June 1964 the Blue Lake Advocate said one man had been its lookout for the last two summers.[^2]

The register dates the lookout's dismantling to 1979 and says nothing is left on the site.[^1]

The Six Rivers National Forest page says entry into the Marble Mountain Wilderness needs no wilderness permit, though state campfire permits are required.[^3] No source read describes the summit after the 1979 removal.
