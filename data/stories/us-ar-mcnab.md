A fire tower stood on a wooded hill about two and a half miles northwest of the village of McNab, in Hempstead County, Arkansas.[^1] The hill was the highest point in its immediate area.[^1] A federal survey station description dated 1951 calls the tower Unit 10, Tower 4 and describes a four-legged steel frame with a glass-enclosed crow's nest at the top.[^1] It puts the height at about 112 feet (34 m).[^1] The same description names the operator as the Arkansas Resources and Development Commission, Division of Forestry and Parks.[^1] The site's header gives the Arkansas Forestry Commission instead.[^1]

The sources disagree about the tower's fate. A column in the Hope Star of 14 July 1950 described a staffed fire tower north of McNab, then reported that the tower had burned.[^2] The column places it north of McNab, where the survey says northwest.[^1][^2] The 1951 description still calls it a standing tower, and the sources do not explain how a tower reported burned in 1950 could be described as standing the next year.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the hill today.
