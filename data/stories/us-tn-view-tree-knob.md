View Tree Knob is a summit on the Scott and Campbell county line in northeastern Tennessee, and it stands at 2,750 feet.[^1] The Tennessee lookout list gives its tower site as 2,749 feet, in Campbell County.[^2]

The encyclopedia says the summit once hosted a Tennessee Division of Forestry wildfire lookout tower.[^1] It also says the agency chose the knob for its prominence and unobstructed views over the surrounding woodland.[^1]

The encyclopedia's general description of steel lookout towers gives a cab measuring seven feet by seven feet at the top, where a lookout was posted during fire season.[^1] The same general description says a telephone in the cab let the lookout reach fire wardens or crews.[^1]

The encyclopedia also says a Braden Mountain tower was taken down and reassembled at View Tree Knob, though that tower is gone.[^1] The Tennessee list marks the Braden Mountain entry as moved.[^3]

From the mid-1970s, the encyclopedia says, forestry crews relied more on spotting planes and 911 calls, which made the manned tower network obsolete.[^1] The encyclopedia also says gradual statewide decommissioning of lookout infrastructure ended active use of this installation, but it gives no closing year.[^1] The Tennessee list marks the View Tree Knob tower as removed.[^2]

Today, the encyclopedia says, only the base of the tower survives at the summit.[^1] It also says much of the surrounding landscape lies in the North Cumberland Wildlife Management Area.[^1] No source reached says whether the summit is open to the public.
