The Snake Ridge Lookout Tower was originally built in 1939 with Civilian Conservation Corps labor.[^2][^3] It stood on a ridgetop south of Nelsonville, Ohio, in the Wayne National Forest.[^1][^5] The register describes a 100-foot tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree about the county of that first site. The Forest Service marker places it in Athens County, while the Eastern U.S. lookouts page and the Forest Fire Lookout Association list say Hocking County.[^3][^2][^7] The sources read do not name anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched. The marker says the Forest's towers "were actively used until the 1970s."[^3]

The register says that in 1964 the top 20 feet and the cab were moved to Hocking College.[^1] Natural Ohio Adventures says the top section went to the college for exercise drills, and the Forest Service dates the dismantling to the 1970s.[^4][^5] The two dates are not reconciled in the sources. The Forest Service rebuilt the tower at the Wayne National Forest headquarters in 2007.[^1][^5] In 2010 the Ohio chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association reported that the tower had been moved, opened and certified by the association.[^6]

The tower and its marker stand on the grounds of the Wayne National Forest headquarters on US 33.[^3] The Forest Service's photo caption says the tower now stores radio and other communication equipment and is not open to the public.[^5] A third-party guide says it is open for limited public use.[^4] Neither source is dated, so check with the Wayne National Forest before visiting. Do not climb it.
