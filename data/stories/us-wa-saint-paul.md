The Saint Paul lookout stood on a peak in Pierce County, about 16 miles west-northwest of Mount Rainier.[^2] A 1952 biennial report from the state Forestry Division called a lookout on the site urgently needed, and said a new road to it was partly built.[^3][^4] The Register and firelookout.com date the tower to 1954 and describe a 40-foot wooden L-4 tower.[^1][^2]

Not everyone agrees on the height.[^1][^2][^3][^4] A later report described a 20-foot tower with a 14x14 cab on top.[^3][^4] Eric Willhite thinks that figure is too low, and says the tower looks more like a 30- or 40-foot structure.[^4] Reaching the site took six miles of converted railroad grade and about three-quarters of a mile of new road.[^3][^4]

The Register says the tower was removed in 1972.[^1] Firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed that year, and Forest Lookouts marks it simply as removed.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Around 2014 the summit was logged, and the slash from that work now covers much of the possible remains.[^4] When Willhite visited in 2016, the footings sat to one side and were visible.[^4] No tower stands there now.[^4]

The land is private timber.[^4] Willhite says that in 2016 the owner was Hancock Forest Management, which required an expensive permit, and he thinks ownership and rules have likely changed since.[^4] The road is gravel and the site can be reached by car, but you need a permit to go up.[^4] The Register, by contrast, names the state Division of Forestry/DNR as the administrator.[^1]
