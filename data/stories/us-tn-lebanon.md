The Lebanon Fire Tower stands inside Cedars of Lebanon State Park in Wilson County, Tennessee.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it a central feature of the park and lists it as US 1263, TN 30.[^1] The Register records that it was registered on January 15, 2018, after a nomination by Richard Panther.[^1]

Forest Lookouts, which files the site under the name Hurricane, says a 108-foot steel tower was erected there in 1936.[^2] Tennessee Landforms lists a Hurricane tower in Wilson County as standing.[^3] The sources read do not say why the tower was built.

The park's land has a New Deal history. In September 1935 President Franklin Roosevelt approved the Lebanon Cedar Forest Project, and the Resettlement Administration began development on the land two months later.[^4] Tennessee Encyclopedia says a lookout tower and a nursery were also built on the site, but it does not name or date the tower, so it is not clear that it is this one.[^4]

The sources also differ on the setting and the figures. The Register places the tower in the state park, says the Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation administers it, and gives an elevation of 782 feet.[^1] Forest Lookouts places the site in the Cedars of Lebanon State Forest, and Tennessee Landforms gives 794 feet.[^2][^3]

The Register page carries a photo dated December 30, 2017, courtesy of Richard Panther, and Tennessee Landforms also lists the tower as standing.[^1][^3] The sources do not say whether visitors may climb it. Because it stands in a state park, check with park staff before going up.
