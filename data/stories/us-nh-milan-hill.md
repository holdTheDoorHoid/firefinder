Milan Hill stands in Coos County, New Hampshire, about ten miles north of Berlin, inside the state park that carries its name.[^2] The 45-foot steel fire tower on its summit, topped with a standard New Hampshire wooden cab, went up in 1932.[^1] The park around it grew out of a Civilian Conservation Corps camp established in the 1930s, though nothing found here says outright that the CCC built the tower itself, only that a camp operated in the area in those same years.[^5]

During World War II the lookout did double duty as an aircraft spotting station, watching the skies alongside its forest-fire job.[^1] The New Hampshire Division of Forests and Lands, under the state Department of Resources and Economic Development, has been its cooperating agency.[^1] It was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on March 12, 2002, nominated by NH registrar Chris Haartz, as US 412 / NH 13.[^1]

A 2019 inspection for the register found the tower in generally good shape overall - glass intact, stairs sound - though the wooden siding needed paint and some shingles had come loose on the grounds.[^1] The inspector noted it is staffed only "for a few short weeks of the year," mostly to support New Hampshire Fish and Game's moose-tracking work rather than as a year-round fire watch, and that no NHLR sign or directional signs were posted at the time.[^1]

The tower still stands today in Milan Hill State Park, one of the park's draws alongside yurt camping and cross-country ski trails.[^2] Visitors can climb to the observation deck just below the cab, with views reported to reach into Maine, Vermont, and Canada on a clear day.[^2]

One loose end: the register's own page gives coordinates for the tower that sit about a mile from where two independent sources - OpenStreetMap's mapped building and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's listing - place it.[^3][^4] This record already uses the latter, better-corroborated location.
