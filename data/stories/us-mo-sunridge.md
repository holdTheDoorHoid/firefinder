Sunridge is a former state fire lookout on a six-acre site on a ridge off Highway 21, north of Hillsboro in Jefferson County.[^2] The Missouri Conservation Commission bought the land on 16 December 1948 for $1 and other valuable considerations.[^2] The first tower there was built of pine poles and stood about 60 feet or more tall.[^2]

Sources disagree on when it went up. A 2014 local history, drawing on the state agency's records, gives 1940 and says the Civilian Conservation Corps may have built it.[^2] A 1968 newspaper report dates the old tower to 1949.[^1] A public dedication, announced for 17 June 1951, drew at least a dozen civic, conservation, state and federal groups, plus perhaps 150 citizens.[^1]

Jack Koch was the fifth man the state assigned to the tower. The four before him served five years between them, and by 1968 he had been running the tower for 15 years.[^1] In April 1968 he reported four fires in a single day, and he said the number of fires had declined in recent years.[^1]

By 1968 the wooden tower was badly deteriorated and becoming unsafe. The state took it down by early September and put up an 80-foot steel tower on the same site.[^1] A state annual report for 1968-69 confirms that a new steel tower went up on the site.[^1] In 1980 the conservation agency deeded the tower to Jefferson County Parks, since it had no further need for it.[^2]

Today the tower stands in Sunridge Park, a Jefferson County park open from dawn until dusk, with picnic tables, a pavilion and walking trails.[^3] Sources give different heights. A 2014 history estimated about 100 feet, and a Leader article gives 100 feet.[^2][^4] A St. Louis magazine piece gives 80 feet.[^5] The county listing does not say whether the tower can be climbed.[^3]
