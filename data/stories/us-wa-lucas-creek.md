The Lucas Creek lookout stood on a summit in Lewis County, Washington, about 14 miles east of Chehalis.[^1][^2] The Washington Forest Fire Association built a new tower there in 1945 to fill a gap between observation points.[^3] Weyerhaeuser assisted the build by pushing roughly a mile of access road up to the summit and carrying one of its cabins to the top as quarters for the lookout.[^4]

In 1950 a replacement went up: a 12-foot timber tower topped by a cab measuring 14 by 14 feet, standing in place of an earlier tower.[^3] Over several years in the early 1950s, James Robert Fitzhugh worked as lookout.[^4] In 1952 a Chehalis paper reported that Mr. Challender was back at work as lookout.[^3] In 1957 the state Department of Natural Resources opened the post.[^3] In 1964 Rosalie Hendricks of Onalaska was on duty there.[^4]

The register and the lookout inventory say the tower was removed in 1967, while Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^4][^2]

Leslie Romer, a repeat visitor, saw cement footings there around 2003, before logging took place.[^4] On later visits, around 2010 and 2016, she saw only cement steps and a block that had been shifted.[^4] A 2018 visit found scotch broom and blackberry obscuring whatever traces of the lookout remained.[^4]

The site sits within Weyerhaeuser's Vail Tree Farm, and visitors need a recreation access permit to go there lawfully.[^4][^5] Holders of a motorized permit can drive up to the summit.[^4]
