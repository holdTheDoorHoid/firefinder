St. Mary Peak Lookout sits on the summit of St. Mary Peak in the Bitterroot National Forest, in Ravalli County, Montana.[^1][^3] The site is a trail destination: the nomination calls it remote and accessible only by trail, and the volunteer history describes a 3.8-mile trail to the summit.[^3][^4]

The first lookout is hard to date. The NHLR and firelookout.com both place a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab in 1931.[^1][^2] In the nomination's account, the forest upgraded the site in 1934 with a lookout house on a 40-foot pole tower.[^3] The volunteer history also uses 1934, but says that original tower was 50 feet tall and was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^4]

High winds ended that first lookout. The nomination says a violent windstorm in 1951 blew the cab 100 feet down the mountain, and the volunteer history says high winds destroyed the lookout that spring.[^3][^4]

The nomination puts the cost of the present cab, finished in 1953, at $4,970.[^3] The volunteer history gives 1952 to 1953 as the build years and credits Forest Service engineer Clyde Fickes, whose L-4 design aimed at fast, low-cost building in remote spots.[^4] Firelookout.com says the cab went up in 1952 on an 8-foot timber tower, with the base enclosed in stone the next year.[^2] The stone base is 8 feet in the nomination and 10 feet in the NHLR listing.[^3][^1]

The volunteer history says the Forest Service staffed the lookout from 1952 until 1972, when it closed the post because fog and low clouds blocked the view.[^4] The nomination instead says the forest has stationed unpaid volunteers there since 1975.[^3] Firelookout.com's list marks 1971 as not staffed.[^2]

From 1977 to 2009 staffing came and went, and the volunteer history blames weather damage, vandalism and neglect for the lookout's decline.[^4] In 1998 Passport in Time volunteers restored the exterior, and the cedar roof was replaced in kind in 2008.[^3] The NHLR registered the lookout on November 27, 1992.[^1]

As of the nomination, prepared in January 2018, volunteers covered summer staffing at the lookout.[^3] Firelookout.com also lists it as manned in season by volunteers.[^2] The volunteer history is by Gordon Schofield, a volunteer lookout host.[^4]

The summit height is also disputed. The NHLR lists 9,241 feet, while the nomination, firelookout.com and the volunteer history all use 9,351 feet.[^1][^3][^2][^4]
