Goode Ridge sits in Chelan County, about 15 miles northwest of Stehekin, inside the Wenatchee National Forest.[^2][^1] The register gives the site's elevation as 7,400 feet, and firelookout.com matches that figure, but Willhite lists the lookout at 6,780 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

An L-4 cab went up in 1937 and was blown over a cliff in 1938.[^1][^2][^3] A second L-4 cab was built in 1939.[^1][^2][^3] Panoramic images of the ridge were taken in September 1934, about three years before the first cab went up.[^1][^3]

The register says the second cab was removed in 1950, while firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3]

The trail was originally built to serve the lookout, and it is now a National Park Service hiking trail in North Cascades National Park.[^4] The Park Service says the lookout was removed years ago, but the views remain.[^4] The trail runs five miles one way from the Upper Stehekin Valley Trail, gains about 4,400 feet, and is rated strenuous.[^4] Snowpack can obscure the upper trail well into late July in most years, and there is no camping along the trail; overnight stays need a backcountry permit, and permits are limited.[^4]

Willhite's page says he had not visited the site when he wrote it.[^3] The Park Service notes that what is left of the lookout building is mostly broken glass among the rocks.[^4]
