The Weddell Tower is a fire lookout inside Sesquicentennial State Park in Richland County, South Carolina.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1936 to mark Columbia's sesquicentennial, 150 years after the city was founded in 1786.[^1] The tower is 90 feet tall, an Aermotor LS-40 built of steel, and its cab measures 7 by 7 feet.[^1] It stands on a prehistoric sand dune.[^1]

The park itself came out of the same celebration.[^2] The Sesquicentennial Commission acquired 1,445 acres in 1937, paid for with surplus money, and Company #4469 of the Civilian Conservation Corps was tasked with developing the park.[^2] The Register's 1936 build date is a year earlier than that land purchase, and we could not reconcile the two.[^1][^2]

The South Carolina Forestry Commission operated the tower until 1993.[^1] The Register does not name the people who staffed it or any fire it watched, and we found no other account of either.[^1] The tower later went to the South Carolina Department of Parks, Recreation and Tourism, which now administers it.[^1]

The Register lists the tower as registered on October 2, 2012, as US 950, SC 11, on the nomination of Mike Finch, Jr.[^1] The page describes the tower in the present tense.[^1] A post from the Friends of Sesqui State Park says the tower is no longer safe to climb, and we found no official closure notice.[^3]
