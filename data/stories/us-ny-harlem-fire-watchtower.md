The Harlem Fire Watchtower, also called the Mount Morris Fire Watchtower, is a 47-foot cast-iron lookout in Marcus Garvey Park, in Harlem, Manhattan.[^1]

Sources give slightly different build years. Wikipedia's infobox says 1855 to 1857, the New York Landmarks Conservancy describes a structure from 1855 to 1856, and Wikidata gives 1856.[^1][^5][^2] Julius H. Kroehl built it for $2,300 from a design by James Bogardus.[^1] It went into service in 1857, in response to demand from Harlem residents.[^1]

It was one of 11 cast-iron watchtowers the city placed starting in the 1850s, and it is the only one that survives.[^1]

Volunteers used the tower as a lookout for fires. When they spotted one, they spread the word by ringing the bell.[^1] The Conservancy says a coded pattern of peals from the five-ton bell alerted local fire brigades.[^5]

Newer technology, such as pull boxes, made the watch system obsolete.[^1] It was discontinued, and the other towers were eventually destroyed.[^1] This one endured because it stood in a park.[^1]

During the New Deal, Works Project Administration workers rebuilt the ground around the tower, adding a plaza and wide steps.[^1] The city made it a landmark in 1967, and the National Register listed it on June 21, 1976.[^1][^3]

Over the years neglect did damage.[^1] Water got in through a damaged roof, many interior steps went missing, and a fence kept visitors back.[^1] In 2013, community groups and the city parks department set out to raise $4 million for restoration.[^1] From late 2014 the city took the tower apart and sent it out of state for work.[^1][^5]

The tower reopened to visitors in December 2019.[^1] A NYC Parks event on December 14, 2019 offered ranger-guided visits inside the tower.[^4] Wikipedia's infobox gives 2020 as the restoration year, which conflicts with the 2019 reopening.[^1] The restored tower now stands without the steel cradle that had held it up.[^5] It stands on the hilltop near the middle of the park.[^4]
