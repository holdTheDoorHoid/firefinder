The lookout stood on Goat Ridge in Lewis County, about 12 miles southeast of Packwood.[^1] Firetower.org describes it as an L-4 cab with a basement, built in 1933.[^2] WillhiteWeb says Oscar Boyer and other CCC workers built it in 1933, with the finishing work done in 1934.[^3] Panorama photos from the lookout are dated July 27, 1934.[^2]

A newspaper item dated June 29, 1963 says two Packwood district lookouts were manned on Thursday, Goat Ridge among them.[^4] WillhiteWeb says the Forest Service was still using the lookout that year.[^3]

WillhiteWeb says the cab was likely removed in 1966.[^3] Firetower.org says it had disappeared by the 1960s.[^2] A 1976 newspaper hiking piece called the spot the former lookout site.[^4]

The hike starts at the Berry Patch trailhead and makes a loop of about 2 miles.[^3] WillhiteWeb warns that the lookout site is a dead end off the loop, and that many unsure hikers have probably stumbled into it by mistake.[^3] One of its photos is captioned "Old lookout parts," and the page says the view today is largely unchanged from the 1934 panoramas.[^3]

Forest Road 21 begins about 2.7 miles west of Packwood, and WillhiteWeb says to follow its gravel road 13 miles before turning onto Forest Road 2150 for about 3 miles to the trailhead.[^3] No source I found describes the site's remains in more detail than that caption.
