The Pender tower was built in 1934 by Civilian Conservation Corps camp P-71, working from Camp Recovery in Prince William County [^1]. The state forest service planned a steel tower of about 100 feet, to stand beside a highway near Pender, Virginia [^2]. A December 25, 1933 item said it would be within a mile of a nudist camp in Fairfax County [^2]. Local residents later said the concern was exaggerated [^1]. A 1943 Coast & Geodetic Survey entry gave the tower's material as metal and its roof as green [^1].

Forestry Department interviews say the FCC used the tower until about 1978, when it was taken to Suffolk, Virginia [^1]. The register's removal field gives 1957 to 1966 instead [^1]. That range matches the years the tower dropped off the maps, so the two accounts do not agree, and both are reported here [^1].

The Virginia list now marks the site as gone [^3], and the register gives the current administrator as Fifty West Fairfax [^1]. No source describes what, if anything, stands at the site today, or whether the public can visit it. The same state list shows a standing tower called Dismal Swamp in the City of Suffolk [^3], but no source ties it to Pender, so it should not be taken as the same structure.
