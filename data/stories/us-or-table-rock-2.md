Table Rock Lookout sits at the southern tip of the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest in Baker County, Oregon, roughly 12 miles southwest of Unity.[^1][^2] Its first structure was a cupola from the early 1920s.[^1] An L-4 cab replaced it in 1937 by most accounts, though one lookout history dates the cabin to 1936.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout was first manned in 1939.[^3] A newspaper notice from that year called it a new lookout-fireman station, with Roy Weikal and his wife stationed there in the first season the point was staffed.[^3] The register says a new L-4 was built in 1949, and a lookout history also records the new house as completed that year.[^1][^3] A 2018 visit report, though, calls the cab 1937, so the dates conflict.[^4]

In 1962 a notice reported that Lou Maher had gone into the service and that Gary Squires had replaced him at Table Rock.[^3] In June 1997 thieves took 10 solar panels from the lookout, and the Forest Service offered up to $1,000 for their recovery.[^3] A July 2002 entry records that the lookout was evacuated when a fire the observer had reported earlier became extremely active.[^3]

The 2016 Rail Fire came up to the summit, and crews wrapped the lookout in protective foil to save it.[^4][^3] Accounts differ on which outbuildings burned. One says an old outhouse was lost, and another says an old wooden garage and the former outhouse were reduced to ashes.[^3][^5]

The site joined the National Historic Lookout Register on June 1, 1998, and the register still describes the lookout as in active service.[^1] A 2018 visit found it staffed for the summer.[^4] The Oregon Hikers field guide says it is staffed from late June or early July until the first snows.[^5]

Visitors can drive the 0.8-mile road or walk about half a mile from the Table Rock South Trailhead.[^5] The field guide notes that the staffer is used to receiving curious guests, but asks visitors to come between 9:00 a.m. and 6:00 p.m.[^5]
