The Pilot Knob tower stands on Flat Top Mountain, where Mercer and Wyoming counties meet.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register gives the site an elevation of 3,274 feet, or 998 metres.[^2]

No source I checked gives a construction date or a builder. The oldest dated mention I found is an October 1926 item, quoted on the Eastern U.S. Lookouts page from the Charleston Gazette.[^3] It says the tower was linked by telephone to the Bluefield Telephone Company's exchange at Motoaka.[^3] So a tower was already in use by then. When it went up, and why this summit was chosen, remain unrecorded.

I could not find details of its design, its height to the cab, who staffed it, or any fires it helped to spot.

The National Historic Lookout Register added the tower on January 26, 2017, after a nomination by Ron Stafford, who also supplied a photograph dated November 2011.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a standing tower.[^4] Abandoned Online, however, lists it as closed, with no date or reason.[^1] The two lists disagree, and I could not find a source that explains why.

The West Virginia Division of Forestry administers the tower.[^2] I found no source saying whether the public may visit or climb it, so its access is unknown. Check with the Division before going, and please do not climb it.
