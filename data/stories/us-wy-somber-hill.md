Somber Hill sits in Albany County, Wyoming, about 40 miles southwest of Laramie, in the Medicine Bow National Forest.[^1][^2]

The lookout began in 1911. The register says a wooden platform went up at the site that year.[^1] A May 1911 Laramie Republican item reported that the Forest Service was building a phone line from Foxpark to Somber Hill, where a lookout station would be kept.[^3]

A steel tower replaced the platform in 1927.[^1] An August 1927 item in the Pinedale Roundup described a glassed-in observatory at the top of a 60-foot steel tower.[^3]

In 1922 the man assigned to the station, Lee Hall, resigned because dizziness kept him from getting up the tower's ladder, and the post was not filled.[^3]

A July 1922 notice called Somber Hill a secondary lookout, with the primary watch kept on Medicine Bow Peak.[^3] A 1930 Casper paper, quoted by the register, later described both stations as primary lookouts, manned from June to October.[^1]

In September 1922 a guard reported smoke from the tower. A cross bearing from Medicine Bow Peak placed the fire about three miles south of Mountain Home, on the Colorado forest.[^3]

The last lookout named in the sources reviewed is Oscar Shoemaker, in 1932.[^1][^2]

The register lists the tower as removed in the 1960s. Firelookout.com marks the site gone, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's county index agrees.[^1][^2][^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today.
