In June 1922 a newspaper reported that E. Jennings was to serve as fire guard at the lookout station on Brannan Mountain from July 1, working for the Trinity Forest Service.[^1]

In May 1931 a lookout was established on the mountain, just across the Humboldt line and a short distance south of the Hoopa reservation.[^1] Its purpose was to watch for fires that the Ironsides lookout, near Burnt Ranch, could not see.[^1]

In August 1932 the lookout T.K. Walker reported a fire to the Salyer ranger station. Fireman Clem Bussell brought it under control, with three acres burned over.[^1]

The Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the site as gone, and records the tower type as unknown.[^2]

A hiker's trip report describes a rough road with no gate that goes up to the top of Brannan Mountain, and says the road from town is paved over much of its distance.[^3] At the summit the writer found a large green radio reflector and views that were severely limited.[^3] The page does not show when the trip was made, so this description may be out of date. No source says whether the summit is open to the public, and none describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout.
