The Civilian Conservation Corps put up a lookout tower on Veterans Peak in 1939.[^1][^3] The peak sits about six miles southwest of Sturgis, in Vanocker Canyon.[^2] Photos on the register page show a wooden tower of roughly 30 to 40 feet, with a log cabin whose windows face all four directions.[^1]

In June 1940 a newspaper item named Veterans Peak as a backup lookout, to be used only when fire conditions were bad.[^3] In August 1948 an emergency lookout at the Veterans Tower spotted a fire 25 miles south of Deadwood and Lead. The paper classed it as human-caused.[^3] A July 1954 fire near the lookout burned only half an acre.[^3]

A lookout history page lists Byron Hazeltine in 1949 and Royce Coffield in 1962.[^4] An April 1962 item, quoted on another lookout page, said the lookout was manned and that Coffield was stationed there.[^3] In June 1963 a newspaper said the Veterans Peak and Norris Peak stations would be staffed according to the weather.[^3]

The register and the summit guide both date the tower's removal to the 1950s.[^1][^2] The 1963 item suggests the station was still in use that year, so the sources do not agree on when the tower came down.[^3]

Today a radio tower stands on the summit.[^1][^2] The register reports no remnants of the lookout, since a radio tower and an equipment building now occupy its site.[^1] The summit guide notes that one of the radio towers now on top sits where the old lookout stood.[^2]

The summit guide names Vanocker Canyon Road as the quickest, best approach.[^2] The guide describes a shaded road to the summit and says the hike, brief and enjoyable, draws both hikers and geocachers.[^2] The Northern Hills Ranger District in Spearfish oversees the land.[^2] With no lookout left, there is no tower to climb.[^1]
