White Mountain History credits the Mount Pleasant Hotel Company with the region's earliest lookout, put up around 1904 on the summit of Mount Rosebrook.[^1] The fires had already blackened the top.[^1] The peak, at 3,004 feet, is in Bethlehem.[^2]

The lookout was a log structure, probably only about 20 feet tall.[^1] Guy wires tied it to boulders so that wind would not topple it.[^1] A telephone line connected it to the hotel, and it was on watch during times of need.[^1] NH Family Hikes calls it a fire tower, built shortly after the fires.[^2]

In 1929 a steel tower on Mount Hale replaced the Rosebrook lookout.[^1]

According to White Mountain History, what little survives today is guy wires and part of a roof, now covered by leaves and spruce needles.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the structure as gone, with its type unknown.[^3] NH Family Hikes files Mount Rosebrook among the mountains that once had a fire tower, in line with the other two accounts.[^2]

No source I read says whether the site is open to visitors today.
