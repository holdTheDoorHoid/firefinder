The Gardner Lookout stands on East Peak in Mount Tamalpais State Park, Marin County, California.[^1][^2] Civilian Conservation Corps veterans of Company 1920V built it of sturdy stone, wood and steel, hauling materials up the peak by hand and by cable.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1934 as the built year and says the crew worked there from 1934 to 1936; the state parks page gives 1935 to 1936.[^1][^2]

The new tower replaced a wooden building that had long housed the lookout on the peak. A 1935 newspaper report said it would be named for the late fire warden Ed Gardner and built of field stone from the ridges.[^4] By July 1936 the CCC had finished it, and the lookout had moved in.[^4]

In April 1936, with the new tower still unfinished, the ranger Hank Long was on duty around the clock.[^4] He had been the Mount Tamalpais lookout for six years when he died in January 1940.[^4] Fred Petri, a Mill Valley fire warden, was appointed to succeed him that May.[^4]

The lookout does not appear in these sources as the scene of any large fire. In September 1946 it spotted a San Anselmo fire that engines had trouble finding, and about half an acre of grass burned.[^4]

The lookout stayed in use. In 2014 a $207,000 system of four remote fire-detection cameras, funded by a Pacific Gas and Electric grant to FIRESafe Marin, began keeping watch over the county from four peaks. Two of the cameras give round-the-clock coverage on Mount Tamalpais and Mount Barnabe, where the lookouts are staffed only in daytime by volunteers.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Marin County Fire Department was still using the Gardner Lookout as of 2019, and it registered the tower on September 21, 2015, as US 1115, CA 108.[^1]

Today the state parks page says visitors get a good view of the tower from the parking lot at the base of East Peak.[^2] Nothing consulted says the cab is open to visitors, and because it is still an active county lookout, the sensible way to see it is from the ground.[^2]
