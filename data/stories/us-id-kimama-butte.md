Kimama Butte, about 15 miles northwest of Rupert in Minidoka County, Idaho, carried a fire lookout run by the Bureau of Land Management.[^1] Forest Lookouts lists the Grazing Service before the BLM as the agency.[^2] The first cab, 14 by 14 feet with trailer quarters, dates to 1937, and a new cab followed in 1975.[^1][^2]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1950 calls the house a square frame structure with windows on all four sides.[^2]

Named keepers appear in the lists.[^2] Walter Chapman was keeper in 1958.[^2] In 1962 Boyd Walton and his wife came back to Shoshone from a summer at the station.[^2] Bob Locke kept a regular fire watch there in 1970.[^2]

On July 11, 1946, a range fire broke out on the butte between Paul and Kimama.[^2] Lookouts at the station reported that it was still not under control that afternoon, with about 100 acres burned.[^2]

In 1947 the station went unmanned for lack of money.[^2] The district grazier said a lightning-set fire, the Laidlaw park fire, went unseen until Monday because it was far from any lookout and the station was not manned.[^2] It burned about 20,000 acres of brush and grass.[^2]

In September 1953 a lightning bolt struck within 50 feet of the station and started a range fire in Lincoln County that burned 4,000 acres, according to the Post-Register.[^2]

Two sources date the cab's destruction to 2003.[^1][^2] The climbing guide says the lookout was demolished in the early 2000s.[^3] The sources disagree on when staffing ended. The climbing guide says, as best it can tell, the butte was manned in summers from 1937 to 1975, while SummitPost says the BLM used it from 1937 to at least 1970.[^3][^4]

Today the butte houses several radio towers, and SummitPost says the fire lookout is no longer present.[^4] The climbing guide places the summit boulders between the antenna towers, and a two-track road climbs toward the top, steep and rocky near the summit.[^3] SummitPost says entrance to the BLM land is unrestricted, but it advises a high-clearance vehicle on the roads.[^4]

No source says whether any part of the cab or its footings survives, and none describes public access to the tower area itself.
