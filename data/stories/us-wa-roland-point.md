Roland Point is in Whatcom County, Washington, on Mt. Baker National Forest land. It rises about 400 feet from the Ross Lake shore when the lake is full.[^3][^4]

A lookout went up in 1933, with an L-5 cab and a crows nest. WillhiteWeb says a 35-foot tower may have been added later.[^1][^4]

During the war years the site also served as an Aircraft Warning Service station, active from April 6, 1942 to October 16, 1943.[^4][^3] The AWS also put sleeping quarters on the site.[^3] When the post closed, the Forest Service kept the site and continued it as a fire lookout.[^4]

The lookout was gone in 1959.[^1][^3] The register and the Forest Lookouts page say it was removed, FireLookout.com says it was destroyed, and WillhiteWeb says removed or destroyed.[^1][^3][^2][^4]

Photo captions credited to Eric Willhite show the remains of an outhouse, and WillhiteWeb's captions label footings at the site.[^1][^4] No source describes the remains in more detail than these captions.[^1][^4]

No trail gets up to the site, but the forest is easy walking from the lakeshore or boating camps.[^4] WillhiteWeb puts the walk at under a mile from the lake.[^4] A 2022 trip report describes a boat-in campsite limited to two tent sites, and says the writer got a camping permit for it.[^5]
