The Kentucky Division of Forestry put up the Charleston Tower in 1957, on the Niles Ford road just west of the community of Charleston in Hopkins County.[^1] A ranger from Dawson Springs supervised the construction, and the state highway department helped prepare the short road that led up to it.[^1]

The tower replaced an older wooden lookout that had stood east of Beulah on Highway 70.[^1] The newspaper said the new steel tower was 60 feet high, twenty feet taller than the one it replaced, and that it would cover about 125,000 acres, most of them in Hopkins County, with the area also reaching Caldwell and Christian counties.[^1]

A 1962 survey description portrays the tower as a gray, four-legged steel frame that stands on its own, with an observation room at the top.[^2] The survey station marks the center of the tower's base, about 3 1/2 miles north of Dawson Springs.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page now marks the Charleston tower as "Removed," with no date given.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the land is open to visitors, so do not assume the tower is still standing.
