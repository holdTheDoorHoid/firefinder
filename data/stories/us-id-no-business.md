No Business Lookout stands on Southern Idaho Timber Protective Association land about eleven miles from McCall, Idaho, and the association administers it.[^5][^1] The mountain has been a fire-detection site since the 1920s, when it was first a camp.[^1] A November 1936 report in the Idaho Statesman announced a new station there, modeled on Packer John lookout near Smith's Ferry, and it placed the site about 15 miles southwest of McCall.[^2] The same report quotes the association's secretary as fed up with Bald Mountains, and it calls the new name a departure.[^2]

The two-story log house, roughly 20 feet square, went up in 1937 with Civilian Conservation Corps labor.[^2] Accounts differ on its materials. The NHLR describes a log structure, while firelookout.com describes a log and frame house.[^1][^3] Its 1963 replacement was built to the same plan.[^1][^3] Keeping Watch describes the building as a D-6 cabin, essentially a small house with two floors, and calls it one of the only remaining D-6 lookouts in the Northwest.[^5]

The NHLR says the lookout is staffed during fire season and welcomes visitors, and firelookout.com ties its staffing to the summer.[^1][^3] In September 1966 four smokejumpers controlled a fire of less than a quarter acre southeast of the lookout. Officials were not sure what had started it.[^2]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as a drive-up, staffed lookout.[^4] The National Historic Lookout Register added it to its listings on October 3, 2020.[^1][^4]
