The tower stood on Johnson Hill, near Meagher's Grant in Halifax, Nova Scotia.[^1] It was erected in 1962 as a 30.5 m (100 ft) steel tower with a fibreglass cab.[^2] The Nova Scotia Department of Natural Resources administered it.[^1]

It replaced an older tower about 40 km (25 miles) to the north. It was built to spot fires in a region of active timber harvesting.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register added the tower on August 15, 1998.[^1] Its 1998 entry says the tower is staffed each summer.[^1]

A 2009 list of the department's seasonal fire towers places Johnson Hill near Lower Meaghers Grant.[^4] A Nova Scotia Department of Natural Resources geology report also mentions the road that runs to the fire tower on Johnson Hill.[^5]

CBC News reports that the department decommissioned its fire towers in 2015 and later dismantled them.[^3] The report does not name Johnson Hill; the link to this tower is an inference from the 2009 list, which names it as a department tower.[^4] The department said the towers were removed because most wildfire detection still happens through aerial surveillance and calls from the public.[^3]

No source describes the Johnson Hill site today, so nothing here says what, if anything, remains of the tower, or whether the hill is open to visitors.
