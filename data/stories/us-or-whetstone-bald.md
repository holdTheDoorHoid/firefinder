The lookout on Whetstone Point, which one account calls the Bald Mountain Lookout, stood about three miles northwest of Prospect, Oregon.[^2][^4] Forest Lookouts lists the site under both the Crater National Forest and the Rogue River National Forest.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the first structure, a camp and a crow's nest in a tree, to 1916.[^1] A 1916 account by Harold D. Foster describes the nest as reached by climbing the limbs of a Douglas fir.[^3] The same site page also lists a crow's nest on an 80-foot tree in 1914, two years before the register's date.[^3]

In 1920 a cupola cabin on a 10-foot pole tower replaced the platform.[^1][^2] A visitor's page on the nearby Halls Point lookout describes the cabin as an elevated D-6 cupola on a tower built that year.[^4] Forest Lookouts gives a different sequence. A Medford newspaper reported in July 1924 that the Forest Service was putting in a new standard lookout house on the peak, and the site page says a house was built that year, with lumber cut at the Medford warehouse.[^3] The sources do not reconcile 1920 with 1924.

Harry Richards was the lookout in 1930. A Medford paper reported that June that he had shot a 200-pound panther prowling outside the cabin, and it called the peak Whetstone Peak, or Old Baldy.[^3] Nils Olson was back at his post in 1940, which the paper counted as his eighth year as lookout patrolman.[^3]

Its duties passed to newer lookouts at White Point, built in 1951, and Halls Point, built in 1956.[^3] Forest Lookouts says a forestry crew burned the cabin in 1958, after a short period with no one posted.[^3] The register and the Halls Point page say the tower was removed that year.[^1][^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the public can reach it. The White Point lookout, a separate tower on the same ridge, still stands but is not staffed, according to the Halls Point page, which describes a July 2023 visit.[^4] Do not mistake one site for the other.
