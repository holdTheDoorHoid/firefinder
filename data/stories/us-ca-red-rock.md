Red Rock Lookout stands on a peak in Plumas County, in the Plumas National Forest, at about 7,595 feet.[^1] The register says the present station replaced an earlier lookout dated to 1933 or 1934, and describes it as a BC-301 cabin on an enclosed timber tower.[^1]

The new station went up in 1941.[^1] A June 1941 newspaper report said a spike camp would house a Civilian Conservation Corps crew to do the building, and that the job was to be rushed for completion by June 30.[^2]

The lookout's staff appear in the local papers.[^2] In September 1949 a fire set by a hunter who thought he was lost was spotted by the Red Rock lookout.[^2] From 1954 the papers name Wanda Hansen as the lookout, sometimes spelled Hanson, and report her spending summers there with her children.[^2] In 1962 one item said that she "will tend the lookout."[^2] The newspaper items I found end there, so I could not tell when staffing stopped.

The register lists a registration date of November 30, 2019.[^1] In 2026 the Forest Service sought quotes to renovate the existing lookout.[^3] The notice describes deferred maintenance, including roofing, a lightning protection system, off-grid solar, and interior and exterior finishes.[^3] Responses are due October 21, 2026, and an August 27 amendment added organized site-visit information.[^3]

I found no source saying whether the cab is open to visitors or whether anyone staffs it now. Do not climb it without checking with the Plumas National Forest first.
