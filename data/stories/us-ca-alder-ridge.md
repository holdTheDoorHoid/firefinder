Alder Ridge Lookout stands in the Eldorado National Forest, in El Dorado County, California, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1]

The Register credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it in 1936. It is 60 feet tall, an Aermotor design, with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1][^2] HistoriCorps gives 1937 instead, and it also describes a ranger cabin and a single-car garage on the site.[^2] A July 1936 Mountain Democrat notice, quoted by Forest Lookouts, announced plans for a 60-foot tower on Alder Hill, along with a house and a garage, which fits the earlier date.[^3]

Most sources say 60 feet, but a September 2000 Mountain Democrat report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, gives the height as 80 feet.[^3] The Register lists the elevation as 6,192 feet, while HistoriCorps gives 6,225 feet.[^1][^2]

The Register added the lookout on September 21, 2004, the same year a Passport In Time project completed work on the tower and the ground residence.[^1] A 2004 Central California lookout report says the cabin was restored to serve as a rental for recreational visitors.[^4] HistoriCorps says volunteers worked on the cabin the season before that, and its 2024 project was set to finish the job.[^2]

A Campflare listing says the site is shut to visitors and offers no public camping or bookings.[^5] It adds that the Eldorado National Forest is handling deferred maintenance and restoration with Great American Outdoors Act money, and that a recreation rental may be possible later, though no timeline has been announced.[^5] The 2004 report describes the cabin as set up for rental, while the current listing treats a rental as a future possibility, so the sources do not agree on whether one ever opened.[^4][^5]

The sources read do not say who staffed the lookout or what fires it watched. Anyone planning a visit should check with the Eldorado National Forest first.
