Indian Hill has watched over the Nez Perce National Forest since 1920, when the Forest Service kept lookout simply by posting a tent camp at several points along the ridge.[^1] A more permanent setup followed in 1928: a cabin, still used for storage today, along with a 50-foot pole tower carrying a small 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] The structure standing now dates to 1951, when crews replaced the earlier tower with an L-4 cab on a 41-foot treated timber tower.[^2][^3]

The site has a long staffing record, though it's patchy in places. Thomas Morris held the post from 1937 to 1941, and Gail Renshaw is remembered there around 1950.[^1] John Crawford then held the post for more than three decades, from 1976 to 2008, followed by Bob Zimmerman in 2009-2010 and Lawrence Gilley from 2011 to 2013.[^1] Kris Anderson was on duty in 2014 and into 2015, and had to come down early that year when the Wash Fire bore down on the tower.[^1][^4] The fire damaged the structure, and by one account it stayed closed for roughly five years afterward before John Crawford returned, staffing it again from 2022 through 2024.[^1]

Indian Hill was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on April 6, 2009, recognizing it as one of the historic fire-watch structures still doing its original job.[^2] It had also been threatened once before, by the Slims Fire in 2003, though that blaze didn't reach the tower.[^3]

The lookout is drive-up accessible and remains an active, staffed Forest Service lookout on the Nez Perce National Forest, sitting at about 6,810 feet elevation.[^1][^2] Sources differ slightly on exactly when the earliest lookout presence began and on small details of the pole-tower years, which is common for sites with this long a history, but the core timeline and present use are consistent across the records.[^1][^3]
