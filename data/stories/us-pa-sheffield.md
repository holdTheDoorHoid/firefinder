Warren County was allotted two new fire towers in 1925, one at Torpedo and one at Sheffield, and by August crews were laying the cement foundations.[^2] The register describes the Sheffield tower as an Aermotor steel tower erected in 1925 and taken down in 1994.[^1]

A 1971 newspaper dated the tower's 41 years of service from 1930.[^2] It began as a state tower under the Pennsylvania Department of Forests and Waters.[^2] Around 1954 the U.S. Forest Service took it over in a trade for Coal Knob Tower.[^2]

Hundreds of visitors have climbed its steep stairs, and it was manned during the spring and fall fire seasons. The 1971 article puts the cab 93 feet up.[^2] An August 1960 newspaper report on the Sheffield District of the Allegheny National Forest said that in April a gas line supplying the tower with bottled gas had been cut in two by a rifle bullet.[^2]

In June 1971 the Warren Times-Mirror and Observer reported that the tower had been sold and would be dismantled that summer.[^2] The register gives 1994 as the year it was removed, so the two dates do not match.[^1]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site now, or whether the public can reach it.
