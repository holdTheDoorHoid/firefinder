Blacktail Butte stands in Grand Teton National Park, about nine miles north-northeast of Jackson Hole.[^1] A climbing guide describes the lookout as a plain frame structure whose windows sit on three of its sides, and says the Forest Service built it in the 1930s.[^2] A National Park Service nomination form for the Huckleberry Lookout says work on six lookouts in the northern part of the Teton National Forest started in the 1930s, and it names Blacktail Butte among them.[^3]

By late summer 1951 the lookout was staffed by the Park Service.[^4] A Rigby Star item dated August 30 that year, as reproduced on a lookout history site, names Mr. and Mrs. Stanley Frieberg as Park Service lookout staff on the butte.[^4] The same item describes elk bolting from the woods, a mother bear with two cubs, and lightning that glanced off the cabin and jolted Mr. Frieberg.[^4]

Firelookout.com lists the lookout as gone.[^1] The climbing guide, titled as a former fire lookout, asks readers to email photos of its footings.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.

Today the butte is a hiking and climbing spot.[^5] The Park Service trailhead lies a mile north of Moose Junction, on the highway's east side, and serves hikers heading for the summit and climbers on bolted routes.[^5] The trailhead has no facilities, so bring water.[^5]
