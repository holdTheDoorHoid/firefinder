Shadow Mountain Lookout sits near the summit of Shadow Mountain above Grand Lake, in the southwestern corner of Rocky Mountain National Park, at about 9,923 feet.[^1][^2] It is the only fire lookout surviving in the park.[^2]

Fire detection began on the peak in 1930, when a phone line was run up from the Grand Lake Ranger Station.[^1] The tower went up in 1932 to a design from the Park Service's San Francisco Landscape Architecture Division, and Wikipedia counts it among the best rustic Park Service buildings.[^2] The lookout register says construction was first planned for 1931 but the bids were too high, so work began in 1932 and the stone tower was finished the next year.[^1] Its opening summary gives 1934 instead.[^1]

Sources disagree on the builder. Wikipedia credits Civilian Conservation Corps labor,[^2] while the Rocky Mountain Conservancy says a local contractor built it.[^4] Early on, rangers staffed the tower for several days at a time, watching for fire and sending daily reports.[^4] After World War II, married couples became seasonal residents.[^4]

The end of its lookout years is reported differently. The register says the tower was in service until 1969, and the Conservancy says use lasted until the late 1960s.[^1][^4] Wikipedia gives 1978 as the last summer season.[^2]

Wikipedia gives August 2, 1978 as the date of its National Register listing, and the federal record gives the same day as its publication date.[^2][^3] The register gives 1987 instead.[^1] It joined the lookout register on August 13, 2003.[^1]

Its condition has been a concern. A 2019 note on the register describes the cab exterior and stone tower as in poor condition, with rotted decking and support beams.[^1] The Conservancy says it helped fund stairway and balcony repairs in 1996, then a structure report for 2020 and 2021 and a rehabilitation design for 2022 and 2023.[^4] Around $300,000 has been committed to the tower by the Conservancy and its donors.[^4] Planned work would secure the masonry foundation and take out the rotted stairway and railings to the upper floors.[^4]

Today the tower is closed to the public. The Conservancy says access has been closed since 2015 because of deterioration.[^4] Nearly two thousand hikers a year reportedly walk a five-mile rugged route to the tower.[^4] It should be seen from the ground, not climbed. No source consulted mentions a rental or says whether the tower will reopen.
