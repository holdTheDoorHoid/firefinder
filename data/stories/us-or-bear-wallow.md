Bear Wallow is in Curry County, Oregon, on the Siskiyou National Forest, about 13 miles east-northeast of Brookings.[^1][^3]

The first cab went up in 1934.[^1][^3] Forest Lookouts describes a 10-by-10-foot L-5 lookout house, built as a cabin for a fire guard.[^2] A May 1940 forest plan for guard placement favored the station over Mount Emily, because it covered higher ground.[^2] In 1941 the station was staffed for 94 days, with reports relayed to the Gold Beach station on a West Coast Telephone Company line. It also had radio.[^2]

Around 1946 the L-5 cab was replaced with an L-4 cab.[^1][^3] The register says the L-4 cab was removed in the 1960s.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed in the 1960s,[^3] and Forest Lookouts marks the site as removed.[^2] The register listed the site on November 18, 2010, with Ray Kresek as nominator.[^1]

The register says the site is administered by the U.S. Forest Service.[^1] No source describes what, if anything, is left at the site now, or whether the public can visit, so check with the Forest Service before going.
