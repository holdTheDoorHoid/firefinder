The tower stood on Maple Hill, near a spot where Duncan, Charleston and Bloss townships come together.[^1] In August 1921 State Forester Paul Mulford was overseeing the erection of a 60-foot steel tower there for the state forestry department, with a telephone link so observers could report fires quickly.[^1] A September 1921 report said it was nearly complete, and the register dates it to 1921.[^1][^2]

A towerman named Wood sighted a fire from the tower in May 1925.[^1] The blaze was contained at seven acres.[^1] In April 1926 the Maple Hill towerman spotted a fire that a spark from a log-train engine at Morris had started. It burned roughly forty acres of state forest before it was stopped.[^1]

In June 1939 the Department of Forests and Waters announced that the Maple Hill tower would be moved to Bloss Mountain, which it called a better location.[^1] The register says the tower was moved in 1939 and renamed Bloss Mountain.[^2] An October 1939 report gave the Bloss Mountain tower as 80 feet high and said it had formerly stood on Maple Hill.[^4] The sources do not say whether the tower was made taller when it moved. The register records the Bloss Mountain tower as removed in 1986.[^3]

The Maple Hill site was entered in the Former Fire Lookout Sites register on September 16, 2008.[^2]

Today, no source I read describes what remains at either the Maple Hill or the Bloss Mountain site, or whether either is open to visitors.
