The East River station is in Harris County, and the Forest Lookouts page lists the Texas Forest Service as its agency.[^1] A National Geodetic Survey description places the station about 8.5 miles north-northwest of Huffman, just southwest of a lookout tower near the edge of a small clearing.[^1] No source I read describes the East River site today.

Where the tower came from is disputed. Texas Highways and Bob Bowman both say the Lufkin tower came from near Conroe.[^2][^3] Bowman gives 1976 as the year of the move.[^3] A March 1974 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts Lufkin page, instead says a 100-foot tower "will be built" on the proposed museum site and would remain Texas Forest Service property.[^4] The sources do not agree on whether the tower was moved or newly built.

Texas Highways, in a March 2026 article, says a 100-foot fire tower built in 1936 overlooks the museum grounds in Lufkin.[^2] Bowman adds that the Civilian Conservation Corps built towers of this kind in 1936.[^3] The Texas Forestry Museum opened in 1976 as a repository for artifacts from the state's lumber industry, and its campus covers two acres near the city's eastern boundary.[^2] Admission to the indoor exhibits costs visitors nothing.[^2]

The tower itself is not for climbing. Bowman says it has been altered to keep climbers out, and a lookout cab from another tower is on display inside the museum's main building.[^3] Plan a visit around the exhibits and the grounds.
