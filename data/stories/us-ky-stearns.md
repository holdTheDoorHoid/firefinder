The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Stearns Fire Tower in 1934 on Sunset Rock, the highest point in McCreary County, Kentucky.[^3][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register places it on the Cumberland National Forest, a name since changed to the Daniel Boone National Forest.[^1] It was a 110-foot steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab at the top.[^1][^3]

The U.S. Forest Service first leased the land from Stearns Coal and used the tower to spot forest fires.[^4] By 2016 a local newspaper described the tower as no longer in use for spotting forest fires.[^4]

Once Stearns Coal stopped operating, the tower was left unattended, and Richard Burke could buy it.[^4] In 1988 Burke sold the tower and six acres of land to Nolan and Sondra Johnson for $10,000.[^4] The Johnsons have long wanted to restore the tower and turn it into a historic tourist draw.[^4] In 2016 they raised framed, lit letters to the top of the tower, and people began coming to Stearns to see it.[^4]

Both registers list the tower as torn down by 2024, and a house now sits on the site.[^1][^2] Because the registers give only an "as of" date, the year it came down is still unclear.[^1][^2] Today the land is privately owned, and no tower remains to climb.[^1][^2]
