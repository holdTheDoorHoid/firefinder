In 1922 the Fisher Forestry and Realty Company put up a fire lookout on a rock mountain in the town of Watson, Lewis County.[^1] A May 1922 report from the Journal and Republican, transcribed by Forest Lookouts, places it southeast of Half Moon Lake.[^1] The company meant it as a backup station for emergencies.[^1] According to the newspaper, the company kept a watchman up there during dry spells, and the tower was to back up the Beaver Lake Mountain and Stillwater Mountain stations.[^1]

The New York State chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association names it Rock Mt.[^2] Its entry says a private contractor from Lowville built the wooden tower that year, with the cooperation of the Conservation Commission.[^2] It places the tower three miles southeast of Crystal Lake and the Number Four road, which names different landmarks from the newspaper report.[^2]

Clarence Fisher, a landowner and conservationist, put the tower up partly to guard his large holdings and those of others nearby.[^2] In 1928 the company put up a steel tower at Number Four, about 80 feet high, with a glass-enclosed observatory.[^1] The state chapter says the steel tower gave a far better view, so the wooden one on Rock Mt. was no longer needed.[^2]

The sources disagree about what is left. The state chapter marks Rock Mt. as removed.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's state list shows a standing windmill tower under the name Rock Mountain, at 43.81416, -75.23833.[^3] CNY Hiking's introduction names Rock Mountain (Lewis County) among the privately owned towers it lists, but its table has no row for it.[^4] We could not tell whether a tower stands there now or whether the FFLA entry describes a different structure, so its status is unknown.

We found no public access described for the site. Because it appears to be private land, treat it as off limits unless the owner says otherwise.
