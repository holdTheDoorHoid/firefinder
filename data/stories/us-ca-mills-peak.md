Mills Peak Lookout stands in Sierra County, in the Plumas National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The Register says the first lookout on the site was an L-4 ground house, a type it calls unusual for California, and that it was built in 1933.[^1] A visitor guide reports an older Forest Service brochure that gives 1932 instead.[^3] Newspapers place a new lookout there in 1934, when Ben Hankey was moved from Mount Elwell.[^2] The sources give 1932, 1933 and 1934, so the visitor guide's best answer is the early 1930s.[^1][^2][^3]

A 1946 newspaper item says construction was finished in August 1934 and that Hankey had worked every season there apart from 1945. It credits him with first spotting 117 fires.[^2]

By 1960 T.L. Louton was in charge, and the Forest Service counted 1,520 visitors that season.[^2] In 1971 Audrey Flury was posted there, and the lookout drew close to 2,200 visitors a year.[^2] In 1975 Lin and May Ness were new to the post.[^2]

According to the Register, the lookout was restored and put back to work spotting fires in 2005.[^1] Its entry carries a registration date of September 21, 2004, which sits oddly beside that later work, and the sources do not explain why.[^1]

Today the Register lists the lookout as staffed, and a comment on the entry says it is in good condition.[^1] A visitor guide describes a tower with stairs, a platform and a compact cab.[^3] The guide says entry can hinge on staffing, fire conditions and posted rules, and it advises asking before entering.[^3] The Register's visitor comments say a car can make the roughly three-mile drive to the tower, while the guide recommends a high-clearance vehicle for the narrow last stretch.[^1][^3]
