The McPherson Mountain lookout went up in 1934 as a ground-level cabin on a peak in the Los Padres National Forest, Santa Barbara County.[^1] Craig Carey's 2011 account says the lookout sat on the ground rather than on a tower, a choice he ties to the peak's views and prominence.[^3]

In June 1935 the district ranger named two CCC men to fire lookouts and put Bill Hoey of Santa Maria in charge at McPherson.[^2] Hoey ran the lookout's radio, reporting to Santa Barbara every hour that summer.[^2] In July a fire being fanned by a 35-mile wind came within 400 yards of the station.[^2] Conrad Munson was assigned there in 1937. In December 1939 he was relieved as the last of the district's protection force at the close of the season.[^2]

Sources disagree on what became of the structure. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the structure was taken down at some point after 1984; only the mounting piers and a concrete cistern remain.[^1] A photo from November 1984 shows the lookout in poor condition and out of service.[^4] Carey dates its removal to the late 1980s.[^3] A trail guide says the facility was built in 1934 and burned on April 7, 1987.[^5]

When Carey visited in 2011, the summit was taken up by US Air Force communication arrays and related buildings.[^3] According to the trail guide, the only surviving piece of the old lookout is a large concrete tank for collecting rainwater.[^5] A 1967 notice had said bids were being taken for an eight-mile fire protection road to the lookout.[^2] None of these sources says whether the summit is open to visitors now.
