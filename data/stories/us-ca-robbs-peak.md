Robbs Peak rises to 6,686 feet near Union Valley Reservoir in the Eldorado National Forest.[^1][^3] A Mountain Democrat item from July 3, 1915, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, said Mr. Fred Carpenter would soon go on duty as lookout there.[^2] A 1936 notice said a new lookout house would be built on the peak.[^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built the present cab in 1937.[^1][^4] The register describes it as an L-4 design, 14 feet by 14 feet, with outriggers on a nine-foot enclosed wooden base, and dates its listing to September 21, 2004.[^1]

The Forest Service says it staffed the lookout from 1934 through 1978 to detect fires.[^3] Those dates sit awkwardly beside the 1915 notice, and the sources do not say whether staffing lapsed in between.[^2][^3]

A February 18, 1987 notice said a former lookout station atop the peak would open as a mountain hut after a two-day open house, with a $15 fee per night per party.[^2] The Forest Service now describes the hut as sharing the mountaintop with the lookout, so the record does not show which building that notice meant.[^2][^3]

The Forest Service rental guide, which is undated, says the lookout is open to the public for day use during the summer and is still used for fire detection during emergencies. The guide says lookout use is not included with the hut rental.[^3] The register also marks the site as available for rental, without saying whether that refers to the hut.[^1] Vehicle access is in summer only, by the Robbs Peak road (13N31).[^3]

A HistoriCorps project, led by staff with the help of volunteers, is replacing the hut's deteriorated cedar shingle roof and doing exterior and interior work on the lookout. The stated goal is public and administrative use of both structures.[^4] The lookout was named to the National Register of Historic Places in 2004, and the hut in 2015.[^4]
