Quartz Mountain is in the Umpqua National Forest, in Douglas County, Oregon.[^1] An August 1915 newspaper item about a bear and its cub says the mountain was one of the lookouts fire wardens used to spot fires in the Umpqua forest reserve.[^3] That suggests a lookout was in use there before the 1923 date that the register gives.[^1][^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says a cupola cabin built in 1923 was replaced with an L-5 cab in 1933.[^1][^2] A September 1933 Roseburg paper reported that a second crew had finished a 14-by-14-foot lookout on the mountain.[^3]

In June 1923 the Roseburg News-Review reported that George Spaur was going to Quartz Mountain to take a government job at the lookout.[^3] A 1937 "Believe It or Not" item, printed in the Nebraska State Journal, names Howard Church, of the lookout station, as the person who found a pilot's lost goggles.[^3]

In July 1920 an airplane patrol reported a fire near Quartz Mountain several hours before the lookouts found it.[^3]

The register says the L-5 cab was removed around 1972.[^1] The firelookout.com page says it was destroyed that year, and the Forest Lookouts page says only that the lookout was removed in 1972.[^2][^3] The sources do not agree on whether it was taken down or destroyed.[^1][^2][^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can go there. Ask the Umpqua National Forest before visiting.
