Big Hoodoo Mountain lies about 11 miles south of Libby, in Lincoln County, Montana, on the Kootenai National Forest.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com puts its elevation at 5,236 feet.[^1]

A July 1930 Western News item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, reported a new lookout station going up on the peak, with a trail and a telephone line to the top.[^2] The paper said a tower 85 feet high was needed for a clear view, and that it would be the highest on the Kootenai.[^2] Jack Halfstead was to be posted there.[^2] Firelookout.com instead dates the lookout to 1934 and describes an L-4 cab on a pole observation tower.[^1] The two dates do not match.[^1][^2]

A September 1938 item, quoted from the Montana Standard, describes a truckload of CCC youths sent out on the orders of a Hoodoo lookout, bringing a farmer with arthritis to town for medical care.[^2]

Firelookout.com says the lookout was abandoned in 1942 and is now gone.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list also reads "Tower Gone."[^3]

No source describes what remains on the summit, and none says whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
