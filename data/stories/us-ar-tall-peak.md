Tall Peak Fire Tower stands at the top of Tall Peak, in the southwestern part of the Ouachita National Forest in Polk County, Arkansas.[^1] Members of the 742nd Company of the Arkansas Civilian Conservation Corps, stationed at the Mena camp, built it around 1938 to guard against the blazes that had devastated the forest.[^2]

The descriptions of the tower do not quite match. The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 12-by-12-foot cab built for living in, with a catwalk, on a one-story stone base.[^3] The National Register form describes a two-story structure of fieldstone and wood, with an observation deck on the second floor.[^2] The tower was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on October 20, 1993.[^4] The lookout register later registered it on July 16, 1996, as US 183, AR 2.[^3]

The register counts 43 lookouts that once stood on the Ouachita, and says six remain.[^3] The register also says the tower has been inactive as a lookout for many years and was heavily vandalized.[^3] The registration form reported some vandalism and repairs, and called the structure deteriorated at the time.[^2] The sources do not say when the last watch was kept.

Two 2021 visit reports on the register describe its condition. The July report says the tower needs minor repairs and a fresh coat of paint.[^3] The September report found the building door locked, the catwalk in okay condition, and the tower reachable only by a hike of more than four miles.[^3] Wikipedia, however, says a forest service road reaches it, so the two accounts disagree on the route.[^1]

A February 2020 trip report describes the walk in, which begins at Shady Lake, a recreation area in the forest, and crosses narrow boulder fields and scree on the way up.[^5] The writer also noted frequent defacement.[^5]

None of the sources read says the cab is open to visitors, and the building door was locked at the September 2021 visit. Treat the tower as one to look at from the outside rather than one to climb.
