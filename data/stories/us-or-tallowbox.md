Tallowbox is a peak in Jackson County, about 16 miles southwest of Medford.[^3] The first lookout there was a camp set up in 1918.[^2][^5]

A D-6 cupola house went up in 1923, and a 1933 newspaper report says the season's first lookout went on duty on June 14.[^1][^5] In World War II the garage became an Aircraft Warning Service winter residence, and the site was staffed year-round.[^1]

A new lookout went up in 1963, and the district's annual report, quoted on an Oregon lookouts page, says the old one was removed.[^5] The NHLR describes the replacement as an Oregon Department of Forestry flat-top cab with a catwalk, on a timber tower.[^1] The sources give the tower's height as 10 feet in one place and 15 feet in another.[^1][^2] One register page says the lookout was staffed into the 2000s.[^2]

In December 2006 a fire burned part of a radio building at the foot of the tower.[^5] The lookout itself did not burn, but its support beams were charred.[^5]

Vandalism was a recurring problem.[^5] A September 1990 approval letter put a gate across the access road to prevent a repeat of recent theft and vandalism. The August request for it noted that a regional communications agency needed only a key or lock to get through.[^5] In 2007 the Oregon chapter surveyed the lookout to assess fire damage from vandals, and planned a new steel tower for spring 2008.[^4]

Sources differ on when the cab was lost.[^2][^3] The firelookout.com page says an arsonist damaged the structure and that it was destroyed in 2007, and the register page says it was removed that year.[^3][^2] An Oregon lookouts page says the structure was torn down in 2008, with the debris burned.[^5]

A steel tower was flown in by helicopter in 2009 as a base for a camera detection system, according to the Oregon lookouts page.[^5] The Oregon lookouts page is the only source here on the gate, and no current source says whether it is still in use or whether the public may visit.[^5] The camera tower is not a lookout to climb.
