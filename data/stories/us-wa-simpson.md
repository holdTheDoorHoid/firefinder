Simpson Lookout stood about 11 miles northwest of Shelton, in Mason County, Washington.[^1] The register lists it as administered by the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR.[^2] The tower was built partway down the southern slope of Simpson Peak, and a 2022 hike write-up places the site in the Green Diamond tree farm.[^3]

An early picture shows a crude structure on the site before 1936, but its start date is unknown.[^4] In 1936 the state built a 40-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab.[^4][^2][^1]

In 1944 the Aircraft Warning Service used the site as station Charlie 2-1, run jointly by the Simpson Logging Company and the state.[^4][^5] When that service ended, the state kept the site as a fire detection point.[^5]

A 1956 Daily Chronicle item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, announced a new South Mountain lookout to replace the Simpson lookout, and said Mrs. Roe Franklin would move from Simpson to the new station.[^5] Willhite names her as the lookout at that time.[^4]

The sources disagree on the elevation. The 1956 item gives 1,000 feet, firelookout.com gives 1,200 feet, and Willhite and the 2022 write-up give 1,240 feet.[^5][^1][^4][^3] Willhite places South Mountain nearby but over 2,000 feet higher.[^4]

Firelookout.com and Willhite say the Simpson tower was destroyed in 1961, and the register says it was removed that year.[^1][^4][^2] Around the 1980s the site became a logging landing, and crushed rock now covers the site.[^4]

Willhite found little to see, and one footing, half buried and thrown over the side.[^4] A 2022 hike write-up found no recognizable artifacts, although its author did not search the salal thoroughly.[^3]

Willhite gives the hike as about 3 miles on good gravel roads.[^4] He says roads off the 800 line are usually signed with a red dot, which means no motor vehicles.[^4] The 2022 write-up says no pass or permit is required, and that non-motorized visits are allowed year-round when forestry work is not posted.[^3]
