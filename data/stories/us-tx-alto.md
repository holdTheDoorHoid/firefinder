The Alto lookout tower stands in Cherokee County, and the Texas Forest Service is named as its agency.[^1][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list shows it as a standing tower.[^2]

A June 1939 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, calls it Tower No. 10.[^1] The sources read do not say when it was built or what it was made of, so its construction date stays unknown.

A National Geodetic Survey station near the tower is stamped "ALTO 1948," but the page does not connect that date to the tower.[^1]

Three 1946 reports from the Rusk Cherokeean name Forest Patrolman N.W. Foster of the Alto lookout tower. In January, Foster reported and suppressed three of the nine forest fires in Cherokee County; two were caused by careless brush burning and one by a careless smoker. In March he handled seven of the 34 forest fires recorded in the county.[^1]

In January 1952 the Alto Herald reported that J.A. Benge, who had managed the Alto tower, was moving to Maydelle to take over the fire tower there.[^1] The sources read do not say who staffed the Alto tower after that.

The Forest Lookouts page records maintenance work in August 2018.[^1] A survey description on the same page mentions a gate in a hedge row around the tower.[^1] Nothing read says whether the public may visit the grounds or climb the tower today, so check with the Texas Forest Service before planning a trip.
