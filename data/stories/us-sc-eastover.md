The Eastover Tower was built around 1936 as a 120-foot Aermotor LS-40 steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2] The Register says the South Carolina Forestry Commission named it Eastover Tower, and the Forest Lookouts site lists the Commission as the agency for the Richland County site.[^1][^2]

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched. The Commission ended its fire tower network on October 1, 1993.[^4]

The Register says Dr. Ken Huggins bought the tower in 1995 and moved it to family property. The Forest Lookouts site also says it was sold and moved to private property that year.[^1][^2] Huggins sold it to Ray Littlejohn in 2007, and the Register notes that it still had not been moved.[^1]

Today the Register lists Ray Littlejohn as the owner. A June 2018 Columbia Metropolitan Magazine feature describes a visit to the tower on Huggins's property, rising about 120 feet from the forest floor, about fifteen years earlier.[^3] Taken together, these accounts suggest the tower may still be on the family property, but we found no current report that confirms where it stands. We found no public access, so please ask the owner before going near it.
