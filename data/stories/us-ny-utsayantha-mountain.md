Mount Utsayantha, east-southeast of Stamford in Delaware County, New York, has carried towers on its summit since the 1880s.[^1][^3] A Stamford citizen bought 20 acres on the top in 1889, built a four-story observation house, and deeded the land to the town as a park, and the Register says that house still stands.[^2]

The first tower on the summit was a tourist attraction, built in 1882 according to a 2026 FFLA newsletter. Strong winds blew it down several times, in 1892, 1895, 1901 and 1916.[^3]

The steel fire tower that still stands was built in 1934.[^1][^2][^3] The Register says the state Conservation Department built it, and the FFLA and Wikipedia credit Civilian Conservation Corps crews.[^2][^3][^1] An August 1933 Catskill Mountain News report had said construction would be finished that September, so the date is not fully settled, although the Register, the FFLA and Wikipedia all give 1934.[^4]

The FFLA says the tower's first year of operation, 1935, brought 450 visitors and 13 reported fires.[^3] A 1953 Oneonta Star report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said fire observers had agreed to scan the skies and report to the Albany Filter Center, and named James Davies of Stamford as being at the summit.[^4]

Fire lookout work ended at the close of the 1989 season.[^1] The FFLA says the tower was decommissioned that year and later sold to the Village of Stamford, which owns the land around it.[^3] By 2004 the village had bought the tower from the state.[^5] That year the FFLA gave a $200 restoration grant to this tower, the only tower to apply for and receive one.[^5] AmeriCorps workers from Delhi and FFLA members painted it in October.[^5] Wikipedia says restoration begun in 2004 was finished in July 2005.[^1]

Today the Register lists the tower as open to the public for climbing, and says village staff do its maintenance.[^2] The Register's October 2017 visit report says the tower is in good overall condition but needs paint and graffiti removal.[^2] The FFLA's 2026 newsletter says the tower is reached from Mountain Road in Stamford, a seasonal and narrow road, and suggests parking at the top, where the shoulder is wide, then walking to the tower.[^3] The same newsletter says the FFLA is starting a steward program at the site this year.[^3]

Conditions may have changed since the Register's 2017 visit, so check with the Village of Stamford before going up.
