The summit of Monumental Mountain, eight miles southwest of Colville in Stevens County, Washington, held a fire lookout from 1945 until 1996.[^1][^2] The first was a 60-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6-foot cab and a cabin, built in 1945.[^1] In August 1945 the local office of the state forestry division reported that the first phase of work on the road to the summit was complete.[^3] An observation cabin went up at the summit, staffed by Dan Reeder of Rice.[^3]

The Washington Department of Natural Resources replaced the pole tower with a 40-foot timber tower and a live-in cab.[^1][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites record and the National Historic Lookout Register both give 1965 for the new tower, while the Forest Lookouts page quotes a state report that gives 1964 to 1966.[^1][^4][^3]

Viola Frahm is listed as a lookout in 1957, and Margo Moulder in 1980.[^2] The Sites record says the tower was last staffed in 1980.[^1] The Lookout Register says it was staffed until the mid-1980s and then declared surplus.[^4]

The tower was taken off the summit in 1996 to clear room for a further radio tower.[^1] The Lookout Register says it was sold that year to Rod Fosback, a member of the Forest Fire Lookout Association, who relocated it to wooded land of his own.[^4] A firelookout.com page says Fosback set it up on a hillside he owned in 1998, and no source explains the gap between the two dates.[^5]

The Sites record does not describe what stands on the summit today, so the summit's condition and access are not known.[^1] The top of the tower does survive, on private land.[^1][^4] Fosback rebuilt the top 10 feet, cab included, on his property east of Colville.[^1] The Lookout Register gives the surviving cab as 14 by 14 feet with a catwalk, on a 10-foot wood tower on private land overlooking the Little Pend Oreille River.[^4] Treat it as private property and ask the owners before going near it.
