Dogtown was a steel lookout tower on McGarr Ridge, in the south part of the White River Ranger District in Ozark County, Missouri.[^1] The page places it in the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1]

Work was under way by December 1936. A new 80-foot steel tower was going up about a mile southwest of the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) camp at Hammond Mill, and the local paper said it would help spot and locate fires.[^1] The same report counted Dogtown as one of four towers on the Spring Creek and White River ranger districts.[^1] The completed towers in that group had living quarters and a garage on the ground, built for a full-time lookout guard.[^1]

By February 1937 the tower was finished and was being manned by the CCC crew from Hammond Mill. A temporary telephone line was in use while the permanent line was under construction.[^1] The paper expected the tower would make fires in that part of the district easier to detect and would let crews be dispatched to them.[^1]

In late February 1937, a three-day fire training school at Camp F-6 was attended by local men employed to man the Siloam Springs and Dogtown lookout towers.[^1]

No source describes what remains at the Dogtown site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1]
