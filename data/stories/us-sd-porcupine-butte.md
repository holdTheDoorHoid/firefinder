Porcupine Butte rises on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation, about five miles south of Porcupine.[^1] The name comes from the prickly pines that grow on the butte's crest.[^2] A fire watchtower stands at the top, and KILI Radio, 90.1 FM, keeps broadcast facilities there.[^2]

The lookout is a steel tower, and a South Dakota list on firelookout.org also marks it as standing.[^1][^3] I could not find when it was built, who put it up, or who has staffed it, so its early history is unknown.

The sources disagree on two details. The directory gives the elevation as 3,691 feet and places the butte in Shannon County.[^1] Wikipedia gives 3,665 feet and places the butte in Oglala Lakota County.[^2]

Near the butte, on 28 December 1890, the U.S. 7th Cavalry intercepted a band of Miniconjou and Lakota people. The Wounded Knee massacre followed the next day.[^2] I found no link between that event and the tower.

No visiting rules turned up, so ask the tribe before going up; the butte lies on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation.[^1] I found no recent report on the tower's condition.
