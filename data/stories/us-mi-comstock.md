The Comstock state fire tower stood near Twin Lakes, close to Lewiston in Montmorency County, nearly on the Oscoda County border.[^1] It was named for C. W. Comstock of Lewiston.[^1]

By 1935 a newspaper was describing a 100-foot structure there.[^1] A 2008 Flickr upload of a postcard photo captions a tower "built with inside stairs in 1939," so the sources do not agree on when the tower went up.[^2]

Towerman John Ackles worked the cabin at the top in 1935.[^1] In 1937 Leuen Eagling was the towerman, and by early July he reported that 341 people had climbed the tower.[^1] In the late 1950s H. Robert Baker manned it for about five years, his son recalled in a 2017 email.[^1] The son also says his mother, Mildred Baker, was the last person to man it, for about two weeks in the late 1960s.[^1]

The family account says the state shut the tower down in the mid-1960s, when aircraft were doing more of the fire spotting.[^1] In a dry summer in the late 1960s the state reopened it, at least temporarily.[^1] Afterward the state decided it should be dismantled, partly because the state could be held liable if people were hurt climbing it.[^1]

A Pinterest pin of the same photo says that by the 1970s the tower was gone, and that it had burned down.[^3] The family account says it was taken down, so the two accounts disagree.[^1]

The son's 2017 email says only concrete footings where the legs stood remain, and attaches an April 2015 photo.[^1] No source I read says whether those foundations are on public land or how to reach them. A map database still lists a "Comstock Fire Tower" point, but no source I read confirms that it marks the exact site.[^4]
