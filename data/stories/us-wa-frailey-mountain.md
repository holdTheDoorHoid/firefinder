Frailey Mountain rises about 10 miles northeast of Arlington, in Skagit County.[^1][^2] In a 1946 annual report, the state Forestry Division described a 40-foot tower that was framed in its shop and then erected on the mountain.[^3][^4] The tower was made of treated timber and had an L-4 cab.[^1][^2]

A June 1960 item from the Arlington Times, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page, names Helen Fosnaugh as staffing a tower on "Mt. Fairley" for the U.S. Forest Service.[^3] That disagrees with the state credit given elsewhere, and the item spells the peak differently, so the sources do not settle who staffed this tower.[^1][^3]

Willhite says Frailey came close to being an AWS post, but road trouble and the war's end meant it was never needed.[^4] It was taken down in 1970.[^1][^2][^4]

Today the lookout site is occupied by a communication tower inside a fence.[^4] Willhite looked for the old tower and found no sign of it.[^4] A visitor's blog from January 2026 describes a road walk from Deer Creek Road at Lake Cavanaugh that includes a brief climb to the former lookout site, with views that include the North Fork Stillaguamish river valley.[^5] Willhite lists DNR as the landowner along the whole route. He describes a gate with room to park on either side of the road, then a walk or bike ride of 2.7 miles to the road's end and an ATV track up through a clear-cut.[^4] No source we read says whether the public may enter the fenced compound on the summit.[^4]
