Copper Peak, also called Chippewa Hill, lies a few miles from Ironwood in Gogebic County.[^1] By August 1927 a steel fire tower 80 feet high stood on its summit, with a small house on top where a lone sentinel kept watch through the daylight hours.[^1]

Joseph Bergeron was the towerman in 1928. That May he caught the first sign of a blaze near the Triplett farm in Ironwood township.[^1] In the same month a fire in Erwin township, in the district he watched, had burned about 10 acres of slashing.[^1] His 1934 obituary says he had served the state conservation department as towerman at Chippewa Hill for the past decade.[^1]

In 1938 Camp Norrie enrollees finished a 5.5-mile telephone line from the tower to the county park at Black River.[^1] In June 1943 a newspaper named Frank Cvengros as towerman and described a porcupine that climbed the 80-foot tower after him every day.[^1]

A ski-flying hill went up on the summit in 1969 and opened a year later.[^2] The state designated the hill a Michigan State Historic Site in 1971, and the National Register listed it in 1973.[^2]

Visitors reach the landing by chairlift, then take an elevator 18 stories up the ski tower.[^4] Wikipedia says the hill is open on Sundays in summer and fall so visitors can watch the construction.[^2] A 1974 state marker on the hill notes the 1845 copper tunnel, which is still visible.[^3] Local press calls the ski tower "the lookout from Copper Peak," but the ski tower has 18 stories, while the 1927 fire tower was 80 feet high.[^5][^4][^1] No source I read describes what, if anything, remains of the fire tower on the summit.
