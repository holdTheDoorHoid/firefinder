The Fire Lookout Museum Lookout stands in the backyard of Ray and Rita Kresek's home, about seven miles north of downtown Spokane, Washington.[^1][^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 328 and WA 34, and registered it on November 20, 1999.[^1]

The register gives the cab's size as 8 by 8 feet, a wood USFS design, and says more than 200 of those cabs were once in place across the Pacific Northwest.[^1] The museum's own page says its cab is one of only two surviving L-6 towers from the 1930s, restored from what remained of the last of 200.[^2] The register tells a different story. It says the cab is assembled from parts taken from seven L-6 lookouts that were torn down, and that only three of the original cabs still stand where they were first put up.[^1] These sources do not agree on the count or the origin, so we report both. We found no source that dates when the cab was put together.

We found no source saying the cab was ever staffed as a working lookout. The 2025 Spokesman-Review profile of Ray Kresek says he got his start at 16 as a DNR fire lookout in the 1950s.[^3] The museum page also says the cab won the highest awards at the 1984 Interstate Fair in Spokane.[^2]

The restored structure sits on a six-foot wood tower.[^1] Beside it stands a 60-foot replica of a 1929 spar-tree lookout, according to the museum page.[^2] A Forest Fire Lookout Association list labels the museum site a replica, but the register describes a restored structure.[^4][^1]

The museum is open to the public by appointment from March through November, and admission is free.[^2] The register says visits are by appointment with Ray Kresek.[^1] A June 2025 profile says visits from people interested in firefighting have tapered off.[^3] Because the cab sits in a private yard, call ahead. The sources do not say whether the cab can be climbed, so please do not climb it.
