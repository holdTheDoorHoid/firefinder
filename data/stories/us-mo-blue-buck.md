The Blue Buck Fire Tower stands in Douglas County, Missouri, inside the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1935, and an online trail write-up names the crew as Camp Willow, Company 1739.[^1][^2]

It is a 110-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register accepted the tower on March 29, 2010, as US 878, MO 2. It lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator.[^1]

The 2010 entry says the tower is "no longer used," though it also calls the tower a familiar landmark.[^1] The sources do not say when the lookout watch ended, and none of them name a fire the tower helped catch.

The 2010 entry says the tower is in need of restoration.[^1] A visit in February 2026 found that the cab work and stair repairs noted in 2010 had been completed. onX reports that the cab was restored with new flooring, and that the bottom flight of stairs was being re-constructed at the time.[^2] The two accounts are sixteen years apart, so the newer one is the better guide to the tower's condition now.[^1][^2]

The Forest Service road up to the tower, FS 121, has a closed gate about halfway up. Past that gate, onX says visitors can continue on foot uphill along the road.[^2] The roads in the tower area are for day use only.[^2] The sources do not say whether the tower itself may be climbed, so look at it from the ground unless the Forest Service says otherwise.[^2]
