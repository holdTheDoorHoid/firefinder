Fort Noble Mountain sits in the Adirondacks, on the boundary between the towns of Ohio (Herkimer County) and Morehouse (Hamilton County).[^1]

The Forest, Fish and Game Commission put up a 30-foot wooden lookout on the mountain in July 1910.[^1] The Eastern lookouts site gives its cost as $300.56.[^2] In 1916 the Conservation Commission put a 50-foot steel Aermotor LL25 tower in its place.[^1]

The sources give two accounts of the stairs. Wikipedia says wooden steps were added inside the tower in 1918 or 1919.[^1] The Eastern site dates to 1930 a switch from the original outside ladder to a self-supporting stairway.[^2] These may be two different changes, so we report both.

A March 1945 newspaper item, quoted by the Eastern site, names Arch Blue as a fire observer on the mountain for the Conservation Department.[^2]

Fire lookout work ended in 1978.[^1] The tower was later taken down, because it was no longer needed for fire detection and had been classed as a "non-conforming" structure in the Fort Noble Primitive Area.[^1] Wikipedia says that taking it down made it possible to reclassify the land as Wilderness.[^1] None of the sources we read gives a removal date. CNY Hiking lists the tower as removed,[^3] and the FFLA list marks it as "Tower Gone."[^4]

The tower is gone.[^4] We found no source that describes the site today, including whether any of the footings remain or whether a trail reaches the summit.
