Table Hill had a lookout cab on the end of a southwest spur, and a listing dates it to 1937.[^1] That cab was an L-4.[^1] The Forest Service describes the L-4 as a 14-by-14-foot pre-cut house, produced between 1929 and 1953.[^4] By 1939 the Flathead National Forest had 147 lookout buildings, along with 247 other buildings.[^3]

The site lies 18 miles east-southeast of the Spotted Bear Ranger Station.[^1] A historical overview of the Flathead National Forest describes that station as being in the South Fork, reachable by road.[^3]

The listing says the cab was abandoned and is now gone.[^1] The association's Montana list shows the site as a ground lookout that is gone.[^2] Writing about the Bob Marshall Wilderness, the same overview says the extensive fire lookout network there has been almost completely dismantled.[^3]

No source describes the Table Hill site today or says whether the public can reach it.
