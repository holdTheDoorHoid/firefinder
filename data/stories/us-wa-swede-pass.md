The Swede Pass lookout stood on a ridge in Stevens County, in northeast Washington, above the Columbia River valley.[^1][^4] It was an L-4 ground house on a concrete foundation, with a patrol point at Lookout Mountain about a mile east.[^1][^4]

Work started in 1933.[^4] A newspaper notice from May 1934 says Willis Ward of Orient had just finished the lookout house.[^3] FFLOS says it was built in 1933, while Firelookout.com dates the L-4 cab to 1934, so the sources differ by a year.[^1][^2]

Few lookout names survive. An August 1941 newspaper item says Fred Garringer was stationed on Lookout Mountain, but WillhiteWeb notes it is unclear which Lookout Mountain was meant.[^3][^4] The later names are Robert Wallace (1948), William Zimmer (1950) and Wayne Francis (1951).[^4] Firelookout.com also lists Evelyn Johnson for 1945.[^2]

During the Second World War the site served the Aircraft Warning Service.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb gives a start date of March 7, 1942, and Forest Lookouts gives March 27. Both say the service ended on July 1, 1943.[^4][^3]

Two lookouts at Swede Pass reported a fire roughly 12 miles north on July 25, 1945.[^1] FFLOS says Forest Service investigators concluded that a Japanese balloon bomb started it.[^1] WillhiteWeb repeats that account, but we found no second source for it.[^4]

The lookout was removed in 1952.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed in the 1950s.[^2] The sources also differ on who ran it. FFLOS names the U.S. Forest Service, while Firelookout.com credits the Washington Division of Forestry.[^1][^2]

Today the route starts on dirt roads that WillhiteWeb says cars can use in dry weather. The last turn runs down a drainage, and the hike is about half a mile one way.[^4] WillhiteWeb's photo captions include "Bed springs" and "Cap of cab," but the page does not say where they lie.[^4] We found no source describing what remains at the site today, who owns the land, or whether the public may visit.
