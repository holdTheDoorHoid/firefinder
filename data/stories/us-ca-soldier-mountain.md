Soldier Mountain sits in Shasta County, California, near the eastern edge of the Shasta-Trinity National Forest.[^1] The register puts the lookout at 5,454 feet.[^1] Wikipedia gives the mountain itself as higher, at 5,531 feet.[^1][^2]

Someone was stationed on Soldier Mountain by 1920, when a district newsletter mentioned a lookout man there.[^3] In spring 1934 a Civilian Conservation Corps camp constructed the Soldier Mountain Road and a fire lookout station on the peak.[^4] Newspapers from the 1930s mention the station on the peak, and a 1936 item says a man had summer work there with the Forest Service.[^3]

In October 1947 a local paper reported that weather had led to two lookouts, Soldier Mountain and Bunch Grass, being discontinued.[^3] The reports I found do not say whether that closure was permanent.

The tower standing today replaced a CCC-era lookout in 1974 and 1975.[^1] Its frame is braced with angle iron and steel rods, and the register counts only five lookouts based on this plan.[^1] Steel-column towers like it began with Fredonyer Peak.[^1] A 1977 survey description gives the lookout building a height of about 20 feet, from its concrete foundation to the top of the roof.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register added the lookout on October 26, 2019, as US 1348, CA 171.[^1]

Inside, the register says the walls are unfinished and the framing shows.[^1] The Cal Fire Shasta-Trinity Unit administers it, and the register says the site is open to visitors and about ten miles from a paved road.[^1] Wikipedia describes the tower as reachable by dirt road.[^2] I found no recent staffing or rental information, so check with Cal Fire or the Forest Service before you go.
