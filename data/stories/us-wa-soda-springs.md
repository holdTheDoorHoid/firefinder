The Soda Springs lookout stood on a knob in Chelan County, Washington, in the Wenatchee National Forest.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com puts the site seven miles west of Lake Wenatchee.[^2] A 2009 trip report calls Soda Springs a canyon campground, a few miles upstream of Lake Wenatchee on the Little Wenatchee River.[^5]

The register dates the tower to the 1930s and calls it an L-4 tower.[^1] Panorama photos were taken there on August 24, 1934.[^4] Willhite notes that the 1934 panoramas were shot from 45 feet, which suggests a tower of about 30 feet, and he reports an L-4 cab on it.[^3]

Elevation estimates differ. The register gives 2,500 feet, Willhite gives 2,440 feet, and the trip report gives 2,451 feet.[^1][^3][^5]

The register says the tower is now gone, and Firelookout.com lists it as gone.[^1][^2] Washington Lookouts also lists it as removed.[^4] In 2009 a trip report found four concrete foundation blocks on a rocky knoll.[^5] Willhite's photos, some labeled September 2017, show footings spaced far apart, with all four visible in one view.[^3]

Willhite suggests parking by the campground's entrance road, then walking through open woods around the knob's base.[^3] The hike is about a quarter mile with 150 feet of gain.[^3] Roughly midway, signs mark where National Forest land meets private land.[^3] The footings are at the highest point of the knob.[^3] He calls the access easy, though the route is unmarked on the ground, and he warns against continuing south, where a large rock quarry lies.[^3] No source we read says whether the top is open to the public.
