The Umpqua National Forest put a lookout on Abbott Butte, in Douglas County, Oregon, about 20 miles west of Crater Lake.[^1][^2] The 1928 lookout was a D-6 cupola-style house on a 15-foot tower.[^1][^2]

Sources differ on when the present tower went up. Forest Lookouts gives 1938 for a 20-foot sawn-fir timber tower with an L-4 cab, citing an undated report.[^1] The other site dates the L-4 tower to 1939.[^2]

An August 1927 newspaper report named George Barrows as the lookout, and said he helped a packer carry the lookout house materials from Woodruff Meadows in four days.[^1] An August 1940 Roseburg paper said that Mr. and Mrs. Delbert Poole were spending the summer at Abbott Butte, and that Mr. Poole was the Forest Service lookout there.[^1]

In 1984 the area became part of the Rogue-Umpqua Divide Wilderness, and all vehicle access was closed.[^1][^3]

The cabin was still standing in 2015.[^3] Sometime after that it collapsed, although the tower stayed up.[^3] The cab later caved in, the tower came down soon after, and the remains were reportedly burned.[^2] The post dates the rest of the lookout's collapse to some point in the winter of 2021 to 2022, though it says the exact time is unclear.[^3] A November 2022 post reported, through a geocache owner, that the tower had finally collapsed.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list marks it as gone.[^4]

A visitor who hiked up in 2023 found the collapsed tower still there.[^3] That visitor later learned the Forest Service had burned the remains, probably out of liability concerns.[^3]

According to these reports, no tower remains on the summit.[^2][^3] Vehicle access was closed when the area became wilderness in 1984, so the site is reached on foot.[^1][^3] A 2023 hiker found that the trail did not seem to be maintained and was overgrown.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit now.
