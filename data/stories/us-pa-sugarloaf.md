The Sugarloaf fire tower stood on Sugar Loaf mountain in Fayette County, Pennsylvania, in what is now Ohiopyle State Park.[^1][^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register calls it a steel Aermotor tower built in 1921, and gives its elevation as 2,923 feet (891 meters).[^1] A 1921 newspaper item, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, says the new tower overlooks nearly three-quarters of Fayette County's forestry area.[^2]

A watchman worked around the clock, lodging in a cabin at the base, and a four-mile telephone line ran out to the tower.[^2] In late October 1921 the watchman saw smoke from a brush-heap fire about ten miles off, raised the alarm with the wardens, and they pinpointed it fast.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts list gives the tower's removal as 1979.[^2] The register says it is no longer standing.[^1] Two online items still use the name: a map of Ohiopyle lists a Sugarloaf Fire Tower as a landmark, and a video is titled Exploring Historic Abandoned Sugarloaf Fire tower.[^4][^5] We could not confirm from either that a structure stands, so the removal record remains the working answer.

Sugarloaf Knob, a well-known summit in Ohiopyle State Park at the southern end of Laurel Ridge, can be approached by a park road that ends just short of the top, then a brief off-trail climb through brush.[^3] No source we read describes what remains at the tower site today, or whether it sits on that summit.
