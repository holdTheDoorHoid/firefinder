Sugarloaf Mountain sits in the Nash Stream State Forest in Coos County.[^1][^4] The summit is open and rocky.[^5]

In 1910 a lookout station was established on Sugarloaf, according to the Forest Lookouts page, which cites a Report of the Forestry Commissioner for that year.[^1] A White Mountain history page credits the New Hampshire Timberland Owners Association with building several lookouts, Sugarloaf among them, which overlook the Nash Stream watershed.[^2] A New Hampshire Family Hikes history lists Sugarloaf among seven lookouts from 1910 that the association paid for.[^3]

One hike report gives 1911 instead.[^4] It calls the structure a cabin built on the bare rock summit, established by the Timberland Owners on Groveton Paper Co. land.[^4] The final cabin went up in the mid-1960s, was closed in 1980, and burned in 1982.[^4]

Reaching the former tower site means following Emerson Road from Route 110 for 2.2 miles to the gravel Nash Stream Road, then climbing an old road that stays steep for nearly all of its 2.1 miles.[^4] A hike report says the only signs that a tower once stood there are blots in the ledges, a USGS benchmark and part of an old cast iron stove.[^4] A listing site, however, says remnants of the burned lookout are still visible near the upper trail.[^5] The New Hampshire lookout list marks the site as gone.[^6]
