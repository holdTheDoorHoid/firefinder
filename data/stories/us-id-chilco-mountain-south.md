The south summit of Chilco Mountain, known as South Chilco, held a log and frame fire lookout cabin built in 1915.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page names Mr. Elton Bently as staff in 1929, citing a book on the North Fork of the Coeur d'Alene.[^2] A July 1922 Spokesman-Review item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, places a ranger named Elwyn Webster at the Chilco lookout station without saying which summit it meant.[^3]

The cabin fell out of use in 1939. Two sources tie that year to a tower on North Chilco about two miles northeast, but the 1996 guide gives 1948 for that tower.[^1][^2][^3][^4] None of the sources we found says when the cabin was destroyed or removed.[^1][^2]

A 1996 hike guide describes a spur off Trail 14 reaching the middle summit, at 5,634 feet, which it calls a former Forest Service lookout site.[^4] Forest Road 406 leads nearly a mile to the trailhead, and the route is open to hikers, mountain bikers, horses and motorcycles.[^4] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page files the site under hike-in and destroyed.[^2] The peak lies in Coeur d'Alene National Forest.[^1] No source we found describes what remains on the site now.
