The Mistinikon Lake Fire Tower is in the Mistinikon Lake Uplands Conservation Reserve, a reserve of about 4,330 hectares of Crown land roughly 10 km west of Matachewan, Ontario.[^1][^2]

Lookout towers began going up in Ontario in the 1920s, and by 1962 the province had 316 steel towers in service.[^3] Ontario's fire towers came under the Department of Lands and Forests, which became the Ministry of Natural Resources in 1972.[^3]

A Statement of Conservation Interest for the reserve, dated November 2004, describes an abandoned fire tower at the southeast corner of the site, just west of Mistinikon Lake.[^2] The report calls the tower old and unmaintained, and it advises against climbing it.[^2]

The two sources describe different ways in. The register describes a route by canoe or boat from the Mistinkon River, past Bell Island, with a 3 km (1.9 mi) return trail and a 110 m (360 ft) elevation gain.[^1] The 2004 report says the reserve can be reached by boat along Mistinikon Lake, and it describes a trail of about 1.5 km off the west shore of the lake, with steep sections as it nears the tower.[^2]

The report describes the view from the foot of the tower as good.[^2] No source found describes what remains at the site today, and the most recent account is the 2004 report.[^2]
