The first lookout on Lookout Butte, on the Nez Perce National Forest, was a simple 45-foot pole tower put up in 1923, with a cab added two years later.[^1] That gave way in 1935 to a 50-foot pole tower topped with an L-4 cab, the standard wood-frame design the Forest Service used across the region in that era.[^1] The structure on the summit today dates to 1962: a flat-roofed R-6 cab, 15 feet square and ringed by a catwalk, mounted atop a 55-foot timber tower that replaced the aging L-4.[^1]

Idaho Fire Lookouts, which tracks staffing histories for the region's towers, says it has not yet documented who staffed Lookout Butte over the years.[^2] That gap doesn't mean the tower went unused — only that the record hasn't surfaced yet.

The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2009.[^3] It still stands on the Moose Creek Ranger District, and rather than being abandoned or torn down when fire detection shifted to aircraft and cameras, it found a second life: the Forest Service now rents it out through the recreation rental program, letting visitors stay overnight in the cab.[^1][^4]

Access today is by whatever road or trail serves the Moose Creek Ranger District; anyone wanting to visit should book through the recreation rental system rather than show up unannounced, since it is an active rental cabin, not open to climb at will.
