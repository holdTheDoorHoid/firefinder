Snow Peak Lookout stands in Shoshone County, Idaho, on one of the most distinct peaks in St. Joe country.[^1][^2] The register lists its elevation at 6,749 feet, while Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 6,760 feet.[^3][^4]

The site was first developed in 1929 with a cupola cabin.[^1][^4] The lookout that stands there now is newer. It has an R-6 flat cab on a 10-foot concrete base, and it dates to 1963.[^1][^3][^4] The register gives 1963 as the build year.[^3] The other two pages also mention the 1929 cabin, so the dates describe two stages of one site rather than conflicting accounts of a single building.[^1][^4]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists John M. Johnson as known staff in 1950, citing a former lookout registry, and Kjell Peterson for 1968.[^4] Both pages name William Lassiter for 1960.[^1][^4] The firelookout.com page also notes a patrol lookout one mile west, atop Spotted Louis Peak.[^1]

Idaho Fish & Game uses the cab to watch a local mountain goat herd and to house communications equipment.[^1][^4] When Idaho Fire Lookouts hiked up in August 2024, they found the lookout locked up and the St. Joe National Forest refurbishing it for a future rental program.[^4]

The Forest Service says the lookout cannot currently be rented or reserved.[^2] It also calls the trip a day hike, with the trails managed jointly with Fish and Game.[^2] One 2024 hiker estimated the round trip at about 9 miles.[^4]
