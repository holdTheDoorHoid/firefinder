Pinto is one of Alberta's steel fire lookouts, and its record still marks it as active.[^1] The record comes from the province's Wildfire Lookouts layer, which Alberta derives from its FIReS database.[^4] An Alberta open-data map places 127 wildfire lookout sites in the boreal forest and along the Rockies.[^2] The province says lookouts are sited where sightlines are good, which helps in detecting and reporting wildfires.[^2]

The record gives 2019 as the year the present tower was built.[^1] It is 30 m (98 ft) tall and sits at an elevation of 1,044.0 m (3,425 ft).[^1] The record codes its access mode as drive and lists a weather station at the site.[^1]

Alberta.ca says Agriculture and Forestry wildfire staff have put Alberta BearSmart recommendations into practice at many of their sites.[^3] It adds that each site is staffed by a lookout observer who watches for wildfire throughout the day, usually from April into October.[^3]

The lookout is still listed as active, so it remains a working post.[^1] No source we read describes public access to the tower.
