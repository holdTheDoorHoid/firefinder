Muscatatuck began as a state park. It opened in 1921 as Indiana's fourth state park and was renamed Muscatatuck State Park in 1922.[^1] The park's historical marker says the Works Progress Administration and the Civilian Conservation Corps built its stone shelters, stairways, retaining walls, trails and a fire tower during the 1930s.[^1]

Two newspaper items from the period describe a plan, then the work. A 1935 item said a fire tower would be erected in Jennings County by the State Department of Conservation.[^2] An April 1938 item reported two enrollees on detached service helping erect a fire tower at the park.[^2]

The Indiana Fire Towers site reads a 1936 county map, which shows the park but no tower, as a sign that the tower did not yet stand that year.[^3] The Forest Service list still marks the tower's location as unresolved, with a question mark.[^5]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone,[^4] and the Indiana Fire Towers site lists it as not standing.[^3]

The park is now Muscatatuck County Park, and Jennings County took it back for recreation in 1968.[^1][^3] No source describes what remains at the tower site today.
