The Clam Lake fire tower stands in Sawyer County, Wisconsin.[^1] Its listed agency is the Conservation Department's District 7.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^2]

A newspaper report from May 1932 counted Clam Lake among the towers already completed in District 7.[^1] The Wisconsin Architecture and History Inventory gives 1932 as the year it was built, and says it has not been demolished.[^3]

The local Clam Lake history site presents a September 1933 newspaper item as a record of the tower being built by a Civilian Conservation Corps camp.[^4] That item says the foresters were overseeing work on an 80-foot fire tower, and credits Company 1604 at Camp Beaver.[^1] It does not name the site, and it does not fit well with the 1932 completion report, so we cannot be sure it describes this tower.[^1][^3]

Company 1604 was formed at Fort Sheridan, Illinois, in May 1933, and moved that summer to Camp Beaver in Clam Lake.[^5]

The local site recalls many stories from people who spent time manning the tower, but we found no list of who staffed it or when.[^4] It gives the tower's elevation as 1,522 feet and notes a view of over a dozen lakes and the West Fork of the Chippewa River.[^4] We found no fire history for the tower in the sources we read.

A 2016 post on the local site says the tower could still be seen across Lower Clam, and the association's list still shows it as standing.[^4][^2] We found no recent visit report and no public-access information. Please ask the landowner or the managing agency before going up, and do not climb it unless access has been confirmed.

WJFW reports that 72 towers still operating were decommissioned in 2015. Our sources do not say whether Clam Lake was one of them.[^6]
