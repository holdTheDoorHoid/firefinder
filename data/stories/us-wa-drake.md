Drake Lookout, also called Reed Hill, stood in Grays Harbor County, Washington, at about 1,443 feet.[^1][^4] The register describes a live-in tower built in the 1940s.[^1] A lookout history site lists the station as activated on August 9, 1942, at the Olympia Filter Center.[^3]

The Simpson Logging Company ran the site as a private station for detecting fires.[^3] A May 1944 report gave it the name Baker 9-1.[^3]

A September 1951 newspaper item said the Drake lookout was closed when the person staffing it left for college.[^3] In 1952 Drake closed on September 8, along with two other lookouts, because ocean fog hangs over the area at that time of year and makes them pointless.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1963.[^1] Firelookout.com and the trip report both say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^4] The trip report also says aerial photos from the 1950s show the structure.[^4]

A National Geodetic Survey station description on the same page records that the tower has been removed.[^3]

Logging has reached the summit more than once since the tower came down.[^4] A 2016 harvest left the entire summit open.[^4] Trees have been planted, and the trip report expects them to block the view of the top in about eight years.[^4] Another searcher turned up a footing and metal bars under debris, and a later update places concrete and metal about 20 feet southwest of a large stump.[^4]

The road is gated where it meets the paved Wynoochee Valley Road, about 20 miles from Montesano.[^3][^4] Firelookout.com puts the distance at 23 miles instead.[^2] The trip report describes about 6 miles and 1,000 feet of climb, and recommends biking up.[^4] Part of the south slopes are private land.[^4]
