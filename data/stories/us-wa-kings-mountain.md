Kings Mountain sits about six miles northeast of Usk in Pend Oreille County, Washington, and the U.S. Forest Service administered its lookout.[^1][^2] The site lies within the Kaniksu and Colville national forests.[^1] The tower was built in 1936 as a 50-foot pole structure with an L-4 cab.[^1][^4]

Few lookouts are named in the sources. A 1943 Colville Examiner item, quoted by two of the sites, says Mrs. Earl Hupp was stationed there.[^3][^4] Willhite's photo captions spell the name Hump, so the spelling is unsettled.[^4] Firelookout.com lists Nadine Barrett for 1951 and 1952, and Thomas Jenkins for 1956.[^2]

An October 1956 inspection found rot in two legs and in the second and third bents. A January 1957 estimate put the repairs at $687.50.[^4]

Willhite reports that Disney sought a tower to burn for a film, and that Kings Mountain was offered because of its poor condition.[^4] The burn appears in "A Fire Called Jeremiah," released in 1962, and the footage was used again in "Fire On Kelly Mountain" in 1973.[^4]

Sources disagree on the year the tower burned. Firelookout.com gives 1960, while Firetower.org and the Forest Lookouts site give 1965.[^1][^2][^3] Willhite's local source said Disney was required to replace the tower with a new one, and that the new tower also burned. Willhite thinks that second fire would likely be the 1965 date.[^4]

The tower is gone.[^2] Willhite's 2019 photos show a lookout footing and a stairway he labels "Stairs to nowhere," and his captions note that more views are obstructed.[^4]

Willhite gives directions from Usk up Kings Lake Road and says most cars should be able to make the climb.[^4] He labels the site drive-up. We found no source that says the road is closed.
