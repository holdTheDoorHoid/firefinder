Panther Fire Tower stands in McDowell County, West Virginia, inside Panther State Forest.[^1] The West Virginia Division of Forestry built the 41-foot tower in 1940 to oversee the forest.[^1] Its 7-by-7-foot metal cab has windows that swing open in blocks of four panes, and the Register counts it among the few distinctive Blau-Knox towers still standing.[^1]

The state forest dates from the same year.[^3] West Virginia State Parks says it was set up in 1940 after the Welch Daily News ran a donation drive that raised more than $9,000 from the community.[^3]

The tower's job has changed. The Register's 2013 visit report says forestry no longer uses the tower, and that State Parks maintains the tower and grounds and uses it as a radio repeater site.[^1] The Register still names the Division of Forestry as administrator, so its own sources disagree about who looks after the tower now.[^1]

The same report found the tower in decent condition, though the wooden steps needed replacing.[^1] Only authorized vehicles can drive up to it, and the public is discouraged from driving there.[^1] A hiking trail from the group camp reaches the tower, but the report says the trail is unmarked and not well maintained.[^1]

None of the sources we read names a watcher or a particular fire for this tower.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association list still shows the tower as standing.[^2] The newest visit report we found is from 2013, so its present condition is not known. Please leave the tower alone and ask West Virginia State Parks before any visit.
