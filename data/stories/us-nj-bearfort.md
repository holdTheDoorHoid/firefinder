Bearfort Mountain has carried a fire watch for longer than almost any other spot in New Jersey. A watcher was already stationed on a tower there in 1913, when the Weeks Law brought telephone lines and a proper watch house to the site through an agreement with the City of Newark, which owned the surrounding watershed land.[^3] Sources differ on exactly how old that first tower was: the National Historic Lookout Register traces the site back to a wooden platform around 1880 and an iron-frame tower built in 1909, while a 1923 newspaper account instead describes a forty-foot frame tower built for the Forest Fire Service in 1916.[^1][^3]

Whatever stood there before, it didn't last. A contemporary news account reports that the earlier Bearfort tower was destroyed by fire in April 1932, after stovepipe sparks ignited a hole in the lookout box's tar-paper roofing; Charles Stalter of Hewitt, the fire warden on duty that day, learned of the blaze only after he had already left the mountain.[^3] For two years afterward, observers worked from a makeshift tree platform, until the 68-foot steel tower standing today went up in 1934, a McClintock-Marshall structure that may have been supplied under an Aermotor contract.[^1] That steel tower remains the New Jersey Forest Fire Service's active lookout for the area.[^1][^2]

Bearfort was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in December 1997.[^1] Observer reports filed with the register describe an active, staffed tower, though one visit noted the cab itself needed cleaning and repainting and its instruments were in poor condition.[^1]

The tower still stands and continues in service as a working fire-watch structure for the state forest fire service, not a public rental.[^1] It sits in a well-used recreation area on the Newark watershed property, with hiking trails leading past it, but the cab is reserved for Forest Fire Service lookouts and is not open for public climbing.[^1]
