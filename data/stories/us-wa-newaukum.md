Newaukum Lookout stood on an unnamed summit high above Newaukum Lake, in Lewis County, Washington.[^1][^4] Three sources place it in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1][^2][^4] Washington Lookouts instead lists the Snoqualmie National Forest.[^3]

The cab was built in 1934 as an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] FireLookout's page calls it a cabin.[^2] Panorama photographs were taken on July 30, 1934.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites photo captions credit the 1934 panoramas to the National Archives, Pacific Northwest Region.[^1] FireLookout gives the elevation as 3,244 feet, while WillhiteWeb gives 3,791 feet and adds a note of "3,760+ today" without explaining it.[^2][^4]

Sources disagree on how the cab ended. The Former Fire Lookout Sites says it was removed in the 1960s, and FireLookout says it was destroyed then.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb repeats earlier researchers' record of destruction in the 1960s.[^4] Washington Lookouts gives only the word "Removed".[^3] The same register lists the site as US 1288 and registered it on June 18, 2012.[^1]

WillhiteWeb says a road still reaches the summit, rough and overgrown in places.[^4] The page lists the approach as gravel roads, with a rough last half mile.[^4] Spokane's Fire Lookout Museum holds a sign that came from the lookout.[^4] No source we read describes any remains of the cab itself, and none says whether the site is open to the public.
