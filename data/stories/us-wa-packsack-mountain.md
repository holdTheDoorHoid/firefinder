Packsack Mountain's lookout site lies about nine miles northeast of Raymond, Washington, according to Firelookout.com.[^2] The sources disagree on the county. The register and Firelookout.com say Grays Harbor County, while Forest Lookouts and WillhiteWeb say Pacific County.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The first lookout here dates to about 1930: a 10-foot tower with an enclosed platform.[^1][^2][^3] A 110-foot steel tower followed in 1937, with a cabin for living quarters.[^1][^3] In 1950 the Washington Division of Forestry erected a 40-foot wooden tower with living quarters, the last of the three.[^1][^2][^3]

According to WillhiteWeb, the Bailey sisters staffed the lookout from 1952 to 1955, and the page links to a video interview with them.[^3]

The final tower was given up in 1967.[^1][^2][^3] The register and Firelookout.com say it may have been taken down around then, while WillhiteWeb says a few years later.[^1][^2][^3] Forest Lookouts lists the site as removed.[^4]

A 1976 survey crew described the old tower foundation as four angle irons, each 16 inches high, set in concrete.[^3]

The sources we read describe no standing structure at the site.[^1][^3][^4] WillhiteWeb's account of a visit found the summit so overgrown that the footings could not be found, though the writer thought some must remain.[^3]

WillhiteWeb lists a Weyerhaeuser permit as required for the access route, so check that before going.[^3]
