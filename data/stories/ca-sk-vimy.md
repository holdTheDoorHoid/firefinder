The Department of Natural Resources began building fire control towers roughly 80 km (50 miles) apart across northern Saskatchewan, a spacing chosen so that tower men could reach one another by radio.[^1] Vimy was one of those towers, in the Big River district about 15 km north-east of Chitek Lake.[^1] The provincial tower list gives its position as 53-49-38.4 X 107-32-45.4, or about 53.83 degrees north and 107.55 degrees west.[^2] Canada Lookouts reports that a modern steel tower took the place of the earlier lookout in 1965, and cites the Department of Natural Resources annual report for 1965-66 as its source.[^3]

In 1997 and 1998 the Big River district grew to cover the Chitek Lake area and the Dore Lake and Green Lake areas. It went from one fire tower to seven.[^1] The towermen list on websiteservice.ca gives Ron Hook two years at Vimy Tower, and Lourie Booker from 1987 to 2002 at Vimy and Birch.[^4]

A Big River history on jkcc.com says Robert Weal was the tower man when Vimy closed in 2001, working from the Chitek Lake base on fixed-wing patrols and maintenance.[^1] The towermen list and the Big River history do not clearly agree on who held the tower at the end, so this story names each person without choosing between them.[^1][^4]

In August 2001 all fire towers in the Big River district were condemned, after safety concerns about how reliable they were. Their tower men were moved to other work, including fixed-wing patrol.[^1]

No source we found describes what remains at the Vimy site today, or whether the public can reach it.
