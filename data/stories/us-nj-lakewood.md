The Lakewood Station fire tower was an 80-foot, all-steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, put up in Ocean County, New Jersey, in 1944.[^1][^2] Five employees of Jersey Central Power & Light built it in their off-hours for $360, planning to finish in about two weeks.[^1][^2]

During the Second World War, the tower was used to monitor German U-boat communications in the Atlantic, about 12 miles to the east.[^1] In April 1953 a blaze burned a two-mile path through more than 1,000 acres of Lakewood pine country, and the tower operator raised the alarm.[^2] A property dispute forced the tower to move 300 feet in 1974, and it stayed in service.[^1][^2] The Register listed the tower on March 3, 2004, with Bob Spear as nominator.[^1]

Its service ended in early July 2024, when the New Jersey Forest Fire Service took it out of service, effective July 5 by one account.[^4] The Lakewood Scoop reported the closure on July 7 and said officials had not given a reason.[^3] According to 1st Responder News, the land under the tower had been sold and the new owner had not renewed the lease.[^4]

A September 2024 Scoop video showed crews taking the tower down after the Forest Fire Service had decommissioned it that summer.[^5] One Scoop article gives its height as 136 feet.[^3] That figure matches the Register's elevation for the site.[^1] The Register and the Eastern lookouts page both say 80 feet.[^1][^2]

A replacement New Jersey Forest Fire Service tower has been completed on East Veterans Highway in Jackson Township.[^5] Construction was underway through 2025, and the article said no opening date had been announced.[^5] The 1st Responder report had said the new tower would be called Veterans Tower and built at the Forest Resource Center in Jackson.[^4] The tower on Massachusetts Avenue was taken down in 2024, so there is nothing to visit there.[^5]
