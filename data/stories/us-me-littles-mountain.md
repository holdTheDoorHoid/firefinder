Littles Mountain sits in Washington County, Maine, and its fire tower stands in what a 1983 Bangor Daily News account calls Cobscook State Park.[^1] A travel guide says visitors can park on South Edmunds Street, outside the park, and walk a short trail to the summit.[^3]

The sources we read do not say when the first tower went up. By 1963 it was a 32-foot tower whose main problem was that it gave no clear view above the surrounding trees.[^1]

In 1963 the Forest Service raised the old tower by 28 feet, building a new structure directly on top of it.[^1] A work crew took down a radio tower from the Moosehorn Game and Wildlife Preserve and put it up over the old wooden tower.[^1] The work took about ten weeks, and the article puts the project's cost at about $500.[^1]

The tower was in use each summer until 1972.[^1] That year Guy Cook of Dennysville, who had worked there for about twenty years, retired, and pilots flying surveillance flights took over fire spotting.[^1] Cook said that on clear days he could see for 30 miles, and that he reported fires on Grand Manan and Deer Islands in New Brunswick.[^1] He named the worst local fires as one near Lubec in 1960 and another at Centerville in 1965.[^1] The article does not say the tower watched either one.

After 1972 the tower was deactivated.[^1] The article says it was then given up, and it deteriorated over the following eleven years.[^1]

In summer 1983 the Maine Forest Service restored it.[^1] Rangers built new wooden stairs, and a lookout was posted there before the repairs were finished.[^1]

Today the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, though its cab is gone.[^2] A travel guide says it is a fire tower you are not allowed to climb.[^3] We found no record of restoration or staffing after 1983, so the 1983 account is the most recent history we could confirm.
