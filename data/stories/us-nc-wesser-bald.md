The Wesser Bald lookout stands on the Appalachian Trail in the Nantahala National Forest, in Macon County.[^1][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register dates it to 1936 and describes a live-in cab with a catwalk on a metal tower.[^1] A 1934 survey note, though, places a benchmark in a new lookout tower that year.[^3] Willhite's history says the Civilian Conservation Corps built a 30-foot tower with a live-in cab on the summit, and that an older structure stood there before it.[^3] The building year is therefore not settled.

In 1948 Earl Shaffer slept in the tower during his thru-hike.[^3] Willhite calls him the trail's first thru-hiker, and says the trail gained national popularity after word of Shaffer's hike.[^3]

In 1979 the live-in cab burned.[^1][^3] The register says vandals burned it, while Willhite attributes the fire to arson.[^1][^3] According to the register, the Forest Service swapped the cab for a viewing platform in 1993.[^1] Willhite places that change in the early 1990s.[^3]

The register listed the tower in April 2008, with Peter J. Barr as its nominator.[^1] That summer, chapter member Charles Nuckolls repaired the tower, swapping out its wooden stairs, railings and deck boards.[^2]

In November 2016 wildfires tore through the Nantahala Forest.[^4] Wikipedia says the tower was first reported destroyed, but it was later found to have survived.[^4]

Willhite describes an observation deck for hikers, and notes that the Appalachian Trail goes by the tower.[^3] Wikipedia gives a hike of roughly 1.5 miles from Tellico Gap, climbing about 767 feet, and notes that the gap is reached by an unimproved road.[^4] Wikipedia calls the tower wooden, while the register calls it metal, so the sources disagree about what it is made of.[^4][^1]
