The cabin began as an Aircraft Warning Service post on the ridge above Salisbury Potrero, in the Los Padres National Forest.[^1][^2] The register dates it to about 1943 and gives the original spot as 5,188 feet.[^1] When Craig Carey wrote to the cabin's owner, the reply placed it on that ridge and called it an air craft warning station.[^2] The Aircraft Warning Service was a civilian unit under the Army Ground Observer Corps during the Second World War.[^3]

Carey, who traced the cabin in a 2011 post, used a 1950 forest visitor map.[^2] It shows a service road and a building above Salisbury Potrero, and no building at Black Willow Spring.[^2] He also cites a 1984 Los Padres overview, which counted two such cabins still standing on the forest, one of them at Black Willow Spring.[^2]

The register says the cabin was later relocated to a site nearby at Black Willow Spring, where it still stands, but it gives no date for the move.[^1] The two coordinate pairs in the register put the spots about 4.7 km apart.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list shows the Salisbury site as gone and the Black Willow Spring cabin as standing.[^4] The register added the relocated lookout in December 2019 as US 1395, CA 218.[^1]

Carey describes a rundown wooden building beside the Jackson Trail; he says a ranching family owns it and leases the ground beneath.[^2] A 2022 camp survey on Hike Los Padres describes a rustic cabin on Salisbury Potrero, which does not match the relocation account.[^5] I could not tell from the sources whether another building stands on the ridge. The register names the Forest Service as administrator, while Carey says a family owns the cabin, so check with the owners and the Los Padres National Forest before going.[^1][^2]
