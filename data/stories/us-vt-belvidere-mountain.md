L. T. Kinsley, the watchman on Belvidere Mountain, put up a cabin in 1919 and a lookout tower the following year.[^1][^2] The Green Mountain Club says the builders mostly used material taken from the mountain itself.[^2] In 1924 a federal forest service inspector rated the station the state's finest and recommended enclosing it in glass, as funds allowed, and replacing the ladders with stairs.[^1]

Lightning struck the tower in 1931 and splintered it.[^1] When the old wooden tower was blown down in the September hurricane, a January 1939 newspaper said a steel replacement with a 7-by-7 cabin would be built with WPA money.[^1] A state report for 1938 to 1940 credits the New England Forest Emergency fund with building the new steel tower instead, so the two accounts differ on who paid.[^1]

Through the late 1940s and early 1950s the state installed a half mile of telephone line, painted the stairs and cab, and added a cook stove.[^1] In October 1968 a specially built trailer-style cabin was flown onto the summit by helicopter, because access to the mountain was hard.[^1]

A watchman was employed on the summit in June 1956, and a fire warden began the season there in 1961.[^1] A 1956 guidebook, however, calls the tower "recently abandoned," so the sources do not agree on how active it still was.[^2]

By 1984 the state had let both the tower and the cabin fall into severe disrepair.[^2] That summer the Green Mountain Club began a restoration project that saved the tower, while the cabin was torn down.[^2][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing without its cab, and marks such towers with an asterisk.[^4] The restored tower is the only structure left on the summit.[^3] The cabin now exists only in guidebooks, photos and the recollections of earlier visitors.[^3]

Hikers reach the summit on foot.[^5] Trail Finder's route starts at the Eden Crossing lot on VT-118, follows the Long Trail about 2.2 miles to the Forester's Trail, then goes 0.2 miles to the top.[^5] The Green Mountain Club maintains the tower, and from it the Green Mountains can be seen south to Camel's Hump.[^5]

The asbestos mines at Eden and Lowell were a major presence on the mountain's east side for decades.[^3] They first opened in 1900 and closed in 1992.[^3]
