A 1969-70 state report, quoted on the Eastern US Lookouts site, describes a lookout tower near Summerville in Dorchester County.[^1] The tower was financed by West Virginia Pulp and Paper Company and was first known as the Middleton Gardens tower.[^1]

According to the report, 1929 was the year fire detection on private land in South Carolina truly began. It credits C.P. Cuthbert, a West Virginia Pulp and Paper employee, with overseeing construction of the first steel lookout built on private land in the state.[^1]

The report adds that the tower is widely counted as South Carolina's first fire tower.[^1] The Forestry Commission says its first tower went up near Camden in August 1930.[^2] Both claims can stand if the Summerville tower dates to 1929, since it would then predate the Commission's first.[^1][^2]

The report, covering July 1969 to June 1970, says the tower is still in year-round service under a cooperative agreement with the state's forest fire detection system.[^1]

The Commission shut its last forest fire lookouts on October 1, 1993.[^3] None of the sources we read says when the Summerville tower left service, or what remains at the site today.

Map data tags the site as an observation tower.[^4]
