The Forest Service built this tower as Devil Mountain Lookout on the San Juan National Forest in southwestern Colorado.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry for the Devil Mountain site says the tower went up in 1955 and took over the work of the Chimney Rock lookout.[^2] The two registers disagree on its size.[^1][^2] The Atole entry describes a 14 by 14 foot metal cab on a 40 foot steel tower, built to the Forest Service's CL 100 series design.[^1] The Devil Mountain entry gives a 13 by 13 foot steel cab on a 30 foot steel tower.[^2] I could not tell which set of figures is right.

The register says the Bureau of Indian Affairs claimed the tower once it was declared surplus.[^1] The Devil Mountain entry gives a different account: in the 1990s the tower was sold to the Jicarilla Apache Nation and moved to the tribe's land, where it is called Atole.[^2] The register dates the move to 1996 and says it took three trips by a Nevada Army National Guard Chinook.[^1] A cultural resource survey of the relocation, dated 1995, is titled for the Tecolote Mesa Fire Tower Lookout and puts the move on Jicarilla Apache lands.[^3] The register says the Tecolote Mesa site was chosen to give detection over 131,000 acres of additional land the tribe had bought.[^1]

I found no notable fires and no named lookout staff in these sources. The register says the lookout is in active service by the BIA, but it gives no date for that.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4] Its map point is about 9 km from the register's coordinates, so the two sources do not agree on where it stands.[^1][^4] The land is Jicarilla Apache land and the BIA runs the lookout, and I found no public access or visiting rules.[^1][^3] Do not climb or enter the tower. Ask the tribe or the BIA before any visit.
