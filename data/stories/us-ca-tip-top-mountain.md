Tip Top Mountain stands in the San Bernardino National Forest, about 12 miles east of Big Bear, at an elevation of 8,500 feet.[^1]

In April 1943 a San Bernardino National Forest lookout named Raymond W. Smith lived at the station on the mountain.[^1] That month he was bitten by a rattlesnake while cleaning up debris near the station.[^1] He gave himself first aid, and a radio message went to the Big Bear ranger station, where the ranger called a doctor.[^1] The doctor gave Smith anti-venom at the lookout and then brought him back to Big Bear for observation.[^1] A newspaper reported that he was expected to recover.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Tip Top Mountain as gone.[^2]

No source we read describes the site today, so we cannot say what remains on the mountain or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2]
