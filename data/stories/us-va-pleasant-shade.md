In October 1933 The Times Dispatch reported that Brunswick County had agreed to share the cost of a lookout with a neighboring county [^2]. The spot chosen was the highest point between Lawrenceville and Norfolk, and a district fire warden said about 60 percent of recent fires in the area had happened within view of the proposed tower [^2].

According to the National Historic Lookout Register, the tower went up in 1933 [^1]. It was made of galvanized steel by the Aermotor Co. and is listed as about 100 feet tall [^1]. When the register listed it in December 1996, the tower was still in use during spells of high fire danger, to aid spotting from the air [^1]. The register names the Virginia Department of Forestry as its administrator [^1].

The 1996 register and the current state list describe different times: the register says the tower was operating in 1996, while the state list now marks the site as gone [^1][^3]. No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, and I could not find whether the public can visit it.
