The tower is also called Garden Mountain or ET-12, and it stands in Bays Mountain Park.[^1][^2] The Bays Mountain History page places it on Holston River Mountain, which borders the park's northern edge, rather than on Bays Mountain itself.[^2] Two newspaper accounts quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, from 1948 and 1953, put it on Bays Mountain.[^4]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1937, and the Forestry Service used it into the 1960s.[^1][^2]

The register and Tennessee Landforms place it in Hawkins County.[^1][^3] A 1948 Kingsport paper, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, calls it the Forestry Division's only tower in Sullivan County.[^4]

Accounts of its height differ. The 1948 article describes a 60-foot steel structure, and a December 1953 article describes a seven-foot-square lookout shelter atop a 60-foot steel tower.[^4] The 2020 register entry and the Bays Mountain History page both give 100 feet.[^1][^2] We could not settle the difference.

In 1948 George McCloud had been the towerman for six seasons.[^4] By December 1953 Govan Smith Jr. was spending about five months a year in the shelter. For days on end, the article says, his only outside contact might be brief, curt exchanges with the Holston Ordnance Works fire department and with lookouts in neighboring towers.[^4]

In October 1953 a state forester, on patrol from the lookout, discovered a brush fire that burned over about 10 acres at the foot of Bays Mountain.[^4]

In October 1955 the state bought twelve 80-foot steel lookout towers, and a Kingsport paper's list of locations placed one for Sullivan County atop Bays Mountain.[^4] We found no source saying a tower of that kind went up on this ridge.

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on March 24, 2020 as the Garden Mountain Fire Tower, US 1401, TN 37, on the nomination of Don Gouge.[^1] The City of Kingsport administers the site.[^1]

Visit Kingsport listed a capped early-arrival hike to the tower, about 3.3 miles round trip, with a park ranger talking about the reservoir and the Fire Tower.[^5] The listing allowed 12 people and said registration was full when we checked.[^5] The last part of the route is steep, with steps and switchbacks, and the listing says the tower is not ADA compliant.[^5] Climbers who go inside can look out over the park, the Holston River and Kingsport.[^5]
