The Sinkut lookout stands on Sinkut Mountain, roughly 22 kilometres south of Vanderhoof, British Columbia, on Saik’uz First Nation territory.[^1] The provincial names database spells the peak Sinkut Mountain.[^2] The provincial base map classes the structure as a fire lookout tower.[^3]

Canadian Geographic dates the lookout to 1927, a period when fire watchers sat on high ground and watched for even a thin trace of smoke.[^1] The two-storey building is painted dark green, now flaking, and juts out from the peak of the crag.[^1] Its top floor gives a 360-degree view through windows on every side, and it has a wraparound balcony.[^1]

The article says British Columbia once had some 300 active lookouts.[^1] By 2024 it called Bart Vanderlinde the last regular fire lookout in the province.[^1] During fire season, usually late June to the end of August, each spell on the lookout typically lasts 14 days, depending on the weather.[^1] His shift runs from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., and he scans for the BC Wildfire Service during it.[^1] When he sees a fire, he works out its location with the fire finder, a scope above a 360-degree map on a wooden cabinet, though he can also use an iPad app. He then relays the location by radio.[^1]

The September 2024 feature is the most recent account I could open.[^1] It describes Vanderlinde as still on watch there.[^1]

The article also notes that a public road could bring anyone to the lookout at any time, day or night.[^1] No source I could open says whether visitors may enter the building or go through the gate, so the road alone does not settle access. Nothing I could open describes the lookout after 2024.
