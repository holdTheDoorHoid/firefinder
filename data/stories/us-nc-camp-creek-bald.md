## Camp Creek Bald Lookout

On the North Carolina side of the Appalachian Trail, right where it crosses into Tennessee, a round-topped steel tower stands on Camp Creek Bald in the Pisgah National Forest.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates its construction to 1928, with a 14-by-14-foot cab set on a 21-foot steel tower.[^1] A separate site cataloguing eastern lookouts instead gives 1929 and a 20-foot tower, for what appears to be the same square cab; neither source explains the gap, and it isn't resolved here.[^1][^2]

The tower's most unusual feature came later. Sometime in the 1960s the Forest Service swapped the original square cab for a round one — one of only two prototype round cabs it is known to have built.[^1][^3] A local history blog ties the change to the mountain's brief life as "Viking Mountain," home to a ski resort that ran for a single season before closing in 1972; by that account the tower predates the resort and kept its older name once the resort failed.[^4] The same blog states that Earl Shaffer, the first person recorded to hike the whole Appalachian Trail, spent a night at the tower in 1948 — a detail no other source checked here confirms.[^4]

The lookout was run by the Forest Service's Appalachian Ranger District, with the Andrew Johnson Amateur Radio Club listed as a cooperator.[^1] It is no longer staffed for fire detection: a 2020 visit report logged it as unstaffed, and the cab's trapdoor has been locked to keep people out, its catwalk gate wrapped in razor wire.[^1] Condition has swung over time. A 2008 visit found the glass intact and the cab in good shape, with the visitor noting it "still has furnishings/equipment inside."[^1] By September 2020, a volunteer visitor instead found peeling paint, broken windows, and wood in poor repair, and warned that without attention the Forest Service might eventually take the tower down.[^1] The Forest Service and a local radio club have carried out a partial restoration at some point, though no source gives a year for it.[^1]

Communication antennas now crowd three sides of the tower, cutting into what view is left; the tower itself is only about 20 feet tall, so encroaching foliage blocks sightlines in most other directions.[^1] Hikers can reach it by a short side trail off the Appalachian Trail, a detour of roughly 0.2 mile with about 150 feet of climb.[^3] The cab's trapdoor and the catwalk gate are both locked, and the structure is not open to climb — this is a tower to view from the ground, not one to count on for a summit view.[^1][^3] The site is registered with the National Historic Lookout Register as US 719 / NC 13, added April 2, 2008 on a nomination by Peter Barr.[^1]
