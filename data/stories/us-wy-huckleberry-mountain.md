The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Huckleberry Mountain lookout in 1938, following a standard Forest Service design.[^1][^2] It stands near the summit of the mountain, in the northern part of the Bridger-Teton National Forest.[^1][^3]

The register describes a 14-foot-square L-4 cab on a rare log crib base, and it dates the cab to 1932, six years before the 1938 build year. It does not explain the gap.[^1] The National Register nomination gives the building as 15 by 17 feet and 20 feet high, so the sources do not agree on its size.[^1][^3] They also disagree on the elevation: the register gives 7,209 feet, and the nomination gives 9,615 feet.[^1][^3]

A 1942 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout history site, calls Huckleberry one of the permanent stations created that year. It says the buildings were first assembled at the Forest Service property in Jackson and then trucked to their sites.[^4] That does not match the 1938 completion date in the nomination, and the sources I read do not settle the question.[^3]

The nomination says the lookout was first manned in 1939.[^3] In 1940, during the 1,500-acre Sheffield Creek fire, forest lookout Dave Adams had to evacuate the building and move to safer ground.[^3] In July 1954 a newspaper item said Huckleberry would be staffed within a week.[^4] After the 1957 season the lookout was taken out of regular lookout duty.[^3]

The National Register listed it in 1983.[^2] The nomination describes it as boarded up but still standing.[^3] The register entry, added in August 2011, describes it as unusually sound and says restoration is planned.[^1] In August 2016 crews covered the lookout with protective material in case the Berry Fire reached its ridge.[^4]

Firelookout.com lists it as a standing two-story log cab.[^5] Nothing in the sources I read confirms a finished restoration, public access or an overnight rental. The register names the Buffalo Ranger District as a cooperator, so ask that office before planning a visit.[^1]
