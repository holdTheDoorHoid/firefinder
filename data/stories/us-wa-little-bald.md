Little Bald Mountain rises to 6,108 feet (1,862 m) in Yakima County, in the Wenatchee National Forest, and a Forest Service lookout once stood on its summit.[^1] The register dates the tower to 1936: a 20-foot treated timber tower topped with an L-4 cab.[^1][^2][^3] WillhiteWeb reads 1934 panoramic photos as showing a man on the roof of an earlier structure, so the site may have had a lookout before 1936.[^3]

Some of the people who worked there are on record. WillhiteWeb reports that during the summer of 1943 one of the women on the lookout was a grade-school teacher.[^3] In late summer 1958, Tom Bigley filled in for the regular lookout, and he later wrote about the job online.[^3] In the early 1970s, Barbara Jones was the lookout.[^3]

The lookout ended in fire.[^1] The cab was lost to fire in 1980, while the lookout was on a day off.[^1][^2] Tom Bigley suspects a propane stove started it.[^3] Plans called for a replacement tower, but the Forest Service ended up staffing Cleman Lookout instead.[^1]

Today the sources describe concrete footings rather than a tower.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb's photo captions say the old footings are now used as guy-line anchors.[^3] A Forest Service survey disk dated 1938 was set in a northeast concrete footing, according to a 1971 description attributed to the U.S. Geological Survey and posted on one lookout-history site.[^4] Cars can still reach the summit, and the final two miles are rough rock.[^3] The Washington Trails Association describes a hike to the summit and notes that motorcyclists, bicyclists and horseback riders may use the trail too. It also says the former lookout burned down in 1980.[^5]
