Tahquitz Peak Lookout sits within the San Jacinto Wilderness on the San Jacinto Ranger District of the San Bernardino National Forest.[^1][^5] The register dates the present lookout to 1937.[^1] An earlier California Region 5 Plan 4A cabin came first. The register says it was built at a lower spot on the mountain in 1917, later moved to the top, and then replaced by the current lookout.[^1] The current tower stands 20 feet high, enclosed in timber, with a wooden cab that is 14 feet by 14 feet.[^1] The Mountains Foundation says crews did all the building by hand, since power tools are banned in the wilderness.[^2]

Newspapers from the early years give a few glimpses of the station. A January 1921 report tells how the lookout was incapacitated that fall, and the district ranger's wife, Mrs. Reindorp, held the post for more than a week.[^4] A 1938 report says the station was run cooperatively by the national forest and Riverside County.[^4]

The Mountains Foundation says the lookout was a working fire lookout until the end of the 1993 fire season.[^2] Both the Forest Service and the Mountains Foundation say it is the forest's longest-running station, with about 77 years of service.[^5][^2] None of the sources explains how they reached that count, and the current lookout dates only to 1937.[^1]

The lookout was restored in 1998, returned to service that October, and reopened with volunteer staffing.[^1][^2] The register describes it as staffed seven days a week by volunteers.[^1] An October 2017 visit recorded in the register found that the stairs needed repair and that there was rotten wood on the supports and peeling paint.[^1] A Forest Fire Lookout Association report from the same year said rotten wood had been replaced on Tahquitz Peak and at Keller Peak, and that young helpers hiked wood up to the lookout.[^3] The report also lists replacing the steps up to the lookout as a task still to do.[^3]

When I read it, the Forest Service page for the lookout showed a temporary-closure notice and a closed-for-the-season banner.[^5] The Mountains Foundation also said the lookout was currently closed.[^2] Neither copy I read showed a date, so check the current status before you plan a trip. The hike is 4.5 miles via either Devil's Slide or the South Ridge trail. Both need a wilderness permit, available at the Idyllwild Ranger Station.[^5] The Forest Service gives the peak's elevation as 8,846 feet, the Mountains Foundation gives 8,828 feet, and the register lists 8,752 feet.[^5][^2][^1]
