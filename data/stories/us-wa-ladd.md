The Ladd lookout stood in eastern Lewis County, Washington, and the state built it in 1955.[^1][^2][^3] It was a 40-foot wooden live-in tower with a 14-by-14-foot cabin on top.[^1][^3] The state's report says the cabin followed a newer Division of Forestry design, constructed from plywood and glass, with an aluminum roof projecting past the walls to shade it.[^3]

Willhite says the lookout sits several ridges south of the nearby Ladd Mountain.[^4] The weebly page, however, places the lookout on Ladd Mountain itself.[^3] The sources disagree on which peak it stood on.[^3][^4]

The state abandoned the tower in 1969.[^1][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and the Fire Lookout website both list it as gone.[^1][^2] The register added the site on May 26, 2012.[^1]

Willhite's photo captions name three footings, a pole tower and an old communications building.[^4] The register's photo captions show a pole tower and a footing as well.[^1]

Forest Road 74 leaves Highway 7 about four miles south of Elbe.[^4] Willhite says the road's last half mile is too choked with growth to drive.[^4]
