The Woodlawn lookout is a 100-foot steel tower in Cecil County, Maryland, built to cover a difficult stretch of the county's northwest.[^1][^2] Construction began on January 2, 1935, under District Forest Warden Charles W. Bayles, and the one-half acre site and its driveway were bought from local owners for $50.[^1] The reprinted newspaper account explains the reason for the tower.[^1] The older Foys Hill tower, 47 feet tall and erected in 1924, was poorly placed for spotting smoke along the Susquehanna River bluffs.[^1]

Harry Hartenstine, who had run the Foys Hill tower for more than ten years, took up his new post at Woodlawn, and a new towerman went on duty at Foys Hill.[^1] By November 1944 the tower had two-way radio to keep in touch with two patrol trucks, and Hartenstine was still operating it.[^1]

The first smoke the tower reported, on April 3, 1935, came from a field that its owners were burning off.[^1]

The FFLA's Maryland list still marks Woodlawn as standing.[^2] The newspaper items on the Forest Lookouts page end in 1944.[^1] We found no record of when staffing stopped, of any demolition, or of who owns the site now.[^1] We also found no public access information, so anyone interested should ask the owner before going near the tower.[^1][^2]
