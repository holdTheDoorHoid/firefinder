Kings Pass is a ridge site in the Coeur d'Alene National Forest, about 10 miles north-northwest of Wallace in Shoshone County, at 4,404 feet.[^1][^2]

A 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab went up on the west end of the ridge in 1934.[^1][^2]

The Idaho Fire Lookouts site lists three known staff members: Henry Johnson in 1934, Abe Curley in 1935 and Roy Norman in 1937.[^2] The first two entries cite Wallace Press-Times items from June of those years.[^2]

Both sites date the tower's loss to the 1950s.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Idaho list marks Kings Pass as gone too.[^3]

Today the site is a hike-in.[^2] The road is gated, so visitors walk a few miles from the pass to the point where the lookout once stood.[^2] The Idaho Fire Lookouts visit found scattered debris, old footings and some classic number 9 wire.[^2] The most conspicuous feature is a large microwave or antenna installation that appears much newer than the lookout era.[^2] The writers could not establish whether it was ever a Forest Service electronics site, or whether another agency used it.[^2] The page describes the gate but does not call the site closed.[^2]
