The Rich Mountain tower stood on Rich Mountain in Randolph County, West Virginia, at 3,643 feet, or 1,110 meters.[^1] The West Virginia Division of Forestry administered it.[^1]

The register dates the tower's construction to no later than 1925 and gives no exact year.[^1] In the sources we could read, we found no record of its builder, its design, or the fires it watched.

The register says the tower was sold to Snowshoe Mountain, Inc. in 1999, and was then dismantled and moved to the Snowshoe ski resort.[^1] A second page says the Department of Natural Resources sold it for one dollar before it was taken apart and moved.[^3] The register does not name the seller or give a price.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower was restored to original condition in 2000.[^2] The second page says it was reconditioned to its original specifications, then painted and reassembled that year.[^3]

Today the tower stands at the Snowshoe Mountain ski resort, in Pocahontas County.[^2][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register describes the resort as privately owned, and its June 25, 2021 visit report marks the tower's condition as good.[^2] The second page calls it an 80-foot tower that is now used as an attraction.[^3] Because the tower stands on private resort ground, visitors should check with the resort before going, and should not climb it.[^2]

The Randolph County site is listed as gone.[^4] A July 2022 photograph on the register is captioned as showing the tower site, and another as showing its footings.[^1] We found no description of who owns the site now, or whether the public can visit it.
