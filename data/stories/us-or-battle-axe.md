Battle Ax Mountain's lookout stood about 6 miles north of Detroit in Marion County, Oregon, at an elevation the register gives as 5,558 feet.[^1][^2] A 1952 newspaper item gives 5,540 feet instead.[^3]

The start date is disputed. Firelookout says the site was developed as a camp in 1911, while the register says the first lookout there was a camp built by the Forest Service in 1912.[^1][^2] A 1952 item says the point was first manned in 1909, when a guard made a daily hike there and back from Elk Lake.[^3] A standard lookout house was under construction in November 1922.[^3] Firelookout dates a D-6 cupola cabin to 1922, while the register dates the cupola replacement to 1925.[^1][^2]

A few incidents and staffing notes survive. A June 1918 item says the lookout caught sight of a fire 15 to 20 miles to the west.[^3] In September 1924, lightning struck the peak and knocked out the telephone line to the station, but the bolt missed the station and the people inside.[^3] The lookout was staffed for 60 days in 1941, and in 1944 Bennie Wriglesworth returned after several months at the station.[^3]

The register and Firelookout both say a 6-foot enclosed L-4 tower went up in 1951.[^1][^2] A 1952 item describes a new 14-by-14, glass-surrounded house that replaced an older cupola-style lookout built about 30 years earlier on that spot, at a cost of $3,000.[^3] The sources do not agree on whether the 1951 change was a tower or a house.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout was removed in 1969. The register says removed, Firelookout says destroyed, and Forest Lookouts logs it as removed that year.[^1][^2][^3]

The most recent account in these sources is a 2014 hiker's trip report on the Oregon Hikers Forum. It says eight foundation piers remained at the summit.[^4] A photo caption in the same thread describes old foundation blocks from a lookout that is no longer there.[^4] The hike started at Elk Lake.[^4] A quoted newspaper column in the thread places the peak in the Bull of the Woods Wilderness and says the last six miles of the drive are very rough.[^4] No source here describes the site today, and none says it is closed.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
