Taylor Mountain has been a lookout site since the 1920s, when a small 6-by-6 cabin and a camp went up on the peak in Lemhi County, Idaho.[^1][^3] Sources disagree about that early building. The Forest Lookouts site quotes a 1924 forest report that describes a 9-by-9 house on the site, listed as a primary lookout.[^2]

A September 1925 Salmon Herald item says a man was home again after a stint of about two months on duty at Taylor Mountain.[^2]

In 1930 the site got a 10-by-10 log crib ground cabin, the building that still stands today.[^1]

Use of the site ended in 1956.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register added the cabin on October 2, 2020, and says the building is still standing.[^1]

The sources also do not agree on elevation. The register gives 7,414 feet, while Rex's Fire Lookout Page and Idaho Fire Lookouts both give 9,960 feet.[^1][^3][^4]

Idaho Fire Lookouts describes the site as hike-in and abandoned.[^4] It is not a staffed lookout, and none of the pages I read say whether visitors can go up to it. The register places it in the Salmon-Challis National Forest, while the other three pages say Salmon National Forest.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Check with the Forest Service about access before planning a trip, and do not climb into an old structure you happen to find.
