The Hibriten Mountain fire tower was built in 1927, and it still stands above Lenoir.[^1][^2][^3] The Register groups it with other towers that shape Lenoir's skyline, and it records the site as registered on December 15, 2014.[^1] The sources we read do not name who built the tower.

The North Carolina Forest Service used the summit for a fire tower under a lease with the landowners.[^4] To cut down on traffic on the private road to the top, the Forest Service built a gate in the early 1940s.[^4] The site's history page calls Dorothy Dellinger one of the last people to work in the tower, and points readers to a Lenoir News-Topic article about her, published November 5, 1987.[^2]

We did not find a record of any notable fire at this site.

The tower is no longer in service.[^2] It still stands among radio and cell towers, and its base is now surrounded by barbed wire fencing.[^2] The site's history page says that for years, people could climb as high as the door into the tower's cabin before the fence went up.[^2]

The road to the top is now effectively closed to the public.[^4] The News-Topic reports that this followed an April 17 order from the Caldwell County Superior Court, and the text we could read does not give the year.[^4] The land is owned through Hibriten Mountain LLC.[^4] Wikipedia describes the mountain as privately owned and open only to authorized people, and says the dirt road to the top is not a public hiking trail.[^3] Please do not go past the closed road or the fence.
