Warner Mountain Lookout is a newcomer dressed as an antique. The 41-foot tower itself is older: it was moved here from Grass Mountain in 1986.[^2] On top, builders added an oversized replica of the cupola-style cabins that were common on lookouts in the 1920s, a design normally set on the ground or on a short crib rather than a tall steel tower.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register, which calls the result "of recent construction (1987)," considers it the only known cupola cabin ever placed this high, and it was added to the register on June 18, 1994.[^1]

The lookout sits on Warner Ridge in the Willamette National Forest, about 14 miles southeast of Oakridge, Oregon.[^1][^2] The register gives its elevation as 5,667 feet; a lookout-history site gives 5,723 feet.[^1][^2] It replaced an older lookout nearby: a cupola cabin on Logger Butte, two miles to the northeast, which had been removed in 1969.[^2] (An earlier version of this record listed that 1969 removal as Warner Mountain's own history; it belongs to Logger Butte instead.)

The lookout has carried double duty for decades, staffed by the Forest Service through the summer fire season and then opened to winter visitors through the agency's recreation rental program.[^2] Today it is rented through Recreation.gov as a winter-only stay.[^3] The 14-by-14-foot cabin, on its catwalk-ringed 40-foot tower, sleeps up to four on one double bed plus floor space, with propane for heat and cooking but no running water or electricity.[^3] Getting there is the hard part: the ten-mile access road is not plowed, and reaching the lookout means two to ten miles of skiing, snowshoeing, or snowmobiling, with no guaranteed cell service and real exposure to sudden mountain weather.[^3] Guests melt snow for water and pack out their trash.[^3]

Warner Mountain Lookout still stands and functions as both a working fire lookout in summer and a remote winter rental, one of the more unusual pairings of 1920s cabin style with 1980s construction in the Pacific Northwest's lookout network.
