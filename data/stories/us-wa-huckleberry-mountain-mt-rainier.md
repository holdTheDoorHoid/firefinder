Huckleberry Mountain is in Lewis County, Washington, between the headwaters of the Skookumchuck and Deschutes rivers.[^1][^3] A camp was first set up on the peak in 1930, and in 1931 the Snoqualmie National Forest built a gable-roofed L-4 cab there.[^1][^2][^3] The site now sits inside the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1]

The register says the Washington State Division of Forestry, later the DNR, had staffed the lookout by 1953.[^1] Willhite places the start of staffing later in the 1950s.[^3] Those two dates do not quite agree. The lookout was abandoned in 1965, although one hobbyist page puts a question mark after that year.[^1][^2][^3]

Ruins remain on the summit.[^1][^2] Willhite reports plenty of debris up there, including a Firefinder post set in the rock, and says large trees now block every view.[^3]

Forest Service Road 71 leads to a private gate near Lookout Mountain.[^3] Willhite says the gate is open only part of the year.[^3] He adds that a locked gate means a long walk in.[^3] He gives the trip as three miles, maybe four, with about 1,000 feet of gain from the gate, and notes that the original trail can be followed below the lookout.[^3] Check the gate and road conditions with the Gifford Pinchot National Forest before you go, and do not go around a closed gate.
