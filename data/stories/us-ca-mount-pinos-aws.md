Mount Pinos rises to 8,847 feet in the Los Padres National Forest, in Ventura County.[^1] The post there was built in 1942-43 as a standard Aircraft Warning Service structure, with a ground cabin and a cupola.[^1] It served two jobs: spotting enemy aircraft and detecting fires.[^1]

The Forest Service equipped and manned the post, and the Army paid the Forest Service back for all of its costs.[^1] Operators were supposed to pass all aircraft activity, and every fire or smoke, on to Forest Service dispatch.[^1] Aircraft details were then passed on to the Army.[^1]

A Peakbagging page describes it as a plane-spotting site from the Second World War, and says it may also have been a fire lookout for some years.[^2] The register notes, citing newspaper reports, that forest fire detection was happening there as early as 1927, but says it is unknown whether any structure stood there before the war.[^1]

The register's list shows the site as Gone.[^3] Its detail page gives no removal date and says the post may have kept detecting fires after the war.[^1] The register added the site on April 16, 2020, on the nomination of Brad Eells.[^1]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether the public can visit the site.
