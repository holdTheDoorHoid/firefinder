The Hayfork Bally lookout stands at 6,268 feet in Trinity County, California, on the Shasta-Trinity National Forest.[^1] The Register says the site has been a lookout since at least 1920.[^1]

The current station is a Forest Service cabin of the CL-100 type, 13 by 13 feet, set on a 42-foot steel tower that went up in 1965.[^1] Older sources describe other towers. A 1939 newspaper item mentions a thirty-five foot steel tower, and a 1961 survey note describes a four-legged steel tower 50 feet high.[^2]

The early staff are named in newspapers. In July 1915 a paper said Frank Hoffman was in charge of the lookout.[^2] By July 1920 the Forest Service lookout on the summit was Miss Elsie Luckie, who lived alone there, 15 miles from the nearest ranch house. An August 1920 report said she counted 1,250 deer in July alone.[^2]

The site grew and had its troubles. In 1931 a two-room house and a horse barn were planned for the station, and a string of six mules packed in the lumber.[^2] A break-in at the station, reported in August 1932, took binoculars and other equipment. In October 1932 the station was abandoned for the season.[^2] In 1939 a newspaper described an observer caught on the tower during a thunderstorm, when the tower itself carried a charge.[^2]

From 1958 to 1963 the U.S. Air Force held about a quarter acre beside the lookout, where it ran an unmanned Gap Filler radar station.[^1] The Forest Service took the site back in April 1963.[^2]

The Register accepted the lookout on October 29, 2019 as US 1366, CA 189.[^1]

A July 2025 visitor account describes the summit as a still-active fire lookout tower, staffed by the Forest Service. It also describes solar arrays powering a small building of radio repeaters, and an Osborne Fire Finder inside the station. The writer joined a local amateur radio club for a picnic there.[^3]

The sources read do not say whether the public can visit the summit or climb the tower. Because it is an active fire station, check with the Shasta-Trinity National Forest before going.
