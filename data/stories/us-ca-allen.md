Allen Lookout is a steel tower on Allen Peak in San Mateo County, California.[^1][^2] The register says it started life as an air traffic control tower at Salinas Army Air Base, and that inmate labor moved it to this spot in 1966.[^1]

The site had a lookout before that. In August 1941, a San Mateo newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said men from the Gilroy CCC camp were building a lookout tower on Allen Peak, a mile north of the Skyline Boulevard and La Honda road intersection.[^2] The register says the 1966 tower replaced that 1941 wooden lookout, whose remnants survive as a utility building on the site.[^1]

A January 1966 photo caption, also quoted by Forest Lookouts, says a new steel tower would soon replace an old wooden structure whose timbers had been broken by 80-mile gales.[^2]

In November 1956, during unseasonably dry weather, the county put its Allen lookout tower back into use to watch over the south county's forest areas, according to a newspaper item quoted by Forest Lookouts.[^2] The register says the tower was closed in 1981.[^1] It also says heavy foliage now surrounds the tower on all sides.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list shows the tower as standing.[^3] The register names the California Department of Forestry & Fire Protection as the administering agency.[^1]

The sources describe the location differently. A 1966 caption places the lookout one mile west of Skyline Boulevard, on West Bear Gulch Road, while the 1941 item puts it a mile north of the Skyline and La Honda intersection.[^2] The register's coordinates also sit about half a kilometer from the point the Forest Fire Lookout Association list gives.[^1][^3]

I found no public visitor information for the tower in the sources I read, and the sources do not show it reopened after its 1981 closure. Do not climb it.[^1]
