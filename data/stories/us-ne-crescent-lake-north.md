Crescent Lake North is a 60-foot steel fire tower in the Crescent Lake National Wildlife Refuge, in Garden County, Nebraska.[^1][^2] The refuge was set aside in 1931 to protect its wetlands for migrating birds and other wildlife.[^5] The register gives the tower site's elevation as 3,979 feet (1,213 metres).[^1]

The register dates the tower's erection to 1935.[^1][^2] Its own quotation of the Fish and Wildlife Service, however, says the refuge's two fire towers were built in the early 1940s by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] A January 1937 newspaper item, quoted on the register page for the south tower, says work at the refuge began under the Works Progress Administration in 1936 and that crews built a 100-foot steel lookout tower there.[^4] That item does not name which tower it means, and the three accounts do not agree on the date or the builder. The register and West Lookouts pages may draw on a common source, so their agreement on 1935 is not independent confirmation.[^1][^2]

Nothing we found names who staffed the north tower or records any fire it watched, so its working life is largely undocumented.

The Fish and Wildlife Service, as quoted by the register, says the towers have stopped being used to spot fires and now serve wildlife.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the north tower as standing.[^3]

The register added the tower on August 9, 2026, as US 1844, NE 2, and lists the Fish and Wildlife Service as its administrator.[^1] No source we reached says whether the public may visit or climb it. Visitors should ask the refuge before assuming they can go up, and should not climb a tower that is closed.
