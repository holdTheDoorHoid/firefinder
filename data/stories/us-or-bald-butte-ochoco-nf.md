Bald Butte sits about 15 miles northwest of Burns, in Harney County, Oregon.[^5] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it on the Ochoco National Forest and names the U.S. Forest Service as its administrator.[^1]

The first lookout went up in 1931, when George Roba and Ira Swift finished a wood tower on August 12.[^2] The Forest Lookouts history quotes an Ochoconian report saying the tower stood 42 feet high, with an 8-foot-square house on top, and that it could see country the other Snow Mountain lookouts could not.[^2] The register describes that first structure as a 45-foot pole tower, with a cab and quarters at ground level, built in 1931.[^1] A 2018 visitor's page gives 1935 for a 45-foot pole tower with a ground cabin.[^3] The sources disagree on the first tower's height and date.[^1][^2][^3]

Frank Merritt occupied the tower for the rest of that first season.[^2] The current tower dates from 1959 and is 41 feet tall, with a wooden R-6 flat-topped cab.[^1][^2] The register says it is staffed as needed, but a 2018 visit found it had not been staffed in decades.[^1][^3]

In 2007 the Oregon chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association listed Bald Butte among three Oregon lookouts lost that year.[^4] The chapter's report, signed by its director, says the writers made two trips to salvage windows, a door and lightning rods before the tower was burnt to the ground.[^4] The report does not say how the fire, if there was one, started.[^4]

A visitor on May 25, 2018 found the tower still standing, abandoned and in bad shape, with fragments of it lying further down the slope.[^3] A fence around the base, put up by the Forest Service, is meant to stop people going up.[^3] Firelookout.com calls the tower condemned and severely vandalized, though that page is undated.[^5] The 2007 and 2018 accounts disagree on whether the tower survives, and these sources do not settle it.[^3][^4]

A spur road branches off Road 41 toward the lookout, but in 2018 a fallen tree blocked it about half a mile up, so the visitor parked and walked the rest.[^3] Do not climb the tower. The fence and the condition reports are reasons to stay off the structure. The sources describe no rental.
