Red Meadow was a platform lookout in the Flathead National Forest, about 13 miles west of Polebridge.[^1] Firelookout.com dates the platform tower to 1923 and notes a camp at the southwest end of Red Meadow Lake.[^1] The listing gives the site's elevation as 5,650 feet.[^1]

The lookout may have been in use before that.[^2] An October 1921 item from The Columbian, quoted on a Montana lookout history page, says Frank Crause had spent two months working as a forest lookout at Red Meadow.[^2] That is two years before the platform date, so the two dates may refer to different structures.[^1][^2] The item does not say what kind of post it was, or whether it was this platform.[^2]

Firelookout.com says the lookout was abandoned in the 1930s.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list gives the platform the status "Abandoned."[^3] Neither source says whether any part of the platform survived.[^1][^3]

The Forest Service now runs a small campground at Red Meadow Lake, at 5,500 feet, with three campsites and three picnic-table day-use areas.[^4] The page says the date when access opens is not fixed.[^4] No source ties that campground to the old lookout site.[^1][^4] None describes what remains there, or whether visitors can reach the spot.[^1][^3][^4]
