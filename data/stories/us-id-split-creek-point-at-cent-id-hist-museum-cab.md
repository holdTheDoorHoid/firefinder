Split Creek Point is in Valley County, Idaho. The National Historic Lookout Register lists the Payette National Forest as a cooperator, and says the lookout and a residence cabin were constructed in 1933. The lookout was an Aermotor 7x7 observation cab set on a 72-foot X-braced steel tower.[^1]

The tower came down in 2008.[^1][^2] A Forest Fire Lookout Association chapter report from that year says the steel tower was dismantled, and that Forest Service staff intentionally damaged a cabin there. The report calls it only "the Split Creek Point cabin," so it does not say whether that was the lookout cab or the residence cabin.[^2] The register agrees the tower was taken apart that year, and says the residence cabin stayed at the original site.[^1]

The lookout cab itself was moved to the Central Idaho Historical Museum in McCall, Idaho, and visitors can tour it. The register gives no date for the move.[^1] The site was registered on October 3, 2020.[^1]

A climbing guide published in 2016 says the peak was once home to a 75-foot lookout tower, and that its summit still has a cabin from the 1930s with a working roof.[^3] The guide's 75 feet and the register's 72 feet do not match. The guide also does not say which cabin it means, so the sources do not settle which cabin remains on the summit.[^1][^3]

Reaching the summit takes two trails, FST-081 and FST-086.[^3] The guide puts the distance from the trailhead at just under five miles.[^3]
