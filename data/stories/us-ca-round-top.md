The Round Top lookout was planned in 1923 and built in 1927 on Round Top, in the Berkeley Hills.[^1]

A graded road to the summit came first. The Contra Costa Hills fire protection committee started it in January 1927, and District Fire Ranger William Jordan built it.[^2] The Pacific Gas and Electric Company agreed to install poles and wires to the summit, at its own expense as a donation.[^2]

The steel structure rose 60 feet on X-shaped bracing, and a small 7-by-7-foot cabin at the top was for watching only.[^1] The newspaper said its view would cover ground the Grizzly Peak station could not see.[^2]

The sources disagree on when it opened. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the lookout's completion and dedication to May 1927.[^1] A September 1927 Berkeley Daily Gazette report, quoted on Forest Lookouts, says the committee completed it on June 1 and would dedicate it that week.[^2]

On July 1, 1927, the new tower spotted a brush fire in Claremont Canyon before it could spread, and it was quickly put out.[^2] The paper noted that the fire's scene could not be seen from Grizzly Peak, and that many serious grass and brush fires had been averted since the watch plan began.[^2]

The register says the tower was active as late as 1973. It was removed after that and before the 1986 Thornton survey, and the register gives no closer date.[^1]

Round Top is an extinct volcano in the Berkeley Hills, just east of Oakland.[^3] Its summit is inside Sibley Volcanic Regional Preserve, which began in 1936 as Round Top Regional Park and was renamed in 1958 for Robert Sibley, the park district's second president.[^3][^4] The preserve has at least two stone labyrinths of recent origin.[^4]

The lookout is gone, so there is no tower to climb.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site. The sources also give no current hours or rules for the summit, so check with the East Bay Regional Parks District before you go.
