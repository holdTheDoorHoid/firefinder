The Rockhill lookout began as a wooden tower.[^1] A 1926 newspaper item says the Rockhill Coal and Iron Company put up a 55-foot tower near Robertsdale in 1920 and installed a coal miners' telephone in its cabin.[^1] Another column calls the first wooden tower on eastern Broad Top Mountain the Round Mountain Tower, built around 1924 or 1925 northwest of Wood.[^2] A 1972 account dates the wooden tower to 1927 and places it northwest of Woodvale, so the date and the spot are not settled.[^1]

The later tower is steel. A 1941 federal survey description puts the steel tower at roughly 65 feet, with four legs and a glass-enclosed cab on top, and says the Pennsylvania Department of Forests and Waters owned it.[^1] The 1972 account dates the present tower to 1942, when it was built on ground the Rockhill Coal Company owned, and puts its height at about 60 feet.[^1]

The sources also differ on which office ran it. The 1972 account says the Huntingdon District office maintained it, while the column says the Rothrock District forest office controlled it.[^1][^2]

Isaac "Ike" Williams of Robertsdale watched for fires from the top in the early 1960s, according to a photo caption.[^2] Ann D. Wilson of Todd is believed to have been the last person to staff the lookout.[^2]

The tower was last used on March 28, 1988.[^2] The column calls it retired, and says it still rises on Wray's Hill, about five miles south of Wood, at 2,336 feet above sea level.[^2] No source we found describes the hilltop today or says whether visitors may go up there.

[^1]: Rockhill - Forest Lookouts, Eastern US Lookouts (a compilation of 1926 and 1972 newspaper items and a 1941 National Geodetic Survey description), https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/rockhill.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
