Mount Chase, near Patten in Penobscot County, Maine, got its first fire tower in 1909, when a wooden structure went up on the summit.[^1][^2][^3][^4] A steel tower replaced it in 1917.[^3][^4]

The sources do not agree on how tall that steel tower was. The Forest Lookouts page records a 16-foot steel tower erected in 1917, but its 1920 inventory lists 12 feet.[^2] The hiking blog and Maine IAT both say 16 feet.[^3][^4]

The watchmen lived in a cabin below the summit.[^4] A one-room cabin built in 1909 was used until 1922, when a second and larger cabin replaced it.[^4] We did not find a lookout named in the sources we read, and we found no fire linked to this tower.

In 2001 the tower was taken down and moved to the Patten Lumbermen’s Museum.[^1][^3][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association’s Maine list marks the old mountain site as gone and the museum tower as standing.[^5]

Today the tower is a display at the museum, which charges admission and invites visitors to learn about Maine logging history.[^4][^6] The sources we found do not say whether the tower can be climbed, so ask the museum before you plan a visit.
