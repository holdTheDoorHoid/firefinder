The steel tower that became Limestone County's fire lookout began its working life in Georgia.[^1] The register describes it as an Aermotor MC-39 with a 7-by-7-foot cab, first set up in the Chattahoochee National Forest.[^1] That model was the most common steel lookout in Alabama's forests, according to a 1819 News feature.[^3]

In spring 1971 the county ranger said the U.S. Forest Service had declared the tower surplus and that the state commission had assigned it to Limestone County.[^2] Crews took it apart in Georgia, carried it to O'Neal Road, and rebuilt it that summer, with completion expected in mid-July.[^2] The sources disagree about the exact spot. The April announcement put it about four miles north of Athens, while a November report placed it west of town.[^2] They also disagree on height: 60 feet in April, 46 feet in June and 50 feet in November.[^2] The register's description also says 50 feet.[^1]

In November 1971 Alabama Forestry Commission officials Howard Swanner and Wallace Tucker began manning the tower to spot fires in the county.[^2]

The last event the sources record for the tower itself is a June 2012 dismantling.[^2] The tower was taken down and moved to storage while a new site was sought for the refurbished structure.[^2] The register says it was removed sometime between 2009 and 2017 and gives April 24, 2017 as its registration date.[^1]

Today no source describes the site on O'Neal Road, says whether the tower has been re-erected anywhere, or says whether the land is open to visitors.
