Steliko Point sits on a modest summit in the Okanogan-Wenatchee National Forest, seven miles northwest of Entiat, but it commands one of the more useful views in that stretch of the Cascades' east slope.[^1] The Forest Service first used the site as a lookout camp in 1925, watching for smoke from a simple station rather than a built tower.[^1] The small L-4 cab that stands there today, ten feet to a side, went up in 1947.[^1]

For decades afterward it did the ordinary work of a fire lookout: a person posted through the dry months, glassing the ridgelines toward Tyee Mountain, Stormy Mountain and the Waterville Plateau for the first sign of smoke.[^3] The Forest Service kept using it that way into the 1990s, after which regular staffing ended; rangers out of Ardenvoir are said to still climb up during periods of high fire danger.[^3][^2] By the time the National Historic Lookout Register documented the tower in 2011, it read as plainly abandoned: broken shutters screwed back onto the frame, the old power line pulled out, no sign of recent use, and a volunteer's note calling for a coat of paint.[^2] The register formally listed Steliko on January 3, 2015, nominated by Bob and Susan Adler.[^2]

Rather than let it fall further, the Forest Service eventually opened Steliko to overnight rental through Recreation.gov. Guests who make the 1.5-mile climb up Forest Road 5310 — snow-free from about mid-April to mid-November, otherwise reached on foot, skis or snowshoes — now sleep in the same small cabin, on foam mattresses over a two-burner propane stove, under the same wide view the lookouts once watched for fire.[^3] The cabin has had some work done on it lately but is still meant to be rustic, so guests bring their own supplies; a simple outhouse with a vault toilet stands forty yards away.[^3] It remains federal property, open to the public by reservation rather than by right of trespass, and visitors are asked to come prepared for rough weather and no guaranteed heat.[^3]
