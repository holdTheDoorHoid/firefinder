The Kelso tower stands in Lincoln County, on land the state still holds.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes an 80-foot Aermotor LS-80, and its 2023 visitor called the spot picturesque.[^1]

The register gives no build date and lists it as unknown.[^1] The Tennessee Division of Forestry (TDF) is credited with constructing 208 fire lookout towers statewide between about 1930 and 1970, but none of the sources I read ties Kelso to a specific year or builder.[^2] Tennessee Landforms gives the tower's state forest number as 5-13, from a 1976 inventory.[^3] The register now names the TDF as the administering agency.[^1]

I found no account of fires, lookout staff or moves at Kelso in the sources I read.

A Tennessee Historical Commission document prepared for the National Register lists Kelso among the fire towers still standing on March 26, 2014.[^2] The register records a site visit on November 24, 2023, and its registration on December 16, 2023.[^1]

The old county forester's house now holds forestry offices, and the register notes that the state still owns the site.[^1] The register does not describe any public access, so treat the site as restricted. Please do not climb the tower.
