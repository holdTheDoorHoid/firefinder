The register and FireLookout.com both date the first Steamboat Mountain lookout to 1928, when a 14-by-14-foot cabin was built.[^1][^2] Will Hite's page says a cathedral-style lookout was built that year.[^4] The Washington lookouts page instead dates a cathedral-style house to 1930, and cites a January 1931 newspaper story about a newer station built "this summer."[^3]

In 1956 the cabin was replaced by a 20-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 flat cab.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Will Hite's page says it was staffed for about ten years.[^4] In May 1964 the Gifford Pinchot forest supervisor said only six of the forest's 37 lookouts would be manned, and that Steamboat would be the only one in the Mt. Adams district.[^3]

The register says the tower was still staffed in 1965.[^1] FireLookout.com says photos from 1964 and 1965 show it being staffed.[^2]

Sources differ on how it came down. The register and FireLookout.com say it was gone by 1966.[^1][^2] Will Hite's page says it was removed by burning by 1966, and the Washington lookouts page dates the burning to the mid-1960s.[^3][^4] The Oregon Hikers guide says the tower lasted about a decade before it was torn down.[^5]

Oregon Hikers identifies four corner blocks in the summit area as remnants of the 1956 timber tower.[^5] Will Hite files the mountain in the Indian Heaven Wilderness, gives the hike distance as three-quarters of a mile, and says the trail climbs 600 feet from the parking area.[^4] The trailhead sits at a huge quarry, reached by gravel road.[^4]
