In 1951 the B.C. Forest Service planned a new fire lookout on Hamilton Mountain, a peak of 1,509 m (4,950 ft) about 5 km (3 mi) southwest of the west end of Douglas Lake, in the Merritt district.[^1] A May 1951 notice in the Merritt Herald said the site would have an excellent view over the upper Nicola River watershed, and that spotting fires early would mean smaller fires and considerable savings in firefighting costs.[^1]

By July the same paper reported that the lookout would open the following week. It would start with a tent, and a permanent pre-fabricated building was expected later in the season. Johnny Wenlock of Chu Chua was named as the planned lookout attendant.[^1]

The later record is thinner. A hiker's blog calls the lookout abandoned and says few people reach it, because nearby landowners no longer allow public use of the service road.[^2] The Rainlander, a BC outdoor publication, describes the tower as surrounded by private property with no public access.[^3] Firelookout.ca gives the same picture: the site is on private land, with no access.[^5]

A travel guide lists the tower among those believed to still be standing.[^4] No source describes what remains at the site today. Because the land is private, visitors should treat it as off limits.
