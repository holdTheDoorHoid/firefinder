The cab in Bly's city park is what is left of a Forest Service lookout tower at the Bly Ranger Station.[^1][^5] The register gives 1950 in its Built field, but its own description says a 20-foot wooden tower with a 7x7 flat-roof cab and catwalk went up in 1958.[^1] The Weebly page also dates the tower to 1958.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites record says the Forest Service put the tower up in its own compound after the Klamath Forest Protective Association station closed in the mid-1950s. That compound was several blocks from the old station.[^3]

The first lookout at Bly, a 1934 platform on a warehouse roof at the association's station, came before the tower.[^3] The tower was 20 feet tall, built of treated timber, with a 7x7 flat-roof cab.[^2] A 1959 inspection report recorded it as under emergency status.[^2] A 2022 travel blog says the tower saw use only in emergencies.[^4]

The lookout was decommissioned in 2006 and scheduled to move to a local park as an interpretive center. That move was put off a year because there was no way to move the building.[^2] In 2007 the tower and cab were dismantled.[^2] The tower's framework was destroyed, and the cab went to the community park.[^4][^5][^6]

As of a June 2022 visit, the cab sat in a small community park that the blog calls The Perch, next to the Bly Rural Fire Protection District building and less than a mile from the Ranger Station.[^4] The cab then held fire-related items, and the blog lists the lookout as maintained for educational purposes.[^4] The blog describes the cab's contents but does not say whether visitors may go inside.[^4]
