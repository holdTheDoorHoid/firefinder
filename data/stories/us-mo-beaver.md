The Beaver lookout was a steel tower standing on four legs, with a closed-in observation room on top, in Butler County, Missouri.[^1] A 1963 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, quoted on the Central US Lookouts page, says the U.S. government owned it, that it was painted aluminum, and that it stood about 110 feet overall.[^1]

The survey places it about 12 miles northwest of Poplar Bluff and 12 miles southeast of Ellsinore, on land of the Clark National Forest.[^1] That forest was administratively combined with the Mark Twain National Forest in 1973, and absorbed by it in 1976.[^2] The page files the tower under the Mark Twain National Forest, but its location code (25N-4E-23) and the survey (section 14) do not name the same section, so the exact spot is not settled.[^1]

Today the page marks the tower as removed.[^1] No source read describes what, if anything, remains at the site, or whether the public can reach it.
