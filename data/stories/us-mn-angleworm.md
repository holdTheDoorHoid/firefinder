Angleworm stood on a steep, rocky, well-wooded hill on the west side of Angleworm Lake, about 14 miles north-northwest of Ely.[^1] Ken Jackson places it in the western part of what is now the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness.[^2]

A St. Louis County booklet gives the tower a date of 1928 and lists it on a 1928 Superior National Forest map.[^3] Ken Jackson dates a photograph of the lookout cabin to August 1928.[^2] A 1934 survey described the tower as 90 feet high, built of steel, and maintained by the Forest Service.[^1][^3] The booklet also notes a square cab.[^3]

Ken Jackson places the tower and its tiny cabin within what he calls the Canoe Fire Department.[^2] A logging railroad spur reached the lake's eastern shore by the early 1920s.[^2] The Forest Service used a small gasoline-powered speeder to reach the tower until 1937, when the logging company pulled up the rails.[^2]

The booklet marks the tower as removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as gone.[^3][^4] When Ken Jackson visited in the summer of 2009, he found pieces of the tower a few hundred yards away, over the brow of the hill. It looked to him as if the tower had been toppled with explosives and then disassembled into pieces a person could carry.[^2]

There is no standing tower to climb.[^4] The site is reached on foot.[^2] Ken Jackson describes a hike of about 2½ miles from the Echo Trail, and Portage North calls the Angleworm Trail popular.[^2][^5] Portage North says plenty of the tower remains, and that many hikers likely walk past the former tower and cabin site unaware of its history.[^5] The 1934 survey note gives three miles of trail from a road marker, while Ken Jackson's figure is about 2.5 miles from the Echo Trail, so the two figures differ.[^1][^2]
