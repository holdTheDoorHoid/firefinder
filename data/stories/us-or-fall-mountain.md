Fall Mountain Lookout sits above the Malheur National Forest in Grant County, Oregon, about nine miles south-southwest of John Day.[^1] A lookout was first put here as an emergency fire-watch point in 1930.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register instead dates construction to 1933, describing an 18-foot pole tower topped with the 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab and a wooden catwalk, cooperatively maintained by the Blue Mountain Ranger District; it notes that the cab is largely original but that the tower itself was rebuilt in 1953.[^2] The two accounts likely describe the same site at different stages: an emergency post in 1930, followed within a few years by the standing L-4 structure, with the pole tower beneath it replaced two decades later.

The lookout was nominated for the National Historic Lookout Register by Gary McAtee and Ron Johnson of the Oregon Chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association, and it was formally registered on March 8, 1995, as US 115 / OR 15.[^2] A 2007 visitor who rented the cab reported it well equipped — a working refrigerator and a new stove and oven, a futon, and an outhouse kept clean — though the paint was due and a cluster of nearby communications towers, with a generator audible at times, interrupted the view toward sunrise.[^2]

Today the lookout is not staffed for fire detection; the Forest Service instead rents it out through Recreation.gov for overnight stays.[^2][^3] The one-room cabin sleeps two on a futon and has electricity, with an electric stove and refrigerator, pots and dishes, and a catwalk running around the outside for 360-degree views toward the Strawberry Mountain Wilderness. There is no water on site, so guests bring their own, along with bedding and anything else they need. A vault toilet stands by the tower's base, and hiking trails into the wilderness are roughly half an hour's drive away.[^3] The structure is reachable by vehicle, but Recreation.gov describes the final stretch of road as narrow, rutted and rough, and recommends a high-clearance vehicle — a rougher account than the "no ruts in road" a 2007 renter gave, so conditions likely vary with the season.[^2][^3]
