The Observation Peak lookout stands at 9,402 feet in Yellowstone National Park, in Park County, Wyoming.[^1][^2] The National Park Service built it in 1939 with help from the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] It was a secondary lookout, put up to see ground that the three main lookouts on Mount Washburn, Mount Sheridan and Mount Holmes could not see.[^1]

The register treats wildfire as a major driver of trail and lookout building in Yellowstone, and it recalls the huge 1910 fires that burned across the West.[^1] Firelookout.com calls the building a stone and frame cab.[^2] The register also carries a 2011 photo of it.[^1]

The sources read do not say who staffed the lookout or name any fire it watched. Captions in the park's slide collection label it a lookout in 1964 and a lookout station in 1975, but they do not say whether anyone worked there.[^3]

A Wyoming listing on firelookout.org marks the lookout as standing.[^4]

The National Park Service trail to the peak is a 9.6-mile round trip from the Cascade Lake trailhead, climbing 1,400 feet in 2.6 miles.[^5] That page covers the hike only. It does not say whether the cab itself is open to visitors, so follow park signs and do not climb the structure unless it is marked as open.
