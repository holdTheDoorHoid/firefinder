The White Oak Mountain tower is hard to pin down, because the sources describe what may be two different structures under one name.[^1]

A Harrison Times item dated October 5, 1928, quoted on a lookout-history page, reported a "48 foot lookout tower" on White Oak Mountain near Waldron, in Scott County, as nearly complete.[^1] A Forest Service station description from 1971 describes a four-legged steel tower with a metal cabin on top, 85 feet overall, on land the state leases.[^1] The station record places it by distance from Clarksville, Ozark and Altus, and gives the county as Franklin rather than Scott.[^1]

The sources do not say whether the 1928 tower and the 1971 station are the same structure. The Scott County heading and the 48-foot figure point one way, while the Franklin County field and the 85-foot description point the other.[^1]

The lookout-history page's entry for the site includes the note "1974: Removed."[^1] It does not say which tower that refers to, and the sources read do not say what happened to the 85-foot tower after 1971.[^1]

Forest Service notices list the summit as a site for radio and phone equipment.[^2] The Ouachita National Forest's project archive lists a White Oak Electronic Site, with a proposed special use authorization for New Cingular Wireless (AT&T Mobility) and another for the Arkansas State Police.[^2] A notice in the same archive describes repurposing an old, disused NRCS tower site on White Oak Mountain as a microwave link between Mt. Magazine and Waldron.[^2] The notices do not say whether that old tower site is the lookout's site, and none says whether any part of the lookout remains.[^2]

Public access to the summit is not described in the sources read. Because Forest Service notices list communications sites on the summit under special use authorizations, a visitor should not assume that the old tower can be reached or climbed.[^2]

[^2]: Ouachita National Forest project archive, USDA Forest Service, https://www.fs.usda.gov/r08/ouachita/projects/archive (accessed 2026-10-10).
