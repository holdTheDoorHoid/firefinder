The Howell Hill lookout near Colfax in Placer County grew out of a dispute between the state and the county. In December 1927 the state forester, M. B. Pratt, asked the county for one thousand dollars toward a lookout near Colfax, and the supervisors refused.[^2] A 1929 notice said a large part of the county was left without lookout coverage as a result.[^2]

The state went ahead in 1930.[^2] A March notice said work would start on a station on McDonald Hill near Colfax. Lions clubs from Auburn and Colfax and other local residents donated time and money to build the road up to it.[^2] The station was erected under District Ranger Al Frost, and a May 1930 notice announced its dedication as the Mt. Howell Lookout Station, on the McDonald ranch near Landers Station.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the state erected a 60-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab in 1930. It says the cab was reworked and a catwalk added around 1957, and that a house built under the Emergency Conservation Work program in 1935 still reflects its CCC origin.[^1]

Ed Hinchey of Colfax was to be in charge of the station after June 1, 1930.[^2] A November 1930 notice said a station called the Landers Lookout would close for the winter, and that that year's fires were spotted much more quickly. The sources do not say whether that station is the same tower.[^2]

Today the register lists the lookout as administered by the Nevada-Yuba-Placer unit of Cal Fire, and it dates the registration to October 24, 2019.[^1] A 2024 hiker's trip report describes a CalFire lookout tower at the top of the hill.[^3] The writer parked below a locked gate at the junction with Mt. Howell Road, and found the buildings near the tower boarded up and apparently disused.[^3] The same report also describes concrete footings from a fire lookout that once stood on Camels Hump, a different summit it visited.[^3] Because the gate is locked, do not go past it. Check with Cal Fire before planning any visit.
