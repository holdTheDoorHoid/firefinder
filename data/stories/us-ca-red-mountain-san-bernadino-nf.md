Red Mountain sits in the southern reaches of the San Bernardino National Forest, southeast of Hemet, in Riverside County, California.[^3][^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 290 and CA 23, at about 4,536 feet.[^1]

The register says the lookout was completed in 1936. Its cab is a 1934 standard Region 5 design, set on a 20-foot steel H-brace tower.[^1] The San Diego-Riverside chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association also says 1936.[^2] The Southern California Mountains Foundation dates the original construction to 1937 and credits the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^3] The sources disagree on the year, so we report both.

The lookout went back into service after a rehabilitation. The register says volunteer hosts brought it back in October 1998, and that it was registered on January 6, 1999.[^1] The foundation says it re-opened in 1999.[^3] The FFLA chapter says the foundation's volunteers operated it, and that the lookout coordinated daily by radio with the chapter's volunteer lookouts on Palomar Mountain.[^2]

The Fairview Fire started on September 5, 2022, near Hemet, and the fire covered 28,098 acres.[^5] The FFLA's 2022 year-end report says the fire destroyed Red Mountain Lookout on 9/8/22.[^4] The foundation and the FFLA chapter both record the loss in September 2022.[^3][^2]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site. The foundation's page still gives driving directions, by Forest Road 6S22, but it does not say whether the road or the site is open today.[^3] So we cannot say whether the site is public. Anyone planning a trip should check with the San Bernardino National Forest before going.
