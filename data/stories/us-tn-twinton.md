The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Twinton Fire Lookout Tower around 1933, as part of the Division of Forestry's new fire-management program.[^1][^2] The tower sits at an elevation of 1,863 feet, just northwest of Threet Road in Overton County.[^1] It is an eighty-foot steel truss structure of the Aermotor MC-39 standard plan, topped by a seven-foot square cab.[^1] Multi-light windows ring the cab and give a 360-degree view.[^1]

The registration form describes a contract with the landowner that let the state use the property for fire detection, reach the tower, and run a telephone line to it.[^1] The form also ticks "Public – State" under ownership, which does not sit easily with that contract.[^1] If the state abandons the property, the land title will revert to its original owners, but the structures stay state property.[^1]

No source we read names who staffed the tower or any fire it watched. The form ends the tower's period of significance in 1971, roughly when Tennessee's lookout towers were largely abandoned for other ways of spotting fires.[^1] It also says the tower has not been altered since it was built.[^1]

As of its 2021 listing, the form gives its current use as an emergency fire lookout.[^1] A radio and cellular tower went up about forty-five feet away in 2014.[^1] The tower was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on June 28, 2021.[^1] The form also gives the name Colliers Rock Lookout Tower.[^1]

A gravel drive, about a tenth of a mile long, leads in from Threet Road.[^1] The sources do not say whether the cab is open to visitors. Because the form lists the tower's current use as an emergency fire lookout, treat the site as a state facility, and ask the Division of Forestry before you go.
