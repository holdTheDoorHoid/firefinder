Baker Point, also called the Baker Lake Lookout, was a lookout site in Whatcom County, Washington.[^3][^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the tower was built in 1935 and that the U.S. Forest Service administered it.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same year.[^2] The register places the site at 2,264 feet, or 690 meters, in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1]

The lookout was abandoned in 1956.[^1][^2] A visitor's account on willhiteweb.com also says the Upper Baker Dam was not finished until 1959, after the lookout had been abandoned. The same account describes a scenic vista over the Baker River valley, with only a tiny view down to Baker Lake.[^3]

A visit described on willhiteweb.com found four footings, lookout debris half buried in moss, and several eye-bolts in the area.[^3] The page does not give a date for that visit, and the writer did not measure the tower, whose height they say is not known.[^3]

The account describes a walk of about 2.5 miles, with roughly 1,000 feet of climbing, from a gated road off road No. 1152. It says it was unknown whether a trail exists.[^3] The register places the site in national forest land, but no source we read says whether it is open to visitors today.[^1]
