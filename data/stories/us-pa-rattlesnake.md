The register dates the original Rattlesnake Fire Tower to 1933. It stood on a rocky ridge in Moshannon State Forest, Centre County.[^1] In July 1933 the state ordered three new 80-foot observation towers, one of them near McCord, and an August 1933 paper described an 80-foot steel tower newly built on the Rattlesnake Pike.[^2]

The register describes that tower as 80 feet tall, with a metal cab measuring 7 by 7 feet, and says it remained in active service.[^1] A February 2017 visit report says the tower was still staffed, at least at times.[^1]

Under the state's late-2010s fire tower upgrades, the original was swapped for an 80-foot steel structure of the same height. The original was taken down, and its stone observer's cabin is still standing on site.[^1] The register gives no exact year for the replacement. A May 2019 WHYY report says 16 new towers were built in Pennsylvania the year before, and most of the new lookouts replaced older ones at the same location.[^3] The report does not name the Rattlesnake tower.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower as US 155, PA 6, on December 15, 1995.[^1]

The register names the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry as the administrator, so check with the bureau before visiting. Do not climb a tower that is closed or fenced.[^1]
