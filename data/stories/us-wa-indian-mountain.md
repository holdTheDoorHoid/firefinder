Indian Mountain Lookout stands on a summit a quarter mile inside Washington from the Idaho border, in Pend Oreille County, where the Forest Service's Priest Lake Ranger District administers it.[^1][^2][^3] The first tower on the site went up in 1933 as a 30-foot pole structure with an L-4 cab.[^2][^3] In 1953 it was replaced by the 40-foot treated timber tower that stands there now, topped by a 14-foot-square live-in cab with a catwalk.[^1][^2][^3]

NHLR says the lookout has been staffed every season since 1933, and a SummitPost photo entry from 2014 says it is regularly staffed during summer fire seasons.[^1][^4] NHLR notes that when the Kaniksu National Forest had more than a dozen active lookouts, Indian Mountain led them in initial detection reports.[^1] The only watch-keeper firelookout.com names is Melvin Haberman, listed for 1967.[^2] The forest appears under two names, Kaniksu and Idaho Panhandle, but both describe the same national forest.[^1][^2][^3]

The 1933 tower was replaced, not abandoned, and the 1953 tower is the one still in use.[^1][^3] The NHLR registered the lookout on August 1, 1995.[^1] TrailChick, last updated in March 2024, says it is staffed in the summer.[^3]

Getting up there depends on the road.[^3] Between July 1 and Labor Day the road usually reaches within a mile of the summit, and a short ridge trail covers the rest.[^3] Outside those dates the road is often gated near 4,200 feet, a step aimed at keeping huckleberries and wildlife habitat healthy.[^3] With the gate closed, the hike is 3.3 miles round trip and climbs 860 feet.[^3] The sources describe a ridge hike for views of Priest Lake, and they do not say whether visitors may enter the cab.[^3]
