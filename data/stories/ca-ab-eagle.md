The earliest public record I could find for the Eagle lookout is Alberta's 2009 map of its lookout towers, which marks a site called EAGLE[^1].

Alberta's history of its boreal lookouts explains that the towers stood on hilltops, lifting the observer above whatever blocked the view[^2]. From the 1920s, steel came in and gradually replaced wood as the material used to build towers[^2]. Some towers were slimmer, up to 18.3 m (60 ft) tall, and capped with a small cupola lookout[^2].

The province counts 127 wildfire lookout sites[^3]. Each site has an observer on duty, watching for fires through the day, usually from April to October[^3].

Alberta's bear-safety guidance describes its wildfire lookout sites as isolated[^3]. Priority sites have permanent electric fencing around the residences, towers, outhouses and generator buildings[^3]. The fence posts carry high-visibility paint, a year-round warning to backcountry visitors[^3].

No public source I found describes what stands at the Eagle site today, or whether the public may visit it.
