Pilot Knob was a lookout tower in Stone County, Missouri, southeast of Viola.[^1] The Central US Lookouts page places it in the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1] A December 1939 report in the Cassville Republican, quoted on that page, says the tower had been erected three years earlier and was 99 feet (30.2 m) tall.[^1] Its ranger in charge, Jess Peterson, a former deputy sheriff, had held the post since the tower went up.[^1]

On Saturday, December 9, 1939, Peterson fell from the top of the tower and was killed.[^1] He was to file his report with the Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Shell Knob by 9 o'clock that morning.[^1] A while after nine, an official from that camp drove out to the tower and found him at its foot.[^1] The newspaper believed he had fainted while climbing the steps.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists a different Pilot Knob lookout in Phelps County. Its entry notes that there was at least one other Pilot Knob lookout in Stone County, which may be this tower; the register does not say so.[^2]

No source we could read describes the site today, so we cannot say whether the tower still stands or whether the public can reach it. Check with the Mark Twain National Forest before you go looking.
