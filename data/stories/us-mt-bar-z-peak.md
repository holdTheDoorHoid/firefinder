In May 1934 the Kalispell Daily Inter Lake reported that a Forest Service crew was constructing a new lookout on Bar Z Peak. The planned tower was 30 feet tall, with a house and living quarters on top, and was to be ready for use that season.[^1]

A 1940 Coast and Geodetic Survey description places the tower on a bare north-south ridge, about three miles south of the west end of McGregor Lake.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the peak's elevation as 6,304 feet.[^2]

The survey description puts the tower in the Cabinet National Forest, while firelookout.com puts the site in the Flathead National Forest.[^1][^2]

A Missoulian item dated July 30, 1936, says Roland James was stationed on the lookout that week.[^1] An October 1940 Sanders County Independent-Ledger item describes a new lookout under construction on Pine Mountain, about 1 1/4 miles from the site of the earlier Barzee lookout, which had only a tent and no regular cabin.[^1]

Firelookout.com lists a camp and a tower from the 1930s and marks the site abandoned and gone.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list also marks it gone and gives it no register number.[^3]

No source describes the ridge today, so what remains there is unknown, and none says whether the site is open to visitors.
