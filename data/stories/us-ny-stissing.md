The Stissing Mountain fire tower stands near the summit in Pine Plains, Dutchess County, New York.[^1][^2] Wikipedia says the Boston Corners CCC camp built the steel International Derrick tower in late 1933, and that the U.S. Forest Service had provided it to the state.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower opened in 1934 and gives 1934 as its build year.[^1] The register lists the height as 86 feet, while Wikipedia gives 79 feet, six inches. I could not settle either the date or the height from these sources.[^1][^2]

The register says the tower served as a fire observation tower from March 1935 through April 1971.[^1] Wikipedia says it was first staffed in 1935, reporting 91 fires and 610 visitors.[^2]

The register says the tower eventually came under threat of dismantling.[^1] Wikipedia instead says it ceased fire lookout operations sometime between 1979 and 1982.[^2] The two accounts do not agree on when the tower stopped working as a lookout.

The Friends of Stissing Landmarks was incorporated in 1989, and the state transferred ownership of the tower to the group that May.[^1] The Nature Conservancy transferred one acre beneath the tower to the group in May 1993, the same year the tower was restored for the first time.[^1] A later restoration started in 2017, with completion expected in 2018.[^1] Wikipedia says the restored tower is open to the public.[^2]

According to the register, the Friends look after the tower and make it available to the public year-round at no charge.[^1] Wikipedia says the restored tower is reached by hiking trails and was open to the public as of 2023.[^2] Check the Friends' current hours before you go.

[^1]: Stissing Fire Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/ny/stissing-fire-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-08).
[^2]: Stissing Mountain, Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stissing_Mountain (accessed 2026-10-08).
