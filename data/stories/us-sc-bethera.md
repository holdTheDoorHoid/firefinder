The Bethera site is in Berkeley County, South Carolina, inside the Francis Marion National Forest.[^1][^4] The lookout that first stood there went up with a ground house in the 1930s.[^2] The register counts it among the several lookouts the Forest Service used for fixed-point fire detection in its early years.[^2]

The tower that stood at Bethera is the 100-foot Aermotor steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab that now stands at Witherbee.[^2] In 1952 it was moved to the Witherbee Ranger Station on the Francis Marion National Forest.[^2][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and the Forest Lookouts site both give 1952 for the move.[^1][^4]

According to the register, only the footers are left at the Bethera site.[^1] The register gives no date for that remark, and we found no newer description of the site.[^1]

The tower itself is the one now known as the Witherbee lookout. The Forest Service used it actively until 1989, and the register listed it on December 1, 1998.[^2] A news story, which our copy does not date, says the Witherbee tower is one of two Forest Service lookouts in the Francis Marion Forest that are still standing.[^3]

The register lists the Witherbee station as a Forest Service site, and it does not describe public access to it.[^2] Please ask the Forest Service before going near the station, and do not climb the tower.
