Mount Bonaparte has had someone watching for smoke since about 1904, when a lone watchman camped on the summit with just a tent for shelter.[^2] A permanent cabin followed in 1914, a 12-by-12-foot log structure with a pyramidal roof and a small viewing platform, designed by Forest Supervisor C.C. Reed.[^2] It was the first permanent lookout built on the Colville National Forest, and it's still there today, now used as a storage building.[^1][^2][^3]

A taller lookout went up next to the cabin around 1930, with a crow's-nest cab perched on its own tower, and it served through 1960.[^2] The structure standing today, a 15-by-15-foot flattop cab on a 20-foot timber tower, was built in 1961 (some sources say 1963) and remains in service.[^3][^1] It received a round of maintenance in the 2000s that added a hip roof.[^3]

Named lookout staff recorded over the decades include Sidney Swain in 1923, B. Forrester in 1929, and William Foote in 1952.[^3] The Forest Service kept the tower staffed every fire season for decades, and according to Wikipedia that continued under Forest Service control until 2025, when staffing shifted to volunteers.[^2] The surrounding area has come close to burning more than once; the 2021 Walker and Spur Mountain fires reportedly approached the summit without reaching it.[^2]

Both the 1961 tower and the original 1914 cabin are listed together on the National Historic Lookout Register, registered February 5, 1996, and the 1914 cabin on its own is separately listed on the National Register of Historic Places.[^1][^2] The lookout sits at roughly 7,259 feet in the Okanogan Highlands of north-central Washington, reachable by trail #306 after a hike up from one of several trailheads around the mountain.[^2] On clear days visitors can see the North Cascades, Chopaka and Baldy mountains, the Colville Reservation, and, at the horizon, Mount Rainier.[^2] It is not set up as a rental cabin; it remains an active or formerly active Forest Service lookout open to hikers who reach the summit.
