The Harris Mountain lookout stood on Mount Harris in Dixmont, Penobscot County, Maine.[^1][^2][^3]

The U.S. Forest Service first put up a wooden tower on the Allegheny National Forest in Pennsylvania.[^1][^2] In 1940 the Maine Forest Service took the tower apart, bought it and moved it to Harris Mountain.[^1][^2][^3] The register describes a live-in cab measuring 14 x 14, with a catwalk.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page names no lookouts and no fires for this tower.[^3] A 1943–44 forest commissioner's report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, describes a new 41-foot tower started on Mount Harris in 1944 and not due until 1945.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page does not say whether that tower is the one that stood until 2006.[^3]

After years of vandalism and decay, the Maine Forest Service removed the tower on July 26, 2006.[^3] The register also gives 2006 as the year it was removed.[^1] A commenter on the register picked up the remains that August and drove them to the Pennsylvania Forest Fire Museum that October.[^1] The remains included a six-foot section of stairway, one set of leg bolts and bushings, and a telephone line insulator.[^1]

In its 2007 survey report, the Forest Fire Lookout Association said Harris Mountain "has not been forgotten."[^4] The association had helped the Dixmont Historical Society include a section on the lookout in a new history book for its centennial.[^4] None of the sources we read says what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
