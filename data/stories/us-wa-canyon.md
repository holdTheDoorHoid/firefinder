The Canyon Lookout stood in King County, about 9 miles northwest of North Bend, Washington.[^2] The register gives the site's elevation as 1,403 feet, but Willhite gives the summit elevation as 1,422 feet, so the sources disagree on the height.[^1][^3]

The register says the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR administered the site.[^1] Its only description is that the tower was built in the 1930s.[^1][^2] Willhite notes that the Kresek inventory reports a tower there in the 1930s, and he says more information is needed.[^3]

Willhite suggests the site may be the High Point Lookout named in a 1948 Mountaineers annual trip report. He writes that this is most likely the Canyon lookout, but notes that Tiger Mountain is also in the region, so the identity is not certain.[^3]

Both the register and firelookout.com list the tower as gone.[^1][^2] Willhite points to a 1944 aerial with a road climbing to the summit, but says forest canopy hides any tower.[^3]

Willhite says the summit, which he calls the highest point on Grand Ridge, is private property, and that several homes stand on top.[^3] The road is marked as a private drive, though Willhite encountered no trespassing signs, and it becomes private at a sign reading End of County Maintenance.[^3] He also notes that much of Grand Ridge is now a county park, but the highest point is not part of that.[^3] Willhite lists the access as paved and private.[^3]
