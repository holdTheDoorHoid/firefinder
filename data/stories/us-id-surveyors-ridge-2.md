The first lookout on Surveyors Ridge, on the St. Joe National Forest in Idaho's panhandle, went up in 1931.[^1][^3] It was a modest structure: a gabled L-4 cab set on a 20-foot pole tower.[^1] That original tower was replaced in 1964 with the lookout that stands today — a 15-by-15-foot R-6 flattop cab with a catwalk, mounted on a 20-foot treated-timber tower.[^1][^3] Sources differ slightly on the tower's exact height: the National Historic Lookout Register and firelookout.com both describe a 20-foot timber tower, while recreation.gov lists the lookout as sitting atop a 30-foot tower.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout overlooks the Mallard-Larkins Pioneer Area and remained in active fire-detection service for decades.[^1][^3] Detail on exactly when staffing ended is thin in the available sources, but by the time firelookout.com's account was written, the tower was described as being on "emergency status," a designation Forest Service lookouts often carry once they're no longer staffed full-time but are kept ready for use during high fire danger.[^3] Two other patrol lookout sites once stood nearby — one about a mile and a quarter to the northeast, another two and a half miles east atop Surveyors Peak — though no detail survives here on when those were abandoned.[^3]

The tower has since been added to the Forest Service's recreation rental program and is listed on the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1][^3] It's reached by a narrow one-lane road dotted with pullouts; the Forest Service warns against attempting it in a low-clearance vehicle, and wet conditions can make it slippery.[^2] The cabin rents as a non-electric site with phenomenal views of Snow Peak and the surrounding mountains of the Idaho Panhandle National Forests.[^2]
