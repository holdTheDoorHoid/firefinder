Price Peak stands about ten miles north-northwest of Corvallis in Benton County, Oregon, at an elevation of 1,920 feet, and the Oregon Department of Forestry is listed as its agency.[^1] One listing dates a crow's nest on the hilltop to the 1930s.[^1]

In 1942 the site was proposed as an Aircraft Warning Service post, to fill a gap that the Fourth Fighter Command had asked to have covered.[^2] The district warden who inspected the land that August recommended a ground station rather than a tower.[^2] He noted that an 80-to-100-foot tower would be needed only if the site were used for fire detection.[^2] Approval followed in August for a 16x18 ground house, modeled on the regular garage but used as living quarters, plus a 10x12 storage building and a woodshed for about six cords of wood.[^2] A September memo called a portable building fully acceptable and named a standard 18x20 AWS building as the substitute if that could not be obtained.[^2] One listing calls the site an AWS cabin.[^1]

The page gives the activation date as December 7, 1942, under the Eugene Filter Center.[^2]

The site was later abandoned, according to the same listing and the lookout directory.[^1][^3] A status line on the Oregon Lookouts page reads "Removed."[^2] The crow's nest was reportedly still standing in 1995, but it was not located in 2024.[^1]

No source describes what remains on the hilltop now. Anyone planning a visit should check with the Oregon Department of Forestry about access before going.[^1]
