Knotwell Lookout Tower stood in Phelps County, in the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1] That forest was established in 1939.[^2] A gazetteer that lists the tower as historical places it at about 37.87 degrees north and 91.95 degrees west.[^3]

According to the Missouri Conservationist, fire towers were the best way to detect fires for decades.[^4] A 1941 notice from the Rolla Herald, quoted on a lookout history page, says a Regional Manual had ranked Knotwell as the most-visited lookout in the 9th Region.[^1] The same notice says that on one Sunday the October before, Clarence Wehmeier reported 368 visitors, and 320 on Easter Sunday.[^1]

The lookout history page marks the tower as removed, with no date or method given.[^1] The research found no source that says what, if anything, remains at the site today or whether the public can visit it.
