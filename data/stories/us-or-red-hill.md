Red Hill is in Wallowa County, Oregon, inside the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest, and people have watched for fires from the site since 1917.[^1] The earliest setup was a pole platform with a tent.[^3] By 1922 the lookout was working from a large tree with steps spiked to its trunk, and a climb to a fork near the top gave the view.[^2] A 1924 note describes a new 50-foot tower that replaced that tree as the lookout.[^2]

The dates for the current tower are disputed. Firelookout.com credits a 26-foot pole tower with a 10-by-10 cab in 1936, and says the present 40-foot treated timber tower with an L-4 cab went up in 1947.[^3] The register gives 1949 for the 40-foot open timber tower with an L-4 cab that stands there now.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page for Oregon quotes a Wallowa National Forest history that dates the new tower's completion to the spring of 1949.[^2] The sources agree on the height and cab type, but not on the year.[^1][^3]

Firelookout.com lists known lookout personnel from R.K. Stubblefield in 1936 to Marian Cook in 1971.[^3] The page labels these as known lookout personnel.[^3]

Firelookout.com now calls the tower abandoned.[^3] The register listed the site in July 2022 under US 1679, OR 166.[^1] A visitor in September 2022 found the tower still standing, with a cab that was not in the best shape but a fairly sturdy frame, and the first flight of stairs removed to keep people from climbing.[^5]

Today the Forest Service lists a Red Hill Lookout Viewpoint beside the tower. It is open 24 hours a day and has minimal facilities.[^4] The directions start in Enterprise, run north on Highway 3, and then follow Forest Road 46 and other roads that the Forest Service says are mostly gravel.[^4] None of the sources read gives a current climbing rule, so treat the tower as something to view from the viewpoint, and check current access before you go.
