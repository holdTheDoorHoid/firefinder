Black Mountain's first lookout was a cabin on a short open timber tower, built in 1921.[^1] In 1935 it was replaced by a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^1] The tower that stands today is a 53-foot timber tower with a Region 6 flat-top cab, first built at Faset Peak in 1963.[^1]

The register says the tower moved to Black Mountain in 1975.[^1] A 1975 Bonners Ferry Herald note, as quoted by Idaho Fire Lookouts, describes the move to the new tower in August, but that site's timeline dates it to 1976.[^2]

The Idaho Department of Lands administered the site, and the lookout was last staffed by the department in 1997.[^1][^2] The register notes that the lookout survived vandal damage to its tower legs.[^1]

In 2019 the tower was moved about half a mile east-northeast of its original site.[^1][^2] The Forest Service took it apart and rebuilt it at a new site, after communication towers and electronic equipment had built up around the old one.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's northern Idaho and Montana chapter supplied materials to refurbish the relocated lookout that year.[^3]

The Boundary County historic group calls the tower complete and says it is awaiting inclusion in the Forest Service recreation rental program.[^4] Campflare, however, lists it as not currently available for rental and says it needs restoration work before it can open to the public through the rental program.[^5] A work party was scheduled for July 20 to 22, 2025, to build a pit toilet modeled on the historic outhouse and do general maintenance.[^4] The tower stands, but it is not open to visitors yet, and the sources disagree about how much work remains.[^4][^5]
