Groundhog Mountain sits on the Blue Ridge Parkway in Patrick County, Virginia, at about 3,042 feet.[^1] The tower on top is a large log observation tower, about 20 feet high, styled after an old tobacco barn.[^1][^2]

The dates do not agree. The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1935, and describes the tower as built in the 1930s.[^1] The National Park Service says the Virginia State Forest Service first erected it in 1942, and a photo caption on Wikimedia Commons gives the same year.[^2][^3] A French Wikipedia entry also places it in the 1930s.[^4]

Whoever built it, the job was watching. The tower was used to look over the surrounding mountains for hazards and fires.[^2]

The register listed the tower on September 3, 2014, nominated by Kristin Scholetzky.[^1] The National Park Service now administers the site.[^1]

Today the tower still stands, and the Park Service runs the spot as a picnic area with 28 picnic sites and parking for cars.[^2][^5] Visitors can climb one flight of stairs to the top and see Buffalo Mountain and Rich Hill.[^2] A Virginia lookout list calls the tower two-story, while the Park Service mentions only one flight of stairs, so the number of stories is unclear.[^5][^2]
