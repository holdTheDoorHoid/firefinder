The Two-Ocean Plateau is in Teton County, Wyoming, on what was then the Teton National Forest.[^1][^3]

In July 1954, after two weeks of hot, dry weather, the Teton Forest decided it was advisable to man several of its lookouts.[^1] A Jackson Hole Courier item, quoted on a lookout history site, said the Two-Ocean Plateau position was among the lookouts to be manned within the coming week, alongside Huckleberry Mountain, Baldy Mountain, Hawks Rest and Deer Creek Point.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wyoming list places the plateau among its unknown/undocumented sites, with no coordinates. It says a site moves to its lookout lists only if evidence shows regular fire-lookout use.[^2]

The Teton National Forest is now part of the Bridger-Teton National Forest, after the Bridger and Teton forests were consolidated on July 1, 1973.[^3]

No source I could read describes what, if anything, remains on the plateau today, or whether a visitor can reach the spot.
