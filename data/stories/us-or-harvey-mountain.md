Harvey Mountain, in the Willamette National Forest in Lane County, was planned as a fire lookout in 1933.[^1] In June that year, Perry A. Thompson, the forest supervisor, said three new lookout houses would be erected on forest peaks that summer and that the building materials had already been sent out.[^1] One of them was to stand on Harvey Mountain.[^1]

A July 1933 announcement tied the work to the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] It described three fire lookout houses planned for the upper McKenzie River country, one of them on Harvey Mountain, and said construction would begin as soon as the snow melted enough to haul materials up the trails to the summit.[^1]

These items describe plans for 1933, and they are the only records of the mountain's lookout on the page that quotes them.[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, and we found nothing on whether the site is open to visitors.[^1] Check with the Willamette National Forest before planning a trip.
