Mount Tom's summit got its first summit building in 1897, when the Holyoke Street Railway Company put up a two-story pavilion to draw tourists and weekend visitors.[^2][^3] A fire destroyed it in October 1900.[^2] A seven-story replacement with a copper dome opened the next year and stayed popular through the 1920s.[^2] The register places the lookout, first operated in 1914, in the top room of the old Mount Tom Hotel.[^1] The state could use the observation room for fire detection, and the room had 18 powerful telescopes.[^1] That makes the 1901 house the likely home of the lookout, though the register does not say so.[^1][^2]

The rebuilt summit house burned in May 1929, in a fire visible from Hartford.[^2] Wikipedia attributes the fire partly to breakdowns in the building's water systems.[^3] A much cheaper structure was then built beside the old foundation.[^3] Sources disagree on what it was. Estuary Magazine describes a temporary steel building with a dining room, a souvenir shop and an observation tower, while Wikipedia calls it a sheet metal structure.[^2][^3] It was removed when the park closed in the late 1930s, and Wikipedia gives 1939 for its dismantling.[^2][^3]

Today the summit holds a large concrete foundation that carries antennas and transmitters for local radio and television stations, and the foundation is off-limits to the public.[^2] Parts of a boardwalk survive at the former hotel site.[^2] The old summit house foundations are still conspicuous on the mountainside.[^3] The most direct route to the top is a steep 0.8-mile ascent on the New England National Scenic Trail from the MA 141 trailhead.[^2]
