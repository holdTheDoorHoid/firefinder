Grasshopper Peak Lookout stands on Grasshopper Mountain in Humboldt Redwoods State Park, in Humboldt County, at about 3,380 feet.[^1][^3]

The site's history goes back before the tower most visitors see. A photo caption on the National Historic Lookout Register page dates an original structure under construction to 1933 and says the photo was courtesy of the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] A May 1957 clipping, reprinted on a volunteer history site, says a new tower was being built at the site of an older one that had been declared unsafe.[^2] The register, the Mattole Restoration Council and Peakbagging.com all give 1958 as the build year.[^1][^3][^4] A 1967 clipping calls the lookout one of the newer ones in the state and says it was built ten years earlier.[^2] The sources differ by about a year, and the 1957 clipping suggests work began before 1958. The cab is eight-sided, with a catwalk, and was built to a CalFire design on a 29-foot enclosed steel tower.[^1]

A Eureka newspaper reported in May 1957 that Lee Ball, a veteran lookout attendant, had been assigned to the station and would live in temporary trailer quarters from June 3.[^2] In October that year the paper reported he had been relieved of his duties a month early, after early rains ended the fire danger.[^2]

By 2014 the lookout needed work.[^3] The Mattole Restoration Council reported that it was not operational because of a broken water system, rotten deck boards and no radio.[^3] The council then ran a repair project with CAL FIRE and local fire groups. Its stated goal was overnight staffing for the 2014 fire season.[^3] An undated Peakbagging.com page describes the tower as in good shape but not used for fire lookout observation, and says that, with its antennas, it appears to be used mainly as a radio repeater.[^4] I found no source confirming staffing after 2014.

The register describes the lookout as reachable by the public on a trail.[^1] The Mattole Restoration Council describes a seven-mile trail to it, and Peakbagging.com describes hiking or biking a good road of about six miles from the north side.[^3][^4] None of these sources says whether visitors may enter or climb the cab. Check with Humboldt Redwoods State Park before you go, and do not climb the tower if it is closed.
