Breezy Point is a former lookout site in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest in Washington.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register dates it to the 1930s and says the lookout is now gone.[^1] None of the sources we read names who built it or who staffed it.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Willhite's notes describe Breezy Point as a patrol point for the Spencer Butte Lookout.[^3] Fred Barstad, who wrote a hiking guidebook to the Goat Rocks country, said it was used as an emergency fire lookout. It gave a view down into the Lewis River Canyon that the top of Spencer Butte did not, and it was used after lightning storms, when the chance of a lightning fire was high.[^3] Willhite also says a recently found image shows a shed structure at the site.[^3] His party found a pole with an eyehole for the telephone line, which he says confirms a phone line ran to the point.[^3]

The trail, number 30A, leaves the Spencer Butte Trail.[^4][^3] The Forest Service gives its length as 1.2 miles.[^4] Willhite places the junction 0.3 miles from the summit and gives 0.8 miles from there to the point, a figure he attributes to a Gifford Pinchot guide.[^3] His directions follow paved Forest Road 93, about 7.6 miles from Forest Road 25, to a high saddle where there is room to park.[^3]

Today the sources disagree about what is left.[^3][^4] The Forest Service says a fire finder base is still visible, though the canopy hides the distant views.[^4] Willhite quotes the same guide and adds, in parentheses, "since removed."[^3] He describes two tie-down cables anchored in the ground at the site.[^3] The sources do not say whether any of the shed remains.[^3][^4]
