Ohop Lookout stood in Pierce County, Washington, at 2,335 feet (712 meters).[^1][^2]

Lookout-history sites list the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as the agency.[^1][^2] Completed in 1937, the lookout was a timber tower 70 feet tall, fitted with an L-6 cab, and a ground cabin provided the living quarters.[^1][^2][^3] Phone lines went in that same year.[^3]

In 1942 the site was used by the AWS, which was activated on March 9, 1942.[^3] The lookout kept working for fire after that, and the structure stood until 1969, when it was removed.[^1][^2][^3]

WillhiteWeb calls the spot the Ohop Benchmark.[^3] The author was there in 2003, when the views were good after a timber harvest, but the trees have since grown back.[^3] We found no source that describes tower remains on the ground.

Hancock Timber bought the tree farm, which now falls within its Eatonville Forest, and entry there requires a permit.[^3] The route on WillhiteWeb starts in Eatonville and runs about five miles on roads to a gate.[^3]
