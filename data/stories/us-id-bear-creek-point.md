Bear Creek Point Lookout is an L-5 cab in Valley County, Idaho, built in 1934.[^1][^2][^4] Three sources give its size as 10 by 10 feet.[^1][^2][^4] Forest Lookouts describes a two-story, 12-by-12 log cabin instead.[^3]

The sources also disagree about the forest. The register places the site in the Salmon-Challis National Forest, while firelookout.com and Forest Lookouts say Salmon National Forest.[^1][^2][^3] Forest Lookouts also quotes a 1945 survey description of a "Bear Creek Pt." tower and cabin in the Nez Perce National Forest. That may describe a different point with the same name.[^3]

Forest Lookouts quotes a July 12, 1951 Recorder-Herald item saying Charles L. Powell, Jr. of Kennewick, Washington, had taken over a "Bear Creek lookout." The item does not clearly name this site, so it may not be about this cab.[^3]

The cab was abandoned in the 1950s.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register listed it on November 20, 2008, as US 748, ID 50, on the nomination of Jill Osborn.[^1]

Firelookout.com says the cab "is still there," but that page carries no date, so it is the most recent description we found of the cab's condition.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Idaho list marks the lookout as standing.[^5]

The register says the Forest Service administers the site and that it is reached only by trail, inside the River of No Return Wilderness.[^1] Firelookout.com calls it the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness.[^2] The sources do not say whether visitors may go inside the cab, so check with the Forest Service before going.[^1]
