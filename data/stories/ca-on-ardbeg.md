The Ardbeg fire tower stood on a small hill in the community of Ardbeg, where the road meets the railway.[^2] Ardbeg is part of the Municipality of Whitestone, created in 2000 from the Unorganized Centre Parry Sound District.[^2]

Firelookout.ca describes the tower as a steel structure with an elevation of 261 m (856 ft).[^1] Its notes say that Nesbitt's father, Lou, helped to build it and was assisted by four other men. The materials reached the site with a team of horses.[^1]

The record says E.R. Nesbitt manned the tower from 1928 to 1938.[^1] Ontario's towers answered to the former Department of Lands and Forests, which became the Ministry of Natural Resources in 1972.[^3] The record also places the tower on the department's 1963 Tower Detection System Map.[^1]

Wikipedia says the Ardbeg tower was among the final staffed lookouts in Southern Ontario, a role that ended in the early 1970s as aerial detection took over.[^2]

Wikipedia also says the tower was once a popular attraction.[^2] No source here says what, if anything, remains on the hill today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
