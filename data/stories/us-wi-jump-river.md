The Jump River Fire Tower stands in Taylor County, Wisconsin, inside the Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register accepted it on January 2, 2026, as US 1794, WI 51.[^1]

The Register says the tower was built during the occupancy of a Civilian Conservation Corps camp, which sat about half a mile east by southeast of the site.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page gives 1934 as the year a steel tower was erected with CCC labor.[^2] A Wikipedia article on Taylor County says CCC camps, one of them near the current tower, began reforesting parts of the cutover soon after the federal government agreed in the early 1930s to manage the land for the county as National Forest.[^3] A historical marker entry names Camp Jump River among three CCC camps in the Medford area.[^4]

The sources read do not say who staffed the tower or which fires it watched. The firelookout.org listing shows it as standing.[^5]

The Register also notes that a small outhouse about 30 feet from the base remains.[^1] The sources read do not say whether the public can climb the tower, so check with the Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest before any visit.
