Ukonom Mountain Lookout is in Siskiyou County, at 4,535 feet, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1] The register says the Civilian Conservation Corps completed an earlier lookout on the site in 1934, and that it had an L-4 cab on an enclosed timber tower.[^1]

The present lookout dates from 1978.[^1] The register describes it as a modified version of the CL-100 cabin design, on a 14-foot block tower.[^1] The cab itself came from the Lower Devils lookout.[^1]

Local papers name several people who worked the post.[^2] In 1951 John Thomas of Happy Camp went back to work as lookout at Ukonom.[^2] In 1961 Ida Lake was working as lookout in the Ukonom District, and a 1963 item said she had worked the post for a number of years.[^2] In 1971 the Forest Service introduced a lookout-at-home post, which Edith Crawford worked from her summer home.[^2]

The register lists the Klamath National Forest as administrator.[^1] It says the Six Rivers National Forest actively staffs and manages the lookout.[^1] A 1971 item said the public was invited to visit, but the Forest Service warned that the lookout's duties must not be hampered.[^2] I found no current visiting rules or staffing schedule in the sources read, so this story does not suggest going up the tower.
