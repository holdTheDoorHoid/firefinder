Sterling Fire Tower stands on Rough Mountain above Sterling Lake, inside Sterling Forest State Park near Greenwood Lake.[^1][^2] Area fire wardens built it in the fall of 1922 on the Harriman family's Sterling Park estate, and the register describes a 60-foot Aermotor LS-40 with a 7-by-7-foot steel cab.[^1] Older reports call the ridge Sterling Mountain.[^3] A state report written in January 1923 said the erection was still being completed, and a newspaper item from the same month called it a new sixty-foot steel tower.[^3] The register says it was first used in the spring of 1923.[^1]

In its first year the tower logged 137 visitors, and it and its companion, the Jackie Jones tower, spotted 104 fires between them.[^1] By March 1930 a local paper was noting that Peter Sarneckey was beginning his seventh season watching from it.[^3] In 1968 the tower and cabin were improved, and a new access road was bulldozed to the site, with a phone line carried on 38 numbered cedar poles.[^1]

The register gives June 12, 2006, as the date the tower and its observers' cabin were placed on the historic register.[^1] The National Park Service record for the National Register listing gives 7/28/2006 as its publication date, so the two dates do not match.[^2] A radio log dated July 30, 2006, posted by the register, has the Sterling tower being told that a fire in Harriman had passed 700 acres with no control.[^4]

A comment on the register page says the state park refused volunteer help and the tower was left without regular upkeep.[^1] A restoration project under way in 2015 aimed to reopen the tower to the public with a volunteer steward presence.[^5] Work parties replaced all of the tower's lumber in 2017 and repaired the observers' cabin.[^1] The FFLA's January 2022 report covers 2021, when the tower was open 35 days and 16 volunteer stewards welcomed more than 800 visitors.[^5]

The chapter's annual report for 2023 lists Sterling among seven New York towers that are closed while engineering inspections are awaited.[^6] The report adds that in some cases the inspections have been done without a written report.[^6] Its homepage repeats the notice, with no date beside it.[^7]

The tower was still standing in the latest sources read, and several trails lead to it.[^1][^6] The 2023 chapter report and an undated homepage notice list it as closed to the public pending inspection, and they do not say when it might reopen.[^6][^7] Do not climb it while it is closed, and check with Sterling Forest State Park before planning a visit.
