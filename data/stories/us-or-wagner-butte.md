Observers have watched for fire from Wagner Butte since the 1910s, but the sources do not agree on the start date.[^4][^1] The Oregon Hikers page dates a tent camp and an Osborne alidade there to 1913.[^4][^3] The register dates a rag camp to 1915.[^1] By 1916 an Osborne fire-finder sat on the summit, so an observer had to go down over the rocks to reach the telephone, which a forest report called awkward.[^3]

Crews leveled the summit with explosives in 1923 to make room for a cabin.[^3][^4] Ranger Port and J.J. Deadmond went up on October 23 to complete the building.[^3] According to the register, it was the site's first permanent structure, a D-6 cupola cabin finished that year.[^1]

Observers worked the cabin through the 1920s and 1930s, and local papers recorded some of their work.[^3] In 1934 lightning struck the station and set a box of matches alight, causing no damage.[^3] In 1938 an observer reported a fire from the summit, and a crew had it fully out by 9 p.m.[^3] In 1941 lightning struck 500 yards from the station.[^3]

In 1961 the old cabin was dismantled and a prefabricated cab, about 15 feet square, took its place.[^3][^1] Concrete went up by helicopter, and that summer's observer, a junior at Stanford, lived in a tent from late June.[^3]

The 1961 cab came down a decade later.[^1] The register says it was removed in 1971.[^1] firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] Oregon Hikers says smokejumpers deliberately burned it in 1971, once spotting from aircraft had become the more efficient method.[^4] A Forest Service trail page dates the removal to the 1970s and gives the same reason.[^5] The Forest Lookouts history lists the lookout as discontinued in 1964, but no source says when staffing ended.[^3]

Today the summit holds a few reminders. Oregon Hikers reports that the foundations and a metal railing still stand, and a faded sign recounts the spot's history.[^4] No source describes a cabin or other structure standing there now.

The site is on National Forest land, reached on foot by Trail #1011, which begins on Forest Road 22.[^5] The trail page describes it as a pleasant outing near the Rogue Valley.[^5] Oregon Hikers says the final stretch is a brief climb over big boulders to the lookout site.[^4] The trail page says the route stops at the foot of an abandoned lookout, even though the same page says the lookout was removed.[^5] It also says stock are not recommended for now, because a landslide area is difficult to cross.[^5]
