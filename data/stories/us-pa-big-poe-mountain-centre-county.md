Big Poe Mountain, in Centre County, first carried a lookout in 1915, when the state set up a tree observation post about 25 feet above the ground.[^1] The register gives the same year for its tree tower, which it says was still in use around 1917.[^2]

A September 1919 newspaper notice said the State Forestry association had authorized steel observation towers for Pennsylvania's forest fire protection service, that the first would be erected within eight weeks, and that one of the first would go on Big Poe.[^1] A May 1920 notice said the new steel tower on the mountain, on the Kishacoquillas State Forest, was almost complete and would stand 60 feet high. A watchman on its platform, it said, could detect fires over a wide area of the state forests in the Seven Mountains district.[^1]

In June 1921 a severe storm brought lightning that struck the telephone line and the tower, and the tower was demolished. Four telephone poles nearby were splintered, and the observer's cabin was badly wrecked.[^1]

A 1958 National Geodetic Survey station description, quoted on the Eastern US Lookouts page, describes a four-legged steel tower about 80 feet high with a metal cabin, on the highest point of Poe Mountain, 4 miles southeast of Spring Mills.[^1] The survey adds that the station was probably the same as a Poe Mountain fire tower of 1935.[^1]

The sources disagree on height: 60 feet in the 1920 notice, about 80 feet in the 1958 survey.[^1] The two descriptions may fit different towers.

The Eastern timeline lists the tower as removed in 1993.[^1] The register says the site is no longer standing and was added to its list on September 16, 2008.[^2]

Today, no source I found describes what remains on the summit, or whether the summit is open to visitors. A Pennsylvania DCNR event listing for Poe Valley State Park mentions a Watcher's Cabin where a fire tower once stood, but it does not name the tower or give a date, and no source I found ties it to Big Poe Mountain.[^3]
