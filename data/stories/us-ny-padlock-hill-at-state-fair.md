The state Conservation Department chose Blackman Hill in the Town of Caroline, Tompkins County, as the site for a fire observation tower.[^1] Ten CCC boys from Camp SP-125 at Slaterville spent about two months building the steel tower in 1940.[^1] A 2025 FFLA newsletter also dates the tower to 1940.[^2] A 1940 Ithaca Journal item gives its height as 75 feet, and its headline called it the Caroline Fire Tower.[^1][^3]

The tower went into service in April 1941.[^1] A news item from that month calls it the first fire tower erected in the section, gives its height as 68 feet, and names Russell J. Boyer of Slaterville Springs as the spring observer.[^1] The two heights do not agree, and the sources I read do not settle which is right.[^1] Fires spotted from the tower were to be reported to the Slaterville camp, whose enrollees would put out fires on nearby state reforestation land.[^1]

In the early 1980s the tower was moved to the State Fairgrounds and joined a state Department of Environmental Conservation exhibit.[^1] The DEC says the tower is now on permanent display at the New York State Fairgrounds, and that it once stood on Blackman Hill in Potato Hill State Forest.[^4] It is a display now, not a working lookout. The sources I read do not say whether the cab can be entered, so ask the fairgrounds or the DEC before you expect to go up.[^4]
