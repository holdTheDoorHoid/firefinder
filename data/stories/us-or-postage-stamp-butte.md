Postage Stamp Butte sits about 21 miles south of The Dalles in Wasco County, Oregon.[^1] A rag camp was set up at the site in 1927, and a 10-foot wooden live-in tower replaced it around 1932.[^1][^2] Accounts differ after that. The Forest Lookouts history describes a 12-foot timber tower with a 12-by-12 cab, built in 1933 with Civilian Conservation Corps labor.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both describe an 8-foot wooden L-4 tower put up in 1945, with its cab replaced in 1963.[^1][^2] An Oregon Hikers field guide credits the Mount Hood National Forest with building the lookout.[^4]

Some early staff and events turn up in newspaper notes that the Forest Lookouts history reproduces. Jean Frances Frey, who was 18, had been stationed at the lookout since June 1926 while working as a fire dispatcher for the White River district.[^3] In July 1931 a Dufur district ranger reported that water had to be hauled seven miles to the station, the first time in six years.[^3] In September 1960 the station reported the White River #1 fire on September 9 and the White River #2 fire on September 15.[^3]

The state took over the site in 1950.[^3][^4] The sources disagree about 1963. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the cab was replaced then, while the Forest Lookouts history says material from the Lost Lake Butte lookout was salvaged and set aside for this site.[^2][^3] In 1964 that material was bought, pre-cut and sent for treatment.[^3] The 1965 entry says there were no first fire reports from the station that season, and the page then lists the lookout as removed, with no date.[^3] firelookout.com gives 1963 as its date and leaves the end date open.[^1]

Accounts of the site today are thin. The Oregon Hikers field guide says little remains of the lookout, and that an antenna stands on the butte's second-highest summit.[^4] The same guide describes a moderate loop of about 4.4 miles that reaches the spot, and passes on a former lookout's note about numerous large rattlesnakes in the area.[^4] We did not find a land manager's statement on access, so check before you go.
