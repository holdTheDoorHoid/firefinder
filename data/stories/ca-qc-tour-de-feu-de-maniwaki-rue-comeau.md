Quebec's provincial heritage register lists a fire tower, a tour à feu, under the name Tour de feu, in the municipality of Maniwaki in the Outaouais region.[^1] It dates construction to about 1945, describes a metal frame on a rectangular plan, and lists a stair among its projecting parts.[^1] The register places it near 12 rue Comeau, in the La Vallée-de-la-Gatineau regional county municipality, and links the record to the Outaouais built-heritage inventory of 2009 to 2011.[^1]

A few decades ago, Quebec's network counted more than 500 observation towers, and several of them stood in the forests around Maniwaki, according to the city.[^2]

Today, the city says, visitors can climb one of these towers, a newly rebuilt one beside the Château Logue, through the interpretation centre on forest-fire protection.[^2][^3] The city gives that centre's address as 186 rue Principale sud.[^3] A listing for the centre gives its address as 8 rue Comeau, and describes a new observation tower of 24 m (80 ft).[^4] Both sources describe a new tower at the centre, and neither says whether it is the same structure as the 1945 tower on the register.[^2][^4]

No source we read says whether the 1945 tower still stands, what remains of it, or whether the public can reach it.
