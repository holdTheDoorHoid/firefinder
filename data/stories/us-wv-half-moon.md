Half Moon Mountain, in Hardy County, West Virginia, had a fire lookout tower by November 1921.[^1] A newspaper item from that month says John Dellinger manned it, on what the paper called the Shenandoah National Forest.[^1]

The Shenandoah National Forest was established in 1918, and it was renamed in 1932 after the first president.[^2] The registry and a 2011 hiking blog now place the peak in the George Washington National Forest.[^3][^4]

The paper also reported that a carrier pigeon flew into the observer's cab, ate heartily from Dellinger's midday meal, and drank from his canteen.[^1]

The county list of fire towers classes the site as a ground site and marks it gone.[^5] Later visitors describe a tower, though. A 2011 hiking blog found the remains of an old fire tower at the lookout site.[^4] A 2025 trail report saw what appeared to be an old lookout tower's base, a pile of rocks with mortar between the stones.[^6]

The site lies in the George Washington National Forest and is reached by trail.[^4][^6] The round trip in the 2025 report is just over nine miles from a parking area outside Wardensville, West Virginia, and the route starts along a closed fire road.[^6]
