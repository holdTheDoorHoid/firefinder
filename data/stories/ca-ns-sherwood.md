Sherwood was one of the fire lookout towers that Nova Scotia's Department of Natural Resources ran in the Lunenburg area.[^1][^2][^3]

A list of the province's towers on the Maritimes Scanning Site, last updated September 18, 2009, names a Sherwood site and marks it as also an IMRS site, a term the list does not explain. It gives the talk group as DNR Bridgewater.[^1] The list says its names are as used on the radio, as reported by scanner listeners, and that starred names are only tentative.[^1] It also says it is likely that some of the towers it lists were already decommissioned by the time it was last updated, and that the department operates 33 seasonal fire lookout towers across the province.[^1]

A CBC News report, carried by Yahoo, says the towers were taken out of service in 2015, later dismantled, and replaced by aerial surveillance.[^3]

A 2012 provincial news release gave a different timeline.[^4] It said 19 towers would be replaced by aerial detection the following year, and 14 would keep providing primary detection in remote areas.[^4] The two accounts do not agree on when the network ended.[^3][^4]

Randall Freeman worked for 18 years as a fire tower operator in the northern Queens area.[^3] He described overlapping views between neighbouring towers, so that one tower's view reached into the next tower's territory.[^3]

No source I found describes what remains at the Sherwood site today, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
