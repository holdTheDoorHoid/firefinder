Dog Mountain sits in Flathead County, within the Stillwater State Forest, and firelookout.com lists the Montana DNRC as the agency.[^1][^2]

The earliest dated record we found is a January 1925 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page. It describes a steel lookout tower 35 feet high that overlooked the Stillwater basin and was linked by telephone to the state forest warden's office and the U.S. forest ranger's headquarters.[^2] The same item says the tower had proved useful in the fire season before, so it was standing by 1924.[^2]

A 1986 history of the Stillwater State Forest describes a tower built on Dog Mountain, in a passage about early road work, as the state's first lookout. It gives no year.[^3] Firelookout.com instead dates a 20-foot pole tower to 1935.[^1] A 1959 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, describes a windowed wood-frame cab on a four-legged pole frame, with an overall height of 45 feet.[^2]

Firelookout.com lists Charlotte Slack as a lookout in 1952.[^1] The 1986 history also quotes a worker who was hired as a lookout on Dog Mountain, then moved down to packing. He says he was a packer from 1929 to 1933, and the book does not date his lookout job.[^3]

Firelookout.com says the tower was destroyed in 1961.[^1] A 1967 survey note, also quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the tower burned about three years earlier.[^2] The two dates do not match. The same note says the survey station on the mountain has been destroyed.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether it is open to visitors. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a gone tower.[^4]
