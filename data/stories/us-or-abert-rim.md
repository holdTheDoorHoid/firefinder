The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the Abert Rim tower to 1934 and places it close to the south end of the fault rim. It describes a 60-foot steel tower with a ground house.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history gives an earlier start.[^2] The site was picked as an auxiliary lookout station in May 1931, and the tower was completed in 1932.[^2] The labor came from the Forest Relief Program, a work program for people without jobs.[^2] The same history quotes a 1959 report that repeats the 1934 date and marks it as incorrect.[^2]

The heights disagree too. The history quotes the 1932 plan at 60 feet, but its completion note gives 72 feet with a question mark.[^2] firelookout.com gives 60 feet and says the tower was destroyed in 1976.[^3] The Lake County Fire Lookouts blog lists a 72-foot Aermotor LS-40 built in 1932.[^4]

In 1959 the Herald and News reported that a University of Oregon student was manning the lookout.[^2] By 1969 the lookout was used only for emergencies.[^2] A 1972 report listed it as an emergency tower that had not been inspected.[^2]

The register and the Forest Lookouts history both say the lookout was removed in 1976.[^1][^2] The sources differ on how it ended: firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the site as gone.[^5]

The register places the site on Fremont National Forest land in Lake County.[^1] A National Geodetic Survey record, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, describes a survey mark stamped ABERT RIM 1935 set close to the lookout's living quarters.[^2] We found no source that says whether that mark or any other part of the site remains today, or whether the public may visit.
