The Flambeau Ridge tower sits on the ridge of the same name, in the town of Birch Creek, Chippewa County, Wisconsin.[^1] Local people and the state DNR sometimes call the ridge Flambeau Mountain.[^2] It is not the Flambeau tower in Vilas County, which is a separate structure.[^3]

The Wisconsin Conservation Commission built the tower as part of a reforestation program.[^1] It was a 100-foot steel observation tower with a six-foot steel cab at the top. The foundation was in place by October 1931, and the tower was finished the following spring.[^1][^2]

The plan was to keep a watch on duty day and night in dry weather.[^1] By May 1932 a tower man was on the job. That spring he called in fires starting near County Trunk M, by Foster Lake, and fire wardens and firefighters arrived in time to check a blaze that could have turned into a disastrous forest fire.[^1]

In September 1935 the Conservation Commission began moving the tower farther north, to a higher point with a better view of the Mud Brook Valley.[^1] By April 1936 it stood on its new site, ready for the fire-permit season that opened on April 14.[^1]

The summit guide says the tower was still standing in the summer of 2019.[^2] It also notes a 2015 announcement that all Wisconsin fire lookout towers would close in 2016, and that each would be torn down or cut off from public use.[^2] WJFW instead says 72 towers still operating were decommissioned in 2015, so the two sources disagree about the timing.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^5] The summit land is privately owned, and fences keep visitors away from the radio towers there.[^2] Tower Road, about 0.4 mile long, is the only legal route to the summit area.[^2] The guide says the end of the road on the summit area seems to be about as far as one can go without obtaining permission.[^2] We would not climb this tower or go past the fence.
