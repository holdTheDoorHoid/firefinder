The peak lies just northwest of Baldy Village and is also called Old Baldy Lookout.[^1] A June 1915 newspaper item reported that a lookout station had been established on a peak near Mount San Antonio.[^2] The register dates the tower to 1915. It describes a 20-foot enclosed timber tower with six window panels and a high hip roof, in the classic California Region 5 Plan 4A design.[^1] Other sources differ. Wikipedia says the first lookout in the Angeles forest was built here in 1914.[^4] Angeles Adventures says the forest service and a fruit exchange planned a tower for Sunset Peak in 1914, but that Lookout Mountain was chosen as the first location.[^3]

The register notes a suggestion that the peak is the same site as the Fuego Vista Lookout, though it says the two structures appear to differ in height and window details.[^1] A June 1915 item said E.E. Cook was to be stationed at the post, with the sole job of spotting forest fires.[^2] In January 1918 the Arcadia Tribune reported that he spent eight months of each year at the station, and that a windstorm had blown in the glass of his tower.[^2]

Accounts of its end differ. The register says severe weather reportedly damaged the lookout in 1926 or 1927, and that it was dismantled and replaced by Sunset Peak in 1927. Its photo caption calls 1926 the last year in service.[^1] An October 1927 Madera Tribune item said the old station on what it called Baldy Lookout peak was to be dismantled, and that a 20-foot steel tower on Sunset Peak would replace it.[^2] Angeles Adventures says the old tower itself was removed to Sunset Peak.[^3] Wikipedia says it burned in 1927.[^4]

The Sunset Peak tower was reportedly taken apart in 1974 or 1975 and hauled to the Ontario air tanker base. The register for that site says its final disposition is unknown.[^5]

Today, Wikipedia says the concrete piers that Albert A. Michelson used for his speed-of-light measurements from 1925 to 1929 still exist on the peak.[^4] We found no source describing footings or other remains of the lookout itself, and none saying whether the summit is open to visitors today.
