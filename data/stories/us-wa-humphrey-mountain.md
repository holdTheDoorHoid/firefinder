The Humphrey Mountain lookout stood on the eastern part of the ridge, just under the summit, in King County, Washington.[^1][^2] The tower was a 20-foot L-4 design built of wood.[^1][^4] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as the administering agency.[^1]

The register and firelookout.com both date the tower to 1935.[^1][^2] Eric Willhite suspects it went up in early 1934, and panoramic photos taken from the lookout on August 8, 1934 show it was already standing by then.[^3][^4] From the tower, the eye could sweep east across most of the railroad route running from Stampede Pass, but higher ground on Humphrey Mountain restricted the view west.[^4]

A cabin for the Aircraft Warning Service went up on the summit in 1942.[^1][^4] The Forest Lookouts page lists the site as activated for the Seattle Filter Center on September 3, 1942, and deactivated on October 16, 1943.[^3]

Fire destroyed the tower in 1973.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page says it was removed by burning that year, and Willhite says it burned to the ground.[^3][^4]

When Willhite visited in 2015, he found all four weather-station posts, and one was still upright.[^4] His photos show footings and foundation blocks, and the hillside is scattered with cans, bottles, boards and wire.[^4] He wrote that a visitor could pass the spot without seeing any sign that substantial structures had once stood there.[^4] Willhite lists road access as restricted.[^4] No source we read describes public visits, so check with the land managers before going.
