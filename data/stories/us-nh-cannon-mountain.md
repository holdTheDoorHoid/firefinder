In 1951 a steel tower went up on Cannon Mountain in Franconia, New Hampshire, built in association with the aerial tramway.[^1][^4] That year it was first shown at the Eastern States Exposition in Springfield, Massachusetts, then moved up the mountain once the fair ended.[^1]

A 1958 survey team noted a fire lookout and an observation platform up top.[^1] A 1959 description put the tower at about 35 feet tall, built of wood on four legs, which conflicts with the steel of 1951. The record does not settle which it was.[^1]

The tower was taken out of service in 1981 and left on the summit, sheltered among the guy lines of a newer observation deck and radio-television facility.[^1] Willhite's history says it came down in the fall of 2007, too far gone to save.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page gives the removal as 2008.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^3]

As Willhite describes a 2010 survey, all that was left were concrete blocks where the four legs had stood.[^1] We found no source saying whether those blocks survive today. A hiking blog's list marks Cannon Mountain as standing, but gives no detail.[^4] The summit is reached by an aerial tram that runs year round, a trip of about 2.1 miles.[^1] There is no tower to climb; visitors who want the view ride the tram.[^1]
