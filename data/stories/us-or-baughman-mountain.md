Baughman Mountain's first lookout was a crow's nest in a Douglas-fir, which the Douglas Forest Protective Association established in 1925.[^1][^2] In 1934 an L-4 cab went up on a short timber tower. The register gives that tower a height of 10 feet, but firelookout.com describes a 20-foot wooden tower dated to 1933, so the early dates and heights disagree.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1942 the Army asked for a yearlong observation post on the mountain, and the station was active from October of that year until October 1943.[^2] In 1953 a new 50-foot timber tower with an Amort-style cab went up about a mile and a half east, and the old tower was removed.[^1][^2]

The 1969 hunting season left the tower badly damaged.[^2] Vandalism forced the replacement of all the window glass in 1970, and in 1974 Baughman took the most severe vandalism damage of the eleven lookouts that year.[^2]

In 1979 the present steel tower, taken from the dismantled Shivigny Mountain lookout, was installed with a new cabin, and the lookout went back into service early that summer.[^1][^2] The register gives that tower a height of 54 feet 6 inches, while firelookout.com says 50 feet.[^1][^3]

Firelookout.com reports that summer staffing ran into the late 2000s and that the tower is now available for emergency use.[^3] The register says it is in active service, but Cheryl Hill's list of Oregon standing lookouts marks it as unmanned and not accessible to the public.[^1][^4] Please do not climb it.
