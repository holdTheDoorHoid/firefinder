Castle Peak sits south of Rochford, about 25 miles west of Rapid City, in the Black Hills National Forest.[^2][^4] The lookout there went up in 1939 as a wooden tower about 30 feet tall, with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1][^2] A hiking guide dates the tower to about 1937, so the sources do not fully agree on when it was built.[^4]

It was a secondary lookout, not a full-time post. A July 1939 Rapid City Journal item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said it would be manned on and off whenever the fire hazard was great.[^3] The register says it was staffed only in the wake of lightning storms or in very dry spells.[^1]

The register dates the demolition to 1961 and says the remains were strewn along the ridge and burned afterward.[^1] The firelookout page and SummitPost both give 1964 as the year it burned.[^2][^4] Forest Lookouts says the tower was pulled down and its remains spread across the slopes of the summit, with no date given.[^3]

Road 238 leads to the top, and SummitPost says most trips to the summit are planned around it.[^1][^4] The same guide says the summit is on public land managed by the Mystic Ranger District, and it notes that residents along the road have made clear they want trespassers kept out.[^4] A heading on that page reads "Fire Tower Ruins," but its text describes the old tower only in the past tense.[^4] No source we read says what remains on the summit now.[^1]
