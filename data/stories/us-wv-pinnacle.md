Pinnacle Knob sits on the Allegheny Front in Mineral County, West Virginia, inside the Allegheny Wildlife Management Area.[^4] A fire station was already in use there by 1916. A state report from that year places it in Elk District, about eight miles from Barnum, at an elevation of 3,327 feet (1,014 m).[^1] Charles H. Broadwater, the watchman, lived five miles off and returned home nightly.[^1] The station had a single story, and a cabin was also planned so the watchman could stay on site overnight during spells of extreme dryness.[^1] The station was linked by seven miles of telephone line, three miles of which were called the New Creek Line.[^1]

A 1926 Charleston Gazette item, quoted on the same page, mentions the "Pinnacle Knob tower" telephone, served from a line out of New Creek Valley.[^1] A photo captioned as a view from the "rebuilt fire tower" was taken in 1964, which means a rebuilt tower stood there by then.[^2]

Use of the property dwindled after World War II, and it later hosted three communications towers.[^3] According to one photo caption, by 2003 the fire tower had been reduced to its frame, and a large communication tower stood on the summit.[^2] Atlas Obscura says only the foundation remains.[^2] Fellinis says that foundation now serves as a scenic overlook at an elevation well above 3,000 feet.[^3]

The sources do not fully agree about what stands today. A January 9, 2024 account says the writer photographed a "Pinnacle Knob Lookout Tower" perched on the knob, and it also calls the remains of a second fire tower an overlook near three communication towers.[^4]

Getting to the top takes care. A visitor's note on Atlas Obscura advises a four-wheel-drive vehicle for the steep final stretch of South Tower Lane.[^2]
