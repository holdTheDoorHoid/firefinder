Hall Mountain is in Boundary County, Idaho.[^1] It sits on the Kaniksu National Forest, about seven miles east-southeast of Porthill.[^2]

The site's first structure, a cupola cabin, was built in 1927.[^2][^3] A 40-foot pole L-4 tower followed in 1934 or 1935, depending on which site you read.[^2][^3] In 1956 a treated timber tower with an L-4 cab went up, by one account a mile south-southeast of the summit.[^2][^3] The 2001 sale notice calls that tower a 1957 lookout.[^4] Sources give its height as 35, 40 or 41 feet.[^2][^3][^4] A platform tower also stood on the summit.[^2][^3] One source gives it as 25 feet and the other as 35 feet, and no date is known for it.[^2][^3]

Two sites list the people who staffed Hall Mountain from 1933 to 1981.[^2][^3] Fred Danquist is named for 1933, and Mr. and Mrs. Don Goff for 1950.[^2][^3] A 1940 entry records a lookout who moved partway through the season.[^3] One name has no year attached.[^3]

In 2001 the Forest Service offered the lookout for sale for off-site removal, with bids opening that June.[^4] A buyer, Gary Webber, bought the 35-foot tower and moved it.[^3][^4] Another site credits Gary Weber of the Forest Fire Lookout Association with removing it that year.[^2] Hall Mountain has no tower today.[^1]

A visit described in a post dated August 21, 2023 found most of the cement footings left by the various towers, plus a flagpole and a fair amount of debris that the writers believed dated from the right period.[^3] The summit platform was gone.[^3] The writers also described the drive to the location as a very easy one.[^3] No source we read describes the site after that visit, and none says whether the land is open to visitors, so check with the Kaniksu National Forest before you go.
