Quebec's provincial heritage register lists a fire tower called the Tour de feu, in the municipality of Blue Sea in the Outaouais region.[^1][^2] It dates the construction to about 1945 and describes a metal frame.[^1] The register places the tower near the north end of a road called the chemin de la Tour.[^1]

A 2016 outdoor-magazine article puts the old tower at the summit of Mont Morissette, 400 m (1,312 ft) above sea level.[^2] The writer calls it an authentic steel fire tower and one of the few survivors of its kind in the Gatineau Valley.[^2] The same article notes that in 1941 Quebec counted 416 wooden fire towers and 48 steel ones.[^2]

At the foot of the tower, information panels tell visitors about the watchmen who once kept watch there, and about their replacement by aerial patrols.[^3]

A 2021 newspaper piece says the tower is preserved and maintained to show how forest fires were watched and controlled years ago.[^3] It adds that the displays also tell the story of the last fire tower in the Gatineau Valley still in place.[^3]

The regional park is free to enter, and parking is free as well.[^2] Its trail network runs to about 13 km, and the 2016 article describes a short but intense 2.1 km climb to the summit.[^2] The 2021 piece says the tower can be reached on foot along the trails, or by car or quad.[^3]

A newer observation tower, 18 m (59 ft) high, stands beside the old one.[^2][^3] From its top, visitors get a 360-degree view and can see the old tower from above.[^3]
