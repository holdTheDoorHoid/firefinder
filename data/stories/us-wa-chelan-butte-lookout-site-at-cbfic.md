Chelan Butte, about two miles south of Chelan, Washington, held a Forest Service lookout from the 1930s.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree about the first tower. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and FireLookout.com both say a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab went up in 1936. It later burned, and a 28-foot treated timber tower replaced it in 1939.[^1][^2] The National Register nomination, written in 1988, says a Civilian Conservation Corps crew built the road, tower and cabin in 1938 under Sim Beeson, and says nothing about an earlier tower.[^3] We keep both dates on the record.

FireLookout.com lists Mac McFadden as a lookout from 1938 to 1940.[^2] The nomination says it served for several years as a main fire-watch station across two national forests.[^3] As of the nomination, it had gone several years without fire surveillance.[^3] The register says it was staffed sporadically into the 1990s.[^1] Those two accounts do not match neatly.

The National Register listed the lookout on December 27, 1990.[^3] In 1995 the tower was moved to the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center, roughly a mile north of Entiat.[^1][^2] The nomination says its original furnishings had been removed and were reportedly in Forest Service storage.[^3]

Today the lookout stands at the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center, just north of Entiat.[^4] The center says the interior is open only during its events or by special arrangement.[^4] The Washington Trails Association describes three short trails at the center and lists no entry fee.[^5]
