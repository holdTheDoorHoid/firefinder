Catamount Mountain, near South Colton in St. Lawrence County, first carried a fire tower in 1911.[^2][^5] That first tower was a wooden structure shaped like a log-framed pyramid, with no cab on top.[^2][^5] In 1917 the state replaced it with a 35-foot steel Aermotor LS40 tower.[^1][^4][^5]

A new observer's cabin went up in 1925.[^3] A state chapter newsletter notes that its author could find no photos of it.[^3]

In 1942 a fire near Mud Lake threatened wide destruction. The observer had a telephone, but the men deep in the forest did not, so he could do little to help the crews below.[^1] The telephone company then put a phone on a service truck, and the district ranger could call the tower from there to direct the men.[^1] At the height of the battle more than 200 fighters were at work.[^1]

In 1950 William McDonald returned to the tower as a fire spotter.[^1] In 1966 Miles McCarthy, the observer on the tower, spotted a blaze in the woods south of South Colton. The fire, of unknown origin, started when a hunting camp burned to the ground.[^1] McCarthy was still at his post in 1969.[^1]

The state closed the tower at the end of the 1971 fire season.[^4][^5] A 2018 blog gives 1970 instead.[^6] The tower was removed in the early 1980s.[^4][^6] The Explorer says the cabin was removed too, by the early 1980s.[^5]

Today the Explorer's writer found the tower's foundations on the summit.[^5] The foundation of the observer's cabin now makes a bench with a view north.[^5] The same article notes a steel building, spiraling cables and telephone poles near the top, and says the New York Telephone Company built a relay station with an 80-foot antenna on the mountain in 1946.[^5] A 2018 blog says the fire tower is no longer there.[^6]

The trail network is on private property, but it is open to the public.[^5] Visitors park near the lodge, where a map sits in a box by the fence.[^5] The Explorer describes the 2.5-mile out-and-back as an easy two-hour hike with about 420 feet of climbing.[^5] Hikers who reached the summit in later years, while an observer was posted, may have received a card signed by the observer, courtesy of the state Conservation Department.[^5]
