Pilot Peak Lookout stands at 6,011 feet in the forest's southern section, on the boundary between Tuolumne and Mariposa counties.[^1][^2] In 1919 a Sonora contractor, Henry Grayson, was building a steel lookout tower on the peak, according to a Mariposa Gazette item quoted on the California Lookouts site.[^2] The sources do not say whether that tower is the one standing today. The Register dates the present cabin, a CL-100 design on a 20-foot open steel tower, to 1962-63.[^1]

According to the Register, the peak has served for fire detection at least since 1921.[^1] That summer the lookout, John Hastie, reported 63 visitors between June 6 and August 31.[^2] In July 1937 a lookout named M. Brown spotted a barn fire in the Deer Flat district near Groveland, and a CCC crew held it to three acres of grassland.[^2]

A 2012 annual report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Sierra-Nevada chapter says Pilot Peak was one of two Groveland district lookouts still in active use for fire detection, and that a Forest Service employee staffed it seven days a week.[^3] The same report says the district was upgrading the electrical system with solar panels and batteries, had installed a 1,200-gallon water tank with a shower, and had cut fuels around the site.[^3]

The Register lists the Forest Service as administrator and gives a registration date of December 1, 2019.[^1] Do not confuse this with the other Pilot Peak. The Plumas National Forest lookout that the Register numbers 600 is a two-story hexagonal cab, built in 1976.[^4]

The newest staffing or condition report I found is from 2012, so the lookout's present condition, staffing and public access are unconfirmed. Neither source I read gives a route for visitors.[^1][^3]
