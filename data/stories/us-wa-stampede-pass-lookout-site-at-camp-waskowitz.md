The Stampede Pass lookout went up in 1934 as a 40-foot timber tower with an L-4 cab, on a small peak about three-quarters of a mile southeast of the pass.[^1][^2] From the cab, lookouts saw the Green River valley off to the west and the Keechelus valley off to the east.[^2] A smaller 20-foot L-4 tower also stood about a mile north for a time.[^1]

In 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service used the site. The Forest Lookouts Washington page gives its activation as August 14, 1942 and its deactivation as September 15, 1943.[^3][^2] A ground cabin was added as well, since the tall tower proved chilly and noisy in windy weather.[^2]

In 1974 a helicopter flew the tower to Camp Waskowitz, a conservation camp near North Bend, where it was meant to stand as a display.[^1][^2] When plans to destroy it came up, North Bend ranger Ken White arranged the airlift.[^2] Accounts differ on what flew. The Forest Lookouts page says the cab was airlifted and mounted on a new tower, while the other two accounts describe the whole tower moving.[^3][^1][^2] The original knoll still has concrete footings; a visitor noticed at least two of them in 2014.[^2]

The camp, also known as Camp Waskowitz, was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in 1935.[^4] Highline Public Schools now operates it for an environmental learning program, and the Forest Lookouts page says the tower is used for educational purposes.[^4][^3] Because it is a school program site, check with the district before any visit, and do not climb the tower.
