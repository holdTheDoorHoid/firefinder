The Lexington Lookout Tower is in Holmes County, Mississippi, and the Mississippi Forestry Commission administers it.[^1][^2]

Notices from The Lexington Advertiser in 1936, copied onto the Forest Lookouts site, describe a steel fire tower that the local CCC camp, the Civilian Conservation Corps, was building on the poor house property north of Lexington.[^2] The tower was meant to help forest rangers locate fires, and it was to have a telephone for sending in reports of fires.[^2] By late August the tower was finished, and county ranger J. R. Brown invited the public to come and look at it.[^2]

The notices say a warden would be chosen later to watch the tower, and that telephone poles were going up to link it with other towers in the area.[^2] The sources we read do not name that warden or any fire the tower spotted.[^1][^2]

The Register lists the tower as an Aermotor LS-40 and gives its height as 90 feet. The July 1936 notice says 100 feet.[^1][^2] We could not settle that gap. We also could not confirm that the 1936 tower is the same structure the Register lists, since the only links are the name and the county.[^1][^2]

The Register gives the build date as unknown. It says the site was once home to the county forester and now holds a house and many other buildings.[^1] It registered the tower on July 9, 2023, as US 1742, MS 59, after a site visit on July 2, 2023.[^1]

We found no record that the tower was moved or taken down.[^1][^2] The sources do not describe its present condition or any public access, so ask the Mississippi Forestry Commission before going to the site.[^1]

[^1]: Lexington Lookout Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/ms/lexington-lookout-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Lexington, Forest Lookouts, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/lexington.html (accessed 2026-10-09).
