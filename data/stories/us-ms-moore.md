The Moore Lookout Tower stands on Scott County Road 503 near Forest, Mississippi, in the Bienville National Forest.[^1][^2] A local contractor working with a Civilian Conservation Corps crew built it in 1940.[^1] The tower is about 100 feet tall.[^2] Its stairs wrap around the outside of the steel frame, unlike the more familiar design with stairs inside, and it tops the frame with a wooden cab.[^3][^2]

The Lookout Register added the tower in February 1997, and the National Register of Historic Places listed it on October 28, 1999.[^3][^2] The National Register nomination calls it a rare, possibly unique example of its design, and the only tower of that type still standing in Mississippi's national forests.[^1]

Its origin is disputed. One lookout history site says the tower was first at the Shockaloo Tower site, and was dismantled and re-erected in Scott County as a dispatch headquarters.[^4] The National Register nomination, though, suggests the design was not completely pre-manufactured and was made for this site.[^1] I could not settle the question from the sources I found.

I found no recent report on the tower's condition or on whether visitors may climb it. The U.S. Forest Service administers the site, with the Bienville Ranger District as a cooperator.[^3] Before planning a visit, check with that ranger district.
