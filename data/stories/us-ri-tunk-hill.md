Tunk Hill Fire Tower stands in the Providence City Watershed, in Providence County, Rhode Island.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 100-foot tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab, and it lists the site at 540 feet above sea level.[^1]

A state annual report from 1935 says the city of Providence had put up the tower and kept a watch at Tunk Hill, cooperating with the state's other towers in that section of Rhode Island.[^2] The register gives the same year for construction and says the tower has served watershed and state fire detection ever since.[^1] A later state report, covering 1952 and 1953, says the Water Supply Board was lent battery-powered sets for use at the tower.[^2]

The sources I could read do not name anyone who worked the watch, and they do not list any fires the tower helped catch, so those parts of its history are still unknown.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] I found no record of it being moved, burned, or rebuilt. The register added the tower to its list on August 6, 1992.[^1]

The register describes the ownership this way: the City of Providence owns the tower, and the Providence Water Supply Board administers it.[^1] The 1935 state report credits the city with building it, which fits that picture.[^2]

Today the register says the public may not enter the Scituate Reservoir lands, though the Water Supply Board can arrange group tours.[^1] I found no public trail, rental, or other visit arrangement. Treat the tower as closed to the public, and ask the Board before planning any visit.
