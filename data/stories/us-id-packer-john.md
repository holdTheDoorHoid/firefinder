Packer John Lookout stood atop Packer John Mountain, a 7,061-foot peak in southern Idaho.[^3] In 1936 the site was owned by the state of Idaho and sat in the middle of a 21,000-acre tract of state forest land.[^3] A 1938 Post-Register photo caption says the Southern Idaho Timber Protective Association supervised the land.[^3]

The first lookout here was a two-story log house, established in 1914.[^1][^3] A similar two-story house replaced it in 1929.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts, citing Kresek, gives that second building as 20 by 20 feet and laced log.[^3]

In August 1924 a fire burning over state land threatened the station. Smoke and gas took the lookout out of service for a time, while Arthur Storey stayed on the peak to report on the fire.[^3] Other named staff are Mrs. Nell Gyver, listed under 1925 and citing a 1943 Wallace Press Times item,[^2] and Mr. and Mrs. Arthur Snyder, who a July 1941 Payette Valley Sentinel item, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, places at the lookout.[^3]

The sources do not agree on how the station was built. A 1936 Idaho Statesman item, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, says the lookout and its road were built with emergency conservation works funds and civilian corps labor.[^3] A 1938 caption says CCC youths built the station a few years earlier.[^3] These accounts are hard to square with the 1914 and 1929 dates given elsewhere.[^1][^3]

The lookout was demolished in 1980.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts says it was destroyed that year.[^3]

A federal survey description written in 1959 puts the station at the center of the two-story lookout house, about 6½ miles south of Smith Ferry and 2½ miles east of State Highway 15.[^3] We found no source describing what remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
