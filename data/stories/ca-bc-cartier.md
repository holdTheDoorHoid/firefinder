Mount Cartier rises to 2,610 m (8,563 ft) about 13 km (8.1 mi) southeast of Revelstoke, and its fire lookout stands on a ledge along the south ridge.[^1][^2]

The trail and the lookout date from the early 1920s.[^1][^2] Kevin Lavelle, a local forestry official who has researched lookout history, gives 1922 as the build year and thinks the trail and a telephone cable probably went in a year earlier.[^1] The firelookout.ca registry says the tower was "Built by CP Rail," but the Revelstoke Review calls it a forestry lookout and names no builder, so the railway claim is unconfirmed.[^3][^1]

The tower was not used for long. Lavelle wrote that fires could become well established before a lookout this high could spot them.[^1] Wikipedia says the lookout has been out of fire-detection use since the 1930s.[^2] The Review says it is believed to have gone unused after 1930, and that a permanent lookout was set up on Mount Sproat, to the south, in 1956.[^1]

The 2014 Revelstoke Review article found the lookout still standing. The writer passed it just short of the summit, and the article says it has weathered about 90 years of storms, with only its roof being redone.[^1] Wikipedia says it still stands, but no source here describes its condition after 2014, so what it looks like today is unknown.[^1][^2]

Getting there is the hard part. The Review gives two ways up, a helicopter or a 15-kilometre trail, and calls the Cartier lookout the hardest in the Revelstoke area to reach.[^1] Wikipedia describes a 15-kilometre trail that starts at the lake, and says helicopters lift mountain bikers to a helipad below the summit.[^2]
