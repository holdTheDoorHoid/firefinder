Dark Canyon has been used to spot fires since 1910, when the site relied on two tall pine trees fitted with ladders and platforms.[^1][^2] A steel tower that another national forest had condemned was brought in, set up and taken down within a year, and the trees went back into use.[^1][^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps later built the cabin and toolshed that still stand beside the lookout.[^1][^2] The sources differ on the year. The register and the Forest Service say the late 1930s, while a 2020 chapter report says late 1939.[^1][^2][^4]

The present tower arrived in 1949 as a surplus steel tower from the U.S. Army.[^1][^2] It is an Aermotor MC-99 type with a 7 by 7 foot steel cab.[^1][^2] The tower started out 90 feet tall, and the Forest Service used only the top 48 feet.[^1][^2] The Forest Service says the tower is one of just two surviving MC-99 types in its southwestern Region.[^2]

According to the 2020 chapter report, the lookout is used for fire detection on an as-needed basis.[^4] I did not find any particular fire credited to the tower in the sources I read.

The register added the lookout in 2015.[^1] A 2016 federal notice proposed a $65 a night fee for overnight rental of the tower and its cabin, which had not yet been open for overnight use.[^3] The 2020 chapter report said the complex would join the cabin rental program once it was running, and that it had been nominated to the National Register and was in the final steps before listing.[^4]

The Forest Service lists the site as an open observation site.[^2] The register marks the lookout as available for rental.[^1] I did not find a current booking listing, so ask the Guadalupe Ranger District about rental and access before you plan a visit. I also could not find a source that says whether visitors may climb the tower, so please do not climb it unless the district says it is allowed.
