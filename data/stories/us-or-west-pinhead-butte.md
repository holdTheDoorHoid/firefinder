West Pinhead Butte is in Clackamas County, Oregon, in the Mount Hood National Forest, about 30 miles south of Mount Hood.[^1][^2] A tree crow's nest and a rag camp went up on the butte in 1929.[^1]

The crow's nest and camp were replaced in the 1930s by a 20-foot wooden tower.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page is the only source we read that gives a year for the tower, 1933, and it describes a native timber tower with an L-4 cab.[^3]

The only lookout we can name is Stanley Applegate, who held the post in September 1936.[^3] A passage quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, signed A.O. Waha, says he rigged a Zenith wind charger on the lookout roof to run his radio and lights.[^3] Because the telephone was hard to hear from the ground, he connected it to his radio.[^3] His job also included cutting up trees that had been felled to improve visibility.[^3]

The sources disagree on how the tower ended. Firetower.org says it was removed in 1950, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register recorded the site on September 21, 2010.[^1] A lookout list on firelookout.org marks the tower as gone.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the butte today, or whether visitors may go there. Firetower.org carries a 2005 photo of the site, credited to Howard Verschoor, but the page gives no description of the image.[^1] Check with the Mount Hood National Forest before you go, because we could not confirm access.
