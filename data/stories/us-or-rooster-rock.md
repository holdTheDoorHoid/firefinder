Rooster Rock is a large pillar of andesite and basalt in the Menagerie Wilderness, within the Willamette National Forest in Linn County.[^4][^5][^1]

In 1927 a lookout went up near the top of the rock: an 8-by-8-foot frame cab that could be reached by about 100 feet of ladder.[^1][^2] An L-4 cab was added in 1935, built behind and above the original spot.[^1][^2]

A 1934 newspaper item said two Albany men had just come back from building a lookout station on the rock.[^1] In June 1938 another paper reported that the Forest Service had already posted a lookout there.[^1] In 1939 William D. Morgan was the lookout fireman, and the station sat 11 miles by road and 3 miles by trail from Cascadia ranger station.[^1] That April a CCC crew had built a new three-mile trail from Trout Creek camp to the lookout.[^1] A 1948 item said a high school boy would spend the summer manning the station.[^1]

The Oregon Lookouts history records the lookout as abandoned in 1963, and the Forest Service trail page gives the same year.[^1][^4] The Register says it was gone by 1966, while FireLookout.com headlines its page "R.I.P. 1927-1935," so the sources disagree about when it ended.[^2][^3]

The Forest Service still lists the trail to the rock, notes that the rock is popular with climbers, and asks visitors to check current conditions at the local ranger station.[^4] A 2017 hiking report describes an opening on the ridge where the lookout's base cabin used to stand.[^6] A hiking and climbing site says nothing really remains of the old lookout at the summit, so what visitors find today is the rock itself rather than a standing lookout.[^5]
