The Bell Knob fire tower stands on a ridge in the Monongahela National Forest, in Grant County, West Virginia, near Dolly Sods.[^1][^5] The National Historic Lookout Register says it was first built on the Rohrbaugh Plains Trail in 1931 and moved to Bell Knob in 1943.[^1][^3]

The first tower was a 30-foot steel structure, completed in 1931.[^4][^3] In 1943 the Army's 13th Corps covered the cost of taking the tower down and moving it several miles north. At the new site it helped spot wildfires on the Army's live-fire range, land that is now Dolly Sods Wilderness.[^4]

The Register describes a wooden cab on 30-foot steel legs.[^1] A 1956 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description quoted on the Forest Lookouts page says the structure is about 70 feet high. We could not settle which figure is right.[^2]

The Register's August 2020 visit report asks whether the tower is staffed, and the answer recorded is no.[^1] In October 1976 a Forest Service employee who handed out wilderness permits at the tower told a newspaper that a windy morning had left her afraid the tower would blow away.[^2]

The Register says portions of the cab had collapsed before October 2015, and that the cab platform, steel legs and stairs remain.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page dates the cab collapse to 2010, under the weight of a record snowfall. A 2013 travel post says a record snowfall blew off the top that year.[^2][^5]

The Register's visit report found the stairs manageable only for the first flight, and the top still locked.[^1] The 2013 travel post says the tower is reached on foot by a grassy road off Forest Road 19, and warns that it may be unsteady. The same post says the top was boarded shut.[^5] We do not encourage anyone to climb it.

The Register listed the tower on November 27, 2016, as US 1148, WV 16, nominated by Brian M. Powell.[^1]
