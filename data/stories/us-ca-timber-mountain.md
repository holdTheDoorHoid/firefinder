Timber Mountain Lookout sits on a summit in the Modoc National Forest in Modoc County, California, at about 5,071 feet (1,546 meters).[^1][^3] Its history reaches back to 1912, when the spot was approved for administrative use.[^2] The register says a platform lookout may have stood here as early as that year, and that an L-4 or Region 5 BC-301 design later replaced the platform.[^1]

The cab that stands now was completed in 1966.[^1] The register describes it as a CL-100 design on a 30-foot steel tower with K-bracing.[^1] A Forest Service budget form dated November 1944 had already described the lookout then in use as having no living quarters underneath.[^2]

Early records name a few of the people who worked the lookout and the fires it watched.[^2] A newspaper item from August 1932, quoted by a lookout history site, names Otto W. Klosterman as the lookout on the mountain.[^2] In August 1938 a fire spread over about 6,000 acres of grass, sage and juniper, and radio contact was established between fire fighters and the lookout so the flames could be watched more closely.[^2] In October 1945 a new lookout with little experience, filling in for the former lookout who had quit, spotted a fire in the dark. He waited for daylight, and the Damon's Butte Road Fire was first reported from Timber Mountain at 6:05 a.m. on October 2.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on October 23, 2019, as US 1333, CA 156.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list shows it as standing.[^3] The sources I could read do not say whether anyone staffs it today or whether visitors may climb it, so check with the Modoc National Forest before planning a trip.
