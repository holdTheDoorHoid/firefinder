Manzanita Mountain is in Santa Barbara County, in what is now Los Padres National Forest.[^1] In 1936 a local paper called the same forest the Santa Barbara National Forest.[^1]

The lookout was already in use by June 1935.[^1] A newspaper that month reported that a man from Paso Robles had been sent to it, and that a ground telephone linked the station to Pine Canyon, where three men rotated shifts to keep radio and telephone contact going.[^1]

A June 1938 newspaper item noted that 13 steps led up to the lookout tower.[^1] Conrad Munson wrote a daily report from the station in 1936, and Warren Rice was its lookout in 1937.[^1] In October 1937 lightning struck between the station and a nearby flagpole.[^1]

Bert Feehan, a Forest Service clerk from the Santa Maria office, took up his lookout duties on June 16, 1938.[^1] New radio and telephone links were set up at the station that same day.[^1] The paper said it was the last station in its district to open for the season, and that it completed a quick-communication chain for responding to fires.[^1]

Feehan finished his lookout work in November 1939.[^1] He was the first staff member released as the Forest Service started trimming its protection staff that month.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association now lists the tower as gone.[^2]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site, or whether the public can reach it. Before going, ask the Los Padres National Forest about access, and do not climb any structure you find there.
