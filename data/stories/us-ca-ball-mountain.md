Ball Mountain Lookout stands on Ball Mountain in Siskiyou County, on the Klamath National Forest, at 7,789 feet.[^1] The site falls within the Goosenest Ranger District.[^3]

The register says a lookout stood on the site from 1922 or earlier.[^1] Newspaper items from that period do not agree on the name. A June 1922 notice says Jack Hayes was to take up his duties on "Goose Nest mountain," but an October 1923 item places him at the station on Ball Mountain, which was closed for the winter.[^2]

Staffing was seasonal. A forest history entry for 1928 lists James Dysert to man the lookout only in emergencies.[^2]

Land was a question too. A July 1935 survey found the old tower on leased land.[^2] The Shasta Forest later bought the whole section, under a deed dated September 26, 1936, for $500.[^2]

The tower was replaced in 1940. An October 1940 Leaf CCC Camp report says work on a thirty-foot replacement tower was well advanced, with 75 percent expected to be finished before the winter shutdown.[^2] The register says an open platform tower with a BC-201 cabin was replaced that year by a Region 5 BC-301 cabin on a 30-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1]

The heights do not agree. A 1960 recollection describes a boxy structure about 60 feet tall, with a Jacob's ladder as its fire escape.[^2] The register and the 1940 report both give 30 feet. A 1952 survey description gives the lookout house as 14 feet square, a width rather than a height.[^2]

The register added the lookout on November 24, 2019.[^1] In June 2024 the Forest Service signed a decision to add two wildfire cameras and a solar array at its communications tower on Ball Mountain.[^3] The project record lists the work as completed, but does not say whether the historic cabin still stands.[^3]

I found no public visiting information. Treat the site as Forest Service land with a communications tower, and do not climb anything there.
