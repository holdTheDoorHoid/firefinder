The National Historic Lookout Register dates the first lookout on the mountain, inside the Coronado National Forest, to 1913.[^2] It was a wood lookout on a 30-foot wood tower, built near the summit at about 9,140 feet.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree about how early fire lookouts began. A local history PDF says a fire lookout has existed on the mountain since just after the Santa Catalina reserve was created in 1902.[^1] A historical marker at the later Lemmon Rock site says the general locale has served as a fire lookout site since the Coronado Forest Reserve was set up in 1902.[^3]

A 1915 newspaper item, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, said the Forest Service would run four lookout stations, manned full-time by men who could telephone in reports. Mount Lemmon was one of the four.[^4]

Named lookouts appear in later newspapers. Orin Western was stationed on the mountain in 1925, and in 1926 W. Kelly and Manuel Henderson were stationed at the lookout for the summer.[^4]

In May 1925 the lookout reported a fire near Stafford, in the Mount Graham district. The lookout called it a large one, and the paper said no details on its size had come in yet.[^4]

In 1928 the summit tower was replaced by a new lookout on Lemmon Rock, just south of the summit, where the view was better.[^1][^2] The marker there says the building follows 1920s standard plans, and it calls the lookout the oldest working one on the Forest.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Mount Lemmon crow's nest as gone.[^5] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.

Lemmon Rock is still standing, according to the association's list.[^5] A local history PDF says it still sees active use when fire danger runs high.[^1] In June 2015 Coronado forest officials closed the lookout to visitors. The trail stayed open, but the lookout structure was off limits.[^6]
