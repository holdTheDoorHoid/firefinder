The Mt. Roosevelt Fire Tower stands in Roane County, Tennessee.[^1] The state wildlife agency says its Roosevelt Mountain wildlife area virtually surrounds the town of Rockwood.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates its construction to between 1933 and 1937 and describes a five-landing International Derrick and Equipment Company tower.[^1] A Tumblr post says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1932, but the post names no source for that date.[^3] The city of Rockwood says the tower is about 92 years old, which fits the Register's range better than the 1932 date.[^4]

The Forest Lookouts site lists the tower under the Tennessee Division of Forestry.[^5] The Register lists the State of Tennessee as administrator but notes that several agencies deny ownership, so the record does not settle who controls the site.[^1] I found no source that names the lookout's staff or the fires it watched, so its working years are poorly documented.

The forestry division abandoned the tower in the mid-1980s, according to the city.[^4] Around 1998 a 25-foot star went up at the top.[^4] It is lit each November and December, and most people simply call the tower "Star."[^4] The Register dates the star to the late 1990s and credits Rockwood Electric with placing it.[^1]

Today the wildlife agency says the tower is fenced off from the public and should not be climbed.[^2] The Register notes that numerous people still climb it, dangerously, and that the steps have rotted out.[^1] The city calls the tower structurally sound, says the rotten steps could be replaced with galvanized, slip-resistant ones, and has asked the state to donate the Star to Rockwood.[^4] The statewide inventory lists the tower as standing.[^6] The Register gives the drive as 1.4 miles of paved road off US 70.[^1] The wildlife agency calls the overlook the best and easiest access point.[^2]
