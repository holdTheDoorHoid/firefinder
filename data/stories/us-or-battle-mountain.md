Battle Mountain's lookout stood in Jackson County, Oregon, about 14 miles north of Rogue River, at an elevation of about 3,982 feet.[^1][^2]

The register dates a tree crow's nest and tent to 1933, and Firelookout gives a crow's nest and camp for the same year.[^1][^2] A 1918 item, quoted on Forest Lookouts, says fire wardens were building a log cabin on the summit that year, so the start date is not settled.[^3] The register says a pole tower and log cabin followed in 1935 and that a 40-to-41-foot tower with an L-4 cab was built in 1936.[^1] Firelookout gives the same 1936 date.[^2] Forest Lookouts quotes a 1935 item reporting a new tower completed in late October, and a 1936 letter that calls the tower 50 feet tall, so the height and dates are disputed.[^3]

Staffing is documented in a few scattered items. In September 1930 a lookout named Roy Edwards fell ill on duty, and forest men carried him five miles down rough mountain trail.[^3] Vern Orr went up to the summer station in July 1935.[^3] Edith Osborn returned home from the station in October 1953.[^3] In late October 1935 the five-and-a-half-mile road to the summit was getting its finishing touches. It was meant to end the packing of supplies up the mountain.[^3]

In October 1957 the station closed, the final one in its district to shut, after being kept mainly as a radio relay point and for spotting possible hunter fires.[^3] The station was staffed for the 1963 season.[^3]

The fate is disputed. The register says the lookout was removed in 1965 and that it was destroyed that year.[^1] Firelookout says it was abandoned and gone in the 1970s.[^2] Forest Lookouts logs it as destroyed in 1967.[^3]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the summit is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
