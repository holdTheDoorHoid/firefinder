Smith Peak Lookout stands on the Groveland Ranger District of the Stanislaus National Forest, in Tuolumne County, at 3,878 feet.[^1] By 1910 the peak served as a fire lookout, two miles out from the Groveland Ranger Station.[^1] In 1931 the peak was set aside as an administrative site, and a wooden lookout tower soon followed.[^1] A metal lookout tower and a garage went up in 1952.[^1]

The lookout burned on August 31, 1987.[^2] A lookout history site quotes a 1988 Merced Sun-Star story: a wood-framed structure on a metal two-story tower burned within two hours of Hamm leaving it around 1 p.m. The site links the loss to the Hamm Fire, part of the Stanislaus Complex, which swept over the summit.[^2] The register says the lookout was replaced in 1988.[^1] The 1988 story says it had been rebuilt and was almost back in service.[^2]

The register says Forest Service employees staff the lookout regularly, and that it is one of two staffed lookouts on the Groveland District.[^1] A 2012 chapter report lists it as staffed with a Forest Service employee and notes routine upkeep.[^3] The same report notes an updated catwalk and a new roof, and it flags rust in the tower girders for mitigation.[^3]

The register gives April 24, 2017 as the registration date, as US 1188, CA 119.[^1] A 2024 visitor's trip report describes the approach: the access road starts just behind the Groveland ranger station, and the road turns from pavement to dirt beyond Pines Campground.[^4] The sources read do not state current visiting rules, so check with the Groveland Ranger District before you go.
