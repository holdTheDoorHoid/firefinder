Morning Knob was a lookout in Alleghany County, Virginia, on the George Washington National Forest.[^1] It was a fire tower, and a forest fire destroyed it in 1952.[^1]

A Pulaski Southwest Times item dated October 28, 1952, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, describes the fire.[^1] It broke out about 15 miles from Covington, on Potts Creek road. By that morning it covered almost 700 acres, and Forester Charles R. Switzer said it had destroyed the fire tower before crews could check it.[^1]

The tower had an occupant during the fire.[^1] Switzer said that person narrowly escaped being burned to death, because the fire moved so fast.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list gives the tower's status as gone.[^3]

Today the summit is reached on foot.[^2] The Morning Knob Trail is 1.8 miles long, starts on Route 600 and follows an old roadbed, and the trail guide says it is not marked.[^2] The guide says the trail winds up to the site of an old fire tower.[^2] Directions from Covington run south on Route 18, right onto Route 614, then left onto Route 600.[^2] The guide mentions no closure.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the summit now.
