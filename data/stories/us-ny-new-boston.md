The Conservation Department erected an 80-foot Aermotor LS40 tower at New Boston, in Lewis County, in 1950.[^1][^7] The state chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association places it north of State Route 177, in the hamlet of New Boston.[^1]

The chapter's tower history ties the closing to the arrival of aerial detection, and says the tower was shut after the 1970 season ended.[^1] In 1982 DEC personnel dismantled it and moved the pieces to facilities in Harrisville.[^1]

The Thompson Park Zoo in Watertown next took the tower on loan.[^1] The chapter's 2016 report says it was moved into the park in 1999, where it now serves as a community educational exhibit.[^2]

The chapter's 2003 report says the zoo restored the tower but kept it closed until it could secure liability insurance for people who climb it.[^3] The 2004 report describes a grand opening that summer and says the tower was open to the public afterward.[^4] The chapter's tower history instead says it reopened to the public in 2016, after restoration.[^1] The sources disagree on when visitors were first let back in, and we could not settle the date.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association gave a $500 restoration grant for repairs in 2016, and the work was finished that spring.[^5][^2]

Today the tower stands on the zoo grounds in Watertown, in Jefferson County.[^7][^2] A 2025 chapter newsletter mentions a garden at its base, which the ADK Black River Chapter maintains.[^6] We could not confirm whether visitors may climb it now. Check with the zoo before you go, and do not climb it unless staff say it is open.

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the original New Boston site.
