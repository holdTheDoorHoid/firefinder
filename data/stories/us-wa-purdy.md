Purdy Lookout stood about three miles northwest of Gig Harbor, in Pierce County.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com dates it to the 1930s and calls it a crow's nest.[^1][^3]

Willhite's research describes the structure as a lookout tree, with perhaps a framed timber structure added later.[^2] John Kingsbury, a retired DNR man, remembered a crow's nest high in a tree, so tall that he climbed only halfway before turning back.[^2]

Don Lee Fraser, who was later appointed DNR Supervisor, recalled that an additional lookout was wanted early in the war.[^2] He helped limb and top the tree, and a small platform was rigged up afterward.[^2]

A 1940s list of existing lookouts turned up while WWII Aircraft Warning Service records were being collected at the National Archives. It marks Purdy as a framed timber lookout at 250 feet of elevation, managed by the Port Orchard District under warden D. Fraser.[^2] That list and firelookout.com do not agree on what the structure was.[^1][^2]

A person who contacted Willhite in 2017 said his friend Charles R. Siegel Jr. spent the summer of 1942 staffing the lookout. He recalled Siegel saying that the spot was not a very good one and that there was some trouble with the landowner.[^2]

Willhite writes that Purdy never became an Aircraft Warning Service site.[^2] Kingsbury, who was working out of the Port Orchard office in 1952, believed the lookout was already gone by then.[^2] Firelookout.com lists it as gone.[^1]

Willhite visited the area in 2017.[^2] He found that the 10 acres named in the old documents is private property, with one home site and new homes going up at the high point.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout. The land is private, and no source describes public access to it.[^2]
