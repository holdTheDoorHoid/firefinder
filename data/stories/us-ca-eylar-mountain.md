Of the Diablo Range peaks above 4,000 feet, Eylar Mountain lies farthest north.[^3] By height it ranks fourth among Santa Clara County's peaks.[^3]

A lookout was on the mountain by June 1926.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page quotes an Oakland Tribune report from that month.[^1] The report names Charles Scott as the lookout, and says he put out a prairie fire one Sunday afternoon.[^1] The report added that this prairie fire was now thought to have started the larger blaze.[^1]

That larger fire had burned for three days on the mountain and threatened to spread to a manganese mine before it was reported extinguished.[^1] The fire burned over 1,000 acres of brush near Devil Hoe canyon, on the Alameda and Santa Clara county line.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Eylar Mountain as gone.[^2] A climbing guide describes a different picture.[^3] It describes a two-story wooden tower, now abandoned, still standing on the summit, with a cement platform or foundation to its north.[^3] The guide does not say when it was written, so we cannot tell which account is more recent.[^3]

The guide says a fire road leads to the summit. Its route begins by crawling under a fence that has a small yellow sign marking buried electrical cable.[^3] The guide names two landowners at gates on the route, N3 Cattle Company and Biel Property, Inc.[^3] No source we read says who owns the summit or whether the public may visit it. Do not cross fences or climb toward the tower without first getting permission from the land's owner.
