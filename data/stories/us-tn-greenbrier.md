Greenbrier is a valley in Sevier County, Tennessee, and is now a recreation area inside Great Smoky Mountains National Park.[^1] A fire tower once stood on Greenbrier Pinnacle, a ridge that drops toward the Middle Fork valley.[^1][^3]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower and its cabin.[^3] A photograph in the park's negative collection is dated 3 September 1935 and is labelled as taken during construction or shortly after, so the work dates to about that year.[^4] The Tennessee lookout list gives the tower's elevation as 4,597 feet (1,401 m).[^2]

At its height, the park used lookouts at seventeen locations: ten inside its boundaries and seven outside, under cooperative agreements.[^3] The park's lookouts were monitored and maintained by the National Park Service during roughly thirty years of active use.[^3]

The lookout list marks the Greenbrier tower as removed and gives no date.[^2] The Mountain Press article says most park towers have been left to decline, or taken down, since their service ended in the 1970s.[^3]

A 2024 hike report says the group did not go up to the old fire tower site on top of the pinnacle.[^5] It says the jeep road once used to reach the tower is now almost too overgrown to walk through, and that the former trail has not been maintained since at least the early 1990s and is now a rough manway.[^5] The report does not describe any tower remains, and no source I found describes what stands at the site now.[^5]

The tower site lies inside the national park, and the old route to it is not a maintained trail.[^1][^2][^5]
