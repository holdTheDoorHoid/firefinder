Slate Point has carried a fire lookout above Idaho's Nez Perce National Forest for nearly a century, though the structure standing today is the third one built on the site.[^1] The original, raised in 1931, was a 75-foot pole tower with a small 7-by-7-foot cab and a separate ground cabin for living quarters.[^1] The Nez Perce National Forest replaced it in 1948 with a more standard L-4 cab set on a 41-foot tower.[^1] That tower gave way in turn to the current lookout, an R-6 flattop cab with a catwalk on a 41-foot timber tower, built in 1979.[^1][^2]

The site's name comes from Slate Creek below it, named by early miners who panned the stream looking for gold-bearing gravel and found only slate instead.[^1]

Slate Point has a long staffing history, though detailed year-by-year records are thin. One lookout, Kris Anderson, is documented as having staffed the tower for five seasons, including 2016 through 2019 and 2021, and a visitor comment on the same page notes it was also staffed by a regular Forest Service hire.[^3] More broadly, the National Historic Lookout Register describes it as having been staffed in most recent years.[^1]

The tower was never abandoned or burned; it remains standing and in Forest Service hands. It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on April 24, 2009.[^1] Today it is kept available for fire emergencies and staffing rather than offered as a public vacation rental, with its status listed as drive-up access and emergency standby.[^2][^3] Visitors reaching the site should check current Nez Perce–Clearwater National Forest guidance before approaching, since an active or standby fire lookout may not be open to climb.
