Mount Prospect Lookout is a split-fieldstone tower at the top of Mount Prospect, inside Weeks State Park in Lancaster, Coos County, New Hampshire.[^3][^2][^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 43, NH 2 and gives the summit elevation as 2,042 feet.[^1] The registration date is August 8, 1992.[^1]

John Wingate Weeks built the tower in 1912 as an observation tower for his summer estate on the mountain.[^1][^2] Weeks, a Lancaster-born politician, had helped get the Weeks Act passed, and that law let federal money buy land for eastern national forests.[^2] The tower also served as a water tower, with a tank inside and stairs to the top curling around it.[^2] One summary gives the 1912 tower a height of 37 feet.[^2]

Weeks died in 1926.[^2] In 1941 his children donated the estate's 431 acres to the state, and it became a state park.[^2][^1] The Register says that was when the Division of Forests and Lands put up a round, enclosed lookout cab that is still in service.[^1] The state's 2026 information sheet instead dates the addition of a fire lookout to after 1941, so the sources do not settle the exact date.[^3]

Division of Forests and Lands rangers, certified as wildland firefighters, can watch wildfire danger from the tower's top cab.[^3] The sources do not say how often the cab is staffed or name any fires it has watched.

The tower is still in use.[^1] The Register calls it active, and a historic-structures summary counts it among the state's 15 working fire towers.[^1][^2] Visitors can drive the scenic Mountain Road to the summit, and the museum, tower and auto road operate between Memorial Day weekend and Columbus Day.[^3] The park invites visitors up its 48 steps.[^3]
