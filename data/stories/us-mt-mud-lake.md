Mud Lake Lookout sits in the Flathead National Forest, inside the Bob Marshall Wilderness, about 22 miles south-southeast of the Spotted Bear Ranger Station.[^1][^2][^3] The register lists it under the Spotted Bear Ranger District, and the US Forest Service administers it.[^1]

The site was first set up with a camp in 1921. The L-4 cab, with its gabled roof, was completed in 1932.[^1][^2] The register notes it keeps the original gabled roof rather than the later hipped roofs.[^1]

The lookout was abandoned in 1972.[^1] The firelookout.com listing has a 1957 entry for known lookout personnel.[^2] In 2000 a forest fire threatened the lookout. The register also notes ongoing restoration work.[^1]

Volunteers from the Northwest Montana Lookout Association began restoring it in 2015 and finished in August, according to their 2020 year-end report.[^3] The 2025 chapter report lists Mud Lake under assessments rather than projects. A crew walked in that year to assess it and packed in paint or stain for the windows.[^4]

The walk is about 2.5 miles one way, with blowdown and a ford to cross, and the crew had no stock support.[^4]

The latest status we found is an assessment. We found no rental or visiting program, so treat it as a remote wilderness structure rather than a place to climb.[^3][^4]
