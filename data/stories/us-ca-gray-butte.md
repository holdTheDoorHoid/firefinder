Gray Butte is in Siskiyou County, California, and the trail to it starts at the edge of Panther Meadows, near Mount Shasta.[^2][^4]

In July 1930 the Siskiyou Daily News reported that the Forest Service would build the snowline highway to a spot below Gray Butte, where one of the lookouts was posted. The agency said it intended to put up an up-to-date standard lookout there that fall.[^1]

In April 1931 the same paper reported that Shasta National Forest officers planned a road camp at Wagon Camp, to put a crew to work on a forest road from there to Gray Butte, where the lookout station then stood.[^1] The 1931 clipping calls the forest the Shasta National Forest. The trail association now names the Shasta-Trinity National Forest as the agency that manages the butte.[^1][^4]

The Forest Lookouts county index lists the lookout as type unknown and status gone.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of it on the butte today.[^2][^3][^4]

Today the butte is a hiking destination.[^3][^4] The Forest Service lists a round trip of 4.0 miles and two hours, and it gives the season as late June, or July or August in heavy snow years, to late October.[^3] The trail association gives the round trip as 3.4 miles, and says that a faint trail leads to the top and that no permit is needed.[^4]
