Mount Lincoln, in Mason County, is a lookout site with an unsettled history.[^1] Two pages say a cabin stood there in the 1930s and is now gone.[^1][^2] Willhite says it is not known when or if a lookout was built.[^3]

On July 18, 1935, Robert H. Mealey climbed the peak to evaluate it as a lookout site, and his report strongly endorsed it.[^3] Maps showed the lookout by 1938.[^3] Willhite estimates a cabin, if one existed, was put up around 1936 or 1937.[^3]

A Forest Lookouts page lists the site as "Activated: June 20, 1942," tied to the Olympia Filter Center.[^4]

The author of the Willhite page found no evidence of a cabin between the north and south summits, apart from one flat spot that seemed to have been modified. That highpoint was the only spot on the mountain that hinted at a cabin site.[^3] The hike begins at Staircase Ranger Station in Olympic National Park, and the trail stops at about 2,650 feet. The page adds that no trail was built on the upper mountain. The hike is 5.5 miles one way.[^3]

The register lists the site under Olympic National Forest, but the other three pages place it in Olympic National Park.[^1][^2][^4][^3]
