In September 1941 the Prince William County Board of Supervisors authorized up to $350 for a fire tower on Bull Run Mountain, at the request of district forester Berlin Eye [^1]. The register notes that the neighboring counties of Fauquier and Loudoun were also expected to chip in [^1]. A September 15 report in the Evening Star said the county had set aside $350 to help buy the tower, working with Fauquier and Loudoun [^2].

The decision drew some controversy [^1]. The register says the Occoquan-Woodbridge-Lorton volunteer fire department, based in the eastern part of the county, harshly denounced the county for offering it little financial support while the county paid for a state tower at the western end [^1].

The tower went up in 1942 [^1]. Aerial imagery captured in 1954 shows it, and the U.S. Geological Survey located it in 1966 [^1]. It also served as a landmark for the nearby Jackson Hollow benchmark [^1].

It was removed at some point between 1966 and 1979 [^1][^2]. A 1979 report said it had been demolished [^1].

We found no source that describes what remains at the summit today. A National Geodetic Survey description of the Jackson benchmark, stamped 1954, places the mark at an airway beacon on top of the mountain, 45 feet north of the northeast leg of the lookout tower [^2]. Its directions run through Bull Run Mountain Park and then along a winding gravel road [^2]. The register lists the site as administered by a private owner [^1], so check access before you go.
