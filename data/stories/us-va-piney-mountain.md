Piney Mountain Lookout stands on the highest point of land in Appomattox County, Virginia, on private property.[^2] The register describes an 80-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] A 2026 news report gives the height as 88 feet, so the two sources disagree.[^1][^2]

Who built it, and when, is also unsettled. The register says the Virginia Division of Forestry constructed it in 1938, though its own build field reads 1939.[^1] The news report says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it, and that it has stood since 1938.[^2] The Forest Lookouts index lists the Virginia Department of Forestry against the tower.[^3]

Krissy Reynolds, chapter head of the Virginia and West Virginia Forest Fire Lookout Association, said the tower would have been used between the 1930s and the 1990s as a fire lookout.[^2] The news report adds that foresters used it to search for smoke from its perch.[^2] Former forester Rick Butler spent 33 years working the tower, which has 109 stairs, and retired in June 2022 as its last employee.[^2]

The tower still stands on private land. Its owner has pledged to restore it instead of tearing it down.[^2] The register added the tower on February 25, 2009.[^1]

According to the register, the tower is privately owned, and as of 2025 visits are by appointment only.[^1] It names Krissy Reynolds as the contact for arranging one, so start there, and do not go up without an arranged visit.[^1] A 2020 blog post called the stairs a bit wobbly, with few guardrails, and said the route crossed private property with a No Trespassing sign.[^4]
