Sunset Mountain Lookout sits on a 7,869-foot summit in the Boise National Forest, in Boise County.[^1] The first cab here was an R-5 design, set on a 10-foot concrete base in 1930.[^1][^2] In 1958 an R-6 cab with a catwalk went up on that same base, and that is the structure the sources describe today.[^1][^2]

A January 28, 1945 Idaho Statesman item, quoted by Daytrip Northwest, tells of Nellie Arabaugh of Pioneerville, who was working the lookout when lightning struck a tree 200 feet away. She brought the burning tree under control with a shovel.[^3] The quoted passage gives the newspaper date but not the date of the strike.[^3]

Sources disagree about whether anyone staffs the lookout now. Firelookout.com and Daytrip Northwest both say it is staffed every summer, and Idaho Fire Lookouts lists it as drive-up staffed.[^2][^3][^4] The Trails Off Road guide calls it an unmanned lookout.[^5] None of these pages says when it was checked, so the current season is unconfirmed.

A 1994 history of the Southern Idaho Timber Protective Association says the association constructed and operated seven lookouts, including one on a Sunset Mtn., and that by 1940 it had stopped using the Sunset Mtn. and Peck Mtn. lookouts.[^7] That history does not say which Sunset Mountain it means, so it is unclear whether it describes this tower.[^7]

The National Historic Lookout Register added the tower on October 16, 2011, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^1][^6]

The road to the lookout leaves Highway 21 at Mores Creek Summit, about 13 miles north of Idaho City.[^3] Daytrip Northwest reports no fees and says the dirt road suits 4WD vehicles in summer and early fall, with snowshoe access in winter.[^3] Trails Off Road says people may go up to the 10-foot concrete base and walk around the catwalk.[^5]
