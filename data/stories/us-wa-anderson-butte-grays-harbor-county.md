Anderson Butte is a former lookout site in the Olympic National Forest, about 19 miles west of Hoodsport in Grays Harbor County.[^2] The Register gives its elevation as 3,358 feet.[^1]

Sources disagree on when the lookout was built. The Register and firelookout.com describe an L-4 cab built in 1937.[^1][^2] Willhite says the site likely started being used in 1937, but that the cab seems to have been built in 1943. He cites a Forest Service structural inventory that lists a wood 14-by-14 standard R-6 plan ground house built that year.[^4] Forest Lookouts gives the same 1943 ground house and cites the same inventory.[^3]

Forest Lookouts also lists a 1942 post here. The station, called 'Charlie 0-2,' was activated on June 15, 1942, under the Olympia Filter Center, and inactivated in September 1943.[^3] The page says the Forest Service erected the lookout structure, and that the AWS funded a 10-by-14 cabin.[^3]

Harold C. Chriswell's memoirs, under a 1942 entry, describe a lookout who lived in a tent down in a saddle and climbed up by hanging onto telephone wire.[^3][^4] In 1944 the Shelton Mason County Journal reported that Kenneth Linscott had worked the summer as forest lookout here, and was attending high school in Shelton.[^3] In 1964 the lookout was manned again in July, and it closed for the season in September.[^3]

The Register and Forest Lookouts give 1968 as the year the lookout was removed.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com says the cab was destroyed that year.[^2]

Willhite says cable posts are still anchored at the final rock perch, the only description of remains we found.[^4] The site was blasted flat enough for a large lookout, and Willhite describes it as interesting but seldom visited.[^4] Willhite gives the hike as 3.5 miles with 2,300 feet of climbing, and says it is steep at times, with downed trees near the top.[^4] WTA's 2018 trip report describes about three miles along a badly overgrown, abandoned road, then a climb straight up a narrow ridge, which it says may be more appropriate to call a cross-country trek.[^5] Neither account mentions a closure or a permit.[^4][^5]
