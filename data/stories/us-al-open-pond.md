The Open Pond Lookout Tower stands on the Conecuh National Forest in Covington County, Alabama, just north of the Florida-Alabama state line.[^1][^2] It sits on the north side of the Open Pond Recreation Area, a 450-acre area where people go to hike, fish, ride bikes and camp.[^3]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1938 and 1939, through Company 3474.[^1][^5] According to Wikipedia, a plaque at the bottom of the tower gives 1938 as the build year.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says Jones & Laughlin Steel manufactured the 100-foot tower.[^1]

According to the Register, only two towers in the state have 12-by-12-foot wooden cabs, and this one and the Parker Springs tower are those two. The other 13 towers still remaining on Alabama's national forests have 7-by-7-foot steel cabs.[^1]

Fire wardens who worked here are named in the Register, and the tower has been unstaffed since about 1975.[^1][^5] The sources read do not name any particular fire it watched.

A rehabilitation in the fall and winter of 2006 replaced the shingle roof, the wooden steps and the catwalk, and the windows and door were swapped for replicas.[^1] The fire finder was repaired and cleaned by March 2007.[^1] A blog post places the renovation at about 2007 and says the map wheel was replaced around then.[^5]

Register visit reports run from October 2007 to July 2015. The July 2015 report rates the tower in good condition but notes that the Register sign is missing and that there are no signed directions to the tower.[^1] A January 2009 report lists state antennae under other encroachments.[^1]

The Forest Service lists the recreation area as open and describes it as offering a diversity of recreational opportunities.[^4] The sources do not say whether visitors may go up the tower. The Register's 2009 report points readers to the Conecuh National Forest ranger for staffing questions, so ask the Forest Service whether the tower is open to visitors before going.[^1]
