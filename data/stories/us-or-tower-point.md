Tower Point's story starts in 1919. A September 1919 item in the Crook County Journal says C. W. Goodnight was assisting Ranger Anderson with a lookout tower and telephone line in the Maury district, at Tower Point.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the start of fire detection at the site to 1919, and says a timber tower went up there in 1940.[^1] The present tower is a 20-foot treated timber structure with an L-4 cab, built in 1955.[^1][^4]

Sources disagree about the cab. The Oregon forest lookouts listing describes the 1955 tower as carrying a 1936 L-4 cab.[^2] The register gives 1955 for the tower and does not date a separate cab.[^1]

Rex's Forest Fire Lookout Page says the tower has a lookout on duty every summer.[^4] When a visitor came in June 2021, it was unstaffed.[^3] A trailer with a generator sat on site, and the visitor guessed that staffing would start soon.[^3] The same visitor noted four lookouts once stood in the Maury Mountains, with Tower Point the lone survivor.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the tower on July 6, 2022, as US 1644, OR 162. It lists the Ochoco National Forest as administrator, with the Bureau of Land Management as a cooperator.[^1]

The site is reached by forest roads.[^3] The 2021 visitor drove from Road 16 along roads 1750 and 1760 to the summit, found them rough in places but manageable, and noted that the last stretch had no gate.[^3] The tower is about 32 miles southeast of Prineville.[^4]

On that visit the visitor climbed the steps as far as possible, but the catwalk hatch was fastened shut, so the cab was out of reach that day.[^3] Check with the Ochoco National Forest before you go, and do not climb a tower that is closed.
