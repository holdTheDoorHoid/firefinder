Maine built its fire towers to catch blazes early, limiting damage to the lumber and papermaking businesses that relied on the state's trees.[^1] A tower stood on High Cut Hill in Garland, Penobscot County, by about 1940, according to a photo record from the Maine Memory Network.[^2][^3]

In the fall of 1944 a new 65-foot tower went up on the hill, according to the forest commissioner's biennial report for 1943–44, as quoted by a volunteer list of Maine lookouts.[^3] A photograph of the tower, dated about 1950, is in the Maine Memory collection.[^2]

The sources disagree on when it came down. The photo record, and a Maine Historical Society photo inventory as quoted by the volunteer list, both put the removal about 1960.[^2][^3] The former-lookout register says the tower was removed between 1971 and 1972.[^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the hill today, or whether the public can reach it. Check with the Garland Historical Society before going up, since the photo record gives its contact details.[^2]
