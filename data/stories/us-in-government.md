The state set up Clark State Forest in 1903, its first state-owned forest.[^1] The history page says the Henryville fire towers, inside that forest, are generally regarded as Indiana's earliest.[^1] A wooden tower stood there in 1926, and a steel one followed in 1928.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Government as a separate tower site in Clark County, and lists the Henryville tower apart from it as standing.[^2] The Indiana Fire Towers author places the older wooden site about a mile northeast of the current Henryville tower, and assumes the name comes from the hill there.[^3] That author would count the two as one site, while the association lists them separately.[^2][^3]

The survey mark on that hill, SUMMIT 1886, was set in 1886 and described in 1914 on a knob known locally as Salaam Knob. By 1935 the survey records call the same hill the Government Lookout Knob.[^4] A 1931 survey note says an old hardwood lookout tower, 25 feet tall, stood over the station.[^4]

The Indiana Fire Towers history says an old wooden tower in Clark County Forest became unsafe and was replaced by a 72-foot steel tower in 1928, but it does not name the site.[^1] The 1931 survey note and the Henryville page both place a wooden tower at the older site.[^4][^3] Whether that is the same tower is not clear from these sources.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association marks Government as gone.[^2] The most recent condition note in the survey record is from 1947, when the marks were found in good condition.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the hill today, or whether the hill is open to visitors. The Henryville tower is standing, but climbing was suspended during repair work in 2023 and 2024.[^3][^2]
