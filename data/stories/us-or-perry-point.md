Perry Point lies in the Mt. Hood National Forest, about 10 miles east-northeast of Mt. Hood in Hood River County, at an elevation of 4,920 feet.[^1]

The sources describe a crow's nest platform on top of a tree.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com's page title calls the site a tower, but the page's text does not mention one.[^1] The page says the platform was set up in the 1930s and may have been a patrol point tied to nearby Fivemile Butte.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page places it atop a Douglas fir.[^3]

Firelookout.com dates its abandonment to the 1940s and says it was still present in the 1980s.[^1] The Association's list records the site as abandoned.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page reports that in 2005 remnants of the crow's nest still remained on the tree.[^3]

The Oregon Hikers field guide, which is undated, says the platform has decayed, but that a ladder's remains can still be seen on a gnarled Douglas-fir with a sawn-off top.[^5] The most recent visit we found is from October 2020.[^4] That hiker found two boards with nails leaned against a large old tree and wrote that they might have found the one the lookout stood in.[^4]

The point is reached on foot.[^5] The Oregon Hikers guide describes a short spur off the Bottle Prairie Trail, #455, and the 2020 hiker started from a small parking area off Road 120.[^4][^5] We found no source that describes the site in more recent detail than that.[^1][^3][^4][^5]
