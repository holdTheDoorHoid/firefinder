The Fremont lookout is a 100-foot steel tower on the Eleven Point Ranger District of the Mark Twain National Forest.[^2][^1] Its build date is not settled. The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower was likely built in 1939 by the Bartlett CCC Camp 3752, and that the tower site lay along the newly constructed U.S. Highway 60, with a towerman's residence, a garage and a recreation site with a pavilion.[^1] A Forest Lookouts entry gives 1934 for the tower but names no source.[^2] Newspaper items it reprints show the Forest Service developing picnic grounds at the site in 1938, and a recreation area there opened that October.[^2]

The lookout had towermen for decades.[^2][^1] A 1940 picture from the Chicago Daily News, reprinted in a local paper, showed 'Smoky Joe' Steininger at work in the fire tower, and a 1957 item names Lloyd Black as towerman.[^2] The register says the tower was staffed, on and off, until 2019, when a longtime employee retired and the lookout was left vacant.[^1]

Since 2016 the lookout has been used for prescribed-fire monitoring, and the Missouri Department of Conservation fitted it with an antenna to track a reintroduced bird.[^1] Solar panels added after 2010 powered a wildfire monitoring system that the register calls largely unsuccessful.[^1] The added parts let water into the structure, and the damage will mean replacing some lower interior walls.[^1]

According to the register, the lookout remains usable, its stand and fire finder still installed, and restoration is at the planning stage, subject to approvals.[^1] Replacing the stairway landings with metal grating is probably set for the 2027-28 fiscal years.[^1] We found no public visiting or rental information, so please do not climb it. Check with the Eleven Point Ranger District before visiting.[^1]
