The Register lists this site as the Milton Fire Tower, but the building is a weather observatory on the summit of Great Blue Hill, south of Boston.[^1][^2] Abbott Lawrence Rotch started the observatory, and it holds what Wikipedia calls the oldest continuous weather record in North America.[^2] Sources disagree on the founding year: the same Wikipedia article gives both 1884 and 1885.[^2] The Register uses 1885 for the building.[^1]

The building went up in 1885 in a Late Gothic Revival style, designed by Arthur Rotch and George T. Tilden.[^2] It was designated a National Historic Landmark on December 20, 1989.[^2]

The state used the observatory as a fire lookout, which the Register says was active from 1911 to 1915.[^1] A Massachusetts fire-tower history places Milton's lookout at the weather observatory, and places the Adams lookout in a tower adjacent to a mountain hotel.[^5] A Massachusetts forest-lookout listing says the building was used as a fire lookout for several years in the 1910s.[^3] A 1916 annual report of the State Forester, quoted on the same listing, says the Blue Hill Observatory ended about two years before that report, which points to about 1914 rather than 1915.[^3] I did not find the names of the people who staffed the lookout or any fire they reported.

Do not confuse this building with the Great Blue Hill Observation Tower, known locally as Eliot Tower. That is a separate stone tower near the summit, built in 1933-34 by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^4]

The Register lists the Massachusetts Department of Conservation and Recreation's Urban Parks Division as administrator.[^1] Wikipedia says the observatory remains active.[^2] I did not find a current statement on whether visitors may go inside or climb the building, so ask the observatory or DCR before visiting, and do not climb without permission.
