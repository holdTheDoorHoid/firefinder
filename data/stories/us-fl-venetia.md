The Venetia fire tower stood near Venice in Sarasota County, Florida, where the state Division of Forestry used it to spot fires.[^1] A 1969 Tampa newspaper says it was constructed in 1956, and that a couple, Margie and Charles "Pete" Knauff, had been working it since 1952.[^1] The articles disagree on the road. One puts the tower on US 41 between Venice and Englewood, and a 1974 report puts it on State Road 775, about four miles south of Venice.[^1]

In October 1974 the district forester said the tower would lose its full-time spotter that November. In February 1975 a Division spokesman said the tower would stay open, and the district forester credited federal civil-service jobs money with restoring full staffing.[^1] In 1988 a state forest ranger was still climbing the 106 steps to watch for fires.[^1]

The newspapers describe a tower 85 feet high with 106 steps.[^1] A project page from a galvanizing company calls it an MC-39 model, says it was hot-dip galvanized in the 1940s, and gives its height as 80 feet.[^2] That page also guesses it was first built around 1945 and says it was decommissioned in the 1970s. Those dates do not fit the 1956 construction date or the 1988 report of a staffed tower, so this story follows the newspapers.[^1][^2]

The tower was dismantled for removal in March 2019, and its parts were shipped to New York.[^1] Davana LLC, a restoration firm, bought it for $1,000 and aimed to place it at the Maurice D. Hinchey Catskills Visitor Center in Mount Tremper.[^2] The state opened it there as the Upper Esopus Fire Tower in October 2019.[^2]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the Venice site, or whether that place is open to the public. The company's page says the land and tower were sold to a private party, and that housing built from the 1960s onward now surrounds the tower site.[^2] The tower itself left that site in 2019.[^1]
