McConnell Mountain sits in the Clearwater National Forest in Idaho County, and the register places the lookout inside the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness.[^1] The site was first used as a lookout in 1918, when a tent camp and an alidade were set up there.[^1][^2] A log cabin followed in 1922.[^3][^4] A six-by-six-foot log cupola came later, and the sources disagree on its year. Three say 1934 and two say 1935.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]

According to the register, the living quarters were a one-room cabin, 14 by 14 feet, set in a meadow about 450 yards northwest of the summit.[^1] Bud Moore helped build and staff the lookout in 1935, according to an interview with his son, Bill Moore, that the University of Idaho's Keeping Watch project posted.[^5] The known-staff list on Idaho Fire Lookouts runs from 1919 to 1939 and names Bud Moore for 1935 and again for 1937 to 1939.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in 1951.[^2][^3][^4] A Havre Daily News item from September 1951, quoted on Kemnow's site, reports that a man had just wrapped up a summer working the McConnell lookout for the Forest Service.[^2]

Kemnow's site dates the loss to July 2013, when the California Point Fire took the cab and its outbuildings.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Northern Idaho/Montana chapter report lists three McConnell structures as wildfire losses: a log cabin, an "observatory" and an outhouse.[^6] Keeping Watch says the same fire destroyed the lookout.[^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts calls the site a hike-in and says no remnants survived the fire.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, is left on the summit now, or what the view is like today. The site lies in the Selway-Bitterroot Wilderness, so plan a wilderness hike, and check with the Clearwater National Forest for current conditions before you go.[^1]
