Colquhoun Mountain, also called Colquhoun Peak, rises to 5,173 feet (1,577 meters) in King County, Washington, on the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1][^2] Sources tie its name to Carl Colquhoun, a young packer and lookout worker who drowned.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb dates his drowning to 1933, while the blog dates it to 1931.[^3][^4]

The lookout was an L-4 cab, put up in the 1930s.[^1][^2][^3] The blog says the Board on Geographic Names approved the petition to rename the peak on October 5, 1932.[^4]

During World War II the cab served as an Aircraft Warning Service station, and its observers had to stay at their posts for hours on end.[^3][^4]

WillhiteWeb says the station ran as a summer post and was inactivated on October 16, 1943.[^3] The blog gives the same end date.[^4]

By 1958 the cab was gone.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register says it was removed that year, while FireLookout.com, WillhiteWeb and the blog say it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

No source we read describes footings or any other remains on the summit. WillhiteWeb says trees are now growing over the top, though cliffs still give views.[^3] The blog calls the summit unoccupied and the view overgrown.[^4] The trail to the top is about half a mile one way from a gravel road.[^3]
