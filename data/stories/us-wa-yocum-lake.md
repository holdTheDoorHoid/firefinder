The Yocum Lake lookout went up in 1935 in the Colville National Forest, Pend Oreille County, Washington.[^1][^2] It was a 40-foot pole tower topped with an L-4 cab, and the U.S. Forest Service administered the site.[^1] Sources spell the name both Yocum and Yokum.[^1][^4]

Sources date its abandonment to the 1940s and describe it as gone.[^1][^2][^3] A regional index marks the site as removed.[^4] Willhite notes that the lookout appears on a 1957 Metsker map but not on a 1941 one.[^3]

Getting to the site means a rough forest road. Willhite's route turns off West Branch LeClerc Creek Road onto Antiquity Road, and beyond the last houses the road turns rough in spots.[^3] He lists access as high clearance 4WD or a one-mile walk.[^3] Willhite found no trace of the lookout on the ground, but he says the terrain points to the right spot.[^3] His visit is the only on-the-ground account in these sources.[^3]
