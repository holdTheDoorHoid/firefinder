Williams Peak lookout stood on a rocky summit not far from Mariposa, in Mariposa County, California.[^1][^2] Civilian Conservation Corps workers put up the two-story tower in 1935.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says its design closely follows the L-101 model, and that the tower stood a short distance above a parking area.[^1]

In July 1935, a newspaper reported that a state telephone line would connect with the lookout station, and that the county fire marshal said a fire's location could be phoned to crews once it was seen from there.[^3]

On October 16, 1936, a newspaper reported that seven brush and forest fires had cut Yosemite Valley off and interrupted communication and power lines.[^3] The same report named W.P. Wales as the state's lookout at Williams Peak and said he had reported blazes that endangered Indian Lodge and Pine Grove.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists the tower as removed in 2017-18.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says only the stairway remains.[^4] A Mariposa Gazette article described the abandoned tower as neglected, with flaking paint and weathered wood.[^2] It also said the 2017 Detwiler Fire reduced the tower to ashes and left only the granite steps.[^2]

The two registers disagree about which Cal Fire unit ran the site. One names the Madera-Mariposa-Merced Unit and the other names the Mendocino Unit.[^1][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register registered the site on October 22, 2019.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register listed it on February 1, 2020.[^4]
