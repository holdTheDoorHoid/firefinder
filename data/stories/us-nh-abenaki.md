The Abenaki Tower stands in Tuftonboro, in Carroll County, New Hampshire.[^1][^2] A historical marker at its trailhead, on New Hampshire Route 109, says the tower was dedicated in 1924 and built by residents of Melvin Village.[^1] The marker also says the name is meant to recognize the trails nearby and the continuing contributions of the Indigenous peoples of the area.[^1]

The origin story, as The Laker tells it, begins on one summer day in 1923. Joshua Litchfield, who headed the Agassiz School in Jamaica Plains, Massachusetts, took a stroll with Frank Speare, president of Northeastern University, and the two men ended up on Edgerly Hill, looking out over open pasture.[^2]

The Laker says the original tower had become dangerous, and a taller one was rebuilt, with construction beginning in 1977 and a re-dedication on July 22, 1978.[^2] The tower is built of wood and has a peaked roof.[^2] A visitor who climbed it in 2018 stopped at each level on the stairs and saw Lake Winnipesaukee and the Belknap Range.[^2]

Many people believe it was a fire tower, or that it watched for airplanes during World War II.[^2] The Laker says neither idea is true.[^2] The marker page, which quotes the Abenaki Tower Trail Association, says the tower was never a fire tower.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list of New Hampshire lookouts still files it as an observation tower, in standing condition.[^3]

The marker describes free public access. A small parking lot by a gate leads to the trail, and the walk takes about five minutes.[^1][^2] We found no current rule on climbing. We also found no record of staffing, fires watched or closures in the sources we read. Anyone planning a visit should check with the trail association named on the marker before going up.
