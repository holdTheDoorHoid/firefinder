The Snowy Mountain fire tower stands on the summit of Snowy Mountain in Hamilton County, New York, inside the Jessup River Wild Forest.[^1] A lookout was set up there in 1909, on a 15-foot tower that cost $989.02.[^2] A 22-foot steel tower followed in 1917.[^2] Wikipedia says it was a prefabricated Aermotor LS40 design.[^3]

In 1933 a 20-foot extension was added so the cab could see over the trees.[^1] Wikipedia describes the same year's change as four more flights of stairs, added as the surrounding trees grew.[^3] The two accounts also disagree on height. The Register's figures add up to about 42 feet, while Wikipedia gives 45 feet (14 metres).[^1][^3]

The Register says the tower was in operation until 1971.[^1] Wikipedia says that with the advent of aerial detection, the tower was closed in 1971.[^3] The tower was added to the National Register of Historic Places on September 23, 2001.[^4][^3]

The Register says DEC staff, Student Conservation Corps and local citizens restored the tower in 2001, with support from a state police helicopter that delivered materials.[^1] An FFLA report written in January 2005 lists Snowy among the Adirondack towers already restored.[^5]

Today the tower is described as restored and open to the public.[^3] The FFLA's New York chapter says it maintains the Kane and Snowy towers and their trails under an agreement with DEC.[^6] Its April 2024 newsletter announced repair work on the tower's safety fencing for July 13 and 14. That was a plan, and no source we read confirms the work was done.[^6] The trail on State Route 30 has taken heavy use, and the newsletter reports trail widening and damage to trailside vegetation.[^6] A contract went to the Adirondack Mountain Club to design a new sustainable trail or plan a rebuild of the current one.[^6]

The sources do not describe the tower's current condition or whether the cab is open to climbers now. Check with DEC before going up.
