In 1970 the Bureau of Land Management's Alaska fire report listed Lazy Mountain among the sites in the Anchorage-Palmer area where lookouts were manned that year.[^1] The report called fixed observation towers a special difficulty in Alaska, and noted that suitable sites were scarce near populated areas.[^1] The terrain often did not give lookouts broad views, but the Bureau still used lookout towers during 1970.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Alaska page reports that the state's lookouts are poorly documented.[^2]

The summit is now reached by a hiking trail that the Mat-Su Trails and Parks Foundation lists in a Mat-Su Borough park.[^3] The hike is about 2.2 miles long, and its first mile runs up a steep, unmaintained dirt path.[^3] It is a long-running favorite with residents and visitors.[^3] The foundation advises looking up closures on the borough website, since spring and early summer may be muddy.[^3]

No source describes any remains of a lookout on the summit today.
