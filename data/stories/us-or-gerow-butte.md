Gerow Butte Lookout sits in Crook County, about 20 miles east of Prineville, on private land in the middle of the Ochoco Timber Company's holdings.[^1][^2][^3]

The first lookout went up in 1934, when Ira Swift and his crew finished a wooden tower with an observatory and cabin at the company's center. Until then the fireman had been posted at a shearing plant, where he could do no detection work at all. The new site let him watch for fires and patrol the timber as well.[^2] Sources disagree on the first tower's height: a 1934 newspaper says 66 feet, while firelookout.com gives 50 feet.[^2][^3]

Gordon McNely and his wife took charge in July 1934, and newspapers name a string of couples and families in the years after.[^2] In June 1953 a high school teacher's wife and her 12-year-old son manned the station while her husband was at summer school.[^2] No single large fire turns up in the pages read.[^2] The station's work shows up as counts instead: seven fires reported in 1964, and 12 first reports in 1965.[^2]

The tower was kept up through the 1950s and 1960s. It was painted in 1956 and its stairs were repaired in 1965.[^2] In 1967 treated poles were bought and delivered for a new tower.[^2] According to the NHLR, the present tower and cabin date from 1968 and took the place of the 1934 structures.[^1] Firelookout.com and a 1969 annual report put the rebuild in 1969, with the tower finished as winter set in.[^2][^3] The current design is an R6 Flat Top cabin on a 60-foot open timber tower.[^1] Firelookout.com says the Oregon Department of Forestry cab is staffed every summer, though that page is undated.[^3]

The site joined the National Historic Lookout Register on June 23, 2022, as US 1616, OR 140.[^1] It is private land, and the NHLR says the lookout is not accessible to the public.[^1][^3] Visitors should not try to climb or enter the tower.
