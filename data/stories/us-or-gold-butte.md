Gold Butte Lookout stands on the summit of Gold Butte in Oregon's Willamette National Forest, an L-4 house measuring 14 by 14 feet with a catwalk around it.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1934.[^1] Eight years later, in World War II, a separate cabin went up several hundred feet north of the lookout, and the station was staffed around the clock as part of the Aircraft Warning Service — typically by a husband-and-wife team who reported every aircraft they saw or heard to a central filter center.[^1][^2]

That wartime cabin and its garage are gone now, but the lookout itself survived. Sources differ on exactly when it stopped being staffed: the National Historic Lookout Register says the late 1960s, while firelookout.com puts it in the 1970s.[^1][^3] Either way, the structure fell into disrepair afterward, with rot and insect damage serious enough that a later restoration effort had to tear the cab down to ground level and rebuild it from there.[^3] Accounts differ on which group did the work — the National Historic Lookout Register credits a hiking group and the Pacific Crest Trust Fund with saving and restoring the lookout, while firelookout.com attributes the full rebuild to the Sand Mountain Society.[^1][^3]

The lookout is listed on the National Historic Lookout Register and remains in occasional use during periods of high fire danger.[^1][^3] It's also part of the Forest Service's recreation rental program.[^3] The cabin sits on three-foot stilts, wrapped in glass windows, with refurbished or replica furniture inside and sweeping views of the Cascades, including Mt. Jefferson, Three Fingered Jack, Mt. Hood, and the Three Sisters.[^2] It's open for rental from early July through mid-October; the drive in needs a high-clearance vehicle over rough gravel roads, and guests still have to walk the last half-mile over steep terrain to reach the lookout.[^2]
