Little Frog Mountain sits inside the Cherokee National Forest in Polk County, Tennessee.[^3] A February 1929 item in The Cherokee Scout describes the Sassafras Knob Lookout Tower on Little Frog Mountain.[^1] The Tennessee Landforms list calls the tower Little Frog, so the two names most likely refer to one structure.[^2]

The Forest Service had just finished a road to the tower from the Kimsey Highway, near Jenkins Grave Gap.[^1] The road was meant to let the lookout man drive up to the tower, which suggests the tower was already in use.[^1] The road runs three-quarters of a mile on a seven percent grade and loops around the top, so drivers could circle the summit.[^1] The paper said the road would let the public drive up for the view.[^1] It also said the lookout man would be posted at the tower in the dry months, handing out Forest Service pamphlets to visitors.[^1]

A Forest Service handbook for the southern Appalachian national forests lists a picnic stop near the Sassafras lookout tower on the Kimsey Highway.[^4]

Tennessee Landforms marks the Little Frog waypoint, which sits at 3,322 feet, as removed.[^2]

None of the sources read for this story describes what remains at the tower site today. Wikipedia names several trails and the Kimsey Mountain Highway as ways to reach the mountain, and says it anchors the Little Frog Wilderness.[^3]
