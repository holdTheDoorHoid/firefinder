The University Lookout Tower stands in Stone County, Mississippi, in the De Soto National Forest.[^1] The land around it has an unusual past. The National Historic Lookout Register says the U.S. Navy once held 23,000 acres in southern Mississippi to supply virgin longleaf timber for ship masts. After wooden sailing ships fell out of use, the state received the land to endow the University of Mississippi.[^1]

Until 2000 the tract had been run as University Forest Lands. That year Congress bought it and added it to the De Soto National Forest.[^1] The tower came with that purchase.[^1] The register describes it as a 100-foot steel tower from International Derrick Co., with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and does not say when it was built.[^1]

The tower has been taken out of service, but the U.S. Forest Service keeps it up for emergencies.[^1] Its register number is US 525. It was registered on July 24, 2003, on the nomination of a district ranger on the De Soto Ranger District.[^1]

The register gives no public access information.[^1] Please do not climb the tower. Ask the De Soto Ranger District, which the register lists as a cooperator, before planning any visit.

[^1]: University Lookout Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/ms/university-lookout-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
