Shake Butte, about 17 to 18 miles north of Bly in Klamath County, got a lookout in 1931.[^2][^3] The Klamath Forest Protective Association's 1931 annual report says it built a road and a telephone line to the top and set up a small lookout tower.[^2] A June 1931 newspaper item described the road as nine miles of fire protection road from the Bly-Silver Lake road to the summit.[^2]

The sources disagree on the building. The register says a cabin built in 1933 was replaced in 1950 by a 30-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10-foot cab.[^1] firelookout.com and the Rex page both date the steel tower to 1954.[^3][^2] A note on the Rex page says it was 90 percent complete that year, and new steel steps were finished in 1956.[^2]

Lookouts worked the butte in the 1940s.[^2] A 1940 entry says the lookout was staffed for 180 days.[^2] A 1943 newspaper item said Mrs. Rosella Cline Thompson was stationed at the butte, and her husband was to be the warden.[^2] In July 1948 a relief man covered the lookout over the Fourth of July holiday.[^2]

In August 1945 a Herald and News story about Japanese balloons said that lookouts at Shake Butte had seen one come down in their area three weeks earlier, and that it was never found.[^2] In August 1967 the Shake Butte lookout spotted a small ground fire that lightning had started in a snag, and a crew put it out.[^2]

The register and firelookout.com both say the tower was used for emergencies in the 1970s and is now gone.[^1][^3] Neither gives the year it came down.

A 1951 list noted that this lookout sat on Forest Service land.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the summit is open to visitors.
