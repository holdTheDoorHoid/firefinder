Red Rock Peak lies in Lemhi County, Idaho, about 28 miles southwest of Salmon.[^1][^2] Its lookout was a two-story log cabin, and the sources date it to 1929.[^1][^2][^4] A Salmon Forest report from 1924 places the lookout's camp about a mile below the top of the mountain, which suggests a lookout was working the peak before the cabin was built.[^4]

The sources do not agree on the forest. The Forest Fire Lookout Association register and the Forest Lookouts page both name the Salmon National Forest, while Idaho Fire Lookouts gives the Salmon-Challis National Forest.[^1][^3][^4] None of the pages we read names who built the cabin or explains why it went on this summit. The Forest Lookouts entry puts the word "Kresek" in parentheses after the 1929 line, but gives no explanation.[^4]

Two items from the Recorder Herald, as quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, are the only staffing details we found. In June 1934 the paper said F.R. Barber, a former school superintendent, would be lookout at the Redrock forest station in the Yellow Jacket country that summer.[^4] In September 1944 it reported that a Mrs. Hayden had come in from the Red Rock lookout after spending the summer there. The item does not say she worked as the lookout.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts says it has no documented staffing history for the site.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts page gives 1975 as the year the lookout was removed, with no further detail.[^4] The register and the other two sites describe the lookout as gone but give no date.[^1][^2][^3]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors. The Forest Lookouts page quotes a survey marker description that says a lookout house crowned the other small rocky point, about 500 feet southeast of the marker. The page credits that description to the U.S. Geological Survey in 1912.[^4] If that date is right, a lookout house stood on the summit 17 years before the 1929 cabin. None of the other sources describes a building there before 1929, and we could not reconcile the two dates. Anyone planning a trip should ask the national forest office about current conditions first.
