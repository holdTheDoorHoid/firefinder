The Wagontire Mountain Lookout stands on a summit in Harney County, about 32 miles southwest of Burns, at an elevation of 6,510 feet.[^2][^1] The Bureau of Land Management administers it.[^1] The structure dates to 1967 and is the first and only lookout ever built on that summit.[^1][^2][^4]

It is a two-story building.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register describes the cab as a live-in space that combines concrete block and a wooden frame.[^1] A 2022 trip report calls it a two-story BLM-style cab that needs repainting but is otherwise in good shape.[^4]

The sources disagree about staffing. The Register, which gives no date for this, says the lookout has summer staff each year.[^1] A visitor who went up on May 27, 2022, listed it as no longer staffed.[^3] firelookout.com says it is now available for emergencies.[^2] The sources do not say when regular staffing ended, and they do not name any fires the lookout watched.[^1][^2][^3]

The Register gives January 3, 2013 as the date it was registered, on the nomination of Howard Verschoor.[^1]

Getting there takes some work. The 2022 visitor turned off Highway 395 onto a gravel road with no sign, which led to a gate.[^3] The gate was open on that trip, but the other trip report says it seems to have been locked in earlier years.[^3][^4] From the gate, the walk to the summit is about three miles.[^3][^4] The lookout is kept for emergencies, so the sources do not suggest visitors can go inside. Check with the BLM's Burns District before heading out, and do not assume you may enter the cab.[^2][^4]
