American Camp Lookout stands on a 3,405-foot peak in Tuolumne County, within the Stanislaus National Forest.[^1][^3] Until a steel tower was built, the lookout station was a platform high in a large tree.[^2] In April 1927 the forest service and the state put a steel ladder on a tall yellow pine above Columbia, and the platform sat 60 feet above the base of the tree.[^2] In 1929 the local forest supervisor said a 50-foot steel tower with a glass enclosure would replace the tree platform.[^2] By February 1930 a crew of state forest rangers was building a 60-foot tower on American Camp Peak.[^2]

The register describes a 60-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, built by the California Department of Forestry in 1930.[^1] The Stanislaus album says the cab was meant mainly for watching state lands, with a secondary job of watching Forest Service land.[^3] A 1930 newspaper said the lookout was named for the large number of Americans in a nearby mining camp.[^2]

The 1929 plan had the state and the Forest Service each paying half of the lookout's dry-season wage.[^2] A March 1932 newspaper report said J.H. Kling, a prospector and fire lookout, was found dead after a cave-in in a mine tunnel near Rose Creek.[^2] The paper added that he had spent three years as the lookout at American Camp.[^2]

The register says the lookout ran until 1969 and that the site is now abandoned as a standing ruin.[^1] The Stanislaus album says it qualifies for the National Register of Historic Places.[^3] I found no notable fires tied to this lookout in these sources. The register added it on April 24, 2017.[^1]

I found no current opening notice or visiting information. The album places the lookout in the Mi-Wok Ranger District, so ask there before you go, and do not climb the ruin.[^3]
