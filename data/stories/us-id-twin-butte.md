Twin Butte Lookout stood on Twin Butte, about 12 miles east-southeast of the Moose Creek Ranger Station, at 6,878 feet.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on the early dates.[^1][^3][^4] The Forest Service list gives 1925 for a 20-foot platform and 1929 for a log cabin with a cupola.[^1] Firelookout.com puts the platform in 1928 and the cabin in 1929.[^3] Forest Lookouts quotes Kresek for a log cabin with a cupola in 1925.[^4]

An undated inventory of the Moose Creek district, quoted by Forest Lookouts, describes the 1929 lookout as having a rock and cement foundation, a high front porch, and a tiled roof with "TWIN" in white tile.[^4]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Eldon "Pat" Dunlop under Known Staff, with no dates, and asks readers for more on who staffed the tower, or when.[^2] A bear broke into the lookout on August 21, 1947.[^4]

The lookout was destroyed in 1951.[^1][^2][^3][^4] In 1957 its administration moved from the Bitterroot National Forest to the Nez Perce National Forest.[^4] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts both name the Nez Perce National Forest as the agency today.[^3][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as gone.[^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts describes the site as a hike-in.[^2] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the site, and none says whether the site is open to the public.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
