The first lookout on Dunn Peak was a 20-foot pole tower of the L-4 type, built in 1932.[^2][^5] The cab that stands there now is an R-6 flat cab on a 20-foot treated timber tower, built in 1958, and the register gives that year as the build date.[^1][^2] Both dates are right for different structures, as firelookout.com explains.[^2]

The peak sits about eight miles northwest of Avery, at 5,607 feet.[^2] Dunn Peak was Don Hammond's first post, and the couple's fire seasons were in 1972 and 1973. Nancy Sule Hammond later wrote about them in *The Last Lookout on Dunn Peak*.[^4] The cab measures fifteen feet on each side and stands two stories off the ground.[^4] Forest Lookouts gives 1972 as the last year the tower was staffed full time.[^5] The Forest Service then made the call to close the lookout. In the summer of 1973 the couple spent time at the Middle Sister Peak tower, ten miles southeast of Avery.[^4]

The tower went to emergency status in the 1970s, and today the site has electronic equipment and solar panels.[^3] The register says the cab no longer seems to serve as a lookout, and that it houses communication equipment.[^1]

Access is unclear. An off-road vehicle route listing on OuterSpatial shows motor vehicles, bicycles, horses and pedestrians as allowed.[^6] A visitor in July 2024 had been told the gate was always locked, but found it open, and reported that the lowest flight of stairs is gone.[^3] I found no official rule on whether visitors may climb the tower, so ask the Forest Service before planning a visit.
