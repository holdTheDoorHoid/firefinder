The Brule Fire Tower stood near Brule Lake in Cook County, Minnesota, and forum posters called it the Brule Lake Lookout Tower.[^1][^4] The county's tower survey lists the U.S. Forest Service as its owner, and the tower was also called the Star Lake lookout.[^1] Survey notes from 1934 and 1958 place a survey station near the center of its base, which suggests the tower was standing by 1934.[^1]

A 1966 photograph shows a steel tower with a cab far bigger than the usual Minnesota design, and a platform circling the cap.[^2] A piece of the steel left at the site carries a Jones & Laughlin stencil.[^2] The site's author took the stencil and the steel as signs that the tower was not a standard design.[^2]

The 1936 fire season was a memorable one in the Sawbill country.[^3] On Saturday, July 11, the afternoon was unusually hot, and heavy lightning came before a shower that passed north of Sawbill Lake.[^3] The Brule Lake Lookout reported smoke at about the same time as the Kelso Lookout.[^3] Comparing the two reports placed the fire near the portage between Gordon and Cherokee lakes.[^3]

The tower has since been taken down.[^4] A 2012 forum reply says it came down years earlier, and it mentions a hiking trail that might pass where the tower once stood.[^4] An undated local history page says steel from the removed tower remains on the site, along with a stair section lying abandoned and a concrete anchor pad shown in a photograph.[^2] With the tower gone, there is nothing to climb.[^2][^4]

Getting to the old spot is hard.[^4] A 2020 party tried to find the trail using old maps.[^4] They said it was almost impossible to find, with fallen logs about every 10 yards, and judged that it had not been maintained in about a decade.[^4] Without gear for bushwhacking, they decided not to push on.[^4]
