Call Mountain Lookout is a 30-foot tower with a cab on top, on a ridge in the Call Mountains of San Benito County.[^1] The Register files it under the "Dutch Windmill" type: an L-601 tower with a BC-301 cab, put up in 1935.[^1] It credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building many towers of this kind in the 1930s, but does not name a crew for this one.[^1]

A July 1953 photograph by Mace Pewtherer, from the Division of Forestry collection now housed at the Cal Fire Museum, shows the tower's exterior.[^3]

Later alterations include aluminum sheeting, a steel fire-escape ladder, catwalk bracing and a composition roof.[^1] Metal bands were fitted along one leg to stop further cracking.[^1] In 1958 a small wood-frame generator building went up beside it, and a 10,000-gallon concrete water tank built in 1967 sits nearby.[^1]

A 2006 photo caption calls it a former CDF-staffed lookout that is now abandoned.[^2] I found no date for when staffing ended.

The Register accepted the tower in October 2019 as NHLR US 1341.[^1] The entry adds that restoration would improve its original build quality.[^1]

According to the Register, the public cannot easily get there: the site is roughly five miles from pavement and sits behind locked gates.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California-South table gives its status as "Private Property, no access," and names Cal Fire as the operator.[^4] The Register also names the Cal Fire San Benito-Monterey Unit as the administering agency. The "Private Property" label suggests the land may be privately owned, and I could not settle who controls it.[^1][^4] Cal Fire says it does not rent its lookouts.[^5] Please do not try to reach or climb the tower.
