A tower with an L-4 cab went up on Huckleberry Mountain in 1933, on a summit where a platform had stood since the 1920s.[^1][^2] The register and firelookout.com give its height as 22 feet, but a Baker newspaper item from September 1933 and a 1967 Forest Service memo say 20 feet, so the height is not settled.[^1][^2][^3] A Civilian Conservation Corps crew erected the native timber tower that fall, with a cab 14 feet square.[^3]

Lookouts were posted on the summit from at least 1921 into the 1960s.[^2][^3] In 1921 the lookout was the first to see a timber fire between Whitney and Sumpter, which destroyed eighty acres.[^3] In 1934 it spotted a lightning fire of about a third of an acre, and two men from Sumpter had it under control by the next morning.[^3]

A Forest Service inspection in November 1967 found the four tower legs structurally safe but showing heart rot. It estimated that repairs would give at most five more years, and it said replacement or abandonment should be planned for after 1973.[^3] The register and firelookout.com put the end in 1974, the register saying destroyed and firelookout.com saying abandoned. The Forest Lookouts page dates the destruction to 1976.[^1][^2][^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today. A 1946 survey description places a bronze disk in the bedrock about 58 feet from the old tower.[^3] The Oregon lookout list marks the site as gone.[^4] We found no current access information, though a 1961 account mentions a mile-long Huckleberry Trail.[^3] The land is part of the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest.[^1]
