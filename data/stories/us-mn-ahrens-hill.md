Ahrens Hill is a high hill near Brainerd in Crow Wing County, overlooking Gilbert Lake and the Mississippi River.[^1] The Minnesota Forest Service, now the state Department of Natural Resources, had a steel tower on the hill by 1911.[^2][^3][^4] A 1911 newspaper plan called for a 40-foot steel tower and said no more desirable place could be found.[^1] The county lists give that first tower 50 to 60 feet, so the sources disagree on its height.[^2][^3]

In 1928 eight men from the Northern Pacific shops bought the roughly 64-acre tract to keep its natural beauty.[^1] That November the Brainerd Daily Dispatch reported that a replacement steel tower with an enclosed observation cab would be built that fall.[^1] By December a 47-foot tower with a stairway was going up.[^1] The county lists date the tower to 1929 and call it the forest service's second lookout on the hill.[^2][^3] By 1931 the state leased an acre there at nominal rent, and the site had that stairway tower and a cabin for the observer, sided with logs.[^1] A 1960 survey described the standing tower as a steel tower on four legs with a metal observation room at the top, and put its overall height at 65 feet.[^2][^3]

The earliest staffing note we found is from 1927, when John R. Crossman of Pine River served as tower watchman through the spring fire season.[^1] In 1929 a cabin for the watchman was planned, and J.W. Wicklund was to be stationed on the hill that year.[^1] The 1931 paper reported 300 tourists and other visitors a day on summer weekdays, with more on Sundays, coming to climb the tower.[^1]

In April 1929 vandals damaged the tower, and the district ranger put a padlock on the top and barred the public unless a state employee was present.[^1] The last staffing note we found is from 1943, when John A. Pederson, a ranger leaving for army training, had been stationed at the tower.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Minnesota list marks Ahrens Hill as gone.[^5] The 1960 survey is the last description of the standing tower in these sources.[^2][^3]

No source describes the site now, including what, if anything, remains on the hill or whether visitors can reach it.
