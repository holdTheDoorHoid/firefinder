Agency Hill sits about six miles north-northwest of Chiloquin in Klamath County.[^2] In 1929 the Klamath Indian Agency planned a crow's nest in a tall tree atop the hill, for use in May and June.[^3] A 106-foot tree cab went up in 1930.[^1][^2]

That same year a steel Aermotor LS-40 tower of about 107 feet went up.[^3][^5] Two sources say the Indian Service built it in September and October.[^3][^4] A county list credits the Crater National Forest with erecting it, with help from the Indian Agency.[^5] The register tells a different story. It says a 110-foot steel tower and house replaced the tree in 1932.[^1] A 1931 newspaper item and a 1953 article give 107 feet, and the county list agrees, while a 1940 survey note gives 105.[^3][^5]

Our sources name a few lookouts. In 1941 Roy A. Yoder, spelled Yader in one item, was employed as a forest ranger lookout there.[^3] Marian Doty spent the summer of 1943 on the lookout.[^3] By 1953 a newspaper said Mrs. Claude Parazoo had been the lookout for the past eight years.[^3] In 1949 one lookout was terminated after 31 days on duty, and Delphine Parazoo finished the season.[^3]

WillhiteWeb says that in 1961 the Oregon Department of Forestry took over maintenance and operation, though the register names the Klamath Indian Reservation as administrator.[^4][^1] Two fires were reported from the station in 1969.[^3] The lookout was dismantled in 1979.[^1][^2][^3][^5] A county list says the 1930 crow's nest tree has come down and is heavily decayed.[^5]

A WillhiteWeb photo gallery has captions reading "Tower footings" and "Ground house foundation," but the captions give no date.[^4] No dated source we read describes the site's condition now. WillhiteWeb lists the site's hiking distance as drive-up.[^4] Because the register names the Klamath Indian Reservation as administrator, ask the administrator before going.
