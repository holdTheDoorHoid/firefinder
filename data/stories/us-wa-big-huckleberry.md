The Big Huckleberry lookout stood on the summit of Big Huckleberry Mountain, on the divide between Panther Creek and the Little White Salmon River, just north of the Columbia Gorge Scenic Area.[^4] The Weebly index of Washington lookouts says the site was developed as a lookout point in 1920.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a 1920s cab that was swapped in 1931 for an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] Willhite writes that an L-4 went on top in 1931.[^4] An L-4 was a 14-by-14-foot frame cab that could be set atop tall firetowers, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^6]

Few records describe the lookout's working life. Willhite says little is known about the old lookout on the summit.[^4] Panoramic photographs were taken there on July 9, 1941.[^3] A Geodetic Survey team referred to the lookout in 1946.[^4] I found no source that names who staffed it or any fire it reported.

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the cab is gone, and the Weebly index lists the site as removed.[^1][^3] Willhite found some guy-line anchors, glass and cable on the summit, but he could not tell when the lookout was destroyed.[^4]

The summit is reached by a short spur trail that branches off the Pacific Crest Trail.[^4][^5] The Washington Trails Association calls it another former fire lookout site, with views that are still quite good, though not as all-encompassing as at Grassy Knoll.[^5] WTA describes the trail as a steep, undulating path and mentions a rough road approach.[^5] Willhite's page gives no date for his visit, so his description of what remains may be out of date.[^4]
