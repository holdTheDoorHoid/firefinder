The Pack Forest lookout stood in Pierce County, Washington, on University of Washington land.[^1][^3] Sources disagree on where it stood relative to Eatonville. Firelookout.com puts it two miles south of town, and a 2012 blog post says two miles east.[^2][^4]

The University of Washington College of Forestry had the tower built in 1929 to guard its Pack Forest.[^1] It was a 66-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and the site also had a ground cabin for living quarters.[^1][^3]

Willhite includes 1934 panoramic photos that show the view from the tower.[^3] The USGS set a monument called PACK at the site in 1941.[^3] The following year the AWS used the tower for a short time to spot airplanes during the war.[^3] Willhite's photo captions include AWS observers and say the setup began in January 1942. The site was dropped by April because other sites gave good coverage, and it was no longer in use by May 2.[^3]

Willhite says fire use continued until the lookout was given up at some point in the 1950s.[^3] Firelookout.com gives 1950 as the abandonment year, and the register says the tower was removed in the 1950s.[^2][^1] Willhite says the lookout's final location is unknown.[^3] A reader who replied to a 2012 blog post says relatives took it apart and moved it to a ranch in Alder.[^4]

The old lookout site sits at the forest's high point.[^3] A short side trail from the road leads to the highest spot, where a memorial rock stands.[^3] Willhite places four tower footings in the brush about 15 feet to one side, slightly downhill from the rock.[^3] Willhite says all the roads and trails in the forest are open to hiking.[^3] The university says hikers may use Pack Forest, but its site posts a notice about active logging and temporary recreational closures, so check before you go.[^5]
