Pickett Butte takes its name from William T. Pickett, a homesteader who rode into the Jackson Creek drainage on horseback in 1898 and settled near the butte that now carries his name.[^2] The Forest Service put up the first lookout there in 1934, a 25-foot pole tower.[^2][^3] That structure didn't last: in 1948 the district replaced it with a 41-foot treated-timber tower topped by an L-4 cab — the National Historic Lookout Register describes the cab as 14 feet square, while Recreation.gov's facilities listing calls it a 12-by-12-foot room — the building that still stands today.[^1][^2][^3] One register lists the construction date as 1958 rather than 1948, so the exact year of the current tower is not fully settled between sources.[^1][^2]

The lookout sits on the Umpqua National Forest in southwest Oregon, giving a clear view over the Jackson Creek drainage and toward the peaks of the Rogue-Umpqua Divide.[^2][^3] It was staffed as an active fire lookout for decades, continuing through the 1998 fire season.[^3] After that it moved onto the Forest Service's recreation rental program, but demand and fire-detection needs brought it back into service: it was staffed again starting in 2001, and the Forest Service has kept using it for summer fire watch ever since, even while renting it out the rest of the year.[^1][^3]

Today Pickett Butte works a dual role. It's on the National Historic Lookout Register, registered there in September 2000, and it remains in use for actual fire lookout work during fire season.[^1] Because it is still equipped with a fire finder and other lookout gear, overnight guests get a close look at a working historic site rather than a stripped-down cabin.[^1] The cabin can be rented through Recreation.gov from October 15 through July 15, outside the fire season.[^1][^2] Reaching it requires a climb up a steep, narrow 40-foot stairway, and the access roads are not plowed or maintained for passenger cars, so visitors in winter should be ready for four-wheel-drive travel or, in snow, a hike, ski, or snowmobile ride of up to four miles.[^2]
