Eagle Creek Ridge was a Forest Service fire lookout in the Mount Hood National Forest, about 11 miles east of Estacada in Clackamas County.[^1] The Forest Service built an L-4 ground cab on the site in 1937, about an eighth of a mile east of a minor summit and downslope from it.[^2][^1] The site sits at about 2,600 feet (792 m).[^2]

The early record is not tidy. A panoramic photograph captioned as the Eagle Creek lookout, dated 20 March 1934 and credited to the National Archives' Pacific Northwest Region, is earlier than the cab's build date.[^2] None of the sources says whether a structure stood there before 1937.

A district historical report for 1953, quoted in a note dated 10 February 1954, says the tower had recently been condemned and destroyed.[^3] The report adds that the loss left the Estacada district with only one full-time lookout.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the cab was gone by 1957, and firelookout.com gives the same year.[^2][^1] Read as a latest possible date, that does not rule out the earlier loss the 1953 report describes.[^2][^3]

Nothing in the sources describes what, if anything, remains on the site today. A June 2026 trip report on the Oregon Hikers forum places the lookout on Eagle Creek Butte, at the western extreme of Wildcat Ridge.[^4] The writer could not locate the branch trail to the former lookout at the butte's eastern end.[^4] The forest road the trip route meets, FR 3626-255, has long been abandoned.[^4] The land is part of the national forest,[^1] but no source gives a visitor route to the site itself.
