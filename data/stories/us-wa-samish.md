Samish Lookout sat on Butler Hill, a rise that stands alone in the broad Skagit River valley, about six miles northwest of Sedro-Woolley.[^2][^4] Its elevation is 886 feet.[^1][^2] Willhite reports it was built around 1938 as a two-story, 10-by-10-foot smokehouse.[^4] The Register's build-year text is cut short, but firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb both give 1938.[^1][^2][^4]

The lookout was on a supplemental list for the Aircraft Warning Service and was active from August 27 to September 15, 1942.[^3][^4] Willhite quotes an AWS letter describing the lookout as manned by day, with voluntary service only.[^4] He thinks local volunteer observers worked during the day, and that paid staff were considered but never hired.[^4]

Dr. Quentin Belles, who began as summer help under Harry Osborne at age 13, recalled Samish as one of several points used to triangulate fire locations by azimuth.[^5] Reports came in by radio to the Sedro-Woolley fire station.[^5] He said he may have dreamed it.[^5]

Willhite's page says an inventory records the lookout as destroyed around 1952.[^4] The Register says it was removed that year, while firelookout.com and Forest Lookouts say destroyed.[^1][^2][^3]

The Register names the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as administrator.[^1] Willhite's page lists the landowner as private land.[^4] He warns visitors not to go without permission, notes the area is heavily signed to keep people out, and says there is no access at this time.[^4] His photo captions mention anchors being used for a ham tower.[^4] The text does not say what, if anything, of the lookout itself remains.[^4]
