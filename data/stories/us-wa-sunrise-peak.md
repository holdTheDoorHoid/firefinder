Sunrise Peak is a former lookout site in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, in Skamania County, Washington.[^1] The Register lists its elevation as 5,892 feet (1,796 meters).[^1]

Willhite says that in 1924 Sunrise was a lookout camp with a tent and probably a telephone.[^3] In June 1929 a newspaper reported plans for towers on Sunrise butte and two other buttes, each to get a fire finder.[^4] Willhite dates the lookout house on the summit to 1930, when a newspaper reported that a man had gone up to build a station.[^3][^4] It describes an L-4 cabin on a 15-foot wood tower, with a 10-by-10-foot storage building below it.[^3] The Register and firelookout.com instead date a 15-foot pole L-4 tower, which replaced the 1924 camp, to 1934.[^1][^2] The sources conflict, and we could not reconcile the two dates.

A July 1930 Chehalis newspaper item says Frank Slagle was posted there for the summer and had been using a tent.[^3][^4] In August 1937 a forest fire burned through the telephone line to the lookout, leaving it cut off but not threatened.[^4] In July 1955 the Forest Service activated Sunrise as a ground observer post, with Duane A. Priebe stationed there.[^3][^4] Observers reported aircraft sightings by radio to the Randle headquarters.[^4]

Willhite says the lookout was removed in the early 1960s, and that a USGS party in 1964 reported it destroyed.[^3] The Register says it was removed in the 1960s.[^1]

The sources do not describe the summit today. A photo caption on Willhite's page says "Hand railing is still there," but it does not say what the railing belonged to.[^3]

Willhite's directions run from Randle along FR 23 (Cispus Road) to a signed road for the Sunset Trail, then up 5 miles to the trailhead.[^3] That last road was repaired at the end of 2019 after years of closure.[^3] From the trailhead the hike is 1.7 miles, with 1,400 feet of gain.[^3]
