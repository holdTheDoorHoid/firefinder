The page's record of the Daniel Boone lookout starts with the land. In 1942 the Missouri Conservation Commission acquired a 160-acre towersite, and a 1943 entry says a pole tower was erected there.[^1]

The 1949-50 annual report says a 60-foot steel tower replaced the pole tower.[^1] Newspapers from August and September 1950 describe a new steel lookout 70 feet tall, with a 14-by-14-foot cab and a catwalk, placed in the Daniel Boone Memorial Forest seven miles southwest of Jonesburg.[^1] An August 1950 article names Hurley Baker as towerman in charge and says the tower was open during the day, Monday through Saturday.[^1]

The sources disagree on height. The annual report gives 60 feet and the newspapers 70 feet. A 1970 U.S. Geological Survey description of the survey station on the roof, quoted on the same page, calls the tower about 125 feet high.[^1] We did not find a way to settle which figure is right.

The fiscal 1975-76 report notes new garage doors and a new garage roof at the tower site. The fiscal 1977-78 report says the siding and catwalk on the tower were replaced.[^1] The latest entry we found is for fiscal 1977-78, and no source we read says whether the tower still stands.

The 1950 articles call the land the Daniel Boone Memorial Forest. The Missouri Department of Conservation's current page for the Daniel Boone Conservation Area gives driving directions from Jonesburg: take Route Y southwest five miles, then Tower Road southwest two miles.[^2] We have not confirmed that the two names refer to the same land, or whether the tower still stands there. Check with the department before you go.
