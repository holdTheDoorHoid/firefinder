The Willamette National Forest planned a new glass-enclosed lookout on Jump-Off Joe in 1935, and a Forest Lookouts page says an L-4 house went up there in 1936.[^1][^2] The register and firelookout.com give 1935 for the L-4 cab.[^1][^3]

The cab sat on a large flat rock with a sheer cliff on its north side.[^2] From the cab, a fireman could survey the upper South Santiam basin.[^2]

Newspaper items quoted by Forest Lookouts document staffing in three years. In 1937 Wayne Wood was on duty there for the summer, and in 1939 William C. Cook was named emergency lookout fireman. In 1943 volunteers from Mountain States Power Company used their vacation time to staff the lookout.[^2]

The cab came down in 1965. The register and Forest Lookouts call it removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit it.
