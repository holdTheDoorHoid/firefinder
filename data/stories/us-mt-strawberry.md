Strawberry Butte tops out at 6,100 feet in the Elkhorn Mountains.[^3] A lookout went up on the summit in 1941: a 65-foot steel Aermotor tower with a small metal cab at the top, and a one-story log cabin on the ground below.[^1][^5] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it the last structure of its type on the Helena National Forests, and lists it as US 631, MT 39, registered March 1, 2005.[^1] The cabin follows a C-46 plan, one of the standard designs used to house crews at intermediate stations and lookouts.[^3] The Forest Service says William Fox designed that plan.[^3]

The sources I checked do not say when the tower last kept a fire watch, who staffed it, or whether it spotted any notable fires. Anyone with Forest Service station records would be the best place to fill that gap.

Recreation.gov now describes the tower as no longer an active lookout and not open to the public.[^2] The cabin has found a second life as a rental. The Forest Service says the cabin can be rented all year,[^3] and an undated Flickr photo post says the Helena Ranger District rents it out year-round for $50 a night.[^4] Firelookout.com instead calls it a wintertime recreation rental.[^5] Two sources point to year-round use, so this story follows them, but the disagreement is real.

Today the cabin is the part of the site a visitor can use. The Recreation.gov listing says there is no water on site, so guests bring their own.[^2] The Forest Service also says the last mile of access is non-motorized from October 15 to May 15.[^3] Because the tower itself is closed to visitors, the sensible plan is to book the cabin and leave the tower alone.[^2]
