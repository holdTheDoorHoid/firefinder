The Georgia Forestry Commission completed the Seminole County Fire Tower in 1952.[^1] The register calls it a late-generation design: a 100-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] It was last staffed in 1988, and the register says it is still operational for emergencies.[^1]

That timing falls within a wider change. In the late 1980s the Georgia Forestry Commission moved away from staffed towers toward aerial patrols, and many towers were scrapped and sold.[^2] A few of the towers still standing are used as a backup when the air service is down.[^2]

The tower was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on January 2, 2008, as US 717 and GA 12.[^1] The register does not describe any particular fire the tower fought, and it does not say whether visitors may climb it.[^1] Because the register says it is still operational for emergencies, ask the Georgia Forestry Commission, which administers it, before visiting.[^1]
