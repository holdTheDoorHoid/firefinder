Buck Mountain, in Lake County on the Fremont National Forest, was checked for fires from its summit before any lookout is mentioned in the sources.[^1] In 1910 T.C. Johnson went up daily, walking or riding, to look for fires, and a camp was established at the Gaylord place.[^1]

In July 1911 Walt Dutton was told to ride up Buck Mountain every day, its top about a mile and a half to the east, and watch the surrounding country for fires.[^1][^2] His account, dated November 1962, says that no established lookouts stood in that area at the time, and that no telephone was closer than Thomas Creek Ranger Station.[^1][^2] That August he spotted his first forest fire, a dark, billowing plume rising above the Parker Hills about six miles north.[^1]

By 1916 a ranger's journal refers to a lookout on the mountain.[^1] The journal says that summer Ranger J.S. Elder and Guard Shanyo visited it on brief outings lasting anywhere from about twenty minutes to two hours, then went back to telephone-line construction and other Forest duties.[^1]

No source describes the summit today, what remains there, or whether it is open to visitors.

[^2]: History of the Fremont National Forest, Chapter 3, npshistory.com, https://npshistory.com/publications/usfs/region/6/fremont/history/chap3.htm (accessed 2026-10-21).
