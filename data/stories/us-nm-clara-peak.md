Clara Peak Lookout stood on the Espanola Ranger District of the Santa Fe National Forest, in Rio Arriba County, New Mexico.[^2] The Forest history says the Forest built Encino Lookout in 1950 and Clara Peak in 1951, and that these were the last lookouts it erected on new sites.[^1]

Under a 1951 cooperative agreement, the Forest supplied fire protection for the area, and the SCS and the Indian Service covered a large share of the building cost.[^1] The register describes the cabin as a 14-by-14-foot L-4 ground house of stuccoed cinder block.[^2] The register says the outhouse, storage shed and cistern went up with the lookout.[^2] The Forest history says the storage shed was moved from Puye Lookout, so the two sources disagree on that point.[^1]

Apollinario (Paul) Trujillo manned Clara Peak from 1962 to 1975, and his wife, Adelires, served as relief.[^1] The history says the lookout was regularly staffed through 1984.[^1] A run of wet summers, and the Los Alamos National Laboratory developing its own ways of detecting smokes, led to reduced staffing in the late 1980s.[^1]

The Forest history says the laboratory reportedly developed other detection methods, which ended its reliance on the lookout and contributed to its closure.[^1] The register only brackets the removal, placing it somewhere between 1988 and 2015.[^2] The Forest history says the lookout was demolished in 1993 or 1994.[^1] A road guide dates the demolition to the winter of 1993-1994.[^3]

The register includes a Google Earth view labeled as imagery of ruins, and no source we read describes the site in more detail.[^2] The road to the summit starts at State Highway 565, which the road guide calls 31 Miles Road, and runs about 3.1 miles.[^3] The road is unpaved and set high in the Jemez Mountains, and it is not plowed in winter.[^3] The guide suggests that drivers stop about a quarter mile from the peak and hike the rest, unless their vehicle is rugged and has good clearance.[^3] Check with the Santa Fe National Forest before you drive up.
