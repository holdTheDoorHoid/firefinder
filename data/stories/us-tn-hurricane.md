A tower called Hurricane in Moore County appears on a 2014 National Register study's list of Tennessee fire lookout towers still standing that March.[^1] The study covers the Tennessee Division of Forestry's towers, which it says the Division oversaw at a peak of 208 in the late 1960s.[^1] The same list has a second Hurricane, in Wilson County, so the name alone does not identify this tower.[^1]

A Tennessee tower list kept by Tom Dunigan gives the Moore County tower an elevation of 1,148 feet and marks it as standing.[^2]

We found no record of when it was built, who staffed it, or whether it ever spotted a fire. The sources also give no owner and no visiting policy, so do not climb the tower or go onto the land without the owner's permission.

[^1]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers multiple property document, National Park Service, https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/8986b1c9-543a-4d56-9482-166cde732e78 (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Tennessee lookout towers, tnlandforms.us (Tom Dunigan), https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
