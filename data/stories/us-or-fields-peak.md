Fields Peak is the highest point in the Aldrich Mountains, in the Malheur National Forest of Grant County, Oregon.[^1][^4] It stands at about 7,362 feet.[^1][^2]

A standard lookout house was built on the summit in 1922.[^3][^4] A July 1922 newspaper report said lumber had reached the Murderers Creek ranger station, ready to be taken up to the summit.[^3] A December 1922 report said the house was secured with steel cable.[^3] The register gives 1933 for the first cabin, but firelookout.com and the 1922 reports agree on 1922, so we use that year.[^1][^2][^3]

A lookout was on the summit by August 1921, when a newspaper named John Scharff as the lookout.[^3] On July 27, 1922, the lookouts at Fields Peak (Hinkle) and Strawberry (Faiman) both reported a small puff of smoke from an oil-burning wood-saw engine on private land outside the forest, and the ranger headed straight for it.[^3] In 1924 O. L. Starr was appointed to the lookout.[^3] A 1937 account describes a lookout dispatcher on the summit during a dry lightning storm, trying to take fire reports from three other lookouts.[^3] In 1942 Wilma McKearn is listed among the known lookout personnel.[^2]

The register says an L-4 cab replaced the earlier cabin in 1941, and firelookout.com also lists an L-4 cab from that year.[^1][^2] Both give 1967 as the end of the cab.[^1][^2] The register says it was removed, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2] The Oregon history lists it as removed, with no year.[^3]

A hiking guide says the summit still has detritus from the old lookout structure, and that the old building's foundation is still visible there.[^4] The old lookout road is now a trail and ATV track.[^4] The guide describes an out-and-back hike from the McClellan Trailhead and calls it family friendly, so the summit appears open to hikers.[^4] It also notes that a snowdrift or two can block the way to the top until June.[^4]
