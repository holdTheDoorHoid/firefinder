Mendenhall Peak sits above Little Tujunga Canyon at the western end of the San Gabriel Mountains.[^1] The Forest Service administered a fire lookout on its summit, which the sources date to 1936 or 1937.[^2][^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1937 and describes a California Region 5 cabin on a 10-foot open timber tower.[^2] Angeles Adventures gives 1936, so the two sources disagree by a year.[^1] The lookout was also known as Highline Lookout.[^2]

Arthur Giesen was on the lookout in 1949, and Peggy Hotchkiss served there from 1960 to 1966.[^1] In 1960 a brush fire set deliberately in Little Tujunga threatened the lookout. One lookout had to leave her post and was rescued by helicopter.[^1]

The lookout was lost in 1975.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register blames the Big Tujunga Fire for its loss and says the site was never rebuilt.[^2] Another history dates the loss to November 1975 and says everyone on site was evacuated safely.[^3] Crescenta Valley Weekly describes the Mill Fire starting high in the Angeles Forest on Saturday, November 22, and then burning through Big Tujunga Canyon.[^4] The names differ, but the dates and place line up, so these sources appear to describe one fire.[^2][^3][^4] Angeles Adventures says the blaze razed 43 buildings, including the lookouts on Josephine Peak and Mendenhall Peak.[^1]

The tower itself is long gone.[^5] According to SummitPost, the summit still has a concrete foundation, a water tank and the ruins of two small shacks.[^5]

To walk up, park on the right near the gate at Dillon Divide and walk around the gate onto the dirt road. Follow that road about four miles to Mendenhall Saddle, then take the abandoned road about a mile to the summit.[^5] SummitPost warns that the last section, from the gate at Mendenhall Saddle, is overgrown, with a couple of washouts to navigate.[^5]
