Bassoo Peak is a lookout site at 6,391 feet in Sanders County, Montana.[^1][^3] A June 1938 report in the Sanders County Independent-Ledger, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, placed the peak where the Cabinet Forest meets the Flathead Indian Reservation.[^2] The firelookout.com listing gives the agency as the Lolo National Forest and the reservation.[^1]

The first tower was a 30-foot pole platform, which appears in 1934.[^1][^2] In 1938 a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab followed it, and a surplus L-4 cab was fitted in 1964.[^1][^2] A 1940 survey description, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, gives the wooden tower's height as about 30 feet.[^2] That does not match the 50-foot figure for 1938, and the sources do not explain the gap.

The current lookout dates from 1983.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com describes a wooden cab on a 10-foot concrete base, and the Forest Lookouts page describes a two-story cab with a shed roof.[^1][^2] The tribes' fire program dates the current lookout to 1983, with a 12-foot cinderblock base and a 16-by-16-foot cabin, and says it opened for service in 1984.[^3] The two accounts disagree on the base height.[^1][^3]

The tribes' fire program says the peak was first posted in the late 1950s or early 1960s, which is later than the 1934 date above.[^3] The same page calls it the nicest lookout on the reservation and says it is manned every summer.[^3] We found no named lookouts or notable fires for Bassoo Peak in the sources we read.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Bassoo Peak as standing, with a two-story cab.[^4] The tribes list it among the lookouts on the Flathead Reservation.[^3] A 1938 report put it on the boundary between the reservation and the Cabinet Forest, so the sources leave its exact position unclear.[^2] We found no public visiting route. Anyone interested should ask the tribes' fire program before going there.
