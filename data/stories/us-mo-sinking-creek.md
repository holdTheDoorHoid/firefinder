The Sinking Creek lookout tower is in Carter County, inside the Mark Twain National Forest, and a 1934 paper placed the Sinking Creek camp about eight miles south of Fremont.[^1][^2] The Current Local reported on May 17, 1934, that steel was about half up on one tower, foundations for the second were nearly finished, and both towers would stand 100 feet tall.[^1] The same report said each tower would get lookout quarters, to be built that summer.[^1]

The Forest Service says the Civilian Conservation Corps constructed the tower in the 1930s, when it stood 100 feet tall with crew quarters.[^2] A 1962 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls it a four-legged steel structure with an enclosed observation room at the top, and puts its overall height at about 90 feet.[^1] The 100-foot and 90-foot figures disagree, and no source we found explains the difference.[^1][^2]

A February 16, 1950 notice in The Current Local asked the owner of a strayed sow and three young pigs to collect them at the Sinking Creek lookout tower.[^1]

On November 5, 1970, the Van Buren Current Local reported a heliport near the tower, where a helicopter was based to respond to fires on the Winona, Doniphan and Van Buren ranger districts.[^1] The Forest Service dates the heliport to the 1970s.[^2]

In October 2008 the CCC-built lookout and its related structures were refurbished through the Passport in Time program.[^1]

Near the trailhead, the Forest Service notes an old cabin and a fire lookout tower.[^2] Visitors may walk the grounds around the tower, but the Forest Service says they cannot climb it or go inside the buildings.[^2] The Sinking Trailhead connects to the middle of the Ozark Trail's Between the Rivers Section, and visitors generally find parking there without trouble.[^2]
