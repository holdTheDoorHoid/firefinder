Stag Dome is a rocky outcrop in Fresno County that stands roughly 3,500 feet above the Kings River's South Fork.[^1][^3] The register gives the site's elevation as 7,640 feet and a build year of 1934.[^1] The register describes the cabin as a Plan R1 design on a short pole with an open timber tower.[^1] A California lookout listing shows 1933 beside the site name, so the build year is not settled.[^2]

The register places the site in the Sierra National Forest, but Bob Burd's trip report puts the summit on the boundary of Kings Canyon National Park and the Sequoia National Forest.[^1][^3] The register gives the removal date as unknown.[^1]

No source read describes what remains of the lookout today. Burd's 2018 hike began at the Deer Cove trailhead, a short way outside the national park boundary, and followed a Forest Service trail he found reasonably well kept.[^3] The last stretch was cross-country, because no trail reaches the summit.[^3] His report mentions a register he left at the summit and an old cast-iron stove at the first outcrop below, but it mentions no lookout parts.[^3] No closure appears in the sources read, but the only visit described is from 2018.[^3]
