Big Sandy Lake had a steel fire tower by 1950. A Department of Natural Resources annual report for 1950-51, quoted on Canada Lookouts, records a new steel tower put up at the lake in the department's Northern District.[^2] The Firetowers of Saskatchewan list, which gives no date, records a tower at the site 24.4 m (80 ft) high, of heavy steel.[^1] The site lies at 662 m (2,172 ft) above sea level, in Unorganized Division No. 18.[^3]

A local history quoted on the Firetowers of Saskatchewan site says Albert Sager was towerman there during the late 1960s and until his death in 1976.[^1] A towermen list on the same site gives Richard Sevigny's years as 1992 to 2002, with Big Sandy among the four towers he manned.[^4]

The sources disagree about the years after 1962. The 1962-63 annual report, as quoted on Canada Lookouts, says the Big Sandy Fire Tower was dismantled, with its material carried away by helicopter.[^2] The staffing entries above put towermen at Big Sandy after that date.[^1][^4]

A note in Sevigny's entry on the same towermen list says the last tower was manned after mid-August 2001, and that all towers were then condemned and due to be rebuilt.[^4]

Firelookout.ca's record gives no tower type or structure height for the site, and no notes on its condition.[^3] None of the four sources cited says what remains there today, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
