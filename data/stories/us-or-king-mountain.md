King Mountain rises about 18 miles north-northeast of Burns, in Harney County, on the Malheur National Forest.[^1][^2] Before the current tower, the summit held a cupola cabin, which two pages date to 1923.[^1][^3] A July 1923 newspaper note reports that building material for the lookout house had arrived on site and that a lookout was framing it.[^2] The register dates a cupola to the 1920s and a cabin to 1933, so the early chronology is not settled.[^4]

The present lookout went up in 1957, as a 10-foot steel tower with a steel cab.[^4][^2] The register calls this Forest Service steel design very functional, notes that it is unexpectedly quiet in strong wind, and says it takes little upkeep.[^4] A 1957 newspaper item says a contractor was building the tower and cabin that year.[^2]

In September 1924 the station was closed for the year, and its smoke chasers were relieved.[^2] A 1926 item reports that a newly graded road from the base of the mountain would serve the lookout that season.[^2] A structural inventory dates a 16-by-18-foot garage to 1934.[^2] In 1946 two lookouts, Mrs. Harold Patton and Mrs. Ray McKinnon, were to alternate on the post.[^2] The sources I read do not name a notable fire from the lookout's career.

By the July 2021 visit, the lookout was no longer staffed. The site was then used for communications equipment, and the Malheur National Forest had proposed taking the lookout down.[^3] Another page describes the cab as available for emergencies.[^1] The sources do not say when that removal was proposed.

The latest visit report I found is from July 2021.[^3] The summit is reached by about 2.5 miles of Road 503, which leaves Road 2820, and the road is rough and rutted near the top.[^3] On that visit the writer parked and walked the final 0.7 miles.[^3] The sources do not say whether the cab itself is open to visitors, so check with the Malheur National Forest about road conditions and site rules before you go.
