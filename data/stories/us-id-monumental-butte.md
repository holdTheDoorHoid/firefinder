Monumental Butte is a former lookout site on the St. Joe National Forest in Shoshone County, Idaho.[^1][^2] The butte rises to 6,994 feet.[^3] The first lookout there was a log cabin, put up around 1919, and it burned in 1921.[^1][^3] The next cab, a 13-by-13-foot structure with an open roof platform, stood on the north butte from 1922.[^1]

Lightning destroyed that cab in 1924. The following year a cupola cabin went up on the south butte.[^1][^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Fred Dugan as staff in 1919 and 1924, and Lester A. Spurgeon for 1940 to 1941, noting that Spurgeon moved about halfway through the season.[^3]

Lightning struck again in July 1933. According to a Spokesman-Review report quoted on a Shoshone County lookout history page, the strike surged in over the telephone line and shattered the cabin, but the lookout man was not hurt.[^4]

A reminiscence in volume four of *Early Days in the Forest Service* calls the lookout "Monumental Buttes" and says Pete Snyder held it for many years.[^5] The same volume recounts a night in 1926 when a lookout called Monumental counted 240 fires from its station. That account mentions Priest River and Nordman, so we cannot be sure it refers to this butte.[^5]

The cupola cabin was destroyed in 1969.[^1][^3] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the butte today. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as gone,[^2] and Idaho Fire Lookouts marks the site "Hike-In Destroyed."[^3]
