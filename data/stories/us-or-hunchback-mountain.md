Hunchback Mountain is in Clackamas County, in the Mt. Hood National Forest, about 12 miles west-southwest of Mt. Hood.[^1]

Firelookout.com dates three crow's nests along the ridgetop, a quarter mile apart, to the 1930s.[^1] The Roaming Civic, a blog that describes visiting old crow's nests in the Mt. Hood National Forest and finding remnants, says a crow's nest is usually a perch set atop a tree and reached by a ladder fastened to the trunk.[^3]

Both firelookout.com and the FFLA list call the site abandoned.[^1][^2]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.

The Forest Service's Hunchback Trail runs along Hunchback Ridge toward the Devil's Peak Lookout, and the Forest Service says that lookout is closed to public entry.[^4] No source we read connects that closed lookout to the crow's nests, so do not assume they are the same place.
