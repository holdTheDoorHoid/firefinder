Crawford Mountain in Thurston County had a fire lookout about four miles east-southeast of Tenino.[^1][^2]

The first structure was a two-story cabin, 10 by 10 feet, that the sources call a smokehouse.[^1][^3] It was used for fire detection in 1942.[^1][^3]

In 1955 a timber tower replaced the cabin. Its 14-by-14-foot cab was built of plywood and glass and topped with an aluminum roof.[^1][^3] The register calls the tower 40 feet tall, but a July 29, 1955 item quoted on the Forest Lookouts site says a 45-foot tower was being built.[^1][^4] Larry Hayes was the lookout that year.[^3][^4]

The register dates the removal of the replacement to about 1968.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says the building was torn down around that time, and that by 1969 the state was leasing the summit for a microwave facility.[^3]

A January 2023 trip report describes a bare summit strung with communication towers.[^5] WillhiteWeb's photo caption shows a building that uses the old lookout footings.[^3]

The gate on Johnson Creek Road was locked on that visit, and a sign there says the gate may be locked at any time.[^5] WillhiteWeb notes that the north access its author used is no longer allowed.[^3] None of these sources settles whether the summit is open to visitors today.[^5]
