Woody Mountain Lookout is a 46-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, at 8,006 feet in Coconino County, Arizona.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers it, and the register names the Peaks Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1]

The spot was in use well before the current tower. A Forest Lookouts timeline says that on January 20, 1912, Woody Mountain was chosen as a primary lookout point, with a man on duty at all times and a telephone connection to the Supervisor's office.[^2] The register says a tree stand stood on the site from 1910 to 1921, and a wood tower replaced it, standing from 1922 to 1936.[^1] The timeline records a wood tower with a cab in 1922, and says a CCC crew erected the present 46-foot tower in 1936.[^2] The register ties the current tower's construction to the end of the wood tower's use in 1936.[^1]

The register says Pacific Coast Steel provided the tower, one of relatively few towers that company supplied.[^1] The National Register record lists the CCC and Pacific Coast Steel Co. as its architects.[^3] It lists the tower in a multiple listing of national forest fire lookouts in the Southwestern Region, dated January 28, 1988.[^3][^4]

The register lists April 1, 2001 as its registration date, with a nomination by Dave Lorenz of the AZ/NM FFLA.[^1] The register gives no staffing status for the tower, and the timeline lists no staffing after 1912.[^1][^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list shows the tower as standing.[^5] The timeline has no entries after 1936.[^2]

I found no public visitor information, trail or rental listing for the tower in the sources I read, so whether the public may climb it is unknown. Check with the Coconino National Forest before planning a visit, and do not climb the tower unless the Forest Service says it is open.
