The Fanno Mountain lookout stood in western Polk County, Oregon, about two miles north of Valsetz.[^1] In 1939 the Civilian Conservation Corps built 8½ miles of truck road to the site, and at that location it also constructed a combined garage, tool house and woodshed and largely completed a cabin.[^1] The same year, enrollees from the Cold Springs side camp of the Arboretum camp erected a 100-foot timber tower.[^1] A 1941 newspaper described it as having a cabin on top, windows on all sides and a catwalk around it.[^1]

The two Fire Lookout records describe the first tower differently. They say a 99-foot pole tower stood in the 1930s and was replaced in 1940 by a 50-foot pole tower.[^2][^3] This story follows the newspapers and annual reports quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, which date the 100-foot tower to 1939.[^1]

In July 1940 the Polk County Fire association posted lookouts at Fanno Mountain, which was a new post that year.[^1] A 1941 newspaper named the Hayes as the first permanent occupants.[^1] In June 1942 the lookout was activated as an Aircraft Warning Service observation post, and a June 1942 report said it was manned by a husband and wife.[^1]

That same year, a fire that began when a Valsetz school teacher burned trash ran up the hillside toward the tower, according to a 2005 letter quoted on the Forest Lookouts page. A Dallas crew fought it.[^1]

On November 14, 1942, a gale of at least 90 miles per hour shattered the 100-foot tower.[^1] The cabin at its foot, where a man and his wife were living at the time, was undamaged.[^1] A new 100-foot tower was under construction by June 1944.[^1] By 1956 the old tower cabin was gone, and concrete footings for the new legs had been poured.[^1] In 1957 a new 40-foot tower with a 14-by-14 cabin was finished.[^1] The Columbus Day Storm destroyed the lookout on October 12, 1962.[^1][^2][^3] In 1964 the district traded the lookout property to the Willamette Valley Lumber Company for a building site at Philomath.[^1]

No source we read says what remains at the site today. The Forest Lookouts page prints a National Geodetic Survey data sheet for a survey mark stamped FANNO 1972, set in a concrete monument. Its reference marks sit on concrete posts that were legs of the old tower's foundation, but the excerpt does not say whether the marks or the posts survive.[^1] We found no information about who owns the land now or whether visitors are welcome, so anyone interested should ask the owner first.
