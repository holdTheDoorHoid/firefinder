English Cabin Fire Tower is listed on Lars Road in Torch River No. 488, Saskatchewan, at an elevation of 455 m (1,493 ft).[^1] A Saskatchewan tower list gives its height as about 24 m (80 ft), records it as light steel, and notes a new cupola in 1957.[^2]

The tower sits in the Fort a la Corne Forest Reserve.[^3] A forestry report for the 1915 to 1916 season says two steel lookout towers of 24 m (80 ft) were erected in that reserve, but it does not name English Cabin, so the record cannot be tied to this tower.[^4] By 1952 the tower needed major repairs to keep it in service, according to the annual report of the provincial Department of Natural Resources for 1952 to 1953.[^3][^5] The department completely overhauled it in 1963.[^3]

Towermen lists name several people who worked at English Cabin.[^5] Einer Wicker, listed with dates unknown before 1960, worked at Pasquia Hills, English Cabin and Thunder Mountain at various times.[^2][^5] He returned to Norway, his birthplace, in 1961 and died there in 1965.[^5] John Charles is listed there from 1986 to 1993, Allan Klamot from 1995 to 2002, and Joan Pelech from 2000 to 2002.[^5] Travis French worked there on a casual basis in 2000 and 2001.[^5]

The English Fire began on May 8, 2020, and was named for a fire tower near English Cabin where it was first reported.[^6] Global News called the location a destination for tourists, northeast of Prince Albert.[^6] According to the Saskatchewan Public Safety Agency, the blaze was human-caused and remains under investigation.[^6]

The towermen entries for English Cabin run to 2002, and Joan Pelech's entry notes a move to aerial detection.[^5]

Firelookout.ca gives no status for the tower. None of the sources we found describes what remains at the site today or whether visitors can reach the tower.[^1]
