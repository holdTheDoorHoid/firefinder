Schonchin Butte Lookout stands on a cinder cone inside Lava Beds National Monument, near Tulelake in northern California.[^4][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the lookout and the trail to it from 1939 to 1941, carrying every material up by hand.[^3]

The sources do not agree on the exact build years. The register dates the CCC's work there to summer 1939 and summer 1940, and the National Register nomination dates construction to 1940 and 1941.[^1][^2] The 1942 date on the doorstep records when the step was put in, not when the building was completed.[^3]

The lookout is a rustic, wood-framed, one-story building with a basement and a veneer of lava rock below the windows.[^2] Its Osborne fire finder and stand are still inside.[^1]

Roger Reid became the first lookout to staff it, in 1941.[^1]

Electricity arrived with a park-built power line in the 1950s, and that line was removed in the 1980s. Solar panels provide the power for a radio, a repeater and a few small lights.[^1][^3]

The monument got nearly $50,000 in 1993 for restoration.[^3] In June and July 1994 the interior was stripped of paint, and decayed boards and sheetrock were swapped out.[^3]

Listing dates also differ. Wikipedia says the lookout was added to the National Register on September 5, 2017.[^4] The register's own narrative says the building was on the Register by 1992, so the two sources disagree.[^1]

The register says the lookout is staffed roughly May through September.[^1] The National Park Service says regular use has ended, but visitors may still hike to the top.[^3] The nomination describes it as still functioning as a fire lookout, with broad views of the region.[^2] The trail is about 0.7 miles each way, and there is no water at the top, so bring your own.[^3] The park's page was last updated December 6, 2023, so check the monument for current conditions before you go.[^3]
