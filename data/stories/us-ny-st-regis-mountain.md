St. Regis Mountain Fire Tower stands on St. Regis Mountain in the Adirondack Forest Preserve, in Franklin County, New York.[^1] An observer was posted on the mountain in April 1910. No tower was needed, because fires and erosion had left the summit open to view.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the lookout site to 1909, a year earlier.[^1] The tower itself went up in 1918, when the state put up a 35-foot Aermotor LS40 steel tower that still stands.[^1][^2]

George F. Brown was the first observer.[^2] During his first year, Brown logged 55 fires, and a few of them were dangerous.[^2] On May 31, 1915, a small fire, probably started by fishermen, was pushed along by fast winds and destroyed 1,550 acres of state land.[^2] Staffing ran without a break from April 1910 through 1990, which the Friends group says makes it the longest operating fire tower in the Adirondacks and in New York State.[^2] In 1990 the state closed the last operating fire towers in New York, St. Regis among them.[^2]

After that the tower deteriorated.[^2] The lower stairs were removed to keep the public off it, and it stayed closed for many years.[^2][^3] It was listed on the state and national registers in March 2005, and the Friends group was organized in May 2013.[^2][^5] A DEC management plan issued on November 7, 2014 allowed restoration to proceed.[^2] Friends members, volunteers and the Student Conservation Association installed railings, stairs and new cab flooring in 2015, and a new roof followed in 2016.[^2] The tower reopened to the public on September 1, 2016.[^2][^1]

Today the Friends group puts seasonal stewards on the tower.[^6] Its June 2026 update says two stewards are at the tower this hiking season, one from Friday through Sunday and one on Mondays and Tuesdays.[^6] A 2018 visit report found the tower in excellent condition, with the trail and parking area well signed.[^1] The Adirondack Explorer puts the summit about 2.3 miles from a lakeside trailhead.[^3] A DEC web page still describes the tower as being restored so it can open, which does not match the 2016 reopening.[^4]
