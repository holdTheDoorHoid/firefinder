Packsaddle Mountain is a former lookout site in the Siskiyou National Forest, about 13 miles east-southeast of Brookings in Curry County, Oregon.[^1][^2] The site sits at 2,569 feet.[^1]

The register says the lookout cab replaced a 1913 frame shake cabin on a 15-foot platform tower.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page quotes 1914 and 1915 accounts that do not match this.[^3] In 1914 the lookout was working from a tent, and in 1915 a ranger reported plans for a lookout house.[^3] A 1939 forest history says that house was of the shake type, with a ceiled interior.[^3] The sources do not agree on the first structure, so this story does not settle it.

The next cabin, dated to 1930 by the 1939 history, was an R1-type lookout cabin.[^3] The register and firelookout.com instead date an L-4 cab with a gable roof to about 1933.[^1][^2]

Lookouts appear in the records for several years.[^3] In August 1914, after Louis Yarbrough failed to come on as lookout, a ranger secured Ralph Keizer to take the post.[^3] Paul Weller was the lookout in July and August 1933.[^3] The 1941 season ran 84 days, with reports sent to the Gold Beach station by telephone.[^3]

One incident stands out. In October 1917 a group that included Ranger W.J. Jones had a narrow escape at the lookout.[^3] Their bedding caught fire several times when sparks came through the shakes.[^3]

According to the register, the cab was left unused by the 1940s and was gone by 1964.[^1] firelookout.com gives its dates as 1933 to 1964.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page lists the site as removed, without a date.[^3]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, including whether any footings survive.[^1][^2][^3] None says whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3] The register names the Forest Service as administrator.[^1] Check with the Siskiyou National Forest before planning a trip.
