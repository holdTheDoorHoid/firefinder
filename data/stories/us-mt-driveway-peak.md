Driveway Peak sits roughly 10 to 11 miles west of Thompson Falls, in Sanders County, Montana.[^1][^2] Observations began in 1929, when a lookout climbed a tree close to the peak.[^3] A cabin was put up a short way down the ridge as living quarters for the lookout.[^3] Several years on, a platform went up, and lookouts sighted fires with an Osborne firefinder from it.[^3] Firelookout.com dates a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab to 1939.[^2]

Fred Benson spent the summer of 1929 on the peak, according to an October 1929 Sanders County Independent-Ledger item.[^4] In July 1957, Sterling Clawson, the lookout assigned there, reported a lightning-caused blaze and hiked half a mile down the slope from his post to extinguish it.[^4] The Forest Service built the last four miles of access road that year, and by July 1958 the road was open to visitors.[^3][^4] Firelookout.com lists Jack Truckner (1955), Robert Calkins (1958) and Steve Hart (1968) as lookout staff.[^2]

The lookout was retired in 1969 after its final season on duty.[^3] Accounts of what came next disagree. The 2020 Ledger article says the Forest Service brought down the building and pole structure afterward, while firelookout.com says the tower was destroyed in 1978.[^2][^3] Skiers often used the historic cabin near the base of the pole structure as a warming hut, until an accident destroyed it in 1991.[^3] A 2020 Forest Service proposal calls the last structure a rental cabin that burned accidentally in the early 1990s, blaming a poorly installed wood stove. It also says all three structures on the site burned, and that two of those fires were deliberately set once the buildings had deteriorated.[^1]

In 2020 the Forest Service proposed relocating the Priscilla Peak lookout, a cabin dating to 1929, to Driveway Peak, where it would be restored and placed in the district's cabin rental program.[^1] We found no record that the move was carried out.[^1] A 2020 article noted that Driveway Peak had gone nearly three decades without a historic structure of its own.[^3] The access road, NFSR 876, was then open to public motor vehicles all year.[^1] No source we read describes what stands on the peak today.
