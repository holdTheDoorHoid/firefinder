The Shine lookout stood on a hill above Shine, along Twin Lake Road in Jefferson County, about 10 miles east-northeast of Quilcene.[^2][^4]

The first structure was an AWS cabin, built in 1942.[^1][^2] A 35-foot wooden platform and a house took its place in 1949.[^1][^2][^4] A May 1949 Port Townsend Leader report, quoted by WillhiteWeb, said a new station had been built and that it was hoped to be in operation that July.[^4]

The register says the platform and house were removed in 1958. Firelookout says they were destroyed that year, and WillhiteWeb says removed or destroyed in 1958.[^1][^2][^4] WillhiteWeb also quotes a 1962 Port Townsend paper that dates the dismantling to 1957, the year a live-in tower went up at Skidder Hill.[^4] Forest Lookouts marks the site as gone.[^3]

The site entered the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register on January 20, 2012, as US 1207, WA 108, with Ray Kresek as nominator.[^1] The register lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as administrator.[^1]

The only description of the site today is WillhiteWeb's visit.[^4] Young trees now cover the site and block most of the outlook.[^4] The highest point seems to have a flat, open patch that grows nothing, and the author found nothing there.[^4]

The route WillhiteWeb describes is about two miles with 240 feet of climb.[^4] It starts at a gated dirt road off Thorndyke Road, then follows a faint skid road up the tree line toward the top.[^4] A photo caption names Olympic Resource Management at that gate.[^4] None of the sources says whether the public may use the route.
