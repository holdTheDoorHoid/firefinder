Westborough's fire tower stands on Fay Mountain, which at 707 feet is the highest point in town.[^1] The first tower on the summit began operating on October 11, 1911. It was 38 feet tall and had a small square cab where rangers could watch for fires.[^1][^2]

Lookouts staffed the station in its early years.[^3] A 1914 report, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says two lookouts, John Giblin and Michael E. Lyons, logged 385 fires that season.[^3]

In 1915 the first tower gave way to a 68-foot tower with a 10-foot cab.[^2][^1] The register says the earlier tower went to Chester, while Patch says it was dismantled.[^2][^1] Newspaper items from that spring, also quoted by Forest Lookouts, give 60 feet and then 71 feet for the new tower, so the early reports disagree with the register and Patch.[^3]

The Great New England Hurricane of 1938 destroyed that tower on September 21, 1938.[^3][^2] A temporary tower stood in its place until a 68-foot steel tower with three legs went up in 1939.[^2][^1]

Later the U.S. Army built a radar station on the summit, and a radar tower replaced the fire tower.[^1] When the Army stopped using the radar in 1970, the state built a new 85-foot fire tower on the radar tower's steel frame.[^1][^2] The register describes it as a new cab on the three-legged radar tower.[^2]

The state put the tower on its Standing Inactive List in 1982, the same year the register says use of the site ended.[^1][^2] The land then passed to the city of Westboro. Later it went to a private owner.[^2] The summit next carried commercial radio antennas.[^1]

By 2001 the last fire cab had been removed and set on the ground beside the tower, and the summit was fenced with chain-link.[^1]

Today the register describes the site as private property, reached by a private right-of-way, with no sign and no signed directions.[^2] The register says the cab was swapped for a platform that holds antennas.[^2] Patch, writing in 2011, describes a communications tower with a small building where the fire tower stood.[^1] The register also records that the tower is not staffed.[^2]

We found no public access to the site. Enjoy it from a public road if you can, and ask the owner before going any closer. Teenagers in town once climbed it as a coming-of-age custom. The structure is on private land now, and it is not one to climb.[^1]
