Scotchman Peak, the highest point in Bonner County at 7,009 feet, got a Forest Service lookout in 1922.[^4] The magazine calls that first setup a "rag camp," essentially a small tent beside a tree that a watcher climbed to look for smoke.[^4] The next structure, a cupola cabin, came four years later; the Forest Lookouts history dates it to about 1926 and gives it as a D-6 design.[^1][^4] A photo caption on the Idaho Fire Lookouts page says Dr. Broady, a doctor from Sandpoint, was stationed there in 1924.[^3]

A magazine article about mountain goats reports that two lookout staffers died from lightning strikes on the peak before the lookout was abandoned in the 1950s.[^4] The article gives no publication date in the text I read.

Two sources give the 1950s for abandonment.[^1][^4] The firelookout.com header, "R.I.P. 1926-1970's," puts the end in the 1970s, so the sources disagree.[^2]

Very little is left.[^4] According to the magazine, what survives is scraps of wood, hardware and part of the lower rock work.[^4] The route is about three miles long and gains nearly 3,700 feet.[^4] A photo caption places the goats in the Scotchman Peak Wilderness.[^4] The article also says the Forest Service closed the trail in September 2015 after several people were hurt in encounters with goats, and that it reopened in 2016.[^4] I found no source giving current access rules.
