The Lockhart (Alabama #3) Lookout Site is in Winston County, Alabama, with a listed elevation of 810 feet, or 247 meters.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added it on April 24, 2017, at the nomination of Andrew Zerbe, under the numbers US 1696 and AL 11.[^1] The Alabama Forestry Commission administers the site.[^1]

Alabama Forestry Commission records describe a 79-foot tower made of pressure-treated wood.[^1] Thomas Kaufmann, writing an opinion column for 1819 News, says that over 200 of these towers once dotted Alabama.[^2] He names aircraft as the most common explanation for the decline, because planes could see fires over a far wider expanse than a 100-foot tower could.[^2] He also says the state now keeps around a third of its former lookouts.[^2]

The register says the site has been reclaimed and that no trace of the tower exists today.[^1] With no tower left, there is nothing to climb, and no source we read says whether the grounds are open to visitors.[^1]
