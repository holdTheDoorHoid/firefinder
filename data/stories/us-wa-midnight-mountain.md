Midnight Mountain sits in the Okanogan National Forest, about 15 miles west-northwest of Twisp, Washington.[^1][^2] An L-4 lookout cab went up on the summit in 1931.[^1][^2][^3] Willhite's notes add that a camp probably stood on the spot before that.[^3]

A 1931 map on Willhite's site labels the place a guard station, and a 1938 map calls it a lookout.[^3] In July 1932 the Wenatchee World reported that 11 large sending and receiving sets were being installed at Okanogan and at the Midnight Mountain lookout station.[^4]

Willhite names Grover Brown as the lookout in 1932.[^3] FireLookout.com lists Colin "Scotty" Gillies among its known lookout personnel for 1933 to 1935.[^2]

Panoramic photos were taken from the roof, but the sources disagree on the year.[^3][^4] Willhite gives 1934, while Forest Lookouts gives May 26, 1936.[^3][^4]

A 1950 Coast and Geodetic Survey description called the building a standard Forest Service structure and placed the survey point at the roof's apex.[^4]

The cab's end is recorded two ways.[^1][^2][^3] Firetower.org and Willhite say it was removed in 1952.[^1][^3] FireLookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] The sources agree on the year but not on how the cab ended.[^1][^2][^3]

Today, no source describes a lookout standing on the summit. Willhite's photo captions include an undated line, "Pole still sticking up," and the 1950 station mark, according to USGS notes he cites, is no longer there.[^3] No source says whether the site is open to visitors.

Willhite's hiking notes describe a route of 5.2 miles one way from the Twisp River road, with about 4,400 feet of climb.[^3] He also reports that the Forest Service has ripped up an old ridge road and formally retired it.[^3]
