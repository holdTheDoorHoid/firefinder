Baty Butte is a 5,052-foot summit in Clackamas County, Oregon.[^1] Oregon Hikers places it at the western fringe of the Mount Hood National Forest.[^4]

The summit's first lookout was a tent equipped with a phone.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the Forest Service placed it there in 1914.[^1] Forest Lookouts and Oregon Hikers both date the first tent lookout to 1911, so the sources disagree on the start date.[^1][^2][^3][^4] In the 1930s the tent gave way to a ground L-4 cab.[^1][^2][^4]

Oregon Hikers says the lookout was among the first equipped with an Osborne fire finder, an alidade-type sighting device that W.B. Osborne of Portland invented.[^4]

In September 1919 the Molalla Pioneer reported that two men had been in charge of the Baty Butte lookout while they were away at Bagby Hot Springs.[^3]

The cab was gone around 1949.[^1][^2] Oregon Hikers dates the lookout's decommissioning to before 1950.[^4]

Oregon Hikers describes a brief scramble to the summit along the south ridge from the Baty Butte Trail, and says an ammo box holds the summit register.[^4] The same guide calls the southwest face precipitous and advises keeping back from its edge.[^4] None of the sources we read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit today.
