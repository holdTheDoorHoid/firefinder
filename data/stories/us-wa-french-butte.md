French Butte is a highpoint in Skamania County, Washington, within the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1][^2][^4] The first lookout here was a cabin from the 1920s, though none of the pages we read gives its exact year.[^1][^4]

In August 1929 a newspaper reported that the Forest Service was putting a lookout house at French Butte.[^3] The cabin that went up was L-shaped and 11 feet by 11 feet, with a cupola six feet square for observation.[^1][^2][^4]

A newspaper report from August 1953 says a graduate student from Washington State College kept solitary watch in a tiny cabin.[^3]

The sources disagree on when the cabin came down. The register says it was removed in the 1960s, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed in that decade.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says it was destroyed in the 1940s and that its parts went to a nearby Ferrous Point lookout station.[^4] The 1953 report describes a tiny cabin at the site, so if it is the same cabin, the 1940s date cannot be right.[^3]

WillhiteWeb calls the site a former lookout site. Its summit has no views, and the whole top is now wooded.[^4] None of the pages we read describes what, if anything, remains of the cabin today.

The site is reached by forest road. WillhiteWeb recommends high clearance on the dirt and gravel roads, and says to park at a four-way intersection just south of the summit.[^4] From there, the old road to the top is about a five-minute walk, though WillhiteWeb says it is overgrowing.[^4] On the way in, the road passes Pole Patch, an old Forest Service campground in huckleberry-picking country.[^4] We found no closure in the sources. Road conditions may have changed since the guide was written, so check with the Gifford Pinchot National Forest before you drive.
