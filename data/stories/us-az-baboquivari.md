Baboquivari Peak rises to 7,730 feet in Pima County, Arizona, on the Papago Indian Reservation.[^2] In the 1930s, Civilian Conservation Corps crews put up a fire lookout tower on the summit.[^2][^3] The CCC's Indian Division also built a road to the Baboquivari Campground.[^3]

The crews built the trail as well.[^3][^4] To make the lookout easier to reach, they put wooden ladders and platforms over the most dangerous stretches of the route.[^2]

A September 1938 newspaper item, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page, reported a telephone booth on the summit, where a call required a nickel.[^2]

Sources differ on what stood on top. A May 1947 visitor found a ranger cabin there in tumble-down condition, while the climbing guide calls it a fire lookout tower.[^2][^3]

A 1974 article says that a few years later the fire lookout was judged unneeded, and the ladder system was then abandoned. Over the decades, most of the ladders, platforms and stairways rotted and tumbled part of the way down the mountain.[^2] The Arizona lookout table lists the site as gone.[^1]

According to the climbing guide, the only reminders left today are a few steel supports drilled into the rock of the 4th class wall, plus concrete foundations and piles of wooden boards on the summit.[^3]

A second climbing page reports that some bolted angle iron is still left in scattered spots in the rock.[^4]

The summit sits on Papago Indian Reservation land, and that same climbing page says a permit is available as a download.[^4][^2]

The standard route is technical and has seen several accidents.[^4] Tucson climbers warn that relying on the weathered remains of the ladder system could result in a serious fall.[^2]
