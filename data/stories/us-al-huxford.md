The Alabama Forestry Commission is listed as the agency for a lookout called Huxford, in Escambia County, Alabama.[^1] Across Alabama, many of the early wooden towers were later swapped for steel ones during the New Deal.[^2] The Eastern U.S. Lookouts site records that in 2006 the tower was declared surplus and sold to a private buyer.[^1] It was then moved to a site in Butler County, though the page gives no date for the move.[^1] The page does not describe what remains at the Escambia County site today, or whether the tower still stands in Butler County.[^1] Because the tower was sold to a private buyer, check with the owner before visiting any site connected with it.[^1] Probably fewer than 75 of Alabama's historic lookout structures remain standing today.[^2] Many are in rough shape and unsafe, and climbing them is against the law.[^2] Please do not climb any tower you find.

[^2]: Thomas Kaufmann: Keeping Alabama's historic lookout towers, 1819 News, https://1819news.com/news/item/thomas-kaufmann-keeping-alabamas-historic-lookout-towers (accessed 2026-10-21).
