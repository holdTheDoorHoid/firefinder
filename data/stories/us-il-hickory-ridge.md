Hickory Ridge was a Forest Service fire tower in Jackson County, Illinois, on the Shawnee National Forest.[^1] A description written about 1942 says it was built for fire detection and offered a scenic view of the surrounding country from its cab.[^1] It stood half a mile south of a town.[^1] A two-day hiking trail, marked out in 1946, started at the tower, which lies seven miles southwest of Murphysboro.[^1]

In June 1949 a Murphysboro newspaper reported that the tower was one of 15 lookouts covering 475,000 of the 800,000 acres in the Shawnee area.[^3][^1] The same article tells of a visitor halfway up the tower whose new straw hat was carried by the wind down a wooded hillside. From the top, a forester gave directions while a second forester retrieved it.[^1]

The forest supervisor, Roy Olson, said fire protection had made meaningful progress in cutting losses to fire.[^1] When the forestry service took over the area in 1935, there were 390 fires that burned over 6,000 acres.[^1] The year before the report, there were 62 fires with damage covering 1,300 acres.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Hickory Ridge site as a tower that is gone.[^2] The Central US Lookouts page marks it removed and reproduces a 1972 Southern Illinoisan advertisement that solicited bids to dispose of this tower and seven other steel towers on the forest.[^1] According to the Forest Service, Trigg is the sole surviving one of the 16 fire lookouts built on the Shawnee.[^4]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the site, or whether the spot is open to visitors.

Do not confuse this tower with another Hickory Ridge. Listings for a different tower in Indiana's Hoosier National Forest give a 1936 date and construction by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^6] That Indiana tower is still standing, and it stays open to the public all year as an observation tower.[^5]
