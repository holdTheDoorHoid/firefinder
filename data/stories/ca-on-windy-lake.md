The fire tower at the southern end of Windy Lake, near Sudbury, was known as the Cascaden Tower after the township it stood in.[^1] The tower was 24.4 m (80 ft) tall; the Sudbury Star gives its height as 80 feet.[^1] The Star says it went up sometime in the late 1920s or early 1930s.[^1] Clayton Self, a fire tower enthusiast, recalls it as a 1928 tower and one of the first in the Sudbury area.[^1] A Canada Lookouts entry also gives 1928, for a light steel tower with an octagon cab.[^2]

For many years the tower was manned by Jack Naylor.[^1] Artist Ron Greig wrote that Naylor travelled by boat to a small cabin at the base of the hill, where he stayed through the week.[^3] Each day he began with a long, steep climb along a rough trail to reach the foot of the tower.[^1] A photo from about 1942 shows him at the circular map table in the tower, where fire sightings were fixed with an alidade.[^1] He kept at the job until he was 75 and retired in the late 1960s, according to Greig.[^1]

In 1949 a Department of Lands and Forests report recorded about 13 1/2 miles of telephone line from Cartier to the tower.[^2] In 2018 the Star described it as among the oldest towers still standing in the region.[^3] Greig said the tower closed in 1972.[^1][^3]

The cupola had blown off at some point, and the Ministry of Natural Resources and Forestry judged the tower too dangerous to leave standing.[^1] Crews began the work on July 8, 2024, and cut the tower down.[^1] Debris was lifted out by helicopter because access to the site was limited.[^1]

The ministry says the site has been restored to a natural condition.[^1] No source I found describes what, if anything, remains there now, or whether the public can reach it. In 2018 the approach was about six kilometres on a gated, plowed camp road, then a shorter but steeper trail.[^3]
