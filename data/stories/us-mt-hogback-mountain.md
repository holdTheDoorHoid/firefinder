Hogback Mountain rises about 21 miles northeast of Helena, in the Helena-Lewis and Clark National Forest.[^1][^2]

The first lookout had an L-4 cab, dated to 1936 by the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1] That August a Northern Region News item reported that a telephone line was within striking distance of the lookout, built with Civilian Conservation Corps labor.[^3]

The present cab, an R-6 flat cab on a ten-foot concrete base, was built in 1961.[^1][^2] In July 1945 smoke seen from the lookout led to a fire that spread from about five acres to 25 in under two hours.[^3] A 1949 plan to install a radio at the lookout was reported, and in 1958 a walkie-talkie radio and binoculars were stolen in a break-in there.[^3]

Its later history is less settled. In September 2018 the Forest Service started a Hogback Lookout Disposal project.[^5] It said the lookout had not been used in several years, had deteriorated, and had been determined excess to the agency.[^5] A 2024 Forest Fire Lookout Association chapter report, which tracks proposed lookout removals, said Hogback seemed to be the primary lookout threatened.[^6] The Register registered the lookout in April 2022 and says it may be staffed in summer.[^1]

Today the nearby Indian Flats Cabin has a Recreation.gov listing, but the site does not take bookings for it.[^4] Its listing says the lookout is reached by passenger vehicle and is manned by Forest Service personnel from mid-June through September.[^4] That conflicts with the 2018 notice, and I could not confirm what happened after it, so the lookout's current status is unsettled. Check with the Helena-Lewis and Clark National Forest before going up.
