The Craney Hill Lookout stands on a wooded hill in Henniker, New Hampshire, and the Town of Henniker owns it.[^1][^3][^4] A lookout has been on the hill since 1912, when the site was first developed for one.[^1][^2]

In 1932 the state's Forestry Commission put up a 41-foot International Derrick tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1][^2] Afterward, a wooden cab measuring 10 by 10 feet was added on top.[^3] The Register says that cab raised the tower by ten feet, but a 2019 newspaper still describes a 41-foot tower, so the total height is unclear.[^1][^3]

Staff manned the tower on a regular basis until 1981.[^1] Sources give two dates for the change in control. A 2019 newspaper says the town received it in 1980, and a 2021 hiking blog says the state closed it in 1981 and Henniker took on its preservation.[^3][^4] The sources I read name no particular fire the tower watched.

In 1997 the structure faced demolition to clear room for a cell tower.[^3] After word of a proposal to replace it with a commercial electronics tower spread, Henniker residents mobilized to save it.[^1] The Register listed it on February 17, 1997.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^5]

A 2016 visit report in the Register says the Craney Hill Fire Tower Association keeps the tower in great condition.[^1] The Town of Henniker, the Henniker Historical Society and that association now manage it.[^1]

The tower is kept locked against the public, and a 2021 hiking blog says it is opened one weekend each October.[^4] A 2019 newspaper gave the hours as 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. on two October days, weather permitting.[^3] The walk in covers about half a mile of the Lester Lane service road, starting at Craney Hill Road.[^4] Check with the Town of Henniker for this year's open days, and do not try the tower on other days.[^4]
