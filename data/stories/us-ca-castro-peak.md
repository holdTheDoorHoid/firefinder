Castro Peak Lookout stood on Castro Peak, the tallest summit in the Malibu Hills west of Los Angeles.[^1] The county's forestry department built and ran it from 1925, as an 8-by-8-foot metal cab on a 22-foot tower.[^1]

The county ran the lookout until 1971, when service ended.[^1][^3] The sources we read name no individual staff members and no notable fires.

About 1978 the cab was moved to the county fire department's Henninger Flats Forestry Unit.[^1][^3] That unit occupies county land inside the Angeles National Forest, on the south slope of Mt. Wilson.[^1] There the lookout was refurbished, furnished with 1925-era objects, and placed on a 7-foot tower for public education.[^1] The Register says the display was part of a visitor center and museum.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the cab on December 8, 2018.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the peak site on February 26, 2020.[^1]

In 2025 the county fire department dismantled the cab at Henninger Flats.[^1][^3] The plan was to put it on the Los Angeles County Fairgrounds at Fairplex in Pomona.[^1][^3] Neither source confirms that the reassembly has happened; both describe it as an intention.[^1][^3]

Today the cab is not on view at Henninger Flats.[^1][^3] The original peak is listed as private property with no access.[^4] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the peak.
