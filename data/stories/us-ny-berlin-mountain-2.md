Berlin Mountain is a peak in the Taconic Mountains, and the New York-Massachusetts state line lies east of its summit.[^1] Around 1915, Massachusetts put up a 50-foot steel fire lookout on the mountain.[^1] It was one of a pair meant to take over from the Mount Greylock tower, which had poor visibility.[^1]

Vermont, New York and Massachusetts jointly funded the tower's operation for roughly three years.[^1] Massachusetts relied on it, with towers on Osceola Mountain and Mount Everett, to alert New York's wardens to fires along the border.[^1] In 1922 New York set up fire-observation posts on two other hills in Rensselaer County.[^1]

Late in 1923 Massachusetts relocated the tower to Brodie Mountain in Hancock, Massachusetts.[^1][^2][^3] Just the footings survive on Berlin Mountain.[^1]

A 1952 notice said a fifth tower on Brodie Mountain, previously abandoned, would return to service.[^3] Two 1954 newspaper items said the state wanted to reopen the Brodie Mountain tower, which had been shut since before World War II, but a right-of-way dispute had stalled the project.[^3] A 50-foot tower was built on Brodie Mountain in 1967, and a new tubular steel tower went up there in 2009.[^3]

Today a large part of Berlin Mountain's upper slopes and summit is protected conservation land, and the 37-mile Taconic Crest Trail runs over the mountain.[^1] No source describes the footings as they are now.

A 2026 hiking guide places the former Hancock tower on East Mountain, about three miles south of Brodie Mountain's summit.[^4] It says the tower, with its cabin still intact, has recently gone out of service and now carries radio and microwave equipment.[^4] The guide also says the tower has kept watch since 1923, which conflicts with the 1954 item.[^4] It adds that the public route has no official designation and crosses several private parcels.[^4]
