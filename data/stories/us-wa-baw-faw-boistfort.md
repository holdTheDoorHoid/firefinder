The Baw Faw lookout stood on the summit of Baw Faw Peak, also called Boistfort Peak, in Lewis County, Washington.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com places it 13 miles west of Winlock.[^2]

The register dates the first cabin, a 15-by-18-foot shake building with a quarter-size second-floor observatory, to 1919.[^1] The Washington Forest Fire Association contributed the larger portion of its construction, and the Long-Bell Lumber Company helped clear the top of the peak and build the trail.[^3] A 1920 annual report says a lookout house was constructed that year, so the sources do not agree with the register on when the building went up.[^1][^3]

Newspapers name H.O. Roundtree as the lookout in 1925, 1931 and 1936.[^3] In 1937 a newspaper reported that he had found a wild tiger lily with 61 blossoms on one stalk a few yards from the lookout.[^3] In 1925 a weather station was installed on the summit, with the Weather Bureau's cooperation and its cost shared by the Long-Bell company and the forest fire association.[^3]

The register describes an 82-foot treated-timber tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, built in 1936.[^1] A Division of Forestry annual report gives the tower's height as 83 feet.[^3]

The page lists an activation date of March 4, 1942, with the Olympia Filter Center.[^3] In 1958 Edgar Thomas manned the lookout.[^3] In the 1958 to 1960 biennium, the department's own carpentry crew modernized a lookout at Baw Faw.[^3] In 1960 the tower was reduced to 55 feet, and a live-in cab for DNR was set on top.[^1] Edgar Thomas was named to man it again in 1961.[^3]

The register says the site was fully abandoned in 1967 and that the lookout no longer exists.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same 1967 date.[^2] A 1937 survey station description calls the tower a Forest Service lookout, though the register places it with the Washington State Division of Forestry/DNR.[^3][^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the site is open to the public.
