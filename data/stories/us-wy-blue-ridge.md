The Civilian Conservation Corps built Blue Ridge Lookout in 1938.[^1][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register places it in the Shoshone National Forest, near the Continental Divide in the Wind River Range.[^1] The register describes an open rock structure with a fire finder, a flag and a stone wall, and a long stone staircase leads up to it.[^1] The CCC also built the groundhouse, which the register calls the Louis Lake Guard Station.[^1]

The register lists Burns Davison, district ranger of the Washakie Ranger District, as the nominator. It was registered on August 22, 1998.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^1]

Its present condition is unclear. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wyoming list records it as Stone Walls and Abandoned.[^3] A firelookout.com page for the lookout says Gone.[^2] One of the register's photographs of the lookout is dated September 29, 2013.[^1]

A family blog of unknown date describes a one-mile round trip on foot from a trailhead on the Sinks Canyon-Louis Lake Road, about 23 miles southwest of Lander.[^4] Firelookout.com gives 18 miles southwest of Lander.[^2] The two figures may measure different points. No source we read describes what the structure looks like today, so check with the Shoshone National Forest before you go.
