Monks Hill in Kingston got its first fire lookout in 1916.[^1] According to the Register, the first tower was 38 feet tall, was built to replace the Plymouth tower, and both towers stayed in service.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history quotes a 1916 state report that describes a 30-foot tower, and says the Plymouth tower would be kept as a substation during severe droughts.[^2]

The record has a gap. A Forest Fire Lookout Association chapter post says the first tower was briefly abandoned from the late 1930s to the mid-1940s, after which power was brought to the structure.[^4] A Town of Kingston historical sketch of the Monk Tower logbook, though, describes residents volunteering to stand guard in the tower during the Second World War to watch Kingston Bay for suspicious activity. The logbook runs from December 1941 into 1943, with typically three-hour watches.[^3] The sources do not explain how those two accounts fit together.

The present tower dates from 1956.[^1] The Register describes a 75-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10-foot cab, while the 2017 chapter post gives 68 feet.[^1][^4] The Register records a registration date of December 15, 2004, and names the Mass. Bureau of Forest Fire Control as administrator.[^1]

The hill is where Camp Nekon sits.[^5] The Town of Kingston owns the property, which has at least two miles of trails and unpaved roadways.[^5] The sources do not say whether the tower itself is open to visitors, so treat the hill as a place for a walk, not a climb.
