Blue Ridge is a long, flat ridge in Coconino County, Arizona, within the Coconino National Forest.[^2][^3] A lookout once stood at Blue Ridge Point, on the ridge's southeast tip, overlooking East Clear Creek Canyon.[^3]

The earliest dated item we found is a July 1930 newspaper report. It describes fire guard Felix Spence, who had been on duty at the lookout and was struck by lightning the day before the report. The paper spells the ridge's name "Blur Ridge."[^2] Ranger Eastburn R. Smith, who was at home at the Bly Ranger Station, went to his rescue.[^2]

The Arizona lookout table lists the Blue Ridge tower as gone.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page marks it removed.[^2]

A hiker's trip report describes an open patch with a pair of giant junipers, plus the old lookout's foundations, at Blue Ridge Point.[^3] The forest is too thick for views, and the roads were rough, with many rocks and fallen trees on them.[^3] The hiker walked forest roads to reach the point and does not mention any closure.[^3]

Blue Ridge Point is not the same site as Moqui Lookout, a standing tower at the east tip of the same ridge.[^1][^3] The Arizona table lists Moqui as standing and as NHLR US 678.[^1] The trip report also quotes a May 1954 inspection note on the Moqui tower's construction.[^3]
