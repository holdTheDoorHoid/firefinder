The Foulweather lookout stood on Otter Crest, about four miles south-southeast of Depoe Bay in Lincoln County, Oregon.[^1][^2]

In 1936 the Lincoln County FPA's annual report recorded money spent on constructing two lookout stations, one of them on Otter Crest.[^2]

The sources disagree about the timeline. Firelookout.com calls it a live-in tower, dates it to 1940, and says it was abandoned in the 1940s.[^1] Forest Lookouts lists it as removed around 1938.[^2] That same page records an inspection by the State Forester's office on August 22, 1938, and a $6 tent floor for an "Otter Creek Lookout," with labor done by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^2] We cannot settle which account is right from these sources.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list marks the Foulweather tower as gone.[^5] A Lincoln County index says almost all of the county's lookouts had been given up by the late 1960s, with aircraft doing the spotting instead.[^4]

No source we read describes what remains of the fire lookout on the ground, or whether anyone can reach the site.

Next to the Otter Crest state viewpoint stands the Look-Out. Wilbur and Ana Badley built it in 1937 as a coffee shop on the seaward side of the highway, and it later became a gift shop.[^3] The firm doing its rehabilitation says the gift shop is expected to reopen next spring.[^3] That page is undated, so check before planning a visit. It does not say whether the Look-Out stands on the old lookout site.
