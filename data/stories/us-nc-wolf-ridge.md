The Civilian Conservation Corps built a fire tower and a fire warden's cabin on Roan High Knob in 1933.[^1][^2] The mountain straddles the North Carolina and Tennessee state line.[^2] The tower saw little use. WillhiteWeb says it was a poor vantage for spotting fires, because on cloudy days the summit often sat above the cloud layer.[^1] Wikipedia calls the tower ineffective and short-lived.[^2] Renovated in 1980, the cabin now serves Appalachian Trail hikers as the Roan High Knob Shelter.[^1][^2]

The North Carolina tower list calls this site Wolf Ridge, at 36.10447, -82.12240.[^3] Those coordinates match the Roan High Knob summit that Wikipedia gives.[^2] TopoQuest's Wolf Ridge is a different feature, a ridge in Mitchell County at 4,445 feet.[^4]

WillhiteWeb marks the site as a former lookout, and its photo captions label lookout footings and a former lookout location.[^1] From the Appalachian Trail, the site page gives a 0.2-mile detour that climbs 120 feet, and a blue-blazed side trail reaches the top in about five minutes.[^1] Beyond those footings, no source describes what remains at the summit today.
