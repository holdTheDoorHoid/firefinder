Black Mountain Lookout is a fire tower on Black Mountain in Union County, Georgia, inside the Chattahoochee National Forest.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 33-foot metal tower with a CL-100 series metal cab, and says it was constructed in 1949.[^1]

The dates are not fully settled.[^1][^2] A paper sign posted on the fence credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building the lookout in 1949.[^2] Willhite's page also says the lookout first appears on a 1938 map, and that a Coast and Geodetic Survey team used it as an azimuth mark in 1935.[^2] Both point to a lookout on the mountain before 1949, so the 1949 date may describe the current tower.[^2]

Willhite's page lists the lookout as standing.[^2] It also says the tower currently houses emergency communication gear for Lumpkin and Union counties, Camp F.D. Merrill, Blue Ridge EMC, the Civil Air Patrol, the Forest Service and amateur radio.[^2] A 2014 visitor noted that radio repeater equipment appeared to be installed on the tower.[^1]

Willhite reports that the tower is locked and ringed by a six-foot barbed wire fence.[^2] A 2018 register visit described it as fenced and appearing structurally sound, with its windows removed and boarded over and the cab door locked and padlocked.[^1] The same visit noted a small building at its base and what the visitor assumed was a generator box.[^1]

Sources disagree on how to reach it.[^1][^2] Register visitors found the access road gated and rutted, and suggested hiking in.[^1] Willhite says taking the access road is easiest, and describes an unmarked trail from Woody Gap that may overgrow.[^2] Willhite also notes that the bald summit rock offers great views.[^2]
