Hyland Hill sits on the Vermont line in Cheshire County, New Hampshire, at about 1,294 feet, just outside the western edge of Keene.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the lookout was placed in service in 1930, and a hike guide says it was built that year.[^1][^2] A May 1939 newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, describes the state council authorizing the purchase of 20 acres in Westmoreland for a fire lookout station, with the tower to be erected at once on Highland hill.[^3] The sources do not agree on whether the tower dates from 1930 or 1939, and we could not settle it.[^1][^2][^3]

The register describes a 53-foot steel tower topped with the standard New Hampshire wooden cab.[^1] A July 2016 newspaper item, also quoted by Forest Lookouts, calls the tower 80 feet.[^3]

Visit reports disagree about staffing. A 2012 visit found no staffing, while a 2017 visit said the state of New Hampshire staffed it sometimes.[^1] In July 2016 a newspaper quoted Karl M. Fredette, who was responsible for watching over 33 towns and three states, as he looked up at the tower.[^3]

The lookout was listed in the register on March 12, 2002.[^1] The tower had not been used since 2005, and a 2020 report describes it as deteriorating and vandalized.[^4] In early June 2020 a state contractor took it down, and the New Hampshire chapter's report says the contractor likely scrapped the steel.[^4]

The 2020 report says only one tower footing remains at the site.[^4] A 2017 visit noted remains of the cabin south of the tower, and cell towers stood on the summit at that time.[^1] No source we read describes the site after 2020 or says whether the summit is open to visitors. A hike guide describes an unmarked trailhead and a walk with no views at the top.[^2]
