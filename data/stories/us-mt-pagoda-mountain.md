Pagoda Mountain stood on the Flathead National Forest in Flathead County, Montana, about 21 miles south-southeast of the Spotted Bear Ranger Station, at an elevation of 8,030 feet.[^1] Firelookout.com dates its L-4 cab to 1929.[^1]

Harold Hatton was the Forest Service lookout there in July 1937.[^2][^3] On the afternoon of July 13 he and a companion were repairing a telephone line near the lookout when lightning struck them both.[^2][^3] The Forest Service history and a Northern Region News report name the companion as Roy Root, and the news report says Root was knocked out for 15 or 20 minutes before fully recovering.[^2][^3] A July 15, 1937 Billings Gazette report, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page, instead names J. Wirth and says he suffered no ill effects.[^2] Hatton never regained consciousness and died at a hospital.[^2][^3]

Accounts disagree on the horseback distance from the lookout to the Black Bear landing field.[^2][^3] A 1937 newspaper report gives 12 miles, while the Forest Service history gives 11.[^2][^3]

Firelookout.com says the cab was abandoned and is now gone, without giving a date.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list also marks the site as gone.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit it.
