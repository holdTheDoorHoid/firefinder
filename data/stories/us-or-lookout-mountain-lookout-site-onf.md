Lookout Mountain is the highest point in the Ochoco National Forest, close to 7,000 feet and about 22 miles east of Prineville.[^4][^2]

A 1930 Ochoconian item quoted on the Oregon Lookouts page says a ranger was told to build a lookout here, and the page dates that instruction to 1912. A June 13, 1912 Crook County Journal item gave the planned tower as 30 to 40 feet high.[^3] The register dates the lookout house to 1925, a 14-by-14-foot cab, and the Oregon Lookouts page says it stood on a base about 11 feet tall.[^1][^3]

The register says a 20-foot pole L-4 tower replaced the cab in 1931, patrolling ground half a mile to the northwest and southeast.[^1] Firelookout.com instead gives a 10-foot L-4 tower in the 1930s.[^2]

Items quoted on the Oregon Lookouts page name the lookouts: Jack Ireland, who was due to start on July 1, 1919; Marion O'Kelley in 1925; Gordon McNely in 1928; and Roy Mattson, who was moved off the mountain when the 1929 season closed.[^3] The last staffing the sources name is Jessie Quant, who left for her post in June 1945.[^3] A 1927 item says the lookout reported nine fires that season.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in the 1950s, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed in the 1960s.[^1][^2] The Rex page marks it removed.[^3] An Oregon Wild essay on the roadless area says a fire lookout once stood on the summit.[^4]

Today the Oregon Hikers field guide says the summit still has the foundations of an old lookout building, plus a corral, on an exposed lava bed.[^6] The Forest Service lower trailhead is open 24 hours a day and leads to Lookout Mountain Trail 804.[^5] The guide says the peak draws both hikers and mountain bikers.[^6]
