The lookout site on Johnson Mountain sits at 6,721 feet (2,049 m) in Snohomish County, Washington.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb.com says it lies farthest south of any mountain in the Glacier Peak Wilderness.[^3]

An L-4 cab went up on the site in 1938.[^1][^2][^3]

The site was given up in 1959.[^1][^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both list the cab as gone.[^1][^2] The register numbers the site US 1964, WA 463, and gives April 3, 2020, as its registration date.[^1]

A photo on WillhiteWeb.com carries a caption that names footings at the site, which suggests some may remain.[^3] The caption is undated.[^3] Neither of the other two sources mentions footings; both say the cab is gone.[^1][^2]

Visitors reach the mountain on foot.[^3] WillhiteWeb.com calls the North Fork Sauk River trailhead the likely best start, and a gravel road leads there that ordinary vehicles can use.[^3] The one-way hike covers about 9.5 miles and gains roughly 5,700 feet; people often pair it with the North Fork Sauk Loop, a backpacking trip that lasts more than one day.[^3]
