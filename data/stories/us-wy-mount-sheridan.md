Mount Sheridan Lookout stands on a peak inside Yellowstone National Park, at 10,215 feet.[^1] The Park Service drew blueprints in 1930, and in 1931 a single-story cabin 16 feet square was built on a concrete base.[^2] Rubble masonry forms the base, and the upper section is framed under a hipped roof.[^1] The register describes the design as standard for Yellowstone peak lookouts of the 1930s.[^1]

Sources disagree about the year. An Outside Bozeman column gives 1926,[^3] while the register and WillhiteWeb both give 1931, the year after the 1930 blueprints.[^1][^2] The county is disputed as well: the register says Park County, but a lookout listing says Teton County.[^1][^4]

The register lists it as US 577, WY 11. It was nominated by James Muhn and registered on June 1, 2004.[^1]

The lookout was a lonely post. In 1934 a newspaper described Floyd Keller, the fire lookout on the summit, and his "lonely outpost."[^2] In 1935 Harvey Crowder was the lookout when a snow drift he was bending over broke loose and sent him tumbling nearly a thousand feet down the slope.[^2] The next summer the lookout station reported heavy smoke from a fire believed to have been started by lightning near Madison Lake, and about fifty CCC enrollees were sent in by pack horse.[^2]

A lookout-history listing names Jim McCown as the lookout from 1963 to 1987, but we found no other source for that name.[^4] A visitor who went up in 1975 met a lookout called only Jim, who told him how few people made the climb. By 1994 that Jim had moved to Mount Washburn, saying Sheridan was too isolated.[^3]

A lookout listing marks the site as standing, and an Outside Bozeman article includes a photo of its interior.[^5][^3] We found no source describing its current condition or any restoration since it was built.

Reaching Mount Sheridan is a long hike: a former Yellowstone employee described about eight miles just to reach the base, then almost 3,000 feet of climbing over three more miles.[^3] No source we read says whether the cabin is open to visitors, so check with Yellowstone National Park before planning a trip.
