Crystal Mountain sits in Pierce County, Washington, at the ski area of the same name, about 14 miles east-northeast of Mount Rainier.[^1][^2][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com both describe a National Park Service cab built in 1934.[^1][^2] The Washington lookout list calls it a two-story cab.[^3]

Not everyone agrees the lookout ever stood here. Willhite visited the summit in summer 2016 and doubts that a fire lookout was ever there.[^4] He thinks the details belong to Crystal Peak, a knob several miles south, where he says old hiking books show a lookout standing well into the 1970s.[^4] He also argues that Crystal Mountain lies outside the park, while the Forest Lookouts site files it under Mount Rainier National Park.[^4][^5]

The elevation is disputed too. The register gives 6,605 feet, and Willhite says that figure came from a 1945 map at the spot where the lookout was said to be. He gives the summit as 6,840 feet.[^1][^4]

Both the register and firelookout.com put the cab's disappearance in the 1970s.[^1][^2]

Today the summit sits within the ski area. The reported spot is just north of the Summit House, reached by the Crystal Mountain Express Lift.[^4] In summer, Willhite says, a gondola ride reaches the ridge, and the high point is an easy walk north from there.[^4] Willhite reports that the spot now hosts communication towers and equipment.[^4] He found no footings there, though he noticed a brass mark in concrete that might have been one.[^4] Check access with the resort before you go.
