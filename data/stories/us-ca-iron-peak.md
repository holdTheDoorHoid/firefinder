Iron Peak Lookout stands on Iron Peak, about 4,492 feet up in the Mendocino National Forest, northeast of Laytonville in Mendocino County.[^1][^2] A 1935 item places it about nine miles northwest of Laytonville, so the direction is not settled.[^2]

The first lookout here went up in the mid-1920s.[^1][^2] In December 1925 the county board of supervisors recorded that the state could draw $500 of leftover fire suppression and prevention money to set up a lookout on Iron Mountain.[^2] The register says the present tower took the place of an earlier 1926 lookout.[^1] A December 1927 clipping says the state swapped its own land with the government to secure the 40-acre station site, which had been government land. A March 1929 report described a bill to move about 200 acres on the summit into state hands.[^2]

Early lookouts included J.E. Frazier, named in 1930, and Kenneth Dryden, appointed in 1935.[^2] A 1932 item spells the surname as Frasier.[^2] A 1956 entry says the lookout also served as a spotter for the Ground Observer Corps Skywatch program.[^2]

The metal tower that the register and Peakbagging.com describe was built in 1953.[^1][^3] The register says Cal Fire staffs the tower on an as-needed basis, and records its registration on March 20, 2016.[^1] Peakbagging.com has photographs from June 2006.[^3]

A directory list on firelookout.org marks a separate Iron Peak entry, FFLOS US 1892, CA 87, as Gone.[^4] I could not tie that entry to the 1926 station or to today's tower, so the conflict stays open.

The sources I read say nothing about public access today. The site lies in a national forest, and Cal Fire staffs the tower when needed.[^1] Check with the Mendocino National Forest or Cal Fire before you go, and do not climb a tower that is closed.
