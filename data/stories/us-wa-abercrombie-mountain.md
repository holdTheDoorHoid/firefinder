Abercrombie Mountain sits in the Colville National Forest, in Stevens County, Washington.[^1][^2] A lookout camp was started on the peak in 1934, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the U.S. Forest Service as its administrator.[^1]

Sources disagree on the first tower. FireLookout.com lists a 30-foot L-4 tower in 1938.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and a climbing guide both describe a 20-foot L-4 tower built in 1952, and the guide says it was removed in the 1960s.[^1][^3] A 1938 newspaper report also describes a carpenter at work on a Forest Service lookout station that year.[^4]

A family named Wagoner staffed the lookout in the summer of 1944.[^4] Spokane Chronicle reports say the Wagoners would act as lookouts on the mountain, and that a man with the same surname spent the summer on the lookout.[^4] We found no later staffing history in the sources.

The tower is gone. The register and the climbing guide say it was removed, while FireLookout.com says it was destroyed in the 1960s.[^1][^3][^2]

Today the summit is a hiking destination. Forest Service trail 117 starts at a small trailhead at the end of road 300, which the guide describes as single lane and passable by passenger car, though barely. The guide gives the trail's distance as 3.5 miles.[^3] The climbing guide says the peak is popular with people climbing county high points and prominence peaks.[^3] A photo caption on the guide's page points to stairs at the footings, which the author believes predate the 1952 tower.[^3] We found no source that describes the remains in more detail.
