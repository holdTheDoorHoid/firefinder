The Missouri Conservation Commission acquired 20 acres on Coot Mountain in Shannon County for a fire tower.[^2] A 1939 Commission annual report listed a 67-foot tower at Coot Mountain among three towers being erected that year, and a 1940 entry records a 68-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab erected on the site.[^2]

The Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower in 1940.[^1] It also says the cabin and garage appear younger than the tower and were built in the Department's modern style.[^1] An onX trail listing says, citing the Department, that the first tower was made of wood in 1939 and rebuilt as steel in 1967.[^4] The Register and Forest Lookouts do not mention a rebuild, so I treat that account as unconfirmed.[^1][^2]

Upkeep followed over the years.[^2] Garage doors were hung in fiscal 1968–69, and new roofs went on the residence and garage in fiscal 1977–78.[^2]

Staffing needs have changed.[^3] A 2022 Missouri Conservationist feature says about 50 fire towers remain on Department property and do not need full-time staff during fire season.[^3] Most staff may climb a tower only a few times a year, mainly on high-risk days or when someone must pinpoint a fire.[^3] I found no staffing record specific to Coot Mountain.

Today the Register, which added the tower on January 2, 2026, lists it as Department-owned and says its steps were older but functional as of July 2024.[^1] The onX listing describes a gravel county road that leads toward the tower. It says the service gate is likely closed and locked, and that a 0.2-mile hike reaches the tower.[^4] No official visiting rule turned up, so ask the Department before going, especially if the gate is closed or locked. I found no rental listing.
