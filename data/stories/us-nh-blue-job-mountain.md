Blue Job Mountain Lookout stands on a 1,357-foot summit in Farmington, New Hampshire, in Strafford County.[^1][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register lists the state's Department of Resources and Economic Development as administrator, with the Division of Forests and Lands as cooperator.[^1]

The state's Forestry Commission first used the summit for fire detection in 1912 or 1913, depending on the source.[^3][^1] A 32-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10-foot wooden cab went up in 1915, and a state report for 1915 to 1916 records the new tower.[^1][^3][^5] The state set up Blue Job State Forest the next year.[^3]

For many years the tower provided fire detection for both New Hampshire and Maine under an interstate agreement.[^1][^4]

Stairs replaced the ladder to the cab in 1929 and 1930, and visitation rose sharply afterward.[^3] The cab was rebuilt in 1940.[^3] Sometime between 1970 and 1980 a new cab went on top of the original, which became an open observation platform and added about eight feet.[^3] The Register and Trailspotting both give the tower as 32 feet, but adding Historic Structures' eight feet would make it about 40, so the sources do not agree on its total height.[^1][^3][^4] A 2008 New Hampshire chapter report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association says a new tower cab was installed that year.[^2]

In August 1948 the lookout phoned Rochester's fire department about a load of hay that had caught fire on a truck out in a field.[^5]

The Register says the tower is still staffed by the state, though only sometimes, and that it is in active service.[^1] A 2016 visit report says the state had recently redone the tower and that it is in great shape. The watchman's cabin door was in bad shape, and several cell towers now stand on the summit.[^1]

Trailspotting says visitors can go into the cabin on weekends, 11 a.m. to 5 p.m., when fire risk is moderate or high. At other times the deck below the cab still offers views.[^4] Hikers set out from the First Crown Point Road trailhead, where parking is free.[^4] Because the public hours depend on fire conditions, check before you go.
