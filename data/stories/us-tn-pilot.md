Pilot is a Tennessee Division of Forestry lookout in Overton County.[^1] The Forest Lookouts directory lists it as "Alpine (Pilot)" in the agency's District 4.[^1] Tennessee Landforms gives its elevation as 1,768 feet and marks the tower as standing.[^2] We found no record of when it was built, what design it followed, or who put it up.

The Division of Forestry built towers as part of a large program.[^3] A 2014 National Register study, prepared by Tennessee Valley Archaeological Research, says the Division built 208 lookout towers between about 1930 and about 1970.[^3] Pilot was one of the towers the study first planned to document.[^3] The firm's field survey, however, found the Pilot tower in ruinous condition, and the tower was replaced in the study.[^3]

The study's list of Tennessee fire towers still standing on March 26, 2014 does not include Pilot.[^3] Tennessee Landforms still shows the tower as standing, but that entry carries no date.[^2] The two sources disagree. The 2014 survey is the firmer evidence we have, and it points to a tower in poor shape. We found no later report on repairs, removal or current use.

Tennessee Landforms advises against climbing its towers, since steps are frequently missing or decayed.[^2] We found no public visiting arrangement for Pilot, so please do not climb it. Check with the Division of Forestry before going near the site.
