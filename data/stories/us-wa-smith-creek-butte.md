Smith Creek Butte sits in Skamania County, Washington, about six miles east of Mount St. Helens.[^3] The lookout is gone.[^1][^2][^4]

The first tower dates to the 1930s.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes it as a 12-foot wooden L-4 tower.[^1] Willhite's history is less certain. He says the butte already showed as a lookout station on a 1932 Forest Service map, but he does not know when it started.[^3]

In 1946 the first lookout was taken down and rebuilt roughly 30 feet north of its former site, as a 20-foot treated timber tower with an L-4 cab.[^1][^3] A 1947 Forest Log article, quoted by Willhite and by Washington Lookouts, reports that the Columbia National Forest's supervisor called a recent experiment in moving building materials by air a success.[^3][^4] About 4,000 pounds of lumber and other supplies were flown in, a job that would otherwise have needed 25 pack horse loads.[^3][^4]

The second tower and cab came down in 1968.[^1][^2][^3] The register says it was removed, Firelookout.com says it was destroyed, and Willhite says it was "removed or destroyed." The sources agree on the year but not on the word.[^1][^2][^3]

Willhite places the summit on the margin of the blast zone and writes that it came through the blast that flattened the forest.[^3] A photo caption on his page says the USGS set up a Geodimeter station at the lookout site shortly before the eruption. The photo was taken April 25, 1980.[^3]

Willhite's visit photos include a phone insulator along the old trail, nails in a tree used for a phone line, and an eyebolt.[^3] His page notes a visit in 2023 and describes two routes in. From the north, a gated road runs about 8 miles by bike, followed by a short bushwhack. From the south, the route starts at a trailhead and requires a ford of the Muddy River.[^3] Washington Lookouts files the site under Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^4] No source we read says whether the public may enter the site.
