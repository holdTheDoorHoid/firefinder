Ivers Peak is in Coos County, about 18 miles east of Coos Bay, at 2,087 feet.[^1][^2] The register says two cabins and a 20-foot platform tower were built there in the 1920s.[^1] A July 1927 Coos Bay Times item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, describes the lookout as still under construction.[^3]

Jack Scott was put in charge of the lookout in July 1934, and Bert Palmer of Portland was named its lookout in July 1941.[^3]

The register says that in 1942 the site was found to be a poor location for an AWS station.[^1] Forest Lookouts gives the post's dates as March 18 to December 21, 1942, and names the Roseburg Filter Center.[^3] According to the register, a new lookout was built at McKeever's and the lookout and AWS duties moved there.[^1] A July 1942 letter, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, instead describes an old 16-by-16 shake cabin built around 1920.[^3] It says a new point, Vaughn Peak, had been studied as a substitute, and that work at Ivers Peak was on hold until that point was inspected and approved.[^3] The sources disagree about where the replacement stood.[^1][^3]

Forest Lookouts marks the lookout as removed but gives no date.[^3] Firelookout.com describes the site as abandoned for a long time.[^2]

According to the Forest Lookouts page, the Coos FPA erected a 90-foot steel tower at the former lookout site in 2008 as a base for a smoke-detection camera.[^3] That is the last entry on the Forest Lookouts page, and none of the sources we read says whether the tower still stands.[^3] No source we read says whether the site is open to the public, so check with the Coos FPA before visiting.[^1][^2][^3]
