The Corona AWS Post was a World War II observation post, even though the register files it among former fire lookouts.[^1] The register calls it a Corona tower of the Aircraft Warning Service, a civilian arm of the Army's Ground Observer Corps that watched the skies for enemy aircraft.[^1][^2] The register says citizen volunteers manned these towers, and it names one Corona volunteer who gave her time to staff the watchtower.[^1]

The register gives a build date of circa 1934. Its own description places the post in World War II, yet volunteers were organized into the Aircraft Warning Service in May 1941, so the two dates do not fit.[^1][^2] The exact location is also in doubt. The register says the tower stood on Foothill Ranch, local sources put it at Upper Drive just west of South Main Street, and some historical estimates place it around the corner of Chase and Main.[^1]

The register lists its removal date as unknown.[^1] The Aircraft Warning Service was shut down on May 29, 1944.[^2]

Today, no source describes anything that remains at the site.[^1] The register notes that items donated by the Dillon family are on display at the Corona Heritage Museum, and a 2018 Facebook post the register quotes points visitors to the Corona Heritage Park & Museum for an exhibit on Corona's AWS tower.[^1] Because the post's location is unconfirmed, no source says whether the site itself is open to visitors.[^1]

[^2]: Aircraft Warning Service, Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_Warning_Service (accessed 2026-10-10).
