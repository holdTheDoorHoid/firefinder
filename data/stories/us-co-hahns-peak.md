Hahns Peak Lookout sits near the summit of Hahns Peak in the Routt National Forest of northern Colorado.[^1][^2] The Forest Service built a stone shelter on the peak in 1908.[^3] Four years later, more than a ton of cement and building materials was hauled to the summit to build a permanent lookout tower.[^3] Historic Routt County dates the construction to 1908 through 1912, and calls the lookout one of the first officially funded lookouts in Colorado.[^4]

The sources differ on the elevation.[^1][^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 10,759 feet, while the Forest Service and Colorado Preservation put the site at 10,839 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

Damage in 1933 was followed by a rebuild in 1939.[^1] Around 1942 the building was enlarged and upgraded, and during the Second World War it served as an Aircraft Warning Service post.[^1] Use of the site fell off in the late 1940s, and the Forest Service took it off line once the war ended.[^3] Historic Routt County places its decommissioning sometime in the late 1940s and early 1950s, so the exact end of staffing is not settled.[^4] Colorado Preservation also mentions a lightning-caused fire inside the structure, though it gives no year.[^2]

Once the lookout was decommissioned, weather at that elevation, neglect and occasional vandalism wore the building down.[^2] Colorado Preservation listed the lookout among Colorado’s Most Endangered Places in 2014.[^4] Between 2014 and 2017, Historic Routt County, the Forest Service, HistoriCorps and Rocky Mountain Youth Corps teamed up on the restoration, which also added lightning protection.[^4] The Forest Service dates the completed restoration of the building and trail to 2017, while Colorado Preservation says the site was declared “Saved” in 2016.[^3][^2] In July 2018 vandals broke multiple windows and badly damaged the entrance door, and the sheriff’s department and Forest Service law enforcement both investigated.[^3] HistoriCorps completed repairs in 2019.[^2]

Today the Forest Service describes the lookout as a popular day hike and a year-round destination for backcountry visitors.[^3] Colorado Preservation says Historic Routt County volunteers still watch over and maintain the site, keeping it safe for the many hikers who reach it each year.[^2] A 2021 trip report describes hiking up from the lower trailhead and walking up the stairs for a look around the old lookout.[^5]
