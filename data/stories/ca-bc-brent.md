BC Parks describes a former BC Forest Service fire lookout sitting on the summit of Brent Mountain, in a protected area about 22 km west of Penticton.[^1]

The Peakvisor website says a lookout tower went up on the summit in the 1930s, to help guard the region's timber from fire.[^3] No other source here gives a construction date.

BC Parks now calls the tower unused, and says it serves as a warming hut when needed.[^1] A hiking blog goes further and calls it an abandoned tower, with 360 views from the summit.[^2]

The protected area was set up on April 18, 2001.[^1] The summit trail is roughly 5 km long, and BC Parks advises allowing four to five hours for the round trip.[^1] The park puts the climb at 444 m of elevation gain, while the hiking blog gives 490 m.[^1][^2]

BC Parks recommends a high-clearance vehicle for the access road, which has some deep water bars, and advises drivers to watch for oncoming logging trucks.[^1] The park lists hiking, horse use and snowmobiling among the area's outdoor recreation.[^1]
