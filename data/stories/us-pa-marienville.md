The National Historic Lookout Register lists a Marienville Fire Tower in Forest County, in the Cornplanter State Forest, as US 1814.[^1] It was added to the Register on January 2, 2026, and nominated by Coltin Carr.[^1]

The tower went up in 1921.[^1][^2] An October 1921 McKean Democrat item reports that the first fire tower in the section was going up, a steel structure 60 feet tall.[^1][^2]

A towerman was on duty there by 1924. A newspaper item from that year describes the towerman watching two bears roam the brush about a quarter mile from the tower.[^1][^2]

The tower's history takes a turn in 1952. The Forest Lookouts site says the tower was taken down and rebuilt at Chestnut Ridge in Fayette County that year.[^2] A November 1952 Charleroi Mail item, quoted on another page of the same site, reports plans for an 80-foot tower on Chestnut Ridge to replace the Banning Knob lookout. It says that tower was being moved from near Marienville.[^3]

The heights disagree: the 1921 item says 60 feet and the 1952 item says 80 feet, so the two may not describe quite the same structure.[^1][^3]

We found no source that shows a tower standing at Marienville today, and we could not confirm whether the Chestnut Ridge tower still stands. The Register entry does not mention the 1952 move, and its photo is a postcard from about 1930 to 1945.[^1] Check with the Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry before planning a trip.[^1]
