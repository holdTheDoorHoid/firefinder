A steel fire tower went up on Hause Hill, above Newfoundland in Wayne County, in April 1922.[^1][^2] The landowners, Raymond Hause and his sister Mamie, let the state build it there.[^2] The tower bears the name of J. Gould, whose fortune began in the tanning trade.[^2] Crews carried the materials up by horse team.[^2] The lookout page quotes a 1967 item giving its height as 70 feet, a family memoir says 75 feet, and a 1959 survey description gives about 65 feet overall.[^1][^2] The survey describes a four-legged steel frame with a glass-enclosed cab.[^1][^2]

The tower's warden was William "Bill" Hause, a brother of the landowners. He kept watch in April, May, October and November, the fire-danger months.[^1][^2] A 1967 newspaper item calls that year his 29th as warden, which points to a start around 1939. The family memoir says he began around 1930.[^1][^2]

The state retired the tower around 1967 and planned to demolish it.[^2] The family made an offer in 1976, when the state took bids, and they ended up owning it.[^2]

Today the tower is privately owned, so visitors should not expect access.[^2] The family's undated account says a member of the family still goes up in spring and fall to watch for fires, which suggests the tower still stands.[^2]
