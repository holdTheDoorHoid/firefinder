The Roaring Gap lookout tower stood in Alleghany County, North Carolina, and the state Division of Forest Resources is listed as its agency.[^1] A tnlandforms.us list of North Carolina lookout towers ties the site to survey station FZ1901.[^3]

In June 1935 the Conservation Corps put up a 59-foot 3-inch steel tower, with a one-room cabin at ground level, for $963.05.[^1] Its funding came through the Emergency Conservation Works program.[^1]

In April 1952 a temporary woman lookout died after a fall down the tower's steps, according to a Statesville Daily Record report quoted on the Forest Lookouts page.[^1]

A 1954 survey note places a survey station 112 feet north of the lookout tower, on land it says was leased by the U.S. Forest Service.[^2]

A 1970 recovery note by the North Carolina Geodetic Survey said the fire tower would be taken down within a year and set up on Green Mountain, about six miles northwest of its present location.[^2] The same note places the station in woods on the northwest side of a clearing that it calls the tower's former site.[^2] A 1991 note by the same survey recorded the lookout tower as dismantled and taken away.[^2] No source we read says whether the Green Mountain move happened, or where the tower stands now.

A 2002 recovery found the station in good condition.[^2] A separate NGS point called Roaring Gap Lookout Tower is listed with a code meaning its surface mark was reported destroyed.[^4]

No source we read says whether the public may visit the site.
