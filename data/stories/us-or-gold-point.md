Gold Point is a hill in Oregon's Willamette National Forest, about 14 to 15 miles north of Oakridge in Lane County.[^2][^3] Its elevation is 3,959 feet.[^1]

The first lookout here was a tree. One register dates it to 1924, when a 90-foot tree was fitted with an enclosed crow's nest.[^1] Another page says the tree was in place around 1920.[^3] In 1925 a lookout named Frank Verdier built a platform high in a fir, because he had been climbing a tree every day to take his observations.[^3]

Towers came next, and the sources do not agree on their order. The register says a 30-foot round timber tower with a 6-by-6 cab went up in 1936.[^1] Rex's page lists a 37-foot tower erected in 1928 and a 35-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14 cab in 1936.[^3] The firelookout.com page describes a 20-foot open cab in 1928 and a 30-foot L-4 tower in 1939.[^2]

A forestry student, Samuel Miller, was a lookout fireman on the point during the summer of 1927.[^3] In 1942 an Air Warning Service cabin was added.[^2][^3] The post's record gives August 11, 1942 as its start and September 22, 1943 as its end, with the Eugene Filter Center listed for it.[^3] A former observer, Paul Puente, wrote in 2012 that he was stationed there from late May to early September 1957, between his freshman and sophomore years at university.[^1]

A 28-foot treated timber tower went up in 1959.[^1][^2] The register says it was removed in 1968, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2]

The Forest Service's Gold Point Trail #3468 reaches the top through a side trail, and its page notes that a fire lookout once stood on the point, although trees now block the view.[^4] The trail climbs 1.8 miles from Logan Creek to the ridge.[^4] The page says the Bedrock Fire affected the route and that parts may be impassable, and it lists a seasonal closure to all users from January 1 to July 31.[^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains of the tower footings or the cabin site on the summit today.
