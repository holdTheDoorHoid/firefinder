The Moore Farms Botanical Garden lookout stands on the garden grounds in Lake City, South Carolina, where it is the central landmark.[^3][^2]

The garden's own page describes a steel tower more than 100 feet tall with a bright red top.[^2] It says the tower once stood in Olanta, where it did its job for years before being taken out of service.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower first went up in 1934 in Olanta, roughly six miles northwest of Lake City, and was bought from the South Carolina Forestry Commission.[^1]

The garden says the tower was bought at a silent auction in Olanta in 2004 and moved to the garden that year.[^2] As the garden tells it, the successful bid seemed a good deal until the moving bill came in.[^2] The register's Built field says 2004, which conflicts with its own description of a 1934 build. We use 1934 for the build and 2004 for the move.[^1]

The register added the tower on April 16, 2014, on the nomination of Ron Stafford.[^1]

The tower no longer spots fires.[^3] The garden calls it a landmark.[^2] Wikipedia says it now acts as a support for bald cypress specimens, and a bog garden of pitcher plants sits beside it.[^2][^3] The sources we read do not name any lookout who staffed the tower.[^1][^2]

The garden is not open daily to visitors. You can ask to visit, join a guided tour, come on an open garden day, or attend a seasonal event.[^3] The garden's page asks visitors to book a visit.[^2] None of the sources says the tower itself can be climbed, so please do not climb it.[^2]
