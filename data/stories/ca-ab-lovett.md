A lookout archive files Lovett Lookout under the Brazeau Forest of Alberta.[^1] In September 1938 the Alberta forestry branch reported four radio-equipped lookout stations in operation, and said a fifth was to be built at Lovett.[^1]

The original lookout building was made of logs.[^1] The archive dates the start of a lumber cabin to replace it to 1945, and its completion to 1946.[^1] The archive's 1946 entry quotes an annual report that planned the new cabin as quarters for a married lookout man, with lumber construction and a brick chimney.[^1]

In 1948 the archive says the wagon road was graded, so a car could drive to the tower.[^1] In the year to 31 March 1949, reserve field staff built a 10.7 m (35 ft) steel tower there, according to that year's annual report.[^1]

Alberta reports 127 wildfire lookout sites, each staffed by an observer who usually works there from April to October.[^2]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public may visit it.
