The Armintrout Fire Tower was a steel lookout that Civilian Conservation Corps crews erected in 1934 on Armintrout Road in Pike County, Ohio.[^1][^2] William L. and Harriet B. Armintrout sold .35 acres to the state for $1 so that a forest fire lookout could be built.[^1]

In October 1934 the Chillicothe Gazette announced that a steel tower had been received for W. L. Armentrout's land, with CCC boys from Camp Pike to put it up.[^2] Two 1934 Gazette items spell the name Armentrout, while an October 27 item and the panels use Armintrout.[^2][^1]

Sources disagree on its original height. The panels give 80 feet, and Ohio's Country Journal gives 71 feet.[^1][^4] On October 27, 1934, the Gazette reported that H. G. Armintrout, of Shyville, had taken occupancy of the new tower.[^2]

The tower's ownership passed in 1978 to the Pike County Public Service Trust for civil defense purposes, the same year the last Ohio fire lookout closed.[^1] In 2016 Geiger Brothers moved it from its Pike County site to the Ohio State Fairgrounds.[^1] Crews dismantled it and shortened it to 60 feet, measured from the ground to the bottom of the cab.[^1][^4] The legs were given a sandblast, an acid dip and re-galvanizing, and the cab was refinished with new glass.[^1]

The tower now stands directly behind Smoky Bear in the Natural Resources Park at the Ohio State Fair, in Columbus.[^4] Ohio's Country Journal reports that visitors cannot go up without assistance, but the tower has a good view of the State Fair.[^4] The National Historic Lookout Register registered it on September 3, 2014, as US 1021, OH 10.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on Armintrout Road.
