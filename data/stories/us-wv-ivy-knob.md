Ivy Knob Fire Tower stands on Guyandotte (Bolt) Mountain, on the Raleigh and Wyoming county line in southern West Virginia.[^1][^2] Sources disagree about which county it is in. The register says Raleigh County,[^1] the Forest Lookouts page is headed Raleigh/Wyoming Counties,[^2] and the Forest Fire Lookout Association list places it in Wyoming.[^3]

The register says the first fire tower in West Virginia stood on this spot in 1916. Emory 'Pop' Wriston built the present tower in 1924 to replace it.[^1]

A 1952 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls the tower a steel structure with four legs, about 65 feet high, with a glass-enclosed cab. It says the tower sits on land the U.S. Forest Service leases.[^2]

In 1960 a 20-by-22-foot concrete block cabin was built to replace a small frame shed that had served as living quarters.[^2] In August 1960 a state conservation employee assisted at the tower while the regular observer was in the hospital.[^2]

A November 1, 2016 visit report says a fire at the tower had damaged some stairs and blown the glass out of the cab. The report rates the tower in poor condition, and it does not say when the fire happened.[^1]

The tower was registered on January 3, 2015, and the register reports that an access road runs from WV 99 to the site.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] The West Virginia Division of Forestry administers it.[^1] I found no source saying whether the public may climb it today, so anyone planning a visit should first check with the Division and stay off the damaged stairs.
