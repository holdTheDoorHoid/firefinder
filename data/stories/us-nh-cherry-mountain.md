Cherry Mountain is a ridge in Coos County, New Hampshire, and its highest peak, Mount Martha, is a former fire tower site.[^1][^2]

The Forest Lookouts page for Cherry Mountain quotes an August 3, 1939 Portsmouth Herald notice. The notice spells the name "Charry" and lists a tower planned there, to be built by Camp Gale River of Franconia.[^3]

ScenicNH says the Forest Fire Lookout Association dates the tower to 1939 and its dismantling to 1989.[^4]

The sources disagree on when the tower came down. ScenicNH's text also gives the tower a span of 1939 to 1968, which does not fit its own 1989 date.[^4] The hiking article says the tower was razed in 1982 and dismantled in 1989, citing FireLookout.org.[^1]

ScenicNH also reports a 1980s demolition attempt by the 10th Special Forces Group from Fort Devens that did not succeed, so crews had to bring the structure down by hand.[^4]

According to ScenicNH, all that is left today is the footings.[^4] A photo in the hiking article shows two hikers on the old tower footings on Mount Martha.[^1] Mount Martha, at 3,573 feet, is on the 52 with a View hiking list, and the same article describes a traverse of the ridge from Route 115 to Cherry Mountain Road.[^1]
