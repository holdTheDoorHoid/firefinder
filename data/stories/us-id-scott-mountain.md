Scott Mountain Lookout stands 8,215 feet up on a granite peak in the Boise National Forest, about 11 miles northwest of Lowman.[^4][^3] The first structure on the summit was a 12-by-12-foot log cabin, erected in 1920.[^5][^1] In 1934 an L-4 cab took its place, set on an unusual stone foundation that also serves as a catwalk.[^1][^5]

A 1959 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, reproduced on one Idaho history site, instead describes a wooden structure 20 by 20 feet, with a concrete foundation, a garage and outbuildings.[^5] The two accounts disagree about the foundation: the registry says stone, and the survey says concrete.[^1][^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts' known-staff list names Gerald Pickford under 1922 and Teresa Beall under 2003, but it does not say which structure either one served.[^2] The 1922 entry predates the 1934 cab.[^2][^1] The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the tower on September 24, 2011.[^1]

Daytrip Northwest, in an undated post, says the lookout is still manned during fire season.[^4] Firelookout.com says summer staffing is possible.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts labels the site "Drive-Up Staffed."[^2]

Getting there is a drive. From Highway 17, Deadwood Reservoir Road runs about 10 miles from the pavement to a junction, and then it is a little over 4 miles to the summit on Forest Road 555.[^4] Daytrip Northwest recommends a 4WD or AWD vehicle, and says the usable window is summer, possibly reaching early fall.[^4] Plan a summer trip and check road conditions before you go.
