South Point is a 5,980-foot summit in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest, about six miles south-southeast of Packwood, Washington.[^1][^2] The Forest Service administers the site.[^3]

The site began as a camp in 1930.[^3][^4] A 5-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab went up in 1933.[^2][^3][^4] Panoramic photos were taken from the site in 1934.[^4][^5] In 1952 a 41-foot timber tower with an L-4 cab replaced it.[^2][^3][^4]

Bob Baldwin was the lookout in 1951.[^2][^4][^5] In May 1964 the forest announced that the 37 stations manned in earlier years would be cut to six, and that air patrols were expected to take over most of the lookout coverage.[^5] South Point was one of the six, mainly to support communications across the forest.[^5]

Sources disagree about how the 1952 tower ended. The register and the Forest Lookouts list say it was removed in 1972.[^3][^5] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] WillhiteWeb gives April 3, 1972 as the day the tower and cab were destroyed, and suggests it may have burned.[^4]

Today the summit has no lookout to climb.[^3] A 2023 trip report describes two sheds and a tower on top,[^6] and WillhiteWeb mentions a small communication tower that a helicopter services.[^4]

The Forest Service describes Trail 123 as starting on Forest Road 20 and climbing east through an old open burn area to the former lookout site. It is open to hikers, horses and bicycles, and closed to motorized use.[^1] Space at the trailhead is limited, and the agency suggests carrying in water, because backcountry streams can run dry.[^1] From Packwood, go west on State Highway 12 for 3.5 miles, turn left on Forest Road 20, and follow it 4.8 miles to the trail.[^1]
