Henness Ridge Lookout stands at 6,339 feet in Yosemite National Park, and the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1934.[^1][^2] It was designed in the National Park Service's rustic style, meant to blend with its surroundings rather than stand out like the metal towers the Forest Service used.[^2] The first floor is a garage and the second holds living quarters, while the third is a 13-foot-square cab with a catwalk.[^1]

The Park Service ran the lookout for fire detection through the 1960s and 1970s, a time when human spotters were still the main way to spot fires in remote country.[^4] One account says it was still in use into the 1980s, so the sources do not agree on when the watch ended.[^2][^4] The register says the cab is now reserved for emergency use.[^1]

The Park Service lists the lookout among properties determined eligible for the National Register of Historic Places, a finding that means it meets the criteria without being formally listed.[^3] It was registered on the National Historic Lookout Register on June 4, 1998.[^1] That register also says the Yosemite site has since been restored.[^1]

Today the cab is reserved for emergency use, so visitors cannot count on going inside.[^1] Hikers can still walk toward it.[^2] One account describes a trailhead at the end of Azalea Lane in Yosemite West, then an asphalt road past water towers and about 0.7 miles to the tower.[^2]
