Ouabache Fire Tower stands in Ouabache State Park near the city of Bluffton, in Wells County, Indiana.[^1] The register notes that some references may use the name Wells County Fire Tower, because the park was originally the Wells County State Forest.[^1]

Sources disagree about when it went up. The register says it was constructed in 1935 by Civilian Conservation Corps workers, going by signage on site, and the Forest Lookouts site gives the same year.[^1][^4] Indiana Landmarks and Mark A both give 1939, and Mark A is quoting a September 2024 post from Indiana State Parks.[^2][^3]

Indiana Landmarks describes a galvanized steel tower whose windowed cab sits about 100 feet above the ground and commands a view across four counties. Park staff and volunteers climbed 106 oak treads to staff the tower as lookouts during the warm months, when fire risk was highest.[^2]

A November 1978 Outdoor Indiana article, as Mark A quotes it, says the tower was no longer in use for fire watching.[^3] Indiana Landmarks says that as the tower's original purpose faded, it became an iconic symbol for the park.[^2]

In 2015 the park closed the run-down tower and hoped to repair it, but it lacked the $75,000 needed for renovation.[^2][^3] The Friends of Ouabache State Park, a nonprofit formed in 2012, raised the full amount in nine months.[^2]

The Friends group carried out the work that made the tower safe to climb, and an August 23, 2019 dedication marked its reopening to the public.[^4][^1]

The Indiana Department of Natural Resources administers the site today.[^1] Mark A rates the tower as standing, climbable and recently restored.[^3]
