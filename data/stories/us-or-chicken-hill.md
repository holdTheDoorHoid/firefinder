Chicken Hill stands in Union County, Oregon, in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest near Anthony Lakes, at about 2,130 meters.[^1][^2] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as the administrator.[^1] A September 1933 item from The Record-Courier said the lookout's house was coming soon and would sit atop a 20-foot tower. The same item noted that the point was high and offered a good view across a wide area of forest.[^2] A July 1934 item called the station new and said Harry Craig would be posted there that summer.[^2] The register and firelookout.com both date the 20-foot pole L-4 tower to 1935.[^1][^3]

Sources name a planned summer occupant in 1934, then summer staff at the station from 1938 through 1940.[^2] A 1938 La Grande Observer item, as reproduced on a lookout history page, named Raymond Pederson as the person in charge.[^2] In 1939, when fire hazard led to a doubling of station forces at various lookouts, Bernal Coles of Haines was posted at Chicken Hill.[^2] The last staffing item we found is from 1940, when a Wolf Creek schoolteacher and her husband went up to the station for the summer.[^2]

In 1942 the register says the original tower was replaced with what it calls a 10-foot Supervisor Hall live-in tower.[^1] The register and Rex's page both give 1968 as the year that tower was removed.[^1][^2] firelookout.com says the lookout was destroyed that year, so the sources agree on the date but not on how it ended.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the site today, and none says whether the ground is open to visitors. Check with the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest before planning a trip.
