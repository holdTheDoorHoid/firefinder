Bald Knob is a summit near Alto Pass in Union County, Illinois, inside the Shawnee National Forest.[^1][^4] A 1930 newspaper called it the high point of the hill country across southern Illinois.[^2]

That year, a newspaper reported that the State Department of Conservation had put up a steel tower, sixty feet tall, near Bald Knob.[^2] The newspaper said it was the first of its kind in the state, with a glass-enclosed cab at the top and a telephone line that would link the observer to forest headquarters.[^2] The sources disagree on who built it. A listing from about 1942 says the U.S. Forest Service erected a tower named Bald Knob Lookout Station.[^2]

The summit became known for its Easter services. The first sunrise service was held in 1937, and it has been held annually since.[^4] In 1939 a lighted electric cross, 70 feet high, was mounted on the 60-foot tower to guide worshipers to the top.[^2]

In 1953 plans called for a 150-foot cross of steel, glass and concrete, with a lookout post at the top for forest rangers. The Forest Service agreed to take down the old fire tower once the cross was finished.[^2] The cross that was built stands 111 feet tall, and Wikipedia says it was first completed in 1963.[^4] A 1974 article says the 111-foot cross replaced a 40-foot neon cross that had been mounted on a fire tower.[^2]

The register says Forest Service maps show a lookout tower was once located here, and that a large cross with an observation platform now stands on the site.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^3] The cross, officially the Bald Knob Cross of Peace, has its base 1,034 feet above sea level.[^4]

The cross has had a rough patch. It deteriorated in the early 2000s, which the marker attributes to lack of interest in the organization that cared for it. Disputes then arose among board members, and they ultimately led to litigation in 2006. A court order then barred upkeep until the case was over.[^5] A separate group, Friends of the Cross, raised funds toward the restoration once a court decision was made.[^5] The marker describes that fundraising but not when the restoration was finished.[^5] None of the sources I read says whether visitors can drive up to the cross or use its observation platform today.[^1][^4]
