Blackbird Mountain is in Lemhi County, Idaho, in the Salmon National Forest, about 26 miles west-southwest of Salmon.[^1] Firelookout.com gives its elevation as 9,096 feet, and a climbing guide gives 9,083.[^1][^4]

A climbing guide, quoting a 1982 history of the Salmon National Forest lookouts, says the peak was first mapped as a lookout in 1918.[^4] A 1924 Salmon Forest report lists it as a primary lookout, with a 14x14 house.[^3] Firelookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page both date a two-story log cabin there to 1935.[^1][^3]

The climbing guide says the lookout ranked as primary until 1929, when it was moved to emergency status.[^4] Newspaper items from the 1920s to the 1940s name people who worked there.[^3] A 1926 item says one man was at the station for the season, and a 1939 item says a son was posted there as fire guard.[^3] An August 1940 item reports that four large forest fires could be seen from the lookout.[^3] A former fire lookout registry, as listed by Idaho Fire Lookouts, names a worker there in 1938.[^2]

Sources disagree on when the cabin was destroyed. Firelookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page date its destruction to 1965.[^1][^3] The 1982 history, as quoted in the climbing guide, suggests the cabin probably burned in the 1950s.[^4] A Forest Lookouts listing also carries the single word "Removed," with no date or detail.[^3]

The 1982 history says a few remains were reportedly on top, though this was not verified.[^4] We found no recent source that describes the summit today, and no source we read says whether the public may visit.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as drive-up.[^2] The climbing guide describes a steep four-wheel-drive road to the summit from the Bighorn Crags Road, and it advises checking with the Forest Service for current road conditions.[^4]
