Fairview Peak Lookout stands on the summit of Fairview Peak in Gunnison County, Colorado, within the Gunnison National Forest.[^1][^2] The Register gives its elevation as 13,214 feet and calls it the highest elevation lookout in the United States.[^1] The build date is unsettled. The Register gives 1911, but its description says the Forest Service built it in 1912.[^1] A Forest Lookouts history gives an earlier window: plans began around 1907, and a trail, a telephone line and a stone lookout house went up in 1908 and 1909 under Ranger Jack Cammann.[^2]

The walls are rock taken from the site, mortared with a mix that includes native soil.[^1] The wooden cupola was taken off in the 1960s, and the roof was redone to make room for radios.[^1] The Register calls that radio setup short-lived, since lightning kept forcing trips to the remote site to fix the radios.[^1]

The sources do not agree on how long the lookout was staffed. The Register says it is not known how long the lookout was occupied, and, from hearsay, it was likely abandoned within a few years because of lightning strikes.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history says it was staffed in the early 1910s, found inadequate, and abandoned for better sites, with lightning storms a further worry.[^2] A 1919 guidebook, quoted on the same page, describes a Forest Service lookout where a man was stationed in dry periods during the summer.[^2]

The Register says the walls were restored in 2008 and 2009.[^1] The 2008 chapter report says Forest Service archaeologist Justin Lawrence led a major renovation, with volunteers packing in for a week of work to restore what the chapter calls the highest lookout in America.[^3] The 2009 report says a Passport in Time crew joined local volunteers, the stonework was finished up to the roof line, and budget limits made more Forest Service work look bleak.[^4]

The chapter's March 2019 survey says that the year before, the roof was rebuilt, a replica of the original cupola was added, and the windows were replaced.[^5] The same survey says the door has a latch but is not locked, so hikers can take shelter if needed, and that vandalism so far amounts to leftover trash and carved names.[^5] A Colorado lookout list on firelookout.org still shows the lookout as standing.[^6] The newest dated report read is from 2019, so current access is not confirmed here. The Register lists the Gunnison Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] Check with the district before a visit.
