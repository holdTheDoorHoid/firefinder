The Nestor Peak lookout stood near Husum in Klickitat County, on land the Washington Department of Natural Resources administered.[^1][^4][^5]

State crews started a road to the top in July 1957, for a proposed lookout site.[^5][^4]

A 40-foot wooden tower went up in 1958.[^1][^4][^5] The state's biennial report, as quoted on the Weebly page, says a lookout was completed in the 1956-58 biennium.[^4]

In the spring of 1960 Pat Neal was about to finish high school. She took the summer lookout job after the first woman hired for Nestor Peak gave it up.[^2][^5]

Neal's account, as WillhiteWeb reprints it, describes a 30-foot tower with a catwalk running around its living quarters and observation space.[^5] The lookout had neither power nor running water, and she hauled her water up by rope and pulley.[^5]

The register and Willhite give 40 feet for the 1958 tower, while Neal's account describes 30.[^1][^5]

In 1977 a two-story lookout building on a concrete base took the tower's place.[^1][^4][^5]

Sources disagree on how the structure ended. The register says it was removed in 1995, while Willhite says it was removed or destroyed that year.[^1][^5] FireLookout.com and the Weebly page say it was destroyed that year.[^3][^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
