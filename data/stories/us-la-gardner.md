The Gardner tower, in the Evangeline Unit of Kisatchie National Forest in Rapides Parish, was made by CCC Camp F-3, a Civilian Conservation Corps camp.[^1][^3] The Southern Forest Heritage Museum credits the camp with the tower and its cabin.[^1]

The museum record gives two heights.[^1] Its description says 100 feet, and another field in the same record says 110 feet.[^1] The two figures conflict, and this story does not choose between them.

The record includes a copy of a CCC publicity photograph of the tower and cabin dated March 16, 1935.[^1] A forest history by Anna C. Burns reproduces a set of 1937 views, and one caption places the Gardner Tower on the Evangeline Ranger District.[^2] The same history counts 18 fire towers among the projects the CCC built on Kisatchie.[^2]

In the forest's early years, towers were the main way fires were spotted. Aircraft patrols have since taken over most of that work.[^2]

No source describes what stands at the tower site now, or whether the public can visit it.

The Forest Service now calls this area the Evangeline Unit of the Calcasieu Ranger District.[^5] It describes about 97,000 acres of forests and lakes about 20 minutes west of Alexandria, with day hikes and off-highway trails.[^5] OpenStreetMap, a volunteer map, marks a tower at the spot and tags it as a lattice observation tower.[^4] A map entry shows where a point was placed, not what stands there.[^4]

The Forest Service page gives a Calcasieu Ranger District office address in Boyce for contact.[^5]
