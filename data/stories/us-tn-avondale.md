Avondale Lookout Tower stands on a ridge in Grainger County, Tennessee, at about 2,482 feet elevation.[^1] The register kept by Tennessee landforms researcher Tom Dunigan lists it under the code GRT001 and marks it simply "standing," with no note of when it went up, who built it, or who has managed it since.[^1] OpenStreetMap's mapping of the structure describes it as a lattice-built observation tower, the classic steel-frame design used on fire lookouts across the eastern mountains.[^2] A regional lookout-history site, Forest Lookouts, has a dedicated page for Avondale carrying a single photograph, but it gives no caption, date, or further description.[^3]

Beyond these bare facts, nothing turned up about Avondale's construction year, builder, staffing history, or current ownership and access. No newspaper account, Forest Service record, or National Register listing naming this tower was found. It may be a state Division of Forestry tower from Tennessee's lookout-building era, as many in the region were, but that is a guess, not a documented fact, so it is left out here. What is known is modest: the tower exists, it is of lattice-tower design, and as of these sources it was still standing.
