Johns Mountain is in Walker County, Georgia, inside the Chattahoochee National Forest.[^1][^3] The Civilian Conservation Corps built a lookout on Johns Mountain in 1940, on the site of a Confederate signal flagpole from the Civil War.[^1] The wooden tower stood 60 feet tall, with an 8-by-8-foot cab and a catwalk around the outside.[^1]

In 1961 the Forest Service tore the wooden tower down and put a steel tower up close by.[^1][^2] The steel tower was 80 feet tall, with a six-by-six-foot metal cab.[^1]

In 1979 the steel tower was dismantled and donated to the Walker County Correctional Institute.[^1][^2] One site, quoting the Armuchee Ranger District history, says a section of it was still serving as a lookout at the institute.[^1] A map database gives 1979 as the tower's start date, which does not fit the local histories.[^4] They date the tower to 1940 and use 1979 for its removal.[^1]

In 1964 the Accelerated Public Works Program built an overlook platform on the former tower site.[^2] In 1980 the area officially became a recreation area, developed by the Youth Conservation Corps.[^2]

Today a road runs up to the overlook from Pocket Road, about four miles along it from the Villanow crossroads.[^2] The road is unpaved, and from the top visitors can see East and West Armuchee.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the steel tower beyond the platform.
