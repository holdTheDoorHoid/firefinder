Richloam is a former community inside the Withlacoochee State Forest, in Hernando County, Florida.[^1][^3]

A 2018 blog post dates the fire tower to around the 1930s and 1940s and says it was used to monitor wild fires.[^2]

In 1936 the federal government, through the U.S. Land Resettlement Program, obtained the Richloam land from its private owners, and the U.S. Forest Service then took control of it.[^1][^3]

The sources disagree on when the state took over. The historical marker says the State of Florida bought the land in 1954 and added it to the Withlacoochee State Forest.[^3] Wikipedia says the Forest Service handed it to the Florida Board of Forestry, which made it a forest tract in 1958.[^1]

A 2018 blog post carries the title "Abandoned Richloam Fire Tower," yet the same post says the tower is open to the public.[^2]

Today the trailhead carries the tower's name: the Richloam Fire Tower Trailhead sits roughly 0.6 miles southeast of State Road 50, on Clay Sink Road.[^4] The 2018 historical marker stands by the tower, beside a DeSoto Trail kiosk.[^3] A posted sign at the stairs tells climbers they go up at their own risk.[^2] The trail network also includes the Florida National Scenic Trail.[^4]

The latest source found on the tower's condition is a 2018 blog post, so check the tower before you visit.[^2]
