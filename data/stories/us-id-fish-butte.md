Fish Butte Lookout stood on the Clearwater National Forest in Idaho County, about 22 miles east of Kamiah, at an elevation of 5,346 feet.[^1][^2] A camp lookout was set up there in 1915, and a log cabin was built in 1921.[^1][^2]

According to a May 9, 1958 item quoted on the Weebly page, Jack Parsell started with the Forest Service as a Fish Butte lookout in the summer of 1917.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists named staff from 1922 to 1929.[^2]

Sources disagree on the next cab.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts date a gabled L-4 cab on a 6-foot log base to 1931.[^1][^2] The Weebly page dates the work to 1930 and gives an 8-foot-high log base.[^3]

The Weebly page quotes The Clearwater Story for August 1934. Spot fires surrounded the lookout, so McHone fled to the Lochsa Ranger Station, and the lookout building burned.[^3] The same page says a cabin was manned through the 1953 season.[^3] None of the three sources explains how a lookout building that burned in 1934 came to be manned through 1953.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1953 a new cab went up: a hip-roofed L-4 on a concrete base, with a concrete floor and an eight-foot understory of concrete block. The older cabin was dismantled once the new one was done.[^1][^2][^3] A road up from Fish Butte Saddle, described as steep and narrow, was also built that year.[^3]

A 1981 Lochsa Ranger District history, quoted on the Weebly page, dates the last use to the late 1960s or early 1970s, followed by abandonment.[^3] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts instead give 1985, the year the lookout was confirmed gone.[^1][^2]

Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the site "Drive-Up" and "Removed / Destroyed" without explaining either tag.[^2] No source describes what remains on the butte today or whether the road to it is open.[^1][^2][^3]
