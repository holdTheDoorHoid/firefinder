The Model Fire Tower stands on the Land Between the Lakes National Recreation Area in Stewart County, Tennessee.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower went up around 1954 for the Tennessee Division of Forestry, on land that agency leased the same year from the Heath and Molloy families.[^1] Forest Lookouts lists a Stewart County tower called Barrow Hollow, and tnlandforms lists one called Barrow; both names probably refer to this tower, though no source says so outright.[^2][^3]

The tower is an 80-foot Aermotor LS-40 with a metal cab seven feet square and wooden steps.[^1] The recreation area was originally run by the Tennessee Valley Authority and later passed to the Forest Service, which the Register lists as the administrator.[^4][^1]

The Register entry, made on November 3, 2009, describes the tower as still used for fire detection.[^1] The sources we read do not name who staffed it or any fires it watched.

The 2009 entry is the most recent dated source we found, so the tower's condition since then is unconfirmed. The tnlandforms list, which carries no date, marks the Barrow listing as standing, with an elevation of 650 feet, where the Register gives 611 feet.[^3][^1] Access for visitors is not covered by any source we read. Wikipedia names the Forest Service as the recreation area's governing body, so check with that agency before any visit.[^4]
