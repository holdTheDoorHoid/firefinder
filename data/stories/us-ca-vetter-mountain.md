Vetter Mountain Lookout sits above the Chilao-Charlton Flats area of the Angeles National Forest, northeast of Los Angeles.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service built it in 1935 as part of a chain of 19 lookouts across the forest, a 14-by-14-foot wooden cab set directly on the ground rather than on legs, with steel shutters to protect it from vandals.[^1][^2] Sitting flush with the hilltop instead of up on a tower made it unusually easy to reach, and it became known among regulars as the "friendliest lookout" because visitors with limited mobility could get right up to it.[^2]

The Angeles National Forest Fire Lookout Association (ANFFLA) reopened the lookout to the public with volunteer staffing on May 30, 1998, and the structure was added to the National Historic Lookout Register that October.[^1][^2] For over a decade afterward it was one of only two lookouts still standing on the Angeles National Forest, the rest having been lost over the years.[^1]

That run ended on September 1, 2009, when the Station Fire burned through and destroyed the original cab.[^1][^2] Rather than let the site go dark, ANFFLA volunteers kept watch from a temporary structure on the mountain for the next ten years while plans for a rebuild came together.[^2] In September 2019, exactly a decade after the fire, the Forest Service and ANFFLA partnered with Southern California Edison to reconstruct the lookout, this time with concrete board and steel framing instead of wood.[^1][^3] Construction finished in the spring of 2020, and the rebuilt lookout was first staffed again over the Fourth of July weekend that year.[^1][^2]

Today Vetter Mountain is back in regular service, staffed by around 60 trained ANFFLA volunteers who hike up to watch for smoke, typically one to three people at a time from sunrise to sunset.[^3] It remains a popular and accessible day hike off the Angeles Crest Highway, open to visitors, with an Osborne Fire Finder inside for those who climb up to see it.[^1][^3]
