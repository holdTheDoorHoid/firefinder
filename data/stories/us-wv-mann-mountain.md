Mann Mountain sits on state land in Fayette County, at about 3,335 feet of elevation.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it the first lookout registered in West Virginia, and says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1935.[^1] The tower was a 45-foot Blaw-Knox steel structure with a 7 by 7 foot cab.[^1][^2] Stone also went into the living quarters and the wall around the tower. The register calls the house a stone ground house.[^1][^2]

A 1950 newspaper item, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, reports that the road to the tower was finished and the tower now had electricity.[^2] A 1961 item describes a state crash program that employed several men to improve the road and maintain the tower and its observer's cabin.[^2]

The register says the Division of Natural Resources administered the tower, with the Division of Forestry as a cooperator. The Forest Lookouts page names the Division of Forestry as the agency, so the two sources disagree about who ran it.[^1][^2]

The state's encyclopedia says the forest fire towers were closed in 1990. I found no source that gives the date this tower stopped being used.[^4] The West Virginia list still marks Mann Mountain as standing.[^3] That same list includes Gartin (Mann) Knob, a different standing tower in Lincoln County, so do not confuse the two.[^3] I found no current road, gate or visiting information. Check with the managing agency before you go.
