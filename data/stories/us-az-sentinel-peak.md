Sentinel Peak is the southernmost summit in the Chiricahua Mountains, at 8,999 feet, on the Coronado National Forest.[^1] It once carried a fire lookout on its top.[^2]

In May 1945 a Tucson newspaper report, quoted on a lookout history site, tied the hiring of women lookouts on the Coronado forest to a wartime shortage of men.[^3] It named Mrs. Alice Hultz as stationed on Sentinel Peak.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a ground lookout that is gone.[^5] According to a trail guide, the lookout was removed long ago, but a foundation and a commemorative metal plaque remain.[^4] A 2019 hiking blog reached a similar view: little is left at the top beyond some litter and a foundation.[^2]

The Forest Service describes the summit as reached by the Crest Trail from Junction Saddle.[^1] The trail guide adds a 420-foot spur to the top, and warns that the trail beyond South Fork has not been surveyed for several years, so conditions are unknown.[^4] The summit is partly screened by trees, though the Forest Service says the trees open to a wide view a short distance below the top.[^1]
