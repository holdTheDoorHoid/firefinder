Beckler Peak had a lookout by about 1924.[^1][^4] WillhiteWeb says the first setup was a little platform set among three living trees, and that a 25-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6-foot cab was later built on top of it.[^4] The register describes a 25-foot pole tower with a cab and log cabin, and the site sits at 4,950 feet (1,509 meters).[^1]

WillhiteWeb also says a 10-by-16-foot log cabin later took the place of the tent, and that a source credits the equipment and tent with coming over from Cleveland Mountain in 1924.[^4] Before a telephone line reached the ranger station, the lookout signaled with a heliograph, Willhite writes.[^4]

The Washington lookouts page quotes an April 1930 report. It says the lookout saw a Great Northern work train near Bridge #412 and reported a fire there on August 1 to the Skykomish Ranger Station. The report talks about "the past season" and does not give the fire's year.[^3]

Panoramic photos were shot on August 10, 1934, from a point 42 feet above the ground.[^3][^4]

Sources differ on how the tower ended. The register says it was removed in 1958.[^1] firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^4] Willhite also blames a winter storm that snapped the guy wires and brought the tower down, but the page text we read gives no source for that account.[^4]

On a 2015 visit, Willhite found footings: four large sloping ones and four smaller square ones.[^4] He suspects a third structure once stood on the summit, though he has not found a picture of it.[^4] He says the only view left today looks south into the Alpine Lakes Wilderness.[^4] The Washington lookouts page also says there is plenty of lookout debris spread across the site.[^3]

Willhite calls Beckler Peak one of the Wild Sky Wilderness's most popular hikes. He describes the road as dirt and gravel, in good condition, and says the drive from Skykomish runs 3.1 miles east before a left turn onto FR-6066.[^4] We list access as public.
