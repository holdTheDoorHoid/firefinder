Lookout Mountain is a peak in Sheridan County, Wyoming, on the Big Horn National Forest.[^1][^2] A lookout was in use there by 1921, when a deputy forest supervisor said lookouts posted at Lookout Mountain and the Hunter ranger station could watch all of the Big Horns' forest land.[^1] In 1922 the forest planned a lookout for the peak near Woodrock.[^1] A June 30 report said one was in place during periods of fire danger and that no other proper lookouts were in service.[^1]

Staffing was patchy. In 1925 the forest could not hire a man who was competent or willing to make the reports it wanted, so no lightning-storm records were obtained.[^1] In 1935 two men from the Tongue River CCC camp manned the lookout from July through September.[^1]

In October 1938 the Big Horn forest supervisor announced a new station on Black Mountain, which would improve watch over the west side of the range and replace the old station atop Lookout Mountain.[^1][^3] Two transcriptions date that announcement to October 22 and October 23, so the exact day is uncertain.[^1][^3]

The Black Mountain lookout is 14 by 14 feet, with a catwalk on a stone foundation, and was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^4] The register dates construction to 1939, while a 1940 CCC inspection note says it was completed but not inspected.[^3][^4] At about 9,500 feet, the site sits above timberline and draws hikers, though it is no longer used for fire detection.[^4]

None of the sources read describes what, if anything, remains of the Lookout Mountain station itself.
