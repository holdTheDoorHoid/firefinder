Figueroa Mountain Lookout stands on the summit of Figueroa Mountain in the Los Padres National Forest, Santa Barbara County, at about 4,480 feet (1,366 meters).[^1][^2] The Forest Service moved its lookout station there from Zaca Peak in 1917.[^2] A lookout named Zollo Ontiveros, whom the papers also call Z. A. Ontiveros, worked the lookout from at least 1926 to 1931.[^2]

According to the register, the first cab was a C3 cab on a timber tower.[^1] In 1922 newspapers reported that work had begun on a new station with a building largely of glass, and an August 1922 item said it was hoped to finish the station that year. The sources we read do not confirm when it was finished.[^2] The register says the cab was replaced in 1965 by a 13-by-13-foot CL-100 metal cab on a 12-foot concrete block base.[^1]

The last staffing item in the history we read is a 1939 notice that William Green would start as lookout.[^2] The register says the station has been unstaffed since it closed, presumably in the 1970s.[^1]

The register was accepted on June 19, 2010, after a nomination by Brad Eells.[^1] At its November 2020 visit, the register's reviewer found the windows broken and the furnishings destroyed. The Forest Service had cleared debris from inside and secured the door with a chain.[^1]

In November 2022 the report's author and Mike Guerin met the acting district ranger at the lookout to inspect it and discuss refurbishing.[^3] In January 2025 the report's author met District Ranger Benjamin Gray and three archaeologists on site about refurbishing the stripped-out cab.[^4] The report says that meeting fell on Inauguration Day, when executive orders to cut back the federal workforce were issued that same afternoon, and that staffers did not return messages after that day.[^4] The project is listed as suspended.[^4]

Today the Forest Service lists a day-use site beside the lookout as open daily and year-round, though it may close in winter because of wet weather.[^5] The access road is dirt.[^5] From Highway 154 it is 13 miles north on Figueroa Mountain Road.[^5] The register's 2020 visit found the approach road behind a locked gate, so the two sources do not agree on access.[^1][^5] When the register visited, the cab's furnishings were gone and its door was secured with a chain.[^1] The Forest Service page describes the adjacent site but does not say the tower is open to visitors.[^5]
