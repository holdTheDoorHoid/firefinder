Big Bar Mountain sits in Butte County, in the Plumas National Forest, at about 4,343 feet.[^1] The earlier lookout was a three-foot-wide platform fixed to a tree trunk about 60 feet up.[^2] In 1924 the Forest Service supervised construction of a new station on a steel tower to replace it.[^2] By August 1925 a steel lookout station had replaced the tree lookout.[^2] The registry gives 1949 as the build date of a 55-foot steel tower, built to a California Region 5 plan.[^1]

James Hanson was the lookout man at Big Bar in 1924, Cass Wilkins was the lookout in August 1925, and Roy Deer was stationed there in October 1928.[^2] Peakbagging says no one has staffed the lookout since about 1976, and that it served as a relay point for communications.[^4] The same page says CAL FIRE maintains it and brings in staff when needed during fire season, so we could not settle this.[^4]

The Camp Fire destroyed the lookout in 2018.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2018 report for its California-Northern chapter says the Big Bar Lookout was destroyed during the Camp Fire.[^3] Peakbagging dates the loss to November 2018.[^4]

The registry records the lookout as removed in the 2018 Camp Fire, and no source we read describes a surviving tower.[^1][^3] We found no current access information, so we cannot say what remains on the summit or whether anyone can visit.
