Windy Peak rises to 8,334 feet in the Okanogan National Forest, in Okanogan County, Washington.[^1][^2]

E.W. Allen and Raymond Johnson built the lookout in 1932, according to a Wenatchee paper from that August and to WillhiteWeb.[^3][^4] It was a gabled-roofed L-4 cab.[^4] The register and FireLookout.com describe the same design and date.[^1][^2]

Radio equipment was also being installed that year. A July 23, 1932 item in The Wenatchee World said the Forest Service's new short-wave sets were being installed in the Chelan forest, and it named Windy Peak as one of the lookout stations to get them.[^3]

Panoramic photos were taken from the site on September 18, 1934.[^1][^4] The Aircraft Warning Service used the lookout briefly in 1942, from July 20 to September 6.[^3][^4] In 1956 a survey party said the lookout would soon be demolished.[^4]

The sources give the same year for the end but differ on the word. The register says the cab was removed in 1963, and FireLookout.com agrees on the year.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says it was destroyed that year instead.[^4]

WillhiteWeb says recent fires have swept every side of the peak, and a few stunted old trees remain near timberline.[^4] It also mentions an old phone line that still follows the northwest slope down to the saddle.[^4] None of the four sources describes what remains of the cab itself.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Visitors reach the summit on foot.[^4] WillhiteWeb describes the 7-mile Windy Peak Trail starting at Long Swamp Campground, and a shorter Windy Creek Trail from the Cathedral Driveway Trailhead, which it lists at 5.5 miles one way.[^4] The gravel roads to the trailheads are described as good.[^4] None of the sources describes a closure or a permit for these routes.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
