Alberta's wildfire register lists the present Whitefish lookout as a steel tower, 30 m (98 ft) tall and about 705 m (2,314 ft) above sea level. It dates that structure to 2003.[^1]

Earlier newspaper items show a Whitefish tower with tower men posted to it. In May 1958 a tower man resigned, and a replacement was to be taken on soon.[^2] One tower man finished his season at Whitefish in November 1958.[^2] In November 1959 the fire season closed and the tower men left, one of them having been stationed at Whitefish.[^2] By May 1967 the tower men had returned to work.[^2]

Those items come from the Peace River Record Gazette, as quoted by a lookout-history site. They predate the 2003 date in the register, so a Whitefish lookout was in use before the steel tower was built.[^1][^2]

The register lists the tower as active and reached by road, and it lists no weather station.[^1] Alberta calls its lookouts integral to wildfire detection and sites them where visibility is good.[^3]

No source I found describes the site on the ground today, or says whether the public may visit. Check with the managing agency before planning a trip.
