A lookout went up on Palisade Mountain in 1930, in the Bitterroot National Forest.[^1][^2] A 1965 survey places the station on a rocky, double-topped ridge between Willow Creek and Burnt Fork Lake.[^2] In July 1930 the Ravalli Republican reported that building material for a lookout station had been taken on pack mules over nine miles of steep trail to the high point.[^2] By November the Missoulian described a new standard lookout cabin at the head of Willow, Gold and Burnt Fork creeks.[^2] The cab was a gabled L-4 design.[^1] One listing also says a log and frame cabin was built on the summit in 1926, followed by the L-4 cab about 1930. The newspaper items do not mention the earlier cabin.[^1][^2]

A listing records lookout staff on the peak from 1956 through 1965, and Ravalli Republic items from 1962 and 1963 name lookouts too.[^1][^2]

A tower with a concrete base and a flat R-6 cab went up on nearby Willow Mountain in 1966, and sources say it replaced Palisade.[^3] The sources disagree about the end of the old cab. One listing says it was destroyed in 1969, while the Montana Forest Lookouts page says it was removed in 1967 when its duties moved to Willow.[^1][^2] They also disagree on direction: the listing places Willow two miles northwest of Palisade, and the Willow page says two miles southeast.[^1][^3]

Willow Mountain is still staffed each summer.[^3] No source describes what remains on the Palisade summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors. The survey also places a brass Forest Service cap in rock northeast of an "old L.O.T.", an abbreviation the survey does not explain, but nothing says whether the cap is still there.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Palisade Mountain as gone.[^4]
