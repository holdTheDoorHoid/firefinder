The Leasburg fire tower was built in 1939 and 1940 on a five-acre site in Crawford County.[^1]

In 1939 the Missouri Conservation Commission acquired the five acres for the tower.[^1] That year a report of towers being erected put the Leasburg tower at 67 feet (20 m).[^1] By late September 1939 the Commission had authorized a dwelling and garage for the site, and work on them had begun.[^1] A 1940 report gives 68 feet (21 m) for the steel tower erected on the site, the figure used here.[^1]

The Sullivan News reported in September 1945 that Merrell Stone was back from the war and had returned to work as the fire watcher at the Leasburg tower.[^1]

Later annual reports list upkeep at the site from 1964 to 1978.[^1] In fiscal 1964-65 the dwelling was completely remodeled, and the work added two bedrooms and a bathroom.[^1] Later years brought major repairs to the residence (1967-68), a new furnace and tower entrance road (1972-73), a two-room addition and another new entrance road (1973-74), repaired doors and a new water heater (1976), and a field drain (1977-78).[^1] The last entry on the source page is for fiscal 1977-78.[^1]

No source we read describes the site today, so we cannot say whether the tower still stands or whether the towersite is open to visitors.
