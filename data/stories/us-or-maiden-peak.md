Maiden Peak, a shield volcano on the Cascade Crest, sits on the boundary between Lane and Deschutes counties.[^5] Rex's page and Willhite's history both date observation use of the summit to 1908; Rex's page adds that there were no improvements at the time.[^3][^4] By contrast, the register and firelookout.com date the first camp to 1920, which was replaced in 1923 by a cupola cabin.[^1][^2] Rex's page quotes a June 1923 Bend Bulletin item: Jim Gauldon of Bend had been assigned to build the station at a cost of about $650.[^3]

Early lookouts named in the histories include Ed Mahn in 1912 and Charles Bowers in 1913.[^3][^4] The station was discontinued temporarily in 1918 and was discontinued for the year in 1922.[^3] The last person named as staffing it was James Stearns, in 1942.[^3][^4]

The sources disagree on how the lookout ended. Rex's page and Willhite's history both say District Ranger Tom Greathouse and Ray Koski burned the lookout house in the fall of 1955.[^3][^4] The register, firelookout.com and Wikipedia instead date its removal or destruction to 1958.[^1][^2][^5]

A hiking guide says a former fire lookout once stood on the summit, and a photo caption in it says metal parts of the lookout remain.[^4] Hikers reach it on foot: a dirt road leads to a small parking area and trailhead, and a trail climbs toward the Pacific Crest Trail and on to the summit.[^4] No source we read describes the remains in more detail.
