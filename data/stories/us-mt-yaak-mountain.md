Yaak Mountain has carried some kind of fire-watch structure for well over a century. The earliest was a 45-foot pole platform put up in 1915, followed around 1919 by a frame cupola cabin.[^1] In the 1930s crews replaced that with a 50-foot pole tower topped by an L-6 cab, along with a separate log cabin for living quarters.[^1] The tower standing today dates to 1958: a 41-foot treated-timber tower carrying an R-6 flat cab, built by the Kootenai National Forest to replace the aging 1930s setup.[^1][^2]

The lookout was staffed regularly until 1988, after which the Forest Service brought it back into service only occasionally, including seasons in 1995, 1998, and 2000.[^3] By the time of its National Historic Lookout Register listing, no Osborne fire finder remained in the cab, though the register's reviewer noted it could still see sporadic staffing.[^3] It was added to the Register on January 3, 2015.[^4]

The tower has since gone into the Forest Service's recreation rental program and is open to overnight guests from mid-June through late September.[^3][^2] Sitting at about 4,977 feet, the tower has a catwalk around the cab, sleeping space for up to four people, a propane stove and heater, and an outhouse nearby; there's no electricity or running water, so guests bring their own.[^2] Getting there means driving primitive, high-clearance roads to a gate on Forest Road 4407, then following that road about 5.3 miles to the lookout.[^2] Hikers can also walk in along the road, past the remnants of the earlier structures that once stood on the mountain, though the road itself is closed to motorized use except for registered guests.[^2]
