Bald Mountain Lookout stands on Bald Mountain in the Eldorado National Forest, in El Dorado County.[^1] Its site is about 12 miles east of Georgetown.[^2]

The earliest notice in the sources read dates from 1911. An October 1911 notice, quoted on a Forest Lookouts page, says the government was putting up a dwelling that would serve as a lasting base for a lookout man on the summit.[^2]

A steel tower followed. Two 1912 notices give its height as 60 feet, one from May describing it as ordered and another from August as finished.[^2] An October 1912 report instead gives 62 feet, with a concrete base, and names Ranger Jones, Guard Melson and lookout Kelley as its builders.[^2] The sources disagree on the height.

Winds toppled the tower in February 1915, and a May 1915 notice said a more substantial structure would replace it.[^2] By July 1915 a crew was erecting a new steel tower, which the paper called the latest model, built to resist exceptional wind stress.[^2]

Lookouts appear by name in the local papers. R.F. Kelley was assigned to the post in 1914, and Orin Murdock was sent to the station in April 1931.[^2] In June 1932 Mr. and Mrs. R.O. Murdock went up to the station for the summer, with Mr. Murdock as the lookout in charge.[^2] An October 1938 item still names a Lookout Orrie Murdock on Bald Mountain.[^2]

In October 1958 the Forest Service advertised sealed bids for the lookout and for dismantling the existing tower.[^2] The sources read do not say when the old tower came down. The register gives 1965 as the build date of the present lookout, a 14-by-14-foot cab of metal construction on a 30-foot steel tower, and says the site has had four stations.[^1]

A 1982 Mountain Democrat feature describes a woman named Delma who lived in the tower's cab from Sunday through Thursday, May through November.[^2] The forest's fire specialist called it the area's main tower, with electricity, water and easy access.[^2] A 300-gallon water tank was stolen from the station sometime during the winter, and the theft was discovered in May 1983.[^2]

The register added the lookout on September 21, 2004, and names the Georgetown Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] I found no current staffing, rental or public-visit information. Check with the district before planning a trip, and do not plan a climb without asking first.
