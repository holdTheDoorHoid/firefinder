Bald Mountain sits about two miles south-southwest of Ketchum in Blaine County, and a lookout has stood on it since 1921, when a wooden structure went up.[^4][^1][^3] A 1946 newspaper item quoted by one local history calls it Baldy Mountain.[^3]

The second building is where the sources disagree. The register says an L-4 cabin on a stone base went up in 1937.[^1] A Forest lookouts history instead dates a new building to work begun by Civilian Conservation Corps crews in June 1940 and completed in 1941. It describes separate restrooms for men and women and a public lounge.[^3] The sources do not say whether these accounts describe the same building.

The current structure, a frame cab on a concrete base, dates to 1958.[^1][^4]

Fire watchers staffed the lookout from at least the 1940s into the 1990s.[^3][^1] In 1946, Bob Hewitt was stationed at the lookout for the fire season.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Mr. and Mrs. Sluder in 1951, Mr. and Mrs. Arnold Zenk in 1954, and Fred Judd in 1971.[^2] The Zenks were local teachers.[^3]

Staffing ended in the mid-1990s.[^1] It no longer serves for fire detection, but it still operates as a communications site.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register listed Bald Mountain on September 28, 2020.[^1]

Idaho Fire Lookouts files the tower under "Drive-In Emergency Status."[^2] We found no source describing public visits or rentals, so treat it as a working site. Check with the Sawtooth National Forest before planning a trip, and do not climb any part of it without permission.
