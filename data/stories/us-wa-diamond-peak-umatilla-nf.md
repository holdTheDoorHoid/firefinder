Diamond Peak is one of the higher named peaks in the Blue Mountains, in Garfield County, Washington, on the Umatilla National Forest.[^1][^2][^3] The register and FireLookout.com put it at 6,397 feet (1,950 meters), while WillhiteWeb's hiking figures give 6,379 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

Sources disagree on when the lookout went up. The register says 1937, while FireLookout.com and WillhiteWeb say 1931.[^1][^2][^3] WillhiteWeb says a crew of forest staff built the house in 1931, and that Ernie Colwell completed it in 1932 while working there as the lookout dispatcher.[^3] The structure was a 14-by-14 cab on a 20-foot timber tower, according to a Umatilla National Forest inventory.[^3][^4] National Archives panoramas of the Diamond Peak Lookout were taken on August 6, 1935.[^1][^4]

An account that WillhiteWeb quotes, headed September 1929, describes a night call for help.[^3] The lookout had called in to say a snake had bitten him.[^3] Rescuers reached him around one in the morning and found him well.[^3] The rescuers concluded a yellowjacket had bitten him, but the lookout insisted it was a snake.[^3]

The register and FireLookout.com both say the tower came down around 1960, and WillhiteWeb says it was likely removed about then.[^1][^2][^3] A photo in the register's gallery is labeled 1953.[^1]

No source we read describes lookout remains on the summit today. WillhiteWeb mentions only a small antenna used for communications and a survey marker at the top.[^3] The route follows the Mount Misery Trail from a trailhead reached by FR-4030.[^3] The trail covers just under a mile to a saddle and then continues to the summit.[^3] WillhiteWeb warns that one section is unmaintained and that trees may lie across the trail.[^3] The summit falls only barely within the Wenaha-Tucannon Wilderness.[^3]
