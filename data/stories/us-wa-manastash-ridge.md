The Manastash Ridge lookout sat in Kittitas County, Washington, about nine miles southwest of Ellensburg, at about 3,930 feet.[^1][^2] A two-story structure went up there in 1942, which the register and Firelookout.com both call a smokehouse.[^3][^1][^2]

A 1960 DNR photo shoot with the lookout Barbara Gerwig left excellent photos of the building.[^3]

In 1962 the Department of Natural Resources replaced it with a 40-foot wooden tower, which its carpentry crew built for roughly $8,000.[^1][^3] A state biennial report covering 1960 to 1962 gives an average cost of $8,000 for each of the nine lookouts the crew completed.[^4]

The register says the tower was removed in 1970, while WillhiteWeb and Firelookout.com call it destroyed that year.[^1][^3][^2]

The site is now a University of Washington observatory building, and it is fenced off.[^3] From outside the fence, WillhiteWeb's author saw no footings.[^3]

A UW observatory page says the observatory is open from May through November, and that approved student groups can propose for observing time.[^5] A 1998 Seattle Times report said the university was closing it and moving its money to a New Mexico site; the sources read do not show whether that closure happened.[^6]

The road is gated; when the gate is open, a car can reach the top, and a seasonal closure runs from December 8 to May 1.[^3] WillhiteWeb also says the site might be reached on foot as a long off-season hike from Ellensburg along the Manastash Ridge Trail.[^3]
