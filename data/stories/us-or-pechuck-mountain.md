Fire detection began at Pechuck Mountain in 1918, when the site held nothing more than a primitive rangefinder for spotting smoke.[^1] The stone-and-wood cupola cabin that stands there now came later, built in 1932 by John Oblack, a Czechoslovakian from nearby Molalla, with help from an unnamed stone mason from Portland.[^1][^2] They used native stone quarried close to the site, giving the lookout a look often compared to Timberline Lodge on Mount Hood, and making it the only stone lookout left standing in Oregon.[^3][^1] The lower story served as living quarters for whoever staffed the lookout, while the upper story's windows on all four sides gave a full 360-degree view for spotting fires.[^1]

Pechuck was run by the Oregon Department of Forestry, and sources differ slightly on when staffing ended: one account gives 1963, another says the Forest Service (through what is now BLM) decided in 1964 that the lookout was no longer needed as part of fire detection, as aerial patrols and better roads made ground lookouts less essential.[^2][^1] Either way, it sat unused for decades afterward. According to the National Historic Lookout Register, the building survived mainly because tearing it down would have taken more effort than leaving it alone.[^3]

Restoration work began in 1991, when volunteers organized by Jim Borders and the Molalla area community, with help from Doug Newman, Don Allen and Bill Joy of the Sand Mountain Society, re-roofed the structure during the autumn rains.[^2] The project was later carried on by a group called Friends of Pechuck.[^2] The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 1990, the second site ever listed there, and to the World Fire Lookout Register in 1992.[^3][^1]

Today Pechuck Lookout sits on land managed by the Bureau of Land Management near the south end of the Table Rock Wilderness, and it is open to the public for day visits and overnight stays on a first-come, first-served basis, with no reservation needed.[^1] The most scenic approach is the long, difficult hike from the Rooster Rock Trailhead on the Table Rock Trail; a steep, closed fire road from South Molalla River Road offers another route in. Good map and orientation skills are recommended either way.[^1]
