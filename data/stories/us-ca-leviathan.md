Leviathan Lookout stands on Leviathan Peak in Alpine County, California, inside the Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest.[^1][^5] The U.S. Forest Service administers it, and the register lists the Carson Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1]

The register gives 1957 as the build year. It calls the lookout an unusual R4 adaptation of a flat-roof design similar to R6, with a concrete-block ground floor and a cab on a catwalk.[^1] A 1958 Reno newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, tells a different start. It says temporary lookout facilities stood on the peak in 1957 and 1958, and that work on the permanent station began that October.[^2]

The idea followed a 1956 incident in which two fishermen at Topaz Lake spotted a fire on the West Walker River and hiked out to report it.[^2] The 1958 item says spotters found six fires each year, and that the result is a permanent lookout.[^2] The station was to be built for a man and wife lookout team. Its cab was a glass design meant to let spotters see well with little glare, set on a cinder-block base about ten feet high.[^2]

The register added the lookout on June 4, 1998, with Mark Swift as nominator, as US 282, CA 16.[^1] A photo on the register page from August 2024 shows the lookout still standing.[^1] That same year, a Forest Fire Lookout Association schedule lists an August 14 evaluation of the lookout, with a report to be submitted to the association's Sierra Nevada director, Jeff Baggett.[^4]

A lookout photo site says the tower is in good shape but has not been staffed recently. The page gives no date for that statement.[^3]

Today the route to the summit is Leviathan Peak Road (Forest Road 31057), an unpaved road that a third-party guide recommends driving in a 4WD vehicle.[^5] Drivers reach a locked gate and then walk about a quarter mile to the tower; the final section is open only to service vehicles.[^5] The guide says winter closes the road, which usually reopens between early April and late November.[^5] The same guide calls the lookout old and abandoned, which does not match the photo site's note that it is in good shape.[^5][^3] The sources read do not say whether the cab is open to visitors, so check with the Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest before you go.
