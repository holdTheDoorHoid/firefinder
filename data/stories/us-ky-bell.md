The Bell Lookout Tower stands in Clay County, Kentucky, on land the National Historic Lookout Register places in the Daniel Boone National Forest. The Register says the Forest Service presumably runs the tower but does not know when it was built.[^1]

The earliest clue is a 1941 item the Register quotes from a Somerset newspaper. It says the Forest Service lookout called the Icy Cliff tower in Clay County was renamed the John William Bell Tower, in memory of a man who was district state forester when he died.[^1] The Register files that renaming under this entry, which suggests the same structure, but no source describes what the tower looked like in 1941.

A 1950 federal survey description gives more physical detail. It calls the tower a four-legged steel structure about 80 feet high, with an observation room painted gray at the top, and says the survey point sat at the center of its base.[^2] The Register also places the tower on a 1954 USGS map of the area.[^1] Eighty feet is roughly 24 meters.

No source says who staffed the tower or what fires it watched.

The Register describes the tower as being in rough shape. A fenced building nearby holds a radio antenna and appears on the Forest Service's visitor map as the Bell Tower Comm site.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page lists the tower as standing in Google Earth imagery dated April 4, 2023.[^2] No source reports a fire, a closure, or a move.

The Register recorded the tower on June 28, 2026, with Tyler Helton as nominator, and gives its elevation as 1,959 feet (597 meters).[^1] The sources do not say whether the public may visit or climb it. Given the rough shape they describe, anyone planning a trip should ask the Forest Service before going up.
