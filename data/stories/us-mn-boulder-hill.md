The Boulder Hill lookout was built in 1934 by the Civilian Conservation Corps on the Chippewa National Forest, near Bena in Cass County.[^1][^2][^3] Its 100-foot steel tower had a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and the site also had a towerman's cabin and a garage.[^1]

A 1959 Coast and Geodetic Survey description places the tower about 1-3/4 miles west of Bena, about half a mile south of U.S. Highway 2.[^2] That description gives its overall height as 110 feet, while the National Historic Lookout Register says 100 feet, so the sources disagree on height.[^1][^2] The Minnesota Historical Society says logging-era forest fires led the state to create the Minnesota Forest Service, a forerunner of the Department of Natural Resources.[^3]

According to the Register, the tower was in use until the late 1960s.[^1] In 1976 it was taken down and relocated to the Minnesota Forest History Center in Grand Rapids.[^1][^2] After the move, the Register says, it was not open to the public. It also records a 1997 restoration to its original condition and an April 1998 registration.[^1]

No source we read describes what remains at the Bena site today. The tower itself now stands at the Forest History Center, where the Register says interpreters staff it and it is open every day from Memorial Day through October 15.[^1] The Minnesota Historical Society gives different hours: the tower keeps regular business hours, is closed from November through May for safety, and admits no more than four climbers at a time.[^3] Climbers must be at least 6 years old, and the tower closes in thunderstorms, strong rains and high winds.[^3] Check the center's hours before you go, since the two sources do not agree on the season.[^1][^3]
