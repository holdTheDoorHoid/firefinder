Spotted Bear Mountain Lookout stands in the Flathead National Forest, about 4.5 miles air-line southeast of the Spotted Bear Ranger Station.[^4] The register says the first lookout there was a two-story log cabin, 13 by 13 feet, set up in 1916.[^1][^2] A Forest History Society timeline gives an earlier date, 1914, for a lookout on the mountain, which it says was used until it was replaced in 1933.[^3] The two accounts do not reconcile.

The present tower is 20 feet tall, built of treated timber, and the register dates it to 1963.[^1][^2][^4] Newspaper items tell a longer story. A 1959 item mentions a 50-foot tower being built, while a 1961 item says a helicopter flew in a dismantled tower and cabin to replace one rated unsafe, and that the new tower would be 20 feet high.[^4] A January 1962 item calls the October helicopter lift the first helicopter project in the Flathead National Forest.[^4]

A July 1936 newspaper item describes smoke from the station that turned out to be no fire, and a lookout there who asked a friend for pipe tobacco, saying it was lonely up there.[^4] The register says the tower is regularly staffed in summer.[^1]

In August 2015, as the Bear Creek fire approached, the lookout was wrapped in fire-resistant material. The next day its staff were evacuated by helicopter.[^4] The register records its registration on 9 January 2021.[^1]

Today the lookout is staffed in summer.[^1] An older Forest Service station description, quoted on a lookout-history site, puts the trail at about 8 miles southeast from the ranger station, mostly uphill.[^4] The sources do not say whether visitors may climb the tower, so check with the Spotted Bear Ranger District before planning a trip.
