A newspaper item of April 30, 1922, describes materials sent from Tucson for a fire lookout on a peak it calls Mount Fly, in the Chiracahua forest.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page, which reprints that item from the Tombstone Epitaph, places the peak in Cochise County, Arizona, and spells its name Fly Peak.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association list spells it Flys Peak.[^2]

The item says the materials went to Ranger E.R. Scofield for a 60-foot lookout.[^1] It describes a timber tower with a glassed-in cupola, held at its base by four angle irons set in concrete. It puts the site at 9,790 feet.[^1] The article says it is the first lookout in the district built to newly adopted standards.[^1]

The 1922 item describes the lookout as planned, and it does not say when the tower was finished.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list shows the Flys Peak tower as gone.[^2] The three names are treated as one site, matched on name and county, because the Forest Lookouts page gives no coordinates to confirm the match. We found no source that describes what, if anything, is left on the peak today, or whether the public can reach it.

[^2]: Arizona Lookouts, Forest Fire Lookout Association, https://firelookout.org/lookouts/us/az/ (accessed 2026-10-10).
