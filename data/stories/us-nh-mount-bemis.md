Mount Bemis is a 3,725-foot peak in Crawford Notch, in the White Mountains.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists its lookout tower in Grafton County as gone.[^2]

The 1940 edition of the AMC White Mountain Guide describes a new lookout tower on the peak.[^3] A hiking blog describes the tower as a wooden structure 29 feet tall that ran for about eight years, from 1940 to 1948.[^4] The blog's end date does not match the guides. The 1960 edition still lists a lookout tower on the peak, and the 1972 edition says the tower was dismantled in 1968.[^3][^4]

A photograph by Ben English, taken while the tower still stood, is according to Iris Baird the only known picture of it.[^4]

Parts of the tower are still on the mountain.[^4] Photos from the blog's visit show part of the torn-down structure, and other pieces lie scattered around the site.[^4]

The tower sat on what the blog calls a sub-peak, and the true summit lies about 400 feet to the west and is reached by a bushwhack.[^4] The route leaves the Nancy Pond Trail where an old fire warden's trail branches off about 1.6 miles in.[^4] The blog puts the tower site about 3.7 miles from the trailhead on US 302.[^4] The old trail is unmaintained.[^1] The blog warns that removing artifacts from the National Forest is illegal, which suggests the site is on National Forest land.[^4] Its view is limited to a narrow gap facing the Presidential Range, according to the blog.[^4]
