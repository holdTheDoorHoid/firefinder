Black Mountain Lookout stands at 6,735 feet in the Plumas National Forest, in Plumas County, California.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1934 (the National Historic Lookout Register also lists 1935 as the tower's "built" year, a common discrepancy between when construction started and when it was finished or commissioned).[^1] It's a classic 14-by-14-foot C3 live-in cab with a catwalk, following the Forest Service's R-5 design used across the California region, mounted on an 11-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1]

Records available for this tower don't give a clear staffing history, so it isn't known how long Black Mountain was actively staffed as a fire lookout or when regular staffing ended.

The lookout was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on April 23, 2007, nominated by Daniel Elliott, the district archaeologist, under the sponsorship of the Beckwourth Ranger District.[^1]

Black Mountain's working life as a fire lookout gave way to a second life as a rental cabin. The Forest Service held a grand opening for the lookout as a recreational rental on May 26, 2011, at a nightly fee of $60.[^1] Today it's booked through Recreation.gov. The cabin has two single beds and can sleep up to four, with a small table and chairs, a refrigerator, a stove, a heater and lights, all running on electricity; there's also room for tent camping next to the tower for up to four more visitors.[^2] Guests bring their own food, sleeping bags, towels and similar supplies.[^2] Access is by dirt road, with high-clearance vehicles recommended, and entry to the cabin is by combination lock, with the code available from the Beckwourth Ranger District.[^2]
