Braswell is a fire lookout in Oregon County, on land of the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1] It shares its name with an unincorporated community that had a post office from 1894 to 1915.[^2] The community takes its name from Thomas Braswell, an early settler.[^2]

A 100-foot steel tower was erected here in 1934.[^1] In September 1936 a newspaper reported a cabin going up at the tower, built by local ERA men working with men from Camp F-17, to serve as living quarters for the lookout.[^1] In January 1937 the tower was refloored and lined with plywood below its windows.[^1] A November 1938 item said work had started on picnic grounds at the site, with tables, benches, a bonfire ring, fireplaces and sanitary facilities planned.[^1]

Staffing shows up mostly in family news.[^1] In November 1941 a newspaper said Harley Dickerson, the towerman at Eastwood, was being moved back to the Braswell tower.[^1] An April 1942 item placed Harvey Dickerson and his family at the Braswell Tower.[^1]

None of the sources read describes the site today. They do not say whether the tower still stands, whether the cabin or picnic grounds survive, or whether visitors may go up or drive in. Check with the Mark Twain National Forest before you go.
