The National Historic Lookout Register lists the Puryear Fire Tower in Henry County as an 80-foot Aermotor LS-40, a design the Tennessee Division of Forestry built in large numbers.[^1][^3] The Division administers it, and the register gives no build date.[^1]

The LS-40 has a steel cab seven feet on each side, with an inside stairway that doubles back between two levels.[^3] Forest Service specifications and standard plans drawn from Aermotor windmills shaped its design.[^3] After the Second World War, the Division resumed building LS-40 and MC-39 towers until about 1970.[^3]

Tennessee Landforms lists a tower called Nelson in Henry County, numbered 8-6 and standing.[^2] That number comes from a 1976 list of Division towers, so if Nelson is this tower, it was already listed in 1976. The match is not confirmed: the register gives an elevation of 627 feet, while Landforms gives 623 feet.[^1][^2]

The register records a site visit dated January 28, 2023, and says the state owns the tower, though private property surrounds it.[^1] Landforms adds that many towers have been taken down and that most sites are now private land.[^2] Because the land around it is private, this story does not suggest visiting or climbing the tower.[^1]

We found no published history of its construction, staffing or fires, so those parts of its story remain unknown.
