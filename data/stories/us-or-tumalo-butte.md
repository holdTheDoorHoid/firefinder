The Tumalo Butte lookout started as a camp in the 1930s, about three miles north-northwest of Bend in Deschutes County.[^1][^2] The Oregon Department of Forestry administered the site.[^1] In 1937 the Bend Bulletin reported that the Black Butte protective association and the state forest service were putting up a new lookout station near Bend, and that Earl Updyke had been chosen as its lookout.[^3]

A cabin and a 10-foot pole tower with a 7-by-7 cab were added in 1950.[^1][^2] In 1957 they were replaced by a three-story enclosed tower with a 14-by-14 cab.[^1][^2] A 1956 district report described the new tower as a 20-foot laminated tower with a standard 14-by-14 lookout house, and said almost all of its structural lumber was donated by Brooks-Scanlon Lumber Company.[^3]

The lookout was staffed through the fire season.[^3] In 1940 it was staffed for 150 days, with Fred Bembrey working it for the state fire association.[^3] A September 1957 Bend Bulletin item said John and Olive Allen had been on the lookout seasonally since 1951, and that Mrs. Allen was the state lookout in the new tower.[^3] The station stayed busy into the mid-1960s, and in 1965 it called in nineteen first reports during one season.[^3]

The sources also link the lookout to two fires.[^3] In July 1954 fire fighters from the lookout put out a grass fire of about a quarter acre, about a mile and a half north of Shevlin Park.[^3] On June 25, 1962, the lookout reported what would become the Cinder Butte fire.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1981, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page gives no date and lists the site only as "Removed."[^3]

Firelookout.com says the site is on private property with no public access.[^2] The cited sources do not say what, if anything, remains on the ground.
