The California Division of Forestry put a fire lookout on Shaffer Mountain in 1931.[^3] The peak forms a divide between Honey Lake and Secret Valleys in Lassen County.[^3][^1] A local history blog says the peak is named for the Shaffer brothers, who lived near its base; in 1862 they bought a 1,280-acre property with a station on it.[^2][^3] A climbing guide says the peak's prominence and location led to a lookout on its summit.[^4] In May 1931 a Reno newspaper reported that a state forest ranger from Sacramento had been inspecting the location for the station, and that a six-mile telephone line was planned to link it with the Mapes ranch.[^1]

In September 1940 a newspaper reported that several acres of scrub and grazing land had burned on ranches near the James Mapes property. A staffer posted at the "Schaffer Peak" site spotted the fire, and forest service men arriving by truck soon had it contained.[^1] A June 1946 item says a lookout had been stationed on the mountain.[^1]

A Lassen County history blog says the state ran the lookout until 1949, and Landon Lookout near Eagle Lake then took its place.[^2][^3] A climbing guide dates the state lookout from 1931 to 1947 instead, so the end year is uncertain.[^4] The Forest Lookouts page marks the site as removed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as gone.[^6]

The Peakbagging table of California lookouts gives the summit a "LO site" label, a tag it applies when a lookout building is gone and little or nothing of it may survive.[^5] A climbing guide describes what stands on the summit today: wind-measuring towers and cell and communication facilities leased through the BLM, which manages the land.[^4] A public road reaches close to the top, but a gate about a quarter mile from the summit restricts access.[^4]
