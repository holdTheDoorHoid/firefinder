Sugar Hill is a lookout on a peak just east of Goose Lake, in the North Warner district of the Modoc National Forest in Modoc County.[^1][^2] The Forest Service runs it, and the steel tower still stands.[^1][^5]

A tree-top lookout stood on the summit before the steel tower.[^2] For about a decade, its platform was built into the top of a large pine, and a felled tree leaning against the trunk served as the ladder.[^2] In 1930 the Forest Service built a road to the summit, which had until then been reached by a steep pack trail.[^2] That September work began on a steel tower planned at 30 feet high.[^2] The register dates the tower to 1930 or 1931 and calls its design a rare California Region 5 type.[^1]

By early 1932 a newspaper described the tower as a recent design with an all-glass room, and said a lookout named Keith Wells had been posted there since the previous June.[^2]

Fire touched the mountain more than once.[^2] In July 1929 the Sugar hill fire burned more than 8,000 acres of timber, and a forest lookout named D.M. Miller was dazed by the smoke and wandered for several hours before he met a fire crew.[^2] In July 1940 a fire that started at the Hess Ranch after a burn had not been fully put out raced up the slope and into the old 1929 burn. It was so hot that the lookout had to be taken off the tower.[^2]

Later records describe wear and damage.[^1][^2] A 1944 Forest Service plan listed a standard lookout on a tower, with a garage, woodshed, water storage and a latrine.[^2] A 1953 inspection flagged dry rot in the shutters and catwalk.[^2] In June 2001 vandals pulled off the shutters, broke the windows, and wrecked the furniture and much of the interior paneling.[^2] A 2007 photo caption rated the lookout in fair condition, with the wooden cab needing paint and repair while the steel tower looked good.[^3] The register says the cabin is open to the weather and deteriorating.[^1]

A 2025 report from the Forest Fire Lookout Association says its author met forest staff at the lookout to talk about relocation or restoration.[^4] The project status is listed as ongoing.[^4]

Today the lookout is listed as standing.[^5] The sources read do not say whether the public may go up, or whether the road is open now. A 2007 caption said volunteers might staff it in summer, but that note is nearly twenty years old.[^3] Check with the Modoc National Forest before planning a trip, and do not climb the tower unless the Forest Service says it is open.
