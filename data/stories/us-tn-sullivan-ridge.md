The Sullivan Ridge lookout stood in Davidson County, Tennessee, at about 902 feet (275 m) of elevation.[^1][^4] It was one of the fire towers of the Tennessee Division of Forestry, part of a state network for spotting fires from 1933 to 1975.[^3] Many of those towers went up in the 1930s with help from the Civilian Conservation Corps, and the state's nomination treats them as utilitarian structures put up in the countryside.[^3]

On the state's 1976 list the tower carried the number 6-8, in the district-and-tower format the lookout list uses.[^1][^2]

A 2014 National Register study of the state's lookouts first planned to include the Davidson County tower.[^3] A field survey by Tennessee Valley Archaeological Research found it in ruinous condition, and the final group of ten towers did not include it.[^3] The Tennessee lookout list now marks the tower as removed.[^1]

No source describes what remains at the site today. The lookout list adds that most former tower sites are now in private hands, and no source says who owns this one, so anyone hoping to visit should ask the landowner first.[^2]
