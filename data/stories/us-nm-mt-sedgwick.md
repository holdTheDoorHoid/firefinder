Mount Sedgwick is the highest summit in the Zuni Mountains of Cibola County, New Mexico, and it once held a Forest Service fire lookout.[^1] The register places the lookout in the Cibola National Forest and gives 1918 as the year it was built.[^1]

The register says David and Lucy Whiteside were the first to staff the lookout, in 1918. David served as the lookout and Lucy as the smoke chaser, and the couple ran a ranch at the foot of the mountain.[^1] The register also names Lucy as New Mexico's first woman to work as a fire lookout.[^1]

The Cibola Citizen tells the story with an earlier start. Its account says newspaper reports strongly suggest the couple did informal lookout work on the mountain around 1905 to 1910, although formal records from those years are scarce.[^2] The same article says David was killed near Toltec in March 1919 and that Lucy then stepped into his post.[^2]

A 1920 item quoted by the Forest Lookouts site names a Mrs. D.B. Whiteside as guard and lookout on the mountain that year. That item places the mountain in the Manzano National Forest, while the register names the Cibola National Forest.[^3]

The station stayed in use after that. In June 1931 a Forest Service supervisor was in touch with the lookout station atop the mountain and heard that two of the blazes were under control.[^3] In June 1939 the Forest Service posted emergency lookout guards on the mountain, around the time a fire in the area was believed to have been started by lightning.[^3] In July 1943 a lookout there helped guide a pilot or crew that had lost its way in a heavy rain storm to safety, the Gallup Independent reported.[^3]

The register gives 1975 as the year the lookout was removed.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list marks the site as gone.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
