Baldy Mountain is Manitoba's highest point, at 831 m (2,726 ft) above sea level, and it sits inside Duck Mountain Provincial Park.[^4][^2]

According to the province's park sheet, one of the earliest lookouts on the mountain dates to the early 1900s, when the mountain was a federal forest reserve.[^4] A forestry report from 1915 already records the tower as built, using poles cut nearby, at a cost of $40.[^3] The writer judged it less sturdy than a steel tower, but considered it a good buy given the cost.[^3]

Today a 12 m (39 ft) observation tower stands at the top, and visitors can drive up to it.[^4] BorealBase regards the tower as historic, and it is kept in repair as a place for visitors to look out from.[^2]

The site is 32 km (about 105,000 ft) north of Grandview, on Provincial Road 366.[^1] Walkers set off from a parking area close to the summit.[^2] BorealBase puts the total walking distance at about 2 km (about 6,560 ft), mostly on pavement.[^2] Early spring and late autumn can leave the tower stairs icy, but climbing them opens up a wide view.[^2]
