Midway Peak lies in Kern County, southwest of Fellows.[^1][^2] A May 1939 report said a lookout would go on the summit as a PWA project.[^2] An April 1940 report said the road to the top had been built with WPA labor.[^2] A July 31, 1940 report expected the station to be finished within ten days, and a September 6, 1940 report described it standing on the peak.[^2] The register dates the station to circa 1945, from a Thornton survey, so the sources disagree on when it was built.[^1]

The register says the Kern County Fire Department administered the lookout.[^1] The 1940 reports describe a wooden building with a cement floor, one room downstairs with a garage, and a glass-enclosed second floor holding an Osborne fire finder.[^2] The register instead describes three stories, with an engine shed, living quarters and a glass observation room on top.[^1] The two accounts do not match on the number of floors.[^1][^2]

A two-way radio linked the lookout to its main base at Taft.[^2] A short-wave link to the fire station in Fellows was planned.[^2] The two 1940 reports describe staffing differently. One says a person would be posted at the lookout during the summer months. The other says a licensed radio operator would be on duty about six months a year.[^2]

The register lists the removal date as unknown.[^1] A peakbagging photo page reports that nothing remains but a concrete slab.[^3] The April 1940 report called the one-way road open to the general public, but the July 1940 report said it was not.[^2] No source we read says whether the road is open to visitors today.
