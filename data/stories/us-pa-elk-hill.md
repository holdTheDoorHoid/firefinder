The Pennsylvania Department of Forestry put up a steel fire tower on Elk Hill, in Susquehanna County, in 1921.[^1][^2] A Montrose newspaper item from November 1921, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said the tower stood 60 feet high.[^2] It gave a view wide enough to spot woodland fires early.[^2] The item noted that the tower could not cover the whole county.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register describes it as a steel Aermotor tower, built in 1921 and removed before 1989.[^1] The register dates its entry to September 16, 2008.[^1]

The survey page says the benchmark at the tower site was recovered on October 12, 2008, in a destroyed state. It says several concrete supports and a concrete step still remained.[^3] The page says the concrete slab holding the mark formed the footing of the stairway up the lookout.[^3] The survey page lists the setting as a fire lookout tower.[^3]

The survey mark carries the name Elk Mountain.[^3] The sources we read do not say whether visitors may reach the site.[^3]
