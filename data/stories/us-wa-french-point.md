French Point is a former fire lookout in Snohomish County, about eight miles west of Darrington.[^1][^3] It stood on a low knob on the floor of the Stillaguamish valley, and the tower was a 35-foot wooden pole structure with an L-4 cab.[^2][^3] Most sources date it to 1935, and photographs on two of the history pages are dated July 15 of that year.[^2][^4] The register and Firelookout.com give 1934 instead, so the build year is not settled.[^3][^5]

We did not find a source that names anyone who staffed the lookout as a fire watch. During the Second World War, the Aircraft Warning Service used the site as a station it called "Yoke 5-3".[^2] Forest Lookouts gives the station's dates as July 29, 1942 to July 1, 1943, and says a sleeping quarters measuring 10 by 12 was added using AWS resources.[^4] Afterward the Forest Service kept the site as a fire detection point.[^4] The lookout itself was shut down and left in 1959.[^3][^4]

Accounts differ on how the tower came down. The register says the removal date is unclear.[^3] Discover Darrington says the lookout was knocked down or fell down sometime before 1970, and that its ruins remain on site.[^1] Forest Lookouts lists it simply as removed.[^4]

No source we read describes a standing tower. A WillhiteWeb visit found tower poles with old hardware in the brush, along with concrete footings.[^2] The same account describes a walk of about one mile on decommissioned logging roads, and says the trees have grown enough to block the views, so few are left.[^2]

A gate and timbers block the road about 200 feet up, and the same page says it is decommissioned for now, pending the next timber harvest.[^2] The register places the site in Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest and names Washington State Schools as its administrator.[^3] Gates and road closures can change, so check the current status before you go.
