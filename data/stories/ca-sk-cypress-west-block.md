The Firelookout.ca record places the Cypress Fire Tower in Maple Creek No. 111, Saskatchewan, at 1144 m (3753 ft), and files it in the West Block.[^1] A Saskatchewan tower list puts a Cypress (West Block) tower at 49-36.0800 X 109-55.3000, which does not match the record's own coordinates, 49.631, -109.3159.[^1][^2]

The Saskatchewan side of Cypress Hills Interprovincial Park has two blocks.[^3] Centre Block is the more developed side, with year-round tourist facilities, while West Block is wilderness with rustic camping and contains Fort Walsh National Historic Site.[^3] The provincial park was established in 1931 and extended into Alberta in 1951.[^3] The interprovincial park, created in 1989, brought the Saskatchewan and Alberta parks together.[^3]

A visitor comment on the record says the site was first set up in 1931, the year the provincial park was established.[^1][^3] The comment also describes a steel structure with no copula (a cab, or cupola, on top) and says it was removed in the early 2000s. Its wording does not make clear whether the whole tower or only the cab came down.[^1]

The same comment says the tower stands in the Centre Block, not the West Block, and gives different coordinates.[^1] No official source we found confirms that.

PeakVisor, a peak database, lists a Cypress Hills Fire Lookout in Maple Creek No. 111 at 1,373 m (4,505 ft), against the 1144 m (3753 ft) on the Firelookout.ca record.[^4] The two figures disagree, and the sources do not say whether they describe the same point.

No source we found describes what remains at the tower site today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
