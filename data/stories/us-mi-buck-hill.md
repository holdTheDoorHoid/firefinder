The Buck Hill lookout is in Alger County, Michigan.[^2] Its steel tower is about 100 feet tall and overlooks the Beaver Basin area.[^1] The Register lists the State of Michigan as the administrator.[^1] I did not find when the tower was first built.

In October 1935 the Escanaba Daily Press reported that the two men who had worked the tower for six summers lived in a small, dilapidated shack left over from an old logging camp.[^2] The old building had reached an almost uninhabitable stage, so a new cabin was ordered.[^2] Laborers from the state Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Cusino built it.[^2] Its main room measured 12 by 20 feet and was built of logs, and a cobblestone chimney was added.[^2]

A later state report, covering 1943 and 1944, describes a portable-type cabin, built from salvaged materials, that was set up at the tower.[^2]

The Register also records that David O. Evans worked at the tower in the mid-1950s.[^1]

Today the Register reports that the cabin floor has failed, though the steel remains in good condition.[^1] The tower also carries a Michigan State Police radio repeater.[^1] The Register added the lookout on December 12, 2013.[^1]

I could not find any public visiting information. Because the tower carries a state police radio repeater,[^1] treat it as a working site and ask the owner before going near it.
