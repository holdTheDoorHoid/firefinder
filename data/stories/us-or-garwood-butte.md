Garwood Butte was once called Bear Butte, and a lookout cab went up on its summit in 1942.[^1][^2] The register describes the cab as a newer BC-301 variant of the familiar L-4 design.[^1]

The butte and lookout were later renamed for Leroy E. Garwood.[^2] Firelookout.com and the Oregon Hikers field guide both date the renaming to 1946.[^3][^4] The sources describe his job differently: a former Umpqua National Forest employee, an administrative officer of that forest, or a former Diamond Lake district ranger who died in 1944.[^3][^4][^2]

The register says the lookout stopped being staffed after 1973.[^1] Firelookout.com describes it as abandoned since the 1960s, and the field guide notes it has sat unstaffed for many years.[^3][^4]

The Forest Service says the structure is in poor shape, and in 2012 firefighters wrapped the whole cabin in protective material.[^2] Firelookout.com says work to restore it has continued in recent years.[^3] The field guide says restoration has halted, pointing to an old rehab sign that names a Forest Service employee who has since retired.[^4]

The Forest Service directions run up Three Lakes Road (Road 3703) to Forest Service Road 3703-370, and the Garwood Butte Trailhead is 1.5 miles on the right.[^2] The climb starts gently, then turns steep and rocky, with narrow switchbacks, and the final few feet are hard going underfoot.[^2] Visitors should take care near the cabin.[^2] The register lists the lookout as registered on June 23, 2022.[^1]
