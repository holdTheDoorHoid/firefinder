The Aimwell Fire Tower stands in Marengo County, Alabama, and the Alabama Forestry Commission administers it.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the commission built it in 1951.[^1] It is a 100-foot Aermotor tower, and the Register calls it the second such tower the commission built in the county and the only one left there.[^1]

The Register gives the site elevation as 305 feet (93 m).[^1] Its entry was registered on December 24, 2010, as US 894, AL 52, and Thomas Kaufmann nominated it.[^1]

The sources I read say nothing about who staffed the tower, what fires it watched, or whether it was ever closed or moved.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page for Aimwell lists only the county and the agency.[^2]

Kaufmann's December 2025 column on Alabama's lookouts says the Aermotor MC-39 was the steel tower type found most often in the state.[^3] The Register does not name a model for Aimwell, so the sources do not show whether it is one.[^1] The same column estimates that probably fewer than 75 historic towers still stand in Alabama, and says many are unsafe and illegal to climb.[^3]

Nothing I read says whether the public can see or climb Aimwell.[^1][^2] Anyone planning a visit should ask the Alabama Forestry Commission first, and should not climb the tower without its permission.
