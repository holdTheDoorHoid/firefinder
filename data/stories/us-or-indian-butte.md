Indian Butte, in Lake County, had a 10-foot wooden tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1932 in the Deschutes National Forest.[^1][^2][^3]

Plans came first. In August 1930 the Morning Oregonian reported plans for a standard lookout house on the butte.[^3] In April 1931 a Bend paper said a fireman from Indian Springs would take the butte's lookout post.[^3] Papers of the period also call the summit Indian Spring Butte, which we take to be the same peak.[^3]

In June 1932 a Lake County paper named Avon Derrick as a Forest Service lookout on the butte.[^3] A 1934 Forest Service survey description says the tower, built in 1932, was 10 feet high and supported a wood house.[^3]

In July 1939 the Indian Butte lookout located a third fire within a week on Tom Butte, just south of Newberry Crater. That Tuesday fire touched two acres before it was suppressed.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com both list the tower as gone, and the Oregon list agrees.[^1][^2][^4] The register holds a 2006 photograph of the site, but nothing we read describes what remains there now, and we found no current access information.[^1]
