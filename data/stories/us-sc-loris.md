Forest Lookouts lists Loris as a South Carolina Forestry Commission lookout in Horry County.[^1]

The commission's own history gives general context for its towers rather than detail on any one of them. The first went up in August 1930 near Camden. Many early towers in the state were framed in wood, and eventually every wooden tower was replaced with steel.[^2]

Staff did not routinely watch the towers at night. When danger was moderate or high, operators climbed up after dark to look for glows.[^2] The towers also took fire reports from the public and kept in touch with firefighters in the field.[^2]

The commission shut down its tower system on October 1, 1993.[^2] A Post and Courier article, undated in the copy we read, says the state phased out its towers in the early 1990s and that just 30 are still standing. Some towers elsewhere in the state now carry radio gear.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Loris site today, or whether the public can reach it.
