Horsepasture Mountain, also written Horse Pasture, has a summit at 5,660 feet in the Willamette National Forest, roughly eight miles from McKenzie Bridge to the south, in Lane County.[^1][^3]

The register says the site began in 1915 as a camp, and an August 1915 newspaper item reported that Ralph Allen was stationed there.[^1][^3] In 1920 a newspaper said a house would be built, and that the general plan for such buildings called for a glass cupola on top.[^3] The register dates the cabin to 1922, and a January 1923 item reports that a standard lookout house went up on the peak during the 1922 season.[^1][^3]

Women staffed the post in the late 1910s and early 1920s.[^3] A University of Oregon student, Dorothy Dickey, was lookout in 1921, and a 1922 item said she would return to the post that summer.[^3] In July 1918 a reading from this lookout, combined with a second station's line of sight, helped firefighters pin down a fire's exact location.[^3]

In June 1950 the Forest Service placed a radio on the mountain to help direct spray planes in a spruce budworm project.[^3]

The register says the cabin was taken down in 1965, and the Weebly history agrees.[^1][^3] firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] A photo caption in a Willamette National Forest history collection dates a Horsepasture lookout house to 1927, which does not match the 1922 date given elsewhere.[^4]

Today a short out-and-back hike of 3.0 miles round trip, with about 945 feet of climbing, reaches the summit from a trailhead that is reached by driving Highway 126 east from Eugene, then taking forest roads.[^5] The Oregon Hikers guide says a level patch beside the summit, marked by metal stakes, is where the old lookout stood.[^5] No source describes what else remains at the site now.
