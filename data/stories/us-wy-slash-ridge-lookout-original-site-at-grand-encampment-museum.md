The Slash Ridge lookout went up in 1949 in Carbon County, Wyoming.[^1][^2] Its 50-foot all-steel live-in tower replaced the Barrett Ridge lookout.[^2]

The U.S. Forest Service took the lookout out of service in the 1970s, when it moved to aerial detection and cut back its staffed lookouts.[^1] The register notes that Slash Ridge was declared surplus.[^1] We found no named observers or notable fires connected with the tower.

The lookout was moved to the Grand Encampment Museum in 1986, according to the register.[^1] A later account calls it a military surplus tower that was moved there at the end of its service.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the original site as gone and the relocated Slash Ridge tower as standing, at coordinates that match the museum.[^6]

The register entry, US 2105, WY 7, is dated January 1, 2026, and was nominated by Dixie Boyle.[^1]

Today the tower stands at the museum.[^5][^6] Its summer hours are Tuesday to Saturday, 9 to 5, with Sunday hours from noon to 4. In winter it is open most weekdays from 10 to 4, and the museum asks visitors to call first.[^4] Admission is free, with donations welcome.[^4] A visitor tip on Roadside America names the Slash Ridge Fire Tower as part of the museum complex.[^5] We found no current statement on whether visitors may climb it, so ask the museum before you go up.
