The White Cap Mountain lookout stood in Piscataquis County, Maine.[^1] The summit is on the Appalachian Trail.[^2]

A 12-foot wooden log tower went up there in 1906.[^1][^3] A 20-foot log tower with a portable house followed in 1914.[^3] A 24-foot steel tower was begun in 1920 and finished in 1921.[^3] Maine Memory's record for the site calls the lookout a Maine Forest Service station.[^4] One Maine Memory caption dates a rebuilding of the wood tower to 1940 and its replacement to 1920. Those dates are out of order, so we follow the fuller timeline.[^1][^3]

The Maine Memory caption calls the camp a Forest Service fire watchman's camp.[^1] In 1917 the district chief warden reported that high winds had partly carried off the tower house the season before, and repairs were needed early that season.[^3]

A visit by the Coast and Geodetic Survey in 1941 described a steel tower topped by an enclosed glass lookout.[^3] The tower was deactivated in 1968.[^3] A National Geodetic Survey team noted in 1978 that it was leaning.[^3] It was removed in 1993.[^2][^3]

A 2011 trip report describes a heap of rocks bearing the summit sign as the spot where the tower once stood, and notes that a piece of the tower's base survives in it.[^2] The willhiteweb history reports wood, broken glass and heavy rusted bolts still in the trees and on the scree field below.[^3] The same 2011 report describes a commercial radio site hidden among the summit trees, serving the North Maine Woods, the Penobscot Indian Nation and a few other users.[^2]

The summit can be reached on foot along the Appalachian Trail. A 2011 trip report describes parking outside a gate on the Frenchtown road and walking to the trailhead.[^2]
