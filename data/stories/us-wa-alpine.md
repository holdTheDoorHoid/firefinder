Alpine Lookout sits on a knob at the eastern end of Nason Ridge in Chelan County, Washington, on Forest Service land.[^1][^3][^4] The register dates the site's first use for fire detection to 1920, when an outdoor alidade, a phone and a tent were set up.[^1] Firelookout.com describes that first stage as a camp.[^2]

A 1936 L-4 groundhouse was built on an unusually large stone base.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same year for its L-4 cab.[^2] In 1975 that cab was replaced by a standard R-6 flat-top cab, the one in place now.[^1][^2]

A 2019 management plan for the Nason Ridge Community Forest disagrees about the 1936 building. Citing a 2009 Forest Service source, it says the original 1936 lookout still stands.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both describe that cab as replaced, and I could not settle the question from the pages I read.[^1][^2]

Firelookout.com lists George and Polly Dennis as known lookout personnel in 1928.[^2] A 2025 hiking guide counts it among the few Cascade lookouts that are still staffed.[^4] The register says the cab is still in active use and reachable only along a trail.[^1]

Reaching it takes a hike. The 2025 guide describes a round trip of about 10 miles with about 2,600 feet of climbing, starting from a trailhead reached by FR 6910 off US 2, and says a Northwest Forest Pass or an interagency pass is required.[^4] The sources I read do not say whether visitors may go inside the cab, so do not count on it.

Firelookout.com says the cab can be used in emergencies and that its roof has been altered since.[^2]
