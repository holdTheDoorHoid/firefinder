Hawshaw Mountain in Avery County got its fire lookout in 1934. The Civilian Conservation Corps built a tower 45 feet and 9 inches tall and a one-room cabin.[^1] The Forest Lookouts list that carries the entry cites a North Carolina state biennial report from 1940 for these details.[^1]

A 2008 Toe River blog post, summarizing Peter Barr's guidebook, says that over time, North Carolina lookouts moved from bare mountaintops to towers that housed the people watching for fires.[^2] A North Carolina tower list places Hawshaw at about 36.045 degrees north and 81.94 degrees west, in Avery County.[^3]

The same blog post says the Hawshaw tower was still standing but not publicly accessible.[^2] That is the only fate statement in these sources, and it dates from 2008.

Today, an FCC antenna-structure record for an address on Hawshaw Fire Tower Road lists a 180-foot lattice tower, registered to the North Carolina State Highway Patrol.[^4] Its coordinates nearly match the tower list's point for the mountain.[^3][^4] The record does not mention the 1934 lookout, so the sources do not show whether the two are the same structure. No source confirms that the public can reach the site now.[^2][^4]
