The Norway tower stood in the western section of today's Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness, close to Ely.[^1] The author's notes mention a 1936 survey monument and describe the Norway lookout as a 100-foot steel tower.[^1] A 1920 Superior National Forest map leaves the tower off, though it does show a trail that roughly follows today's route from Nigh Ranger Station toward Little Trout Lake.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Minnesota list records the Norway tower as gone.[^2] The author writes that sometime in the late 1960s or 1970s the Forest Service brought down the Sioux River tower and its two sisters.[^1] Since the article names only three towers, Norway and Angleworm are the likely two sisters.[^1] The author ties these removals to 1964 legislation requiring that man-made structures be taken out of the wilderness.[^1]

The author says most of the old tower is still on site at these lookouts.[^1] Forest growth has taken back the spurs at the Norway and Sioux River sites.[^1] The list says the tower is gone, while the author describes most of the old structure as still there.[^2][^1]

The author visited the three towers in the summer of 2009.[^1] The route is a seven-mile drive south along a lightly maintained forest road off the Echo Trail, then about a mile on foot into the wilderness.[^1] Locating the old sites means bushwhacking with GPS guidance.[^1] The author describes the spot as where the Norway tower used to be, and no tower is left standing.[^1][^2]
