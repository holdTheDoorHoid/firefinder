A small stone lookout house went up in 1912 on the summit above Hamburg in the Klamath National Forest, though a lookout had already been posted there in 1911.[^1][^2] An August 1911 newspaper report had planned a permanent stone building about 12 by 14 feet, but a June 1912 report says the house was roofed with corrugated iron because it was impossible to secure lumber there.[^2] The register gives the original stone groundhouse as 15 by 15 feet, so the early size is not settled.[^1]

The first lookout named in the clippings is James Clyman. In September 1912 he spotted a fire on Horse Creek that burned more than 600 acres and was judged to have been set.[^2] Lightning struck the lookout house in August 1913 and cut the telephone line.[^2] Will Hicks held the post in the mid-1910s.[^2]

In 1933 a new cab went on top of the old stone house. The register says it was a 14-by-14-foot R-1 wooden cab with a hip roof and a catwalk, and that a Civilian Conservation Corps crew built both the cab and the Lake Mountain Trail to the summit.[^1] A Blue Lake Advocate item from November 1933 says the new lookout house was completed with CCC labor.[^2] The register also describes the cab as sitting atop the 1912 house, which became its first floor.[^1]

The register names Nancy Hood as a long-time lookout.[^1] In 2014, the Happy Camp Complex fire threatened the site, and the lookout was wrapped in flame-retardant foil. That September, Hood lost her home in the fire.[^2]

In September 2025, during the Blue Fire, crews were unwrapping the protected lookout, which the release calls a piece of California history.[^3] A Klamath National Forest release from that month says the tower has operated continuously since the early 1900s and still plays a vital role in fire detection across the Klamath Range.[^3]

A 2012 centennial event at the site limited parking to 16 vehicles, made carpooling a must, and offered a shuttle two miles from the lookout.[^4] The sources do not say whether the public may visit at other times, so anyone planning a trip should contact the Klamath National Forest first.
