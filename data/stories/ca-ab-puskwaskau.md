Puskwaskau is one of Alberta's wildfire lookouts.[^3] The province counts 127 such sites, placed where the view is good enough to help spot and report wildfires.[^1]

The archive says the Puskwaskau tower was completed and began operation in the mid-1940s.[^2] It quotes an annual report from the Department of Lands and Mines, which counted the tower as the twelfth lookout erected in the Northern Alberta Forest District.[^2] The report also says the tower had a thirty-watt radio.[^2]

The archive says lookout towers such as Puskwaskau are central to detecting wildfires.[^2] It adds that observers are responsible for reporting wildfire activity within a 40 km radius of their tower, an area the archive puts at 5,027 square kilometres.[^2]

The Foster family fonds, held by the archive, includes a film with footage taken in 1957 and 1964.[^2] The archive picks out the climbing of the Puskwaskau tower as one of the film's more unusual activities.[^2]

The provincial record lists a steel tower at Puskwaskau, built in 2015 and 30 m (98 ft) tall.[^3] The record gives the site an elevation of 916 m (3,005 ft) and marks the tower as active.[^3] The record does not say whether this tower replaced the mid-1940s one.[^3]

The record gives the access mode as drive.[^3] No source we read says the site is open to visitors, and no source describes the site today beyond that record. The archive says the public is no longer permitted to climb Alberta's lookout towers.[^2]
