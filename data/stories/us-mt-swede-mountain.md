Swede Mountain Lookout sits a few miles east of Libby, in the Kootenai National Forest.[^3]

The first lookout on the mountain was a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1934 overlooking the town of Libby.[^1][^2] In 1964 it was replaced by the present 53-foot treated-timber tower, which has an R-6 flat cab.[^1][^2] The Western News gives the current tower's height as 52 feet, so the sources differ by a foot.[^3]

The register says the tower is staffed every summer.[^1] A 2020 Western News article named it among the Kootenai lookouts still staffed, along with Marston and Black Butte.[^3]

A new roof went on in 2019.[^4] The Western News reported that a Kalispell roofing company worked with association volunteers, that the U.S. Forest Service covered the cost of the roof repairs, and that the work was completed in early October.[^3][^4] More stabilizing of the roof followed in 2020.[^4]

In 2021 volunteers glazed 52 windows and replaced catwalk posts, railing and landing posts.[^4][^5] The lookout was then scraped, sanded and painted.[^4][^5] It was registered on the National Historic Lookout Register on January 9, 2021.[^1]

The association's 2021 newsletter calls it a drive-to lookout above Libby.[^5] The 2020 article says it can be reached by vehicle in season, and that visitors can see the Libby Valley, the Kootenai River Valley and the eastern edge of the Cabinet Mountains Wilderness.[^3]
