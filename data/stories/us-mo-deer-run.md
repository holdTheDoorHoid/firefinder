Deer Run sits in what was once Deer Run State Park, about three miles southwest of Ellington in Reynolds County.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the tower to 1926 and describes it as likely the earliest permanent lookout in Missouri.[^1] The earliest press mention we found is a June 1930 newspaper item describing a fire tower already in place to safeguard wildlife and young pine growth.[^2] The register gives 1926 without a source, and the newspaper item does not name a build date, so we cannot confirm the year.[^1][^2]

According to the register, the structure is 85 feet tall and built of steel, with a cab measuring seven feet square.[^1] A state report covering 1939 to 1941 gives the same 85-foot height.[^2] A 1962 federal survey note, by contrast, gives a height of roughly 100 feet, so the sources disagree on height as well.[^2]

A towerman was working here by 1951.[^2] A newspaper item from that year names Clarence Pratt as the lookout on duty at Deer Run.[^2] State reports say construction in 1942 included a dwelling, a garage with woodshed, a latrine and a cistern, and that in fiscal 1956 to 1957 another bedroom was added to the tower residence.[^2] We found no record of when staffing ended.

The site has carried several designations over time, including state park, refuge, state forest and conservation area.[^3] The Missouri Department of Conservation formed the Current River Conservation Area in 1993 by joining four smaller properties, and the tower now sits within it.[^3][^4]

The tower still stands. The department's list of places of interest for the area names the Deer Run Fire Tower, and the Current River elk driving tour goes past it.[^4][^5] We found no closure notice, but the pages we read do not say whether visitors may climb the cab, so ask the department before going up.
