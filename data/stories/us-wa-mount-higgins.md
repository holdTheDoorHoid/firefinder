The lookout stood on the western sub-summit of Mount Higgins, in Skagit County, roughly midway between Darrington and Oso.[^6]

A camp was set up on Mount Higgins in 1918.[^1][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com say a cupola cabin replaced the camp in 1922.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page quotes a September 1923 notice that a new lookout house had just been completed, and WillhiteWeb says a contract to build a cab was submitted in April 1920 and finished in September 1923.[^3][^4] The sources differ by a year, so both dates stand here.

A 1950 USGS party recorded it as a 12-foot-square wooden structure, painted white and topped by a 6-foot-square cupola.[^4]

The Forest Service says the lookout was used for fire detection in the summers from 1926 to 1949.[^5] In September 1933 the lookout flagged a fire on the edge of an old slash burn. Thirty-five CCC men left the Darrington Ranger Station in two trucks, and the lookout later reported the fire apparently out.[^3][^4] In the late 1930s Bob Craig was on duty there, according to WillhiteWeb.[^4]

The sources do not agree on when use ended. In August 1951 the lookout spotted a fire and reported it, according to an Arlington Times item quoted on two of these sites.[^3][^4] Forest Lookouts says the lookout was abandoned in 1949, while WillhiteWeb says it was abandoned at some point in the 1950s.[^3][^4]

Snow brought the cabin down around 1965. Firetower.org says snow destroyed it that year, and WillhiteWeb says it collapsed under heavy snow in 1965 or thereabouts.[^1][^4]

Ruins remain on the mountain, according to WillhiteWeb.[^4] The Forest Service calls the summit the site of the former lookout, reached by Trail 640, and says the first mile of that trail climbs an old road.[^5] The Forest Service directions start at the Darrington Ranger District office: take Highway 530 west for 11.8 miles.[^5] Older directions on WillhiteWeb describe a gated road that is a walk or bike of about 3 miles, so check current conditions before you go.[^4]
