Sources disagree on when Little Mount Hoffman Lookout was built. The National Historic Lookout Register gives a construction date of 1940, describing the structure as an R-5/R-1 groundhouse with a catwalk.[^1] Recreation.gov, by contrast, says it was constructed in the 1920s.[^2] Neither page explains the discrepancy, and this story can't resolve it either — only flag that the two main sources don't agree.

Whatever its exact birth year, the lookout sits at about 7,260 feet in the Shasta-Trinity National Forest, Siskiyou County, California, with views toward Mount Shasta.[^1] It served the Forest Service in regular fire-detection duty until 1978.[^1][^2] Even after regular staffing ended, the Forest Service kept it in reserve, and Recreation.gov notes it's still used during times of extreme fire danger.[^2]

The building was restored in 1994 and entered the agency's cabin rental program the following year, in 1995.[^1] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on March 2, 1998.[^1]

Little Mount Hoffman remains one of the few historic lookouts still standing in the Shasta-Trinity National Forest, and the Forest Service considers it eligible for listing on the National Register of Historic Places.[^2] It's open to the public today as an overnight rental, reached by car except for a rough final four miles of unpaved road that can be hard on RVs and trailers.[^2] Renters need to pick up the combination for the site's lock from the McCloud Ranger District office in advance, and reservations are required — there are no walk-ins.[^2] The mountaintop itself stays open to the public even when the lookout building is occupied by renters.[^2]
