Carnasaw Lookout sits in McCurtain County, in far southeastern Oklahoma's Ouachita Mountains country. Oklahoma Forestry Services put up a wood tower here in 1926, the first fire lookout tower built in the state, though no source describes exactly what that first structure looked like.[^1][^2] In 1937 crews replaced it with a taller, 100-foot wood tower.[^1][^2]

That second tower came down in a storm in 1974.[^1] The agency's replacement was meant to be another 100-foot structure — this time steel, an Aermotor design with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab. The build ran into trouble partway up: a measuring mistake in the footings meant the tower couldn't safely continue past 75 feet. Rather than start over, the crew stopped there, framed a deck at that height, and set the cab on it, which is why Carnasaw's silhouette looks different from a standard Aermotor today.[^1][^2]

Carnasaw was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on September 16, 1997, as US 238 / OK 1, nominated by Patrick McDowell of Oklahoma Forestry Services.[^1] The register's page — last updated in 2016 — describes it as still in active service, though that status has not been independently checked since.[^1]

Beyond the register entries, little has surfaced here about who staffed the tower over the years, what fires it is credited with spotting, or whether the public can visit today — both cached sources are short lookout-register summaries rather than fuller histories, so those parts of Carnasaw's story remain unknown.[^1][^2]
