East Tiger Mountain, the highest point in Tiger Mountain State Forest in King County, carried a fire lookout tower from 1945 until 1967.[^1][^2][^3]

The Washington Division of Forestry/DNR is the administrator the register lists.[^1] The tower was 83 feet tall, a treated timber structure with a 14-by-14 L-4 cab.[^1][^3] The register and FirelookOut both give the 83-foot height, and a 1945 state forestry report quoted on WashingtonLookouts describes the same tower.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Arlene Brandal is named as a lookout here in 1961.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1967. FirelookOut and Willhite say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3]

Willhite's undated page says communication towers dot the summit and that it is no longer much of a hiking destination.[^3] He adds that the summit sits on a loop trip popular with mountain bikers.[^3] No source describes any remains of the lookout itself.

Access is paved.[^3] Willhite's directions run from Highway 18 to Tiger Summit, and he lists the hike as 4 miles with 1,600 feet of gain.[^3] Bikers, and anyone without a Discover Pass, should start at the lot beside the highway.[^3] Hikers can use a lot two minutes up the left road.[^3]
