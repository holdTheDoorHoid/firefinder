Notch Butte Lookout is a small ground cab on a butte in Lincoln County, Idaho.[^1][^3] A 2024 trip report puts it about four miles south of Shoshone, while firelookout.com says two miles.[^4][^3] The Bureau of Land Management manages the site.[^1] The register says it was built in 1941 as a Forest Service Region 1 plan cabin, and firelookout.com describes it as a 14-by-14-foot ground cab.[^1][^3] One Idaho lookout site also lists an earlier cabin on the butte in the 1930s, which the register does not mention.[^2][^1]

Staff appear in newspaper reports reprinted on that same Idaho site. In August 1944, Julius Imgard, the lookout, was the first to spot a range fire along the railroad east of Besslen, which burned more than 100 acres.[^2] His daughter Doris covered the morning hours.[^2] A fire in July 1945 grew past 2,000 acres, forced one man to leave the grazier's station and lookout tower, and disrupted phone and power service nearby.[^2] A personnel list runs from 1945 to the mid-1980s, ending with Paul Roberts in 1983 to 1985.[^3]

The register says the site was used into the 2000s and then converted to a communications facility.[^1] Firelookout.com calls the cab an enclosed radio building.[^3] A climbing guide dates the change to the early 2000s but describes the result as a radio tower.[^5]

The same trip report found the road unsigned and says an ordinary car can reach the summit.[^4] A climbing guide describes it as a short hike or mountain-bike ride from the highway.[^5] No source says the former cab is open to visitors.[^1][^3] It is now enclosed radio equipment, so leave the building alone and check with the Bureau of Land Management before visiting.
