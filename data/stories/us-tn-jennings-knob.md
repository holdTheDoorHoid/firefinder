The Jennings Knob tower stands in Wilson County, Tennessee, at an elevation of about 1,221 feet.[^1] The National Register form on the state's Division of Forestry towers lists a Jennings Knob entry in Wilson County, but it gives no further detail.[^2]

We could not find a construction date in the sources we checked. The clearest clue is a 1965 recovery note from the Coast and Geodetic Survey.[^3] It says that construction of a forest lookout tower on the site meant the survey station had to be lowered so that grading could go ahead, and that the mark was lowered about 1.7 feet into solid rock.[^3] The tower was therefore going up around 1965, though the note does not say when it was finished.[^3] We found no staffing history or named fire for it.

Tennessee Landforms lists the tower as standing.[^1] The same page notes that many towers have been taken down and that most sites are privately owned.[^1] It also warns against climbing, since the steps are frequently missing or decayed.[^1] We found no public visiting arrangement, so please do not climb it. Ask the landowner before going near the site.
