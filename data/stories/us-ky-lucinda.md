The Lucinda lookout stood on Lucinda Knob in Leslie County, Kentucky, at about 1,853 feet.[^1][^2] In June 1927, W.E. Jackson, the assistant state forester, said work on the station was moving ahead quickly.[^2] The original plan called for a steel tower like those at other stations, but hauling materials to this site was too expensive, so wood was chosen instead.[^2] The 1927 report puts the work in the Red Bird section of Clay County, while the 1929 report and the Register say Leslie County.[^1][^2]

A February 1929 report describes a single wooden tower, 50 feet tall, on Mt. Lucinda in Leslie County.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts page says a 100-foot steel tower was removed, but it gives no date.[^2] A 1950 survey description on the same page describes a steel tower roughly 98 feet tall, supported on four legs and crowned by an enclosed observation deck.[^2] Those accounts do not match the 50-foot wooden tower of 1929, and no source I read explains when, or whether, the wooden tower was replaced by steel.[^2]

Today the Register says a state-owned radio tower stands on the former site, and that the fire tower's concrete footings are still on the ground.[^1] A March 2006 visit report on the same page describes a spring housed in cut stone, built in the shape of a house and set just below the hill.[^1] I found no source that says whether the public can reach the site now.[^1][^2]

[^2]: Lucinda Knob, Forest Lookouts (easternuslookouts.weebly.com), https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/lucinda-knob.html (accessed 2026-10-10).
