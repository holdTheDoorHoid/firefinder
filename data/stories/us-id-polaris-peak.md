Polaris Peak is about five miles southeast of Kellogg, in Shoshone County, Idaho. It stands on the Coeur d'Alene National Forest at 5,585 feet.[^1][^2]

In 1931 a 30-foot pole tower topped by an L-4 cab went up on the peak.[^1][^3]

Firelookout.com lists watchers from 1938 to 1967. Many were married couples, such as Mr. and Mrs. Robert Stevens in 1948.[^1] The Idaho Fire Lookouts list starts earlier, with Fred Kottkey in 1934 and Walter Johnson in 1935, and runs on to George Howard in 1971.[^2] That 1971 date falls three years after the 1968 destruction date that all three pages give, so the Idaho list does not fit that timeline.[^1][^2][^3]

A September 4, 1936 Spokane Chronicle item, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, said Forest Service officials had recalled the lookout because wet, cool weather had lowered the fire danger.[^3]

The lookout was destroyed in 1968.[^1][^2][^3]

Writers for Idaho Fire Lookouts report that they could drive to the site, where two footings and one tie-down remain.[^2] Both footings sit below the summit and align with older photographs of the earlier L-4 tower.[^2] They describe the rest of the site as clear.[^2]

The site is classed as drive-in.[^2] No source says whether the road or the site is open to visitors now. The peak lies in a national forest, so check with the Coeur d'Alene National Forest before going.[^1][^2]
