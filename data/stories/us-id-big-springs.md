Big Springs Lookout stands near the Big Springs Ranger Station, about 15 miles southwest of West Yellowstone in Fremont County, Idaho.[^1][^2][^4] The U.S. Forest Service administers it on the Targhee National Forest.[^2][^3]

The tower went up in 1936, reportedly built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1][^3][^4] It is a steel tower topped with a small cab about seven feet square, made by the Aermotor Company of Chicago, a firm widely known for its windmills.[^1] Island Park Historical Society gives the tower a height of 72 feet, while Idaho Fire Lookouts and firelookout.com both say 100 feet.[^1][^3][^4] Its main job was fire protection, and it was placed to cover two timber sales, the Crow Creek and Coffee Pot Timber Sales.[^1] The site also had a one-room log cabin with a wood stove, a single-car garage and a latrine.[^1]

A 1949 visit by a group of Forest Service women is remembered in one account. Visitors hiked a mile from the bus, and several climbed the 70-foot tower to look for smoke.[^1] By 1963 it was one of only three lookout towers on the Targhee National Forest.[^1] Island Park Historical Society says the lookout was no longer used by 1981, when aircraft patrols had taken over its work.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts dates the abandonment to 1985, and says no staffing history is documented.[^3]

In 1989 the Forest removed the cabin, garage and latrine, and only the tower remains today.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register registered it on January 3, 2015, as US 1061.[^2][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as a hike-in, and the register says no directions to the tower are signed.[^2][^3] No source read says whether the tower may be climbed, so check with the Targhee National Forest before planning any visit.[^2][^3]
