The original Kellogg Peak lookout was a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, about three miles south of Kellogg in Shoshone County, Idaho.[^1][^2] The Forest Service developed it in 1934, according to the firelookout.com page, but the Silver Mountain trail page calls the original a 1935 lookout, so the sources disagree on the year.[^1][^3]

The earliest name listed is George Howard, for 1934 and 1935.[^2] Les Williamson is listed from 1942 through 1945, and Mike Dunn for 1975.[^2]

The original tower was destroyed about 1950.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts and willhiteweb.com both give 1950 as the year.[^2][^5] In 1997, Silver Mountain Ski Resort built the current L-4 cab as a replica that is accessible to people with disabilities and draws visitors.[^1][^4]

The builders used the original four concrete footings poured in 1934.[^5] A retired Forest Service forester, Phil Ruff, pushed for the replica, and a Forest Service archaeologist found 1929 plans to work from.[^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts reports that staffing was sporadic at first, that it has gone unstaffed in recent years, and that it seems to need repairs.[^2] willhiteweb.com is less certain and suggests volunteers may have staffed it for educational purposes.[^5]

Visitors reach the replica by the Silver Mountain gondola.[^5] The intermediate trail gains 870 feet in 1.85 miles one way, can be reached only from the gondola, and cars may not drive to its trailhead.[^3] The Lookout Structures list shows it as a standing ground replica.[^6]
