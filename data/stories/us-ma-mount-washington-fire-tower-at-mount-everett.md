The Mount Everett fire tower stood on the summit of Mount Everett in the Mount Everett State Reservation, in Berkshire County, Massachusetts.[^1][^3] The first tower on the site was a 38-foot structure with a 10-by-10-foot cab, which the National Historic Lookout Register dates to 1915.[^1][^4] A Forest Lookouts page instead says a fire tower was erected in 1918, so the first date is disputed.[^3]

A daily observer was on duty at the tower in 1947.[^4] During a fire emergency that April, a newspaper report said Ezra Whitbeck was on duty there 12 to 14 hours a day.[^3] That year a stone lookout building went up near the tower.[^4]

The tower was rebuilt in 1945 to replace the earlier structure.[^1][^3] In 1970 a new tower went up about 100 feet north of the two earlier towers, which stood where the Appalachian Trail crosses the summit.[^3][^4] The register gives the new tower's height as 40 feet, while Willhite says it may have been 60 feet tall.[^1][^4] The register says the tower was staffed until 1972, and Willhite agrees that 1972 was the last year of staffing.[^1][^4]

In 1999 the Massachusetts Department of Environmental Management wanted to put telecommunications equipment on the tower.[^4] Local residents pushed back, and Willhite reports that the town won.[^4]

In spring 2003 the tower was dismantled and airlifted off the summit by helicopter.[^3][^4] The register gives the reason as disputes with town officials, and says the tower was sold to a private landowner in the same town.[^1] Forest Lookouts reports that the tower was said to be out of use, and that Appalachian Trail foot traffic had cut many paths through the vegetation, doing significant damage.[^3]

The lookout appears on the National Historic Lookout Register as US 431, MA 2, registered August 28, 2002, and on the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register as US 49, MA 49, registered September 3, 2008.[^1][^2]

No tower stands on the summit today.[^1][^3] The summit is reached on foot, and Willhite lists its access as "Trail over the top."[^4]
