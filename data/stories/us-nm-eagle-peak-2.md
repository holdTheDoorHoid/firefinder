Eagle Peak Lookout stands on the summit of Eagle Peak in the Gila National Forest, Catron County, New Mexico, about 18 miles east of Reserve.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register gives its elevation as 9,801 feet (2,987 m).[^1]

A tower went up on the peak in 1929.[^2][^3] The current lookout replaced it in 1955.[^1][^3] It is a Forest Service Standard Plan CL-100 series design, with a 14-by-14-foot (4.3 by 4.3 m) steel cab on a 10-foot (3 m) base.[^1][^2] The sources disagree about that base. The register calls it a concrete blockhouse.[^1] A Forest Lookouts page that quotes a Forest Service history describes a 10-foot (3 m) cinder block base that doubles as storage.[^2]

In April 1967 a Silver City Daily Press item reported a lookout stationed at the tower, with a second person about to join him.[^2] In 1988 the lookout was evaluated for the National Register and found not eligible because of its age.[^5] A 2020 New Mexico chapter report says the tower is staffed by seasonal Forest Service employees and should be re-evaluated.[^5]

The register listed the lookout on May 22, 2022, on a nomination by Mark Gutzman.[^1]

Reaching it means taking Wilson Canyon Trail #768 to Forest Road 38, the Eagle Peak Road.[^4] The Forest Service puts the trail and road together at 1.5 miles one way.[^4] A road guide says the road to the summit is unpaved and has two tight switchbacks near its end.[^6]

Wikipedia calls it an active Forest Service lookout, and the sources do not say whether the cab is open to visitors.[^3] Check with the Reserve Ranger District before going.
