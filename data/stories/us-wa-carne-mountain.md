Carne Mountain's lookout stood in the Wenatchee National Forest in Chelan County, Washington, about 18 miles north of Lake Wenatchee.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^2]

The first structure was a log cabin, built about 1915.[^2] Willhite says a lookout started watching from the mountain around that time. He suggests the cabin probably stood in the meadows below, with the lookout working from the point by day.[^4] His photos include one captioned 1944.[^4] The register says an L-5 cab replaced the log cabin in the 1930s.[^2] Willhite says no structures stood on the summit when panoramic photographs were taken on August 20, 1934, and that the cab went up soon after.[^3][^4]

The sources differ on the exact spot.[^2][^4] Willhite says the observation point was point 6,991, just south of the summit, and that a 1949 USGS map labels the lookout at the 7,085-foot summit instead.[^4] The register gives the site's elevation as 6,991 feet.[^2]

Three sources place the end of the cab in the 1960s, but they describe it differently.[^1][^2][^4] The register says it was removed.[^2] Firelookout and Willhite say it was destroyed.[^1][^4] The Forest Lookouts listing marks it simply as removed.[^3]

Willhite says remnants such as rusty nails, glass and a foundation can be found on the lower summit.[^4] The hike to the high peak starts at the Phelps Creek trailhead.[^4] He gives the hike as 4 miles with a 3,600-foot climb, and describes the access road as rough gravel but accessible by all.[^4] The sources do not describe a closure, but they do not give current conditions, so check before you go.[^4]
