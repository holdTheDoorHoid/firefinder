Miller Peak is a 7,018-foot summit in the Lolo National Forest, roughly nine to ten miles southeast of Missoula.[^1][^2] A 1947 newspaper report says the peak had been in the Bitter Root forest when the lookout was built, and was by then part of the Lolo.[^2]

Work on a new lookout began in September 1930, when two men were sent up to begin building it. The plan called for a 20-foot tower and 14-by-14-foot living quarters.[^2] A May 1931 report said the building was finished, and the 1947 report gives 1931 as the build year.[^2] The firelookout.com entry instead dates a log cabin on the site to 1929, and the L-4 tower on a 20-foot pole to about 1932.[^1] The sources differ by about a year, and the May 1931 newspaper report is the closest contemporary record.[^1][^2]

The lookout was staffed in 1934. C. B. Pitts was posted there in May, and a July notice had Stewart Griffiths at it, placing the lookout southwest of Missoula rather than southeast.[^1][^2] Lewis Phillips, a forest school student, was employed as a lookout in 1937.[^2]

In July 1937 a fire that swept up from Miller Creek reached the peak, and two men died in it.[^2][^3] Phillips kept watch from the tower while his cabin burned.[^2] A 1937 newspaper account said the wooden tower was still holding out against the fire, and that it rose from a heap of rocks next to the cabin.[^2] A 1947 report instead says the fire halted 50 feet short of the structure and tower, which were nearly burned.[^2] A separate Old Missoula account says the tower survived by an odd stroke of luck.[^3]

In September 1947 a newspaper reported that the lookout was being taken apart after 16 years in service.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the tower Gone.[^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the public can reach it.
