The Allison Fire Tower, also called the Rudder Hill Tower, stands in Choctaw County, Alabama, and the Alabama Forestry Commission administers it.[^1][^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 100-foot International Derrick tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] It says the Allison Lumber Company built the tower in 1953 to take the place of an older wooden tower of the same name.[^1] The Register's build field gives 1954 instead, so its own two dates differ by a year, and I have not been able to settle which one is right.[^1]

The University of West Alabama holds a collection of Allison Lumber Company papers.[^4] Its archive listing includes an August 1926 letter from Mr. Allison to Major Bunker about a lookout tower, but it does not say which tower.[^4] I have not linked that letter to Rudder Hill.

The Register lists the tower as registered on January 18, 2010, as US 866, AL 44, with Thomas Kaufmann as nominator.[^1] Jackson's Ramblings repeats the Register's description but gives the state number as AL 45.[^3] I have used the Register's number.[^1]

The sources I read say nothing about who staffed the tower or what fires it watched.[^1][^2]

The Register links a drone video from Jackson's Ramblings, but it gives no date for the footage, and none of the sources I read describe the tower's condition now.[^1] Neither the Register nor the Forest Lookouts page says whether the public can visit or climb it.[^1][^2] Check with the Alabama Forestry Commission before going, and do not climb the tower without its permission.
