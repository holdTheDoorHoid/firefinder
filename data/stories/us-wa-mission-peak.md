The Mission Peak lookout was built in 1934, or possibly the year before, on a peak near Wenatchee.[^1][^2][^4] Three histories describe a 10-foot pole L-4 tower.[^1][^2][^4] A 1934 Wenatchee Daily World story, reprinted on one history site, describes a lookout living in a 15-foot tower.[^3] We could not settle which height is right.

The earliest name we found is Joe Guilberson, listed as Mission Peak's lookout-fireman in June 1933.[^3] The firelookout.com list spells his name Guiberson.[^2] Newspapers from July and August 1934 name Oscar Kuntz as the lookout.[^3] A photo caption on a climbing page names Hank Kuch as the lookout person, and we could not tell whether that is the same man.[^4]

In July 1934 the Mission Peak and Three Brothers lookouts reported a small smoke near Schaser Creek. A CCC crew went out, and investigators concluded the smoke was sheep dust.[^3] An August 1934 story, told by a forest fire assistant, describes a bear at the lookout's ladder one night. The lookout flashed a light down the ladder, and the bear fled.[^3]

No source gives a firm removal date. Firetower.org says the tower was taken down at an unstated time, possibly soon after 1945.[^1] Firelookout.com says much the same and lists the site as 1934-?.[^2] WillhiteWeb says it was removed, possibly around 1945, when the state Division of Forestry developed a lookout on nearby Naneum Ridge.[^4]

A 2019 Washington Trails Association trip report says the lookout is long gone.[^5] The old lookout road, now a trail, climbs about two miles and 1,000 feet to the summit.[^5] From the rocky summit the writer describes views all around, and the report does not describe any remains of the lookout.[^5]

The trip report starts from Liberty-Beehive Road 9712, roughly a mile past the Squilchuck State Park junction on the road to Mission Ridge.[^5]
