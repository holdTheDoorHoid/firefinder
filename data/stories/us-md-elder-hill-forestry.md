Elder Hill is a lookout site in Garrett County, Maryland, at about 859 metres (2,818 feet).[^1] The State of Maryland acquired the site in January 1934, and the steel tower on it was built in the late 1930s.[^1] The register describes the tower as 110 feet tall, and the Maryland Forest Service administers it.[^1]

Maryland's forestry towers were part of a wider effort to spot fires early.[^2] A draft state report puts the problem plainly: "getting early warning of forest fires was a very serious challenge."[^2] I found no published record of who staffed the cab here or how long it was watched, so I cannot say how the tower worked as a lookout in its early years.

As of 2007 the tower carried communications equipment for two agencies: the Maryland Forest Service and Garrett County EMS.[^1] The same draft report, which includes photographs from 2006, says that at least some of the deeds for several tower sites, Elder Hill among them, include clauses that return the land to the heirs or assigns of the original owners if forestry use ends.[^2] It adds that maintaining DNR communications equipment on the towers, or at their sites, satisfies that requirement.[^2]

The state owns the tower site, but the access road is private.[^1] The register says there is no lawful way in, though the tower can be seen from Maryland Route 42.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] Visitors should not try to reach or climb it.

The register added the tower on June 23, 2018, as US 1282, MD 9.[^1]
