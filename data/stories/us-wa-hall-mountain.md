Hall Mountain stands at 6,222 feet in Pend Oreille County, Washington, on Colville National Forest land east of Sullivan Lake.[^1][^3][^4]

The register and Firelookout.com date the lookout to 1930, when a 10-foot tower carrying an L-4 cab was built.[^1][^2][^4] A June 1931 Spokane Daily Chronicle item, quoted on Rex's Fire Lookout Page, says a bolt of lightning struck a Kaniksu National Forest carpenter while he was building a lookout station on the mountain.[^3] The 1930 date and the 1931 report do not line up neatly, and the sources do not reconcile them.[^1][^2][^3]

WillhiteWeb notes that in 1933 the USGS described its summit benchmark as sitting next to a new Forest Service lookout tower. It does not say whether that was the 1930 tower.[^4]

WillhiteWeb says the lookout stopped being used in the 1950s, and that the reason is not yet clear.[^4] The register says the cab was removed in the 1950s, while Firelookout.com says it was destroyed then.[^1][^2] By 1960 the tower had fallen down, and in 1964 surveyors reported that the house had been torn down and that ruins of an older tower were there.[^4]

The register says remains can still be found on the summit.[^1] Firelookout.com repeats that.[^2] No source describes those remains in detail.[^1][^2][^3][^4] None says whether the summit is open to visitors today.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register added the site in December 2012.[^1]
