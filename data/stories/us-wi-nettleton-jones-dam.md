The Jones Dam fire tower stood in Forest County, Wisconsin, about 15 miles north of Argonne, in the Chequamegon-Nicolet National Forest.[^1][^2] A Rhinelander Daily News report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, called it the most recently built and most up-to-date of the 16 lookout towers on the Nicolet, and gave its height as 120 feet.[^1]

The tower is best known for a 1937 shooting. In late November that year, Royal Nettleton, a junior forester on the Nicolet, left a camp near the town of Long Lake to start work on a fire detection map. He climbed a tree to get his bearings from the tower, and two rifle shots rang out.[^3][^4]

The man who fired was tried for manslaughter. A jury acquitted him in June 1938, but found him guilty of hunting out of season.[^1][^3]

The sources give different dates. A 1938 report quoted by Forest Lookouts gives November 28, 1937 as the day Nettleton died.[^1] The Nicolet history gives November 24, 1937 as the day of the shooting and says he died three days later.[^4] WXPR also says he died within three days of being shot.[^3]

After his death the tower was named for him. Forest Lookouts quotes a June 30, 1938 report that the Nicolet's forest supervisor had announced steps toward changing the name, while WXPR calls the rename official.[^1][^3] The Nicolet history calls it the Nettleton Fire Tower on the Argonne District.[^4]

The Wisconsin lookout list marks the tower as gone.[^2]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
