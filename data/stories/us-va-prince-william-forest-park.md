The Prince William Forest Park Tower Site marks a fire lookout that once stood near the park headquarters in Prince William County, Virginia.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes the tower as wooden, gives its build year as 1942, and puts its elevation at 218 feet (66 meters).[^1]

The register says the tower seems to have gone up when World War II began, and that it was rarely used afterward.[^1] The register adds that it may first have stood south of Joplin Road, based on a 1948 transfer of land to the Marine Corps.[^1] An October 1961 item from The Free-Lance Star, reprinted by Forest Lookouts, reports that the tower sat unmanned once the war ended.[^4]

In 1961 the tower was moved to Partlow, in Spotsylvania County.[^1] The same Free-Lance Star item reports that 13 Virginia Forestry Division staff members put it up there that week, on a landowner's property.[^4]

The National Historic Lookout Register's Partlow entry describes the moved tower as a 110-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab. It credits the 1961 build to 13 locals, which differs from the Forestry Division staff described in the 1961 item.[^3][^4] That entry says the tower came from "Prince William State Forest," where it had stood since World War II.[^3] The Forest Park entry and the 1961 item both name the Forest Park instead, so the sources do not agree on which park or forest it came from.[^1][^3][^4] The sources also describe the structure differently: the register calls the original a wooden tower, while the Partlow entry calls the moved one an Aermotor tower.[^1][^3]

The Partlow tower is listed as standing.[^2][^3] A 2019 visit report noted some rust, some missing window glass, and a cell-tower antenna setup on the tower.[^3] It also listed the tower as unstaffed and said the visitor saw security cameras and did not go to the base.[^3] No source read says whether the public may visit or climb it. The Virginia Department of Forestry administers it, so ask the department before any visit, and do not climb it.[^3]

At the original site, the register says a visitor center now occupies the place where the tower stood.[^1] The National Park Service administers Prince William Forest Park, and its contact page lists a phone line for visitor and park information under the park's headquarters address.[^1][^5]
