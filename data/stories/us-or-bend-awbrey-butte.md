In 1919 the Deschutes National Forest proposed a fire lookout on Awbrey Butte, the hill above Bend's north side.[^1][^3] The plan was a combined observatory and fire lookout for the town, and it hinged on a property owner making a lot available.[^1] The forest service planned a 40-foot (12 m) tower, with an auto road built up to its base.[^1]

By mid-July the site was described as lying on land belonging to Mrs. V.A. Forbes, and Judge J.A. Estes was in talks with the owner about a 50-foot (15 m) tower there.[^1] A July 31, 1919 item in the Bend Bulletin said the tower "will be erected in the near future."[^1] The same item reported that staff at forest headquarters could see a blaze in private timber from the top of Awbrey Heights, after heavy smoke had stopped the forest's other lookouts from spotting fires.[^1]

A Deschutes County lookouts blog gives 1919 as the construction date, and then records that no structures were built.[^2] The same blog says the site was used for fire detection, with at least one logged report, and that plans called for a fire tower and a public viewpoint.[^2]

Today the summit is fenced off.[^3] A hiking guide says you cannot reach the top, because the fence surrounds a set of communications towers, and it does not mention a lookout on the summit.[^3] The guide's route follows that fence, then drops through neighborhoods toward the Deschutes River, mostly on sidewalks.[^3] Stay outside the fence and on the public route.
