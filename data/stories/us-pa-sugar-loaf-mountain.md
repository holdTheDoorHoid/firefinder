Sugar Loaf Mountain had a fire lookout tower in Fayette County, Pennsylvania.[^1] An October 1921 newspaper report, quoted on a lookout history page, described the tower as new and said its view took in nearly three-quarters of the county's forest land.[^1]

The same report says a watchman stayed on duty around the clock, housed in a cabin at the base of the tower. A four-mile telephone extension had been run out to the tower so it could be reached by phone.[^1] The watchman, it says, spotted smoke from a brush-heap fire about ten miles from the tower and alerted the fire wardens, who found the blaze quickly.[^1]

The lookout history page marks the tower as removed in 1979.[^1]

A map database, TopoQuest, lists a Sugarloaf Fire Tower in Fayette County at 2,923 feet above sea level, on the Ohiopyle map sheet.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
