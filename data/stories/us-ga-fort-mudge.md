Tom Dunigan's Georgia lookout list places a tower called Fort Mudge in Ware County, at 135 feet (41 m) of elevation, and tags it "gfc-836".[^1] The name is shared with an unincorporated community. Wikipedia says the community takes its name from a fort set up in 1813 during the Florida war and abandoned when that war ended.[^2]

Wikipedia places that community in Brantley County, not Ware, so the community and the tower may not be the same place.[^2][^1] A Charlton County historical society page calls Fort Mudge "a small, temporary installation" and mentions a violent attack on a wagon train that passed nearby.[^3]

The name also turns up in fire history. An April 1955 issue of Georgia Forestry describes a March wildfire that spread out of the Okefenokee Swamp.[^4] Its first major front, the "Fort Mudge" fire, was centered about five miles south of Waycross and ran through Fort Mudge pasture to John's Pond, a swamp.[^4]

No source describes the site today, and none says whether a tower still stands there.
