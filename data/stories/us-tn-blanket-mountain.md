A state forester's report, as quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, describes an early forty-foot chestnut-pole tower on Blanket Mountain that aided fire control. The site dates that report to 1921 to 1928, with a question mark.[^1]

In July 1928, the Nashville Banner reported that the state division of forestry had just completed a lookout tower on Blanket Mountain, overlooking much of the Little River watershed.[^1] The top of the tower had a modern glass enclosure, and a map fixed on a plain table would help locate fires.[^1] Fires were to be reported by telephone to Elkmont and Tremont, and organized crews would then set out for them.[^1] The paper said it was the first tower built in that part of the Smokies.[^1] Neither account says whether the pole tower and the 1928 tower were the same structure.

The Forest Lookouts site lists the tower as removed.[^1] Tennessee Landforms places it in Sevier County at 4,609 feet, with the note "gsmnp historical."[^1][^2]

Reaching the summit today means an off-trail bushwhack.[^3] A hiking guide says hikers follow Jakes Creek Trail to Jakes Gap, then take an unmaintained, unmarked manway that is not an official NPS trail.[^3] The same guide reports remains of an old fire tower foundation and traces of a former ranger/caretaker's cabin.[^3] The summit lies within the Elkmont section of Great Smoky Mountains National Park.[^3]
