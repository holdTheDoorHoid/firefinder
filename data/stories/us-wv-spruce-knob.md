Spruce Knob is the highest point in West Virginia, in Pendleton County, within the Monongahela National Forest.[^1][^2]

A state report from 1916 describes a lookout station being built on the summit, at 4,860 feet, with a broad view over the surrounding land.[^1] Before the station was finished, a watchman named Moses R. Bennett went on duty in the spring of 1916. His job was partly to get to know local people and hand out literature.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page names no other watchman and no fire the station spotted.[^1]

The register of former lookout sites says a stone-faced concrete observation tower later took the wooden tower's place.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's West Virginia list marks the enclosed tower as gone.[^3] The register records the site as registered on January 26, 2017, nominated by Brian Powell.[^2] We could not find when the wooden tower came down.

The observation tower on the summit was built to draw visitors.[^4] Work on it ran through 1966 and 1967, and the summer of 1969 brought its completion.[^4] Senator Robert Byrd gave the keynote at its dedication on September 13, 1969.[^4] The National Park Service describes a two-story observation tower at the peak, located along a half-mile trail that loops around the summit.[^5] The sources do not say outright that this tower replaced the wooden lookout, but the register's wording points that way.[^2][^4]

The U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^2] We found no closure notice in the sources we read, so check current conditions with the Forest Service before a visit.[^5]
