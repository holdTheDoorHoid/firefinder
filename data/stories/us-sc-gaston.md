The Gaston tower in Lexington County was built around 1936.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40 with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and it is one of three towers in the county that the South Carolina Forestry Commission still owns.[^1] Wikipedia's article on the town of Gaston also gives 1936 as the first build year.[^3]

The Forestry Commission's tower network started in August 1930 with a tower near Camden, and the agency closed the whole system on October 1, 1993.[^2]

The register notes a keeper's house near the tower.[^1] It is used part time as a substation for the Lexington County Sheriff's Department.[^1] I found no source that names a fire this tower watched or the people who worked in its cab, so those parts of its history are still unknown.

Wikipedia gives the tower a height of 499 feet.[^3] The register gives an elevation of 499 feet (152 m) and a height of 100 feet for the tower itself, so the Wikipedia figure probably mixes up the elevation with the tower's height.[^1]

I could not find any source that says whether the public may visit or climb the tower today. Please ask the Forestry Commission before going near it.
