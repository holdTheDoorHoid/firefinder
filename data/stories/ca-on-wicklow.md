The Wicklow Fire Tower is a light steel tower about 24 m (80 ft) tall, built in 1922 for the Tweed Fire District.[^1] The district dates from that same year, when the province's Lands and Forests department set it up; that body is now the MNR.[^2]

The name matches Wicklow Township, one of three northern townships in the historic Hastings area.[^3] On 1 January 2001, Hastings Highlands was incorporated by amalgamating the Township of Bangor, Wicklow and McClure, among others.[^4]

The district's headquarters on Hungerford Road in Tweed served as the hub for 21 lookout towers.[^2] Each tower took compass bearings on any fire it spotted and relayed them by radio to headquarters.[^2]

Wikipedia says that during the 1970s every tower in the district was shut down, as aerial methods took over.[^2] A fan-kept list of towers still standing does not name the Wicklow tower.[^5]

The index labels the location Tweed, Ontario, and says the tower appears on a 1977 Pembroke topographic sheet and on a current fourth-edition map sheet.[^6] None of the sources read for this story describes what, if anything, stands at the site today, or whether the public can visit it.[^6]
