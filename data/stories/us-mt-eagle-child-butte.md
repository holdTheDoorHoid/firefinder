In May 1934 the Choteau Acantha reported that the Civilian Conservation Corps director at Hays planned two lookout towers in the Little Rockies, one on Monument Peak and one on Eagle Child Peak at the west end of the range.[^1]

Records do not confirm when, or whether, the Eagle Child tower was built. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list shows a platform at Eagle Child Butte, now gone, with no date.[^2]

The Montana lookout history site places the mountain on the Fort Belknap Indian Reservation.[^1] Most land in the Little Rockies is part of that reservation, and the Gros Ventre have long regarded Eagle Child as sacred.[^3] Tribal medicine men climbed there to fast and meditate.[^3] The tribes are now working to build up tourism on their part of the range.[^3]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or says whether the public may climb it. Because the mountain is sacred to the Gros Ventre and lies on reservation land, ask the tribes before going.
