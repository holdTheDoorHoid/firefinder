Bear Point is in Marion County, Oregon, in the Mount Hood region.[^1] Forest Lookouts places it in the Willamette National Forest.[^2]

In April 1931 the forest supervisor, C. C. Hall, said Bear Point was one of eleven emergency lookouts planned that year.[^2] It was to join the forest telephone system, giving it a link to the fire control office at Fish Lake.[^2] The lookout was built in 1934. The register says the U.S. Forest Service built its L-4 cab that year, while Forest Lookouts says the CCC constructed a 14-by-14-foot lookout house.[^1][^2]

In July 1934 a newspaper reported that Kenneth Wilson was stationed there, and called the lookout the highest in the Willamette National Forest, at 6,035 feet.[^2] The register gives the summit as 6,043 feet, so the two sources differ slightly on elevation.[^1][^2] In 1941 the lookout was staffed for 70 days. Reports were relayed through the Detroit Ranger Station and the Santiam Co-op Telephone Company.[^2]

Forest Lookouts says the structure was burned on October 11, 1968.[^2] The register says the cab was gone by 1968.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register listed the site on August 16, 2010, with Ray Kresek as nominator.[^1]

The register lists the site as administered by the U.S. Forest Service.[^1] No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit now, or whether the summit is open to visitors. Check with the Forest Service before going.
