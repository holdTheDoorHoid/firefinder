The Lafollette tower stands in Campbell County, at about 1,240 feet (378 m) above sea level.[^1] The Tennessee Landforms list marks it as standing.[^1]

We found no build date, builder, or history specific to this tower in the sources we read. A 2014 National Register form lists a Lafollette tower in Campbell County among the Tennessee fire towers still extant as of March 26, 2014.[^2] That form covers the Division of Forestry's lookout network, which ran from 1933 to 1975.[^2]

The early towers in that network were mostly steel.[^2] The form notes that the vast majority of the lookout towers built in Tennessee during the 1930s were steel-frame, and that the Civilian Conservation Corps is credited with building 98 fire lookout towers in the state.[^2] Most of the towers went up later, between the late 1940s and about 1960.[^2]

Today we found no current access information for this tower. The Tennessee Landforms site notes that many towers have been removed and most tower locations are now private property.[^1] It also advises people not to climb the towers, because steps are often gone or rotten.[^1] Ask the landowner before going near it.

[^1]: Tennessee Lookout Towers, Tennessee Landforms (Tom Dunigan), https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers, 1933 to 1975, National Register multiple property documentation form, NPGallery (National Park Service), https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/64501237.pdf (accessed 2026-10-09).
