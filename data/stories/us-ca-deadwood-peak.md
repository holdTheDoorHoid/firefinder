Deadwood Peak sits at the top of Potter Ridge, southwest of Oakhurst in Madera County.[^1] The first tower on the site went up in 1905 as a wood cabin.[^2] The tower people see today is a later one.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower and cab for the Sierra National Forest, and it dates the work to 1934, with a 1952 addition.[^1] Another account says a 20-foot steel tower replaced the earlier structure in 1932, carrying an L-4 or 4AR cab, and that the current C-3(L) cab went on in 1952.[^2] The two accounts disagree on the build year.[^1][^2]

Staffing began early.[^2] One history says the lookout was manned through the 1909 season and was the Sierra National Forest's first lookout to be staffed without a break.[^2] The state forestry agency, since renamed CAL FIRE, took over the site in 1950 and added a utility room below the cab in 1952.[^1][^2] Paid and volunteer lookouts kept watch from the tower until it was retired after the 1994 fire season.[^2]

Since then the tower has had a hard road. A June 1968 item from the Madera Tribune, quoted on a lookout history site, reported a break-in and the loss of a walkie-talkie, binoculars, blankets and gasoline.[^4] A lookout history write-up says the most recent photo its author saw, from 2008, showed the tower in extreme disrepair.[^2] In a state budget request for fiscal year 2025–26, CAL FIRE asked for $587,000 to begin preliminary plans for repairing the structure, as part of a project shared with Penon Blanco Lookout, with total estimated costs of $11,434,000.[^3]

Today the site is not readily open to the public.[^1] The register says it lies about 4.5 miles from the paved road, behind locked gates, and that microwave dishes and radio vaults and towers are at the site.[^1] One write-up places the tower on leased private land outside the forest boundary, while the register ties it to the Sierra National Forest.[^1][^2] The state request covers only the preliminary plans phase.[^3]
