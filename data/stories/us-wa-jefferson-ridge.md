Jefferson Ridge sits between the Hamma Hamma River and the Jefferson Creek valley in Mason County, inside the Olympic National Forest, at 3,850 feet.[^1][^3][^4]

The register dates the tower to 1961 and gives it as a 41-foot treated timber R-6 tower.[^1] Sources disagree on the year and the height. Willhite says helicopter-borne parts arrived in 1960, while a nearby forest fire kept one helicopter stationed at the Hoodsport Ranger Station.[^3] A Port Angeles Evening News item of April 27, 1962, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, reports that the tower, 40 feet tall with a lookout house, went up on the ridge, with the lumber brought in by helicopter.[^4] Willhite says the same paper reported the tower as finished.[^3]

Willhite says the lookout was used for only a few years.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1967.[^1] FireLookout says it was destroyed that year, and Willhite and the Forest Lookouts page say it had outlived its use and was burned.[^2][^3][^4] Willhite also cites a June 1976 Shelton-Mason Co. Journal article about Army trail work in the area, which said crews had taken apart and cleaned up a partly burned lookout here. He judged the cleanup poor: the wreckage was simply heaped up and burned a second time.[^3]

Willhite's page, based on what he saw in 2014, describes much of the lookout lying around the site, including an old foundation.[^3] A Washington Trails Association trip report from November 2022 found burned timbers, metal hardware, four footings, an old building foundation, and what looked like propane stove remnants.[^5]

A 2022 trip report says drivers can reach a gate on forest road 2421 and hike the rest, and that water bars on the road need a high-clearance vehicle.[^5] Willhite calls the road steep and rocky and recommends high clearance, and says it ends at a gate with plenty of parking.[^3] No source we read mentions a permit or closure for the site.
