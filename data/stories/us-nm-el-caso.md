El Caso Lookout stands in the Gila National Forest, north of Apache Creek in Catron County, New Mexico, at about 8,847 feet.[^1][^2] The Works Progress Administration built the complex in 1934, and it was one of three New Deal-era forest fire lookouts built in the county.[^2][^3]

The register describes a 31.6-foot Aermotor MC-24 tower and calls it one of the best examples of that model in the Southwest. The register also says the lookout and its ground house have National Register status.[^1] The National Register listing is dated January 28, 1988.[^2] The lookout was registered with the National Historic Lookout Register on January 2, 2006.[^1]

The register and the 2020 report both describe a restored cab.[^1][^3] The 2020 report, though, says the complex has been neglected and needs major restoration to meet its National Register standard.[^3]

The 2020 report, from the New Mexico chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association, says El Caso is definitely not staffed.[^3] A photo in that report, taken on June 20, 2019, carries the caption "El Caso Lookout abandoned."[^3] None of these sources gives the year the lookout was last staffed, and none describes a notable fire at the site.

The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as the administrator, with the Quemado Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] No source confirms public access or says whether anyone can go up the tower. Anyone planning a trip should check with the Gila National Forest first, and should keep off a structure the 2020 report describes as abandoned.[^3]
