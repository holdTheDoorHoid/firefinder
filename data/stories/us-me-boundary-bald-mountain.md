Boundary Bald Mountain lies in Somerset County, on timberland owned by Hilton Timberlands of Jackman, a family firm that holds more than 45,000 acres across three counties.[^1][^3]

A lookout station went up on the mountain in 1911, and a long-distance telephone line was run through the Heald Pond Camps to reach it.[^2] A trail to the top and a cellar for the camp followed in 1912.[^2] An entry for 1915 mentions a telephone box installed in the tower, and a 1920 inventory described a 12-foot wood tower.[^2]

The sources disagree about the first tower. A 2021 Press Herald article recalls a log fire tower on the peak in the early 1900s, when a rail spur ran along its southern base.[^1] A Maine lookout listing gives three structures: a wood structure of unknown height dated 1911, a 12-foot wood tower dated 1914, and a steel tower dated 1937 and listed at 35, with no unit given.[^3]

A 1916 report noted the outside paint and a well-kept trail, but called the telephone service poor.[^2] The sources we read do not name a lookout.

A Maine lookout listing says the tower collapsed between 1971 and 1976.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register says it collapsed and was removed.[^4] A 2021 Press Herald article ties the collapse to the 1937 fire tower and says what is left of it is a rusting pile on the summit.[^1] The sources disagree about whether the wreckage was taken away.

In 2021 the summit held a helipad, a shed, a solar array and a communications tower, and an orange windsock marked the high point.[^1] The article describes Bald Mountain Road as a private gravel logging road open to the public, and calls the old fire warden's trail up the south side steep and unmaintained.[^1] Remnants of the old telephone line can be found on the upper trail.[^1] The only standing tower the article mentions on the summit is the communications tower.[^1] The newest source we found is from 2021.
