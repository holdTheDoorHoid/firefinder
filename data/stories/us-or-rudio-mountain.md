Rudio Mountain sits about nine miles northeast of Dayville in Grant County, Oregon.[^2] Its lookout was a 106-foot wooden tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab and a cabin, built in 1936 about a mile and a quarter north of the summit.[^1][^2]

The Forest Lookouts page lists panorama photographs taken from the site in August 1938.[^3] A 1941 entry says the fire warden mustered several men to fight a timber fire, and that the lookout carried radio gear that let local people follow its short-wave talks with headquarters at Kimberly.[^3] A July 1947 entry says Mr. and Mrs. Frank Lynch moved to the station.[^3]

The yearly notes from the 1960s give fire counts that vary a lot. The station reported seven discoveries in 1960, two fires in 1963, and no fire reports in 1962.[^3] In 1968 the tower got new exterior paint and an orange roof, which the notes say was meant to make the tower more visible from above.[^3]

The register, firelookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page header all name the Oregon Department of Forestry as the agency that ran the site.[^1][^2][^3] A 1942 survey station description on the Forest Lookouts page calls the tower a U.S. Forest Service lookout instead, so the sources disagree about the agency.[^3]

The register says the tower and cabin were put to emergency use in the 1960s, and it and firelookout.com say both are now gone.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page lists the tower as removed but gives no date.[^3]

Firelookout.com lists no public access to the site. No source describes footings, a foundation or any other remains on the summit today.[^1][^2][^3]
