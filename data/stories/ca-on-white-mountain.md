The White Mountain Fire Tower is a heavy steel tower about 24 m (80 ft) tall, built in 1938 for the Tweed Fire District.[^1] The district dates from 1922, when the province's Lands and Forests department set it up; that body is now the MNR.[^2]

The district's headquarters on Hungerford Road in Tweed served as the hub for 21 fire lookout towers.[^2] Each tower took compass bearings on any fire it spotted and relayed them by radio to headquarters.[^2]

Wikipedia says that during the 1970s every tower in the district was shut down, as aerial methods took over.[^2] A fan-kept list of towers still standing does not name White Mountain.[^3]

The index labels the location Tweed, Ontario, and says the steel tower appears on a 1977 Pembroke topographic sheet.[^4] None of the sources read for this story describes what, if anything, stands at the site today, or whether the public can visit it.[^4]
