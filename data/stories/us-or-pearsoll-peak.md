Pearsoll Peak Lookout stands on the highest point in the Kalmiopsis Wilderness, about 10 miles west-northwest of Selma, Oregon, in Curry County.[^3][^1] Published elevations differ: the register gives 5,007 feet, while the Sand Mountain Society and firelookout.com give 5,098.[^1][^3][^2]

A D-6 cupola cabin went up on the peak in 1920.[^2] In 1954 it was replaced by the L-4 lookout that stands today. The society suspects the building was made from a kit that had been stored for some time.[^1][^3]

The register lists it as US 51, OR 9, registered on November 27, 1992, and nominated by Dottie Boyd, a Forest Service district archaeologist.[^1] An Oregon lookout list marks it as standing.[^4]

The Sand Mountain Society, a volunteer group, took the lookout on as a restoration project. In 1991 its members and district staff re-framed the west and south walls, rebuilt the shutters and replaced the roof, leaving the outriggers for later.[^3] The register says restoration was completed in 1994.[^1]

In 2001 snow snapped several outriggers after the shutters were left up over winter, so crews replaced them and removed attic insulation where mice had nested.[^3] In 2002 the freshly painted lookout survived the Biscuit Fire. Conifers and brush within 50 feet were torched, and the society credits the new finishes and an early shutter drop for saving the building.[^3] In 2008 a north deck that had given way was taken apart and recorded so its material could be reused.[^3]

In 2011, during a windstorm, shutters latched to eyebolts in the west window sill fluttered until the bolts tore the sill out, and the west wall had to be re-framed. The original fir floor, which had been laid the wrong way, was relaid in 2012 and varnished clear, as it was in 1954.[^3]

Fires have also shut the way in. The society names the Labrador (2013), Chetco Bar (2017) and Klondike (2018) fires as closures that came and went over several years.[^3]

The society opens the lookout each spring, lowers the shutters each fall and now handles the annual upkeep.[^3] Its season runs June 1 to September 30.[^3] The last 7 miles of road take about 1.5 hours in low range in a high-clearance vehicle, and the final 1.5 miles is a moderately difficult hike.[^3] Visitors have made changes to the building, and the society's pages do not spell out any visitor rules.[^3]
