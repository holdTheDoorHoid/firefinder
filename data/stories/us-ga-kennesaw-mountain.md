On Big Kennesaw Mountain in Cobb County, the fire lookout was built inside an aviation beacon tower.[^1][^4] The Civil Aeronautics Administration had long operated an airplane beacon on the mountain, predating the national park, and in 1939 it asked to build a taller tower for that beacon.[^1][^2] The National Park Service refused a new tower because of its visual effect, but allowed a stronger beacon, and the Civil Aeronautics Administration agreed to run its cable underground.[^1] The park's history says that decision set a precedent: later tower requests from AT&T, Southern Bell and the state forestry department were also turned down.[^1]

The Civilian Conservation Corps camp at the park was established on July 1, 1938.[^2] Its camp foreman was made park fire chief, and a lookout tower went up on the mountain, equipped with a telephone to report fires.[^1] The lookout was built inside the beacon tower, and the park chronology puts the start of its construction in October 1940.[^1][^2]

According to the park history, the state and the county had no fire protection on the mountain until 1943. That year the superintendent signed a cooperative agreement with the Georgia Department of Forestry, which called for a state employee to take a post on the mountain as its fire lookout.[^1][^2]

The Federal Aviation Administration later operated the beacon. It stopped using the tower in 1984, and the park history says the tower was torn down in 1985.[^1][^2] Because the lookout was built inside that tower, it appears to have come down with it. No source I read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit today.

The park is now a day-use national battlefield park, and its visitor center is open from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM daily, except Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's Day.[^3] The park listed the mountain road as closed to private cars as of January 2026, and says it may open or close the road depending on traffic. A free shuttle runs daily, but parking fees still apply.[^3]
