The First Thought lookout stood on First Thought Mountain near the town of Orient, in Stevens County on the Colville National Forest.[^1][^3] A newspaper item from August 1914, quoted on the Weebly page, describes a station recently built on First Thought Hill near Orient.[^3] The register dates the first lookout to 1916, a 35-foot pole platform with a log shelter at its base.[^1]

The sources differ on the tower that followed. The register describes a 60-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6 cab, put up in 1925.[^1] The Weebly page quotes a 1925 account of a 70-foot wooden tower built that April, and a later note that it stood 76 feet to the roof.[^3]

Steel came in the 1940s. Footings for a steel tower with an L-4 cab were poured in September 1946.[^3] The register and the Weebly page both describe a 40-foot steel tower with an L-4 cab finished in 1947.[^1][^3] The Weebly page also says the tower was recycled from a parachute training tower in Idaho. We found no second source for that.[^3]

A few lookouts appear by name. The Weebly page has Stanley Pierce at the station in 1920, though a 1921 item spells the name Pearce.[^3] It names Walter Spaid as the man chosen for the post in 1925, and Keith Miller as taking charge in 1928.[^3] Keith Miller was leaving the post in September 1929.[^3] Marilyn Buck was on the tower in 1953.[^3] The Firelookout page lists known lookouts through 1959, with Ernie Keyes in 1958 and 1959.[^2]

In 1985 the Orient Improvement Clubs bought the lookout, planning to move it to Orient Community Park as a historical point of interest.[^3] In 1986, after several failed attempts to airlift it, a crane set the cab onto a trailer and moved it to the park.[^3] The register's text also gives 1986, but its photo caption says 2001.[^1]

We could not find a source that describes the cab's condition today, or whether the park still shows it. The register does not say what, if anything, remains on First Thought Mountain.[^1]
