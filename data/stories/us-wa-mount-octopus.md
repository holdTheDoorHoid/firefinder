The Mount Octopus lookout stood on a summit about 16 miles southeast of Forks, in western Jefferson County, Washington.[^2][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places it in King County instead, so the sources disagree on the county.[^1] Reports of a lookout on the mountain go back to 1923, when a Utah newspaper mentioned a new station on the summit.[^1][^4]

The Washington Department of Natural Resources (DNR) completed a 40-foot wooden live-in tower on the summit around 1957.[^1][^3][^4] A 1954 state report had already laid out a road to the site, where construction was waiting on timber removal.[^4]

The tower was manned through the 1960s.[^3][^4] A Port Angeles newspaper notice from April 1964 listed Octopus among seven lookouts to be manned that June, and a 1969 notice listed it among three.[^4] People who shared their memories with Willhite describe full-time work there, with weather readings and radio messages relayed between Forks and Olympia.[^3]

Willhite gives 1974 as the final staffed year, after which the tower was abandoned and stood vacant into the 1980s.[^3] The register gives the same abandonment year and says the tower was destroyed sometime in the 1980s.[^1] Willhite also repeats a local tale that prison crews from the Olympic Corrections Center brought the tower down, and that smoke was seen on the mountain before the lookout was found destroyed. He presents it as a tale and notes a similar story about another lookout.[^3]

Firelookout.com lists the site simply as gone.[^2] None of the four sources above describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.

Willhite's directions send visitors up DNR road H1200 to a gate about 3/4 mile below the summit, then on foot to the top.[^3] He gives the walk as 3/4 mile without snow and up to 3 miles with snow.[^3] He also reports that the mountain was once behind a locked gate, so the gate's status matters.[^3] The sources do not say whether that gate is open now. Check with DNR before you go, and do not pass a closed gate.
