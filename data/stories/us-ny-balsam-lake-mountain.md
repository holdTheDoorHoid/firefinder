Balsam Lake Mountain's fire tower began as a private project: in 1887 the Balsam Lake Club, a sportsmen's group with land below the summit, put up a wooden lookout to watch for fire.[^1][^2] The state's guide calls it the first forest fire tower in New York.[^3] During dry spells, the club hired a watchman for the lookout.[^1] A lightning strike destroyed that tower around 1901, and in 1905 the club built a similar wooden one.[^1][^3]

In 1909 the state took over the mountain's facility as it set up its fire tower system.[^1] A steel tower first went up in 1919, and the one standing today, about 47 feet tall, dates from 1930.[^3] Wikipedia also gives 1930 as the build year but puts the height at 47½ feet, so the two sources differ slightly on that figure.[^2][^3]

The tower was staffed until 1988.[^2] It was closed for much of the 1990s, and it was proposed for demolition as one of five towers left on state land in the Catskill Park.[^2] Hikers and local residents rallied to save it, and the state reversed course and restored it.[^2] The lookout was registered on the National Historic Lookout Register in 1990 and added to the National Register of Historic Places in 2001.[^1][^2]

The NHLR now describes the tower as fully restored and open to visitors.[^1] According to a May 2017 visit report, volunteers staff it on weekends from Memorial Day to Columbus Day.[^1] The route is the old summit jeep road, about three miles from Mill Brook Road, which is now a hiking trail.[^2] Staffing changes with the seasons, so check current conditions before you go.
