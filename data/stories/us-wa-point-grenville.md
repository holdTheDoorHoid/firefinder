The Point Grenville lookout stood on the Quinault Indian Reservation in Grays Harbor County, Washington, near the ocean.[^1][^3] Willhite ranks it among Washington's lowest fire lookouts.[^3]

A July 1930 newspaper report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, describes a newly built road leading to the Point Grenville lookout tower, so a tower already stood there by then.[^2] Sources date the 90-foot steel tower to the 1940s but do not say whether it is the same structure as the earlier tower.[^1][^3]

Sources disagree on the height. A 1953 USGS description gives a 77-foot tower and an 88.55-foot height, and Willhite leaves the two figures unreconciled.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in 1962.[^1][^4] That year the Washington State Highway Commission found the tower in poor shape. Vandals had taken stairs and iron parts, and the state called the tower unsafe to use.[^3] A USGS benchmark survey in 1969 still described a steel structure beneath the tower, so Willhite concludes it was still standing then.[^3] The site was added to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register on February 11, 2020, and the register lists the lookout as gone.[^1]

On a 2020 visit, Willhite found two steel footings partly buried beside the road. Near a giant spruce he also found what was left of a cabin, almost fully decayed.[^3]

The route in is BIA 27, a dirt road that Willhite says is probably unsigned, and he lists the site as drive-up.[^3] Public access is limited on most reservation roads. Willhite found this road open in 2020 and believes its gate stays open because the road is a tsunami evacuation route, but that is his belief rather than a stated rule.[^3] Check with the reservation before you go.
