The Belleayre fire tower stood on Belleayre Mountain, at Pine Hill in the town of Shandaken, Ulster County, New York.[^2] Before the state took over, the steel tower on the summit was privately owned by Eugene E. Howe.[^2] The state's lookout station was in place by May 1909: a 65-foot steel tower that cost $362.50.[^1] A previous owner had used the structure as an observation tower.[^1]

In 1930 a new steel tower went up.[^1] Frank Mead completed it, and at 73 feet it was the tallest in the area at the time, with a new cabin for the observer.[^1][^2] The sources disagree on what it replaced.[^1][^2] The state's 1930 annual report says the new tower was to replace an old windmill tower, while Bygone Hudson Valley says it replaced the original steel tower.[^1][^2]

Charles Y. Persons was the first observer, paid $50 a month.[^2] A 1914 appointment notice spells the name Parsons.[^1] A 1949 newspaper item says Charles served 21 years and his son Walton held the post for 14 years after him, while a 1963 obituary gives Walton's time there as 12 years.[^1] By 1936 the tower register showed a total of 1,500 visitors.[^1]

The state stopped staffing the tower in 1970.[^2] Catskill Country gives 1970 as the year it was decommissioned.[^3] Bygone Hudson Valley says it was officially closed in 1984.[^2] Extensive vandal damage led the state to judge it unsafe, and it was dismantled in 1985.[^2][^1]

According to Bygone Hudson Valley, the tower became more accessible to the public in the 1940s, when the state's Belleayre Ski Center opened.[^2] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains of the tower on the summit today, or whether the spot is open to visitors.[^2][^3]
