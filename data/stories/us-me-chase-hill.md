In the fall of 1931, Maine decided to end the gaps in lookout coverage that had left some districts without a tower.[^1] After a careful survey, the state erected five new 47-foot steel stations on peaks in the organized towns, plus one that had been dismantled.[^1] The commissioner's annual report names Chase Hill in Somerset County as one of the places where a lookout went up.[^1] The sources I could reach do not say whether the Chase Hill tower was one of the new steel stations or the dismantled one, so its original design is unclear.[^1]

I found no source that names the people who watched the tower or the years it was staffed, so that part of its story is still missing.

The Fire Lookout Association's list of Maine towers shows Chase Hill as standing.[^3] A hiker's guide published in August 2024 found the tower in good overall shape, with a few planks missing.[^2] It described climbing the staircase, but heavy cloud obscured the views that day.[^2]

Today the tower is reached on foot.[^2] From Lancaster Road in Canaan, the unpaved Chase Hill Road runs about 0.9 miles to an eroded road on the left, where visitors park off to the side.[^2] The round trip to the tower is about 0.6 miles on an eroded jeep track.[^2] The guide does not mention a gate, a closure or a permission requirement, but it does not say who owns the land either, so check before you go.[^2]
