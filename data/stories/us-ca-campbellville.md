The Campbellville site sits in Tehama County, California.[^1] The register says a lookout has operated here since at least 1922, first for the Lassen National Forest.[^1] It is about 20 miles north of Chico.[^2]

The state built a newer structure in 1937: an eight-by-eight-foot wood-frame observation cabin on a 60-foot treated timber tower, with a BC-201 residence cabin.[^1] A 2011 Assembly analysis gives a different timeline, saying CAL FIRE acquired the property in 1971 to build a lookout.[^3] The two accounts do not agree.

The earliest staffing we found is from April 1922, when a Red Bluff Daily News report, quoted on a lookout history page, says Ranger W.L. Slater sighted a blaze from the Campbellville station.[^4] The man who admitted starting that fire, W.A. Stall, pleaded guilty in May 1922 to setting brush alight and was fined $50.[^4] The blaze covered roughly 800 acres before it was brought under control.[^4]

The register says the structure was gone by 1986, though it gives no exact removal year.[^1] In 2011, AB 1272 would authorize the state to dispose of the parcel.[^2] Its enrolled text calls the parcel about five acres, while the April committee analysis said about 20.[^2][^3] The analysis also says CAL FIRE had no planned future use for the land.[^3] In April 2014, the site and about five acres of forest land appeared on a state list of excess properties under review, flagged for possible sale.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today.
