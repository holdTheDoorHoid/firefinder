The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists a tower on Spear Mountain in Buckingham County, Virginia, and says it was built before 1941.[^1] A Cold War inventory calls the same summit Spears Mountain and places it about 11 miles west of the town of Buckingham.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list also marks the tower as gone.[^3]

The register says the tower was reported in 1954 to be rotten on the ground.[^1] In 1963 the summit was cleared, and AT&T built a troposcatter relay there.[^1] Troposcatter is a long-distance radio method that works beyond the range of ordinary microwave links.[^2]

The Cold War relay began operating around 1964 to 1965 and is no longer in service.[^2] It linked two larger installations in Virginia and North Carolina and was one of five "Project Office" stations in the mid-Atlantic region.[^2] The Center for Land Use Interpretation says AT&T built the site in the 1960s.[^4]

The same center says the concrete paraboloid antennas still stand, on land now in private hands and partly used as a home.[^4] A 2013 WSET report says a landowner bought the site from the government in 2005, built a house there, and keeps the reflectors in his backyard.[^5] The report adds that only the emergency radio equipment mounted above them still works.[^5] It also calls the property private and asks readers not to go looking for it.[^5]

So the fire tower itself is long gone.[^1][^3] The Cold War reflectors still stand on the summit, on private land.[^4][^5]
