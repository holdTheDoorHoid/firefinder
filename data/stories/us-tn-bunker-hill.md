Bunker Hill is a former fire lookout site on the Tennessee side of the Great Smoky Mountains National Park, in Blount County.[^1][^2] The Tennessee lookout list puts it at 2,767 feet, or about 843 m, above sea level.[^2]

Across the park, a 2011 thesis says ten towers and nine lookout cabins were built between 1934 and 1939, and that lookouts kept watch from those sites for decades during both annual fire seasons.[^3]

The list's entry for Bunker Hill cites two sets of photographs: a 1951 picture by Dutch Roth, and 1930 pictures from National Park Service archives, shared by Peter Barr.[^2]

The list now marks the tower as removed, and notes the park's initials beside that entry.[^2] The thesis adds that all but four of the park's towers, and one of its cabins, were taken out of the park in the decade that followed.[^3] The sources disagree on when the park stopped using its lookouts. An NRHP document that cites the same thesis says the National Park Service had stopped using the park's towers by 1968.[^4] The thesis abstract, by contrast, places the end of the park's lookouts in the 1970s.[^3]

No source reached describes what, if anything, still stands at the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
