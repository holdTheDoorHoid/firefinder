Simcoe Butte is about 13 miles north-northeast of Goldendale, in Klickitat County, Washington.[^2][^1] The summit lies on the Yakama Reservation, which the register calls the Yakima Indian Reservation.[^3][^1]

Before any tower, a survey party in 1941 described a temporary fire lookout on the summit, made of a rock windbreak and a field telephone.[^3] In 1948 the Division of Forestry put up a 12-foot ring-connected tower topped by an L-5 cab.[^3] A Goldendale newspaper item from that year, quoted by Forest Lookouts, called the planned site Frog (Conlee) Butte.[^4] The same page quotes the 1948 Annual Report, which places the tower on the divide above Satus Pass.[^4] The register and firelookout.com both give the tower as 10 feet tall, which does not match the 12 feet in the 1948 sources.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The register says the tower was removed in 1958, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] Willhite says it was replaced by the nearby Sopelia lookout the following year.[^3] The Sopelia register says its 1959 cab replaced the Simcoe Butte lookout, and that a 2013 forest fire burned it.[^6]

Today the summit is reached by a road across state land, which Willhite calls good for most vehicles.[^3] The same guide says the summit is officially Yakama Nation land, and that the route passes gated private properties with old signs that mention the Yakama Tribe.[^3] TrailChick says most of the reservation is off-limits to non-tribal members, and visitors are rarely admitted.[^5] The writer was told the only way onto reservation land was to be a tribal member, the spouse of one, or part of a work party, so anyone thinking of going should ask the Yakama Nation first.[^5]

Willhite describes a fallen radio tower at a communications site on the ridge before the butte, which looks similar to a metal fire lookout.[^3] We found no source that describes what remains of the 1948 tower on the summit.
