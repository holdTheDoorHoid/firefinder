A seasonal glass house on the summit of La Cumbre Peak was completed in the summer of 1923 by the Forest Service.[^5] That September a fire came at the station from three sides, and a newspaper reported rangers fighting to save it.[^2] By 1932 the Forest Service had named a lookout for the peak, and a May 1938 notice said the station would open the following Friday.[^2]

The tower that stands today is dated to 1945 by the News-Press and Peakbagging.[^6][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register's description and Wikipedia give 1946 instead.[^1][^5] The Register says the new structure replaced an older cabin on a 10-foot open timber tower.[^1] Peakbagging describes the new cab as a one-off experimental design on a 20-foot K-brace tower.[^3] The two height figures may not describe the same structure.[^1][^3] The Register calls the design experimental, with sloped glass and fairly high walls.[^1] Its $6,500 price was high for the time, so it was the only model built.[^1]

The Register listed the lookout on June 19, 2010, as US 882, CA 92.[^1] Peakbagging notes that communications equipment is on the site.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list for Southern California gives the tower a status of "RF Hazard," without saying more.[^7]

Today the Forest Service describes the tower as closed to the public. It sits beside a day-use area, and the picnic area is a quarter-mile hike past a locked gate.[^4] The sources do not say when the tower stopped being staffed, what it watched in later decades, or what work has been done on it since the Register listing. Please do not climb the closed tower. Check with the Los Padres National Forest before planning a visit.[^4]
