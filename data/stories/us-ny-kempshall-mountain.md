Kempshall Mountain rises 3,350 feet near the hamlet of Long Lake in Hamilton County, New York, and it once carried a fire lookout.[^1][^2] The first structure on the summit was a wooden tower the Conservation Commission built in May 1911.[^1] In 1918 a 35-foot Aermotor LS40 steel tower replaced it, bought and erected that year.[^1][^5]

Observers had cabins on the mountain too.[^5] One went up in 1917 as part of a set of new observer cabins.[^5] New cabins followed in 1925, according to that year's Conservation Commission report.[^6] In 1939 the Civilian Conservation Corps built a new cabin for the observer's living quarters.[^5]

The tower stopped lookout work at the end of the 1971 season, when aerial detection took over the job.[^1] The Adirondack Explorer says the steel tower was closed in 1971 and dismantled in June 1977.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Kempshall and West Mountain towers were removed in the 1980s.[^3] The sources disagree on the date of removal.[^2][^3]

Parts of the two towers were later used to build one exhibit tower in Elizabethtown.[^1] Wikipedia calls the host the Essex County Historical Museum, while the register names the Adirondack History Museum.[^1][^3] The register says the museum bought the tower parts in the 1990s and built a single exhibit tower, and it recorded that tower on May 22, 2022, as US 1596, NY 48.[^3]

The mountain tower is listed as gone.[^4] An Explorer caption describes a small clearing on the summit where the fire tower once stood.[^2] The trail from Long Lake was left largely unmaintained after the tower came down, though some old state trail markers still show along parts of it.[^2]

Visitors who want to climb the exhibit tower must go through the museum.[^3]
