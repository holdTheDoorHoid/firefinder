Billings (Summit) was a lookout on a peak near Headquarters, Idaho, in the Clearwater tree farm.[^1] A 1949 account, quoted by Forest Lookouts, said the peak overlooks more than 300,000 acres of that tree farm.[^1]

A pole platform went up in 1927.[^1][^3] A larger wooden tower followed in the late 1930s. Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts give 1937 for the 84-foot tower, while a 1942 association report says it was constructed in 1938.[^3][^2][^1] A January 1939 item in the Lewiston Morning Tribune said the new 86-foot tower was completed that summer, along with a ground-level smokechaser cabin of L-4 type on a cement foundation.[^1]

Firelookout.com calls the tower an L-6 design, so the L-4 type in the 1939 item seems to refer to the cabin.[^3][^1] The same 1942 report counted it among 20 lookouts the association operated in fire season.[^1]

In 1948 the lookout, formerly Summit Lookout, was renamed for C.L. Billings, a past association president, by action of the State Land Board and association directors.[^1] In the fall of 1955, during hunting season, the tower was nearly destroyed, but the association's report says it was restored.[^1]

After the 1962 fire season, the association's report says the tower, in poor condition, was removed, and better detection methods made it no longer cost effective.[^1] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts both say it was destroyed in 1962.[^3][^2]

Today, none of these sources describes what, if anything, remains on the summit, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
