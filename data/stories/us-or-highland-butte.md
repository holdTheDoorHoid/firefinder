Highland Butte rises about 10 miles southeast of Oregon City, in Clackamas County.[^2] The Oregon Department of Forestry administered the lookout.[^1][^2]

A February 1916 newspaper item listed a lookout camp on Highland Butte among several in the district.[^3] By April 1927 a Molalla paper described a platform up in a large tree, reached by sticks set into the trunk.[^3]

That spring the state board of forestry approved plans for an 85-foot steel tower on the butte, to serve as a central dispatcher station in summer. A motor road reached the summit, and the state forester said visitors would be welcome to climb the tower.[^3]

The sources disagree about when the steel tower went up. The register and firelookout.com say a platform lookout was built in 1929 atop twin spar trees, and that an 80-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab replaced it in 1940.[^1][^2] A January 1928 newspaper report lists a steel tower on the butte among recent construction, saying it stood 81 feet high and cost $45,348.[^3] A 1932 account describes a 90-foot tower.[^3] The 1927 plans said 85 feet, the January 1928 report 81 and the 1932 account 90, so the heights do not agree.[^3]

The last named lookout in these sources is Kathleen Morrison, who was called in July 1952 to take over as fire guard lookout at Highland Butte.[^2][^3] In 1938 CCC crews built a new telephone line from North Fork Station to the butte.[^3]

The Columbus Day storm of October 12, 1962, destroyed the tower.[^1][^2][^3] A 1963 survey note says the tower has been blown over.[^3] A 1962 district report also said plans called for inactivating the lookout, citing its valley location and semi-agricultural surroundings.[^3]

No source here describes what, if anything, remains on the butte today. The sources also do not say whether the summit is open to visitors now.[^3]
