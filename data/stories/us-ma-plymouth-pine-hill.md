The register says Plymouth, Massachusetts, may be the site of the oldest and longest-running fire detection activity in America, with fire towers at five locations in the town.[^1] The current Pine Hill tower is a 70-foot tower with a 10-by-10-foot wooden cab, built in 1966.[^1]

The town ran a 90-foot iron tower on Billington Hill in 1889.[^1] After the state took over fire detection in 1911, it built a new tower on Reservoir Hill.[^1] A replacement 48-foot tower was moved to Telegraph Hill in 1939 and served until 1942.[^1]

In 1942 a 60-foot steel tower was put up on Manomet Hill.[^2] The register says a new tower was constructed there that year, while the former-sites record says the steel tower had been relocated from Pelham.[^1][^2] It served there until about 1966, when its duties moved to the new tower on Pine Hill.[^2][^3]

A March 2014 visit report found the Pine Hill tower in good shape, with sound stairs, sound wood and intact glass.[^1] The same report said the tower was not staffed.[^1] It also noted that no NHLR sign is posted and that directions to the tower are not signed.[^1]

The old Manomet Hill site is now listed separately as a former site.[^2] The Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control administers the Pine Hill tower.[^1] The sources read for this story give no current access rules, so check with the bureau before any visit.
