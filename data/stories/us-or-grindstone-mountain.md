The Grindstone Mountain lookout went up in 1950 in Tillamook County, Oregon, and the Oregon Department of Forestry administered it.[^1] The 1950 annual report to that department lists a 14x14 Amort Special lookout house, which cost $2281.60 in total.[^2] The house was of the aluminum type and rested directly on the ground.[^2]

The lookout was built during the Tillamook Burn, a series of large forest fires that began in 1933 and recurred every six years until 1951.[^4]

In October 1951, Lou Amort, a state forestry engineer, said the lookout had come through the last Tillamook fire in good shape.[^2] The fire burned hard around the lookout, consuming nearby stumps and melting a jug about five feet from the house, yet the house came through without damage.[^2]

In June 1957 the Vernonia Eagle reported that Ruby Wells, who had been on a fire lookout for several weeks, was moved to Grindstone for the rest of the summer.[^2]

The lookout was removed in 1963.[^1][^2] The firelookout.com listing marks it gone.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site on November 7, 2019.[^1]

No source described here says what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.
