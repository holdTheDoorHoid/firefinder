The Henryville Fire Tower stands in Clark State Forest, the oldest state forest in Indiana, which was established in 1903.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register describes the tower as built around 1930, and it was registered on December 15, 2014.[^1]

The early history is murky. A wooden tower stood about a mile northeast of the current site in the late 1920s, and sources disagree on when the steel tower went up.[^4] A 1932 state list gives 1927, the sign on site says 1928, and the register says around 1930.[^4][^1] A 1927 state report counted two fire towers on the state forest, though it says one was erected by the federal government for geodetic survey work.[^4] The Forest Service history says that when Indiana began building lookout towers in 1930, it had only one fire tower, at Clark State Forest.[^5]

In July 1930 a Kokomo newspaper invited people to visit the tower, which overlooks the counties of Clark, Floyd, Washington and Scott.[^2] The paper said that in the fall and spring, when the leaves are dry, the observer phones the warden when and where a forest fire starts.[^2]

Its condition has changed in recent years. The state says the tower is closed for improvements, which restricts use of the stairs, and that it will reopen once the work is finished.[^3] The Indiana Fire Towers site says the tower was under repair and closed to climbing in 2023 and 2024. It also reports a fire in the cab on May 17, 2025, based on Facebook posts.[^4]

The tower is still standing, but it is not open to climbing right now.[^4] Please stay off it while it is closed. The state's notice gives no reopening date, so check with the forest office before any visit.[^3]
