Kelly Butte rises to about 5,400 feet in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest in King County.[^1][^2] The first lookout on the summit was a 12-by-12-foot cupola cabin, which the National Historic Lookout Register, the Forest Service and the Snoqualmie Fire Lookouts all date to 1926.[^1][^2][^3] A regional history page quotes 1924 notes that construction was under way and that a new standard lookout house was finished in December 1924, so the start date is not fully settled.[^4]

The cupola was replaced in 1950 by an L-4 cab.[^2] The Register describes that cab as a 14-by-14-foot ground house from 1932, with a catwalk, and says the lookout was staffed until the 1980s.[^1]

The lookout was last staffed in the 1980s.[^5] The Snoqualmie association says it then stood empty for about twenty years while it gradually decayed.[^3] Starting in 2008, volunteers teamed up with the Forest Fire Lookout Association and the Forest Service on a major restoration, finished in 2011.[^3] The Forest Service returned the lookout to service in 2012.[^3]

A 2006 Forest Fire Lookout Association report shows an award that year toward the restoration.[^6] A trail guide says the volunteers logged more than 3,000 hours of work.[^7] With major help from the Washington Trails Association, the trail was improved, and a new route eliminated the need for a rope on a steep section.[^3]

The Forest Service describes the lookout as staffed by volunteers from the end of June to the end of September.[^2] Visitors can go inside only when a volunteer is on duty.[^7] When the lookout is staffed, the association welcomes visitors from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., but warns that there may be unscheduled days when it is closed.[^3] Camping is not allowed at the lookout or on its deck, and there is no potable water nearby.[^3]

The trail, Forest Service trail 1031, is 1.7 miles long, with about 1,000 feet of climb from the Kelly Butte Trailhead.[^2][^3] The Forest Service recommends a high-clearance vehicle on Forest Road 7030.[^2][^3] One trail guide puts the climb at 1,100 feet.[^7] Check with the Snoqualmie association or the Forest Service before you go.
