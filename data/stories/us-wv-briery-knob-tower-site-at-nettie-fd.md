The Briery Knob lookout stood in Pocahontas County, West Virginia, on Monongahela National Forest land.[^1] The sources we read do not give a construction date. The earliest mention we found is a state warden's report for 1913 and 1914, which describes a lumber company paying for more than twenty-five miles of telephone line to reach the Briery Knob and Job Knob stations.[^2]

A 1916 report placed the station in the Little Levels District, twelve miles from Seebert, with a view of about 1,000 square miles.[^2] It also named a watchman, S.A. Sparks, who lived three miles away at Lobelia and stayed at the station only at night when fire danger was high.[^2] Newspaper reports quoted on the Forest Lookouts site mention a telephone connection in 1926 and a plan in 1938 to improve the road so tourists could enjoy the view.[^2]

The sources also disagree on the elevation. The Register gives 4,517 feet (1,377 m), while the 1916 report gives 4,534 feet.[^1][^2]

The Register, which registered the site on January 26, 2017, says the tower was dismantled and that portions went to the Nettie Volunteer Fire Department.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a standing tower at Nettie FD, marked as relocated from Briery Knob, and lists the original site as gone.[^3] The two accounts differ on whether a tower now stands at Nettie, and neither gives a date for the move.[^1][^3] An April 2024 photo caption on the Register describes debris at the original site.[^1]

Today the sources we read describe no public access. The relocated parts are at a volunteer fire department, so ask the department before going to look.[^1]
