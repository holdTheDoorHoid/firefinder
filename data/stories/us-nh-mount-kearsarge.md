Mount Kearsarge Lookout is a fire lookout in Merrimack County, New Hampshire, and the state's Department of Resources and Economic Development administers it.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Hampshire list, which calls it Kearsarge South, shows it as standing.[^4]

The state forestry commission had a station built on the mountain in the fall of 1909. An early commission report says the watchman there had discovered and reported twenty-six fires since the station went up.[^3] In July 1910 the watchman saw the first curl of smoke from a fire in pine slash near the Kearsarge post office and telephoned the fire warden. The fire was out after four hours.[^3]

A September 1924 notice said the state was erecting four new lookout towers, one of them on Mount Kearsarge.[^3] The sources I read do not say whether that structure is the one standing today, and the Register gives no build date for it.[^1][^3]

In 2012 a restoration replaced the tower legs, the cabin girders, fascia boards and cross bracing. Materials were flown up to the summit.[^1][^3] The Register credits Saco Ranger District personnel, while the Forest Lookouts page says White Mountain National Forest staff did the repairs.[^1][^3] Hikers were given a temporary sign explaining the project.[^1]

A Forest Fire Lookout Association New Hampshire chapter report for 2019 says the cabs on the Oak Hill and Mount Kearsarge towers were replaced, along with other work.[^2]

The Register added this tower on March 12, 2002, as US 410, NH 11.[^1] I found no source that sets out current climbing or visiting rules, so ask the state before you go up.[^1][^4]
