Lead Mountain is in Hancock County, where the Maine forest fire district put up a steel lookout on the summit in 1914.[^1] The sources disagree on its height. A Christian Science Monitor item from August 1914 gives 41 feet, while the Forest Commissioner's report for the same year describes a new 36-foot steel tower, and a 1920 inventory also lists 36 feet.[^1]

The mountain was already listed as a lookout station in the 1910 Forest Commissioner's report.[^1] In 1914 a telephone line was built up the mountain to the new steel lookout.[^1]

The station opened on April 14, 1919, and the season's first fire was spotted from it on April 20.[^1] A Maine Forest Service panoramic map dated 1918 to 1932 has the Lead Mt. fire tower at its center, in Township 28.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association registered the former site in 2008 as US 117, ME 43, and reports that the tower no longer stands.[^3] Its statewide list also marks the site as gone.[^4]

No source read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
