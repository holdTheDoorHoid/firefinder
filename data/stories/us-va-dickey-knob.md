A fire lookout once stood on Dickey Knob, a peak in Smyth County, Virginia, that now lies within the Raccoon Branch Wilderness.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the U.S. Forest Service administered the tower, which it dates to 1934 and says overlooked Damascus and Atkins.[^1]

The date is not settled. The register gives 1934, but a Rye Valley local history says the exact year is unknown and that 1934 has been said because a 1934 penny was set into the wet cement of a nearby USGS marker.[^2] The tower stood 50 feet tall, with a 12×12 enclosed room at the top and no electricity or running water.[^2] The register gives the elevation as 3,697 feet, while the local history cites the marker at about 3,640 feet.[^1][^2]

Fire watchers were employed in spring, before the leaves came out, and again in autumn, when dry growth covered the ground.[^2] Charlie Harrington, a native of Sugar Grove, was one of five men in the district who lived in the towers.[^2] A photo in the local history shows him using an Osborne fire finder at Dickeys Knob in the 1940s.[^2]

The tower came down before 1994, according to the register.[^1] The local history says it was torn down, and that a hiking trail from the nearby Raccoon campground was built afterward.[^2]

Today the site is reached on foot.[^3] The Dickey Knob Trail starts by site 6 at the Raccoon Branch Campground near Sugar Grove.[^3][^4] One hiker described a 4.8-mile out-and-back that includes the summit.[^3] Only the foundation remains at the top.[^2][^3] The Virginia Wilderness Committee says the trail offers spectacular views, but the hiker found the summit enclosed in dense woods and saw the views at an overlook partway along the trail.[^4][^3]
