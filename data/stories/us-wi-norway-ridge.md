Norway Ridge Lookout stands in Monroe County, Wisconsin.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower was built in 1932, and that a new cab went on in 2008 because damage had worn down the old one.[^2] The Register gives its elevation as 1,036 feet (316 meters).[^2]

The Register says the tower was manned irregularly during spring fire season until 2015, when the state shut its lookout network down.[^2] The DNR said it would stop using the towers because it did not consider them a safe or necessary tool for spotting wildfires.[^3] Across the network, the agency's forest bureau director told Wisconsin Public Radio, the towers had been manned for just 17 days the year before.[^3]

The Register says that as of early 2022 the state had this tower up for sale.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wisconsin list still marks it as standing.[^1] The DNR sought new owners for its decommissioned towers.[^4] Towers were generally offered at public auction twice, and any left unsold were to be torn down.[^4][^5]

We found no later record of a sale, a new owner, or public access. The sources do not say whether the tower can be visited, climbed or rented, so anyone interested should confirm the owner and the rules first, and should not climb it without permission.
