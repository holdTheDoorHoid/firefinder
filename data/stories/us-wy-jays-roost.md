Jays Roost is a mountain west of Keystone, in Carbon County, Wyoming.[^1][^3] The site sits within the Medicine Bow National Forest.[^2]

In May 1911 a newspaper reported that a telephone line would be built to the site, and that a young man, Benj. Burch, was to be stationed there.[^1] Each station was to get a compass graduated in degrees. When a fire appeared, every station that could see it was consulted, and the bearings were compared to pin down its location.[^1]

The lookout listing describes a 20-foot wooden platform.[^2] In June 1929 a newspaper reported plans to swap the wooden tower for a steel one, and said bids were being accepted to haul the new structure from the Foxpark railroad to the mountain.[^1] In March 1930 a steel tower was placed on the mountain, so that temporary observers could be posted there in bad weather.[^1] The article also cited the season before, when smoke drifting in from the northwest clouded the view from the primary lookouts on Somber Hill and Medicine Bow Peak.[^1]

In 1921 district rangers served as lookouts on Jay's Roost, one of several secondary sites, during the most dangerous fire periods.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Jays Roost as gone.[^3] The listing's wooden platform does not match the steel tower the 1930 article describes, so the sources disagree on what stood on the summit at the end.[^1][^2] No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.
