The Bell Mountain lookout stood about four miles southeast of Elkton in Douglas County.[^2] The Douglas fire patrol built a station there in 1928, with a platform set in the tops of two Douglas fir trees.[^1][^3] A September 1928 item in The Timberman put the platform at 114 feet, and a July 1928 item in The Sunday Oregonian put it at 117 feet.[^3]

In 1942 the Douglas association bought a 125-foot Richfield beacon that had stood on Highway 99 north of Roseburg and used its steel to build a three-legged lookout tower.[^3] The register puts that steel tower in 1943, at 128 feet, and says it was salvaged from an oil company sign.[^1] Newspapers say it was in use by August 1942, and one of them places the beacon near Winchester instead of Roseburg.[^3]

A 1939 item says J.L. Rowe was taken off the station for the summer. By August 1942 he held the lookout job, and an elevator had been built to take him up the tower.[^3] He was called off the station in November 1942, after the season.[^3] A 1941 Douglas FPA annual report dated the original two-tree tower to 1927, a year earlier than the register's 1928.[^3][^1]

The 1962 Columbus Day storm damaged the tower.[^1][^3] In 1963 it was rebuilt at 105 feet with a new cab, and stairs took the place of the elevator. The lookout was not staffed that year.[^3] The register says it was abandoned in 1965, and the lookout was discontinued after the fire season.[^1][^3] By 1978 the tower had been sold for salvage and removed, and the cabin went to the Coos Bay area to be used as a fishing cabin.[^1][^3]

No source we read describes the site as it is now. The one trace named is survey marks. A 1980 National Geodetic Survey description, quoted on a lookout history page, says the surface mark was destroyed during the dismantling. An underground mark was recovered and a new surface mark was set. The azimuth mark was searched for but not found, and the description says it was probably still in place under brush.[^3] We found nothing on current access or land ownership.
