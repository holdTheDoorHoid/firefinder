Before there was a tower, Fort Hill was the site of a fort belonging to Sassacus.[^4] That fort was abandoned after Capt. Mason captured the fort at Mystic.[^4] The hill lies in Groton, in New London County, Connecticut, at 185 feet (56 meters).[^1][^2]

By 1932 a fire tower stood at the Chasanba lodge on Fort Hill, and officials were using it to station a lookout.[^2] The lookout worked from early morning until 6 o'clock.[^2] From the tower, the lookout might see smoke as far west as Waterford.[^2] A guide to Groton's history says the state used the tower at Chashamba Lodge as a fire lookout.[^4]

On April 25, 1938, a newspaper item reported that the Groton fire tower had spotted smoke on Fort Hill the afternoon before.[^2] At 1 o'clock two trained crews from Groton headed for the scene, and the fire was under control by 3:22.[^2]

In February 1951 a newspaper item said the state forestry department was building a new tower on Stewart Hill, near Wyassup Lake, to replace the one on Fort Hill just outside Mystic. If the new tower was not ready in time, the Fort Hill tower would stay in use.[^3]

According to the register, the steel tower was later moved to North Stonington.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page marks the Fort Hill tower as removed.[^2] The sources do not say whether the old tower went to Stewart Hill, which is in North Stonington.[^1][^3] The 1951 item describes a new tower under construction on Stewart Hill.[^3]

No source we read describes what remains on Fort Hill today, or whether the hill is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
