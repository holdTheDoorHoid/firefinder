Copernicus Peak is the highest point in Santa Clara County.[^2] The register dates the first lookout here to 1931, when it was erected on top of a water tank.[^1] The present tower dates to 1938.[^1] It is a special K-brace design, Plan No. L-901, with a standard live-in cab, and it was built to straddle the existing 10,000-gallon concrete tank.[^1] The register says the tower is only 13 feet high, and it notes the cab has had only minor changes: aluminum roofing, several replaced shutters, some interior work and new stairs.[^1]

Dr. Holden, whom the register describes as the first astronomer at the site, named it for Copernicus.[^1] The lookout sits on University of California land near Lick Observatory on Mount Hamilton, and the observatory is owned and operated by the University of California.[^1][^3] The register lists the California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection's Santa Clara Unit (Cal Fire SCU) as the administering agency, and it was registered on October 26, 2019.[^1] The California lookout list marks it as standing.[^4]

The summit is not open to the public.[^2] SummitPost, a climbing guide, says hiking is the only way up and that the summit is not legally accessible.[^2] It advises visitors to ask the patrol officer at Lick Observatory for permission first.[^2] Treat the summit as closed unless you have that permission. I found no staffing record or recent visit report for the lookout, so its current staffing is unknown.
