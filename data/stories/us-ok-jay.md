Jay's fire tower was a state forestry project, begun in 1957, north of the town in Delaware County.[^1]

Local legislators announced the site in July 1957. The Delaware County Journal, as quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said the tower would give the forestry division coverage from Jay to Ottawa County and to the other towers already in the county.[^1] A committee first chose the town park and hoped the tower would become a tourist attraction. A drop in elevation made that spot unworkable, so the committee tentatively chose a new site north of town, pending division approval.[^1]

Ten Jay business people bought the land and gave half of it to the state for the tower site.[^1] The division planned to develop a public park there in addition to keeping the lookout.[^1]

Surveyors had marked out the tower and building locations by late October 1957. By November the footing holes were being dug, though wet weather slowed the work. Floyd Larmon, the acting district ranger, described the first stages.[^1] The planned height had been about 100 feet.[^1]

By May 1958, district ranger Clessie Ferguson reported the tower at about 60 feet and still going up.[^1]

The state's forestry work had been reorganized in 1937, when the legislature discontinued the Oklahoma Forest Commission and created a Division of Forestry.[^2]

No source I found describes the site after May 1958, so whether the tower still stands, and whether visitors can reach it, is unknown.[^1]
