The Kettle Rock lookout stood in Plumas County, California, inside the Plumas National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administered it.[^1] The first lookout structure went up in 1912.[^1][^2] According to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register, a Plan 4AR building from California Region 5 took the original's place in 1923.[^1] A 1952 newspaper item instead says the first building was reconstructed in 1920, so the sources disagree on that date.[^2]

The register dates the most recent building, a BC-1401 design from California Region 5, to 1953, when it sat on a nine-foot block tower.[^1] The 1952 item says the old structure had been dismantled and a new 14-by-14 building was under construction.[^2] A Peakbagging page gives 1952 for the largely concrete structure, so the sources do not fully agree on when it went up.[^3]

Newspaper notices name people on duty at the station from 1922 to 1959.[^2] R.A. Thompson spent a summer there in 1922.[^2] Bert Terrell, also spelled Terrill, appears as the Plumas National Forest lookout in notices from the 1930s and early 1940s.[^2] In 1952 the Reno Evening Gazette named Mr. and Mrs. Frank G. Clark as that season's lookouts.[^2] Violet Hawson wrote from the station in 1958, and in July 1959 she gave up the post because her husband had fallen ill.[^2] The last notice we found, from that July, names Blanche Le Fluer on duty.[^2]

A Peakbagging page has photos from 2001 and 2009 and says the lookout was in poor shape and looked abandoned.[^3] The register says vandalism was reported in 2012 and places the removal sometime between 2014 and 2017.[^1]

According to the register, a repeater vault has taken over the site.[^1] No source we read describes anything else left on the summit, and none says whether the public can reach it.
