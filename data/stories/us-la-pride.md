The Pride lookout tower stood in East Baton Rouge Parish, in the community of Pride.[^1] TopoQuest places it at about 30.7085 north and 90.9393 west, gives its elevation as 134 feet above mean sea level, and notes that the USGS 1:24K topographic map for Pride, Louisiana shows it.[^2]

Louisiana's lookout towers grew out of fire-protection work. In 1915 money was earmarked for that work, which made it possible to employ lookout watchmen or patrolmen. The first steel fire tower went up near Bogalusa in 1922, and by 1943 thirty-nine towers had gone up.[^1]

Wikipedia's list marks the Pride tower as torn down. The list's note says the tower appears on a 2012 map but not on the 2015 one, and that Google Street View showed it before it was decommissioned.[^1] TopoQuest describes the tower in the present tense, so the two sources disagree.[^2] We follow the list on the tower's status.

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors. Ask whoever owns the land before going there.
