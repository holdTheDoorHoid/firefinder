Pepper Mountain, about 11 miles northeast of Sandy in Multnomah County, Oregon, got a small lookout in 1937.[^1][^2] The register gives a wooden tower 10 feet high, fitted with an L-4 cab, and names the Oregon Department of Forestry as administrator.[^1]

An April 24, 1936 item from The Bonneville Dam Chronicle, quoted on the Weebly page, names Bob Ellis as the man sent to staff the lookout that month, a year before the register's build date.[^3][^1]

Later, the Weebly page lists the site as an Aircraft Warning Service station, activated in November 1942 and inactivated in June 1943.[^3] It says the Forest Service kept the site for fire detection afterward. The register names the Oregon Department of Forestry without a date, so the sources do not agree on who administered the site after 1943.[^3][^1]

The register and firelookout.com both date the lookout's abandonment to 1955 and record it as gone.[^1][^2]

Oregon Hikers' field guide says the summit area offers no views, and that the trees ringing the clearing are about 70 years old.[^4] It also notes a few clumps of daffodils, which point to a lookout operator who enjoyed gardening.[^4]

Oregon Hikers lists a 2.6-mile hike with 700 feet of gain up an abandoned lookout road, starting from a pullout on Brower Road. Its trailhead page warns that the route borders private land and asks hikers to treat neighboring property with respect.[^5]
