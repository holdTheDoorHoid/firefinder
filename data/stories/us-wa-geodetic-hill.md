Geodetic Hill is a summit inside Olympic National Park in Jefferson County, at about 3,018 feet.[^1][^2] The Coast and Geodetic Survey first marked the spot as Spruce in 1913, and WillhiteWeb thinks the Geodetic Hill name reached the map by 1915.[^3]

The register and Firelookout.com both describe a cabin built in the 1930s and abandoned in the 1940s.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb cites a cabin inventory for that 1930s date, but it found no mention of an earlier building in the 1942 papers.[^3]

In 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service took up the site.[^3] A July 31, 1942 memorandum from National Park Superintendent Preston Macy called for a station for yearlong occupancy.[^3] WillhiteWeb reports that the AWS built a 10 by 20 foot cabin of native materials, and one of its photo captions reads "AWS observers at Spruce Lookout."[^3] The same page says a January 1942 list of lookouts is the only item it has found showing the spot as a fire lookout.[^3]

When the war ended, the cabin was marked for abandonment where it stood.[^3] A USGS party in 1954 described it as 12 by 20 feet, which does not match the 10 by 20 feet in the AWS papers.[^3] A 1974 USGS visit recorded that the Park Service had completely burned it around 1972.[^3] The register also gives about 1972 for the fire.[^1]

Nothing standing is described today. The register's photos, dated August 26, 2000, show scattered stove parts and pieces of a wood stove.[^1] WillhiteWeb's photo captions show a flat area where the cabin was located, with a pipe in the ground and wood and bolts among the remains. The page gives no date for these photos.[^3]

The road was cleared to the park boundary in 2020, but a washout blocks the most direct driving route.[^3] From the boundary, the hike along the ridge is about one mile through old-growth forest, mostly cross-country.[^3] The sources we read describe no closure. Nothing remains to climb, so a visit means walking to the flat site and seeing what is left.
