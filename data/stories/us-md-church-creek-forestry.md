The Maryland Forest Service built the Church Creek Forestry Tower in 1932, in Dorchester County, Maryland.[^1] A 1932 notice quoted by Forest Lookouts describes a 120-foot steel tower about two miles south of Church Creek, on a site only 4 feet above sea level.[^3] The notice says the concrete abutments had to be sunk about two feet below sea level, since southwestern Dorchester County has no hills and the site was so low.[^3]

The tower stayed in service for over 40 years.[^2] It was decommissioned around 1978, as fire detection technology advanced.[^2] The register's description, however, says it "is used in emergencies," which does not match that date.[^1] We found no names of the people who staffed it, and no notable fire it watched.

The tower then stood idle for about 40 years, until it was dismantled in 2018 because it had decayed and become unsafe.[^2] DNR saved the top 40 feet, cab included, for restoration.[^2] Under a key agreement with the Maryland Historic Trust, the tower had to be made open to visitors.[^2] By 2024 the state had reduced the tower to 40 feet and renovated it as an educational display.[^1]

The sources do not agree on how far the tower moved. A 2007 state report places the tower 2.2 miles south of the town of Church Creek.[^4] The register places it at the Church Creek Forestry station.[^1] The restored top now stands at the DNR Church Creek Forestry Field Office.[^2] We could not tell whether the tower was moved far or rebuilt near where it stood. The register's map point may not match the state report, so check it before using it for directions.[^1][^4]

Today the field office grounds include a small park with information panels around the tower, plus an accessible parking area.[^2] The tower is less than 200 feet off Golden Hill Road.[^2] The register says the restored tower is not open for climbing, so please do not climb it.[^1] Firelookout.org lists the original Church Creek site as gone, and lists the display tower as standing.[^5]
