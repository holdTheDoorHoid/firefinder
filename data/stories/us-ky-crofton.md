In June 1948 the Kentucky Division of Forestry was putting up a 60-foot fire lookout tower about five and a half miles east of Crofton, a newspaper reported.[^1] The district forester, E.R. Wagoner, announced the work, which was under way that day.[^1]

The concrete base was already in, and the steel beams were going up.[^1] Walter Green, the Division's superintendent of maintenance and construction, was in the county to supervise the job.[^1]

The tower was set on one of the county's highest points, which gave the observer a wide view.[^1] The clipping says that once this second tower in Christian County was finished, observers would be able to watch 85 per cent of the district's forest land.[^1]

The same clipping also mentions a No. 2 tower in the county, which it places in the Pennyrile Park area.[^1] It does not make clear whether that refers to this tower.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists 17 lookouts in Kentucky, and Crofton is not one of them.[^2] No source we read describes what remains at the site today.
