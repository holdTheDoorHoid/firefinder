The lookout at the Sacramento National Wildlife Refuge is a 100-foot Aermotor steel tower with a small 7-by-7-foot observation cab on top.[^1] It was built in 1937 as part of the project to set up the refuge.[^1][^2] The register places the tower in Glenn County.[^1] Wikipedia lists the refuge in Glenn and Colusa Counties, between the towns of Willows and Maxwell.[^4]

In January 1937 the federal government acquired the 10,775-acre Spalding Ranch and named the site the Sacramento Migratory Waterfowl Refuge.[^2] A Civilian Conservation Corps camp housed up to 200 men at the headquarters area, and the men built levees, water control structures and delivery ditches.[^2]

The register notes that several documents describe the structure as a fire lookout tower, but it was likely also used for wildlife observation, as was common at other refuges.[^1] A 1986 survey called it the I-5 Lookout, probably for Interstate 5, which runs just west of the refuge.[^1] The register listed the tower on November 27, 2019.[^1]

A blog post from September 2024 says the tower could easily be seen standing tall behind the buildings from the visitor center parking lot.[^3] Only authorized personnel can get to the tower, which stands behind a gate on the headquarters grounds.[^3] The visitor center was not open during the blogger's visit due to staffing shortages, but the tower can be seen from the refuge's auto route.[^3]

Please treat the tower as closed to the public and do not try to climb it.
