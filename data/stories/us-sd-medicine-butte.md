Medicine Butte, six miles north of Reliance in Lyman County, was picked as a lookout station in July 1942.[^1][^2] A Daily Republic item from that month, quoted on Forest Lookouts, said the view took in all of Lyman County and parts of Stanley, Hughes, Hyde, Brule and Buffalo counties.[^1] The same item said arrangements were being made for someone to watch from it with binoculars around the clock.[^1]

Local farmers were building a telephone line up to the top, and a road already reached the shack on the summit.[^1] Firelookout.com dates the cabin to 1942 and gives the site an elevation of 2,260 feet.[^2] Forest Lookouts files the site under Lyman County and the state, while the Forest Fire Lookout Association list calls it a ground site rather than a tower.[^1][^3]

The cabin is now gone.[^2][^3] The butte stands about 200 feet above the prairie near Reliance, and Wikipedia says the Brulé Sioux regard it as sacred, especially people from the nearby Lower Brule Indian Reservation.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, and none says whether visitors can reach it, so anyone planning a trip should confirm access first.
