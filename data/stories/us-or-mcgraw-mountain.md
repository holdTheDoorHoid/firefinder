McGraw Mountain sits in Wallowa County, Oregon, on the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest, at 6,009 feet by the National Historic Lookout Register's figure.[^1] Fire detection on the site goes back at least to 1930, when a Forest Service lookout patrolman worked there.[^1][^3] The first tower was a 10-foot pole structure topped with a 14x14 cab, built in 1934.[^1][^2]

By 1964 that tower had been declared obsolete and inadequate. The Forest Service announced a replacement house and tower, using prefabricated materials it supplied itself.[^3] A Seattle firm had submitted the lowest bid so far, $5,840, and the new tower was to stand about half a mile from the old one.[^3] The sources disagree on the build year. The NHLR and the Forest Lookouts page date the present tower to 1965, while firelookout.com says 1964.[^1][^2][^3] The present tower is an 82-foot treated timber structure with an R-6 flat-top cab.[^1][^2]

Lookouts staffed the site for decades.[^2][^3] A 1944 notice said Kenneth Blevans would work as lookout there, and firelookout.com lists Larry Leitke in 1971.[^3][^2] In 1967 a local paper reported the station was quite busy because of fires in the district.[^3]

Staffing ended in 1972. Firelookout.com says the tower was staffed until then, saw little use afterward, and is described as presently abandoned.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association still lists McGraw as a standing tower, so the structure appears to remain even though it is no longer in regular use.[^2][^4]

The NHLR registered the tower on July 4, 2022, as US 1633 and OR 151.[^1] The sources read do not settle whether the public can visit or climb it. Check with the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest before planning a trip, and treat the tower as off-limits unless the forest says otherwise.[^2][^1]
