The Marcoot Lookout Station stands near Highway 72 in Reynolds County, inside the Mark Twain National Forest, between Salem and Bunker.[^3][^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1]

A November 1934 newspaper item reported that the foundation was being dug, and that the tower would be finished about a week after the pour.[^2] The same item planned a stairway in place of the ladder used at the Buick and Czar towers, and put the cab floor at ninety-nine feet, nine inches.[^2] A Salem News feature credits Company 732 of the Civilian Conservation Corps, attached to Boss CCC Camp F-1, with building Marcoot along with the Buick, Czar and Black towers.[^3] The sources read do not give a completion date.

The register calls the tower a model MC-39.[^1] A 1939 survey description, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, puts the tower at about 110 feet and describes four legs, a glassed lookout room and a finial on top.[^2] That description does not say where its height is measured, so it cannot be set against the 1934 cab-floor plan with confidence.[^2]

The register added the tower on August 18, 2026.[^1] The sources read do not describe it being abandoned, burned, moved or restored.

The National Park Service lists the site as historically significant to the Trail of Tears National Historic Trail, and names the U.S. Forest Service as its manager.[^4] A Salem News hike feature describes the tower as scenic, and says visitors can park in its area for free.[^3] The same feature places a Trail of Tears sign across the highway from the tower, and mentions a locked gate further west, beside which hikers enter a forest road.[^3] None of the sources read confirms whether the tower can be climbed today, so check with the Forest Service before going up.
