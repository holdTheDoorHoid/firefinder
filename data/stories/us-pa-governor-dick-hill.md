The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists a Governor Dick Hill tower site in Lebanon County, Pennsylvania, and says its wooden tower has come down.[^1] The hill is near Mount Gretna, and a 1994 hiking article puts it at 1,120 feet above sea level.[^2] The name comes from a nickname held by a man who cut wood and burned charcoal for nearby Cornwall Furnace.[^2]

According to the same article, the site's first tower served as a signal station for geodetic surveying.[^2] In 1889 landowner Robert Coleman laid a brief narrow-gauge railroad up to the summit, and it circled the site's first wooden observatory.[^2] Clarence Schock, founder of SICO Corp., later acquired the land.[^2] He opened it to nature lovers and put up four 50-foot wooden towers.[^2]

The towers were often vandalized.[^2] The article says that in 1954 Schock had them taken down, and that he put up a new tower in the old railroad loop, meant to resist vandalism.[^2] That tower is a reinforced concrete and steel structure, and LebTown and UncoveringPA both date it to 1954.[^2][^3][^4] The register's wooden tower may be one of those four, but no source I read says so.[^1][^2]

In 1954 Schock also donated the land to what was then the Mount Joy School District; the district is now called Donegal. In late 1998 the district handed ownership to the SICO Foundation and Lebanon County.[^2]

A 2019 LebTown article describes the hill as Clarence Schock Memorial Park, with 14 miles of trails for hikers and mountain bikers.[^3] The article says trails are open from dawn to dusk, and the observation tower at the top is the park's main attraction.[^3] The hike to the tower covers about 1.5 miles each way.[^4] The Tower House, a summer home the Schocks finished in 1938, came down in 1974.[^3] Only parts of its foundation are left on the trail.[^3] No source I read describes any remains of the wooden towers themselves.
