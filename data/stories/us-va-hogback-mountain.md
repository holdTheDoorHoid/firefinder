Hogback Mountain is the highest peak in the northern part of Shenandoah National Park, and it lies along the border of Warren and Rappahannock counties.[^1][^2]

In the winter of 1942–43, workers from the Civilian Public Service built a fire lookout tower and a cabin on the summit.[^1] The site says the program let conscientious objectors do public work in place of military service, and that this labor eased the park's loss of its Civilian Conservation Corps crews.[^1]

By the late 1950s the park was no longer regularly staffing its five lookout towers.[^3] The site says radio-equipped vehicles let fire control aides do fire detection at the same level the towers had.[^3] The lookout tower on Hogback was removed in 1962.[^1] In the early 1960s the park began taking its lookout towers apart, scrapping them or selling them.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list also marks the Hogback tower in Warren and Rappahannock counties as gone.[^4]

The page also says the old fire watchman's quarters were maintained to hold electronics for communications towers on Hogback Mountain.[^1] The Appalachian Trail passes over the summit, and Skyline Drive gives easy access.[^1][^2]
