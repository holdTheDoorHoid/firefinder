Cascade Peak, nine miles west of Grand Marais, got a steel fire lookout in June 1922.[^1][^2] The Minnesota Forest Service planned it as the first of three towers for the district under Ranger Bayle, and the tallest.[^1] Bayle's crew had been raising the tower when a fire call sent them to Four Mile Creek, and the newspaper said the top moved in a high wind.[^1]

The tower was about 80 feet tall and made of steel.[^1] The county survey calls it a ladder tower with a square cab.[^2] Elizabeth Bachman's history of Minnesota forestry, quoted in a state list, calls it the earliest steel lookout tower, an 80-foot structure with three posts and an open crow's nest.[^3] The sources disagree on that structure, and on the year: the survey gives 1923, a year after the newspaper.[^2][^1]

Bayle's clerk, Mr. Metcalf, climbed after him on the day the tower went up.[^1] In early July 1922 the paper reported that a John Metcalf of the Forestry Department was staying at the lookout, but it does not say whether he is the same clerk.[^1]

A second Cascade, also called New Cascade or the Trout Lake lookout, went up in 1933 as a 100-foot ladder tower. Its hill lay near the lake now called Deer Yard Lake, roughly three miles southwest of the first.[^2][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists its Cascade point as gone.[^4] The Central US Lookouts page ends with the single word "Removed," but gives no date and does not say which tower it means.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, stands at either site today, and none says whether the sites are open to the public.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Sources also disagree on where the towers stood. The Forest Fire Lookout Association point matches the 1933 tower, the county survey places the first one at a different spot, and Wikidata gives a third location.[^4][^2][^3][^5]
