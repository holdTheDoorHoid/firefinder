The Casey Mountain lookout site lies near Cranbrook, in the East Kootenay region of British Columbia.[^1][^2]

The Forest Branch of the provincial Department of Lands listed a Casey Mountain Lookout among its Cranbrook district improvements in 1919, when $960.18 was spent on it.[^1] A June 1919 item from the Creston Review, reproduced on Canada Lookouts, said a new lookout station had been built at Casey Mountain. The paper reported that the view from it ran south as far as Bonners Ferry and east to Elko.[^1] The branch spent another $22.00 on improvements in 1920 and $120.00 on maintenance in 1925.[^1]

The station was staffed. A. Pascuzzo was on duty there on July 30, 1934, and reported a forest fire from that point. The fire was fought at a cost of $1,017. The Victoria Daily Colonist printed the account in March 1935.[^1]

In an October 1944 letter to the Forest Branch News Letter, Ray Monesmith of Casey Mountain Lookout described how he read lightning strikes. Painted grid lines on the window panes, and an eye bolt used as a sight in the centre of the cupola, helped him place strikes on dark, rainy nights, with a flashlight close at hand for the figures.[^1] He recorded sixty fires in 1943, which he called a light year, and eleven of them came from a single thunderstorm.[^1]

A 2026 guide lists a Casey Fire Lookout / Plumbob Mtn, in the Kootenays, among lookouts confirmed to have standing structures or likely to still have unmaintained ones.[^3] The older Canada Lookouts record uses only the name Casey Mountain, and none of the sources read confirms that the two names refer to the same summit.[^1]

Beyond the guide's listing, no source I read describes the site today, gives an access level for it, or says whether the public may visit. Do not climb the tower unless its access is confirmed as public.
