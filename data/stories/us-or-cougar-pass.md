Cougar Pass sits in the Coast Range above Loon Lake, inside the Elliott State Forest, about ten road miles west of Scottsburg.[^1][^4] In December 1935 the Coos Bay Times reported that a new 40-foot tower had been completed there.[^2] The district had been blind to its nearest station, Dean Mountain, because of a high mountain ridge.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1936 as the build year for a 30-foot L-4 wooden tower, so the two accounts do not quite agree.[^2][^3]

The tower that stands today came later.[^3] A 1966 Coos district annual report records a new 50-foot CT3 tower with a 14-by-14 cab that replaced the old one.[^2][^4] The register describes the present 50-foot timber tower with a live-in cab.[^3]

The Coos Fire Patrol staffed the post in the fire season.[^2] A 1940 notice said a Marshfield man had been assigned to it for the rest of that summer, and a 1945 notice announced a new watcher from Coos Bay.[^2]

An Elliott State Forest report says the lookout helped spot and report at least three large fires during its working years, but the part I read does not name them.[^5]

The same report says the tower was active through 1985 and was the last active lookout on the Elliott.[^5] The Education Center's website says it has not been used since 1985.[^1] The register listed it in January 2013.[^3] Its cab is now home only to packrats, according to the register.[^3]

The Cougar Pass Lookout Education Center calls the tower dilapidated, and wants to rebuild it as an education center.[^1] When a lookout-history writer visited on June 16, 2024, the status was listed as abandoned, and the chicken wire that once kept people off the base had been mostly torn down.[^4]

At the bottom of the access road in 2024 there was neither a berm nor a gate.[^4] A 2022 visitor found that the road is state forest road 7700 and noted that only a pole from an old gate remains.[^6]

Sources also disagree on who looks after the site. The register lists the Oregon Department of Forestry/Coos FPA as administrator.[^3] The Education Center says the Department of State Lands manages it for the Oregon Common School Fund.[^1]

I found no official notice about visiting. The Education Center calls the tower unsafe for visitors, so do not climb it.[^1]
