Clingmans Dome is the highest point in Great Smoky Mountains National Park, at 6,643 feet (2,025 m) on the North Carolina and Tennessee line.[^1][^3] The tower on its summit is now called the Kuwohi Observation Tower, formerly the Clingmans Dome Observation Tower.[^1]

A 40-foot (12 m) observation tower already stood on the dome in 1939, when a newspaper item described a young couple choosing it for their wedding.[^2] In October 1958 the lowest bid of $57,000 for a summit observation tower came from the W.C. Norris Construction Co. of Waynesville, N.C.[^2] Bebb and Olson of Knoxville designed the tower that was completed in 1959, and Norris built it.[^3]

The tower is 45 feet (13.7 m) tall and made of concrete.[^4] It climbs to its platform by a 375-foot (114 m) ramp.[^1] It was one of the Mission 66 projects.[^3] Ahead of the Park Service's 1966 50th anniversary, the agency wanted new features and buildings to mark the milestone.[^3] Of the program's many buildings and structures, just nine were observation towers, and this one is among them.[^1]

Time and harsh winters had taken a toll on the tower.[^4] Friends of the Smokies reported that the foundation had settled up to four inches in some spots.[^3] In 2016 the park received a $250,000 grant from the Partners in Preservation program, and it added another $52,000.[^4] Work began in late August 2017 with the entry plaza, moved up the ramp and platform, and officially concluded in August 2018.[^4] Visitors could still use the tower while the work was underway.[^4]

Today the Park Service presents the Kuwohi Observation Tower as a well-known stop in the park.[^1] To reach it, you walk a steep, paved trail of about one mile round trip from the Kuwohi parking area.[^1] The National Register of Historic Places includes the tower.[^1] Clouds and cold are common at the top, and the views come when the weather permits.[^1]
