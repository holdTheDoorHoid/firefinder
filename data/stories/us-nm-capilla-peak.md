Capilla Peak, in the Cibola National Forest near the Manzano Mountains, got its first fire lookout in 1922.[^1][^2][^3] Early newspaper clips spell the name "Capillo Peak."[^2] In May 1922 the Forest Service said it was preparing to give up its Bosque station and put one on the peak instead, and by June a lookout had been posted there.[^2] The first spotters went up on horseback, and pack mules hauled in building materials for the cabin.[^1]

That first building was a small cabin in the L-4 style.[^1][^2] In 1963 the Forest Service replaced it with the steel CL-100 cab that stands there now.[^1][^2] The cab sits on a block base about ten feet high, which was meant as storage space.[^1][^2]

The lookout was staffed during the 1930s.[^2] In June 1934 the Capilla Peak lookout sighted a fire near Chilili, which burned about a hundred acres before it was brought under control.[^2] A 1938 report named Ranger Marvin Johnson and his wife, Colleen, as stationed there.[^2] In August 1940 the station was closed because heavy rains had ended the fire danger in that region.[^2] The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on June 17, 2007.[^1]

It is not open to visitors at the moment.[^3][^4] A Forest Service closure order covers the road from the forest boundary to the lookout, along with the campground.[^3][^4] The order was reported to run until October 31, 2026, while the old University of New Mexico observatory buildings on the summit are taken down.[^4] Please stay out of the closed area, and check with the Mountainair Ranger District before planning any trip. I did not find current staffing information for the lookout.
