Mount Brown Lookout sits at about 7,410 to 7,478 feet above West Glacier, reached by a seven-mile trail, and is the oldest surviving lookout structure in Glacier National Park.[^1][^3] Sources give slightly different construction years: the National Historic Lookout Register and firelookout.com say 1929, while Wikipedia's infobox gives 1928.[^1][^2][^3] It is one of three Glacier lookouts built with a short hip roof rather than the taller pyramidal roof used at nearby Apgar and Loneman, and like those towers it follows a standard U.S. Forest Service cab design that the Park Service adapted to watch over its own backcountry.[^1][^2]

The clearest record of staffing comes from a National Park Service photo caption: from 1949 to 1951, the lookout was staffed by a married couple, Raymond and Marie Matheny.[^1] Firelookout.com reports that Mount Brown was staffed through 1971, after which fire detection shifted away from fixed lookouts in the park.[^3]

The lookout was added to the National Register of Historic Places on December 16, 1986, and registered with the National Historic Lookout Register in 1991 as US 37.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com reports it was rehabilitated in 1999.[^3] In 2017, when the Sprague Fire burned through the area, the Park Service wrapped the structure in fire-resistant material to help it survive; a Wikipedia photo caption confirms this protective wrap was installed, though none of the sources consulted describe the fire's outcome for the building in detail.[^2]

Mount Brown still stands today under National Park Service administration. It is not staffed and, as with Glacier's other historic lookouts, nothing in the sources consulted suggests the public may enter or stay overnight in it; it is best appreciated as a destination to view at the end of the trail.
