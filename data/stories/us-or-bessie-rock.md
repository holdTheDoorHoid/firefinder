The Bessie Rock lookout stood on a rocky summit in Jackson County, Oregon, within the Rogue River National Forest, at about 5,953 feet.[^1][^2] A 1953 newspaper item places it about 10 miles due east of Prospect.[^3] The U.S. Forest Service administered it.[^1]

The former-lookout register and firelookout date it to 1931, when it went up as an enclosed six-foot base with an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] Newspaper reports from those years fit that timeline.[^3] In October 1931 the Medford Mail Tribune reported that Jack Grow was working on the station, and a January 1932 article described it as one of six new lookout and fireman stations.[^3]

That article said the station sat at the top of an 85-foot ladder, and that supplies were hauled six miles by mule from the Imnaha ranger station and then packed up the ladder.[^3] A 1941 account describes a 96-rung ladder.[^3]

In 1941 the lookout was staffed for 105 days, and communications ran through the Columbia Utilities Company to the Butte Falls Ranger Station.[^3] That summer Roy G. Pursel was the lookout.[^3] In August 1953 a 17-year-old fire spotter at the lookout needed an emergency appendectomy, and he had to be carried down 3.5 miles of trail to an ambulance.[^3]

The former-lookout register says the lookout was removed in 1969. Firelookout and Forest Lookouts say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3] The sources differ on that one word.

No source we read describes what, if anything, is left on the summit today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2] A 2015 review of the Geyser Springs Trail says the old telephone line and much of the trail up to the lookout were abandoned in the 1960s.[^4] That is a visitor's account rather than an agency record, so treat the date with care.
