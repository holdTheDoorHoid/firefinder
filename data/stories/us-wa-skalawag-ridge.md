Skalawag Ridge sits in Ferry County, Washington, within the Colville National Forest.[^2] Forest Service maps from 1928 to 1943 use the spelling Skalawag Ridge, and from 1952 onward they switch to Scalawag.[^2]

A 35-foot pole platform with a log cabin went up in 1930.[^1][^2][^3] In 1934 a taller pole tower replaced it, about half a mile southeast of the summit.[^2][^3] Sources disagree on the new tower's size. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com give 60 feet with a 7-by-7-foot cab, while WillhiteWeb gives 40 feet with a 12-by-12-foot cab.[^1][^2][^3] A crew led by E.E. Sizemore finished the tower by mid-June 1934.[^1][^4] Panoramic shots were made from the roof of the new tower on September 29, 1934.[^1][^2]

In November 1934, crews from the Growden CCC camp began building the Skalawag Ridge road, which was to run from the South Fork Sherman Creek road up to the tower.[^4] The site was given up in the 1950s.[^1][^2] A second pole platform stood two miles northwest, and may still have been there in the 1980s.[^1]

A 2017 visit report describes the site as overgrown, though concrete footings and scattered lookout pieces remain.[^2] A gate now blocks the former jeep road, so the walk in is about two miles.[^2] The U.S. Forest Service administers the land.[^2]
