The Red Hill (Manker) Lookout is in San Bernardino County.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register places it in the City of Rancho Cucamonga, while a 1937 newspaper notice puts it northeast of Upland.[^1][^2]

The San Bernardino National Forest built the lookout under Forest Supervisor William V. Jones.[^1] The Register says it opened on July 17, 1937, and that a 1936 newspaper estimated its cost at $3,000.[^1] Its unusual look came from a wish to fit the surroundings.[^1] The original plans kept the living quarters apart, so visitors would not walk through them.[^1] A 1937 newspaper called it one of the most unusual structures of its type in the country, built to fit the rules for the restricted building area where it stands.[^2]

The Register says Conrad C. Tindell was the first lookout assigned to the post.[^1] The lookout served the forests until the 1960s, when the neighborhood had grown up around it, and the Register says air pollution there likely helped lead to its closure.[^1] CBS LA, citing staff at the Cooper Museum, says crews staffed it and watched for enemy planes during World War II.[^4] I did not find a source that names a particular fire it watched.

In November 1939 the lookout was renamed Manker Lookout to honor Fletcher Manker, a local fire chief who was also active in watershed conservation.[^1][^2] CBS LA gives 1928 as the build year, which does not match the 1937 opening.[^4] The sources do not settle that difference.

The Register says a private party bought the property and rented it as a residence and art studio.[^1] The family that rented it bought it in 1975 and later added to the structure in 1982.[^1] Peakbagging also gives 1975 for the private purchase.[^3] CBS LA tells the story differently. It says the county sold the facility after it was taken out of service, and that a family moved in during 1966.[^4] The sources do not agree on the order of events.

The Register describes the site as a private residence where tours and visits are not allowed.[^1] Peakbagging says the tower was altered to serve as a comfortable home.[^3] Treat it as a private home, and do not seek a visit.
