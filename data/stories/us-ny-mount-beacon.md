The Mount Beacon fire tower stands on the summit of South Beacon Mountain in Beacon, New York.[^2] Before it existed, the site was an observer's post with no tower at all.[^1] The NHLR describes an observer who walked a quarter mile to a tree at the end of the phone line, then climbed it to dial out on a temporary telephone.[^1] In 1931 a steel tower was put up where a wooden one had stood. It is an Aermotor LS-40, 60 feet tall, with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1][^4]

Fire observation at the site ran from 1932 until the early 1970s, when aircraft took over that role.[^3] The last fire ranger was assigned there in 1972.[^1] The sources do not agree on when it was finally retired. The NHLR says the tower was decommissioned in 1987, while Historic Structures says it went back into service briefly and was retired for good around 1975.[^1][^3] A local restoration group says the state used it for five decades.[^4]

After the state surplused the tower, no buyer came forward, so it stayed a DEC tower on City of Beacon land.[^1] Afterward, hikers could climb its 72 stairs.[^1] In 2003 interest in restoring it was first raised, and a formal organizing meeting with NYSDEC and the City of Beacon took place that year.[^3][^1] The tower joined the National Historic Lookout Register in 2005, and it was added to the National Register of Historic Places on October 23, 2009.[^1][^2]

Replacement steps were installed in the summer of 2008.[^1] Later, the cross bracing, cabin walls, handrails and paint were finished with help from core volunteers, the Hudson Valley Four Wheeler Association and the New York Army National Guard.[^1] The tower reopened with a ceremony on June 22, 2013.[^1]

Today Scenic Hudson presents the 1931 tower as restored and describes the climb as a strenuous hike, less than a mile from the North Mount Beacon summit.[^5] The restoration work is listed as completed.[^3] Check conditions before you go.
