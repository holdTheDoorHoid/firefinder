County Forester Frank Dunne announced in February 1934 that Mount Solomon, south of Santa Maria, would get a fire lookout once the building money came through.[^1] The county took a ten-year lease on a 200-by-200-foot piece of the summit from Union Oil for the work.[^1] Newspapers did not agree on who built the station.[^1] An April 1934 report said the federal government would build it, and a June report said the state built it under federal forest supervision.[^1] By late June the station was about 95 percent finished and due for use by July 15.[^1] Its fire finder was an Osborn allidade, which the paper called the newest type.[^1]

Reports also disagree on height.[^1] A 1934 item put the station at about 2,000 feet.[^1] A 1948 article gave the mountain as 1,336 feet above the ocean, and a peak table lists the lookout at 1,344 feet.[^1][^3]

Bard Davison, a former county fire patrolman, was the lookout named for the new station, and his family moved up to it at the end of June 1934.[^1] Dan Curryer was appointed in June 1936, and Ed Graff succeeded him that August.[^1] In 1939 Charles Barnes was appointed for the season and cleaned and repainted the station, which the paper described as the county's sole lookout in the north of Santa Barbara County.[^1] By December 1948 Bessie Hampton had worked four summers there, keeping a round-the-clock watch from about May 15 to November and flagging suspect smoke to the fire warden.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^2] The peak table marks the summit with an LO site label, which its key defines as a place where the building has been removed.[^3]

A Santa Maria Times feature, which we could not date, calls the hill the antenna-crowned Mt. Solomon and says it later held oil facilities.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, of the lookout is left on the summit, or whether the top is open to visitors.
