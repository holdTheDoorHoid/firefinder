Mount Diablo Lookout is a sandstone summit building in Mount Diablo State Park, in Contra Costa County, California, at 3,849 feet.[^1] The register says the stone was quarried in the park, and that the building was meant from the start to hold a viewing deck, museum space, a fire lookout, and a rotating aircraft beacon.[^1]

Newspapers reported proposals for a fire lookout on the summit from 1923 to 1926.[^2] Public money for a summit facility was scarce during the Depression, but federal work programs, including the W.P.A. and the Civilian Conservation Corps, offered an alternative.[^3]

The register gives 1938 as the build year, when CCC work on the building began.[^1] State parks says the lookout portion was finished in 1941 and the rest in 1942.[^4] The register also says the building leaked badly and was repaired several times.[^1]

The register says lookouts, often a married couple, occupied the top floor and lived in the basement.[^1] It notes that several park rangers and some books do not mention that use, and that the account rests on a retired ranger who started in 1975.[^1] A July 1931 newspaper excerpt says a fire that climbed the peak's south slope nearly destroyed the lookout station, so a station already stood on the peak by then. The sources do not describe it.[^2]

Today the lookout area holds radio repeater equipment and is not accessible.[^1] The building serves as the park's museum and visitor center.[^4] The register added the lookout on August 1, 2019, on a nomination by Mike Guerin.[^1]

Many visitors go straight to the Summit Museum for the view, and the park road passes CCC-built picnic sites on the way up.[^5][^4] Because the lookout use is documented mainly by one ranger's account, treat that part as a lead rather than settled history. Check the park's current hours before visiting.
