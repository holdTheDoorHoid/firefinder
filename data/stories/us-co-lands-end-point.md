Lands End Point is the name the Forest Lookouts site uses for a stone lookout and shelter house at the western tip of Grand Mesa, in Mesa County, Colorado.[^1] Wikipedia calls the place a ranger observatory on the rim of the mesa.[^2] The National Register nomination also gives the alternate names Land's End Shelter House, Land's End Visitor Center and Land's End Concession Building.[^3]

A photo caption dated August 12, 1937 describes the lookout and shelter house as built of local lava rock.[^1] The Forest Service and the Works Progress Administration built the observatory in 1936-37, with help from two Civilian Conservation Corps side camps.[^2][^3] The nomination says the observatory went up after the Land's End Road was finished, and that a local stonemason, Ivan Anderson, oversaw the work.[^3] Wikipedia says its basalt stones were taken from the mesa itself.[^2]

Each summer in the 1940s and 1950s, concession operators stayed in the apartment, where they sold sandwiches and film and hauled water.[^3] Forest Service staff managed it as a visitor center off and on after that, but did not stay in the apartment, probably because of the lack of water.[^3]

The site was added to the National Register of Historic Places on February 28, 1997.[^2][^3] According to the nomination, the observatory had been used without interruption since it was built.[^3]

Wikipedia says the observatory building was closed and boarded up as of 2022.[^2] Outside it, a large viewing area offers a panoramic look across western Colorado and eastern Utah.[^2] Land's End Road, roughly half paved and half gravel, connects the site to the Grand Mesa Scenic and Historic Byway.[^2]
