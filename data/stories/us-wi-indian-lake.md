The Indian Lake fire tower stood in Oneida County, Wisconsin, at an elevation of 1,721 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 100-foot Aermotor tower with a US Forest Service-style stairway, built in 1936.[^1]

Campers from a nearby girls' camp climbed it every summer as a rite of passage, and runners came to use its steps as a workout.[^2]

A 2017 article says the DNR had decided to decommission its fire towers about a year and a half before the removal.[^2] WJFW reports that 72 aging towers still in use were decommissioned in 2015.[^3] The Indian Lake tower was sold right away and was one of the first taken down.[^2]

A family from New Richmond, Wisconsin, bought it for $130 and took it down over two days in June 2017.[^2] They unbolted the top two-thirds and used a crane to lift that section off and set it in the road.[^2] After re-rigging, they lowered the rest of the tower onto a flatbed truck, where the lower section was marked and taken apart for the trip.[^2] The family planned to reassemble the lower section to match the original and reattach the top section on its property in New Richmond.[^2] The sources do not confirm that the work was finished.

A 2022 DNR inventory of the state's towers, summarized in the Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2025 Wisconsin report, counted 39 conveyed to private landowners and 19 demolished.[^4]

The Wisconsin lookouts list marks the Indian Lake site as gone.[^5] The 2017 article calls the empty spot a hole in the Indian Lake skyline.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site, or whether it is open to the public.
