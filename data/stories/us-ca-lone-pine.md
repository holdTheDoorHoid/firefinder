The Lone Pine lookout stood in Butte County, in the Magalla ranger district.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates it to 1920, while the National Historic Lookout Register says a lookout stood there as early as 1924.[^1][^3] It was a 7x7-foot observation-only cabin on a 40-foot open steel tower.[^1]

In September 1937 the lookout, J. Howell, was testing a Forest Service radio set when he picked up a call from a stranded enrollee.[^2] He passed word to other stations, and a truck was sent to fetch the young man.[^2] In May 1946 a newspaper reported that Lone Pine was the only lookout in the Magalla district that had been manned so far.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says nothing remains at the original site today.[^1] The tower itself survived.[^3] In 1956 the state fire agency moved the CCC-built tower and its house to Platte Mountain in Butte County.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower was extended and its cab remodeled to accommodate a catwalk.[^3] The older entry suggested the cabin might have gone to Pratt Mountain, but the National Historic Lookout Register's dated account of a move to Platte Mountain is more specific.[^1][^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register lists the Platte Mountain site as private land under a lease, so a visit would need the landowner's permission.[^3] A 2017 photo in the same entry shows the tower at that location.[^3]
