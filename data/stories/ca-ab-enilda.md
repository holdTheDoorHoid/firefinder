Enilda is one of Alberta's wildfire lookouts, and its name appears on the Alberta list of the Canada Lookouts website.[^1] The province's map shows 127 such sites across the boreal forest and along the Rocky Mountains.[^2][^3]

Alberta's boreal-forest towers were built on high hills, raising the observer above anything blocking the view.[^4] The province says its lookouts sit where the view suits spotting and reporting wildfires.[^2]

Each site has an observer on duty to watch for fire through the day, usually April to October.[^3] Alberta describes its sites as isolated, and staff are expected to prepare for surprises such as a curious bear.[^5]

Public sources describe the wider network, but we found none on this tower's own history.
