The George Washington tower went up in 1934 inside the George Washington State Forest, on the top of an unnamed knoll at the end of the park's old entrance road.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history site quotes the Department of Agriculture and Conservation's first annual report, which says the tower was built with federal money under a work-project program.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes it as a 65-foot steel tower.[^1]

It did not stay on that knoll.[^1] A 1937 study, recorded in the department's third annual report, found that just four of the existing towers were worth keeping and said this one would move to Escoheag Hill.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was moved there in 1938.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1937 as the year of the move and 1938 as the year it was rebuilt at its present location.[^3] The two sources disagree about when the move happened. They also disagree about height. The National Historic Lookout Register lists the Escoheag Hill tower as an 80-foot modified Aermotor that it traces to the George Washington forest, while the Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 65 feet.[^1][^3] The two figures are not reconciled.

A Newport Mercury report dated March 21, 1952, says forest service staff preparing the towers for service found a break-in at the tower on Escoheag Hill. The intruder had taken a stove, a generator, a telephone and other equipment, and a heavy map table had been hurled out a window and broken on the ground below.[^4] The Forest Lookouts site quotes that Newport Mercury report.[^4]

The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower has not operated as a lookout since about 1990.[^3] At some point after 2009, vandals burned its wooden cab, a non-standard design.[^3] The blaze wiped out most of the upper landings and damaged the upper stairs.[^3]

Today, the register says the structure is not maintained, and it names the Rhode Island Department of Environmental Management as administrator.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Escoheag Hill tower as standing, with its cab no longer in place.[^5] The sister register's separate Escoheag Tower Site listing notes that the site has some abandoned remains of the tower.[^6]

At the original knoll, no source describes what, if anything, remains. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Rhode Island list marks the George Washington site as gone.[^5]

None of the sources describes public access to either place.
