Lava Butte is a cinder cone in central Oregon, about nine miles south-southwest of Bend.[^1][^2] The Forest Service built the first fire lookout on its summit in 1931, an L-4 groundhouse.[^1][^2][^3] In early 1933 a steep, single-lane road was built up the cone to the summit.[^1]

Sources differ on the years that followed. The register says the 1957 replacement had a slant-window flat-top cab with a visitor center downstairs.[^3] Firelookout.com describes a two-story flat cab that year and dates the first-floor visitor-center expansion to 1960.[^2] Wikipedia says the 1957 lookout was enlarged in 1962 to hold a small visitor center and museum.[^1]

That structure came down in 1997 to clear the way for a new lookout and visitor center.[^2] The current tower, the third on the site, went up in 1998 as a gable-roofed L-4 reproduction with a catwalk.[^3][^1][^2]

The register listed the lookout on September 2, 2003, as its Number 100, and noted that Oregon became the first state to reach 100 stations.[^3] The register also says it is among the most visited lookouts in the country.[^3] Firelookout.com also says Lava Butte has been the first place to report more fires on the Deschutes than all other lookouts there.[^2]

None of the three pages says who staffs the tower or whether anyone is posted there now.[^1][^2][^3]

Today the register describes the station as a visitor center for the Lavalands National Monument, while Wikipedia places the butte within the Newberry National Volcanic Monument.[^3][^1] Between early May and mid-October, a shuttle takes visitors from the Lava Lands Visitor Center up Red Cinder Road to the lookout.[^1] Hiking the road is an option all year, and a rim trail about 0.35 miles long circles the top.[^1] Check the shuttle dates with the Forest Service before you go, since the dates here come from an encyclopedia.
