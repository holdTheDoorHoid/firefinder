Arrastra Mountain stands about 10 miles northwest of Lincoln, in Powell County, Montana, at an elevation of 7,976 feet.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list gives the site's type as "Ground."[^3]

In July 1930 a Missoula newspaper reported that a new lookout station had been located near the source of Arrastra Creek, about 8,000 feet up.[^2] The station was meant to serve land the forest had taken on two years earlier, and to give the observer a view of three creek drainages.[^2] A nine-mile telephone line was to be strung to the site first, and only then was the lookout to take up his post.[^2] The association describes the structure as an L-4 cab erected in 1930.[^1]

The 1930 newspaper placed the land within the Missoula forest.[^2] The association lists the site under the Lolo and Helena National Forests, and another listing puts it in the Helena National Forest.[^1][^2]

In July 1948 the Independent Record reported that a plane under contract that year was to stand in for the lookout formerly on Arrastra peak.[^2] The supervisor said that getting supplies to remote lookouts had grown hard, and that air detection sometimes cost less than manning towers.[^2] The association puts the abandonment no later than 1956, and says the removal date is unclear.[^1] The two accounts do not line up neatly.[^1][^2] The 1948 report describes a plan, and 1956 is only the latest year the association gives for abandonment.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today.[^1][^3] We found no information about public access to the site.
