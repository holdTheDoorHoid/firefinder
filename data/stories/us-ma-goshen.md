The Goshen tower on Moores Hill was built in 1952 and sits within what is now the D.A.R. State Forest.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower closed the final large hole in the state's spotting coverage, on the eastern side of the Berkshires.[^1] I could not confirm the tower's height or cab design from the pages I read.

The register marks staffing as sometimes, and notes the tower is staffed seasonally.[^1] In 2023 the Daily Hampshire Gazette reported that the state's Department of Conservation and Recreation aimed to hire 46 seasonal lookouts, enough to staff 22 to 24 towers at a time, with Goshen among the key towers to be staffed.[^2] I found no specific fire in the sources that the tower is credited with spotting.

The tower is still kept up.[^1][^4] The register's comments, which sit beside an April 2016 photo, call it well maintained.[^1] A September 2020 trip report describes a DCR crew making repairs on it, and says visitors can climb it.[^4] The register also says the forest was mostly closed for renovations on the Highland Lake Dam, though the road to the tower can be reached by a back way.[^1] Those comments carry no date of their own, so they may be out of date.

Today the tower stands in a public state forest, and the state's own page points hikers up to it.[^3][^5] Wikipedia notes that it gives views across the Connecticut River Valley.[^3] Check the current DCR notices before you go, since the dam work may have changed the roads.
