Bonanza King is a wooden lookout on the summit of Bonanza King Mountain, in Trinity County, northeast of Weaverville.[^1][^2] The register gives the elevation as 6,956 feet and dates the lookout to 1934, when it was built to California Region 5 Plan 4AR. The register adds that the design was used very late in its life.[^1] A 1953 Coast and Geodetic Survey description gives the lookout house as wooden, about 16 feet square and 20 feet high, on a concrete and rock foundation on the extreme eastern rim, which is also the mountain's highest point.[^2] During World War II a residence cabin for the Aircraft Warning Service was added, which allowed round-the-clock staffing.[^1]

The sources do not say what stood on the peak before the 1934 structure. A 1924 newspaper item already refers to a "Bonanza King lookout station."[^2]

The newspaper record is patchy but telling. In July 1929 a lookout was searching for a fire with binoculars when he slipped on a rock and fell about ten feet.[^2] He died in Dunsmuir in early August.[^2] In the fall of 1941, Harry Churchman, the lookout, spotted a smoke plume 78 miles from his station and placed it on the Bogard District of the Lassen forest.[^2] Staffing passed between several lookouts.[^2] June Osborne opened the lookout on May 20, 1954, and by mid-June Jeannie Dickson had the post.[^2] Betty Carlson said she had been a lookout there every fire season since 1962, and a Forest Service fire officer quoted in a 1987 newspaper piece ranked her among the best the Shasta-Trinity forest has.[^2]

A November 2009 visit recorded in the register found the lookout in good shape, with fresh paint on the deck, though the support buildings still needed paint.[^1] The visitor also reported encroaching electronic sites, best guessed to be an agency repeater bunker and antennas.[^1] The register recorded the lookout on January 3, 2015, as US 1056, CA 101, after a nomination by Mark Basque.[^1]

The summit is reached by a road that runs about ten miles in from Highway 3.[^1] The 2009 visit found it passable in 2WD for most of the way, with 4WD needed in the wet season and on the last half mile.[^1] A road guide disagrees about the vehicle.[^3] It describes the summit road as gravel and rocky, sometimes tippy and bumpy, says it needs 4WD, and gives a closure from October 30 to May 1.[^3] The same guide says the lookout is usually staffed in fire season, and it asks visitors not to go up when thunderstorms are in the area.[^3] The 2009 visitor found the lookout closed for the season when they stopped by.[^1] Check with the Shasta-Trinity office on current access and season dates before you go.
