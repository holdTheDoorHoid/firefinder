Remer had two fire lookouts in the same area, about two miles apart, southwest of town off Highway 200.[^1] The first, likely an 81-foot ladder tower, was probably relocated to the Blueberry site once the second went up.[^1] The second went up in 1938 as a 100-foot Aermotor stairway tower.[^1][^2]

The Remer Ranger District, set up as an administrative unit in 1935, was headquartered at a station in town.[^3] After the Chippewa National Forest grew southward, this tower became a primary U.S. Forest Service lookout, and the Lima lookout to its southeast was eliminated.[^1]

The station typically had three full-time employees.[^3] A former assistant ranger who worked there from 1959 to 1961 recalled that two "tower men" staffed the lookouts in the warmer months.[^3] When fire danger ran high, lookouts covered two towers and kept up regular radio contact with the district office.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Minnesota list marks this tower as gone, and it marks the first Remer lookout as gone too.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
