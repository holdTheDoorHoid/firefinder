In February 1923 the L'Anse Sentinel reported that the Michigan state conservation department would erect a fire tower at Summit that spring.[^1] The paper said it would be 85 feet tall and called it the highest fire tower in northern Michigan.[^1] The paper added that a capable fire warden would oversee it through the fire season, watching the Huron, Pesheka, Sturgeon and Silver river timber districts.[^1]

The 1923 report does not name the hill beyond "Summit."[^1] A federal survey made in 1955 has a station named Cliff on the summit of Summit Mountain, about four miles south-southwest of Negaunee.[^2] Matching "Summit" to Summit Mountain is my inference; neither source makes that link. The survey placed the station at the center of a lookout tower, a four-legged frame about 90 feet high with an enclosed metal cabin at the top.[^2]

The 1961 recovery note still refers to the lookout tower and says one reference mark could not be sighted from the ground or the top of the tower.[^2] That 1961 entry is the last in the survey's history log.[^2]

No source I read describes what, if anything, stands on the summit now, or whether the site is open to visitors.
