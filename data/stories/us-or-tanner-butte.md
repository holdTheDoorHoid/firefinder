Tanner Butte is in Hood River County, in the Mount Hood National Forest.[^1] The first set-up there, in 1927, was a camp with a telephone, which the register calls a "tent rag camp."[^1] An L-4 cab replaced it in 1931.[^1][^2]

In October 1931 an item quoted from Six Twenty-Six reported that the summit now had a new house.[^3] Lookouts also knew the peak as Bughouse Butte, and the writer praised Tom Brown's rock work, carpentry and painting.[^3]

In August 1937 the Bonneville Dam Chronicle reported that a new Forest Service phone line from Eagle Creek Station would be finished within three weeks.[^3] The line was to connect Tanner Butte with five other stations, including Larch Mountain and Nesmith Point, through Eagle Creek.[^3]

The register says the cab was removed in 1955.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2]

A SummitPost user report describes evidence that a fire lookout once stood on the summit, but says only a few bits of glass and metal are left.[^5] No source describes a structure standing there today.

The Oregon Hikers guide describes a 360-degree view from the top that takes in five Cascade volcanoes.[^4] One route starts at the Wahclella Falls trailhead and is a minimum 18-mile round trip out and back.[^4] The same guide says the older Tanner Creek Road, which gave closer access, was closed by the Forest Service because of misuse, including illegal dumping and target shooting.[^4]

The Forest Service trail page lists Tanner Butte Trail #401 as closed because of unstable conditions after the 2017 Eagle Creek Fire, with restoration still underway.[^6] The Oregon Hikers guide also labels the hike "Closed."[^4] Check the current closure before planning a trip.
