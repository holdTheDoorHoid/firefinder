The Brittain lookout stood on a logged-off hill in Grays Harbor County, Washington.[^2][^3] The sources differ on its distance from Aberdeen. Firelookout.com gives 20 miles north,[^2] while a benchmark description quoted by Forest Lookouts gives about 19 miles by airline.[^4] Its story starts with a move. The 1946 annual report of the Forestry Division says an abandoned lookout tower was moved that year from Hobi Peak, near Artic, to Brittain Hill on the Humptulips, where it added protection for a large area of second-growth timber.[^3]

The sources do not agree on its size. The 1946 report describes a 14-by-14-foot tower.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register describes a two-story cabin, ten feet square, and records that it had disappeared by 1963.[^1] A 1953 benchmark description gives the lookout house as 10 by 10 feet at the base and about 20 feet high.[^3]

A June 13, 1957 item from The Daily Chronicle, quoted on Forest Lookouts, reports that Muriel Bodey of Hoquiam was beginning her fifth season there as lookout.[^4] It counts Brittain as the fourth Grays Harbor County lookout to begin operating.[^4]

Willhite quotes a 1970 USGS party that found the benchmark destroyed and said the lookout house had come down several years before.[^3]

Willhite visited the summit later.[^3] A pre-commercial thinning in 2016 had left fallen young trees over the summit area, and he found it nearly impossible to find any foundation.[^3] He did find a directional benchmark under the debris.[^3] His note says the land was Rayonier Timberlands in 2017 and needed an access permit.[^3] His route turns onto the East Humptulips Road from Highway 101, then parks at a white gate about 6.6 miles along. Most of the road route follows an old railroad grade.[^3]
