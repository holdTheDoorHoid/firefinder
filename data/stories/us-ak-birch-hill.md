A Bureau of Land Management fire tower was on the summit of Birch Hill, near Fairbanks, Alaska.[^1] A 1979 University of Alaska study of the hill's park describes it as one of only two such towers in the Fairbanks Fire District, and says it offers a commanding view of the Fairbanks basin.[^1]

The Bureau's Alaska fire report for 1970 lists Murphy Dome and Birch Hill as the two lookout towers in use near Fairbanks that year.[^2] The same report says the lack of roads, housing and labor made erecting and manning towers in outlying areas almost insurmountably difficult.[^2] The 1970 report does not say that its Birch Hill tower is the same one the 1979 study describes, though both name Birch Hill and the Bureau.

The 1979 study reports that the federal government was not planning to use the tower.[^1] It also says the Bureau would not allow public access, citing worries about liability or injury.[^1] The authors suggested the tower could be released back to the military, which could then lease it to the borough for Parks and Recreation use, and recommended careful consideration of that option.[^1]

Today the Birch Hill Recreation Area is a public ski and trail area.[^3] The borough's Parks and Recreation Department makes it available for year-round public use through a partnership with the Nordic Ski Club of Fairbanks.[^3] No source describes the Bureau's tower today, so it is not known whether it still stands, or whether it was handed to the borough or the military. Visitors should not assume the tower itself is open.
