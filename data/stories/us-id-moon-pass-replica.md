MoonPass Lookouts sits on 55 forested acres just outside Wallace, Idaho.[^1][^3] It is a rental property built in recent years, not a restored historic site.[^2][^3]

The owner, Kristie Mae Wolfe, is a Boise entrepreneur who had already built a following on Airbnb for whimsical rentals by 2017.[^2] In 2017 she bought a retired lookout on private land in northern Idaho, fixed it up as a rental, and later expanded the business into MoonPass Lookouts.[^2] Wolfe built the towers almost entirely by herself, replicating earlier lookout structures before adding saunas and see-through ceilings.[^3]

The Full Moon Tower is one of the towers listed for rent.[^4] It stands 30 feet up, with a one-room cab that has wraparound windows.[^4] Seattle Met describes the cabin as glass on all sides.[^3] Inside are a queen bed, a wood stove and a propane burner for cooking, and a skylight is there for stargazing.[^4][^2] The listing warns that the stairs are steep and that phones are mostly useless.[^4] A second tower, Half Moon, is listed separately.[^5]

Sources disagree on how many towers there are. TIME counted two newly built towers in March 2026 and said three more were planned.[^2] Seattle Met counted three standing in September, with two more to come.[^3] The property's own overview says five.[^1]

At ground level, the owner's site says each tower has its own wood-fired sauna and a dry-flush toilet.[^6] TIME instead describes a bathroom and sauna in a separate cabin.[^2] TIME also says guests may have to haul in firewood and water.[^2]

The 55 acres are surrounded by Forest Service land, and the property has two forest trails and a swimming hole.[^4] The rental is managed by a private owner, and the owner's site says there is no front desk.[^5][^6] In winter the Forest Service closes the road when the first big snow hits, and guests can pay for a snow-vehicle shuttle.[^4]

Do not confuse this with the Moon Pass road, a route maintained by Shoshone County public works that runs from Wallace toward Avery.[^7]
