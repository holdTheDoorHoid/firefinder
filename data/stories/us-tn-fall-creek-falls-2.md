The Fall Creek Falls Fire Lookout Tower stands on Bradden Knob in Fall Creek Falls State Park, in Bledsoe County, Tennessee.[^1][^3] It began somewhere else.[^3] The National Register nomination dates the original steel tower to about 1895 and says it was used as a tourist tower at the Chickamauga Battlefield before its later work as a fire tower.[^2][^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1865 instead and ties the tower to the battlefield's dedication.[^1] The sources disagree, and the 1895 date is the one the nomination and the Forest Lookouts page share.[^2][^4]

The nomination describes an eighty-foot skeletal steel tower on four angle-section legs set on concrete footers, capped by a square cab with a ribbon of windows.[^2] The park's plan says it was first built at the Chickamauga battlefield, then disassembled and reassembled on Bradden's Knob, where it served as a fire tower for a number of years.[^3]

The move date is also disputed.[^1][^2][^4] The nomination says the Civilian Conservation Corps moved the tower to its present site in 1941.[^2] The register gives 1943,[^1] and the Forest Lookouts page describes a 1941 conversion of an existing 80-foot observation tower in the eastern part of the park.[^4]

None of these sources name the lookout operators or the fires the tower watched.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The nomination lists a lookout operator's cabin, a crew house, two sheds and two privies on the site.[^2] The park's plan adds a lookout house at the foot of the tower, built around 1950.[^3]

The nomination was listed on the National Register on July 21, 2015, and at that time it called the tower in overall good condition.[^2] The park's 2023 facility inventory rates the tower Fair, but a condition table in the same plan rates it Poor and says the structure has suffered extreme damage and needs repairs.[^3]

The plan calls the tower a popular interpretive location.[^3] It also says a plan is needed to address structural concerns and to make the tower safe for guests to visit.[^3]

The plan says access to the tower itself is discouraged and that it needs extensive repairs.[^3] We could not confirm whether the public can climb it today, so check with Tennessee State Parks before a visit, and do not climb the tower without permission.[^3]
