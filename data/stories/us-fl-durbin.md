The Eastern US Lookouts listing says a 100-foot (about 30.5 m) steel tower with a 7x7 steel cab went up at Durbin in 1953.[^1] A 2011 St. Augustine Record report gives the build year as 1954 and the height as 121 feet (about 37 m), so the two sources disagree on both points.[^2]

The Record calls it the Florida Division of Forestry's Durbin Tower and says it was put up to help spot forest fires. Newer detection methods, from radar and cell phones to satellites and aircraft, had made it obsolete.[^2]

Bidding on the sale began January 5, 2011, and the listing says the tower sold for $255.00.[^1] By the time the Record reported on it, the tower had been partly taken down by crane and was in four sections. The bottom 20 feet had gone to a Tennessee landowner, who wanted to extend an 80-foot tower with it, and bids had come in for the rest.[^2]

As of that 2011 report, the tower no longer stood whole.[^2] No source I could read describes what, if anything, is left at the site on U.S. 1 near County Road 210, or whether the public can get in.

[^1]: Durbin - FOREST LOOKOUTS, Eastern US Lookouts, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/durbin.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Dismantling U.S. 1 fire tower, St. Augustine Record (2011 article, Internet Archive copy), https://web.archive.org/web/2011/https://www.staugustine.com/story/news/local/2011/03/10/dismantling-us-1-fire-tower/16210081007/ (accessed 2026-10-21).
