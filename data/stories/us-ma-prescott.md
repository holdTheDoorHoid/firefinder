The Prescott Tower Site stood on Lighthouse Hill, in the former town of Prescott, Massachusetts. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes it as a 40-foot steel tower and places it in Franklin County.[^1]

The register gives April 1, 1912, as the date the tower went into service, and says it was staffed until 1913.[^1] Prescott was the least populous of the four towns flooded to make the Quabbin Reservoir in the Swift River Valley, with barely 300 residents by 1900.[^2][^3] The town lost its incorporated status on April 28, 1938.[^2][^3]

Much of the old town now lies under the reservoir. According to the register, the tower's old location is still above water.[^1]

Sources disagree on the county. The register gives Franklin County.[^1] Wikipedia says the town was in Hampshire County and that the New Salem portion, most of which is still above water, is now in Franklin County.[^2] That part is known as the Prescott Peninsula, and the public may not go onto it except on an annual tour or on hikes run by the Swift River Valley Historical Society.[^2] We could not tell whether the tower site lies on the peninsula.

No source we read describes the tower site today. Anyone who wants to visit should ask the Historical Society first.

Do not confuse this site with the Quabbin Lookout Tower, a separate structure built in 1940 on Quabbin Hill Road as a radio tower, fire station and observation tower.[^3]
