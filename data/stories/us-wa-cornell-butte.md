Cornell Butte Lookout stands on Okanogan National Forest land in Okanogan County, Washington, about nine miles southwest of Republic.[^1][^2] Its 42-foot all-steel tower has a live-in cab and was built in 1958, so it came late in the lookout era.[^2][^3]

The Forest Service told the National Historic Lookout Register that the tower has seen no regular use since 1971.[^1] The sources I read name no one who staffed it.

In 1996 a private party bought the tower for removal.[^2] The register notes that taking it down is unfunded and that the tower has suffered heavy damage and vandalism.[^1] In September 2015 the North Star Fire burned the ground around the tower, and embers reached the cab and set some floorboards alight.[^3] The bottom stairs have been taken out to protect visitors, though none of the sources I read dates that step.[^1][^3]

Firelookout.com, in an undated entry, places the tower on the summit as of its last update.[^2] TrailChick, last updated in March 2024, reports that the cab is still damaged.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the lookout on April 11, 2022, as US 1524, with Christine Estrada as nominator.[^1]

The tower is privately owned, though it stands on national forest land.[^1] Forest roads lead close to the summit.[^4][^3] The Washington Trails Association says a high-clearance vehicle can get to within roughly 300 yards of the summit, and TrailChick reports driving right below the tower on a 2018 visit.[^4][^3] No source I read says the tower is open to climbing, and its bottom stairs are gone.[^1][^3]
