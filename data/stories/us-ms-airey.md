The Airey Fire Tower stands at the Airey Work Center on the De Soto National Forest in Stone County, Mississippi, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The Register says a Civilian Conservation Corps company with enrollees from New Jersey constructed the tower in 1934.[^1]

The Register describes a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40 tower with a metal cab.[^1] A 1943 Coast and Geodetic Survey description, reproduced on a National Geodetic Survey page, gives different figures: a four-legged steel structure 110 feet high with a glass-enclosed cab.[^2] We could not settle which account is right, so the story reports both.

The Register also lists a radio relay use, and says the tower is on standby for fire detection.[^1]

In December 1936 the new Biloxi Ranger District Lookout Club held its organizing meeting at the tower, according to a Biloxi Daily Herald report quoted on a lookout history site.[^2]

The Register added the tower to its listing on July 24, 2003.[^1] The lookout history site lists the tower as standing in 2023, based on Google Earth imagery.[^2]

Neither source we read gives public access rules, so check with the De Soto Ranger District before going near the tower, and do not climb it without permission.
