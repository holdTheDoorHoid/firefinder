The Stillaguamish lookout was a 99-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6-foot cab on top, and a house stood below it.[^1][^2] The Kresek Inventory, as Willhite cites it, gives 1938 as the year it was built.[^2][^3]

The site sits about three miles south of Granite Falls, in Snohomish County, at an elevation of 735 feet.[^3][^2] The former lookout sites register added it on April 24, 2012.[^1]

Several maps label the site Burn Lookout, but Willhite does not know where Kresek got the Stillaguamish name.[^2]

The tower, cab and house are now gone.[^1][^3]

When Willhite visited in 2020, he found vacant private land owned by a developer, who he said would eventually sell it or had already done so.[^2] His page lists the access as private property where permission is needed.[^2]

Willhite describes a surviving survey disk stamped BURN 1941, a reference mark that points to a missing azimuth mark.[^2]
