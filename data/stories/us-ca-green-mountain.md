Green Mountain Lookout is a wood-frame lookout building in Mariposa County, on private land.[^1] The California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection's Madera-Mariposa-Merced unit is the agency the register lists as administering it.[^1]

The sources disagree on the year it went up. The register gives early 1943 in one passage and 1942 in another, while the fire-lookouts.org page says early 1942.[^1][^2] A California lookouts page gives 1943 as the construction year.[^3] The Forest Service's Los Padres National Forest designed the building. Several dozen buildings of that plan, with a 240-square-foot residence and a 70-square-foot cupola, went up, most often in coastal parts of California.[^1] The square lookout section has three large windows on each side, and the building rests on a concrete base that was poured over a pile of rock.[^1] A researcher in 1983 described it as one of the few lookouts of its era that remain in original condition.[^1]

During the Second World War the lookout was used as an aircraft observation post. According to the first lookout assigned there, Clinton Preston, it also served as an aircraft spotting station in 1942 and 1943.[^1] The register says very little else is known about it.[^1]

The date its use ended is not recorded. The tower was turned back over to the property owner.[^1] In mid-2004 a communications company asked about putting a cell phone tower on the site, and the register says that is the last information it holds.[^1] The register says the structure was standing as of May 2018.[^1]

The land is privately owned, and the register notes that the public can no longer get to the site.[^1] Anyone curious about it should ask the landowner first and should not try to reach it without permission.
