Round Knob's fire tower went up in 1928 on a knob in Raleigh County, West Virginia, near the head of the Little Beaver River.[^1][^2] The register credits five men by name with the build: Pop Wriston, J.J. Goulden, John Hammond, Pat Kirk, and Wint Lilly.[^1]

Sources disagree on the height and the agency. An August 1928 newspaper notice, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, announced a planned 75-foot steel tower and credited the state game and fish commission.[^2] The register describes a 60-foot steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and a 1962 newspaper piece also gives 60 feet.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page lists the West Virginia Division of Forestry as the agency.[^2]

In 1937 a crew of 16 men was set to begin a telephone line from the tower to Freezeland Mountain.[^2] By 1962 the observer was Ray Redden of Crow. Visitors were allowed at the tower with the observer present, during the March-to-May and October-to-December fire seasons.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed in the 1980s and gives no exact year.[^1] The site was registered on July 19, 2016, on the nomination of Ronnie Scarbro.[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Round Knob site today, or whether the public can reach it.
