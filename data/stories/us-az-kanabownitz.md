Kanabownitz Lookout is a steel tower on the North Rim of Grand Canyon National Park.[^1][^2] A park article says the Civilian Conservation Corps constructed it in 1940, near the end of its major building work in the park.[^2] It is an International Derrick steel tower with a cab about 7 feet square, roughly 82 feet 6 inches tall and standing at 8,241 feet.[^1][^2] A peak-bagging site gives 83 feet, a small difference from that figure.[^3]

The park article is a little loose on who did the building. It says the National Park Service built the Kanabownits tower, but its own section on the tower credits the CCC.[^2] Either way, the article says these towers were significant to the park's early fire fighting.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the tower on April 25, 2009.[^1] A ceremony on July 4, 2009 marked the listing of the four Grand Canyon towers, held with the opening of the North Rim fire facility.[^2] The register says the lookout is no longer used for fire detection, and the park calls all four of its towers inactive.[^1][^2]

Visiting is harder to plan. The sources do not describe a public trail, a road to the base, or a visitor rule.[^1][^2][^3] A peak-bagging site puts the tower in a remote stretch of the North Rim.[^3] Ask Grand Canyon National Park about access before planning a trip out there. That site's photo is dated June 15, 2019.[^3]
