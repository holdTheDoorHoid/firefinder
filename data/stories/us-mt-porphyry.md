Porphyry Lookout stands on Porphyry Peak in the Lewis and Clark National Forest.[^2] The Register puts it in Cascade County,[^1] and a National Geodetic Survey station description reproduced on the Forest Lookouts site places the station on the divide between Cascade and Meagher counties.[^3] The firelookout.com page says it sits about six miles south of Neihart,[^2] but an August 1968 item quoted on the Forest Lookouts site puts the station eight miles south of Neihart.[^3]

Sources disagree about when the first tower went up. Two 1933 newspaper items quoted on the Forest Lookouts site describe a 40-foot tower being built that October and reported as built by December.[^3] The Register and firelookout.com instead say the site was first developed in 1937 with a 40-foot pole L-4 tower.[^1][^2] An October 1937 article says the Forest Service was putting lookouts on 50-foot towers in the Lewis and Clark forest, with four towers completed or nearly so, and one of them on Porphyry Peak.[^3]

The present tower, a 40-foot treated timber structure with an R-6 flat cab, replaced the first one in 1960.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts site gives the same year, and the survey note says the tower was constructed in 1959 and 1960.[^3]

In July 1935 the lookout on Porphyry Mountain first noticed a fire in the Moose Creek area, which the Helena lookouts could not see.[^3] In August 1946 the lookouts at Porphyry and Williams Mountain reported a fire near Black Butte, and a county crew went out to fight it.[^3] In September 1952 a Greeley Tribune item, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, described Don and Adair Peterson, who had married the previous June and were Forest Service fire lookouts on the tower. The article said they had not yet spotted any fires.[^3]

The Register accepted the lookout on April 12, 2022, on the nomination of Kyle Stetler.[^1] Both the Register and firelookout.com say it is staffed every summer.[^1][^2] None of the sources I read mention a move, closure or damage to the tower. They also do not say whether visitors may climb it, so ask the Lewis and Clark National Forest before going up.
