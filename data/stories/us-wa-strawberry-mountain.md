Strawberry Mountain Lookout stands on the Colville Indian Reservation in Okanogan County, Washington.[^1][^2] Its 54-foot steel International Derrick tower was built in 1936, and it had a separate L-4 cab for living quarters.[^1][^2][^3]

The tower was staffed into 1938 and then abandoned.[^1][^3] The sources do not give an exact date for the last watch.[^1]

The cab did not survive. The register and TrailChick blame a fallen tree, while firelookout.com and willhiteweb.com blame a falling snag, so the sources disagree on what brought it down.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The steel tower did survive, and one account says trees now grow inside its stairwell.[^4]

The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the lookout on April 13, 2022, as US 1561.[^1]

Today the tower is abandoned, and no trail or road leads directly to it.[^2][^3] TrailChick describes a rough approach that turns onto BIA roads, then cross-country travel through fireweed, for a round trip of about 5.6 miles.[^2] The land is tribal, and TrailChick reports that non-tribal visitors to Colville Confederated Tribal land must buy an annual recreation permit, listed at $80, so check with the tribes before any trip.[^1][^2][^5] Do not climb the tower.
