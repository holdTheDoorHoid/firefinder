The Sullivan Hill tower stood in Crawford County.[^1] In August 1941 the Sullivan News reported that CCC 2728 was building a second wooden tower at Sullivan, and that more might follow if the wartime steel shortage persisted.[^1] The item names the town rather than the hill, so the link to Sullivan Hill rests on the Forest Lookouts page that quotes it.[^1]

A 1956 Coast and Geodetic Survey description of the survey mark describes a four-legged steel tower about 50 feet high, with a six-foot square platform and a guard rail.[^2] The survey station sat at the center of the tower, and the description places the tower in the Clark National Forest.[^2]

The 1941 wooden tower and the 1956 steel tower do not clearly match, and the sources do not say whether they are the same structure.[^1][^2] Missouri's Department of Conservation says steel was the material of most fire towers, and that the earliest were built of timber.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts page lists the Sullivan Hill tower as removed, with no date or reason.[^1] The National Geodetic Survey record for the mark has only one history entry, a 1956 observation.[^2] None of the sources read for this story describes what is left on the hill today, or whether it is open to visitors.
