Steve Peak rises to 5,866 feet in the Rogue River National Forest, in Josephine County, Oregon.[^1] A 1913 district fire plan proposed a lookoutman on the peak and a spur line to its top, but a plan alone does not show that either was built.[^3] By 1916 an observer was posted there.[^3] A call bell was set up at the observer's camp, so he could be phoned in an emergency whenever he was off the summit.[^3]

In August 1918 a fast-moving fire reached the lookout station on the peak.[^3] A Medford newspaper reported that the lookout, a retired Jacksonville physician named Dr. Barker, barely escaped, and that the flames destroyed the station and everything in it.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association register dates a 12-by-12 cab to 1919 and says a 20-foot pole L-5 tower replaced it in the 1930s.[^1][^2] Neither that register nor the newspaper says whether the 1919 cab replaced the station that burned in 1918.[^1][^3] A September 1933 item from the same paper says Camp Applegate CCC crews started work on the lookout, without saying which structure.[^3] The tower was removed in 1968, though firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2]

The sources spell the name several ways, including Steve's, Steves and Stevens Peak.[^3] The register lists the site as a former lookout, and no source I found describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.[^1]
