Soapstone Mountain rises in Somers, inside the Shenipsit State Forest.[^1] The state bought summit land here in 1927 with a fire tower in mind.[^1] The tower was put up in early summer 1929 and began operating in spring 1930, according to a state park and forest report.[^2] A 1935 newspaper item said the lookout helped protect the forest from fire, and that picnic tables were going in nearby for visitors.[^2]

A 1936 National Geodetic Survey station description calls it the Somers Fire Tower and says the survey point sat on the tower's concrete base.[^2]

The first tower on the summit is no longer standing.[^1] A 30-foot (9.1 m) wooden observation tower on the summit was closed in October 2014, when the state judged it unsafe.[^2] A hiking blog gives two dates for the replacement. It says the state put up a replacement in 2018, and that local citizens rebuilt the tower in 2019. The blog adds that only the old columns and concrete were kept.[^4]

The state says the summit tower is there for public recreation today.[^1] The state lists the forest as open daily, from half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset.[^3] The blue-blazed Shenipsit Trail runs over the mountain.[^1]

The same blog says the U.S. Army used the old fire tower as a lookout during the Second World War. It also says a National Weather Service radar dome from the mid-1970s now occupies the spot of the first tower.[^4] I found no second source for the radar dome.
