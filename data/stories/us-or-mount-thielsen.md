Forest Lookouts, a site that tracks Oregon's lookouts, has an entry for Mount Thielsen, a peak on the Umpqua National Forest.[^1] The entry records a 1933 decision to use the site for a lookout, but says no structure was built there.[^1] The same page lists panorama photographs taken on July 30, 1933, by Snyder and Moe.[^1]

Thielsen is a shield volcano in Oregon's High Cascades that no longer erupts, and glaciers have heavily worn down its structure, leaving steep slopes and a horn-shaped summit.[^2] The volcano takes its name from Hans Thielsen, an engineer.[^2] The first ascent was in 1883, and Wikipedia gives the easiest route to the summit as a scramble.[^2]

No source we found describes what, if anything, stands on the summit today.
