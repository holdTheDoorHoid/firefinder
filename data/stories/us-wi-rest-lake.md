The Rest Lake fire tower began as part of a ranger station near Manitowish Waters, in Vilas County, Wisconsin.[^1][^5] The state forestry department set up the station in 1912 as one of four original outposts aimed at curbing the destructive forest fires of the northwoods.[^1] Its first tower was a 55-foot (17 m) windmill tower with a makeshift cab on top, and forest ranger Herman Kruger, who ran the station from the start, lived there with his family.[^1]

In the winter of 1933-34, Civilian Conservation Corps workers from Camp Mercer took the old tower apart and built a 100-foot (30.5 m) observation tower.[^1] The CCC unit's own history records the rebuild and says cement work kept going through the winter, leaving the men free to fight fires once spring came.[^2] The ranger station itself ran from 1912 until 1923, according to the CCC history, and Clio dates its removal to the early 1920s.[^2][^1]

Rangers lived on the forestry land beside the tower until the 1950s.[^1] A 1937 aerial photo shows a clearing around the lookout with several buildings in it, and the Manitowish Waters Historical Society says the clearing is likely the station's site.[^3]

Clio says the tower was taken apart and relocated to Harrison Hills, Wisconsin, in the early 1970s.[^1] A travel guide describes old fire-tower stairs at the top of Lookout Mountain in Harrison Hills that let visitors look down over the treetops.[^4] The guide does not mention Rest Lake, so the link between the two sites rests on Clio alone.[^4] The cabin at the top is not open to visitors.[^4]

No tower stands on the original hill now.[^5] Clio says the footings are still visible on the hill beside Angler's Fine Food and Spirits, and that a town bike trail now winds around the hill that once helped the tower clear the treetops.[^1] Visitors on bikes, on foot or eating at Angler's can make the short climb to view the old footings.[^1] The site's address is Tower Road in Manitowish Waters.[^1]
