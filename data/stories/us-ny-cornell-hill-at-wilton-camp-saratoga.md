The Cornell Hill fire tower went up in 1924 on Cornell Hill in the Luther Forest, a few miles from Saratoga Springs to the southeast.[^5] The New York State Conservation Commission built it to watch over a forest that feeds drinking water to several nearby communities.[^5] Sources disagree on the model. The NHLR page calls it an LS-20, while the Town of Wilton, the FFLA's 2011 grant report and Starr say LS-40.[^1][^2][^4][^5]

The first observer, Noah LaCasse, served from 1924 to 1935.[^5] Fire observers manned the tower for many years.[^3] Under an easement with the Luther Forest, the state ran it through the end of the 1970 fire season, after which it was decommissioned.[^5] It was later vandalized and ended up in poor shape.[^5][^3]

Accounts differ on how the tower changed hands. The Saratogian says William Mackay acquired it from the state for $1 in 1976, in a surplus sale.[^3] Starr describes a later sale to Luther Forest at a nominal price, after decommissioning.[^5] The Mackay family's company, Luther Forest Corp., later funded the restoration and transferred the tower to the Town of Wilton.[^3]

Approvals and paperwork took four years, from 2006 to 2010.[^5] In August 2010, Dave Vana's crew began taking the tower apart on Cornell Hill.[^5] The Saratogian reports the tower was reassembled in early December on its new site near Scout Road.[^3] The NHLR page says the tower was relocated in 2011, but its photo caption dates the re-erection to December 2010.[^1]

A dedication was held on May 14, 2011.[^5] The FFLA also awarded a $500 restoration grant that year, to be spent on equipping the cab with a map table and fire-finder gear.[^4]

Today the tower stands at Camp Saratoga, inside Wilton Wildlife Preserve and Park.[^1] According to the town, it is open on the third weekend of each month, April through October, 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., plus October 2 and 3 for foliage season.[^2] A 2017 visit report on the NHLR page calls it in excellent condition, with wheelchair access to the base, and names the second weekend of each month for volunteer staffing.[^1] At the 2011 dedication, the Saratogian reported that visitors would need a volunteer guide, with insurance given as the reason.[^3] I found no newer rule either way.
