Rustler Peak is a fire lookout in Jackson County, Oregon, on the Rogue River National Forest.[^1][^2] The Register gives its elevation as 6,154 feet, while firelookout.com and Cheryl Hill's standing list say 6,208 feet.[^1][^2][^5]

The Register says the summit was a full lookout by 1913.[^1] In 1917 the Forest Service set up a pre-cut D-6 house there, and an Ashland paper reported that it would stand on an 18-foot steel tower.[^1][^3] The Register and firelookout.com describe a 10-foot steel tower under a D-6 cupola in the 1920s, and a 2021 trip report also gives 18 feet for the 1917 structure, so the sources disagree about that earlier tower.[^1][^2][^4]

According to the Register, the Civilian Conservation Corps put $600 into lookout construction in 1940, the year the cupola was raised onto a tower.[^1][^3] In 1948 an L-4 cab went onto a laminated timber tower, which the Register and Forest Lookouts put at 31 feet 6 inches.[^1][^3] The Register also notes a first for the 1948 lookout: it hosted a solar-powered radio system, the earliest such system in a Forest Service lookout.[^1] Firelookout.com says 30 feet.[^2]

A June 1934 item reports that Mrs. Blossom Smith spent the summer living at the station.[^3] In May 1940 a lookout was posted there because of lightning danger.[^3] A September 1960 item says Mr. and Mrs. Howard Greer had manned the station for three years, and calls the summit the key lookout for the Butte Falls district, which sent out coded weather reports and received check calls from handi-talkie radios.[^3]

As of a 2021 trip report, the lookout was staffed every fire season.[^4] Firelookout.com says it is staffed every summer and is listed on the National Historic Lookout Register.[^2] Cheryl Hill's list marks it staffed and accessible to the public, though the list may be out of date.[^5]

The 2021 writer parked in a pullout before a gate and walked the last mile, which is a gated road.[^4] The writer did not get to look over the cab that day, and advised climbing the tower only when invited up.[^4] Forest Lookouts notes a 2006 contractor job that installed a modern vault toilet at the site.[^3] The trip report also says the lookout is currently standing and that the previous lookout was no longer on the summit when the writer visited.[^4]

Check with the Butte Falls Ranger District before visiting, as the most recent visit described here dates from August 2021.[^1][^4]
