Logger Butte lies in Lane County, about 15 miles south-southeast of Oakridge, inside the Willamette National Forest.[^1][^2]

A lookout was on the butte by 1919. That year the Eugene Daily Guard reported three fires seen from "the lookout on Logger butte," all in the Umpqua district.[^3] The cabin that most sources describe went up in 1926. The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register gives it as a D-6 cupola cabin, built that year.[^1] Newspapers reported that work started in July and that the house was finished by August.[^3] They described it as regulation size, with sides made of glass.[^3]

The papers name some of the people who worked the lookout. Herbert Jones was to return as lookout in 1925.[^3] C.A. Lord, the lookout employed there, was to do the painting in his spare time in 1926.[^3] In August 1928 E.E. Kilpatrick, principal of the Pleasant Hill high school, spent his vacation on the butte as lookout.[^3] A paper that month reported that his wife had shot and killed a cougar with her rifle on the trail to the water.[^3] In 1935 Buck Jones served as lookout fireman there. He was building a wind fan to draw spring water through a hose up to the station.[^3]

The sources do not agree on how the cabin ended. The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register and firelookout.com both say the cabin was taken down in 1969.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page gives 1969 as well, but says the lookout burned, the work of an arsonist.[^3] onX, a trail-map service, gives the same account.[^5] Warner Ridge, about two miles southwest, got a new tower in 1986.[^1] firelookout.com says the new tower's frame was brought from Grass Mountain, with a replica cupola cabin added on top.[^4]

According to onX, all that remains at the top is a pair of small benches, with views the guide calls amazing.[^5] The access road is an easy gravel road that onX says is typically unmaintained in winter and can be subject to closures.[^5] We found no restriction on visiting the summit in the sources we read. No source we read describes footings, a foundation or any other remains at the site, so we cannot say more about what is there. The Warner Ridge tower is further down the road.[^5] firelookout.com says it is staffed in summer and on the rental program in winter.[^4]
