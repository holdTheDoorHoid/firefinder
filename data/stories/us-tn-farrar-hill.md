Farrar Hill was a fire lookout tower in Coffee County, Tennessee.[^1] The Tennessee Landforms tower list puts the site at 1,208 feet (about 368 m) and numbers the tower 5-6, which means district 5, tower 6.[^1] Those numbers are state forest tower numbers drawn from a 1976 list of towers.[^1]

Between 1933 and 1941 the Civilian Conservation Corps erected 98 lookout towers across Tennessee.[^1] The Tennessee Landforms list marks the Farrar Hill tower as removed.[^1] A National Register study of Tennessee's towers reports that many former Division of Forestry towers are gone or slated for demolition, because they went unused or their leases on private land ran out.[^2]

No source I found describes what remains at the Farrar Hill site today, or whether anyone can visit it. The Tennessee Landforms list says most tower locations are now private property.[^1] It also tells visitors not to climb, because steps are often missing or decayed.[^1]

[^1]: Tennessee lookout towers, Tennessee Landforms, https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers MPS, National Park Service, https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/64501237.pdf (accessed 2026-10-21).
