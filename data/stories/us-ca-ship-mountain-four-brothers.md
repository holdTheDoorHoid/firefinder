Ship Mountain, also called Four Brothers, is a peak in Del Norte County, California.[^1] The Register lists the US Forest Service as administrator, in the Six Rivers National Forest.[^1] The Register says the peak was once part of the Siskiyou National Forest, and that a lookout may have been there as early as 1914.[^1] A 1914 Siskiyou Bulletin item, quoted on a lookout history site, names a guard working from a station on the peak that summer.[^2]

A timber tower went up around 1922.[^1] The Register dates it to about that year and describes a Region 5 Plan 4AR cab.[^1] A 1939 forest history, quoted on the same site, says a standard cabin was carried 26 miles in and put together on the peak.[^2] Newspaper items name lookouts in 1922 and 1924.[^2] One lookout reported that lightning struck 2,648 times in his territory during a single night in 1924.[^2] During the Second World War the peak hosted Aircraft Warning Service Station 'Mike 8-1', which was placed on inactive status in October 1943.[^1][^2]

The tower was later replaced by a portable trailer, and the sources disagree on when.[^1][^3] The Register puts that change in the 1960s.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2008 western region report says the trailer had been used at the site since 1973.[^3] The trailer was removed in 2007, the road to the top was improved, and a new two-story station was built in 2008.[^2][^3]

The names also disagree. The Register calls the site Four Brothers #3, while a 1941 survey placed a station on peak 3. A later note says the current house is on Peak #4, the southernmost of the four.[^1][^2]

The FFLA lookout list shows the two-story cab as standing.[^4] I found no visit reports or statement on whether visitors can go up, so check with the Six Rivers National Forest before planning a trip.
