The National Historic Lookout Register lists Fatama Lookout Tower in Wilcox County, Alabama, at an elevation of 548 feet (167 m).[^1] The Register describes a 100-foot steel tower that Civilian Conservation Corps workers from a camp erected during October and November 1941.[^1] It spells the camp's name "Vrendeburgh."[^1]

I could not find a camp under that name in the sources I read. The Encyclopedia of Alabama has an entry for Vredenburgh, a town in Monroe County, and the part I read does not mention a CCC camp. Whether the Register's spelling is a typo, or the camp was a different one, is unconfirmed.[^2]

The Register's build months come before the United States entered World War II in December 1941. A 1819 News article says the CCC stopped building fire towers at that point, leaving some unfinished, and that Forestry personnel took over the work afterward.[^3] The Encyclopedia of Alabama says the CCC had put up 61 lookout towers across the state by 1942.[^4] Neither source names Fatama.[^3][^4]

The Register lists the tower as registered on January 8, 2021.[^1] It gives no managing agency.[^1] I found no source on its staffing, fires, or condition today, and none on public access. Anyone planning a visit should check with the landowner or land manager first, and should not climb a tower that is closed or where they have no permission to be.
