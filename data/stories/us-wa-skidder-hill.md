Washington's Department of Natural Resources built a fire lookout on Skidder Hill, above Discovery Bay on the northeast Olympic Peninsula, in 1957.[^1][^2][^3] The 40-foot wooden tower had living quarters for its lookout, and the site stands at 2,126 feet (648 meters).[^1]

The station opened with an open house that September.[^2] A newspaper account quoted on a Washington lookout history page reports that the radio and fire finder were operating at the open house and that viewing conditions were excellent.[^2] William Muench manned the station for the rest of that season.[^2] Later entries name Mildred Drazkowski in 1960 and Karen Anderson in 1966.[^2][^3] Hite's page spells the first name differently, calling the 1958 to 1960 lookout Milly Draskowski.[^3]

The sources disagree about how the tower ended.[^1][^2][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register says the tower was gone by 1971, after a private buyer took it and salvaged much of its material.[^1] A second history page says only that it was sold and removed.[^2] Hite places the decision to take it down in 1966, the year Karen Anderson was named as lookout.[^3] He describes an attempt to save the top section intact that failed when a brace pole slipped while the tower was being tipped with a winch, and the structure crashed.[^3] The reused parts went to a church remodel, a rental cabin, and a new garage, along with a ridge beam, windows and shutters.[^3] One online account, which Hite passes on second-hand, says the tower sold on a US$4 bid.[^3]

Today the summit area is heavily forested and sits on a mix of private timberland and state land.[^3] Hite reports that all four footings remain. One was visible, and the other two were almost hidden in brush and young trees.[^3] He also saw an eye bolt for a guy line.[^3] On his visit, non-motorized travel was permitted, but none of the three sources says whether the public may enter the summit.[^3] No source describes a standing structure at the summit today.[^1][^2][^3]
