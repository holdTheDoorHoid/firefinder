The Leavittsville tower stood on a hill overlooking the community of Leavittsville in Carroll County, Ohio.[^1][^2] A newspaper report dated April 18, 1951 said the Ohio Division of Forestry had finished it the week before.[^2] It was one of five towers the division put up in eastern Ohio.[^2] This one was meant to guard a tree-planting area east of New Philadelphia, where the Muskingum Conservancy District had set out thousands of trees.[^2] The paper described an 80-foot steel tower with a wooden cab, built on land the state leased.[^2] The register gives the site an elevation of 1,260 feet (384 m).[^1]

The tower was watched only on certain days.[^2] A tower man was to be on duty on dry, windy days in spring and fall, which the district forester called the most dangerous fire period.[^2] The paper named Alpha Moreland of RD 1 as the man who would staff it on set days.[^2] Radios were not yet in place.[^2] The forester hoped to equip all the towers with them by fall, but until then the watcher had to leave the tower to check how serious a fire was and then report it.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Ohio list marks the tower as gone.[^3] The register lists the site as US 1564, OH 10, and it was registered on September 3, 2014.[^1]

No source describes what remains on the hill today, and we found nothing about whether the site is open to visitors. Check who owns the land before going up.
