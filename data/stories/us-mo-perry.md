The Missouri Department of Conservation put up the Perry Fire Tower around 1965, in its 1964–65 fiscal year.[^1][^2] The department's annual report for that year describes a 100-foot steel tower erected at the Perryville towersite.[^2] The Register describes it as an LS40-type steel Aermotor, built to catch and head off forest fires in southeast Missouri.[^1]

The Register places the tower in the Perryville Fire Protection District, which also had several other tower sites in the southeast of the state.[^1] In 1966 the department ran 102 fire towers across Missouri, and the Forest Service ran 43.[^3] Most of the state's towers were about 100 feet tall, and Perry's 100-foot height fits that pattern.[^1][^3] We found no names of the people who worked this tower and no specific fire it watched.

The Register says use of the tower ended in the 1980s, without a more exact date.[^1] The department kept the five-acre site and put a separate communications tower on it.[^1] Its 2018 management plan says the area's purpose today is keeping the radio tower for communications.[^4]

In 2021 the Perry County commission began talks with the department about taking over the site, hoping to keep it as a roadside attraction.[^5] The Register dates the county's ownership to a 2022–23 deed made for historical reasons.[^1]

Today the Register lists the tower in caretaker status, and says climbing is prohibited until restoration plans are complete.[^1] A 2021 newspaper report said a padlock at the top kept climbers off the platform. The structure is 100 feet tall at the cab entrance.[^5]

The site is on the north side of Highway J. Go south on Highway 51 about 10 miles, then turn west on Highway J for about three miles.[^5] The newspaper puts the site about 13 miles from Perryville and mentions a picnic table.[^5] The 2018 plan provides that picnic area with limited public use.[^4]
