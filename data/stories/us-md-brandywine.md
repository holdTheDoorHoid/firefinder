Maryland raised two steel observation towers in 1928. The one at Brandywine was 93 feet tall.[^1]

In February 1938 the State Forestry Department announced that the tower would be rebuilt to 120 feet so its two-way radio set would reach farther.[^1] The set was to be built at the forestry's radio laboratory in Laurel, and the plan was to extend radio service into Southern Maryland.[^1]

A 2007 Maryland Department of Natural Resources report says the property was transferred to the University of Maryland and the tower was moved to the Fairview Outdoor Education Center in Clear Spring, Washington County.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the original Brandywine tower as gone.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the original Brandywine site today.

At Fairview, the tower stands on the grounds of the Claud E. Kitchens Outdoor School, which Washington County Public Schools runs.[^3] A teacher's article published in December 2022 describes the climb up the 104-foot tower as a rite of passage for Grade 5 students for about 40 years.[^3]

That article also says the tower was once an active fire tower on Maryland's Eastern Shore, which does not match the 1938 article's Southern Maryland location for Brandy Wine.[^3][^1] Its 104-foot height also differs from the 120 feet the 1938 article said the tower was being rebuilt to.[^3][^1] The sources do not resolve these differences, so the link between the Brandywine tower and the Fairview tower rests on the state report.[^2]

The article presents the tower as part of the school's teaching program.[^3] It does not say whether the public may visit or climb it, so check with the school before planning a trip.[^3]
