The Ann Bailey lookout stands in Watoga State Park, in southern Pocahontas County, West Virginia.[^2][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a standing observation tower.[^1]

The Pocahontas Times says the Civilian Conservation Corps put the tower up in the 1930s, using American chestnut trees that blight had infected.[^3] The West Virginia Encyclopedia says two CCC camps were set up at the park in 1933 and 1934, a third opened in 1935, and the park began welcoming visitors on July 1, 1937.[^2] The same entry records a fire tower at the park in 1926, but it does not name the Ann Bailey lookout.[^2] I could not tell from these sources whether the 1926 tower was this structure, an earlier one, or a different tower altogether.

The name honors Ann Bailey, a figure the same article describes as a warrior from the Revolutionary War era.[^3] Her fame rests on a deed of courage that began at Fort Lee in 1791, when the fort was nearly out of gunpowder and the nearest supply lay at Lewisburg.[^3]

I found no staffing, fire, or rental records for the tower in these sources.[^1][^2]

Today the tower is reached by park trails.[^4] The Ann Bailey Trail is 3 miles long, follows a former road bed over Pyles Mountain and Workman Ridge, and is open to mountain bikes.[^4] A steep trail also leads up from Riverside Campground.[^4] The West Virginia Division of Natural Resources runs the park, and its office keeps the trail map.[^5] None of the pages I read says whether the tower itself is open to climb. One guide tells visitors to phone the park office and ask if the tower can be climbed.[^5]
