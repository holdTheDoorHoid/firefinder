Deer Springs Lookout stands in Navajo County, Arizona, inside the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The register calls it the oldest active lookout in Arizona.[^1]

The register says it was built in 1923. The lookout history site gives the same year and describes a 50-foot Aermotor LL-25 with a 7-by-7 cab.[^1][^2] Earlier mentions complicate that date. A 1913 Holbrook News item, quoted by the history site, says a lookout tower had been constructed near Deer Spring, and a 1914 item mentions the tower again.[^2] Wikipedia gives the build year as 1923 or 1926.[^3]

Wikipedia also dates the ladder replacement to the 1930s, without a specific year.[^3]

The site has staffing records from the 1940s. In 1940 Ivan S. Williams was in his 20th consecutive year there, and in 1944 a teacher, Annis Jackson, was stationed at the tower that season.[^2] I found no staffing report after 1944.

The register added the tower on March 4, 2002, as US 418, AZ 25.[^1] Sources also disagree on its National Register listing. TreeAlive gives January 28, 1988, while Wikipedia gives December 14, 1987.[^4][^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona list still shows the tower as standing.[^5] I found no current staffing report, rental listing or visitor information for it, so I cannot say whether the public may go up it. Ask the Apache-Sitgreaves National Forest before visiting, and do not climb the tower without permission.
