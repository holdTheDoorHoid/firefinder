Belah was one of the fire towers in Louisiana's statewide network, which Wikipedia says grew to 39 towers by 1943 and 56 by 1950.[^1] A March 1985 report in The Town Talk put the tower's height at 120 feet.[^2]

In March 1985 State Forester Mike Mety said the tower would stop being staffed after July 1, though some crews expected to keep watching for fires from it.[^2]

The tower also kept rain records.[^2] The Monroe News-Star reported in January 1957 that 47.39 inches of rain had been recorded there in 1956.[^2] A 2009 column reprinted a July 1969 story saying the rain gauge at the tower measured about six inches in a matter of hours during a flood-causing storm.[^2]

Wikipedia's list marks Belah as torn down.[^1] The same list notes that the site could not be viewed on street view or in older aerial imagery.[^1] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.[^1]
