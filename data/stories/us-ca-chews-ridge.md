Chews Ridge Lookout sits at the northern end of the Santa Lucia Range in the Los Padres National Forest, about 50 miles southeast of Monterey.[^2] A family lived in a government-owned cabin at the summit in 1919, and that cabin also served as a lookout.[^2] Sources disagree on when the current tower went up. The National Historic Lookout Register says it was built in 1924, while Wikipedia dates the current steel tower and cab to 1929.[^1][^2] I could not settle that from the sources I read.

Beginning in the 1940s the tower was staffed seasonally. C. C. Yates and his wife of Arroyo Grande worked it each season, and they kept at it through the 1950s.[^2]

A fire destroyed the cab in 1977, and it was rebuilt the following year.[^1][^2] Wikipedia names the blaze as the Marble Cone Fire.[^2] In 1984 the cab was replaced with a standard R-6 design, with a catwalk and a flat roof.[^2]

The Forest Service stopped staffing the tower around 1990, and afterward it housed radio repeater equipment and antennas.[^1][^2]

In 2019 the Monterey chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association restored the tower, with approval from the Los Padres National Forest.[^1][^4] Volunteers began staffing it that August.[^2]

The chapter says dozens of trained volunteers have staffed the tower since then. Its site also reports that wind has torn whole sections off the roof, that a full roof replacement is now needed, and that it is running a campaign to raise $20,000 to preserve the lookout.[^3]

Getting there is a drive. The route runs from Carmel Valley Road about nine miles south on Tassajara Road, and most of that road is unpaved.[^2] Inside the cab there is no running water, electricity, or heat, and the stairs up are steep and narrow.[^2]

Because a volunteer group runs the tower on national forest land, treat it as a working lookout and check with the chapter before planning any visit.[^2][^3]
