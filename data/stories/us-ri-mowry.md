The Mowry Fire Tower stands on Woonsocket Hill in North Smithfield, Rhode Island. The hill rises 586 feet and is thought to be the town's high point.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register dates it to 1926 and describes a 65-foot steel tower with a Massachusetts-style 10-by-10-foot wooden cab.[^2]

The dates do not fully agree. A 1923 state report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, says three new observation stations were set up that year, bringing the total to five. It lists Woonsocket Hill among the five and gives the towers' heights as 40 to 68 feet.[^3] Local reporting says an older tower, first put up on Cato Hill in the 1800s, was moved to Woonsocket Hill during the 1920s and replaced in 1956 by the present steel tower.[^4] A 2021 report also gives 1956,[^1] while a 2025 report repeats 1926.[^5] The sources read do not settle which date fits the tower standing today.

The local account places towers like this one in the 1950s and '60s, when they were a main way of spotting rural fires.[^4] It describes observers stationed at the top, watching for fire and alerting local fire departments to danger nearby.[^4] A 1951 state report says every lookout tower had electric wiring except the one on Woonsocket Hill.[^3] A 1954 to 1955 report recommended new cabs for the Woonsocket Hill and Shannock towers, saying both had deteriorated to the point of being unsafe for observers.[^3] The sources read name no particular fire the tower watched.

According to the local account, North Smithfield acquired the tower in 1990, when the state sold it through a grant program for recreation.[^4] The ground beneath it belongs to the town, but the road up the hill is privately owned.[^4] In April 2021 the town council allocated $68,382 for repairs on top of an earlier $2,500 budget, after the emergency director said the tower was in very poor condition.[^1] In 2025 the state's emergency management agency finished a 140-foot monopole beside the tower, after the old tower had fallen into disrepair and could no longer serve its radio purpose.[^5] The state removed its own antennas from the old tower, and the 2025 report says the restoration work had been funded.[^5]

The old tower still held antennas used by the town and a local bus company in 2021.[^1] The sources read do not confirm whether the restoration is finished. The Register's visit notes from 2015 record no signed directions to the tower and say it was not staffed.[^2] Anyone interested should check with the town before going, and should not climb the tower or enter the cab.
