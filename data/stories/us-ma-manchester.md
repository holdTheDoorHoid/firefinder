Manchester's fire tower is in Essex County.[^1] In 1913, a run of local fires led the North Shore improvement association to give seven acres of land and $400 toward its construction.[^1]

The register says the first tower was replaced in 1968 by a 68-foot tower with a 10-by-10-foot cab, and it lists the Mass. Bureau of Forest Fire Control as administrator, with District 5 as a cooperator.[^1] The register does not describe what became of the first tower beyond that replacement.[^1]

The Bureau has helped cities and towns prevent, spot and put out forest fires since 1911.[^2] Statewide, a network of 42 operating fire towers is staffed by trained observers, who use alidade tables, binoculars and topographic maps to pinpoint where a fire is.[^3]

The register gives the site's elevation as 193 feet (59 meters).[^1] Its detail fields do not include a current staffing record or public access arrangement, so please don't assume the tower is open to visitors or climb it without permission.[^1]
