In January 1937 a newspaper reported that Civilian Conservation Corps men from the Clear Lake camp had just finished an all-steel lookout tower on the Tulelake wildlife refuge, in Modoc County, California.[^1] The tower stood 100 feet high and had a small steel house at its top.[^1] Steel base beams, anchored 16 feet into the ground, rested on concrete footings.[^1] According to the paper, the view took in the lower part of Tulelake and the surrounding peninsula country, and the tower was to be used mostly during hunting season.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site files that report under its Peninsula heading.[^1]

A report from July 1938 describes two 100-foot steel towers. One had been taken down, relocated and put back up, and another had been erected at the Peninsula patrol station.[^1] The page does not say whether either of them is the tower from 1937, so this story cannot settle which steel tower stood on the peninsula.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page marks the Peninsula entry as Removed, with no date and no details.[^1] A separate Former Fire Lookout Sites register entry, for a Tulelake NWR lookout site that may have been named Peninsula because it stood on a peninsula at the refuge's southern border, describes an 80-foot Aermotor tower.[^2] The register adds that only a concrete pad is left at the spot, and it gives no removal date.[^2] We could not confirm that the register entry and the Forest Lookouts entry describe the same tower.

The register does not date its description of the site, and no source we read says whether the spot is open to visitors.
