Salt Hill, in Westchester County near Peekskill, got a state fire lookout in 1950.[^2][^1] The Conservation Department erected a 73-foot Aermotor LS40 steel tower there.[^1][^5] Both the NYSFFLA list and Starr's list use the name Nelson for it.[^1][^5] Sources disagree on which way the hill lies from town. Wikipedia places it southeast of Peekskill, while the NYSFFLA entry says four miles southwest.[^2][^1]

It was brought into use in 1951, and its reports counted 22 fires and 70 visitors.[^2] It closed at the end of the 1971 fire season, as aerial detection came into wider use.[^1][^2] At some later point, unknown people cut the tower down or pulled it over.[^2] A hiker who came up Salt Hill in January 2017 spotted the top of the tower through the woods. The post said the wreckage was still on the summit.[^4]

On January 29, 2023, Michael Pellet climbed to the summit and reported that the remains had been removed.[^3] The containers on the site had also been taken away.[^3] The NYSFFLA's February 2023 newsletter carried his report.[^3]

Today the tower is gone.[^3] The same newsletter says the state forest is still open to visitors.[^3] No source I read describes what, if anything, is left on the summit, so visitors should not plan around a structure there.
