Youngstown Fire Tower stood in Bay County, Florida.[^1][^2] The earliest mention we found is from September 1940, when the Panama City News-Herald reported that work on the lookout site at Youngstown was already well along that year.[^2]

A January 1948 item in the same paper described the tower as 100 feet high and built of wood.[^2] A 1964 National Geodetic Survey station description, for a station at a highway junction about four miles north of Youngstown, describes the tower as a square metal cab on four steel channel legs, about 100 feet tall.[^2]

The two descriptions disagree on the material and on how far north of town the tower stood.[^2] The record does not show whether the tower was rebuilt or whether the accounts describe different structures.[^2]

The register says Hurricane Michael blew the tower down in October 2018.[^1] The site was added to the register on February 19, 2019, on a nomination by Brian Powell.[^1] The register lists the Florida Forestry Service as its administrator.[^1]

We found no source that describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can visit it.[^1][^2]
