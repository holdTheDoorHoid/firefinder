The Moon lookout began as a tower on Summit Ridge in Pennington County, South Dakota, inside the Black Hills National Forest.[^1] A Civilian Conservation Corps crew under Ed Mason built it in 1935.[^1] Firelookout.com describes it as a 40-foot wooden tower.[^4] The crew had some trouble finding the exact spot, and they first set the tower on private land about half a mile west, in Wyoming.[^1] After they realized the mistake, they shifted it by balancing it on logs that a tractor pulled.[^1] It was in use as a lookout on Summit Ridge for five years.[^1]

In 1940 the tower was relocated to a meadow on high ground south of Moon Campground.[^1] Forest Lookouts says it was renamed the Moon tower at that point.[^2] There it was a secondary lookout until 1965.[^1]

The sources disagree about how the tower ended. The Former Fire Lookout Sites record says a crew under Kenny Sallee sawed it down in 1965 and buried it where it stood.[^1] Forest Lookouts says the tower was felled and burned in 1960.[^2] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed in the early 1960s.[^4]

No source we read describes what is left at the meadow today. The Former Fire Lookout Sites record says only that the tower was buried where it stood.[^1] The site is listed as Black Hills National Forest land, administered by the U.S. Forest Service, but no source we read says whether the public can reach the meadow.[^1]

The Summit Ridge Lookout that stands today is a different tower.[^3][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 67.5-foot steel tower built in 1942 by the Civilian Conservation Corps, and it lists the tower as available for rental.[^3] Firelookout.com dates its present tower to 1939 and 1940, so the two sources disagree on its age.[^3][^4]
