In May 1962 the Texas Forest Service announced that it was erecting a fire lookout tower in Hardin County near Grayburg.[^1]

Once complete, the tower was to stand 122 feet high and be built entirely of metal.[^1] The O. C. Braly Construction Company, of Overton, Texas, was doing the work.[^1] Fire supervisor Archie T. Nash announced that the tower would help spot fires across southwest Hardin, north Jefferson and east Liberty counties.[^1] With it, the district would have 15 lookout towers.[^1] From one of the district's towers, a person could scan roughly 150,000 acres of East Texas timber country on an ordinary day.[^1]

Grayburg sits in the far south of Hardin County, along State Highway 326 and roughly eighteen miles west of Beaumont.[^3] The town began in 1908, when a lumber firm built a sawmill alongside a newly constructed railway.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas index lists the Grayburg tower as gone.[^2]

No source we read describes what remains at the tower site today or whether the site is open to the public.[^1][^2][^3]
