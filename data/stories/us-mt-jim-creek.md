Jim Creek is a former fire lookout in Lake County, about 16 miles southeast of Polson, on the Flathead National Forest.[^1][^3] The first building was a two-story log house with a hip roof, which FireLookout.com dates to 1916.[^1] A Forest Service history of the forest includes a 1923 photograph of the Jim Creek lookout, and its caption says the lookout matched the style of the original Spotted Bear lookout, which dates to 1914.[^4]

In 1934 a 50-foot L-4 tower replaced the log house.[^1] Newspaper notes quoted on the Forest Lookouts page describe work on a new tower in late 1959.[^2] In October, Forest Service staff were staying at the lookout while they built a new one, and in November, workers who had been stationed there left their posts when high winds hit.[^2] FireLookout.com instead dates the replacement, a 53-foot treated timber R-6 tower set a quarter of the way up the hillside, to 1960.[^1] The two sources disagree on that year.[^1][^2]

During World War II the forest put women in four of its lookouts, and Jim Creek was one of them.[^4] A 1966 Forest Service list of occupied lookouts includes Jim Creek.[^4] FireLookout.com names one married couple as lookout staff for 1957 and another for 1958.[^1] The Flathead Courier reported in June 1949 that a packer had carried lookout supplies to the station.[^2]

The lookout was staffed through 1972 and removed by 1979, according to FireLookout.com.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks it as gone.[^3]

No source I read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
