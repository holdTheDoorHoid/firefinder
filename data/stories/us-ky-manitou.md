In August 1951 a Dawson Springs ranger for the state forestry division announced that the department had signed a 99-year lease with a Madisonville man for a new lookout tower near Concord church, about 3.6 miles north of Manitou in Hopkins County.[^1] The announcement described a fifty-foot steel tower, due to be finished in early November, that would give the county full coverage for spotting forest fires.[^1]

The item said the new tower would be the third the state forestry division ran in Hopkins County, and it named two others then in operation, near Earlington and near Beulah.[^1] The item describes plans rather than a finished tower: it expected the tower to be finished in early November, and no source we read confirms that it was completed.[^1]

The 1951 item and a 1957 newspaper item about the Charleston tower each name a Brandon as the Dawson Springs ranger. The 1951 item gives his initials as L., and the 1957 item gives them as M. L. The two items do not say whether they name the same man.[^1][^2]

The Forest Lookouts page marks the Manitou tower as "Removed," with no date.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today or whether it is open to visitors, so ask locally before going to look for it.
