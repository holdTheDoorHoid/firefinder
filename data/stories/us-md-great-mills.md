In the summer of 1933 Maryland put up a 120-foot galvanized steel lookout on a hilltop at Great Mills, in St. Mary's County.[^1] State Forester Besley said it should improve the fire record in the county's south, where some of the pine woods on the bay's western shore rank among the most valuable.[^1] The Evening Star reported in November that the tower was finished, and called it the tallest lookout in the state west of the Chesapeake.[^1]

The tower was all steel, rose 120 feet and overlooked the St. Marys River.[^1] A DNR history of the state's towers says Maryland's towers were 80 to 100 feet tall, which would have made a 120-foot tower unusually tall.[^2] A later DNR draft report describes a wider spread, typically 50 to 120 feet, which puts this tower near the top.[^3]

The state handed the whole property to St. Mary's County by 2006.[^3] The DNR draft report says the tower was then taken down and rebuilt for new use at the Lathrop E. Smith Environmental Education Center in Rockville, Montgomery County, and it gives no date for the move.[^3]

Montgomery County Public Schools owns and runs the Lathrop E. Smith center, which sits inside Rock Creek Regional Park.[^4] The school system says the site hosts sixth-grade residential programs and day programs.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower there as standing. Its legend marks towers that keep their structure but have lost the cab with an asterisk, and this entry carries one.[^5] Check with the center before you visit, and do not climb the tower without its permission.

The association lists the original hilltop at Great Mills as gone.[^5] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains there today.
