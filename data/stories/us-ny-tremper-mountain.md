Tremper Mountain Fire Tower stands on the summit of Mount Tremper, above the hamlet of Phoenicia in Ulster County, New York.[^1][^2] The New York Department of Conservation built the steel tower in 1917.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as a 47-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^3] Wikipedia gives the same height.[^2] A 1917 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout history site, instead gives 65 feet.[^4] We could not settle which figure is right.

The tower was built to guard the Catskill Park from forest fires, and it was once part of a statewide network of more than 100 fire towers.[^2][^5] Sources differ on whether the 1917 tower first stood on this summit. The Catskill Center says it is believed to be the original structure erected in 1917.[^5] A hiking guide says it was first built elsewhere and later moved here.[^6] We found nothing confirming a move.

For many decades, observers watched for fires from the steel cab.[^1] A 1930 newspaper, quoted on the same history site, describes an observer on duty from 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.[^4] A 1955 notice records a new person in charge of the tower.[^4] The NHLR says the tower served for 54 years and closed in 1971.[^3] The marker instead says it was decommissioned in 1975, then sat unused and in disrepair until restoration began in the late 1990s.[^1] The two dates conflict.

Volunteers, the Catskill Center and NYS DEC reopened the tower in 2001, according to the marker.[^1] Wikipedia notes that the station was added to the National Register of Historic Places that same year.[^2] The NHLR gives a registration date of April 28, 1997.[^3]

Today the Catskill Fire Tower Project, a program of the Catskill Center, says its fire tower cabs are open to visitors on Saturdays, Sundays and holiday Mondays from Memorial Day weekend through Indigenous Peoples Day weekend.[^5] The NHLR page says a volunteer committee recruits and schedules people to man the tower on weekends and holidays.[^3] The marker places the trail about a mile and a half south of Main Street on Plank Road, and says the hike is about three miles with roughly 2,000 feet of climbing.[^1] The sources do not give a schedule beyond the season, so check with the Catskill Center before you go.
