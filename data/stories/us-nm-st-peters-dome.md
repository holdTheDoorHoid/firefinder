St. Peter's Dome lookout stands at 8,464 feet in Los Alamos County, New Mexico, on Santa Fe National Forest land.[^1] Its present cab is a Forest Service standard plan: a metal 14-by-14-foot flat-top with a catwalk, on a 10-foot cinder block base with a storage room.[^1] The register dates it to 1965, when it replaced an L-4 ground house that the register dates to 1932.[^1][^2]

The site was in use before the cab.[^3] A November 23, 1940 newspaper item, quoted by West Lookouts, reports that a Forest Service tractor crew turned back without reaching five rangers snowbound in the Jemez Mountains.[^3] The rangers phoned in to say they were not suffering hardship, and that their Dome Lookout Station was well stocked.[^3]

On April 26, 1996, the Dome Fire broke out from an improperly extinguished campfire. The fire went on to burn 16,516 acres across Capulin Canyon and the Dome Wilderness.[^4] The register says the lookout is now used as a communications site, because of that fire, vandalism, and a 2001 re-burn by the Las Conchas Fire.[^1] A 2020 report to the New Mexico chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association says the lookout was unstaffed at that time.[^2]

The register recorded the lookout on May 22, 2022.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a standing two-story cab.[^5] A 2016 Los Alamos Mountaineers hike notice gives the approach: 4.5 miles along FS Road 142 from its junction with FS Road 289 (Dome Road), on a 4WD road that nature is reclaiming.[^6] These sources give no current visitor rules for the cab, so check with the Santa Fe National Forest before you go.
