Purple Mountain's lookout stood in Yellowstone National Park, about 10 miles east of West Yellowstone.[^1] The park planned 16 lookouts and built 12.[^2] Its three primary towers were Mount Washburn, built in 1921, Mount Sheridan, built in 1926, and Mount Holmes, built in 1931.[^2] Purple Mountain was one of the secondary lookouts.[^2] A trail listing says a secondary station was added to the peak later, as backup for the primary lookouts.[^3]

From 1931, ranger-naturalists stationed at Madison Junction were given fire-patrol duty, climbing Purple Mountain three times weekly to scan for smoke.[^3] The park then began letting visitors join those climbs, and the peak was among the earliest ranger walks.[^3]

Firelookout.com describes the old tower as a wooden cab, marks it gone, and gives its elevation as 8,438 feet.[^1] An article on the park's lookouts says newer detection methods, such as satellites and overflights, have left many lookouts redundant.[^2] The article makes that point about lookouts in general.[^2]

A trail listing describes a hike that ends at a 3.4-mile overlook, and says scouting the area may turn up traces of the old lookout site.[^3] The National Park Service's rephotography papers list 1935 panoramic photos of Purple Mountain and 2007 negatives of the peak.[^4] No source read describes what stands at the summit now.[^1][^3]
