Domke Mountain overlooks Lake Chelan in Chelan County, Washington.[^1][^2] The summit's first structures, a tree platform for a crow's nest and a log cabin, date from 1920.[^4] In 1930 an Aermotor steel tower, 100 feet tall, went up, and the log cabin might have been swapped out.[^1][^2][^4] WillhiteWeb traces the twelve-ton steel by rail to Wenatchee, then by truck to Chelan, onto a barge, and at last by pack mules to the summit.[^4]

During an August 1927 storm, lightning hit the line running to Domke Lake and set several fires on the mountain.[^3][^4] A fireman put out four of them, and rain put out three more.[^4]

Ross Hall manned the lookout through the summer of 1930.[^4] Jack Elliott worked several summers there in the early 1950s, after McGregor Lookout shut down.[^4] In 1959 a Gonzaga University student on the tower was treed by a female bear and her two cubs from late morning until dark.[^3]

The Aircraft Warning Service watched for enemy aircraft from the summit between March 27, 1942 and July 1, 1943.[^1][^3][^4] A 16x20-foot log cabin was added as living quarters.[^1][^4]

The tower no longer stood by 1970.[^1][^2][^4] Washington Lookouts lists the site as removed, but no source says how the tower came down.[^3] The sources also disagree on the height: 100 feet in three of them, and 110 feet in the 1959 newspaper item.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The Forest Service still lists the Domke Mountain Lookout Trail, a day hike originally cut to reach the old lookout.[^5] No roads connect Chelan to Lucerne, so the Forest Service describes a boat trip of about 40 miles from Chelan, or a drive to Twenty-Five Mile Creek State Park and a shorter boat trip.[^5] From Lucerne Landing, hikers walk to the Domke Lake trailhead, then follow the Domke Lake Trail a little over a mile to the junction with the lookout trail.[^5] There is no camping, so hikers should carry water.[^5] None of the sources describes what, if anything, stands at the summit today.
