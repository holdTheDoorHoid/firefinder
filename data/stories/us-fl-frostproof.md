A fire lookout tower on the old Frostproof road, in Polk County, Florida, was complete when a newspaper item dated 30 March 1933 reported it.[^1] The item is credited to the Avon Park Times and presents the tower as the start of a serious effort against woods and grass fires in the area.[^1] Glen Luke was installed as lookout, and the item says Luke took charge of the tower on a Wednesday morning and was on duty all day.[^1]

The tower was linked to the fire station by telephone, and any sign of a woods or grass fire was to be reported at once.[^1] Fire Chief McCarthy asked anyone planning to burn trash or brush to tell him beforehand, to avoid needless runs.[^1]

Map data still marks a lattice observation tower at the spot, 164 feet (50 m) above sea level, but that does not show whether a structure stands there now.[^2]

No source I read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can visit it.
