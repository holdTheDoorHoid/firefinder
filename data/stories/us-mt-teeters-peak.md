Teeters Peak carried a 50-foot pole tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab, and a listing dates the tower to 1934.[^1] The listing also mentions L-4 cab quarters.[^1] The Forest Service describes the L-4, also called an "Aladdin," as a 14-by-14-foot pre-cut house, produced between 1929 and 1953. It has a peaked roof, with wooden panels set horizontally over the windows.[^3]

The peak sits in Lincoln County, 22 miles south-southeast of Libby, on the Kootenai National Forest.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a tower that is gone.[^2] The listing says the tower was destroyed in 1960.[^1]

The sources do not fully agree. A 1966 topographic map reprint, as described by the seller Pastmaps, shows a pack trail to Teeters Peak.[^4] The seller also describes a lookout tower on the peak, in that mid-1960s terrain, as useful for fire management.[^4] If the tower stood then, that conflicts with the listing's 1960 date.

No source describes the Teeters Peak site today or says whether it is open to visitors.
