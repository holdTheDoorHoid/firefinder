Calispell Peak is in Stevens County, Washington, in the Kaniksu/Colville National Forests, and the U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^1] The lookout began as a camp in 1920, and a 10-by-10-foot frame cab went up the following year.[^1][^4] The Washington State Forester's annual report for 1919 and 1920 said a telephone line ran from the station to Locke, but no building had been put up because money was short.[^3] A 40-foot pole L-4 tower followed in 1936.[^1][^2][^4]

The sources name a string of lookouts. Harold Horton is listed as the lookout in September 1922.[^3] Mr. Dunnigan is named for 1923, and a photo caption from that year on Willhite's page calls him the lookoutman.[^2][^4] Robert C. Stegner, also spelled Stagner in another paper, was the lookout in 1926.[^3] Dorothy Robel is named for 1928 and 1929, and Wyatt Moorhead for 1936.[^2] A June 1930 notice said Ruth Downey would be on guard, and a 1934 paper named Bill Crosset at the station.[^3] The brothers Elmer and George Davis spent a summer there in 1938, the last staff named in the sources we read.[^3]

Sources disagree about how the 1936 tower ended. The register says it was removed in 1955, while firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^4] A Forest Lookouts timeline gives 1955 as "Removed" and says nothing more.[^3]

Willhite describes a four-wheel-drive road that reaches the summit radio towers, and says the summit sits above the main tree line.[^4] The sources we read do not describe a surviving lookout building, and they do not say whether the road or summit is open to the public.
