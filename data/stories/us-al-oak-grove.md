Oak Grove Tower is a 100-foot Aermotor MC-39 steel lookout with a 7-by-7-foot cab, administered by the Alabama Forestry Commission.[^1] The Register dates it to 1949 and describes it as the one lookout the Alabama Forestry Commission has ever put up in Cleburne County, meant to work alongside four nearby Forest Service lookouts on the Talladega National Forest.[^1]

A July 1948 Anniston Star report, as quoted on the Forest Lookouts website, said the state forestry division in Cleburne County had ordered a 100-foot steel tower, a tractor, a truck and radio equipment.[^2] Most of the $8,000 raised came from local sawmill and planing mill operators, and federal funds covered the rest.[^2] The county commissioners provided $1,000 a year to run the new equipment, and the tower was planned for a spot about four miles east of Edwardsville.[^2]

The 1948 report calls the operator the state Division of Forestry, while the Register names the Alabama Forestry Commission. The sources do not say when the name changed.[^1][^2]

The Register says views into Georgia are possible from the cab.[^1] The sources I read do not name who staffed the tower or record any fires it helped fight.

The Register entry went in on January 7, 2009, and it describes the tower in the present tense.[^1] I found no later source about its condition, any move, or any removal.

Today I found no current access rules, visiting hours or rental offer for the tower. Because the Alabama Forestry Commission administers it, check with the agency before going to the site, and do not climb the tower unless its owner has said it is open.[^1]
