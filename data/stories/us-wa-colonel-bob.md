Colonel Bob is a peak of 4,492 feet in Grays Harbor County, Washington, inside the Olympic National Forest.[^1] A Forest Service fire lookout once stood on its summit.[^4] The sources do not agree on when it went up. The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register gives 1933, and Fire Lookout gives 1932.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says it was built on or before 1931, and a newspaper item from August 1931 describes a one-room residence on the peak.[^3][^4] A structural inventory entry, quoted by Forest Lookouts, records a 14-by-14-foot standard R-6 ground house built in 1934.[^4] Three sources call the cab an L-4 design instead.[^1][^2][^3]

Mr. and Mrs. F.B. Hall lived in that residence in the summer of 1931.[^4] The same newspaper item also described a structure whose walls were nearly all glass.[^4] In August 1933, newlyweds Gile and Sadie Mead honeymooned in a glass-walled lookout measuring 14 by 14 feet, according to another newspaper item.[^4] A photo caption dated 1943 on WillhiteWeb names Harold Deery as the lookout.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in 1946.[^3] Weather partly destroyed the house in 1966.[^3] Sources disagree on how it ended in 1967. The Register and Forest Lookouts say it was removed.[^1][^4] WillhiteWeb says it was burned as a safety hazard,[^3] and Fire Lookout says it was destroyed.[^2]

Nothing we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, so we cannot say. The peak sits inside its own wilderness area, and a hiking trail climbs to the summit.[^3] WillhiteWeb gives the hike as seven miles, reached by following the South Shore Lake Quinault Road about six miles from Highway 101.[^3] Check current trail and access conditions with the Olympic National Forest before you go.
