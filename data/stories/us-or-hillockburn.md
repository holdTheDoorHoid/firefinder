Hillockburn is a lookout site in Clackamas County, Oregon, about eight miles southeast of Estacada, in the Mount Hood National Forest.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service administered it.[^1]

Work began in 1928 with a rag camp, and a cabin followed around 1936.[^1] Firelookout.com describes the later work as a cabin and a wooden tower.[^2] Panorama photographs of the lookout were taken on October 25, 1933.[^1][^3]

In 1941 the lookout was manned for 73 days. Reports went to a reporting station at Oak Grove, and communications ran through Estacada Telephone and Telegraph.[^3]

In September 1942 the site was activated as an Aircraft Warning Service station tied to the Portland Filter Center, and it was deactivated in October 1943.[^3] The Forest Service kept the site for fire detection afterward.[^3]

In January 1945 a free balloon, thought to be of Japanese origin, was recovered from wooded country about ten miles southeast of Estacada.[^3] The account places the spot a quarter-mile north of the Hillockburn road, which runs from the Dodge district to the lookout.[^3]

By the 1960s the lookout no longer stood.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts marks the site as removed but gives no date.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the site today, or whether visitors can reach it. A Forest Service page for Hillockburn Trail #516 says the lower part of Forest Road 45 remains closed. That trail runs from the road down to the South Fork of the Clackamas River, and the page does not mention the lookout.[^4]
