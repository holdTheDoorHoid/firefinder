The Stonega tower was a fire watch on Little Stone Mountain, near Big Stone Gap in Wise County.[^1] The main account of it is a 2019 local history by Lawrence J. Fleenor, Jr., titled "The Stonega Tower, the First Fire Tower in Virginia."[^1]

The first watchtower on the mountain went up sometime between 1910 and 1917.[^1] The author does not name the builder but judges it most likely McCorkle or the Virginia Coal and Iron Company, and says it was meant to overlook a logging operation.[^1] The author knows of no documentation for its site and offers only a best guess.[^1]

By 1917 the tower had been moved to the ridge top.[^1] The second tower there was built by Virginia Coal and Iron with the state forestry department, and it was framed with sawed wooden beams set into cracks in the rock.[^1]

The Civilian Conservation Corps later built an iron tower with an iron cab on National Forest land, about a mile to the east and roughly 300 feet (91 m) higher than the earlier site at the Stonega 9 survey marker.[^1] The author does not know the exact date but believes it went up not long after 1933.[^1] The Stonega name passed to this newer tower, at least among outsiders, who to this day mix it up with the wooden one.[^1]

Staff worked the towers only in fire season.[^1] The cab held a hand-crank telephone and an Osborne Fire Finder, a flat 360-degree protractor with a pivoting sight bar.[^1] Its sights could be aimed at a smoke plume, and the compass reading was radioed to a central office and to other towers, so crossed readings from several towers could place the fire on a map.[^1] The last full-time attendant was Bob Dorton, who owned a general store beneath the pass where the access road tops out.[^1] Don Fig of Appalachia was among the last part-time attendants.[^1]

The tower sat idle for years before it was demolished in the 1970s, according to the account.[^1] Its parts were stored at an old Jefferson National Forest office in Big Stone Gap.[^1] A private owner bought them and hauled them to a cabin atop Morris's Butt, intending to rebuild the tower.[^1] The account, dated May 4, 2019, says the pieces still lie there, not reassembled.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Stonega tower as gone.[^2] A Hiking Project photo dated December 17, 2020, is captioned "Remains of old Stonega Fire Tower"; the page places it only near Big Stone Gap.[^3] According to the account, the parts sit at a private owner's mountain cabin atop Morris's Butt, so visitors should ask the owner before going near.[^1]
