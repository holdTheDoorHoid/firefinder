The Pony Lake tower stands in the Sand Hills of Cherry County, on the Valentine National Wildlife Refuge. The refuge was created in 1935 by an executive order of President Franklin Roosevelt, after the Bureau of Biological Survey bought roughly 70,000 acres of lake country "as a breeding ground for migratory birds and other wildlife," working with the Nebraska Game Preserve and Fish Commission.[^1] By October of that year, Civilian Conservation Corps crews had arrived and begun work under the Biological Survey's direction; the agency also planned a network of canals and diversion structures to regulate water levels across the refuge's lakes.[^1] The following August, 150 Works Progress Administration workers were also on the refuge, building ninety miles of fence near Hackberry Lake to keep cattle out and mark where hunting was prohibited.[^1]

It was this Depression-era workforce that put up the tower itself, a 100-foot steel structure of the Type MC-39 Aermotor design.[^1] The Bureau of Biological Survey that oversaw the refuge's construction became the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service in 1940, and the Service still administers both the refuge and the tower.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the structure, cataloged as Valentine NWR 2 – Pony Lake, as standing in Cherry County, with its cab still in place rather than reduced to a bare tower.[^2] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in August 2026 as US 1847, NE 5.[^1]

No source consulted here says when fire-watch staffing began or ended, or whether the cab is open to visitors. As a working structure inside a national wildlife refuge, any visit would be governed by the refuge's own rules rather than by a lookout-specific access program, and climbing it should not be assumed to be permitted without checking with refuge staff first. For now, the Pony Lake tower is best known as a surviving piece of the CCC and WPA infrastructure that shaped the refuge in the 1930s, standing watch over the Sand Hills lake country it was built to protect.
