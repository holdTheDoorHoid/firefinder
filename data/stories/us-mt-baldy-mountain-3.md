Baldy Mountain, called Bald Mountain in the early newspapers, had a forest lookout in the Beaverhead National Forest by 1911.[^1] That October a lookout station on the peak supported the forest's riding patrol, and a guard rode to the summit nearly every day to watch the surrounding country for fires.[^1] A telephone line ran up the mountain to the supervisor's office in Dillon.[^1] An August 1912 Dillon Tribune item called the station one of the highest fire lookouts in the country and said it had "just been established," so the 1911 mention and the 1912 wording do not clearly agree on a start date.[^1]

Lookout men worked the peak in the next few years. In 1913 Orlando Connolly was stationed on top, and a windstorm tore his tent away.[^1] In 1915 a new station building on the summit was finished with a sheet-iron roof and lightning rods. A lightning bolt had caused some rebuilding during construction.[^1] That summer Arlie Connelly, a ranger who had been hurt earlier, was seriously injured again and could not return to work that season.[^1]

A 1956 Coast and Geodetic Survey description of a marker on the summit places it 30 feet southeast of a lookout building.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page calls the 1950s structure a cabin and marks it gone.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list also marks the site Gone and gives it no register number.[^3]

No source describes the summit today, so what, if anything, remains is unknown, and none says whether the peak is open to visitors. The 1956 survey notes describe the route as ending at the end of truck travel, followed by a pack of about three and a half hours to the top.[^1]
