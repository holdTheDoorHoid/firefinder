The Fly Lake lookout stood in Jefferson County, Oregon, on the Deschutes National Forest, about 17 miles north-northeast of Sisters.[^3] Its first lookout was a tree platform. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and the Fire Lookout page date it to 1930, while the Forest Lookouts page gives 1934 for a tree with a platform in its top, as a secondary lookout point.[^2][^3][^1]

In June 1941 the Bend Bulletin reported a new position at Fly Lake, with a tower built in a tree and a tent at its foot for living quarters.[^1] Allen Madsen of Camp Sherman patrolled it, and was moved to the Black Butte lookout that August.[^1]

A 1948 Bend Bulletin item reported a new 53-foot lookout tower near Fly Lake, with Paul Striebel as its first lookout (the item spells it Streibel).[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and the Fire Lookout page instead give a 40-foot wooden tower erected in 1951.[^2][^3] The sources disagree on the date and height of the tower.

Paul Striebel was the fireman at Fly Lake in 1946, when the lookout sat in the top of a 73-foot tree.[^1] In June 1953 Mr. and Mrs. Joseph Burns were on duty at the station.[^1] David Snow was the lookout-fireman in 1965, the last year the lookout was staffed.[^1]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and the Fire Lookout page both say the tower was removed in 1965.[^2][^3] The tree platform was still standing in the 1980s.[^2] The register says the Forest Service later decided it needed to remove the old platform tree, which had become an attractive nuisance.[^2]

In 1968 the state highway department held a contract for five miles of road to Fly Lake Lookout, and the remaining four miles were under a Forest Service contract that started at the lookout.[^1] Nothing we read describes what remains of the site today, and we found no current information about access.
