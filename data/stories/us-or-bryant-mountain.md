Bryant Mountain's lookout story starts with a camp and a tree.[^2] Observers used them on the site in the late 1950s and early 1960s.[^2] In December 1963 the Bureau of Land Management (BLM) announced a lookout building for the mountain, southeast of Bonanza, to be built the following year.[^2] A two-story enclosed lookout house was built there in 1964.[^2][^4]

The site later served as a weather station for the National Climatic Data Center, which started in 1967 and closed on October 31, 1976.[^2] The lookout's first life ended in July 1979.[^2] The Bryant Fire was discovered on July 17 about 25 miles southeast of Klamath Falls, just north of the California border, and by July 20 it was reported at nearly 10,000 acres.[^5] A newspaper report quoted by one lookout history site says the fire destroyed the lookout, and two other sites agree.[^2][^3][^4]

A replacement went up about 50 yards from the old site, on Loveness Timber Co. property.[^2] Johnson Construction of Klamath Falls built it for about $90,000.[^2] The register dates it to 1981, while firelookout.com and Peakbagging say it was built in 1980.[^1][^3][^4] Sources also disagree on materials: the register and firelookout.com call it timber, and Peakbagging calls it steel.[^1][^3][^4]

A July 1981 newspaper report says the lookout was back in operation two years after the fire.[^2] The lookout moved in that June and was expected to stay until early October.[^2] Unlike some towers, the living quarters sat inside the tower itself rather than at its base.[^2]

In 2007 the structure was declared surplus and transferred to the landowner, as is.[^1][^2] A photo taken on July 2, 2017 showed the tower in good condition but not in use.[^4] The access road was in poor repair and needed major work to be usable.[^4] The site is on private land.[^4] The register added the lookout on June 22, 2022.[^1] I found no rental listing for it in the sources checked, so none is recorded here.
