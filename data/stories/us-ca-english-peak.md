English Peak Lookout is a 10-by-10-foot ground house rather than a tower, built in 1955 on the summit of English Peak in the Marble Mountain Wilderness of the Klamath National Forest.[^1][^3][^4] Before the house was built, spotters on the summit rock used a magneto telephone to report new fires.[^4]

The Register accepted the lookout on November 1, 1994, as US 94, CA 3.[^1] SummitPost says it was staffed until 1973.[^3] Volunteers restored it in 1996, when it was in poor condition, and repainted it in 2002.[^3]

The Tom Taylor Cabin, about a mile below the summit, housed the lookout's staff.[^3] The sources give different dates for the present cabin. The Register says it was rebuilt in 1940, SummitPost says the present structure dates to the 1940s, and a 1942 ranger's account describes building the English Peak Cabin that year.[^1][^2][^3]

In October 2024 the Forest Fire Lookout Association shared a Klamath National Forest post about a volunteer family. The family "built, painted and installed new shutters which help protect the structure during the harsh winters," and had given the building a fresh coat of paint the year before.[^4]

Reaching the lookout means a hike.[^3] SummitPost puts the trip from Little North Fork Campground at 14 miles, with a 5,300-foot elevation gain.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as standing.[^5] The sources read do not say whether the house is open to visitors, so check with the Klamath National Forest before setting out.
