Bearskull Lookout stood in Shoshone County, in the St. Joe National Forest.[^1][^2] The Idaho Fire Lookouts site says the name came from an actual bear skull, suspended from a tree near the top.[^3]

The first structures went up in 1922: a crow's nest on the peak and a log cabin about half a mile to the southeast.[^2][^3] In 1929 a 60-foot pole tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab was built, alongside a log cabin used as living quarters.[^1][^2][^3]

Two staff members are named, for 1925 and 1930. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists John "Red" Dennis for 1925, who staffed it in the fall.[^3] It lists Boyd Hanna for 1930 and notes that he also worked at the Waldo lookout that season.[^3]

All three sources date the end of the lookout to 1953, but they differ on how.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^3] Forest Lookouts describes 1953 as an abandonment instead.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the site as gone.[^4]

Notes on the Idaho Fire Lookouts page, dated August 19, 2025, describe a brief walk up to the site.[^3] The writers say they did not locate the crow's nest or the tower's foundation.[^3] They did turn up some tie-downs, remnants of the cabin and miscellaneous items.[^3] The page lists the site as hike-in.[^3] No source we read gives its access rules.
