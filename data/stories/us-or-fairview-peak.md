Fairview Peak sits in the Umpqua National Forest in Lane County, Oregon, in the Bohemia Mining District southeast of Eugene.[^1] A cabin went up on the summit in 1912,[^2][^4] and an open-air alidade tower followed in 1914.[^1]

The cupola that came next is where the sources part ways. The register dates it to 1921, and a 1921 newspaper item reported that a lookout house had just been completed on the summit.[^1][^3] Two other histories give 1924 instead.[^2][^4] The tower added in 1936 is also described three ways: 30 feet by the register, 20 feet by two other sites, and 29 feet by a third history.[^1][^2][^4][^3]

Staffing is documented only in patches. A June 1931 item in The Sentinel said a Forest Service employee staffed the tower for about three months, or through the fire season.[^3] In 1934 Rex Wheeler was the summer lookout, and his wife was with him.[^3] From the late 1950s until 1964, an Air Force gap filler radar station used the site.[^1][^5]

The present 53-foot tower with an R-6 cab was built in 1972 to replace the 1936 tower.[^1][^2] Visitors could once stay in the 1921 cupola, which later became a storage building.[^1] The tower still stands.[^4]

The register lists the lookout as available for rental, and the Forest Service's current page offers overnight stays for a fee.[^1][^6]

Sources disagree on when the rental program ended. A 2022 trip report said the lookout had been rented for years and was pulled into active duty for the 2022 fire season,[^5] while a 2026 visit report says it was taken off the rental program in 2015 because of structural issues.[^4] Both describe work on the tower around 2022: the trip report says the lookout appears to have received a new superstructure,[^5] and the 2026 report places the repairs in 2022.[^4]

A visitor on August 30, 2026 found the tower not staffed that season and the road gated.[^4] The same account says 2024 was the last staffed season, citing a TikTok user's record of that season.[^4] It also reports a few days of staffing in July 2025, cut short when the contract was not renewed.[^4]

The Forest Service page, last updated August 10, 2026, marks the site as open.[^6] Travel Oregon, however, says it is temporarily closed for critical infrastructure repair.[^7] The last mile of road is steep, rocky and rough, and high-clearance vehicles are strongly recommended.[^6] Because the listings disagree, check with the North Umpqua Ranger Station before going.[^6] Until the Forest Service confirms the tower is open to visitors, do not climb it.
