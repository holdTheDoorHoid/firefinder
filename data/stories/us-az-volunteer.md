Volunteer Lookout stands on Volunteer Mountain in Coconino County, Arizona, on the grounds of the Navajo Army Depot.[^1][^2] The Army and the Kaibab National Forest operate it together, and the register says it is closed to public access.[^1]

The mountain had a lookout by 1921.[^2] That year the Williams News reported work on a trail up Volunteer Mountain to the lookout tower on top.[^2] H.S. Perry won the bid to build the trail, and a November 1921 item said the trail would let the summer lookout reach fires more quickly.[^2]

The register gives 1963 as the build year.[^1] In its account, a 14-foot-square CL-100 cab, mounted on a 30-foot metal tower and fitted with a catwalk, succeeded a wooden lookout from the 1930s.[^1] The 1921 reports already describe a lookout tower on the mountain, which does not fit a 1930s start date.[^1][^2] The sources disagree on this, and the sources read do not settle it.[^1][^2]

The National Register lists a "Volunteer Lookout Cabin" under number 87002481, added in 1988.[^3] Its record describes a building with a significant year of 1939.[^3] The sources read do not say whether that listing covers the tower, the earlier wooden lookout, or another structure.[^2][^3]

The register accepted the lookout on March 15, 2006, as US 665, AZ 60, on a nomination from Dave Lorenz.[^1]

Today the register describes the lookout as closed to public access, so it is not a site to visit or climb.[^1] The sources read do not give its current staffing, or say whether the cab is still used for fire watch.[^1]
