The Grassy lookout stood in Bollinger County, Missouri, at 781 feet (238 m) above sea level.[^1] A state manual for 1947-48 lists the towersite as owned by the Missouri Conservation Commission.[^2] Forty acres were acquired for the site in 1946-47, and a 60-foot wooden tower was in place by June 28, 1947.[^2][^3]

The towers were meant to guard against wildfire. A 1949 newspaper item quoted on the Weebly page said the Grassy and Gipsy towers went up so foresters could protect the forest from fire.[^2] The same item says a towerman moved in after the towers were up.[^2]

KRCU says the tower was in service in time for the dry years of the early 1950s.[^3] The same article reports that a steel tower replaced the wooden one in 1957-58.[^3] The state annual report quoted on the Weebly page, however, describes a steel tower replacing a temporary wooden tower at Flat Rock, so the sources do not agree on that replacement.[^2]

The tower came down in the summer of 2004.[^1][^3] Sources differ on who took it apart. KRCU says the Missouri Department of Conservation dismantled it.[^3] A January 2006 visit report on the register says the state auctioned the tower, a buyer from Poplar Bluff bought it, and a four-person crew took it apart by hand after vandalism had left it in poor repair.[^1]

In a June 2025 article, KRCU says the base is still on the 40-acre tract that the department manages as the Grassy Towersite.[^3] The article calls the spot a quiet site, reached by a short drive down County Road 508, and says that the places where the towers once stood will be peaceful locations on public land.[^3] The 2006 visit report says the site then sat on State Public Hunting Access land and had no signed directions.[^1]
