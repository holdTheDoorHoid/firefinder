Big Spring State Park, in Carter County, was a Civilian Conservation Corps site in the 1930s, and its fire tower came out of that work.[^1][^2][^3] A historical marker near Van Buren credits Camp 734 with a fire tower in the park's southwest corner, built between November 1933 and April 1934 to aid forest protection.[^2] A March 1934 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout history site, was still describing "the initial steps toward the building of a fire tower."[^1] The marker's dates fall in the same months, so the completion date is not clear.[^2] A state report for 1939 to 1941 lists an 85-foot steel tower.[^1]

The tower had a towerman by 1943.[^1] That September a garage mechanic took the job at Big Spring State Park, after the previous towerman resigned and moved to St. Louis.[^1]

In 1947 and 1948, the state's official manual listed the towersite as owned by the Missouri Conservation Commission and located in the park.[^1] The Big Spring Historic District, which the National Park Service describes as a cultural landscape, entered the National Register of Historic Places in 1981.[^3] The NPS lists a "fire/lookout tower" among the CCC-era features in that district, but its wording does not say plainly whether the tower survives.[^3]

According to the NPS, the state park board handed three parks to the National Park Service in 1969.[^3] The NPS now presents the 3,966-acre cultural landscape as part of Ozark National Scenic Riverways, and it notes that hiking, picnicking and camping are available in the historic district.[^3]

No source describes the tower's condition today, or whether visitors may climb it. Check with the park before you go, and do not climb the tower unless the park says it is open to climbers.
