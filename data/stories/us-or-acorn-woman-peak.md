Acorn Woman Peak Lookout sits in the Siskiyou Mountains of Oregon's Rogue River National Forest, near the California border. It's a classic L-4 groundhouse with a catwalk, built in 1943 according to the National Historic Lookout Register, though the Recreation.gov listing gives 1942 — the two sources disagree by a year.[^1][^2] For decades it was known as Squaw Peak Lookout; the Register notes the name was changed to Acorn Woman Peak roughly five years before an update posted in September 2026.[^1]

The lookout's job was tied to its taller neighbor, Dutchman Peak. Early in the fire season, before snow cleared from Dutchman Peak's lookout, Squaw Peak served as the active station; it was staffed full-time through 1966, after which it continued to be used for emergencies and for that early-season gap.[^3] The structure was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as "Squaw Peak Lookout," as part of a Forest Service multiple-listing covering historic structures on the Rogue River National Forest.[^4]

The lookout has since been restored and is open to visitors.[^1] It's glassed in on all sides, giving an unobstructed view of the steep drop-offs and ridgelines of the Siskiyou range.[^2] Today it's part of the Forest Service's recreation rental program, bookable through Recreation.gov during the snow-free season, with stays capped at five consecutive nights.[^2] The access road is steep and winding, may not suit passenger cars or large trucks, and is gated with a combination lock whose code comes with the reservation confirmation.[^2] Guests should watch for rattlesnakes, ticks, and poison oak in the area, and bring most of their own supplies.[^2]
