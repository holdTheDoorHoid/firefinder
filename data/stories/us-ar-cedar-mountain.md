Cedar Mountain is listed as a fire lookout in Conway County, Arkansas. The Forest Lookouts website gives one date for it: 1937, when the lookout was constructed.[^1] The same site's Arkansas list places it in the Ozark National Forest.[^2]

Towers like this one were part of a wider push to spot smoke early. After a major fire in 1910, the Forest Service adopted a new rule, and finding fires early became urgent.[^3] During the Depression, Civilian Conservation Corps crews put up 3,100 towers, many of them in Arkansas's Ouachita and Ozark-St. Francis national forests. The Arkansas Forestry Commission, set up in 1931, put up many more.[^3] Cedar Mountain's 1937 date falls within that building period.[^1][^3]

Watchers often lived in or near their towers and spent daylight hours searching the woods for smoke. A watcher who spotted a possible fire reported it by telephone. Watch-tower detection was the main way fires were found until the 1960s, after which aircraft offered a more precise and simpler way to locate them.[^3]

Today no source we found describes what, if anything, remains at the Cedar Mountain site, or whether the public can reach it.
