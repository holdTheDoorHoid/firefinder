The Mohican Fire Tower stands in Mohican-Memorial State Forest in Ashland County.[^4] It was built in 1934 and is 80 feet tall, according to the state's marker and a newspaper story reprinted from the Ohio Department of Natural Resources.[^1][^2][^6] The register gives a height of 125 feet, which disagrees with those sources.[^4]

A 1934 item in the Chillicothe Gazette described the tower as the core of a fire protection unit that had not yet been organized.[^3] When built, the tower offered an average view of about 10 miles in every direction, and its towerman could keep watch over roughly 200,000 acres.[^1]

A 1934 newspaper item reported that James Stevenson, caretaker of the property, would staff the tower that season.[^3] In 1940 Stevenson, by then a state forest ranger, spotted a farm fire from the tower. The blaze burned across four acres of timber before volunteer firemen contained it.[^3] By March 1967 the observer on duty was Mrs. Elmina Rohrbaugh, who had been employed there since 1949.[^3]

The state's marker dates the end of fire-tower use to 1978, when Green Ridge Tower in Pike State Forest was the final one still staffed.[^5] The marker adds that the tower remains a symbol of forest conservation.[^2] The sources we read do not say when Mohican itself stopped being staffed.

The register lists the tower as NHLR US 1012 and gives its registration date as September 2, 2014.[^4] Asked whether the tower is staffed, the register answers "No," with no date given.[^4]

Climbers may use the stairs when the weather permits, and they take the risk themselves. From the top, the view takes in forests and hills all around.[^1] A 2018 Richland Source story, reprinted by the Ashland Source, says the view from 80 feet has only been possible since 1934.[^6]
