The Steamboat Point lookout stood in Douglas County, in the Umpqua National Forest, about half a mile west of the summit at 2,956 feet.[^1][^2]

According to Oregon Lookouts, the Roseburg Chieftain reported in September 1933 that the Civilian Conservation Corps was to build a 14-by-14 lookout before the snow flew.[^3] The same history says a 15-foot round fir timber tower with an L-4 cab was built that year.[^3] It is not clear whether that is the structure the paper described. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and firelookout.com instead describe a 15-foot pole tower of the L-5 type, built in 1933.[^1][^2] Lester Moe took panorama photographs there in August 1933.[^3]

The Roseburg News-Review reported in August 1937 that Jack Hutchins was back at the Steamboat Point lookout after several weeks fighting a fire at Randle, Washington.[^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the tower was removed in 1965.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] The Oregon Lookouts history says only that it was removed, with no date.[^3] The association's county list also marks the tower Gone.[^4]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3] Anyone planning a visit should check with the Umpqua National Forest first.
