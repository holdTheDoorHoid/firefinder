Woods Mountain is a group of small peaks in the northwest of McDowell County, North Carolina, and its highest point is 3,646 feet (1,111 m) [^1]. TopoQuest's map database lists a Woods Mountain Lookout Tower at 3,635 feet (1,108 m) above sea level [^2].

In the 1920s and 1930s, forestry groups put up dozens of lookouts across Western North Carolina, forming the core of the region's firefighting effort [^3]. The state's first steel fire towers date from 1928 [^4], and in the 1930s the Civilian Conservation Corps built a large number of towers in the mountains [^4]. By 1940, the state had a network of lookout towers [^4]. Towers were usually staffed during fire season, which ran from October through May [^4].

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the tower's listed location today, or whether the site is open to visitors. A Carolina Country review of Peter J. Barr's guide notes that lookout towers can be dangerous and that many are not routinely maintained [^3]. Do not climb a structure you find there without first checking who manages the land and what condition it is in.
