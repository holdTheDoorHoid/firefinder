A 1917 newspaper report describes forest officers discussing a station on Terry Peak.[^3] Plans were announced in 1948, and in 1949 a local paper hoped the stone understructure would be finished that fall.[^3] The Register gives the build years as 1949 to 1950, while firelookout.com gives 1950 alone.[^1][^2]

The tower was a three-story stone structure.[^4][^2] A 1950 newspaper said its cab would be glass, 14 by 14 feet, and that the lookout would be the only one in the Black Hills with electricity.[^3] The Register calls Terry Peak the first fire tower in the Black Hills to receive electricity.[^1]

The lookout was nearly finished by late August 1950, and Vernon Tollefson, who had been at Cement Ridge, was due on duty there from September 1.[^3] Firelookout.com lists a run of named lookouts into the late 1960s.[^2]

The site had its rough days too. In July 1955 lightning hit the lookout, knocked out the radios and telephones, and only partial power came back that night.[^3] By 1962 so many visitors were coming that the Forest Service split the public from the fire work.[^3] The lookout room was put on stilts, and a visitor room went in between it and the lookouts' bachelor quarters. More than 65,000 people visited in 1962.[^3]

The Register says the tower was out of service as a fire lookout by 1973.[^1] Firelookout.com says the enclosed visitor deck and the upper cab came down in 1978,[^2] and a 2011 history book says the top two levels were removed in the 1970s, leaving the stone base with an open viewing platform.[^3] The dates disagree, so the removal is best placed somewhere in the 1970s.

Today the stone base remains. The Register says the cab has been removed and that the Forest Service turned the site into a viewing area for tourists.[^1] A Black Hills hiking site says the tower is open to the public and warns that the road up can be scary at times.[^5] The Register lists a registration date of January 2, 2026, under the number US 1810, SD 10.[^1]
