Stone Mountain, in the town of Guildhall in Essex County, Vermont, was the site of a fire lookout tower.[^1] In August 1922 the Bennington Evening Banner reported that work had started on two fire towers and their cabins in Essex County, one of them a new tower on Stone Mountain.[^1] The paper said the new tower would let watchers see heavily timbered country that no other tower could see.[^1] The item describes the start of construction and does not say when the tower was finished.[^1]

A state report for the term ending June 30, 1938 says a new lookout cabin was begun, with a log lean-to, fireplaces and tables, and that the old cabin was moved to serve as wood storage.[^1] A biennial report covering 1949 and 1950 says the tower's ventilator was replaced with a weatherproof cap.[^1] A 1957 to 1958 state report notes that a commercial telephone line passed within a mile of the Stone Mountain station. The state connected its tower line to it and salvaged three miles of pole line.[^1]

Staff appear in local papers. In October 1948 the Essex County Herald said a man was employed at the fire tower, and that his family had spent the summer at Stone Mountain with him.[^1] In May 1971 the News and Citizen reported that a woman was stationed on Stone Mountain in Guildhall and would be in charge of the fire tower.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Vermont list gives the Stone Mountain tower as gone.[^2] The same list has a West Topsham site in Orange County, described as a relocated Stone Mountain and as a radio tower, also gone.[^2] Neither source gives a date or a reason for either site being gone.[^1][^2] The clippings page also carries the word "Removed" near a National Geodetic Survey entry for a "Stone Mtn Lookout Tower" marker, and it does not say what "Removed" refers to.[^1]

The list marks both sites as gone and gives no description of either, so no source here says what, if anything, stands at them today, or whether either is open to visitors.[^2]
