Granite Mountain, east of North Bend in the Cascades, was one of the Forest Service's earliest lookout sites. A simple cabin went up first, then in 1924 crews replaced it with a D-6 cupola cabin at the summit.[^1] That structure served through World War II, when it did a stint as an Aircraft Warning Service post watching the skies for enemy aircraft, a duty shared by many Pacific Northwest lookouts during the war.[^1] The Forest Service replaced it again in the mid-1950s, most sources say 1955, with the ten-foot L-4 tower that still stands today, though the Snoqualmie Fire Lookouts Association gives the upgrade year as 1956.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout sits on the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, reached by the Granite Mountain Trail, a steep climb of roughly four miles and 3,500 feet of elevation gain from the Pratt Lake trailhead.[^1][^4] It's a popular, well-known hike, with open slopes that fill with wildflowers and huckleberries in late summer and views of the Cascades from the top.[^4]

Volunteers have cared for the tower for years, staffing it from June through September, and the Snoqualmie Fire Lookouts Association reported it undergoing renovation in the early 2020s with the goal of reopening for full service and visitors.[^1] The Forest Service's current listing says visitors are welcome inside during the day when the lookout is open, and the Snoqualmie Fire Lookouts Association notes that camping is not permitted at the lookout or on its deck and that there's no potable water at the site, so hikers need to carry their own.[^1][^4] Anyone heading up should check current conditions with the Snoqualmie Ranger District first, since staffing and opening schedules depend on volunteer availability each season.
