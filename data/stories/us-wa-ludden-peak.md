Ludden Peak is a 5,854-foot (1,784 m) summit in Jefferson County, inside Olympic National Park.[^1][^2]

In September 1935 an Oakland Tribune item reported that a pair of eagles had attacked Wallace R. Edwards, 22, who was working as a fire lookout atop Ludden Peak on the Olympic Peninsula.[^1] He drove the birds off and kept going until he reached timber, a descent of nearly 1,000 feet on steep ground.[^1]

The lookout cabin the registers record stands on Dodger Point, and the easiest route up Ludden Peak runs via Dodger Point.[^2][^3][^4] The Forest Service built it in 1933, possibly with help from the Civilian Conservation Corps, when the land was Olympic National Forest.[^3][^4] It measures 14 feet by 14 feet (4.3 m).[^3] The sources do not show whether the 1935 lookout was that cabin or a post on Ludden Peak itself.

During World War II the cabin served as an Aircraft Warning Service station.[^3] Of thirteen such stations constructed, only Dodger Point and Pyramid Peak Lookout remain in Olympic National Park.[^3] The National Register of Historic Places added the cabin on July 13, 2007.[^3]

Volunteers restored the cabin in 2017, with grant funding from Washington's National Park Fund and the REI Foundation.[^4] It sits at mile 13 of the Dodger Point Trail, and it is one of the last two lookouts still standing in the park.[^3][^4]
