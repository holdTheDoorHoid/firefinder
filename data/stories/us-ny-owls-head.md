Owls Head Mountain sits in Hamilton County, west-southwest of the hamlet of Long Lake.[^2] A forest fire in 1908 destroyed nearby Long Lake West, and the state answered with a wooden fire tower on the mountain in 1911.[^2] The first observer on the state's payroll started that August, so the post was staffed from the beginning.[^1][^3]

The wooden tower was replaced in 1919 by the steel tower that stands today.[^2] It is a 35-foot Aermotor LS-40 with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and the state's Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) built it.[^1][^2]

Observers watched from the tower for decades, and the path to the summit still runs past the foundation of the old observer's cabin.[^2] Sources disagree on when the watch ended. The NHLR says the tower was staffed until 1970.[^1] The payroll roster places the last observer in 1968 and says the tower was not staffed in 1969.[^3] Wikipedia dates the closure to 1970, when airplanes took over the spotting work.[^2]

The observers' cabin burned in 1979.[^1] A 2003 restoration by the DEC and the Friends of Owl Head Fire Tower reopened the lookout to hikers.[^1][^2] The Friends group works with the DEC on upkeep, though staff changes on both sides have slowed some repairs.[^1] A visit report on the NHLR called for paint on the tower, the landing boards and the stair treads, and it noted significant graffiti inside the cab.[^1] The register listed the lookout in 2006.[^1]

Today the tower is a hike. The ascent is 3.2 miles with about 1,200 feet of climb, starting from a trailhead on Endion Road, off New York Route 30.[^2] The NHLR reports that the trail is well marked and the old cabin site is signed.[^1] Check current conditions with the DEC before you go.
