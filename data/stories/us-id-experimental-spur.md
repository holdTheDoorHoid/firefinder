Experimental Spur was a crow's nest rather than a tower: a platform built in a tree on a knob in the Kaniksu National Forest, about 12 miles north-northeast of Priest River in Bonner County, Idaho.[^1][^4]

The platform went up around 1928 and is described as a 60-foot crow's nest.[^2][^4] Stakes driven into the trunk served as the ladder.[^3]

The lookout was abandoned in the 1940s.[^2][^4] Sources differ on how long the platform lasted.[^1][^3] The firelookout.com page says it was still there in the 1980s.[^1] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page, citing Ray Kresek's book, says the crow's nest was still there as of 2015.[^3]

A September 2022 post by the Idaho Fire Lookouts team says they found the tree by its telephone wire, which was still intact.[^3] Aside from the wire and the ladder stakes, the team reported that nothing is left.[^3] WillhiteWeb's page also reports the tree still there in 2022.[^4] It finds the crow's nest tree still standing but thinks the platform is gone, and it admits the platform was hard to judge because it was so small.[^4]

Directions begin in Priest River, follow the East Side Road to the Experimental Headquarters turnoff, then take Gisborne Mountain Road (597B) to a switchback at about 2,680 feet.[^4] The walk from there is about 0.6 miles one way on an abandoned spur road, and the access road is fair dirt.[^4] Because the platform appears gone, the visit is a walk to see the tree rather than a climb.[^4]
