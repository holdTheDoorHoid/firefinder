Moose Point is a lookout site in Flathead County, about 16 miles north-northwest of Whitefish.[^1] The site is listed under the Flathead National Forest.[^1] Firelookout.com dates its L-4 cab to 1931 and says the cab was destroyed in 1957. It does not say how.[^1]

The lookout was staffed in the 1934 fire season.[^2] The Daily Inter Lake, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, names Charles Burtsfield as the man stationed there.[^2] A September 22, 1934 item says the lookout had already closed for the year.[^2] In August 1951 the Hungry Horse News reported that fire danger had made manning the lookout necessary.[^2] The same item says Frank Kohoutek hiked the 12 miles to the summit on a Sunday.[^2]

The sources disagree on the elevation. Firelookout.com gives 7,531 feet, and the 1951 item says 7,521 feet.[^1][^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the lookout as gone.[^3] We found no source describing what, if anything, remains at the site now, or whether visitors may go there. Check with the Flathead National Forest before you plan a trip.
