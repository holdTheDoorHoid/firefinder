The Civilian Conservation Corps built a steel fire lookout on Petersburg Mountain, southeast of Cobleskill in Schoharie County, in 1940.[^1][^2] The finished tower stood 67 feet 6 inches tall, taller than the 65-foot tower a 1939 newspaper had announced for the spot.[^1][^3]

Observers first staffed the tower in 1941, when they logged 7 fires and 223 visitors that year.[^1] In April 1953, after talks with Air Force defense officials, observers agreed to watch the sky and report to the Albany Filter Center.[^3] That August a six-year-old boy died in a fall from the tower; state police said he had likely slipped off the platform.[^3]

The tower closed at the end of the 1971 season, as aerial detection came into wider use for fire watching.[^1] Schoharie County bought it in 1973 for $10 for communications use, and later removed the cab and added 30 feet of height for more antennas.[^3][^1] In 1974 police arrested six juveniles from the Cobleskill area over an alleged break-in at the tower's communications center.[^3] Later the tower was dismantled, and an antenna support structure took its place.[^1]

The tower's story did not end on the mountain. In 1999 Al Moulin had it trucked to his property in Esperance, NY, to keep it from going to scrap.[^2] In 2002 Barry Knight took possession of the steel and moved it to West Hurley in Ulster County.[^2][^4] Wikipedia reports the sale to Knight and the move but does not mention Esperance.[^1] Knight poured four piers in September 2004 and opened the rebuilt tower, the Knightower, in July 2006.[^2] The register gives its height as 43 feet to the top railing.[^2]

Wikipedia says visitors cannot go to the original site today.[^1] No source read says what the replacement antenna structure looks like now.[^1] The Knightower is privately owned and stands on Ashokan Ridge.[^2] Visits need the owner's permission, and the register withholds its coordinates at the owner's request.[^2] The state's lookout list marks the mountain tower as gone.[^4]
