The mountain near Chewelah, Washington, shows up on most maps as Stensgar, but the lookout that stood on it has always gone by Stranger.[^1] Fire detection there began in 1930, when the Washington Division of Forestry put up a platform tower and cabin on the summit.[^1] That structure lasted only until 1936, when it was replaced by a 50-foot pole tower carrying a small live-in cab.[^1][^2] A standard 40-foot wooden tower with a DNR flattop cab went up in 1959, and twenty-four years later, in 1983, that gave way to the tower that still exists today: a 42-foot wooden tower with a flattop cab and catwalk.[^1]

A lookout on duty had a wide circle to watch: the Columbia Basin and Spokane to the south, Montana to the east, the Cascade Range to the west, and Canada to the north.[^2] The register of former lookout sites records that this tower was staffed as late as the "tinder-dry" 1994 fire season, after which it was no longer used to spot smokes.[^2]

Its working life was not its end. The Washington Department of Natural Resources eventually needed the summit for a Homeland Security facility, and after two years of negotiation with Stevens County, the fair manager arranged for the cab and catwalk to be lifted off the tower by a construction crane already working on the mountaintop. The load was trucked some thirty miles to downtown Colville, where forklifts set it on the ground at the county fairgrounds in 2008 while the tower's own legs were re-set on new concrete piers nearby; days later, another crane that happened to be free that afternoon lifted the cab and catwalk back onto those legs.[^1][^2] By Ray Kresek's account, the move relied on a fleet of donated trucks and forklifts, and in cash terms cost just "$25. The rest was all donated."[^1]

The tower is listed on the National Historic Lookout Register, but the site it once watched over is now occupied by other facilities, not a working lookout.[^1] What survives is the cab itself, re-erected and on public display at the Colville fairgrounds rather than back on a ridge.
