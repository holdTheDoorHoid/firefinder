Damewood Point was a crow's nest in a topped tree on a high point above Mosetown, on the Smith River in Douglas County.[^1][^2] Reports in *The Forest Log* from 1942, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, describe how it was built.[^1]

In late April 1942, District Warden Oglesby chose to float a 90-foot steel ladder about thirty miles down the Smith River to Mosetown, rather than carry its sections ten miles over a trail.[^1] Two old boats carried the ladder and gear, and one had to be submerged to get under a windfall.[^1]

In August 1942, J.W. "Pete" Kibby, a former CCC foreman then working for the Western Lane Forest Protective Association, finished the crow's nest.[^1] He topped the tree at 96 feet and built an 8-by-8-foot platform. A steel ladder salvaged from the old Baker Point lookout in northwest Oregon gave access.[^1] Earlier that spring the building materials came down the river on rafts, and a horse hauled them to the point.[^1]

From the lookout you can see much of the lower Smith River country, including a good share of the ground the 1938 Smith River fire burned over.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page also records $117.67 for snag and tree removal in 1940, and a $214.35 split board structure built in 1943.[^1]

Firelookout.com gives a different account, with a 92-foot crow's nest from the 1930s and a cabin in 1943.[^2] We followed the 1942 Forest Log reports for the build, since they were written at the time. The sources disagree on the height and the dates.[^1][^2]

Firelookout.com lists the site as abandoned.[^2] The Oregon Lookouts index lists it as a ground site that is gone.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, and we found nothing on whether the land is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]
