Annette Lookout was put up in 1939 by the Kern County Fire Department.[^1] The register says the design is found only in Kern County.[^1] By July 1939 the lookout was already in use: it spotted a 400-acre grass fire near Cottonwood Pass and sent the report to the local forestry headquarters.[^2]

The site was private land.[^1][^2] In October 1940 the Bakersfield Californian reported that Kenneth H. Davies and his wife had leased part of their property to Kern County for 15 years, for a fire lookout station.[^2] The register also lists the lookout as privately owned.[^1] A June 1940 item had already listed $500 for the Annette lookout station.[^2]

In August 1954 the lookout crew answered at least two fires near Annette.[^2] One was a grass fire four miles southwest of the lookout.[^2] The other was a storehouse fire, and the crew saved an adjacent ranch house.[^2]

A visit in 2009 found the structure mostly intact, the register notes.[^1] Since then the roof has blown off, and the register describes serious decay.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, with a three-story cab and an asterisk on its status.[^3] Some accounts say the cab is gone, but I could not confirm that. The register speaks of the roof, not the cab.[^1]

Today the register lists the land as privately owned, with the Kern County Fire Department as a cooperator.[^1] I found no visiting information or opening notice. The structure is badly decayed, so do not climb or enter it, and ask the landowner before going on private land.[^1]
