Rose Hill is a steel fire tower in Duplin County, North Carolina.[^1][^3] A history page for the state's lookouts dates the tower to November 1935, when the Civilian Conservation Corps erected a 99-foot 9-inch (30.4 m) steel tower there.[^1] The Emergency Conservation Works program covered the $1,630.04 in costs.[^1] In October 1936, crews from CCC Camp P-68 finished a five-room frame house where the lookout would live.[^1] The same history files the tower under the North Carolina Division of Forest Resources.[^1]

A 1961 entry reports that a lease on the property ran out, and that the state bought the land that summer.[^1]

The Coast and Geodetic Survey first described the station in 1969.[^2] That description calls it a metal cabin on a four-legged steel skeleton structure, 110 feet (33.5 m) high.[^2] That is taller than the 99-foot 9-inch figure from 1935, and neither source explains the difference.[^1][^2] The 1969 description gives directions from the Rose Hill town hall along East Ridge Street, but those directions are more than fifty years old.[^2]

None of the sources checked describes what stands at the site today. The survey's most recent condition note dates from 1986, when a recovery note recorded the survey mark there as in good condition.[^2] The survey office still lists the mark as current control.[^2] A 2021 list of North Carolina towers places Rose Hill at 34.83027, -78.01777.[^3]
