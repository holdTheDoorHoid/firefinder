Wauchecha Bald Fire Tower stands on a bald in the Nantahala National Forest in Graham County, North Carolina.[^1] It is an Aermotor live-in-cab design, the kind the U.S. Forest Service bought most often.[^1] The tower is about 35 feet tall, with a 13-by-13-foot cab and catwalk.[^1][^2] The all-metal body carries storm shutters, and its roof is a pyramid hip covered in standing-seam metal.[^1]

The Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it between 1933 and 1942.[^1] A trail-lookout site says the build date is unclear, and maps show no trace of the tower before the mid-1950s.[^2] A 1933 hiking club work hike along the trail did not mention the tower.[^2] A 1963 USGS survey party recorded a steel tower 30 feet high, which does not match the Register's account.[^2]

The sources we read name no watch-standers or notable fires for this tower.

The Register's 2019 nomination reports that the lowest steps have been removed and the cab's glass is broken or missing.[^1] The shutters were torn away, and most of the loose ones lie piled inside the cab.[^1] The bald also holds a communications tower complex, where the Forest Service signed a decision in August 2022 to replace an AT&T tower.[^3]

The Appalachian Trail once ran beside the lookout, until it was rerouted to the west.[^1] The trail-lookout site lists a 0.85-mile detour with about 600 feet of climb.[^2] Please do not climb it: the bottom steps are gone, so the cab cannot be reached by its stairs.[^1] We found no closure notice, so check with the Cheoah Ranger District before going.[^3] The sources we read mention no rental.
