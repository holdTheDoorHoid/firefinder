Three Corner Rock is a rock pinnacle in Skamania County, Washington, at about 3,550 feet.[^3][^5] Hikers reach it from the Pacific Crest Trail.[^5]

The earliest evidence of a lookout is a set of panoramic photographs from July 10, 1934. The summit appears to have held only a viewing post, with a living cabin below it.[^3] The first cab was a two-story L-4 dated 1938, which the register says was likely developed by the Columbia National Forest.[^1][^2]

In 1948, according to a 1990s interview with Gene Sargent, who was then at nearby Greenleaf, the Three Corner Rock lookout was staffed by Kit Carson McCoy. Sargent said the lookout was used only by day, and that the two slept in a small cabin below.[^3] Kay Love is listed as lookout personnel in 1951.[^2]

In February 1942 the Forest Service told the state it was abandoning the lookout house. The state replied that it would rehabilitate it, and it had already acquired the land.[^3]

In 1955 the state Division of Forestry set up a 14-by-14-foot ground cabin of plywood and glass.[^1][^4]

In August 1969 The Oregonian reported that the lookout was one of five on the district to be discontinued because air patrols were in use, and that it would most likely be burned.[^4] WillhiteWeb says the burning apparently did not happen, because a new cab went up in 1974.[^3]

The register and Firelookout.com date a second, identical DNR cab to 1973. The Washington lookouts site quotes a 1974 annual report describing a new lookout built on the precipice with a little help from a helicopter, and WillhiteWeb also dates the new cab to 1974.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The cab was sold and removed by a private party in 1982.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb names the buyer as Dick Morrison and says he placed the cab on his house in Friday Harbor. The sources do not say whether it is still there.[^3]

Today, the Washington Trails Association says the top is now flat and that a lookout tower once stood there. Concrete steps get hikers to within 12 feet of the top, and the rest is a scramble.[^5] Its shorter route is 4.2 miles round trip, starting where the Pacific Crest Trail crosses Road CG 2090. A gravel road leads from a nearby saddle to a microwave relay tower.[^5]

WillhiteWeb has photos captioned as footings of the lookout, but it does not date them.[^3] The WTA describes it as a hike, and no source we read reports a closure.[^5]
