The Sisters Lookout stood in the town of Sisters, in Deschutes County, Oregon. The Forest Service built it in 1921.[^1][^2][^3]

The register describes it as a 105-foot crow's nest one block east of the present ranger station.[^1][^2] The Central Oregonian describes it as a large ponderosa pine whose limbs had been cut away, with iron steps arched up the trunk and a platform about three feet square at the top. That article puts it immediately north of the current Sisters Ranger District office.[^3]

The Forest Service and the Oregon State Forestry Department used the lookout to search for fires around Sisters, particularly after thunderstorms.[^3] Staff were not eager to climb the ladder, and they often spent several hours on the small platform. In wind, the tree would sway several feet, which prompted a quick descent.[^3]

The Central Oregonian says the lookout stayed in use until 1926, when it began to show signs of rot and had outlived its usefulness. It was then cut down.[^3] The register gives 1934 as the year it was removed, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] Where the sources disagree, we report both.

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the site today, or whether the public may visit it. The Central Oregonian notes that some trees on high points in the local forests still carry remnants of old tree lookouts, and it warns against climbing them. It does not say whether the Sisters lookout is among them.[^3]
