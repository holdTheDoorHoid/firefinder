The Bedford Gate tower is the eastern of the fire towers on the Navy's Crane base in Indiana.[^1][^2] Indiana Fire Towers says that in the 1930s the land was known as the White River Land Utilization Project, and that it was turned into a naval ammunition site as World War II began.[^2] Wikipedia says the Navy's depot there was originally established in 1941.[^3]

Indiana Fire Towers gives 1938 as the year the tower's structure was built, citing a personal resource.[^2] That is earlier than the 1941 founding of the depot,[^3] and the sources I read do not explain how the two dates fit together.

The Forest Service says the Navy's depot at Burns City operated four towers during World War II.[^4] It lists this one as Fire Tower One and says it was used until the mid 1970s.[^4] The sources I read say nothing about who staffed it or what fires it watched.

Indiana Fire Towers says it was escorted through Crane in February 2025 and that the tower was standing as of 2025.[^2] The base is restricted federal Naval property.[^2] The same page adds that visitors cannot get in and cannot climb it.[^2]

The Register recorded the tower on August 2, 2026, as US 1837, IN 17, and lists the U.S. Navy as its administrator.[^1]

The sources disagree about the county. The Register's location line says Lawrence County, while Indiana Fire Towers says Martin County. Wikipedia says the base is mostly in Martin County, with small parts extending into Greene and Lawrence counties.[^1][^2][^3]

Anyone curious about the tower should know that the public cannot reach it, so a visit is not an option.[^2]
