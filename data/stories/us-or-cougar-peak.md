Cougar Peak, on the Fremont National Forest about 16 miles northwest of Lakeview in Lake County, got its lookout house in 1923.[^1][^3] Rex's page quotes two 1923 items. One says the new house was nearly complete in August, and the other says it was finished and being painted by October.[^2] The register calls it a D-6 cupola cabin, and a later account describes a 12-by-12 house on the summit.[^1][^2]

Rex's page quotes a 1924 Lake County Examiner item saying Mr. and Mrs. Jack Glasson had been at the station since April 16.[^2] In September 1938 Joe Frizzell was the lookout on duty, and a Lake County Examiner item says visitors had a first-class view of the Black Cap fire from the lookout.[^2]

In 1954 a 14-by-14 hip-roof house replaced the old cupola house, and Rex's page gives the total construction cost as $4,171.93.[^2] By 1969 the lookout was listed for emergency detection.[^2]

In 1972 the house was lost, but the sources describe its end differently. The register says it was removed, firelookout.com says it was destroyed, and Rex's page says it burned.[^1][^3][^2]

None of the sources we read describes what, if anything, remains on the peak today. The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register still lists the site.[^1]
