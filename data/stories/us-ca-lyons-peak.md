Lyons Peak sits in San Diego County inside the Cleveland National Forest, an island of federal land surrounded by private property.[^1][^2]

A fire lookout has stood on the peak since 1913.[^2] The first structure was a 5-by-5-foot cabin set directly on the rock at ground level.[^1] The chapter describes that first structure as resembling a telephone booth.[^2]

The present cab was built in 1964.[^1] The sources disagree on its model.[^1][^2] NHLR describes a CL-160 metal cab, while the chapter describes a 13-by-13-foot CL-30 steel cab on a 41-foot K-brace tower, which it calls the only such pairing in California.[^1][^2]

Today the only ways in are by helicopter or with a law-enforcement escort, and the chapter says the base of the peak has closed to the public.[^2] A chapter report says private land encircles the tower and litigation sharply restricts who can reach it.[^3]

That report also describes a December visit on which chapter members, flown in by a sheriff's helicopter, covered the cab's windows with plywood against vandals and bad weather.[^3] The same report marks the project as suspended, and the chapter's page says the interior still needs refurbishing before the lookout can return to service.[^3][^2]

NHLR registered the lookout on December 16, 2008.[^1] If the access problem is resolved, the chapter hopes to save the cab for restoration and operation.[^3]
