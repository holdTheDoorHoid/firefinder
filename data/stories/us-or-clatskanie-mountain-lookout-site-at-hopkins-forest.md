The lookout began on Clatskanie Mountain, in the north Oregon Coast Range east of Astoria.[^2] The Oregon Department of Forestry put up a steel Aermotor tower and cab there in 1954, as part of its fire protection work.[^1][^2] The cab at the top measures 7 by 7 feet and is ringed by a catwalk.[^2]

In June 1984 the tower was decommissioned and moved to the Magness Memorial Tree Farm, where the World Forestry Center ran it as an educational exhibit.[^2] The register says the farm is near Woodburn, while the operator says near Wilsonville, so the sources differ on the nearby town.[^1][^2] Over time the tower was damaged by vandals and could not be kept in proper repair.[^2]

In June 2008 Forests Forever, Inc. agreed with the Oregon Department of Forestry to move the tower to Hopkins Demonstration Forest in Beavercreek, for its education programs.[^2] The operator says the move took more than ten years, needed county permits, a full dismantling, re-fabricated components and specialist help.[^2] The steel tower stood on its own at Hopkins for nine years.[^2] In December 2019 Fire Tower Restoration, a New York firm, sent a crew to rebuild the cab on top of it.[^2] Volunteers then replaced the stair treads, railings and safety fencing for public access.[^2]

The former-sites register dates its entry for the site to September 23, 2010. That is a registration date, not the year the tower was built, which was 1954.[^1][^2] The operator planned to open it to students and the public later in 2021.[^2] A 2020 visitor's page places the tower at the edge of the forest's parking lot and lists it as an exhibit at 60 feet, while the operator gives 65 feet.[^3][^2] Directions from that page start in Oregon City, heading south on Highway 213 toward Molalla.[^3] We have not confirmed current opening days or climbing rules, so check with the forest before going up.
