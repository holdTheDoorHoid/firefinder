Hi Mountain Lookout sits on the crest of the Santa Lucia Mountains in San Luis Obispo County, in the Los Padres National Forest.[^1][^2] The register gives its elevation as 3,129 feet, while the project site says 3,198.[^1][^4]

The first lookout here began service in 1926.[^1][^2] In 1961 a steel CL-100 cab replaced it; the cab has a catwalk and flat roof and sits on a 10-foot concrete block base.[^1]

Lookouts were staffing the station by the early 1930s.[^3] A January 1930 newsletter recalls a Christmas Day fire report from the Rinconada that was held to 20 acres on private land.[^3] A June 1932 notice says a lookout opened the station.[^3] A January 1959 newspaper item, quoted on another lookout site, describes a woman then in her thirteenth year at the station, and says winds that registered 70 miles an hour blew down the lookout house, forcing her to flee.[^3]

The lookout later fell out of fire use. According to the project history, it fell into disuse, and for some 15 years across the late 1970s and 1980s it was neglected and vandalized.[^2] Morro Coast Audubon Society volunteers had used it to watch a nesting pair of peregrine falcons, and in 1996 the Audubon society and the Forest Service began restoring it as a biological field station and public education center.[^2]

Today the restored cab has solar lights, a pump, and a propane stove and refrigerator, and volunteers and staff use it in support of "Condor Watch at Hi Mountain."[^1] The project describes the site as a field research base and remote tracking post for the critically endangered California Condor. It offers seasonal internships and volunteer roles.[^4]

The lookout, its visitor center and the gate are closed to visitors at the moment, though the surrounding area is open when conditions allow.[^4][^5] The road is maintained but can close in bad weather, and a creek crossing may be impassable at times.[^5] For the latest access and road-closure information, call the Santa Lucia Ranger District at (805) 865-0416.[^5]
