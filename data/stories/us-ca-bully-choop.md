Bully Choop Lookout stands on the western edge of Whiskeytown National Recreation Area, in Shasta County, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list and a 1954 survey record put it in Trinity County instead.[^2][^3] The register says the name comes from a Native phrase meaning high sharp peak.[^1]

The current tower was put up in 1978 by the California Dept. of Forestry and Fire Control to take the place of a lookout from the 1930s.[^1] A May 1978 newspaper item described a conservation camp near Nubieber finishing its fifteenth lookout tower, which was to be taken down and moved to "Bully Shoup Mountain west of Redding."[^3] That spelling differs from the register, so the match is probable rather than certain.[^3] A 1954 survey had described the earlier summit house as a white frame building, 20-1/2 feet square at its base.[^3]

The cab is a live-in design with slanted windows, and the register describes it as a distinctive, long-lasting CDF design.[^1] The lookout was registered on April 21, 1999, and the register says it is still in active service.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list shows the enclosed tower as standing.[^2]

The register says visitors can reach it by a 14-mile road.[^1] A road guide, however, describes a 1.2-mile unpaved road that needs a 4x4.[^4] Check conditions and any closures with the administering agency before you go, and do not drive past closed gates or onto private land.
