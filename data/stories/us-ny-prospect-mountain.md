In July 1910 the Forest, Fish and Game Commission set up a lookout on the summit of Prospect Mountain, above Lake George.[^1] It was a wooden tower 35 feet tall, built at a cost of $107.16.[^1][^2] The summit had a hotel, reached by an incline railway that failed financially and stopped running in 1903.[^2] That hotel served as the observer's headquarters.[^2]

In 1915 the observer, Adelbert Brown, sheltered in that hotel, and its cupola was his lookout station.[^1] Wikipedia says the building at the summit burned down in 1932.[^2] The state's 1932 report instead presents the new tower as the successor to an earlier building whose upper part had been a lookout for years.[^1] A 47-foot steel Aermotor LS40 tower with an observer's cabin went up that year.[^2][^3]

In 1940 CCC youths began setting poles for a state telephone line up the mountain, to link the tower with the Army air defense command in Watertown ahead of army maneuvers.[^1] A memoir on the state chapter's website, written by a former Lake George Village resident, says the author believed Harold Norton was the man stationed at the tower.[^6]

A road opened to the summit in 1969, letting visitors drive up.[^4] Wikipedia says the tower was shut once the 1970 lookout season ended, as aerial detection took over, and was later removed.[^2] The Starr list marks the tower as removed, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list marks it gone.[^3][^5]

The county historian says the mountain has stayed in a similar state since the road opened, and that a parking lot was made on land near the peak.[^4] No source I read describes any tower remains on the summit.
