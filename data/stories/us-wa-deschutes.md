The U.S. Army built the first tower at Deschutes in the 1930s.[^1] It was a 100-foot wooden tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab, about 10 miles southeast of Tumwater in Thurston County.[^2][^1] The tower stood on a hill at 650 feet.[^4][^1]

In 1942 a 50-foot wooden tower with a 9-by-9-foot cab replaced it.[^1][^2] A 112-foot steel tower followed in 1968.[^1][^2]

Willhite's page includes stories from Madeline Mullen, who staffed the lookout in the summer of 1975.[^4]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register says the steel tower was removed by 1994.[^1] Firelookout.com says Army practice bombing destroyed it that year.[^2] Forest Lookouts describes a 1994 loss during a training exercise.[^3] Willhite notes that some reports give the same bombing account, but base forestry records show use of the lookout well after 1994.[^4]

Willhite's photographs from 2013 show the summit now serves as a turnaround for vehicles, and he writes that nothing else remains up there.[^4] The site lies on Joint Base Lewis-McChord, at the base's far southern edge.[^4] The sources we read do not describe public access to it.
