The Chester Fire Tower stands on Holcomb Hill in Hampden County, in the town of Chester.[^1] According to the register, the site first hosted a temporary lookout in 1915.[^1] A one-acre tract was bought in 1918, and a 40-foot wooden tower went up there.[^1]

The current tower is a 60-foot steel structure.[^1] The register credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it in 1934 for the Massachusetts Bureau of Fire Control.[^1] The same register entry says the steel tower replaced the wooden one 26 years later, which would put the date at 1944, so the register contradicts itself on the year.[^1] Wikipedia says the Corps worked in the Chester-Blandford State Forest from 1934 to 1940, but I found nothing tying that work to this lookout.[^2] I found no second source for the build year, so treat 1934 as the register's date, not a settled one.

In 1965 the tower got a larger cab, a 10-by-10-foot aluminum unit of the Massachusetts standard type.[^1] At the May 2016 visit, the register recorded the tower as not staffed.[^1] I found no notable fires tied to this tower in the sources I read.

The tower was standing at that visit, and the visitor thought the exterior looked excellent despite a large locked fence around the tower.[^1] A communication facility sits around its base.[^1] The tower sits off an unlabeled road and lies outside any state park.[^1] Because of the fence, treat it as restricted. No source says the public may climb it, so do not plan a climb.[^1]
