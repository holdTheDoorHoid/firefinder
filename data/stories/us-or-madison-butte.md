Madison Butte is a summit on the Umatilla National Forest in Morrow County, Oregon. The National Historic Lookout Register gives its elevation as 5,642 feet, while a 2024 visitor's page gives 5,707 feet.[^1][^5] Someone was watching the butte by 1922, when Milt Spurlock was the lookout and a new telephone line was constructed.[^3]

In August 1923 a Heppner paper reported that a lookout house was going up on the butte. The paper described it as enclosed in glass and fitted with up-to-date fire-detection gear.[^3] The NHLR calls that first structure a D-6 cupola ground cab.[^1] In 1957 a 37-foot steel tower with a CL-100 cab replaced it, and both the NHLR and the Forest Lookouts page date the present tower to 1957.[^1][^3][^5] A Forest Service file quoted on the Forest Lookouts page says the new tower was first used in 1958.[^3] A 1969 survey note on the Oregon lookouts page says an earlier tower came down about 15 years before, and a newer one was rebuilt nearby. The sources do not say whether that note describes the 1957 tower, so the early history is not fully settled.[^3]

Lookouts kept watch through the fire seasons. A 1961 district report said the post had been manned without a break since a June storm that year.[^3] In 1967 a Eugene woman, Vicky Ringsdorf, became the district's first female lookout.[^3] In 1980 the Heppner district placed a citizens band radio on the lookout to monitor the emergency channel, so visitors could report fires.[^3]

Fires came often enough to be recorded. In 1925 four fires were reported from the lookout, all on state-protected land.[^3] A July 1932 storm struck the butte four times and shook the lookout house. A 1932 letter quoted on the Forest Lookouts page counted five lightning fires and one man-caused fire that month.[^3]

The tower is still in service. A 2024 visit page lists the lookout as staffed, and firelookout.com describes the present 37-foot all-steel live-in tower as staffed every summer.[^2][^5] The same visitor found it not yet staffed in late May, early in the season.[^5]

Hikers can reach the site on Madison Butte Trail #3054, which starts just past the Tupper Work Center off Forest Service Road 21 and carries no fee.[^4] A rough road also climbs to the top, but the 2024 visitor found it gated at that time of year.[^5] The tower is staffed each summer, so treat the cab as a working space and check with the Heppner Ranger District at (541) 676-9187 before you go.[^2][^4]
