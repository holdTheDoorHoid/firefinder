A telephone line reached the top of Balm Mountain in 1915, and a forest crew pushed the final four miles through eight feet of snow.[^1] In September 1928 a packer set out over the Callapooia trail to drag logs up for a cabin at the lookout.[^1] Sources disagree on the cab itself. The Register and Firelookout.com both describe an L-5 cab from 1933.[^2][^3] Oregon Lookouts instead lists an FC-2 (L-4) house around 1934, with question marks beside the entry.[^1]

In 1941 the lookout was staffed for 75 days, and its reports went through the Oakridge Telephone Company to the Oakridge Ranger Station.[^1]

Both the Register and Firelookout.com say the cab was gone by 1962. The Register says it was removed, while Firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^2][^3] A 1980 Forest Service survey description calls the house now abandoned, which does not fit neatly with a 1962 end date.[^1] The same description places a survey disk stamped 1935 about 16.5 feet south of the house's southeast corner.[^1]

Mountain Plants of the Western Cascades describes remnants of the old lookout at the north end of the ridge, and notes that little of the trail is left.[^4] Its directions run along forest roads to a pulloff, then on foot up the ridge; the old lookout sits about a quarter mile from the road.[^4] No source we read says whether the survey disk is still in place.
