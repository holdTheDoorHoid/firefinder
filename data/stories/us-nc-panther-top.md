Panther Top Lookout sits on a knob in the far southwest corner of North Carolina, inside the Nantahala National Forest.[^1] The tower dates to 1940 and was meant as a fire lookout, but sources disagree about who built it. The Register says only that it was constructed in 1940.[^1] A 2019 Forest Service release credits the Civilian Conservation Corps,[^4] while Blue Ridge Public Radio ties it to the North Carolina Forest Service, as a post for spotting fires.[^2]

The tower stands 30 feet tall.[^2] Its wooden R-8 cab is about 14 feet square, according to the Register.[^1]

A 1993 blizzard knocked out the tower's phone and power lines, and the utilities would not replace them.[^2][^3] After that the tower was no longer run on a regular basis.[^2] The sources name no later watch-standers.

The Register and a 2023 news story both report that the tower still gets used when fire danger runs high.[^1][^3] That sits uneasily with the 2023 radio account, so the sources disagree about how active it is today.[^2]

The Register's 2019 visit report rated the tower in excellent condition, though a fire had damaged the old outhouse.[^1] A few months before its 2023 open days, the Forest Service completed renovations, including painting and fitting new windows and floors.[^2][^3]

The Forest Service usually keeps it closed to visitors, though it often opens the tower during peak leaf season.[^4] In 2023 the tower was set to welcome visitors on October 28-29 and November 4-5, between 10:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m.[^2] Visitors drove through a gate where Forest Roads 85 and 85A meet, then parked in a lot by the tower.[^2][^3] The 2019 visit report also recorded a fallen tree along the route up.[^1]

Check with the Tusquitee Ranger District before you go. The 2019 release listed 828-837-5152 for that office, and the number may have changed.[^4] Please do not climb the tower on any day it has not been announced as open. We found no rental listing for it.
