Spitzenberg Mountain sits in Blue Mountain Reservation, near Peekskill in Westchester County.[^1][^2] In December 1929 the Westchester County Park Commission was building a stone shelter house with a cupola on the summit, for a fire observer.[^1] The main room was to be 16 feet square, and the cupola was to have windows facing the four points of the compass.[^1]

Trucks and tractors could bring materials along a rough road to within a few hundred feet of the summit, but the final stretch needed a stone boat winched up on steel cable.[^1] The paper said an observer would be on duty in dry spells, when fire danger was high.[^1]

Blue Mountain Reservation, a 1,538-acre park, was acquired in 1926.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list records the summit site as stone ruins.[^3] No source I found says when the shelter fell into ruin or what the ruins look like now.

Today the reservation is open seven days a week from 8 a.m. to dusk, all year.[^2] Its trails reach the tops of Mt. Spitzenberg and Blue Mountain.[^2]
