The Jute Lake tower stood in Vilas County.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association places it at about 46.16 north, 89.49 west, and OpenStreetMap, which tags it as an observation tower, gives its elevation as 536 metres.[^1][^2]

Statewide, Wisconsin's fire tower network was large and concentrated in forested regions most prone to fire.[^3] At its peak, 119 towers were in use.[^3] WXPR says the towers were built in the 1930s and 1940s.[^4] WJFW says many of the surviving steel towers were built by Civilian Conservation Corps workers, using frames made by the Aeromotor Company of Chicago.[^3] Typical towers rose about 100 feet, topped by a cab measuring 7 feet by 7.[^4]

For many decades the towers were staffed May through September. Later, staffing was cut back to the spring peak of fire season, between snowmelt and green-up.[^3]

In 2015 the DNR began eliminating the towers.[^3][^4] WJFW says 72 aging towers that were still operating were decommissioned that year.[^3] That December WXPR reported the DNR's announcement and quoted a DNR official who said replacement costs and newer technology were among the factors behind the decision.[^4] A 2019 report from the association's Wisconsin chapter counted 37 towers transferred to landowners, 10 sold and removed, 4 sold with removal still pending and 56 left to dismantle.[^5] Those categories total 107, not 72, and the sources do not explain the gap.[^3][^5]

The association's list marks the Jute Lake tower as gone.[^1] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. Many of the DNR's staffed towers were on private property under easement rights, so check who owns the land before going to look.[^4]
