The Little Snowy Top lookout stood on Little Snowy Top Mountain, on the Shedroof Divide in Boundary County, Idaho, about two miles south of the Canadian border.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register gives its construction as circa 1930 to 1933,[^1] and the Spokesman-Review says it was built between 1930 and 1933.[^2] It was a 14-by-14-foot L-4 groundhouse,[^2][^1] which the register calls the only remaining 1930s groundhouse of its type in the Priest Lake area.[^1]

The register says its lookout work wound down in the 1960s, but the site escaped demolition because it was so remote.[^1] A fan site adds that questions of ownership also played a part, and that the Sullivan Lake Ranger District had staffed it.[^4]

Few staff are named in the sources we read. The Spokesman-Review mentions a grizzly bear that broke in and chased the lookout staffer into the rafters in 1951.[^2] The Idaho Fire Lookouts site lists Howard Thomason for 1933 and Rupert Cutler for 1952, with little detail.[^5]

In 1987, volunteers from the Spokane Mountaineers and the Priest River Back Country Horsemen assisted the Priest Lake Ranger District in a two-year restoration.[^1] The Spokesman-Review says the restoration began in 1988,[^2] while a 1989 item quoted on the Weebly page says the project began in 1987.[^6] The register listed the lookout on June 17, 1995.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added it as a site in 2020.[^3]

In 2006, while the Hughes fires were in its path, crews covered the lookout with retardant and a fire-resistant wrap.[^6] The Spokesman-Review says it had survived several forest fires before the one that destroyed it.[^2]

On September 3, 2016, the lookout burned to the ground in the afternoon.[^6] The Spokesman-Review gives carelessness with the small wood stove as the probable cause.[^2] The Weebly timeline gives the same cause more broadly, as carelessness by people using the building for shelter.[^6]

A 1983 item quoted on the Weebly page refers to a "Little Snowy Lookout Tower" that the Forest Service had donated to Priest River in 1982, and says attempts to move it had apparently failed.[^6] It does not say clearly whether that structure is this cabin, and we found nothing that confirms it.[^6]

The register has a photo captioned "Burned remains of the lookout," but none of the sources we read describes what, if anything, still stands at the site.[^1] Reaching it meant a nine-mile trail, covered on foot or on horseback.[^2] With the cabin burned to the ground, there is no structure left to climb.[^2] We found no current notice of closures.
