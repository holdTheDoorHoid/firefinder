Post Point sits in Wasco County, Oregon, inside the Mount Hood National Forest, at 3,702 feet (1,128 meters).[^1] The site began as a rag camp, which the register dates to the 1920s.[^1] The register says a 40-foot pole L-4 tower, put up around 1937, took the camp's place.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same pole tower and a date of about 1937.[^2]

The early history is not settled. A 1932 Sunday Oregonian item, quoted on a county listing, describes a new ladder and platform at Post Point that totals 130 feet.[^3] That does not match the 40-foot tower, and the sources do not reconcile the two accounts, so the story reports both.[^1][^3]

The 40-foot tower gave way to an L-6 tower of treated timber, 82 feet tall, in the 1940s.[^1][^2] In 1962 an R-6 tower of treated timber, 84 feet tall with a flat cab, went up.[^1][^2][^3]

The county listing also records a set of panorama photographs taken by Robert Snyder in 1934, and a 1936 telephone ring assigned to the station.[^3] The lookout was deactivated in 1964.[^3]

The tower's end came in 1994, in an unusual way.[^1] The register describes a bid to lift the cab and upper half six miles east and turn them into a rental.[^1] An Army Chinook helicopter could not handle the weight, so the piece was let go almost as soon as it left the lower section.[^1] Firelookout.com and the county listing both say the tower was destroyed that year.[^2][^3]

Today, the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a tower that is gone.[^4] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site now, and none that says whether the public can visit it.
