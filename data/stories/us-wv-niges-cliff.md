Niges Cliff went up around 1943, when the Army was using the area for training and the Forest Service had extra land to protect.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the Monongahela National Forest built it with US Army funds.[^1] Eastern US Lookouts says Army money paid for three temporary towers that year, Niges Cliff among them, while the forest history names two towers built with military funds, Niges Cliff and Pigeon Roost.[^2][^3] The forest history also says this tower was one of a few built entirely of wood timbers.[^3]

The register places the tower along North Fork Mountain and says its exact location is unknown, though it also gives coordinates in Pendleton County.[^1] Eastern US Lookouts places it on North Mountain, south of U.S. Route 33 on the Potomac Ranger District.[^2] The two sources do not agree on the ridge.

The register and Eastern US Lookouts both give 1944 for the transfer to the State of West Virginia.[^1][^2] The forest history gives 1944 and 1948 for the two wartime towers without saying which is which.[^3] The register lists the West Virginia Conservation Commission as administrator.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's West Virginia list marks it as gone and shows its location as unknown.[^4] No source describes what remains at the site today, and none says whether the ground is open to visitors. The forest history says most of the forest's towers were torn down, with concrete piers and steps as the only trace, but it does not say that about this one.[^3]
