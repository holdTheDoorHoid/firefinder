A forest fire lookout on Granite Peak in Trinity County dates to 1916, when a wood ground cabin measuring 12 feet 8 inches square went up under a Region 5 plan.[^1] That cabin was replaced in 1937 with a Plan R1 cabin.[^1]

Local papers mention the lookout from 1931. That May, W. H. Williams was on his way back to take his post on the peak as a forest fire lookout.[^2] An October 1932 item names William Williams as the lookout, says he had been on duty all summer, and reports him on vacation.[^2] A June 1941 item says heavy snows damaged the lookout, loosening its windows and shutters and pushing the building off plumb.[^2]

Later clippings name other lookouts. Jack DeCordy spent a few weeks on the peak in 1943, and Leroy Eisele was scheduled to take the post in 1956.[^2] The last dated clipping, from November 1960, reports that the lookout was taken out by pack train on October 10.[^2]

A 1954 Coast and Geodetic Survey description gives the lookout house as a white-framed building about 20 feet square at its base and roughly 16 feet tall. A 3-foot catwalk runs around it, it has windows, and the description says it is Forest Service property.[^2]

The register gives no removal date.[^1] A hiking guide to the Trinity Alps calls the summit the site of a former lookout tower and says the ruins of an old Forest Service lookout can be seen there.[^3] The same guide describes a maintained 8.4-mile trail that is easily reached off Highway 3, and says it is still possible to spend the night on the summit.[^3] The guide gives no detail about the ruins, so check the site before you go.[^3]
