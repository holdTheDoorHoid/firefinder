The National Historic Lookout Register lists Fabius Lookout Tower in Jackson County, Alabama, at an elevation of 1,588 feet (484 m).[^1] The Register says the Alabama Forestry Commission erected it in the mid-1950s, and gives the year as 1955.[^1] It places the tower among a small number of 120-foot International Derrick fire towers the Commission put up, and describes a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1]

A 1819 News article about Alabama's historic lookout towers says the Aermotor MC-39 was the most common steel model in the state's forests, and that other makes, including International Derrick and Equipment Company towers, were put up in Alabama as well.[^2] That is useful background for the tower's design, but the article does not mention Fabius.[^2]

The Register added the tower to its listing on June 30, 2009.[^1] I found no source on its staffing, on any fires it watched, or on its use after the 1950s. The Register does not say whether the tower still stands today.[^1] I also found no source on public access. Before going near it, check with the Alabama Forestry Commission, and do not climb a tower that is closed or where you have no permission to be.
