High Rock is a summit in the Mount Hood National Forest, in Clackamas County, Oregon, on the highest point of Rocky Ridge.[^1][^2][^3] The register describes a Region-5 cab built in 1925 that lasted until 1965.[^1] A July 1926 newspaper item quoted by Forest Lookouts says a patrolman was posted there as the lookout.[^3]

A 1947 survey note describes a white lookout house on a stone foundation, about 12 feet square, with a peaked roof and a spire.[^3] A 1955 survey note refers to a 14 x 14 lookout house at the station, so the size is not settled.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both say a frame cabin was added in 1942 for the Aircraft Warning Service during the Second World War.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts gives the service's station as active from October 1942 until October 1943, after which the Forest Service kept the site for fire detection.[^3]

Forest Lookouts says the lookout was deactivated in 1964, while the register and firelookout.com give 1965 as the last year.[^3][^1][^2] A National Geodetic Survey recovery note says the station was destroyed before July 1970.[^3] None of the sources read say how the cab was taken down.

Today no source describes a structure on the summit. A 2020 trip report describes the top as a peak with a 360-degree view.[^4] The report lists no permits or fees, a round trip of 1.12 miles, and an unmarked final trail, and says the forest road is drivable in a 4x4 vehicle with good clearance.[^4]

Do not confuse this site with the other High Rock, a lookout in Washington's Gifford Pinchot National Forest that is being restored. That is a different structure.[^5]
