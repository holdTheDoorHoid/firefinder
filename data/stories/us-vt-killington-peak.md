The tower on Killington Peak did not start there.[^1][^2] The Vermont Chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association says it was first built on Pico Peak in about 1920 and moved to Killington in the early 1960s.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site quotes a state report saying the lookout was taken apart at Pico and rebuilt on Killington.[^2] WillhiteWeb dates the move to 1963, while the Forest Lookouts entry covers the years 1963 and 1964, so the exact year is not settled.[^3][^2]

The sources we read do not describe the tower's design or height. The same state report says the lookout's living quarters went into a building put up as a joint project with the public safety and highway departments, and that the building also held radio equipment for the three departments.[^2] It adds that the site was reached by the Killington chair lift.[^2]

Staffing shows up in local newspapers. A 1964 notice says a man had gone to the fire tower for the summer.[^2] A 1973 notice says a summer dispatcher would be in charge of the tower.[^2] That June, a newspaper reported that a man died of an apparent heart attack while climbing the tower.[^2] We found no fires that the sources say the tower watched.

The tower was discontinued in the fall of 1984, one of the last two towers in use at the time.[^3][^2] Trailspotting says it was taken out of service that year and now hosts communications equipment.[^4] A 2012 report says it is home to about 20 radio antennas.[^1]

Today the summit holds a group of communications masts and outbuildings, and the tower stands among them.[^4] The Appalachian Trail reaches the summit by a 0.4-mile detour with a 240-foot climb.[^3] Other routes start from the ski resort.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^5]

We found no source saying visitors may enter or climb the tower. It still carries radio equipment, so please do not climb it.[^4][^1]
