The Great Hill Fire Tower stands in Hemenway State Forest in Tamworth, Carroll County.[^1] The Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1934.[^1][^2] The Register's separate build-date field reads 1935, so the dates disagree.[^1] We follow the 1934 construction date given in the Register's description and by the Weirs Times.

The Weirs Times describes a lookout about 35 feet tall, and the town's 2026 maintenance log refers to its steel frame.[^2][^3]

New Hampshire Forestry staffed the tower until 1974.[^1][^2] The town of Tamworth took over the tower in 1977, under a condition that it stay ready for fire detection when needed.[^1]

The Register gives the site elevation as 525 feet, while the Weirs Times places the tower on a 1,300-foot summit. We could not reconcile those two figures.[^1][^2]

The town owns the tower, but the land under it is state forest, and the Tamworth Conservation Commission looks after the structure.[^3] An evaluation last year found it structurally sound.[^3]

In July 2026 the tower closed for about three weeks while a contractor replaced the outside clapboards and primed and painted the structure.[^3] The closure notice said all trails to and across the summit would remain open.[^3] The tower reopened for use on July 28, and volunteers finished most of the cabin painting in August.[^3] The Weirs Times says there is plenty of parking on the shoulder of Route 113A, near the Big Pines Natural Area sign.[^2]
