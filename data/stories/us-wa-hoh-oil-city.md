The Oil City lookout was a cabin on a ridge near the Hoh Benchmark, on the Washington coast in Jefferson County.[^1][^4] The register lists it as a fire lookout, but the building was erected by the Air Warning Service, a program that watched for aircraft.[^1][^3][^4] Early memos say the spot was hard to pick, and reaching it required a trail of 3.5 miles.[^4]

A list dated December 1, 1942 describes a newly constructed shake building, and the register dates the cabin to 1942.[^4][^1] The sources disagree on its size and on the site's elevation.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Forest Lookouts quotes a May 1944 report that gives 12 x 16 for the building, while the December 1942 list gives 12x36.[^3][^4] The register puts the site at 1,214 feet, firelookout.com says 1,058 feet, and WillhiteWeb says 1,000 feet.[^1][^2][^4]

Forest Lookouts gives February 2, 1943 as the date the Air Warning Service station there was activated.[^3] WillhiteWeb's master map labels that same date as the inactivation date instead.[^4] Forest Lookouts also gives two end dates, March 15 and March 20, 1944, so the exact close is unclear.[^3]

When service ended, the building was judged not worth moving and left where it stood.[^3] The register says the cabin is now gone.[^1] The firelookout.com page marks it gone by the 1980s.[^2] WillhiteWeb says the cabin was around for many years, and that traces of it point to use as a hangout.[^4]

No source we read describes a standing structure today.[^1][^4] After some digging, WillhiteWeb reports finding boards, nails, hinges and glass, with remnants buried under leaves and needles.[^4] The exact spot has no trees.[^4] The same account says the property was on Fruit Growers Supply Company land in 2017.[^4] It also says the old access through Oil City is now private property, and that Fruit Growers Supply typically allows non-motorized access to its land.[^4] No source says whether the public may visit, so ask the landowner before going.[^1][^4]
