Mount Cushman is a ridge-top peak in Windsor County, Vermont, between the towns of Rochester and Randolph.[^1][^2]

The first tower and cabin went up in the spring of 1920. A Vermont history quoted on the Eastern US lookouts guide says the Vermont Timberland Owners' Association built them with the Forestry Department.[^1] The state forest page also gives 1920 for the first fire tower.[^2]

The second tower is harder to pin down. In October 1934 The Bethel Courier reported that a new steel tower was expected, built near the old mountain house by CCC crews.[^1] The White River Valley Herald describes a tower built in the 1920s and 50 feet tall.[^3] The two accounts disagree on the date, and the sources do not settle which one stood on the summit.[^1][^3]

Watchers staffed the tower in the postwar years.[^1] Will Stone had been fire warden there for 16 years by April 1946, and in April 1948 he began his 20th season, so the two counts do not agree.[^1] Tug Vinton was the watcher in 1952, and Harry Dean was the lookout in 1957.[^1] State reports tracked the upkeep, including painted stairs and a new cap on the tower in 1949 and 1950.[^1] In August 1952 townspeople from Rochester planned a weekend road-building effort to the tower, as part of a campaign to get the state to accept the summit as a park gift.[^1] A new cabin went up in 1968 under a replacement program.[^1]

In 1972 the state discontinued its fire towers, and in 1973 this one was sold and moved to Topsham, according to the Herald.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Vermont list records the Mount Cushman tower as gone.[^4] No source we read says where the structure stands now.

Today the state forest on the ridge covers only 16 acres. The state says the forest is landlocked, so the public has no legal way in.[^2] The summit lies outside the state forest.[^2] The Herald says a meeting place called the Tip Top house was built at the summit, and that its foundation and fire pits remain visible in spring and fall.[^3] No source we read describes tower footings or any other lookout remains on the summit. Check access with the state before going.
