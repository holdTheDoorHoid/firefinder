Gird Point Lookout sits at 7,702 feet in the Sapphire Mountains of the Bitterroot National Forest, about 11 miles east of Hamilton, Montana.[^1][^2]

The forest put the lookout on its fixed-point fire detection network in 1939, the year it was built.[^1] The National Register nomination says it was probably built with Civilian Conservation Corps labor.[^1] The cab is a 1931-pattern L-4 design, 14 feet square.[^1][^5] Sources disagree on the height of its tower. The nomination and firelookout.com say 10 feet, while the Forest Service page and the NHLR say 8.[^1][^4][^2][^3]

The nomination counts it among the sixteen Bitterroot lookouts staffed in 1967.[^1] A staff list on firelookout.com runs from 1958 to 1971, and the site says the tower was staffed into the 1980s.[^4]

In 1984 the NHLR says Gird Point and seven other inactive lookouts were slated for removal, and that three of them were saved and restored.[^3] The nomination says the 1990 Gird Point wildfire killed most of the stands of subalpine fir that once grew on the slopes below the tower.[^1] Firelookout.com says the fire surrounded the lookout that year.[^4]

The nomination says the lookout was abandoned for nearly two decades before restoration began in 2003. The restoration swapped the log tower legs for like-for-like replacements and rebuilt the catwalk.[^1] The NHLR dates the rehabilitation to 2002, so the two sources differ by a year.[^3] The nomination calls the lookout eligible for National Register listing under criteria A and C, and says it is now a cabin rental.[^1]

The Forest Service rents the cabin. Reservations open from mid-June through mid-September on Recreation.gov, at $30 a night.[^2] There is no electricity or water, so guests bring their own supplies.[^2][^5] Visitors drive most of the way and then walk a moderate trail. The Forest Service page gives that walk as a half-mile in one place and about three-quarters of a mile in another.[^2] The Forest Service describes narrow steps and a catwalk on the 8-foot tower, and notes that the lookout may be hazardous for children and pets.[^2]
