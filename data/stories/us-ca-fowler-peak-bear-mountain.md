The Civilian Conservation Corps built the original tower on Bear Mountain in Calaveras County during the 1930s.[^1][^3] The sources do not agree on the year. The register gives 1933 or 1936. A November 1936 program notice planned a tower on Fowler Peak for a CCC camp, and a 2008 obituary says a former corps member helped build it in 1938.[^1][^2]

The three-story tower was first called Bear Mountain Lookout.[^3] In 1959 it was renamed Fowler Peak Lookout for Oliver E. Fowler, a Stanislaus National Forest ranger who became one of the California Department of Forestry's first district rangers.[^3] The register says the name honors State Forester O.E. "Chick" Fowler, and the sources I read do not link the two names.[^1]

In 1986 and 1987 the tower was moved to the Calaveras County Fairgrounds in Angels Camp. The Department of Forestry, now Cal Fire, had decided to replace it with a more modern facility.[^1][^3] The register describes that newer lookout as a 41-foot steel tower with a 14-foot square cab, built in 1987 and 1988 at a cost of about $70,000.[^1][^2]

The register's own description is hard to square. It calls the tower at the fairgrounds a steel tower made from surplus atomic-test towers, and then describes the demolished structure as a timber tower built by the CCC.[^1] I could not match those descriptions to one structure, so the original design is not settled here.

By 2004 the tower had been cited two years in a row as unsafe by the state Department of Health and Human Services.[^3] The fair's marketing director said the fair could no longer afford the liability.[^3] The fair board ordered it demolished, and inmate crews from the Vallecito Conservation Camp were scheduled to start razing it on February 17, 2004.[^1][^3] Because it had been moved, the fair said it did not qualify for historical designation.[^3]

The register calls the tower demolished, and the 2004 news report says razing was about to begin.[^1][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association still lists a Fowler tower as standing at the register's coordinates. A 2008 obituary, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the tower stands at the fairgrounds today.[^4][^2] I found no later source that settles the question. Since no source confirms a tower there to climb, do not plan a visit around one. Ask the Calaveras County Fair before going to the grounds.
