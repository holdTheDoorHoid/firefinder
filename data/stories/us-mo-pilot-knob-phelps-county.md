The Pilot Knob lookout stands in Phelps County, south of Rolla, inside the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1][^2] The register dates it to 1953–1954 and calls it a metal lookout tower.[^1] The Missouri Conservation Commission's fiscal 1953–54 annual report records a new tower going up at Pilot Knob in Phelps County.[^3]

Who owned and ran it is less clear. The register names the U.S. Forest Service as administrator.[^1] A River Hills Traveler column says the tower seems to be on Forest Service land, and that the Department of Conservation ran it under an agreement. The writer had always thought of it as a state tower.[^2] We did not find that agreement, so the sources do not explain the terms of the state's role.

The cab was refinished in 1969–70, according to the register and the state's annual report.[^1][^3]

A visit in August 2023 found the tower and cab still standing, both in bad shape, with the cab worst.[^1] The base sits inside a chain-link fence with barbed wire on top.[^1] The register added the tower on December 31, 2023.[^1]

The register does not say whether the tower can be climbed, so stay outside the fence and do not climb it. Another Pilot Knob lookout existed in Stone County, so confirm the county before you search.[^1]
