Slumber Mountain is a low, tree-covered summit about seven miles northeast of Metaline Falls, in Pend Oreille County, Washington.[^1][^2] In 1932 Lawrence McKay began a 40-foot pole tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab on the summit, and a tent cabin stood below it.[^2][^3] A September 1932 newspaper report, quoted by a Washington lookouts site, says McKay had just finished a station on Grassy Top and was starting work on this one.[^4]

WillhiteWeb credits a history by Kresek with describing the site as a patrol point, at a time when rotting snags posed a major danger after the 1920s fires.[^2] On July 18, 1935, men from the Sullivan Lake CCC camp were sent to put out a small blaze near the station.[^2]

Abandonment came in the 1940s.[^2][^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register now lists the tower and tent cabin as gone.[^3] Willhite's photographs show timber footings for a ground building, a bedframe, the top of the cab roof, and trees rising through the rubble.[^2]

The land lies within the Kaniksu and Colville National Forests, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^3] The road in is gated. One gate is shut from December 1 to July 1 for wildlife habitat, and the upper gate is also shut and blocked.[^2] The summit is about 2.5 miles round trip on foot, with about 500 feet of elevation gain.[^2]
