The Barnwell tower is an 80-foot steel lookout that the register dates to about 1935 and the SC Picture Project to 1935.[^1][^2] The SC Picture Project says the South Carolina Forestry Commission built it, and the register thinks it may be an Aermotor LS-40.[^1][^2]

Newspaper reports suggest the work ran later than that.[^4][^5] In early December 1935 the Barnwell People-Sentinel reported that the Barnwell County Forest Protective Association had finished its set-up, apart from building the towers and appointing towermen.[^4] On 24 December 1936 the same paper reported that three fire towers had been built.[^5] It names three sites, one of them "at Barnwell, near" the Charleston highway, but it does not name this tower, so I cannot be certain it is the same one.[^5] The sources do not settle whether the tower was finished in 1935 or in 1936.

A state forestry report from 1939, as cited by the Eastern US Lookouts page, says Civilian Conservation Corps enrollees repainted the living quarters at the tower site in 1938 and 1939.[^3]

South Carolina ended fire observation from its lookouts in 1993, and this tower has since been decommissioned as a fire tower.[^2] It now serves as a radio repeater, which receives weak signals and sends them on at a higher power.[^2] The lowest steps have been removed to discourage climbing.[^2] The site is on South Carolina Highway 64 in Barnwell.[^2] The sources do not describe any other access, so treat the site as restricted and do not climb the tower.
