Binarch Mountain is a lookout site in the Kaniksu National Forest, at about 4,170 feet.[^2] The first lookout, built in the 1920s, was a 40-foot pole tower with a cab and a cabin for living quarters.[^1][^3] Forest Lookouts describes the cab as open, while Idaho Fire Lookouts and firelookout.com describe a 7x7 cab.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1934 the pole tower was replaced by an 80-foot steel Aermotor tower with a 7x7 cab, and another cabin went up with it.[^1][^2][^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists known staff from 1930 to 1952: Mr. and Mrs. Sutliff in 1930, Jim Parr in 1931, Al Bennett in 1934, Phil McVay for 1944 to 1946, and Ralph Jaszowski in 1952.[^2] The same page also lists a Robert L McVey for the 1944 to 1946 period, so it is unclear whether that name is spelled McVay or McVey.[^2]

In 1959 the tower was dismantled and moved to Newport Hill.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com says the tower was still there in 1955 and has since been removed.[^3] Newport Hill's own page says the site was developed in 1938 with a crow's nest, a tent cabin and a 40-foot Aermotor steel tower. It says the tower and the cabin living quarters, built in 1959, have since been removed.[^4] The Binarch pages describe the tower moved in 1959 as 80 feet, while the Newport Hill page describes a 40-foot tower from 1938, so the sources do not say what was moved there.[^1][^4]

Today, Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the Binarch site as a hike-in.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the original site, and the Newport Hill tower is also listed as removed.[^4]
