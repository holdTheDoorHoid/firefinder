Table Mountain stands about 12 miles east of Waldport, in Lincoln County, Oregon.[^2][^3] A second register entry, Table Mountain #2, lies less than a kilometre from this site, and the sources do not always say which lookout they describe.[^4][^5]

Firelookout.com lists this site's first structure as a crow's nest and camp from 1924, followed by a shake cabin with an open cupola in 1926.[^1] For 1932 its entry gives a 50-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6-foot cab and a cabin, and says the lookout was abandoned in 1959.[^1] The agencies named there are the Lincoln FPA and the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^1]

Other sources start the story earlier. The Osborne project page dates the lookout's first setup to 1915, when it was reached through a telephone link.[^2] A May 1915 item described a station that was still to be established, and a 1917 Lincoln FPA report mentions a government lookout station on top of the mountain.[^3]

In March 1926 a ranger reported five or six fires and a great deal of smoke, and a man was sent up to the peak to help locate them.[^3]

A Siuslaw Oar item from September 1931 reports that Henry Osterhoff had just finished a 60-foot observation tower there.[^3] In June 1932 the Corvallis Gazette-Times described a new tower, built the previous fall, as 50 feet high.[^3] The two heights do not agree.

Items from 1931 to 1933 name the summer lookout man as Kocher, though the given names or nicknames differ from item to item.[^3] A 1932 item, which calls him Pap, says he had served in the post for 11 years.[^3]

A 1958 Lincoln FPA report says a 100-foot tower taken apart at the Little Hebo lookout was rebuilt on Table Mountain, where a 16-by-22-foot ground cabin was also built.[^3] The report expected the station to be operating for the 1959 fire season.[^3]

The register's second entry says a 102-foot tower moved from Little Hebo replaced the 1930s crow's nest there in 1959, and it mentions a removal in 1973.[^4]

Firelookout.com's 1959 abandonment date sits awkwardly with a 1960 Lincoln FPA report, which describes more work on the ground cabin.[^1][^3]

The Osborne page and the Forest Lookouts page both report a dismantling or removal in 1973, but neither says which of the two sites came down.[^2][^3]

No source we found describes what, if anything, remains of this site today, or whether it is open to visitors. The register notes a monument to a pack mule called Grandma at the foot of the second tower, but does not say whether it survives.[^4]
