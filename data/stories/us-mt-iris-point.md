Iris Point is a peak on the Lolo National Forest, in Missoula County, Montana, about 16 miles southeast of Missoula, at an elevation of 6,425 feet.[^1] Sources disagree on where it lies from Bonita. A 1930 report puts it about five miles southeast of Bonita, while a federal survey datasheet puts it 5.0 miles west-southwest of the Bonita Ranger Station.[^2]

Firelookout.com says a platform set in three trees came first, in the 1920s, and a 30-foot pole tower was built in 1930.[^1] The Missoula National Forest had announced the new station in May 1930.[^2] That plan called for a 50-foot tower with a lookout stationed there during the fire season.[^2] By November 1930 the tower was finished: a 30-foot wooden tower with 14-foot-square living quarters enclosed in glass, which cost about $70.[^2]

A September 1930 report described the planned design: a 14-by-14-foot cabin on a timber frame, with its floor 30 feet off the ground, which would let the lookout stay at his post virtually around the clock.[^2]

According to firelookout.com, a pop-up cupola trailer served as the lookout from 1961 to 1972.[^1] The site has stood unused since, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^1][^3]

We found no source that describes what remains on the peak today. A federal survey datasheet, reproduced on the Forest Lookouts page, says the survey marker was anchored in rock beneath the middle of the lookout tower.[^2] It describes two ways in: a Forest Service trail from Gilbert Creek, about a three-hour pack, or a shorter route up Schwartz Creek, where the truck road ends and the last stretch is a pack along the ridge of about an hour and a half.[^2] The description dates from 1957, and the datasheet does not say whether the marker or any footings are still there.[^2]
