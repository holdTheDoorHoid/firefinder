Fredenburg Butte stands about three miles north of Butte Falls in Jackson County, Oregon, at an elevation of 4,320 feet.[^2] By about 1920 a cupola-style lookout house had been built on the butte.[^2][^3]

The round-pole tower that followed has two dates. A history of the Rogue River National Forest, as transcribed by Forest Lookouts, puts a forty-foot round-pole tower with a 14-by-14 cabin on the butte in 1935.[^3] That history says the station was linked to the Jackson County Fire Patrol Association, which was to staff it and look after it during fire season.[^3] The former lookout site register and firelookout.com both date the tower to 1937 and give its height as 30 feet.[^1][^2] A Forest Service engineering memo from August 1942 puts the tower at 30 feet and lists a 14-by-14 Aladdin-type cabin, which matches the shorter height.[^3]

In August 1936 Mrs. Ira Tungate, the widow of a state forest patrolman, discovered a 30-acre fire on Ginger Creek, about three miles southeast of Butte Falls, at the lookout station. State and CCC crews were fighting it overnight.[^3] A newspaper of the time reported that she was thought to be the Forest Service's only woman lookout.[^3] In 1941 Charles "Jiggs" Shepardson worked as the lookout until he was released from duty on September 21.[^3]

From September 11, 1942, to June 14, 1943, the station was listed as part of the Roseburg Filter Center.[^3] In a June 15, 1943 letter to the state forester, a Forest Service engineer said he understood the Rogue River forest had notified the state warden that Fredenburg was one of the posts to be eliminated. He asked to be given a few days' notice before the post was inactivated.[^3]

Upkeep records run from 1951 to 1965, and the ground cabin was torn down in 1960.[^3] The lookout tower was removed in 1969.[^1][^3] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year, so the sources differ on how it ended.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the butte now, or says whether the site is open to visitors. Check with the land manager before going.
