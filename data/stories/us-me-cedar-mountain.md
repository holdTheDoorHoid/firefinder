Maine put up a steel lookout on Cedar Mountain, in the town of Parsonsfield in York County, in 1920. Work started in July, and crews also built a camp for the watchman and a mile of telephone line on the mountain. The job was finished in early September.[^1]

In June 1922 the Daily Kennebec Journal reported plans to take the tower down and move it to the summit of Green Mountain, just across the New Hampshire line. That summit is about 800 feet higher, and the paper said the new site had a view reaching all the way to the coast. Both states were to share the upkeep, and the Governor and Council approved the plan.[^1]

The sources agree that the tower was moved, though they differ on the year.[^2][^3][^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association and the New Hampshire register both say 1922.[^2][^3] A hiking account in the Laconia Daily Sun dates the move to 1921.[^4] The tower no longer stands on Cedar Mountain.[^2]

The structure now stands on Green Mountain in Effingham, New Hampshire.[^2][^4] The register says the original 47-foot tower was cut to 39 feet in 1977, and that it is now in part-time service.[^3] The Laconia account gives the tower's height as 60 feet and describes a 1.4-mile trail up to the summit.[^4]

No source describes what remains on Cedar Mountain today, so this record cannot say what, if anything, a visitor would find on the mountain.
