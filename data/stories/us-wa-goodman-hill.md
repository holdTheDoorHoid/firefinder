Goodman Hill's lookout is a 100-foot wooden tower with an 8-by-8-foot cab, built in 1955.[^1][^2] It is the lowest elevation standing fire lookout in Washington, though the sources do not agree on its height above sea level: NHLR gives 541 feet, while TrailChick and a blog post say 480.[^2][^1][^3]

The site lies on Joint Base Lewis-McChord, and firelookout.com places the tower on the Fort Lewis Military Reservation.[^1][^4] TrailChick says it found little to no information about the lookout's history, and the sources here do not say why it was built or who built it.[^2]

The lookout has been abandoned since 1970.[^1][^4] In 2002 an effort was made to save the tower, while the Army was considering using it for demolition practice. It was later offered for sale, with conditions for removal set.[^5] The sources do not say what became of that sale. NHLR reports recent threats of demolition, and a WillhiteWeb page with 2013 photographs says it is still standing.[^1][^6]

The lookout is reachable when Training Area 4 on the base is open for recreation, and visitors need a visitor permit and range pass.[^2][^3] NHLR says access is limited but possible by contacting the base.[^1] The tower is fenced and has barbed wire along the top. It is not safe to climb, though eager visitors have done it, so stay off it.[^6]
