In August 1940 the Salt Lake Tribune reported that new fire lookouts had been set up in Yellowstone, including one at Bunsen Peak.[^1] The paper linked the move to hazards in the park's forests and grazing land, and said these were the first lookouts added in several years.[^1]

Bunsen Peak lies in Yellowstone National Park, about two miles south of Mammoth Hot Springs.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wyoming list records the site as a shelter that is gone.[^3] Firelookout.com also marks the lookout as gone.[^2]

Today the summit can be reached on foot.[^4] The National Park Service describes a 4.2-mile trail, there and back, to the top, and warns that grizzly bears frequent the area.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit.
