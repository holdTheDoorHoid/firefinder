Loneman Lookout stands above the Nyack valley in the southern part of Glacier National Park, reached by trail from the Nyack Ranger Station.[^1] It is a 14-by-14-foot two-story cab much like Apgar Lookout, except its catwalk is braced with angle supports rather than plain posts.[^1] Accounts of its early history do not agree. The National Historic Lookout Register and Wikipedia's infobox date the present structure to 1930, while firelookout.com says it was built in 1929 to replace an earlier 1928 cabin that had been destroyed by fire, and Wikipedia's own article text separately states a construction date of 1933.[^1][^2][^3] The fire that destroyed an earlier structure is mentioned only by firelookout.com; no other source consulted confirms it.

Like its neighbors on Apgar Mountain and Mount Brown, Loneman was built to a standard U.S. Forest Service design, adapted by the Park Service as part of a chain of lookouts meant to give overlapping views across Glacier's backcountry.[^2] No source consulted gives details on specific staff members or notable fires spotted from the tower.

Loneman was added to the National Register of Historic Places on February 14, 1986, and registered with the National Historic Lookout Register on September 15, 1991, as US 36.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com reports that the cab was completely renovated in 1998.[^3]

The lookout still stands at just over 7,000 feet elevation and is administered by the National Park Service.[^1] It can be reached by trail, but none of the sources consulted say it is open to the public to enter or stay in, so it is best treated as a historic structure to visit from outside.
