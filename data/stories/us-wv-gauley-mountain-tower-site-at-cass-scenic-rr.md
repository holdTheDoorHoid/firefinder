Its first site was Gauley Mountain in Fayette County, West Virginia.[^2] A Charleston Gazette item from October 1926, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, describes a telephone link from the tower to a coal company at Ansted, so the tower was already in use by then.[^3] We found no date for its first erection. The Forest Lookouts page lists the West Virginia Division of Forestry as its agency.[^3]

In August 1928 the Charleston Daily Mail reported that J.M. Hammond, of Ansted, had been named section ranger with headquarters at the tower. The same item also reported a full renovation covering the roof, the windows, the observation-room floor and the paint.[^3] We found no record of particular fires it watched.

The National Historic Lookout Register gives 2009 as the tower's build year, yet its own description says the tower was moved to Cass that October.[^1] The 1926 and 1928 items show the tower already in use well before then, so the 2009 date seems to mark the move and re-erection, not the original build.[^1][^3]

The Fayette site is listed as removed in 2009.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page dates the Blaw-Knox tower's installation at Cass Scenic Railroad State Park to October 28, 2009, and describes it as meant more to be looked at than looked from.[^3]

Today the Register places the tower at Whittaker Station in Cass Scenic Railroad State Park, Pocahontas County, and says the West Virginia Division of Natural Resources administers it.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^4] It joins a collection of exhibits on West Virginia forestry, and the Register says it is accessible by train from Cass.[^1] The sources do not say whether visitors may climb it, so check with the park before planning to.
