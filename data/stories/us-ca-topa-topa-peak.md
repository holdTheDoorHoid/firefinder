The Forest Service built a fire lookout on Topa Topa Peak in 1938, above Ojai Valley in the Los Padres National Forest.[^1][^2] Mules did the hauling.[^2] A Piru News item from that summer said the steel was carried 15 miles from the valley on a winding trail.[^2] A Santa Maria paper had expected 350 packloads of material in June, and in October it reported that 380 loads had gone up and the station was finished.[^2]

The wooden cab sat on a steel H-brace frame built to California Region 5 plan BC-301. The cab was 14 feet square.[^1][^3] The sources disagree about the height. The register and a July 1938 newspaper say 20 feet.[^1][^2] A June 1939 newspaper says 30 feet, and Craig Carey, quoting a 1945 forest history, gives the same figure.[^2][^3]

The lookout was meant to see what other towers could not. A newspaper said it would look down into Sespe canyons that were hidden from the other lookouts.[^2] It replaced the older Santa Paula Peak lookout, which was to be left empty except in emergencies.[^2]

A June 1939 newspaper reported that Dale Bever, a forestry student from Oregon, had installed himself in the new lookout, which was being used for the first time.[^2] A patrolman was to bring the station supplies by horse and pack animal.[^2] Public access ended in 1959, when the Sespe Condor Sanctuary was enlarged, according to Carey.[^3]

The Day Fire reached the peak on September 16, 2006. It burned the wooden cab away, but the steel tower survived.[^1][^2][^3]

Today the register says the tower still stands. A 2013 blog post describes a shuttered, abandoned site with little left but a steel frame.[^1][^4] The register listed the site on December 19, 2019.[^1] We did not check for a current Forest Service notice, so treat the site as closed and keep off the frame.[^3]
