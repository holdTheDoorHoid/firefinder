The Itasca State Park lookout began as one of a batch of new towers the state erected in the winter and spring before May 1929. A Helena Independent article from that month, reprinted on a Forest Lookouts page, calls it recently completed, 100 feet tall, and all steel, with an enclosed stairway and a glass cab at the top. Rangers would use it to find fires, and the article counts it as one of 30 towers erected that season.[^1]

Visitors signed its register too.[^1] A Brainerd Daily Dispatch report from September 1934 says 3,956 people signed the park tower's register in August 1934, up from 3,166 in August 1933.[^1]

The tower did not stay at the headquarters site.[^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the state Division of Forestry erected the tower in the 1920s and moved it into Itasca State Park in 1937, where it took on visitor education and fire detection work.[^3] A county survey list dates that move to 1939 and says the tower began as the headquarters lookout.[^2]

A replacement, listed as Fire Tower 67, took its place at the headquarters. A 1971 survey note says that tower was shifted roughly 100 feet in 1940, and the county list marks it removed.[^2] The firelookout.org listing for the park gives the same coordinates and marks the tower gone.[^2][^5]

The Aiton Heights tower still stands in the park.[^2][^3] The county list says it is available to visitors in summer, and the NHLR says it continues in service with a park interpreter.[^2][^3] The park's exhibit list includes one called Fire Spotters, Aiton Heights Fire Tower.[^4]

The sources disagree on two details. The move year is 1937 in the NHLR and 1939 in the county list. The height is 80 feet in the NHLR and 100 feet in the county list.[^3][^2] The 1929 article's 100-foot tower with an enclosed stairway fits the county's headquarters tower at Aiton Heights, so the two most likely describe one structure, though no source says so outright.[^1][^2]
