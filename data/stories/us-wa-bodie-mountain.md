Bodie Mountain sits in Ferry County, Washington, on Colville National Forest land, at 5,739 feet (1,749 m).[^1][^2]

A first outlook was under construction in 1914.[^3][^4] A Republic newspaper reported that August that a new outlook station was being built there.[^4] Willhite says a telephone line had been completed to the site that year.[^3] A log cabin with a platform above its roof was completed in 1915.[^1][^3][^5]

Forest Ranger E. W. Wheeler manned the site through September 1914, according to Willhite.[^3] firelookout.com lists other known lookout staff: Dave Gillespie in 1923, William C. Anderson in 1952, and Gary Kelly in 1958 and 1959.[^2]

In 1930 a 20-foot tower with an L-4 cab replaced the cabin.[^1][^2][^3] Mike Moran led the crew that built it.[^3][^4] A September 1930 Republic item had said a 16-foot tower would be built.[^4] The Register, firelookout.com and Willhite all give 20 feet for the tower that went up.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service turned the tower into a three-story house for its sleeping quarters.[^1][^3][^5] The station ran from July 25, 1942 until July 1, 1943.[^3][^4]

A 20-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 flat cab replaced the L-4 in 1963.[^1][^3]

The last lookout came down in 1975.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] The Register and Weebly say it was removed, firelookout.com and SummitPost say it was destroyed, and Willhite says it was "removed or destroyed."[^1][^4][^2][^5][^3]

Today, SummitPost, a climbing site, says drivers can reach the summit with no gate in the way.[^5] It says the summit road has rough stretches, some where high clearance is preferred.[^5] It also says that years after the last lookout was destroyed, a couple of small communications towers went up at the summit.[^5] A photo caption there mentions a "Historic Remnant," but the text does not say what remains.[^5] None of the history pages I read describes what is on the summit today.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
