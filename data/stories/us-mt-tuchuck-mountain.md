Tuchuck Mountain stands at 7,751 feet in Flathead County, about 22 miles northwest of Polebridge, on the Flathead National Forest.[^1] Its time as a lookout began in 1930.[^3] A history of the Flathead National Forest says Roy Davis arrived that season, the first one the peak was used as a lookout, and found that the trail crew's phone line to the point was the only improvement.[^3]

Davis set up a stand for a map and an alidade, hooked up a portable telephone, and cut and hauled up a ridge pole and tent supports.[^3] Later that summer, precut materials and plans reached the summit, and two men assembled a lookout building.[^3] Davis slept one night in it before leaving at the close of the season.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts page gives the same first-season sequence, saying the lookout house was built from precut materials brought up to the site. It lists the site as removed, with no date.[^2]

Firelookout.com gives a different account. It dates an L-4 cab to 1931 and says it was destroyed in 1957.[^1] The Forest history supports 1930 for the building, which leaves the 1931 date unexplained.[^3] The two sources also disagree on the fate: one says destroyed, the other removed.[^1][^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors may go up. Ask the forest before planning a visit.
