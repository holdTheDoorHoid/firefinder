The Hale fire tower stood in Iosco County, in the Huron National Forest.[^1] A newspaper item from August 1935, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, reported that Michigan's newest fire tower had just been completed.[^1] It was a standard stairway type, 100 feet (30 m) from the ground to the peak of the cabin, and the item placed it in Iosco County, south of Hale and east of West Branch.[^1] The same item said the new tower brought the number of manned Michigan towers to 138.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts page treats that tower as the Hale tower. The newspaper names only a spot south of Hale, so the link between the two comes from the page, not from the newspaper.[^1]

The top of the tower is now at Higgins Lake, in Roscommon County.[^2] A historical marker there, erected by the Michigan History Center and the Department of Natural Resources, says it is "just the top part of the fire tower from Hale, Michigan."[^2] The marker's text says the Department of Natural Resources and the Civilian Conservation Corps built 154 towers, 100 to 150 feet (30 to 46 m) tall, in the 1920s to 1940s.[^2]

The marker can be reached from North Higgins Lake Drive near Conservation Drive. A photograph taken January 2, 2023, and listed with the marker, shows the top portion of one of the 154 towers.[^2] No source we read describes the original Hale site today, so we cannot say whether footings or other remains are there.[^1][^2]
