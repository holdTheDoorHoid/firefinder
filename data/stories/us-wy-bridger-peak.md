Bridger Peak is the high point of the Sierra Madre in southeastern Wyoming, at 11,004 feet, in Carbon County.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com gives its elevation as 11,007 feet.[^3]

A plan for a lookout here appears in a 1911 newspaper item. The Saratoga Sun reported that Supervisor James Blackhall meant to put a stone and cement station on the summit and keep a man there through the summer.[^4] The register dates the build to 1925.[^1] A 1921 item from the Encampment Echo, though, describes registrants signing the travelers' register in the lookout house on the peak that summer.[^4] So a building stood there at least four years before the register's date, or the register's date is off. The sources do not settle which.[^1][^4]

The register quotes a Grand Encampment Museum oral history, which describes thick stone walls, a small window facing each direction, and a platform on top.[^1]

The 1911 plan named a watchman, J.P. Aredell, whom the paper linked to the 1889 gold finds at Gold Hill.[^4] Sixty people signed the register in 1921, five times the count for 1920.[^4]

The register lists the tower as removed in the 1960s. Its description says the rock foundation of the lookout and a radio facility remain on the summit.[^1] Firelookout.com marks the site as gone.[^3]

Today the summit is a hike.[^2][^5] A 2022 hiker walked the ridge from a trailhead on the south side of Battle Pass and called it a former fire lookout site.[^5] A 2026 writer hiked up the Continental Divide Trail, which follows a forest road for much of the way, and described the lookout as decommissioned.[^2] Neither account mentions a closure, but no official source describes current access.[^2][^5]

The forest's name has changed over time. A 1911 item names the Hayden National Forest, while the register places the peak in the Medicine Bow-Routt National Forests.[^4][^1]
