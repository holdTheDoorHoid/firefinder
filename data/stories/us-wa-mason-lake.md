The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says an Aircraft Warning Service station was built at Mason Lake in 1942.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page says the station, "Charlie 5-1," was activated on September 11, 1942. It was new construction on private land, and the state kept the site when the station was deactivated.[^4]

By contrast, firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb describe a cab dated to 1952, and neither mentions the 1942 station.[^2][^3] The sources do not explain how the two fit together.[^1][^2][^3]

A June 1947 item from the Shelton-Mason County Journal, quoted on Rex's page, says Mr. and Mrs. Stockman were stationed at the lookout.[^4]

Sources put the abandonment in 1961 or earlier, and the lookout is now gone.[^1][^2][^3]

In 2014 WillhiteWeb's author walked the cut summit on skid roads and could not tell where the lookout had stood.[^3] The clear-cut gave wide views, which the author described as taking in the Olympics and, over the trees, Mount Rainier.[^3] The land then belonged to Green Diamond Resources, which the page says usually allows non-motorized access such as hiking.[^3] The walk is a half-mile hike from a Green Diamond gate off East Trails End Road.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site now, and the access rules may have changed since 2014.[^1][^2][^3]
