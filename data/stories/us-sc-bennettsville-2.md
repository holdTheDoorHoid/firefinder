The Bennettsville lookout was built in 1938 as a 100-foot (30 m) Aermotor LS-40 steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, for the South Carolina Forestry Commission.[^1][^2] It first stood near the town of Bennettsville, in Marlboro County.[^1][^2]

On Commission towers, an operator's hours in the cab depended on fire danger.[^3] On high-danger days, an operator could spend up to ten hours in the cab each day.[^3]

The Commission shut down its fire tower system on October 1, 1993.[^3] Forest Lookouts says a private party bought the tower, moved it, and set it up at a private location, giving the sale year only as 199??.[^2] The register says Billy McIntosh bought the tower from the Commission in the late 1990s, then moved it to Sumter County, where he refurbished it and erected it again on his property.[^1] A magazine feature that covers McIntosh's towers says the state hired him to move one of them in the early 2000s, just after they went up for auction.[^4]

The register listed the tower on February 24, 2013.[^1] McIntosh calls the tower in Sumter County the Mine Hill tower.[^1]

The tower now stands on McIntosh's private property in Sumter County.[^1] Forest Lookouts does not say what remains at the original Bennettsville site today.[^2] Because the land is privately owned, ask the owner before you plan any visit, and do not go onto the property without permission.[^1]
