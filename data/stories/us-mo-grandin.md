The Grandin lookout tower stands in Little Black Conservation Area, southeast of the community of Grandin in Ripley County, Missouri.[^1] The Missouri Department of Conservation's annual report lists 160 acres acquired for a towersite in fiscal 1949–50.[^2] The survey names the previous owners as Glenellyn and Raymond Robertson and Francis and Blanche Keeley, and dates the purchase to 1949.[^1]

Sources disagree on when the tower went up. The Forest Lookouts history gives 1951 for a 100-foot steel tower.[^2] The survey, citing MDC files, gives 1963 for the current tower. It also notes that the site is visible on 1957 and 1960 topographic maps, which conflicts with 1963 and suggests a previous tower may have stood there.[^1]

The tower is steel-framed and stands 100 feet (30.5 m) tall, with wood steps and decking and a 7-by-7 cab.[^1] No maker's plaque was identified, but the survey says the design is similar to Aermotor towers.[^1] Aermotor of Chicago made most of Missouri's fire towers, and most of them stood 100 feet tall.[^3]

In the 1956–57 fiscal year, the state recorded major repairs to the tower.[^2]

By the time of the survey, the bottom two tiers of the stairs had been removed. The survey rated the tower in fair to good condition, noted rust and missing window glass in the cab, and did not recommend it for the National Register of Historic Places.[^1] The survey refers to a planned demolition.[^1]

The tower stands in Little Black Conservation Area.[^1] No source I could open says whether it has been taken down, or whether the site is open to visitors. Please do not climb it. Its lowest steps are missing on purpose, to keep people off.[^1]
