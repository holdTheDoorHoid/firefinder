The Civilian Conservation Corps built Briar's lookout, a 100-foot steel tower, in the mid-to-late 1930s.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the work to 1938, when CCC Camp 3751 also put up a towerman's dwelling and two garages.[^1] A Forest Lookouts history gives 1936 for the steel tower's erection, so the two accounts disagree on the year.[^2]

A 1946 notice moved a worker to the station as lookout-dispatcher, and the Register says employees staffed the tower often until 2005.[^2][^1] After that, it handled afternoon checks and the work of locating reported smoke.[^1] The sources I checked do not name a particular fire the tower helped catch.

Vandals did lasting damage. Vandalism destroyed the Osborne fire finder between 2005 and 2010, and the lookout fell into disrepair until 2016, when district employees and AmeriCorps crews began restoring it.[^1] In 2021 vandalism damaged the fire finder's stand beyond repair; it was later rebuilt, and the interior lower walls and floor were repainted.[^1] In 2024 a fire finder from the removed Fort Leonard Wood lookout was restored and placed in the tower.[^1]

Today the lookout has no dedicated staff. It is used for prescribed-fire monitoring and for occasional visits from fire staff during fire season.[^1] The Mark Twain National Forest's Eleven Point Ranger District oversees it.[^1] Restoration is still under way: a radio repeater is being added to the structure, electric power is being restored, and the stairway landings are to be replaced with grated metal.[^1] I found no public statement on whether visitors may climb it, so check with the Forest Service before going.
