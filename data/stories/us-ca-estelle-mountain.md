Estelle Mountain sits between Corona and Lake Elsinore in Riverside County.[^1][^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps and the California Division of Forestry built a lookout there in 1935.[^1][^2] The cab was a 14-by-14-foot design on a 30-foot steel tower.[^1][^2] The sources name the cab design differently: the register calls it a C-3 cab, while the FFLA-SDRC page calls it a BC-301.[^1][^2]

In October 1936 a California Ranger item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, says a Forest Service lookout named Hutchinson hosted about twenty Scouts inside the tower.[^4] A November 20, 1936 item from the same newsletter refers to the lookout as the 'Wandering Spirit of Estelle Peak.' It says that spirit spotted a fire eight miles away and sent a crew to a bonfire on that exact spot.[^4]

The tower was taken apart and moved to the Hemet-Ryan Air Attack Base.[^2][^1] The sources give different years. The register says about 1958, the marker and Peakbagging say 1958, and FFLA-SDRC says before 1960.[^1][^5][^3][^2] The Forest Service began operating at Hemet-Ryan in 1957.[^2]

The lookout still stands at the air base and works there as the ground control tower.[^2] The marker at the control tower says the lookout was built on Estelle Mountain in 1935 and moved in 1958.[^5] The register lists the lookout as registered on December 16, 2008.[^1]

The tower sits at a working air base, and no source we read describes public visits there.[^2] Check with the base before going.

The original site, just east of Interstate 15, is now a preserve.[^2] FFLA-SDRC calls it the Lake Mathews Estelle Mountain Preserve, while Peakbagging calls the area an ecological reserve.[^2][^3] A high-voltage transmission line runs over the spot.[^3] Peakbagging's photo captions describe a square concrete slab with a survey benchmark placed in 1939, and remains of a wood pole that could have been part of the lookout.[^3] No source we read describes public access to the original site.
