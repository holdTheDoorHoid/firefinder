The Neah Bay site is in Clallam County, Washington, about 9 miles southeast of Neah Bay.[^1][^2]

The sources do not agree on what stood here. The register describes a shake-sided cabin on skid runners, used during World War II and possibly as a fire lookout.[^1] The register names Crown Pacific Timber Company as administrator, while another listing gives the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR.[^1][^2]

A Forest Lookouts page treats the site differently.[^3] It lists an Aircraft Warning Service post, activated on September 20, 1942, under the Port Angeles Filter Center.[^3]

Willhite writes that the cabin is a 1940s frame building of unknown date.[^4] He thinks the site was probably bare in 1942, the year an aircraft warning station named Neah went up about two miles northwest.[^4] He suggests the cabin may have been moved here after the war.[^4]

The register says the structure has caved in.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page lists collapsed ruins.[^3]

The site is on private timberland.[^4] Willhite describes a gate on the signed Sekiu River Road, off Highway 112, where the signs disagree. One sign's wording limits the gate to authorized vehicles, while red lettering on the same sign allows daytime recreational use.[^4] Other nearby signs warn against trespassing.[^4] Willhite thought visitors could probably go in, but suggested making a call or two first, in case they end up stuck behind the gate.[^4] Given those signs, check with the landowner before going past the gate.
