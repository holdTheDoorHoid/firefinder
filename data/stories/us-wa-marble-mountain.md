The Marble Mountain lookout stood near Curlew in Ferry County, on Colville National Forest land.[^1][^4] The first structure was a rag camp with a 20-foot pole platform in 1922, and a log cabin followed in 1924.[^1][^3] A 30-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6-foot cab came in 1926.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on when a glass-enclosed cab went onto that tower. Willhite gives 1927, while a passage quoted on the Weebly page is dated February 1928.[^2][^4]

Clarence Stovik was the lookout in each year from 1930 through 1933, according to Willhite. Firelookout.com also names Stanley Pearse for 1923 and Lloyd Bransford for 1929.[^2][^3]

On June 23, 1933, Stovik spotted a smoke on Big Goosmus Creek. A landowner had set the fire while getting rid of slash on his property without a permit.[^4]

In 1934 a ranger at Curlew started building a cabin for the lookout's use.[^4][^2]

The Aircraft Warning Service used the site from August 3, 1942, until July 1, 1943, and added sleeping quarters.[^4][^2]

In 1944 a 30-foot tower with an L-4 cab replaced the 1926 structure.[^1][^3] Mrs. Mabel Newton and her daughter, who came from Curlew, staffed the lookout that year.[^2][^4]

The last lookout named in these sources is Leonard Wang, listed for 1945.[^3]

The register and Willhite say the last lookout was removed in 1970.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com says the tower was destroyed that year instead.[^3]

The register says a guy-line anchor from the 1926 tower and the footings of the 1944 lookout are all that remain.[^1] A 1942 outhouse below the summit burned in the 2015 Stickpin Fire, and the lookout itself was gone by then.[^1][^2]

A WillhiteWeb photo caption dates a picture of the footings to 2019.[^2]

Willhite suggests approaching from the south on the paved Boulder Creek Road, which runs from Curlew to Orient, then taking Forest Road 6120, which gets pretty close to the summit.[^2] The last stretch needs 4WD clearance, and the hike runs from zero to 1.5 miles.[^2]

No source we read says whether the road is gated or closed. There is no tower left to climb.[^1][^2]
