The register and firelookout.com both date the Sweetgrass Butte tower to 1933.[^1][^2] Willhite's history describes a 30-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab built that year.[^3] It also names George Storms as the lookout in 1932. He did not start observing until mid-July, and Willhite says the site was still a camp at the time.[^3] The sources do not fully agree on when the station began.

In 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service used the lookout to watch for enemy aircraft.[^3] The service added sleeping quarters and refurbished the lookout.[^3] The site was active from March 16, 1942 until July 1, 1943.[^3]

Firelookout.com lists the known lookout staff as Dan Noble in 1952, Mike Lynch in 1954 and Keith Casey in 1958.[^2] Willhite's page names Ward Shafer as the lookout guard in 1962 and reports 240 visitors to his station that summer.[^3]

In 1936 the Brewster Herald suggested a drive to the lookout: go first to Winthrop, then up the Chewack River to the Cub Creek road.[^3]

In 1967 a local resident bought the tower for removal.[^1][^3] During the crane removal, a cable snapped and the cab crashed down.[^1]

A 1973 Herald-Reporter item, as quoted by Willhite, said permanent use had been discontinued in the mid-1960s. It also said the site still served as an emergency lookout during lightning storms and high fire danger weather.[^3] The sources do not say what filled that role once the tower was gone.

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors may go there.
