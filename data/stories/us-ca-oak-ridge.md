Oak Ridge sits in northern Sonoma County, near Annapolis.[^2] In late 1934 the state forestry department approved the site instead of Big Mountain, and members of the Annapolis CCC camp were to build the station.[^2] The register says the CCC built the first tower, a 100-foot wooden structure with a 5-by-5-foot cab, in 1939.[^1] A 1974 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout history page, dates a 90-foot wooden tower to 1942 instead, and says the first road was built in 1932 and 1933.[^2] The dates do not fully agree, so the first build year is still uncertain.

The register says CalFire staffed the lookout from about 1940 to 1994.[^1] In August 1958 a newspaper item named Viola Eldredge of Geyserville as the person serving as lookout, and noted that she had to climb nearly 100 steps.[^2]

The wooden tower was later replaced by a 91-foot steel tower with a 5-by-5-foot cab and a catwalk.[^1][^3] The register's description and Peakbagging both date that replacement to 1959, while a 1974 article and the history page give 1958.[^1][^2][^3]

The history page lists the site as an Aircraft Warning Service station in 1942.[^2] A 1974 article adds that after Pearl Harbor a husband and wife team staffed it as an aircraft spotting post.[^2] The state forestry division has leased the site since 1961, when Cloverdale Redwood Company owned the land.[^2] Masonite Corporation took over the land in 1970 and kept the lease.[^2] In April 1974 the company announced it was deeding the site to the State of California.[^2]

The register added the lookout to its list in November 2009 and names the Cal Fire Lake Napa Ranger Unit as a cooperator.[^1] A 2012 photo note calls the lookout in good condition, says it may be staffed in fire season, and says CalFire maintains it.[^3] The register's photo captions show a residence cabin dated 2018, and a pump house and cistern.[^1]

The site also carries county radio equipment. A June 2015 Sonoma County agenda item says the county has run its emergency communications from the site since 1980, and that Crown Castle, a separate company, owns a tower there and leases ground from the state.[^4]

I found no public visiting information for Oak Ridge, so I cannot say whether visitors may go up. Check with CalFire before you go, and do not climb the tower.
