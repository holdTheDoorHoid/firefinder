Gile Mountain overlooks the 290-acre Woody Adams Conservation Forest in northwest Norwich, and its summit tower is about 70 feet tall, according to Norwich Trails.[^1]

A state report for the year ending June 30, 1940 says the Plymouth Civilian Conservation Corps camp had built a steel lookout tower, a cabin and a latrine on the mountain.[^2] Norwich Trails, citing a Norwich Historical Society article, says the tower was used for fire spotting and, during World War II, for aerial defense observation.[^1]

In the 1949-50 biennium five stair treads that hedgehogs had damaged were replaced, and in 1951-52 the stairs and cab were painted.[^2] The state reports first describe the tower as discontinued in the 1953 to 1954 biennium, though it could be put back in service if needed.[^2] The 1957-58 report says it was kept inactive, with patrol planes covering the area in bad weather.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing. Its key uses an asterisk for towers that have lost their cab, and Gile has none.[^3] The association's 2025 report says cab railings were installed inside the tower, and that new white oak stairs went in.[^4]

Norwich Trails says the work was done by volunteers over several weeks, and a grant from the Norwich Women's Club paid for most of the material.[^5] The 2025 report says the Norwich Town Conservation Commission maintains the tower.[^4]

Norwich Trails says the old treads had worn down after several decades of use by visitors who climbed up for the view.[^5] The trailhead is a small lot on Turnpike Road with room for five or six cars if parked with care, and granite steps cover much of the foot route up the mountain.[^1]
