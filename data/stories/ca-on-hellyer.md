Hellyer Tower was a steel fire lookout near Foleyet in Ontario, standing above Ivanhoe Lake.[^1] A 1943 Ottawa Journal article, which the Canada Lookouts index reproduces, describes a cab 2.4 m (8 ft) square set on steel girders 30.5 m (100 ft) high, about 366 m (1,200 ft) above the lake.[^1] The tower takes its name from a wild township shown on the timber map.[^1]

In 1943 the tower was worked by Mrs. Raoul Languerand, whom the paper calls the only woman lookout in the Ontario Forestry Service.[^1] Most of her working day, seven days a week, was spent up in the glassed-in tower, and she went up the 100 rungs at least three times a day, usually five.[^1] Her home was a small white cabin at the base of the tower, reached by a rough trail of over 2.4 km (1.5 miles) from the lakeshore to the summit.[^1]

The Wikipedia article on Foleyet says Lake Pishkanogami only became Ivanhoe Lake in 1960.[^2] That sits oddly with the 1943 article, which already calls the water Ivanhoe Lake, so this story keeps the newspaper's wording.[^1]

No source found describes what remains at the Hellyer site today, or whether it is open to visitors.[^1]
