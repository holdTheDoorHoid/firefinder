The Hat Creek Rim lookout site is in Shasta County, California, in the Lassen National Forest, at about 5,126 feet (1,562 meters).[^1] The register says the site was built for the Lassen forest in 1934. Its cabin was a Region 5 Plan BC-301 design, set on a 30-foot steel K-brace tower.[^1]

The first staffing we can date comes from a newspaper. A 1938 item from The Searchlight, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, mentions a relief guard, Woodrow McCowan, covering the rim east of the Hat Creek ranger station until a permanent lookout was due to take over on 10 June.[^2] In late September that year the same paper named Stanley Billings as the rim lookout.[^2] The item does not name this tower, so the link is likely but not certain.[^2]

The cabin's end came in 1987. The Lassen County Times, also quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, reported a blaze of about 3,000 acres on Hat Creek that September. Forest Service backfires escaped in high winds, and the fire grew.[^2] The paper listed the Hat Creek Lookout Station among two structures lost.[^2] The item does not say that was this cabin, though the register's 1987 date fits.[^1] The register says the cabin was destroyed in a backfiring effort that year and was never rebuilt.[^1]

Today the steel tower remains, according to the register.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list also marks the tower Standing*. Its key defines that asterisk as a tower whose structure and support remain while the cab is no longer in place.[^3] We found no public access information for the site, so ask the Lassen National Forest before going near the tower.
