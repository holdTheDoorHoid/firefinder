The register calls this lookout Scott (Bald Peter).[^1] It stood on Scott Mountain in Linn County, Oregon, about six miles west of Sweet Home.[^1][^2][^3]

The Linn County Fire Patrol built a steel lookout on Scott Mountain in 1948, in Section 31, Township 13 South, Range 1 West.[^3] The steel tower came from a war surplus sale agency at McGown Field in Boise, Idaho.[^3] Linn association crews took apart four such towers and readied them for shipment to Oregon.[^3]

The register describes a 55-foot steel tower with an L-4 cab.[^1] The Linn District's 1948 annual report describes a 54-foot tower with a 14 by 14 foot steel cabin, and another 1948 note gives 60 feet and a wood cab.[^3] The sources differ on the height, and the two 1948 records differ on whether the cab was steel or wood.[^1][^3]

The lookout was manned about August 1, 1948.[^3] The Lebanon Express reported in July 1948 that Eugene Jacobson, who had worked for the patrol the year before, would be assigned to it.[^3] The firelookout.com page lists Joan Oswalt as known lookout personnel for 1958.[^2]

Winds from the Columbus Day storm damaged the lookout on October 12, 1962.[^3] The patrol repaired the storm damage and built a storage building in 1963.[^3] In April 1966 the patrol bought 13,000 feet of underground cable to carry power to Scott Mountain.[^3]

The register says the lookout was removed in 1979.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year, and the Forest Lookouts history says it was dismantled.[^2][^3]

The Bald Peter name also belongs to a different Linn County site.[^5] That site dates from 1912 and sits at Township 12 South, Range 2 East, section 28, at 4,079 feet.[^5] The Scott Mountain tower in this story has an elevation of 2,136 feet.[^1] A separate Scott Mountain lookout in Lane County, built in 1929 and removed in 1960, is also a different site.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the site can be visited.
