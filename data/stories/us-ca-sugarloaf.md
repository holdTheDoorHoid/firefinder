Sugarloaf Lookout stands on Sugar Loaf Mountain in Shasta County, California, on land the U.S. Forest Service manages as part of the Shasta-Trinity National Forest.[^1][^3] The register gives its elevation as 3,944 feet (1,202 meters) and describes a California Region 5 Plan 4AR cabin perched on a concrete tower eight feet high.[^1]

The register dates the cabin to 1931.[^1] In November 1932 a forest road crew was moved to Doney Creek, where a new road was to be built from the state highway up to the lookout.[^3]

Staffing shows up in clippings from the Dunsmuir News, copied on the Kemnow site. Clyde Norman took a lookout post in 1935.[^3] By 1941 the paper described lookout Harold D. Lenros and his wife living at the station with their small son.[^3] In July 1944 Mildred Jackson of San Francisco was on duty, with her daughter, a cat and a dog for company.[^3] That September the paper said she was the first to report a one-acre fire north of Lamoine, and that a Pollard crew stopped it at that size.[^3]

The Forest Service closed the lookout in November 1954. It had been one of four Shasta-Trinity lookouts reopened the month before because of warm weather, and the paper called Sugar Loaf the last of the four.[^3] The sources I read do not say whether anyone staffed it again.[^3]

Today the FFLA California list shows the lookout as standing, and the register still lists it.[^2][^1] The register's photo list includes a 2019 picture.[^1] I did not find a current access or rental notice in the sources I checked, so anyone planning a visit should contact the Forest Service first.
