Saddle Mountain sits in Pend Oreille County, about four miles northwest of Newport. The Washington Department of Natural Resources administers the site.[^1][^2]

Radio came first. A short-wave radio went in at the lookout site in June 1944, but the Willhite page does not identify the structure that stood there then.[^3] A 1944 newspaper item, quoted on a Washington lookouts site, lists the mountain among the stations linked by the new radio sets.[^4] Charles Heppe, a veteran firefighter, was the lookout that year.[^3]

A 30-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab went up in 1945.[^1][^2][^3] A Spokane newspaper reported that August that the new lookout was complete, with Heppe in charge.[^4]

Staffing went on into the 1960s.[^2][^3] FireLookout.com lists Laura Belle Johnson for 1957 to 1962, and the Willhite page names Sharon Borgford as the lookout in 1965.[^2][^3]

In 1962 the Department replaced the first tower with a 40-foot wooden cab.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Its own carpentry crew built it for about $8,000.[^3]

The tower was destroyed in 1986.[^2][^3] The Weebly page says it was toppled that year.[^4] The register's photo caption shows the final moments of the lookout.[^1]

Captions on the Willhite photos mention footings and a benchmark, but none of those captions gives a date.[^3]

The state agency holds the summit itself, but the old access road is on private land.[^3] The Willhite page gives a hike of 1.3 miles with 800 feet of climbing.[^3] Visitors should check with the landowner first.
