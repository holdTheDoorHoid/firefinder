Graves Mountain, in Ferry County, Washington, had a lookout built in 1934: a 72-foot pole tower with an accompanying log cabin for living quarters.[^3][^1] The register says the Forest Service and the Civilian Conservation Corps built it.[^1] The historical society places the station in the Colville National Forest and dates its construction to the early 1930s.[^2]

Sources disagree on the height. The register and a former-lookout site record give 72 feet, the historical society says 75, and another history page says 72, possibly 80.[^1][^3][^2][^4]

A couple manned the lookout in 1944.[^4] In 1955 the Forest Service replaced the pole tower with a 30-foot timber tower and an L-4 cab.[^1][^3][^4] The historical society gives the new cab's size as 14 by 14 feet.[^2] A published list of lookout personnel starts in 1955 but does not say when staffing ended.[^5] The register says the L-4 cab stood for 30 years, while the historical society says the station worked for almost 50 years.[^1][^2] The pages we read name no notable fires.

In 1985 the Stevens County Historical Society moved the lookout, piece by piece, off the mountain.[^1] The register says it went to the Stevens County Park and Museum. Other pages place it next to the Keller Historical Museum, on a small hill above Colville.[^1][^3][^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on Graves Mountain today. The tower itself was removed in 1985.[^3]

The register calls the restored cab open to the public daily.[^1] A lookout history page says it is open for tours during the summer,[^5] and a regional events listing says the museum opens May 1 to September 30.[^6] The accounts differ, and none says whether visitors may climb. If that season holds, the museum is closed now, so call ahead.[^6]
