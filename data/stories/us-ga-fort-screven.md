The Georgia list of lookout towers has a Fort Screven entry in Chatham County, numbered GA317, and its note includes the year 1808.[^1] Sherpa Guides describes a lookout tower and fort of the Martello type, built between 1812 and 1815 by Isaiah Davenport of Savannah.[^2] The same account gives 1808 as the year the federal government acquired jurisdiction over the area, which is a separate event.[^2] No source names the list's entry as this tower, so the match rests on the name and the place.[^1][^2] If they are the same tower, the list and Sherpa Guides disagree on the date, and this story follows the more detailed Sherpa account.[^1][^2] The fort area lies on the north end of Tybee Island.[^3]

For comparison, Sherpa Guides says the British erected more than 100 Martello towers along the south and east coasts of England in the years 1805 to 1812.[^2]

The tower had several later uses. At various points it hosted the headquarters of the Georgia Telephone and Telegraph Company and also operated as a post office.[^2] In the antebellum plantation era it was a popular site for duels.[^2] During the Civil War it also housed Union troops who occupied Tybee Island.[^2]

The tower burned in 1913 while operating as the Fort Screven post office.[^2] In 1914 it was demolished with explosives to clear a line of fire for the fort's guns.[^2]

Sources give different start dates for the fort itself. One says the government approved it in 1855,[^3] while a Tybee Island blog says the U.S. established it in the late 1890s.[^4] The blog also reports that the fort closed in 1947 and that its property was sold to the City of Tybee.[^4]

Sherpa Guides reports no visible trace of the round structure at present, aside from whatever archaeologists may yet turn up.[^2] Parts of the old fort have since been redeveloped: some of the fort walls are now private residences, and Battery Garland was converted into the Tybee Island Museum.[^4] No source describes the tower site as it is now, or says whether the public can visit it.
