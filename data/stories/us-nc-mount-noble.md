The Mount Noble lookout stands on the Qualla Boundary, the land of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians, in Swain County, North Carolina.[^1][^2][^3] The hike starts at the Fire Mountain Trail System, close to downtown Cherokee.[^3]

The Bureau of Indian Affairs built the tower in 1957 to replace a wooden tower from the 1930s.[^1][^2] It is a 60-foot steel Aermotor with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1][^3]

The tower was used for fire detection until the 1960s.[^2] It now shares the summit with a series of FCC and radio towers, and a trail guide mentions several communication towers there.[^2][^3]

The Register listed the tower on April 2, 2008. It said the lookout had sat unused for years, and that the BIA and the North Carolina division of the Forest Fire Lookout Association were planning a restoration.[^1] We found no record that this plan was carried out.

Sources disagree about access. A 2018 blog found a chain-link fence barring entry, and said the cab looked about to fall down.[^2] An undated trail guide says the tower "remains open to the public," but notes that half of the cab floorboards are missing.[^3] The same guide also carries a May 2018 update saying BIA Forestry rebuilt the chain-link fence around the base of the tower and closed it until further notice.[^3]

The guides also differ on the distance. The 2018 blog gives 2.4 miles, and the trail guide gives 2.6 miles, with about 1,600 feet of climbing either way.[^2][^3] Given the fence and the missing floor, we would not climb past a barrier or go into the cab. Ask locally before you go.
