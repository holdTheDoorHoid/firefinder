The Chewelah Peak lookout began in 1938 as a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, on a peak six miles east of Chewelah.[^1][^2][^3] The pole tower gave way in 1963 to a 41-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 cab.[^1][^3]

The Forest Service kept the lookout staffed into the 1970s. Washington's Department of Natural Resources manned it for a couple of seasons in the early 1980s, and the post was then abandoned.[^1][^3]

In 1985 Frank Scranton acquired the tower, which was moved to his farm beside Highway 395, north of Chewelah.[^1][^2] A relocated-lookouts list places it on private property in Chewelah.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Scranton tower as standing, and marks the original summit site as gone.[^5]

The register says the tower still stands on the farm.[^1] Since it is private property, the sources describe no public access.[^4]
