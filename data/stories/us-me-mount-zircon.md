Mount Zircon is a granite summit outside Rumford, rising to 2,240 feet, in Oxford County, Maine.[^1][^2] Records differ a little on location: an August 1920 newspaper item put the planned station on the Milton Plantation–Peru boundary, while a 1924 photo record places the camp in Peru.[^2][^3]

In August 1920 the department had decided to put up a steel lookout station on the summit.[^2] The size was still open, with 24, 48 or 60 feet under consideration.[^2] A May 1935 newspaper item gives the tower as 60 feet tall.[^2] A trail guide dates the summit's 60-foot steel tower to 1922 and places the watchman's camp lower down, by the gravel road.[^4]

A Forest Service watchman's camp was on the mountain by 1924.[^3] A 1943-44 state report says the lookout cabin was rebuilt.[^2]

The tower's end is dated two ways. The Press Herald says the tower caught fire in 1975, was cut down the next year, and was not removed afterward.[^1] Maine Trail Finder dates both the camp's burning and the tower's felling to 1975.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register notes only that the tower no longer stands.[^5]

The fallen steel still rests on the summit, tipped onto its side, and a cairn marks the spot where the tower stood.[^1] The granite summit still offers views in almost every direction.[^1] The hike is a 2.5-mile out-and-back that starts at a metal gate.[^1] Past the point where the trail leaves the gravel road, it turns markedly steeper and gains almost 900 feet in half a mile.[^1] Today the Rumford Water District holds the spring and much of the land around it.[^4]
