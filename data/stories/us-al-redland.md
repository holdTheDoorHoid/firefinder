The Redland Fire Lookout Tower stands in Elmore County, Alabama, and the Elmore County Commission is listed as its administrator.[^1] The Register describes it as a 100-foot Aermotor tower.[^1]

The build year is unsettled.[^1] The Register's Built field gives 1949, but its description says the Alabama Forestry Commission constructed the tower in 1948.[^1] The Register does not explain the gap, so this story reports both years. Thomas Kaufmann, who nominated Redland to the Register, has also written that the Aermotor MC-39 was the most common steel lookout model in Alabama's forests.[^1][^2] The sources do not say whether Redland is an MC-39.[^1][^2]

Nothing I read says who staffed the tower or what fires it watched.[^1][^2] Kaufmann says that after a lookout was no longer manned, the tower stayed up and was later fitted with antennas for communications.[^2] That fits the tower's current use, though the Register does not say when its radio use began.[^1]

The Register says the tower currently serves as a radio relay site.[^1] It lists the Elmore County Commission as administrator and names the Alabama Forestry Commission only as the builder. The sources do not say when or how the tower changed hands.[^1]

The Register is the only tower-specific source I found, and it does not say whether the public can climb the tower.[^1] Kaufmann warns that many of the state's surviving towers are dangerous and illegal to climb, so please do not climb this one.[^2] Check with the Elmore County Commission before planning any visit.[^1]
