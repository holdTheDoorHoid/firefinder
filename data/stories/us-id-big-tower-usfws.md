The Big Tower stands about three miles southwest of Nampa, Idaho, in the Deer Flat National Wildlife Refuge.[^1][^2] It is a 100-foot steel Aermotor tower at about 2,605 feet above sea level.[^1][^3]

The Idaho Press reports that the tower was first built in 1937 to serve as a fire lookout and a perch for refuge staff along the Lake Lowell shoreline.[^2] The paper also says it was once used to count waterfowl and wildlife.[^2] The Roaming Civic could not find when it was built and found no confirmed record that it was used to detect wildfires.[^4] The sources differ on whether the tower ever served as a fire lookout, and the 1937 date rests on the Idaho Press alone.[^2][^4]

We found no documented staffing history for the tower, and no confirmed record that it was used to detect wildfires.[^3][^4]

The refuge removed the bottom set of stairs to discourage climbers, and a barbed wire fence has always surrounded the perimeter.[^2] Refuge officials plan to stop fixing that fence and take the tower down, and the refuge manager is concerned for public safety.[^2] The Idaho Press article we read carries no publication date, so the timing of these plans is unconfirmed.[^2] An archaeological review by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service is in progress to work out how the tower would be taken apart. A review board may decide to salvage the tower or move it, and a removal timeline is due once that review ends.[^2]

The tower was listed as standing when the Roaming Civic visited on July 14, 2024, and the Idaho lookout list still marks it as standing.[^4][^5]

The area behind the refuge headquarters is closed to visitors, and the tower cannot be climbed.[^4] A 2024 visitor watched it from a roadside pullout by the headquarters gate.[^4] Officials say people still climb it without permission, so please keep off it.[^2]
