Cabin Lake Ranger Station is a Forest Service compound in the Deschutes National Forest, forty miles southeast of Bend.[^2] It began as a district ranger station and was the headquarters of the Fort Rock Ranger District from 1921 until 1945, with five full-time rangers living and working there for most of that time.[^2] The National Register nomination dates the bunkhouse and generator house to 1923.[^3]

A Civilian Conservation Corps camp stood beside the station from 1934 to 1938.[^2] Wikipedia credits CCC crews with building the existing guard station buildings during those years, although the nomination dates two of them to 1923, before the camp.[^2][^3] The nomination dates the cookhouse to 1934.[^3]

In 1945 the headquarters moved to Bend and the station became a guard station.[^2] The station then served as a bunkhouse and supply base for Forest Service fire crews working the forest each summer.[^2]

The Oregon Forest Lookouts list calls the structure a water tank tower and says it served in emergencies as a lookout.[^1] The Wikipedia history describes a ranger station and then a guard station, so treat the tower description as unconfirmed.[^2]

The guard station was shut down by the Forest Service in 2002.[^2] The National Register of Historic Places added the station in 1986, as reference number 86000827.[^2]

Nobody occupies the site today.[^2] The guard station is shut, but the campground next door is open.[^2] Fourteen primitive sites make up the campground, with no water or toilets on site.[^2] Two permanent bird blinds sit a short walk from the guard station.[^2] After restoration, two of the ranger residences could be offered as rental cabins.[^2] The dirt access road can be rough, but a two-wheel-drive vehicle usually manages it from April to November.[^2]
