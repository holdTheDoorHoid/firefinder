The U.S. Forest Service put up the Olustee Fire Tower in 1931, according to the register, which says this was before the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1][^2] It is a 100-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and it stands where Florida's biggest Civil War battle was fought.[^1]

A Florida Times-Union item from September 1931, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site, tells of a watchman who left the trapdoor open when he went down. When he returned, a buzzard had settled on the back of his chair, apparently watching for fire.[^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps appears in the tower's later story too. A historical marker at the site calls the tower "one of many fine CCC projects completed in Florida."[^3] That marker dates the corps to 1933 to 1942, which is after the register's 1931 date.[^3][^1] The sources disagree. The marker may mean later work at the site, but none of the sources we read describes what that work was. A photo on the marker's listing shows CCC Company 418 at Camp P-67 at Olustee in 1937.[^3]

Today the register names Osceola National Forest as the administrator.[^1] The state park that marks the battlefield sits in the same forest.[^4] A 2.3-mile hike through the state park passes the tower, according to a trail listing, and that listing says climbing the tower lets visitors picture how the Union and Confederate troops were positioned in the battle.[^5] We found no official word on whether climbing is allowed now, so check with the forest before you go up.
