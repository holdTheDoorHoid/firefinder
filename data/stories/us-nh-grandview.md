Grandview is a trailless peak in Woodstock, Grafton County, New Hampshire, on the west side of Interstate 93.[^1][^3]

Before the fire tower, the summit held an observation tower.[^3] The North Woodstock Improvement Association built it in 1921.[^3] The Forest Service then built a 40-foot wooden fire tower there in 1939.[^3] An August 1939 Portsmouth Herald list included Grandview among towers planned or being built.[^1]

In October 1940 the Groton Times reported that forest guard Roland Cleveland would fill in as the lookout on the Grandview tower for a time.[^1]

According to a 2012 blog post, the tower operated until 1948 and was torn down about 1960.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page lists it as removed,[^1] and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks it as gone.[^2]

The same post recounts a snowshoe trip up the north ridge a few years earlier, which found the old tower's supports still at the summit.[^3] A hiking list of New Hampshire towers includes a Grandview (East Peak) in Woodstock and says it no longer stands, with artifacts.[^4] The list does not say whether that is the same summit as the one described here.

The 2012 post describes the top as densely wooded, with no views.[^3] No source we read describes the site since then, and none says whether the summit is open to visitors.
