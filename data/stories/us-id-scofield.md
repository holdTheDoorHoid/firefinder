The Clearwater Timber Protective Association was the agency behind the Scofield lookout, on a peak listed at 4,265 feet in Clearwater National Forest.[^1][^2] The site's first structures were a tree platform with a shake cabin, from 1923, and a 108-foot pole tower with an enclosed platform, from 1931.[^2] In 1934 the association built a 100-foot steel tower.[^1] Its own later report said the tower had seen little use over the previous decade, as airplane use spread and logging changed the fuel types, and that keeping it at the original spot was judged inadvisable.[^1]

The removal dates disagree. The Forest Lookouts site quotes the association's 1955-56 report and says the tower was dismantled in August 1955 and moved to two other sites.[^1] The 2022 Idaho Fire Lookouts page dates the dismantling to 1957, and says half the tower went to Shanghai lookout and half to Bertha Hill lookout.[^2] Firelookout.com carries the headline "R.I.P. 1923-1931," which does not fit the 1934 steel tower.[^3]

A 2022 visit report says the site has since been logged.[^2] The visit found only cement footings: four from the 100-foot tower and one at the base of its stairs. The writer also found telephone wire.[^2] The page labels the site a hike-in, and I found no source that describes access rules for it now.[^2]
