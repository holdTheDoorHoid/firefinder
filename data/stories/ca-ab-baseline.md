The Baseline Lookout stood on Baseline Mountain in the foothills of the Rocky Mountains in Alberta.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register places it in Division No. 9 at 1,841 m (6,041 ft).[^1]

The federal Canadian Forest Service built the groundhouse.[^1] The register says it was one of at least three the service surveyed in the late 1920s.[^1] The groundhouse was wooden, measured 3 by 3 m (10 by 10 ft), and had a hip roof on a concrete foundation.[^1] Provincial staff ran it until the late 1940s.[^1][^3]

The register says a new 24 m (80 ft) tower and cabin, about 0.8 km (half a mile) west, replaced the groundhouse, and that the replacement is still an active Alberta Land & Forest Service lookout.[^1] A Travel Alberta listing says the fire tower site was upgraded in the 1990s, when the tower moved to a new location on the adjacent summit, which has an access road.[^4] That date does not match the register's late-1940s changeover, and the sources we read do not explain the gap.

The old groundhouse is no longer in use.[^2] At the top, a trail guide describes a small shelter. Getting there involves a dirt access road and poorly signed trails, and the guide rates the out-and-back hike hard.[^2]

Travel Alberta says hikers, 4x4 vehicles and off-highway vehicles reach the summit by the main access road.[^4] The active tower is staffed in season, usually from May to October.[^3] No source we read says the public may climb the active tower, so this story covers only the old groundhouse.
