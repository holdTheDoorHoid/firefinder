Eagle Square's fire tower is a pretend one.[^1] It is a green cupola on the roof of a stone warehouse in Concord, made to pass for a lookout.[^1][^2]

The building went up in 1870 as a warehouse for the hardware merchant David Warde, whose store was on Main Street.[^1] A century later it had passed through several uses, among them a stint as a restaurant.[^1] By the early 1990s it stood vacant, and the New Hampshire Historical Society purchased it, planning a museum on the upper two floors and collections and administrative work below.[^1]

The cupola went on the roof during that conversion, a nod to the state's history of logging and forestry.[^1] It made the building look like a fire tower.[^1] Inside were a radio and a sighting map of the kind used to read the direction of smoke.[^1] A two-story mural of nature lined the stairway up to it.[^1]

The museum ran there for about two decades.[^1] The society later judged that running two public buildings was not advisable.[^1] The museum then closed, and the top two floors were put up for private commercial use.[^1] Granite Investment Advisors leased the third and fourth floors and opened offices in November 2015.[^1]

In a 2016 Concord Monitor article, the cupola sat atop that office.[^1] The firm's principal said he sometimes climbs the fire tower to take phone calls.[^1] Some visitors still arrived assuming the building was a museum.[^1] The tenant lets some of them up, depending on how busy the office is.[^1] Because the cupola sits atop a commercial office, access depends on the tenant.[^1] The Forest Lookouts list classes it as a replica, still standing on a roof.[^2]
