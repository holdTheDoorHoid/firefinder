On the Shoshone National Forest, a 72-foot steel tower with a 7x7 observation cabin went up on Warm Spring Mountain in 1927.[^1] A February 1928 newspaper report, quoted on a lookout-history site, lists a new 72-foot tower among the forest's construction of the previous year. It places the tower ten miles west of Dubois and says a new five-mile line tied it to the Sheridan Creek telephone line.[^2] A second lookout listing puts the site seven miles west-northwest of Dubois, so the two sources disagree on distance.[^3]

In September 1958 a district ranger wrote to the forest supervisor that the lookout was leaving that week, and that a fire control aid would be sent to the tower as needed.[^2] The supervisor replied that the plans to man the lookout were acceptable.[^2]

The register gives no removal date, and says nothing remains at the site.[^1] The second listing marks the tower simply as gone.[^3] The register also suggests a residence ground cabin probably stood nearby.[^1]

No source describes the site today beyond that note. The register added the site on May 2, 2022.[^1]
