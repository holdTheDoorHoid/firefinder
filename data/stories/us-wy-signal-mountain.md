Before the Forest Service built a permanent station, the first lookout on Signal Mountain was a tent house, and Willhite Web has a photo of Ben Sheffield sitting beside it.[^3] The Teton Forest then built a rustic lookout cabin there in 1938.[^2][^3] A July 16, 1938 item called it the forest's primary lookout, with a view over much of the forest, especially the area where the fire hazard was greatest.[^2] Willhite Web describes stone walls, sliding windows on every side, and a wood-shingled roof in pyramid form.[^3]

Ben Sheffield, a Jackson Hole resident for over 50 years, occupied the cabin in 1938, and the same item said over a thousand people had registered at the lookout in 20 days.[^2] Firelookout.com dates Sheffield to 1938 and 1939 and lists Don Martinson in 1949.[^1] Willhite Web places Sheffield's lookout work in the late thirties and early forties, so the two accounts give different spans for his service.[^1][^3]

A January 25, 1939 item reported that about 7,000 people had signed the register over the previous year.[^2]

An August 9, 1951 Jackson's Hole Courier item reported that lightning hit the ranger lookout station on Signal Mountain on a Sunday night and burned out the telephone lightning arresters.[^2] Willhite Web says lookouts Marie and Bob Baugh were badly frightened but unhurt.[^2][^3]

Firelookout.com lists Signal Mountain as gone, and Forest Lookouts marks it Removed.[^1][^2]

The site is in Grand Teton National Park, 3 miles west of Moran Junction.[^1] Willhite Web lists the hiking distance as "Drive up," but no source describes what remains there today or current public access.[^3]
