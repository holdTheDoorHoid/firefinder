The Whitechuck Mountain lookout was only a cab, built in the 1930s on a peak about nine miles east-southeast of Darrington in Snohomish County, Washington.[^1][^2] The peak's summit is 6,989 feet (2,130 meters) high.[^1][^4]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the land as Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, administered by the U.S. Forest Service, and says the cab was gone by the 1950s.[^1] Forest Lookouts lists the site as removed.[^3] Willhite cites a lookout inventory that records a cab there in the 1930s, and says a visiting USGS party in 1950 found nothing.[^4]

Willhite's page is a climbing guide to the peak, and he says he has found nothing confirming that a lookout stood on the summit.[^4] He also warns against climbing when the slabs are wet.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, so do not expect to find a lookout structure. We found no source that says whether the site is open to visitors.
