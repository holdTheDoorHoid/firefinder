From much of the Grande Ronde Valley the mountain is in view, and its southern edge is a landmark for La Grande.[^5] It stands in Union County, Oregon.[^3] The Union-Wallowa Forest Fire Association put a lookout on the summit in 1913, and Lowell Williamson, son of a local timber operator, was selected as its lookout man.[^2] A state forestry report, under its 1914 entries, describes a five-mile telephone line to the lookout point, built to secure prompt reports of fires.[^2] A 1915 La Grande item, however, dates the line to 1913 and gives its length as about six miles.[^2]

The register dates the summit's cupola cabin to 1916.[^1] A 20-foot platform was added in 1922, and an 81-foot steel tower was built in 1926.[^1] A local paper had proposed a 40-foot tower back in 1916, which is a different height from the one that was built.[^2]

John Blumenstein was reporting from the lookout in 1914, and Frank "Abe" Perry was named lookout for 1916.[^2] In August 1915 the lookout spotted a fire, and Union-Wallowa County fire-fighters reached it.[^2] The record also names Bob Burgess in 1943, Avery Glenn in 1950 and 1951, Wayne Smith in 1954 and Lowell Park in 1958.[^3]

Emergency use of the steel tower continued into the 1960s.[^1] The register says it is now gone, and firelookout.com says it has been gone for a long time.[^1][^3]

Union County now manages the Mount Emily Recreation Area (MERA). The county established it in 2008 and describes it as multi-use recreation.[^4] No source I read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit.
