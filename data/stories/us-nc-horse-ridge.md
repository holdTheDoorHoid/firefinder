Horse Ridge was a fire lookout in Burke County, North Carolina, on the South Mountain protective area.[^1] A 35-foot (10.7 m) steel tower was erected there in 1928, about three miles south of Enola, and observers lived in a wooden cabin at its foot.[^1]

The tower had been completed within the month before a newspaper item of 3 December 1928.[^1] That item says Mr. Flory inspected it and found it adequate.[^1] It also says the tower was linked by phone to the Morgantown water shed and town, spelled that way in the paper.[^1]

In March 1938 a state report recorded a 10-by-10-foot tower cabin built at Horse Ridge by a crew from Civilian Conservation Corps camp P-57.[^1]

A list of North Carolina lookout towers places the tower in Burke County and gives it the code FA3452.[^2]

No source we could reach describes the site today. This story cannot say whether a tower or any footings remain there, or whether the site is open to visitors.
