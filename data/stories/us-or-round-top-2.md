Round Top Lookout stands in Jackson County, Oregon, and the Oregon Department of Forestry administers it.[^1] A December 1949 item from the state forest patrol said a new tower on Round Top would replace the lookout at Railroad Gap.[^3] The Register says the department first put a lookout in service there in 1950, an L-4 cab on a 20-foot pole tower.[^1] FireLookout.com starts earlier, with a 100-foot observation tower in 1934, so the sources disagree about the first structure.[^1][^2]

In 1973 the state entered an agreement with Gene Ash, for $1000, to build a new lookout building from state-furnished materials.[^3] The history page describes that tower as 24 feet tall with a 14x14 flat-roof cab.[^3] The Register and FireLookout.com both give the tower a height of 20 feet.[^1][^2]

The 1950s items describe summer posts, and a June 1959 report says a named lookout manned the tower that morning.[^3] The Register's 2016 visit report says that summer was the last year of active service and that cameras were installed by summer 2017.[^1] FireLookout.com still says the tower is staffed every summer, which conflicts with the Register.[^2]

A 1995 Register-Guard profile, quoted on a history page, says a fire spotter on the tower saw a column of smoke in the Sardine Creek drainage after a lightning storm the month before, and that she had called in several grass fires in the Rogue Valley below.[^3] Other entries record break-ins: a 1971 theft of a fire extinguisher and an LPG light, and a 1993 theft of a shotgun.[^3] The old cab had become unsafe, so a new one went up in 1973, and the catwalk was rebuilt in 1998.[^3]

The Register still calls the tower active in its description, but its 2016 visit report says active service ended that year.[^1] Cherylhill's directory lists the tower as unmanned and the site as legally accessible, while noting that this does not mean visitors can reach the lookout itself.[^4] The Register records that no Register sign is posted and that directions to the tower are not signed.[^1] Anyone planning a visit should ask the Oregon Department of Forestry first, because none of these sources says the cab is open to the public.[^1][^4]
