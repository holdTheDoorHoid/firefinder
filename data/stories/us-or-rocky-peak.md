Rocky Peak rises about ten miles southeast of Port Orford in Curry County, at roughly 3,040 feet.[^1][^3] A lookout station went up there in the summer of 1919, when a deputy state fire warden had it connected to town by telephone and stationed J. E. Atkinson there as lookout.[^2]

The register says the first cabin on the summit dates to 1933, and that a ten-foot wooden live-in tower replaced it in 1937.[^1][^3] The newspaper items do not agree with that date. They describe a station in 1919, and a July 1931 item reports lumber being sledded in to build a lookout on top of the peak.[^2]

Local papers name John Weld as lookout in 1925, Carlos White in 1929 and 1932, and Frank Paris in 1934.[^2] George Schmidt was due to take up the job in the summer of 1935, and George Steiner replaced him that August.[^2]

In the spring of 1938 lightning hit the older house, knocking out every window and breaking its walls, while the newer house beside it escaped damage.[^2] The cabin was renovated and put back into use in 1958.[^2] The register says the 1937 tower was destroyed in the 1962 Columbus Day hurricane.[^1]

The register lists the place under the heading "Former Fire Lookout Sites," and firelookout.com also records the tower as destroyed.[^1][^3] No source here describes what remains on the ground today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
