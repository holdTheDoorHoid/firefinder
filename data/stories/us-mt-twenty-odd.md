Twenty Odd is a peak about three miles northeast of Trout Creek, in Sanders County, Montana.[^1] The lookout sat at about 6,049 feet, on the Kootenai National Forest.[^1]

The start date is less settled than a one-line summary would suggest. In March 1920, local contractor Charles Wicksell was building a standard cabin for the Forest Service on the peak.[^2] The planned cabin was 12 by 12 feet, with a six-foot cupola and windows on every side.[^2] That September the Sanders County Independent-Ledger described the new cabin as built that summer.[^2] A Flathead Courier item from the same month says the design now used on Twenty Odd had been built a year earlier as an experiment.[^2] A 1917 item reports telephone line poles being set toward the lookout, which points to an earlier use of the site.[^2]

The same March 1920 item also gave the view from the peak as roughly 100 miles on a clear day.[^2]

A. E. Clark was the lookout in 1921.[^2] A September 8, 1921 Ledger item said he had recently left the peak and was due to start more active work at a planting camp.[^2] A June 25, 1930 Ledger item reported that Tom Churwell had taken over as lookout.[^2]

The cabin was destroyed in 1955.[^1][^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a ground site, with the lookout gone.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, is left on the peak today, or whether visitors may go there.
