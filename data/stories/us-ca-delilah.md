Delilah Lookout sits in the Hume Lake Ranger District of the Sequoia National Forest, about 45 miles east of Fresno in the southern Sierra, and it has been a fire detection site since before 1916.[^1][^2] In 1924 the Forest Service built a tower with living quarters underneath it on the ground, and that structure served until 1959.[^1][^2] Newspaper accounts from the era give a sense of life there: in 1930 a two-room house for lookout staff went up at the station, and in 1943 crews spent weeks rebuilding three miles of telephone line after it burned in a nearby fire.[^3]

What came next is where the record gets tangled. Sources agree a steel tower eventually replaced the 1924 structure, but disagree on when and where it came from: the National Historic Lookout Register says a 40-foot metal tower with a live-in cab was moved to the site in 1967 from "Lenore Naval Air Base," while another source describes a 70-foot tower from Lemoore Naval Air Station arriving in 1960, preceded by a Civilian Conservation Corps-built 50-foot tower in 1937, and a local history site instead describes a 65-foot Army-surplus tower, possibly as early as 1959, from a U.S. Army training camp.[^1][^2][^3] All agree the tower came from a decommissioned military installation; the exact year, height, and source remain unsettled.

Delilah survived at least one close call: in August 1961 the White Deer Fire burned past the lookout, destroying a nearby wooden storage building and forcing lookout staff to evacuate the tower twice.[^3]

The lookout was staffed every fire season, jointly funded by the Sequoia National Forest and the California Department of Forestry, until 1992.[^2] Since then it has gone up only during periods of extreme fire danger, as budgets allow.[^1][^2] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2001.[^2] The Buck Rock Foundation has run a volunteer lookout staffing program for the site, and when staffed, Delilah is open to the public.[^2]
