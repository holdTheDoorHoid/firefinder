The Prospect Hill lookout in Hingham was a 48-foot tower on leased land, according to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register.[^1] The Norwell register entry describes what appears to be the same tower: the first fire tower in that area, a 48-foot iron tower on Prospect Hill, built in 1916.[^2] The Hingham register entry says the U.S. Army asked in 1955 for the tower to be taken down on national security grounds, and that it was removed.[^1]

The Norwell entry does not mention the 1955 removal.[^2] It names the Navy as the party that asked for a replacement, and says the Bureau of Fire Control built the Norwell Fire Tower in 1956 to take the Prospect Hill tower's place.[^2] The Norwell tower stands 70 feet tall, is built of steel with a wooden cab, and is administered by the Mass. Bureau of Forest Fire Control.[^2]

The two records also disagree on the material: the Hingham record says steel, and the Norwell entry says iron.[^1][^2]

No source describes what remains of the Prospect Hill site today.

[^1]: Hingham Tower Site, Former Fire Lookout Sites, http://firetower.org/lookouts/us/ma/hingham-tower-site/ (accessed 2026-10-10).
[^2]: Norwell Fire Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/ma/norwell-fire-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-10).
