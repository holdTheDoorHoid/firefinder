Little Napa Point Lookout sits beneath Napa Point in Lake County, Montana, with Swan Lake to its northwest.[^1][^5] The register places it in the Swan State Forest and says the Montana Department of Natural Resources and Conservation administers it.[^1]

Its first permanent lookout was a school bus fitted with a cupola, in use from 1969 through 1976.[^1][^2] The present tower, a 20-foot wooden live-in building, went up in 1977 to replace the bus.[^1][^3] Its small square cab has windows on every side and is raised about 20 feet on wooden crossbeams.[^5]

The register says it is available for emergency use but is not staffed on a regular basis.[^1] A 2017 chapter report says the DNRC staffs it part time, so the sources disagree on staffing.[^4]

In 2016, Forest Fire Lookout Association volunteers were repairing the lookout and building a new staircase.[^5] A two-year project wrapped up in 2017 with a larger weighted trap door and new catwalk decking and handrails.[^4]

The chapter's 2025 year-end report lists Little Napa among its assessments. That year's goal was to judge whether the lookout could be stabilized for intermittent use.[^3]

Today the register and firelookout.com both describe it as available for emergencies. We found no public rental or visiting program, so treat it as a restricted state structure rather than a place to climb.[^1][^2]
