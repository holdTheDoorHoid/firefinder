The tower first stood three miles east of West Point.[^1] A March 1969 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, reports that it was relocated to a new location near Saluda, the county seat of Middlesex County.[^1][^3]

The move was needed because Route 33 was due to be widened, and the tower sat on land set aside for the road.[^1]

The new site was seven miles closer to Lancaster County, and the item predicts the move will improve surveillance of the woodland in every direction.[^1] When either tower spotted smoke, the watchman would contact the man in the other tower, so that both could take readings and pin down the fire's location.[^1] The other tower the item names is the Miskimon tower, and the forest warden could then drive directly to the fire.[^1]

In January 2003 the tower was sold at auction, on condition that the buyer take it down.[^1] The lookout list marks it as gone.[^2] The lookout page credits the Virginia Department of Forestry.[^1][^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. The sources also do not say whether the land is open to visitors.
