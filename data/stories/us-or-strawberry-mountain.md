The Strawberry Mountain lookout stood on Strawberry Mountain in Grant County, Oregon, on the Malheur National Forest.[^1] It sat about 11 miles southeast of John Day.[^2]

A phone line was run to the summit in 1913, and a fire finder followed in 1914.[^2] The first lookout house was built of sheet steel in September 1916 for $663.[^3] A 1940 account says the wind destroyed it the following spring.[^3] Some 1916 newspaper reports, however, call the structure a steel tower, so the sources do not agree on its form.[^3] A D-6 cupola cabin came in 1921, and an L-4 cab replaced it in 1941.[^1][^2][^3]

A 1916 newspaper report said a man was kept at the lookout during fire season, and that the station was connected by telephone to the ranger stations.[^3] A 1913 fire on upper Beech Creek was first reported from the lookout, and in 1916 the lookout spotted smoke from a brush fire near John Day.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page names lookouts from 1916 through 1939, and it says one of them received a special radio in 1939.[^3]

The Register says the cabin was removed in 1966. Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2]

A 1946 survey note says a federal survey station sat at the center of the Forest Service lookout house.[^3] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1][^2]
