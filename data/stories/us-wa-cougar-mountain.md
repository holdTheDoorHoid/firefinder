Cougar Mountain, in the Wenatchee National Forest about 10 miles northeast of Lake Wenatchee, began as a lookout camp in 1921.[^1][^2][^3] A log cabin with a platform on its roof went up in 1930.[^1][^3] The Wenatchee Daily World reported that year that a lookout cabin was planned for the mountain, southeast of Mad Lake.[^2]

In 1937 an L-4 cab replaced the log cabin.[^1][^2][^3] The Fire Lookout page names Arnold Hagen as a lookout in 1933.[^3] Panoramic photos were shot from the cab's roof in 1934.[^2]

A 1950 USGS description gave a 14-foot-square building set directly on the ground, its roof about 12 feet up.[^2]

WillhiteWeb and the Fire Lookout page say the L-4 cab was destroyed in 1969. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says it was removed that year.[^1][^2][^3]

A WillhiteWeb photo caption reads "Footing under the ground." The caption is undated, and no source describes what is at the site now.[^2]

The Forest Service says the Cougar Ridge Trail begins 10 miles behind a gated road. Hikers, mountain bikers, motorcyclists and riders with stock may use that road, but cars cannot reach the trailhead.[^4] WillhiteWeb suggests parking at Maverick Saddle and hiking the Mad River route, which it puts at roughly five miles one way.[^2] A Washington Trails Association notice dated July 30, 2026 says the Cougar Mountain (Entiat) trail is closed, with no reopening date, because of the Little Giant Fire.[^5] Check for current closures before you go.
