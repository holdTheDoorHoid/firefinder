The Simmons Ridge site in the St. Joe National Forest is listed at 6,762 feet.[^1] The Idaho Fire Lookouts page says the place was established as a camp in 1924, and that an L-4 tower went up there in 1933.[^1]

The tower was set atop a concrete base in 1953, and the lookout was last staffed in 1972.[^1] The page names two former staffers.[^1] Charlie Kevis is listed for the 1930s, citing a book by Bert Russell, and Tom Boehr is listed for 1967, citing the Kellogg Evening News.[^1]

Lightning burned the tower down in 1984.[^1]

The page lists the site as hike-in.[^1] It does not say what, if anything, of the tower or its concrete base remains, or whether the public can visit.[^1] No other source read describes the site now.[^1]

[^1]: Simmons Ridge, Idaho Fire Lookouts, https://www.idahofirelookouts.com/st-joe-clearwater-region/simmons-ridge/ (accessed 2026-10-10).
