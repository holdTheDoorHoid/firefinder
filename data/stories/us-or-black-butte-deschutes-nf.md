Black Butte sits in the Sisters Ranger District of the Deschutes National Forest, and people have watched for fires from its summit for more than a century.[^1][^2] Sources differ on whether the first lookout went up in 1910 or 1911.[^3][^2] A D-6 cupola cabin was built in 1922, though one site dates it to about 1923.[^1][^4][^3]

In 1934 a taller wooden tower was added for better visibility.[^1] Newspapers reported 83 feet when the tower opened in August 1934, though earlier announcements said 85 and 87 feet. The NHLR gives 82 feet.[^2][^1] Sources also disagree on the cab, which the NHLR gives as 7 by 7 feet and another site as 8 by 8 feet.[^1][^3]

The 1934 tower was condemned in 1990 after winter damage.[^4][^2] It came down under heavy snow, but the date is disputed. Two sites say December 2001, and the NHLR says the winter of 2002.[^3][^2][^1]

A 65-foot tower replaced it in 1995.[^4][^2] Newspaper coverage quoted on a lookout history site describes a 1994 contract for a 65-foot wood tower and cab, and a May 1995 lift in sections by a Sikorsky Skycrane.[^2] A 2016 blog gives the tower's height as 35 feet, which conflicts with the other sources.[^5]

A November 2016 newspaper item, quoted by the same history site, says lookouts staff the tower every summer and now sleep and cook in a yurt at its base.[^2] The log quarters cabin, built in 1979 or 1980, was condemned in 2014 and burned in November 2016.[^1][^2][^5]

Volunteers have been restoring the cupola in recent years.[^4] A 2019 Nugget story describes new flooring, shingles, siding and footings, with money raised by the Friends of the Metolius.[^4]

As of the 2019 Nugget story, the 65-foot tower was still a staffed lookout.[^4] A 2016 blog says a road reaches most of the way up, and the last two miles are on foot.[^5] We found no source saying visitors may enter or climb the staffed tower, so check with the Sisters Ranger District before you go.
