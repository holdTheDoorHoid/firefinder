In August 1923 the Sullivan County Board of Supervisors agreed to spend $1,500 on a 60-foot fire tower for Chapin Hill. The offer was conditional: the state had to keep a man on duty during the fire period and assume all expense.[^1] The tower was a 60-foot Aermotor LS40 steel tower.[^2][^4][^5] It was completed early in the spring of 1924, in time for the whole fire season that year.[^1] A state report later credited county-subscribed funds with the cost, so the sources do not agree on who paid.[^1]

The observer's living quarters got a new cabin in 1930.[^1]

In 1941 a fire destroyed the observer's cabin and burned the first three landings and stairs of the tower.[^3][^4] A 2020 chapter newsletter gives this in a trivia answer, and Wikipedia gives the same account.[^3][^4]

The tower stood through the 1970 season.[^2] Wikipedia says it closed at the end of the 1970 fire season, as aerial detection grew, and was removed later.[^4] A list of New York towers compiled by Bill Starr marks it as removed, with no date.[^5]

No source I read describes what is left on the hill today, whether any footings remain, who owns the land, or whether the public can reach it.
