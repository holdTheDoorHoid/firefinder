The Basin Lookout stood in Snohomish County, Washington, about 14 miles northeast of Monroe, above Spada Lake.[^1][^2][^3] The register lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as the administering agency.[^1]

In 1961 a temporary tower went up, with a small trailer serving as the observer's living quarters.[^3] The register and Firelookout.com both describe a 12-foot wooden DNR live-in tower.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says the tower was there to watch the logging and dam building for Spada Reservoir, since Culmback Dam went up that year and the timber in the valley was logged off the future reservoir site.[^3] It also notes that one observer described the tower as 8 feet tall, which may explain the difference from the 12-foot figure in the inventory.[^3]

WillhiteWeb names only one observer, Glory, whose surname is not known. It places her at the lookout from 1961 to 1963, and says she may have stayed a few years longer.[^3]

According to WillhiteWeb, an October 11, 1962 windstorm tore off the roof, then knocked what remained of the tower over the ridge, and also knocked down the older timber on the mountain.[^3] The register and the Forest Lookouts index both say the lookout was gone by 1966.[^1][^4] WillhiteWeb also says it was gone by 1966, but it dates the tower's fall to 1962.[^3]

WillhiteWeb says a level patch on the crest of the wooded knob marks where the tower stood, and that its debris spreads down the slope on the north side.[^3] A WTA trip report from May 2024 describes reaching the old Basin Lookout Site on a climb that started at the Culmback Dam parking area.[^6]

The route is a long walk. WillhiteWeb describes driving Sultan Basin Road to Olney Pass, where the county road is gated for winter, and then parking at Culmback Dam if the gate is open.[^3] From there it is about 2.5 miles and roughly 1,000 feet of climb, partly on old roads and a very steep dozer trail.[^3] Snohomish County PUD says its Spada Lake recreation sites stay open year-round for day use only, though the road can be shut to motor vehicles in bad weather.[^5] The route crosses DNR land, and we found no agency statement on the lookout site itself.
