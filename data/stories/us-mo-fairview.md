The Fairview Fire Tower stands in Pulaski County, in a clearing beside Highway 17 inside the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1] The US Forest Service administers it, and the register lists the Houston/Rolla/Cedar Creek Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1]

The record of its building is thin. The register gives 1937 as the reported build year for a steel tower, which it says could be an Aermotor MC-39 from 1934.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site describes a 100-foot steel structure and dates it to 1937.[^2] The register says sources on the tower are very scarce, and we found no record of who staffed it or of any fire it watched.[^1][^2]

The tower is now closed. A sign on site gives vandalism as the reason, the register says, and the grounds are watched and ringed with barbed-wire fencing.[^1] We found no date for the closure.[^1]

The register's description calls the cab fairly small and the tower sound, with most windows still in place and stairs that appear to have been recently replaced.[^1] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on August 13, 2026, on the nomination of Audrey LaBeaume.[^1] Because the tower is closed, please do not climb it.

[^1]: Fairview Fire Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/mo/fairview-fire-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-09).
[^2]: Fairview, Forest Lookouts, https://centraluslookouts.weebly.com/fairview1.html (accessed 2026-10-09).
