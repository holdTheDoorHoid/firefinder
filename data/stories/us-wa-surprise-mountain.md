The U.S. Forest Service put an L-4 cab on the summit of Surprise Mountain in 1932, according to SummitPost, which ties the summit choice to its wide views of the Central Cascades.[^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives the same year and design.[^1] Willhite's Washington Fire Lookout page tells a different story. It says that when government photos were taken from the summit in 1934 for the Osborne Photo Survey, no structure existed. It guesses a cab went up soon after, around 1937.[^3] The sources do not agree on the build date.

The Mountaineers staffed the lookout for the 1943 summer season.[^3] An account of that season in the Mountaineer Annals, by club secretary Helen Rudy, says the club agreed to man the post from July 10 to August 31.[^3] Nine volunteers signed up for one- and two-week shifts.[^3] Every two hours, a short-wave radio report went to one of the other lookouts.[^3] That report was then relayed by telephone to headquarters, because Surprise had no direct connection with the Skykomish ranger station.[^3] Pack trains carried supplies the 7.25 miles from Scenic.[^3]

The register dates the cab's removal to the 1950s.[^1] SummitPost says the Forest Service decommissioned and destroyed it during that decade.[^4] The firelookout.com page also dates its destruction to the 1950s, and Willhite says it was likely destroyed then.[^2][^3]

According to SummitPost, the only leftover from the lookout is a tall metal pole on the summit.[^4] An unlabeled photo caption on Willhite's page says a piece of the lookout remained in 2014. That caption sits among captions for the Evergreen Mountain Lookout, so it may not refer to Surprise.[^3]

The summit sits in the Alpine Lakes Wilderness.[^4] A Mountaineers trip report calls it a former lookout site above Surprise and Glacier Lakes and describes a day hike of about 13.6 miles round trip, with about 4,400 feet of gain, from the Surprise Creek trailhead.[^5] Parking there is limited, and the trailhead sits just past a railroad crossing.[^5] We found no source saying the site is closed, and none describes a cab left to climb.
