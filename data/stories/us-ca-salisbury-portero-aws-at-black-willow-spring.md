The cabin began as an Aircraft Warning Service post on the ridge above Salisbury Potrero, in the Los Padres National Forest.[^1][^2] The register dates it to about 1943.[^1] The Aircraft Warning Service was a civilian arm tied to the Army's Ground Observer Corps during the Second World War, and it was shut down on May 29, 1944.[^3] The register lists the Army Ground Observer Corps as a cooperator on the site.[^1] It gives the original location as 5,188 feet.[^1]

Craig Carey traced the move in a 2011 post.[^2] He says the cabin many people call Jackson Cabin is this one.[^2] He matched it to the post above Salisbury Potrero partly through a 1950 forest visitor map, which shows a building above Salisbury Potrero and none at Black Willow Spring.[^2] A 1984 Los Padres history, which he cites, counted two such cabins still standing on the forest, one of them at Black Willow Spring.[^2] The owner's reply, as Carey reports it, described the building as an air craft warning station.[^2]

The register says the cabin was later moved to Black Willow Spring, which it calls nearby, and that it still stands there. It gives no date for the move.[^1] Its two sets of coordinates are several kilometres apart, so "nearby" is loose.[^1]

Carey describes it as a run-down wooden structure beside the Jackson Trail. He wrote that a ranching family owns the cabin and leases the land it sits on.[^2] Treat it as private property and check with the Los Padres National Forest before going.

A camp listing on the Hike Los Padres site, which includes a February 2022 survey, still describes a rustic cabin on Salisbury Potrero. That does not fit the relocation account, and the sources I read do not say whether another building stands there.[^4]

The register added the lookout in December 2019 as US 1395, CA 218.[^1]
