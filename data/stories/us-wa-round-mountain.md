Round Mountain is in Yakima County, Washington, in the Naches Ranger District, near the Goat Rocks Wilderness.[^3][^6] The site was listed under the Wenatchee National Forest, and the trailhead is now listed under the Okanogan-Wenatchee National Forest.[^3][^6]

Most sources date the tower to 1935: a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^1][^2][^4] Panorama photos were taken in July 1934, and WillhiteWeb points out that the 1934 panoramas already show cut trees and some wires.[^3][^4]

The register describes two different structures. Tie-downs near the south edge appear to have held a ground cabin, and concrete footings farther back appear to be from a tower. It is not known whether both stood at the same time.[^1]

FireLookout.com and WillhiteWeb date the loss of the final tower to 1976.[^2][^4] The register says it was removed in 1976 instead.[^1]

The summit is still a short hike.[^5] WillhiteWeb's photo captions show Round Mountain lookout posts and a brass benchmark.[^4] WTA reports that tree growth since the lookout era has somewhat restricted the views.[^5]

The easiest way to the top starts at the Indian Springs trailhead off Forest Road 530, and WTA gives the round trip as 5.2 miles.[^5] The Forest Service says Wilderness regulations apply, and the road to the trailhead is 7.2 miles of rough gravel.[^6]
