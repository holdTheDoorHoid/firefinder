The Mount Hull lookout stood near Oroville in Okanogan County, Washington.[^2] A road to the site was completed in late May 1934, and construction of a tower started there that month.[^2][^4] The register describes the tower as a 72-foot pole tower with an L-6 cab, built alongside a log cabin.[^1]

We found no source naming anyone who worked the lookout as a lookout.[^1][^2][^3][^4] In 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service used the site to watch for enemy aircraft coming in from Canada.[^2] The station was deactivated on July 1, 1943, and the site went back to Forest Service control.[^2][^4]

Sources also disagree on the national forest. The Weebly page names the Colville National Forest and quotes a 1944 report that refers to the Chelan National Forest, while firelookout.com names the Okanogan.[^4][^3]

The register says the tower was abandoned in the 1940s and removed in the 1950s, and that the log cabin was given to the Oroville Boy Scouts.[^1] Willhite says the cabin later burned in the 1960s, leaving only a heli-pad on the summit.[^2]

Photo captions on WillhiteWeb show two footings at the summit, and they describe very little view from there.[^2] Willhite says the site was visited in 2020. The route leaves the Chesaw Road, and Willhite expects the gravel to be well graded as far as Summit Lake.[^2] A high-clearance 4WD can reach the summit, although most people leave their vehicles at the lake.[^2]

Most of the mountain is National Forest land, and its southwest face has a large rock-climbing area.[^2] No tower stands there to climb.[^1]
