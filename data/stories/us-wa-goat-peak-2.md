The Goat Peak lookout covered here stood in Kittitas County, in the Wenatchee National Forest, about 12 miles west of Cle Elum.[^1][^2]

In 1927, $800 was set aside for a lookout house on Goat Peak.[^3] The register and a 2017 hike report both date the first cabin, a D-6 cupola cabin, to 1928.[^1][^4]

An item quoted on the Weebly page, printed in January 1928, describes an August 5 flight over the forest. The note dropped from the plane said no fires had been seen yet. About half an hour later the lookout reported a fire on Cabin Mountain, and both fires were no more than three miles from the lookout.[^3]

The Firelookout page lists Adam Ehl as a known lookout in 1933.[^2]

About 1960, a 10-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 flat cabin replaced the D-6 cabin, according to the register.[^1] The Firelookout page gives the year as 1960.[^2] The 2017 hike report says an L-4 cabin replaced the D-6 in the late 1930s, and that the R-6 went up in 1956.[^4]

In 1969 the cabin and tower were sold to a local resident and moved to the Teanaway Valley.[^1][^3] The hike report says the buyers took it apart first.[^4]

The moved lookout is still in the Teanaway Valley, according to that report. It can be seen from the Teanaway Road, on a hill behind the Teanaway general store.[^4] We found nothing that says who owns it today, so treat it as private property and do not go onto the land without permission.

The 2017 hike report describes FS Road 41 as a rough gravel road, and an unsigned trailhead with parking for three or four cars.[^4] The same report says trees surround the old lookout site, so the hikers went a short way down to a viewpoint for lunch.[^4] We did not find current access rules for the summit, and the 2017 report is the newest source we read.
