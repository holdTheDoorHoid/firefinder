The register places the lookout at the Oregon Department of Forestry's Central Point office.[^1] Firelookout.com puts it about a mile northeast of Central Point.[^2] The register gives its elevation as 1,275 feet.[^1]

The register gives only a decade for the tower, the 1930s.[^1] Forest Lookouts gives a closer date. Its 1938 records put the start of work at March 1, 1938, for a 50-foot tower of creosote-treated round poles with a 7-by-7 cab. The job took 505 man-days of labor and cost $1,430.74.[^3] A 1938 district annual report on the same page credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with a 50-foot log tower and an 8-by-8 cabin, so the page disagrees with itself on the cab size.[^3]

The sources also name different agencies. The register lists the Oregon Department of Forestry.[^1] Forest Lookouts places the tower at the Medford headquarters of the Southwest Oregon Forest Protective Association, where a 1938 Forest Log item says ten men had been assigned to a side camp and that the first of several projects was a fifty-foot lookout tower.[^3]

Forest Lookouts' last dated entries are upkeep records. In 1952 the cabin was painted and the tower creosoted, and in 1955 $8.70 went to maintenance of the Medford tower.[^3]

Forest Lookouts says the tower has been removed, and that a business office now occupies its location.[^3] The register and firelookout.com both list the site as gone, and so does the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list.[^1][^2][^4]

We found no source that describes the site beyond that, or says whether the public may visit it.
