Fort Rock is a ring of hardened volcanic ash in Lake County, Oregon, with cliffs rising nearly 260 feet (about 79 m).[^1][^2] The Oregon Encyclopedia calls it a tuff ring that encircles a vent from a brief eruption long ago. It says the name owes to the landform's shape, not to any military site or fur-trade post.[^2]

A lookout man was watching from the rock by September 1914.[^1] A newspaper item reprinted on Forest Lookouts says a ranger named Harriman had just finished a wire fence telephone line from the rock to his headquarters, so the lookout man could furnish daily reports without making the ride between the two points.[^1] In November 1919 a report described a lookout station the Forest Service had placed at one end of the horseshoe-shaped rim, with a wide view over country settled by homesteaders and stock ranchers.[^1] The page lists the rock under the Deschutes National Forest.[^1]

In July 1920 Fred Monte and his wife took over the Fort Rock Fire Lookout station, and in June 1921 a ranger named Roy Mitchell was keeping a temporary station there.[^1]

The Oregon Encyclopedia describes a mid-1920s photograph of a lookout structure on the south bluff, where a benchmark of similar age marked the spot until recently.[^2] A 1921 photo caption on the same page, however, places a small lookout structure on the west promontory instead.[^2]

In 1962 landowners Reuben and Norma Long gave the rock and its surroundings to the State of Oregon.[^2] The formation became a National Natural Landmark in 1976 and is now the centerpiece of Fort Rock State Natural Area, 349 acres in all.[^2]

A public parking area lies near the south and southeast sides, close to wave-cut benches whose promontories are the most popular hiking destinations.[^2] Public access to the falcon nesting areas is closed from February 15 to August 30, during brooding season.[^2] No source we found describes what remains of the lookout site today.
