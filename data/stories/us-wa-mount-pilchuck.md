Fire spotters were watching from Mount Pilchuck's summit before there was any building to shelter them, using an Osborne Fire Finder mounted on a rock pile and sleeping in a tent nearby.[^3] In 1918 the U.S. Forest Service ordered a prefabricated cupola-style lookout house from a Portland supply company; crews blasted rock from the summit and built a tramline to haul the roughly four tons of lumber and glass up the mountain's final stretch.[^2][^3] The cupola was assembled on top and the lookout was finished and staffed by 1921, with Everett Huff as the first lookout.[^1][^2]

A second, larger building went up around the original cupola in the early 1940s. Sources disagree on the exact year: the Washington State Parks history gives 1941 or 1942, the National Historic Lookout Register says 1943, and firelookout.com and a detailed trip-report site both say 1938.[^1][^2][^3][^4] All agree the result was the 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab that still stands on the summit today, built around the old cupola floor.[^2][^4] The lookout was staffed through the 1950s; the state parks account says fire detection use ended after 1960, while NHLR gives 1957 as the year staffing stopped.[^1][^3]

Washington's Department of Natural Resources transferred the site to the Washington State Parks and Recreation Commission, and by the late 1980s the long-unused lookout needed major repair. In 1988 and 1989, the Everett branch of The Mountaineers led a restoration, with about 105 volunteers logging roughly 10,000 hours of work, assisted by Snohomish County Search and Rescue and an Army Reserve helicopter.[^3][^4] The lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on October 7, 1993.[^1][^3]

Today the lookout is part of Mount Pilchuck State Park and sits within a large Natural Forest Area protected since 1990.[^3] It functions as a hiker shelter and interpretive site rather than an active fire tower, reached only by trail, and continued volunteer upkeep has kept it standing despite heavy foot traffic.[^1][^4] It is not available to rent.
