Dead Indian Mountain is in Lake County, Oregon, three miles west of Summer Lake.[^1][^2] The register places it in the Fremont National Forest.[^1] A Forest Service page now places the summit on the Silver Lake Ranger District, within the Fremont-Winema National Forest.[^4]

The register dates the lookout to 1934 and describes a 20-foot wooden tower of the L-5 type.[^1] Firelookout.com gives the same height and type.[^2] In late August 1934, a Lake County newspaper said that new radio equipment at the lookout station had helped crews get ahead of fires, and that a new forest road had helped bring firefighters in.[^3] Panorama photographs were taken there on October 28, 1935.[^3]

The sources disagree on the height.[^1][^2][^3] The register and firelookout.com say 20 feet.[^1][^2] A 1948 National Geodetic Survey station description, written by the Forest Service, gives the tower as 20 feet high with a 12-by-12-foot base.[^3] A district ranger's memo from August 1942, quoted on Kemnow's page, gives 16 feet and says guy wires held the tower down.[^3]

The register and firelookout.com say the tower was gone by the 1950s.[^1][^2] The 1948 station description still describes the tower in the present tense, which suggests it was standing then.[^3]

The summit now holds a communications site.[^4] A Forest Service decision signed November 26, 2018 reauthorized special use permits for the Dead Indian Mountain Communications Site, which sits atop the mountain.[^4] The project notice says no site-specific construction or ground-disturbing activities are proposed.[^4] We found no source that describes any remains of the lookout, or whether visitors may drive up to the summit. Check with the Fremont-Winema National Forest before you go.[^4]
