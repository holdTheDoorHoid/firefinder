The Maryland Forest Service built the Church Creek tower in 1932 in Dorchester County.[^1][^2] A report from November 1932 described a new 120-foot steel fire tower about two miles south of Church Creek.[^2] A later draft state report places the tower about 2.2 miles south of the town.[^3]

Because the site lay only four feet above sea level, its concrete abutments had to be sunk about two feet below sea level.[^2] The 1932 report suggests this may be the first forest-fire tower with foundations below sea level.[^2] The Register describes a 120-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab and a rooftop catwalk, though the same page also calls it the 100-foot Church Creek Forestry Tower.[^1]

The engineering firm behind its restoration says it was one of eight towers on Maryland's Eastern Shore, and that it stayed in service for more than 40 years.[^4] The sources we read do not name its observers or any fire it reported.

It was decommissioned around 1978, as detection technology advanced.[^4] It then sat idle for about 40 years, until it was dismantled in 2018 because its condition had deteriorated and become unsafe.[^4]

DNR kept the top 40 feet, including the cab, for restoration.[^4] A key agreement between DNR and the Maryland Historic Trust required that the tower be opened to the public and that the work follow the Secretary of the Interior's standards for the treatment of historical properties.[^4] The Register says that by 2024 the state had finished reducing the tower to 40 feet and renovating it as part of an educational display.[^1]

Today the tower stands near Golden Hill Road at the forestry field office.[^4] The site has a small park, explanatory panels and a new accessible parking area.[^4] The engineering firm puts the tower under 200 feet from the road and describes it as ADA accessible.[^4] The Register notes that it is not open for climbing.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as relocated, under coordinates that do not match the Register's location for the station.[^5][^1] The state's draft report places the tower on DNR land fronting Golden Hill Road and does not list the Church Creek tower among those moved to other sites.[^3] The engineering firm also places it at the field office.[^4] Read together, the sources suggest the tower was taken down and put back up at the same station, though none says so outright.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
