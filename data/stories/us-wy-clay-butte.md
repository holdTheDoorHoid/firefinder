Clay Butte has been used to spot fires since the early 1900s, but the tower people visit today is newer than that. The Civilian Conservation Corps started building it in 1941; when the CCC program was shut down, the work wasn't quite finished, and the lookout wasn't completed until 1943.[^1][^2] It's a three-story, wood-sided structure built from heavy 8-by-8-inch beams, standing just over 26 feet tall, with a flattop 14-by-14-foot cab and catwalk on top.[^2][^1] (One source describes it as a concrete tower, which doesn't match the wood-beam construction the National Register nomination describes — this story follows the Register.)[^3][^2]

The lookout sits in the Shoshone National Forest in the northern Absaroka Range, about sixteen miles east of Yellowstone National Park and just off the Beartooth Scenic Highway, reached by a short forest road that climbs to the site.[^2][^1] In 1962 the Forest Service added a visitor center building at its base, turning the spot into more of a public destination.[^1]

Clay Butte has stayed in service rather than falling idle. Since 1975 it's been staffed by volunteers through the summer season, and it draws a steady stream of visitors off the highway.[^1][^3] It was formally added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 8, 2014.[^2]

It's not set up as an overnight rental — it's a staffed, publicly accessible lookout and visitor center rather than a cabin you can book, open to drive-up visitors during the summer months when volunteers are on duty.[^1]
