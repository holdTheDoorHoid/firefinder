The Devilfish Lookout Tower stands in Cook County, Minnesota, inside the Grand Portage State Forest.[^1][^2]

Forest Lookouts dates the build to 1927, describing a steel structure 80 feet tall, and it places the tower in the Superior National Forest.[^3] A Minnesota Forest Service tower list dates it to 1926 or 1927 and identifies it as a Pittsburg-Des Moines ladder type, 80 feet high.[^4] That list names the Minnesota Forest Service as owner and says the service is now the Forestry Division of the state's Department of Natural Resources.[^4] The two accounts disagree about who ran the tower, and the sources we read do not settle it.[^3][^4]

The same list says the tower was moved to its present site in 1962 and that the original site is unknown.[^4] No source we read names a lookout who staffed the tower or a fire it watched.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing. Its asterisk marks towers whose structure remains while the cab is gone.[^5] The Cook County list says the cab is gone and the tower is now used for communications.[^4] A Flickr caption says it now relays radio traffic for ARMER, Minnesota's public safety radio system.[^1]

Today it is best treated as a working radio site. The sources describe no public climbing, so please do not climb it. A sign nearby invites hikers to explore the surrounding area, and a campground at Devilfish Lake sits in the same state forest.[^1]
