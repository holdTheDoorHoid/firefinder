In September 1935 a Florence newspaper reported that the county was getting another steel fire tower, to be built near Timmonsville.[^2] By March 1936 Mrs. D.L. Anderson of Timmonsville had donated ten acres about two miles from town to the state forestry commission, and equipment for a 100-foot steel tower had reached the site.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register dates the tower to 1936.[^1]

The Register describes an Aermotor LS-40 steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot steel cab.[^1] It was once 100 feet tall and was later reconstructed to 40 feet on its original concrete footings.[^1][^2] The Register does not say when that happened.[^1] The Forest Lookouts newspaper items show construction starting in March 1936, with a Civilian Conservation Corps camp finishing the site work.[^2] In April 1936 the paper expected that work to run until about July, so the towerman would not be named until later.[^2] Applicants had already come in for the job.[^2]

In the late 1990s the Forestry Commission sold the tower to Billy McIntosh, who moved it to Sumter County, reconditioned it and re-erected it on his property.[^1] Forest Lookouts gives only an undated note that the tower was sold and moved to private land, marked 199??.[^2] The Commission closed its fire tower system on October 1, 1993, but a Post and Courier piece dates the end of the system to 1994.[^3][^4]

Today the Register places the tower in Sumter County, at an elevation of 218 feet, or 66 meters. The Register lists its current use as hunting and wildlife observation.[^1] The Register added it on February 24, 2013, as US 970, SC 23, on the nomination of Michael T. Finch, Jr.[^1] Because it sits on private land and we found no public access information, please ask the owner before visiting.[^1]
