Reservoir Summit lies in Los Angeles County, with the old Ridge Route highway to its east and today's Interstate 5 and Pyramid Lake to its west.[^1] The road passes many historical landmarks, and Reservoir Summit is one of them.[^2]

Before the tower, a guard station there was built in 1932 and doubled as a lookout site.[^1] An account paraphrased on the register page describes a Forest Service station with eight crew members, the cook included, and a new fire truck.[^1] The lookout that followed was a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 design on a 10-foot open timber tower, built in 1935.[^1]

According to the same account, the crew left oily rags on a wooden floor they had treated with linseed oil, and the rags' spontaneous combustion burned the whole station, which was never rebuilt.[^1] The register says the 1935 tower replaced it.[^1] The register also says Reservoir Summit was abandoned by 1947 because of growing smog, and it gives no removal date.[^1] A lookout guide says most lookouts in the Angeles forest were removed for several reasons, including smog.[^4]

The register added the site on March 11, 2020, on the nomination of Brad Eells.[^1]

The register does not say what, if anything, remains today.[^1] A Ridge Route gallery describes the area above the road as a bare clearing where a campground once stood, along with a reservoir, but it does not mention the lookout.[^3] The Forest Service has closed much of the old road.[^2] We found no source that says whether the site itself is open to the public, so check with the Angeles National Forest before going.
