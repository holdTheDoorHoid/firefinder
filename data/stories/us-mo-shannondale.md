The state built a lookout tower on a high knoll about three miles north of Round Spring.[^1] Footings went in during October 1944, and a local paper said the spot gave unusual views of the Current River and nearby creeks.[^1] The tower was dedicated in January 1945.[^1] It was a wooden tower 60 feet tall, on a site the Pioneer Cooperage Company had given to the state.[^1]

Accounts differ on how much land the site covered. A 1944 state report counts 49 acres acquired, the dedication notice says 50 acres, and a 1945-46 state manual lists 40.[^1]

A towerman's residence was built at the site in fiscal 1950-51, and the state's reports mention another residence in 1962. The reports do not say whether these were separate buildings.[^1] Upkeep followed over the following decades: major repairs in 1956-57, new garage siding in fiscal 1969-70, a new entrance cable, porch and roof in fiscal 1971-72, and a room addition to the residence in fiscal 1977-78.[^1] In 1967 a 100-foot steel tower was erected. The reports do not say whether it replaced the wooden one.[^1]

A National Geodetic Survey station is set at the apex of the tower's observation cabin.[^2] Its station description, credited to a 1963 survey, calls it a steel tower on four legs with an observation cabin on top, about 100 feet tall.[^2] A 1978 recovery note places it on state forest property.[^2] That credit comes before the state's 1967 steel tower, so the survey and the state reports do not line up cleanly.[^1][^2]

The survey's directions end with a sharp left turn and a 0.35-mile run to the tower, which suggests a car can reach it.[^2] No source I could read describes the tower's condition now or says whether visitors may climb it, so this story does not say either.
