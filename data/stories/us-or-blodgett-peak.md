Blodgett Peak rises to about 1,052 feet in the Siuslaw National Forest, in Lincoln County, Oregon.[^1]

In January 1943 the Corvallis Gazette-Times reported that a lookout station was going up in the middle of the Blodgett area, and that planned roads and trails would aid fire control and shield the tract's plantations.[^3] In early February, papers said workers from Camp Albert Angell, a camp for conscientious objectors, were to build the tower. One report said it would be a log tower 20 feet tall.[^3]

By October 1943 the Corvallis Gazette-Times reported that a 14-by-14 house had been put up on the peak, atop a creosoted tower 40 feet tall, and that a good road now let visitors reach the lookout by car.[^3] The Register and firelookout.com both describe a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^1][^2] The February height plan appears to have been superseded.

In 1953 the lookout was moved to Cummins Lookout.[^1][^2] The Register dates a photo of the tower on Blodgett Peak to 1943 through 1953.[^1]

The sources use both "Cummins Lookout" and "Cummins Peak Lookout,"[^1][^2] and they do not say which Cummins site received the tower. A Register entry for a Cummins Peak site in Lane County says a 41-foot timber tower topped by an L-4 cab went up in 1954 and was removed in 1975.[^4] That entry does not say whether it is the same tower.

The Forest Lookouts page marks the Blodgett Peak lookout as removed.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, is left on the summit today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
