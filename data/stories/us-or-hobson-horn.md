Hobson Horn sits at about 4,710 feet in the Siskiyou National Forest, in Josephine County, Oregon.[^1] A 1943 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description, quoted by Forest Lookouts, places the Forest Service lookout about 10 miles southwest of Galice and 14 miles east of Agness.[^3]

Before the tower, the site had a rag camp, set up in 1935.[^1][^2] The tower that replaced it was a 20-foot treated timber L-4 tower, and the register gives 1940 as its build year.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page instead quotes a Siskiyou National Forest history, which says a 20-foot creosoted timber tower was erected in 1939 with E.R.A. labor and that a cabin was built on it in 1940.[^3] On when the tower went up, the register and the history disagree.[^1][^3] Clearing was done for visibility, and the same page records panorama photographs taken on August 25, 1936.[^3]

In 1941 the lookout was staffed for 100 days. Reports went through a station at Rand, and communications ran by forest line to Pacific Telephone and Telegraph.[^3] The tower was last used for emergencies in the 1970s.[^1][^2]

The register says the tower has since been removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the tower as gone.[^5]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower or cabin on the site today. A 2020 Siskiyou Mountain Club report on the Hobson Horn Trail, which drops about 18 miles from Hobson Horn to the Illinois River, describes one stretch as brush so dense it was close to impassable.[^4] On one trip the writer spent most of the hike off the trail, looking for gaps in the brush. He said reopening the route would take a great deal of effort, though the tread he could see was in better shape than he expected.[^4]

We found nothing on whether the site is open to visitors. Check with the Siskiyou National Forest before planning a trip, and do not assume the old trail can still be followed.
