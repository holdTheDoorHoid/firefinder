Bennetts Butte rises to about 2,185 feet in Coos County.[^1] Firelookout.com puts it about ten miles south-southwest of Myrtle Point, while a 1942 federal survey description puts it twelve miles southwest.[^1][^3] The Coos FPA administered the lookout.[^2] A log cupola cabin went up in 1927, according to the register and firelookout.com.[^2][^1] A June 1928 newspaper item reported that a new station was set up that year close to the headwaters of Catching Creek.[^3] A 1937 field report said the lookout house was nine or ten years old, and its foundation had badly decayed.[^3]

The register says a 30-foot wooden live-in tower replaced the cabin in 1942.[^2] The accounts differ on who built it. A 1942 state forestry report, quoted on the Oregon lookout history page, says the association put up a new lookout, paid for with what the report calls AWS and FFE funds.[^3] Another entry says the Air Warning Service staffed it that year.[^3] A 1943 Coos Bay Times item credits the Civilian Conservation Corps, working under the state forester, with completing it.[^3] The 1942 federal survey describes a white wooden structure, 14 by 14 feet and 35 feet high on four legs, which does not match the register's 30 feet.[^3][^2]

The register and firelookout.com both list the tower as gone.[^2][^1] A 2002 recovery note on the national geodetic survey data sheet says the tower was taken down and the summit now carries a communications installation plus two buildings.[^3]

The most recent description found dates from 2002.[^3] No source says whether the summit is open to visitors or whether a car can reach it.[^3]
