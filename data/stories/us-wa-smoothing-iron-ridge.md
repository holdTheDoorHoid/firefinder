The Smoothing Iron Ridge lookout was a cabin on a ridge in the Umatilla National Forest, in Asotin County, southeast Washington.[^1][^2][^3] One source calls it an L-5 cabin.[^2] Former Fire Lookout Sites says it was built in 1935, and it dates archive panoramas of the site to August 7 of that year.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page also gives 1935.[^4] Willhite Web disagrees: it says a Civilian Conservation Corps camp in the Asotin District built the emergency lookout house in 1933.[^3]

During the 1940s the cabin saw emergency use.[^1][^2]

In 1950 the structure was relocated from the ridge to a farm about 4.5 to 5 miles northeast.[^2][^3] Willhite Web says the move was possibly made by Steve Black.[^3]

A state wildlife agency and an elk foundation bought the farm in 2003.[^3] The Schlee family then moved the cabin to family property on another ranch in Whitman County.[^3] firelookout.com likewise places the cabin on a privately owned farm in Whitman County from 2003.[^2]

The two accounts of its fate disagree. Former Fire Lookout Sites says it is now gone.[^1] Willhite Web's 2021 account describes the cabin as in good shape at a secure spot in Whitman County.[^3]

At the original ridge, the foundation has been hard to find.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, is on the ridge today.

The cabin sits on private property.[^3] Willhite Web says Ralph and Peggy Owen visited it in 2021 with special permission.[^3] To reach the ridge, Willhite Web gives forest-road directions from Pomeroy, then a walk of 500 to 1000 feet that it calls easy.[^3]
