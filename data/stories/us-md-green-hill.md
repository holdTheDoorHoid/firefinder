The Green Hill Fire Tower was a Maryland forest fire tower in Somerset County, fronting Route 13 west of Mennonite Church Road.[^1][^2] The register says the Maryland Forest Service administered it.[^1]

A July 13, 1934 item quoted the district forester on four new towers for the Eastern Shore, one of them planned for Green Hill in Somerset County.[^3] The state's deed for the site is dated September 19, 1934.[^2] The register dates the tower to 1935 and describes a 120-foot steel structure built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] A February 15, 1935 newspaper item called the new tower a useful addition for residents of the lower part of Worcester County.[^3] The DNR report gives the installation date as unknown, so the 1935 date rests on the register and the newspaper.[^1][^2][^3]

As of 2017, the register says the Department of Natural Resources planned to remove the tower.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the Green Hill site "Tower Gone" but gives no date.[^4]

A DNR report dated April 2007 describes the tower as still standing. The steel structure was sound, but the wooden steps and cab were in poor shape, and the report recommends considering the site as a possible future communications site.[^2] The report predates the 2017 register, which says removal was planned. Neither source dates the removal itself.

The same report says the first 20 feet of steps had been removed for safety, and that the fenced yard holding the tower, an equipment building and a propane tank was getting overgrown.[^2]

No source we read gives a date for removal. The 2017 register is the latest description, and it says removal was planned, not completed. No source we read says whether visitors may enter the compound.
