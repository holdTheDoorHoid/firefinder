Cedar Point, which a 1942 report calls City Lookout, stood at 2,162 feet inside Seattle's Cedar River watershed in King County.[^1][^3] The register and Willhite both date the first lookout to 1931, a 40-foot wooden tower with a cabin at ground level.[^1][^3] The City of Seattle Water Department administered the site.[^1] In 1961 the lookout was replaced by a 40-foot treated timber tower with a 15-by-15-foot R-6 flat-top cab and a catwalk.[^1][^3]

Willhite quotes a 1942 inspection report that says the city normally manned the lookout and built it.[^3] That year the Aircraft Warning Service got permission to use it as a yearlong observation post.[^3] The Forest Lookouts index dates the activation from March 25, 1942 to October 16, 1943.[^4] The Fire Lookout Association lists Leo Ogle in 1947 and Pearl Collins in 1951 as known lookout personnel.[^2]

The register says the 1961 tower was last used in the 1970s and was removed in 1981.[^1] The Fire Lookout Association and Willhite both say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^3] The sources use different words for the 1981 end, and we have not settled which is right.

The site is closed to public access.[^1] Willhite says tours sometimes visit the lookout and other historic sites, and lists the visit as "Special Permission & Guide Required."[^3] A photo caption on the register, dated 1997, reads "Tower leg foundation overgrown with alders."[^1] No source we read describes the site today beyond that caption.
