Roxy Ann Peak stands about four miles northeast of Medford, Oregon, and its fire lookout is now gone.[^1][^2]

The register and firelookout.com both date a 15-foot pole platform with a cabin to 1941, and both name the Oregon Department of Forestry as the agency that ran it.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page heads its entry with the City of Medford instead, so the sources disagree about who was responsible.[^3]

Earlier, in October 1933, a newspaper report described Medford's plans for a park on the mountain, and those plans included a lookout tower among other buildings. The sources do not say whether that tower is the platform the register dates to 1941.[^1][^3]

Wikipedia says the city created Prescott Park in 1937, and that the Civilian Conservation Corps began improving it in 1933 and stopped work in 1942.[^4] Neither the register nor firelookout.com gives a date for when the platform and cabin were removed.[^1][^2]

Wikipedia says the summit now carries a 180-foot radio tower, built in 2007, that replaced three of four older 80-foot towers.[^4] No source connects that tower to the old lookout or says what, if anything, of the platform remains.[^1][^2][^4] Prescott Park, which Wikipedia calls Medford's largest park, covers much of the summit.[^4] The city reworked the grade of Roxy Ann Road in 1998, and two gates went up near the park entrance two years after that.[^4] Wikipedia also describes road access: the peak and park can be reached via Roxy Ann Road, and Tower Road ascends to the summit.[^4]
