Ted Gilbert Lookout went up in 1963 in Cowlitz County, replacing the Elk Rock lookout, which had been damaged in the 1962 Columbus Day storm.[^1][^3][^4] It was a 40-foot wooden tower with a live-in cab, built by the state Department of Natural Resources, although the land was Weyerhaeuser's.[^1][^4]

The tower was named for Ted Gilbert, who retired from Weyerhaeuser after 40 years.[^2][^3] The sources disagree on the cab.[^4][^3] Willhite gives 14 by 14 feet, while a 1963 newspaper quote gives 14 by 13 feet.[^4][^3]

Thomas Huntington of Toutle was the first keeper in 1963.[^3][^4] He had to truck in water, and there was no electricity.[^4] Leland Peterson followed in 1964.[^4] Rose Hadaller is listed for 1965.[^2] Martin Burrows staffed it in 1967 and again in 1968.[^4]

A private party bought the tower in 1977 and removed it, three years before the nearby mountain erupted.[^1][^3][^4]

Today the tower does not stand.[^1] Willhite's photos are captioned with a half-buried footing and a lookout anchor.[^4]

Willhite's route starts on the Spirit Lake Memorial Highway.[^4] Forest roads near the summit are usually gated. The page advises parking on the highway and getting onto the road network just above it.[^4] The page title places the site in Mount St. Helens National Monument.[^4]
