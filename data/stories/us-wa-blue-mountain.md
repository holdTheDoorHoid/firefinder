In 1936 crews from S & P and the CCC built an 84-foot treated timber tower on the west ridge of Blue Mountain, in Snohomish County.[^1][^2] They also built a ground house for the lookout to live in.[^1] The Washington Division of Forestry/DNR is listed as the agency.[^3] Sources differ on the design: the Former Fire Lookout Sites register calls it an L-6 tower, while a Washington lookout history page describes an L-7 cab.[^2][^4]

In 1939 Blue Mountain took over the area High Rocks had covered after the High Rocks station was discontinued.[^4] A 1940 annual report says the station's lookout carried supplies up roughly a mile of trail, some of it at a 30 percent grade.[^4] That same year a bulldozer from the Washington Forest Fire Association put in the last mile of road to the site.[^1] The association's report said the new road let automobiles in and made the lookout man more useful.[^4]

The site was used by the AWS in 1942 and 1943, and a 12x20 garage may have been built then.[^1] A Seattle Filter Center note gives March 8, 1942 as the activation date and October 16, 1943 as the deactivation date.[^4]

The tower came down in 1969, though the sources disagree on how: the register says it was removed, while the other three say it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The site was listed on the Former Fire Lookout Sites register in January 2012.[^2]

Willhite walked past the site four times without finding it, and he believes the footings could be found when there is no snow.[^1] One photo caption describes a large concrete slab a short distance from the road, and another places a 2007 hiking group at the footings.[^1] Beyond the slab and footings, the sources say little about the site as it stands today.

Willhite's route starts at a gate on the left, 6.3 miles up Sultan Basin Road from Highway 2 in Sultan.[^1] He lists the route at six miles with 2,000 feet of gain from a start near 800 feet.[^1] The sources do not say whether the public may pass that gate.
