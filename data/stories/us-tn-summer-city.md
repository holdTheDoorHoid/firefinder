Summer City Fire Tower stands in Bledsoe County, Tennessee, at an elevation of about 624 metres (2,047 feet).[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab and gives Aermotor as its probable design.[^1] An office of the Tennessee Division of Forestry stands next to it.[^1] A regional lookout site lists it under the Division's District 3.[^2]

The sources read do not say when the tower was built or by whom, and the register's detail list gives no construction year.[^1] The Tennessee tower inventory gives it district-tower number 3-9, taken from a 1976 list of towers, and marks it as standing.[^3] No source read names the people who staffed it or any fires it spotted.[^1][^2]

The register lists the tower as US 1300 and TN 35, registered on March 17, 2019, with Victoria Oliver as the nominator.[^1] Beyond the 1976 list and that registration, the sources do not record what happened to the tower.[^1][^3]

For visitors, the cab is kept locked, but the register reports a sign on the tower saying visitors may go up.[^1] Its photograph is dated May 2018 and credited to Victoria Oliver.[^1] The register entry dates from around 2019, so check with the Division of Forestry before going up, because the sign and the access may have changed.[^1]
