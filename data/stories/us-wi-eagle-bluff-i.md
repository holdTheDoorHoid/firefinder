Eagle Bluff, on the northeast shore of Peninsula State Park in Door County, has had towers on it since the early 1900s.[^1] The Wisconsin Department of Natural Resources (DNR) dates the first Eagle Tower to 1914 and says it and its twin on Sven's Bluff were both built by hand as fire lookouts.[^1]

A 1916 state biennial report, which spells the twin's site Seven's Bluff, describes both towers as already erected and linked by telephone to the superintendent's residence and the local exchange.[^2] The same report says their railed stairways and landings let anyone climb them safely.[^2]

The DNR says the original was taken apart and fully rebuilt in 1932, with a mix of modern machines, horses and tractors.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site describes a 75-foot (23 m) tower from 1932, built with Civilian Conservation Corps labor and meant both to spot fires and to give park visitors a view.[^2]

In the DNR's numbering, the 1932 rebuild is the second Eagle Tower, and it was dismantled in 2016 because it could no longer hold up to time and use.[^1] A hiking blog gives safety concerns as the reason for the 2016 removal.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as Eagle Bluff I and marks it gone.[^4]

A third Eagle Tower opened on the bluff in 2021.[^1] It stands 60 feet (18 m) high, with its top observation deck 253 feet (77 m) above Green Bay, and is open seasonally, roughly from May 1 to October 31; the season can run later into fall when the weather allows.[^1] Visitors can climb 100 steps or take an 850-foot (259 m) fully accessible canopy walk to the top.[^1] No source we found describes what, if anything, of the earlier towers remains on the bluff.[^1]
