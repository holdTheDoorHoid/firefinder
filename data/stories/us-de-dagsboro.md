The Dagsboro tower, a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40, was built in 1931.[^1] The state forester's annual report for fiscal 1931 and 1932, as quoted on a lookout-history page, says work on erecting the towers began on July 29, 1931, and that they opened for service on October 15.[^2] The same report places the Dagsboro tower about a mile and a quarter north of town, on a 35-foot elevation.[^2]

The report describes these steel towers as 100 feet from the ground to the cabin floor and 110 feet overall, with a 7-foot square cabin whose upper half is enclosed in glass.[^2] The towers were normally open about four and a half months a year, two and a half in spring and two in fall, depending on the weather and the risk of forest fires.[^2]

By 1933 the report says wind was eroding the sandy soil around the piers, and that stopping it had been difficult.[^2] It partly blames visitors who drove over the knoll and broke the sod.[^2] The plot was fenced and planted with pine seedlings, and in 1934 seven loads of loam were hauled in to replace the blown sand.[^2]

Later reports mention new window sash in 1939 and repairs to the steps and landings in 1958.[^2]

The register says the tower was removed between 2002 and 2006.[^3] The lookout-history page says it was turned over to the State Police for communications purposes.[^1] A Delaware summary adds that all five of the state's towers were later removed, between 2002 and 2006.[^4]

The register added the tower on September 11, 2008.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, stands on the site today.
