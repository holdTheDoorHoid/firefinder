Rankin Ridge is the high point of Wind Cave National Park, at 5,013 feet.[^1][^4] Plans for a lookout there go back to 1939, but Emergency Conservation Work and CCC activity stopped any planning, and World War II made the question moot.[^4] The ridge received its official name in January 1952.[^4] That July the park moved an old 40-foot cedar pole crow's nest from Elk Mountain to the ridge as a temporary lookout.[^4][^2]

The permanent tower followed in 1956.[^1][^4] The register describes an International Derrick CL100 design with a 14-by-14-foot cab and a catwalk around it.[^1] The park's proposal had called for a 54-foot tower, while firelookout.com gives the finished all-steel tower as 53 feet, so the two figures do not match.[^4][^3] Park staff put the tower up after the parts reached Hot Springs by rail in May 1956, and the work was finished around September 1, costing $14,880.89 in all.[^4]

In July 1954 a local newspaper reported a blaze near Pringle that a lookout on Rankin Ridge had spotted.[^2] Lightning caused it.[^2] Three surveyors controlled it, and the Pringle Fire Department did the mop-up.[^2]

The tower stayed in regular use during fire seasons until 1998.[^4] Since then, public access to the catwalk and tower interior has been restricted, and the last public visit to the top came during the park's 2003 centennial.[^4] Today it is used only now and then, to spot fires or keep an eye on severe weather.[^4]

The tower still stands, and it has been on the National Historic Lookout Register since September 3, 2014.[^1][^3] A one-mile Park Service trail climbs through ponderosa pines to the high point, but climbing the tower itself is not permitted.[^5]
