Oak Grove Butte sits within the Mt. Hood National Forest.[^2][^3]

The site's history starts in 1915, when the Forest Service set up a rag camp there with an alidade platform and a telephone.[^1] An L-4 ground cabin replaced that camp in 1933.[^1][^2]

The lookout was activated on July 12, 1943, and deactivated on October 1, 1943, with the Portland Filter Center listed beside those dates.[^3]

A 1954 item from The Sunday Oregonian, quoted on Rex's page, describes a microwave relay: a transmitter at the dam site, a reflector on the lookout, and a receiver at the Three Lynx power plant.[^3]

A 1959 entry, also quoted there, says a new catwalk and railing were finished on the Oak Grove Butte lookout "tower."[^3] That word does not match the ground-cabin description used elsewhere, and we could not tell which is right.

Rex's page says the lookout was de-activated in 1964.[^3] The register says it was removed in 1967.[^1] The Fire Lookout website says the cabin was destroyed in 1967.[^2] The sources differ on how it ended.[^1][^2]

No source describes what remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register listed the site on September 19, 2010, as US 630, OR 64.[^1]
