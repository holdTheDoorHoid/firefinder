Horseshoe Mountain is a fire tower site in Aroostook County, Maine, which the Forest Fire Lookout Association also lists as Rocky Brook.[^2] A Maine Forestry Service panoramic map centered on the tower is dated 1950 and places it in Township 11, Range 10.[^1] The map shows the tower was in use by 1950, but not when it was built.[^1] I could not find a build date, height, builder or design in the sources I reached.

Maine's towers were a response to the 1903 fires, which burned more than 200,000 acres.[^3] Within two years, timberland owners began building lookouts on their own land, and by 1909 the state had taken over the privately funded ones.[^3] The state's network of more than 100 towers was linked by telephone, with lone observers watching for smoke and fire.[^3] Fire towers were most numerous in Maine between 1930 and 1950.[^4] I found no record of a named watchman at Horseshoe Mountain, or of a notable fire it spotted.

Maine ended its staffed system after a 1991 budget crisis, and some towers were later taken apart or fell down.[^3]

Today the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, with an asterisk showing that its cab is gone.[^2][^5] By the association's key, that means the structure is still up.[^5] I found no current access rules, climbing information or permission for the site. Treat it as unverified, and don't climb it without the landowner's permission.
