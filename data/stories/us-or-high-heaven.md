High Heaven is a hilltop in Yamhill County, Oregon, about six miles northwest of McMinnville.[^1][^2] The Oregon Department of Forestry administered the site.[^1][^2] A 1972 survey description puts the hill about eight miles out, so the sources disagree on the distance.[^3]

Supplies for a 40-foot CT-3 tower and cab were bought in 1952, and the finished 40-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab was completed in 1953.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites entry gives the same 1953 date and a live-in cab.[^1] The 1972 survey description puts the tower at about 70 feet, against 40 feet in the other two entries.[^3][^1][^2] A 1941 account quoted by Forest Lookouts already describes a lookout working on the ridge, so the site was in use before the 1953 tower. The sources do not say what the station consisted of.[^3]

A Lake County Examiner account, quoted by Forest Lookouts, has the lookout on High Heaven ridge spot roadside smoke that it links to a live cigarette tossed from a car.[^3] The item is dated August 7, 1941, but its text also refers to 1938, so its dates are hard to pin down.[^3]

In 1956 vandals destroyed two window sashes, which were replaced, and a Civil Defense communications building went up that same year.[^3]

The tower was abandoned in 1965 and then suffered from disrepair until it was removed in May 1998.[^1] Firelookout.com marks it as "R.I.P. 1953-1998" and says it was promptly abandoned in 1965.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page records the removal as 1998.[^3]

The 1972 survey directions name a triangulation station called HEAVEN, tied to the center of the tower, and describe a bladed road that passes through a gate to the top of the hill.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the hilltop today, or whether that road and gate are open to the public.
