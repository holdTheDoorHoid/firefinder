The Downey Peak crow's nest dates to 1929.[^1] The Idaho Fire Lookouts site says it was set up in conjunction with the Little Guard Lookout tower, and the peak sits at 5,985 feet in the Coeur d'Alene National Forest.[^1] A history of Little Guard on the Idaho Lookouts site gives the same year and records that a cupola cabin and a crow's nest on Downey Peak were both built then.[^2]

The site lists the crow's nest as abandoned.[^1]

On one visit, the site's writers hiked for about an hour up the peak and ridge, gave up, concluded the nest surely was no longer standing, and started back. While they stopped to study the larger trees on the ridge, one of them spotted a spike sticking out of a tree trunk.[^1] Spikes still run up that trunk, and the writers assume they mark where the lookout once stood.[^1] Those spikes are the only remains the page describes, and it calls the site a hike-in rather than a drive-up.[^1]

The building standing on Little Guard Peak today is the third lookout building there, and the series dates to 1919.[^3] Downey Peak lies nearby to the northwest, and the lookout stands at 6,031 feet.[^3]
