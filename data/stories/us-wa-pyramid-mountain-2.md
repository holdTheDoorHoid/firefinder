The Pyramid Peak Aircraft Warning Service Lookout stands on the southern side of Pyramid Peak, inside Olympic National Park.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register calls it Pyramid Mountain Lookout.[^1]

Built in the fall of 1942, it was meant to watch for Japanese aircraft during World War II.[^2] The U.S. Army paid for it, and National Park Service employees Joe and Rena Shurnick built it as one of thirteen such sites in what is now the park.[^2] According to the register, the cabin was specially designed and has a storage shed attached.[^1] Wikipedia describes a 13-by-16-foot structure clad in wood shingles under a simple pitched roof.[^2]

The Aircraft Warning Service used the lookout until June 1944, when that service was abandoned.[^2] A firelookout.com page gives 1945 as the year it was abandoned, so the two sources differ by about a year.[^3] The register says the lookout was dropped and taken out of service once the war was over.[^1] A National Park Service history of coastal defenses says that in 1983 only two park structures used by the Aircraft Warning Service were still standing, and this lookout was one of them.[^4] The National Register of Historic Places listed it on July 13, 2007.[^2][^5]

The firelookout.com page shows it still there in 2013, and the register says it was still standing as of July 2015.[^3][^1] That 2015 note is the most recent condition report among the sources I read, so its current state is unconfirmed.[^1] The lookout is reached on foot.[^5] The National Register record places it 3.5 miles up the Pyramid Peak trail from the end of Camp David Jr. Road.[^5]
