Hamner Tower stands in a county park on North Boulevard.[^5] A November 1950 list of observation towers, quoted by an Eastern U.S. lookout site from the Tampa Tribune, places the tower on Fletcher Avenue, a half-mile west of Florida Avenue.[^2]

The National Historic Lookout Register dates the tower to 1937 and says the Florida Division of Forestry ran it as a fire tower for many years.[^1] The Eastern lookout site also dates a steel tower's erection to 1937.[^2]

A blog post citing a Hillsborough County Water Atlas document gives the structure's height as 85 feet and its build year as 1936, and calls it a fire lookout.[^4] That differs from the 1937 date used elsewhere. The Water Atlas document itself was not reviewed, so the 1936 date and the height are secondhand. A 2004 Tampa Bay Times piece says Hillsborough was Florida's second county, behind Duval, to set up its own forest fire protection unit.[^3]

The only staffing record found is that 1950 list, which names Ruth Foster as the towerwoman at the Hamner tower.[^2] No fires connected with the tower appear in the sources reviewed.

The tower's use faded as the area grew. The blog says the tower stopped being used once the area became urban and gained fire department coverage.[^4] In 2004 the Tampa Bay Times said a 1993 county commission vote to buy the site from the state kept the tower from being torn down.[^3] The register says Hillsborough County later bought it through its Environmental Lands Acquisition and Protection Program.[^1] The tower was designated a county historic landmark in 1995.[^1][^5]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on March 14, 2014, as US 996, FL 5, with Sheryl Bowman as nominator.[^1]

Today the tower is part of Hamner Tower Park, a county park with an open field and a hiking trail.[^5] Parking runs along North Boulevard.[^5] The county page does not say whether the tower itself can be climbed, so visitors should not assume it can be. Enjoy it from the grounds unless the county says otherwise.
