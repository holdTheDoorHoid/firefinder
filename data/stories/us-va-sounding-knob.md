Sounding Knob sits atop Jack Mountain, east of Monterey, in Highland County, Virginia.[^1] In May 1934 a Civilian Conservation Corps camp was being set up near Monterey to build a road along the mountain to the Knob.[^2] A local report from that month says the district forester had arranged for an 80-foot all-steel tower in place of a planned 47-foot one, at no added cost to the county.[^2]

The sources disagree on the build year. The register says 1935, while Skip Jones, the later owner, and the Virginia Association of Counties say 1934.[^1][^3][^5] A March 1935 newspaper item describes a drive along a newly built CCC road to the 80-foot tower, so it was standing by then.[^2]

The 1934 plan said a fire warden would occupy the tower during the dangerous seasons, with a small house nearby.[^2] The register says the tower was staffed until the 1960s.[^1] A March 1948 item says lookout watchman Jesse Wiseman moved into the tower that month, and an October 1950 item says he was still on duty there.[^2]

Jones says fire towers went out of use in the 1960s because small airplanes made them less practical, and that the tower sat in disrepair until 2002.[^3] The register, the county and Jones all date the removal to 2002.[^1][^3][^5] A Recorder item dated July 24, 2004 reports the dismantling that year.[^2] Steve Good of Highland Welding bought the tower, then salvaged and stored its components.[^1]

Jones bought the tower in 2016.[^1] The steel went to Ashland, Virginia, to be re-galvanized, the bolts went to Illinois for the same treatment, and new hemlock steps were made instead of reusing the old ones.[^4] The register says it was reinstalled at Sounding Knob in 2017, with a public dedication on July 8.[^1] The county says it now overlooks Monterey from the top of Jack Mountain.[^5]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the original site as having no tower.[^6] Jones says that if you go up Sounding Knob, about half a mile along on your right, you can see the foundation of the CCC camp.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower at the old site.

The reinstalled tower is privately owned by Jones.[^1] The register says it is gated, but that as of November 2020 it was being left open to visitors during daylight hours.[^1] In 2017 Jones said it was fenced in and that he would open it from time to time.[^4] The county describes a gravel drive off Sounding Knob Road, 1.3 miles south of Route 250, with more than 100 steps to the top.[^5] Access rules have changed before, so check with the owner first.
