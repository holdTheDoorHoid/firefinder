Upper Souris National Wildlife Refuge sits along the Souris River in northwestern North Dakota, about 30 miles northwest of Minot.[^1][^2] It was set up in 1935 as a refuge where birds and other wildlife could breed.[^1]

The refuge headquarters has a lookout tower.[^5] A list of North Dakota lookouts gives 1935 for a 100-foot steel tower.[^3] The list files the tower under another refuge's name, J. Clark Salyer, with Upper Souris in brackets, so the link to this headquarters depends on that label.[^3] A U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service photo caption credits the Civilian Conservation Corps with building the tower, but it gives no year.[^4]

The Fish and Wildlife Service runs the refuge today.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a standing tower at the headquarters, though the record does not confirm it is the 1935 tower.[^5] The sources do not say whether visitors may go up it, so ask the refuge before you plan a visit, and do not climb it without permission.
