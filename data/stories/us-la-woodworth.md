The Woodworth Fire Tower stands in the headquarters compound of Alexander State Forest, in Rapides Parish, Louisiana.[^1][^2] The Louisiana Department of Agriculture and Forestry administers it, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1]

The site held a tower for years before this one.[^1] The current tower is a 175-foot Aermotor that was not built until 1951.[^1] The Register says it stands on four large concrete foundations, set that way to withstand hurricane winds.[^1]

A Forest Lookouts page says a television camera housed in a dome was set on the tower's roof in June 1954.[^3] The page gives no other detail about the camera.[^3]

The Town of Woodworth says the tower is believed to be the world's tallest fire tower.[^2]

The Register gives June 3, 1993 as the registration date, with Edward J. Robichaux, Assistant Chief of Forest Protection, as nominator.[^1]

None of the pages read name fires the tower watched, and none say it has been moved, burned or restored since 1951.[^1][^2][^3]

The pages read do not say who staffs the tower today or whether visitors may climb it.[^1][^2][^3] Because the tower sits in a headquarters compound, check with the Louisiana Department of Agriculture and Forestry before planning any visit.
