Round Mountain is a lookout on a black lava knoll in the Deschutes National Forest, about 12 miles northwest of La Pine.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service administers it, and the Bend-Fort Rock Ranger District is listed as a cooperator.[^1]

The summit had lookouts before the cab was built.[^3] Forest Lookouts, which quotes The Bend Bulletin, records emergency lookouts there in July 1928 and August 1930.[^3] In August 1932 the paper reported that one of eight planned lookout houses would be built on a massive rock here.[^3]

Forest Lookouts gives 1933 as the year the 14-by-14-foot L-4 hip-roof house was completed, at a cost of $709.89.[^3] The Register and Cheryl Hill also date the build to 1933,[^1][^4] but a note on the same page says the station was built in 1932 and observed in 1933, so the build year is disputed.[^3] A 14-by-18-foot wood-frame garage built to plan T-1E followed in 1934.[^3]

Gilbert Bowe was stationed there in July 1935 when lightning apparently struck the house.[^3] Its conductors sent the bolts into the ground.[^3] In 1943 a newspaper reported that 17-year-old Sherwood Jerome, posted to the summit, saw signs of fire before the other district lookouts did.[^3] In 1945 Elizabeth Randell, a Prineville schoolteacher and former lookout, was assigned to the top of the mountain.[^3] The lookout was evacuated during the 1994 Four Corners fire and again on August 18, 2001, during the Crane Fire.[^3]

Cheryl Hill dates the deck to the 1980s and the new shutters to 2017.[^4] The building has moved over the years and no longer sits square, so each shutter had to be made for its own window.[^4]

The Register listed the lookout on March 1, 2003, as US 468, OR 65.[^1] It gives the elevation as 5,835 feet, while firelookout.com and Cheryl Hill give about 5,900 feet.[^1][^2][^4]

Cheryl Hill visited in November 2023, when the lookout was staffed in summer and shut up for the winter.[^4] The firelookout.com page also says it is staffed every summer.[^2] Cheryl Hill's standing-lookout list marks it accessible to the public, while warning that its data may be out of date.[^5] The 2023 visitor parked at a gate and walked 1.4 miles up the road.[^4] The cab was still standing at that visit, about ninety years after it was built.[^4]

Before going, check with the Bend-Fort Rock Ranger District about summer staffing and the gate, since the most recent visit described here is from 2023.[^4]
