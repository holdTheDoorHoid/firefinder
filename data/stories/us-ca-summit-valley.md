Summit Valley Lookout stood on a high, round-topped mountain in Del Norte County, California, roughly 22 miles southeast of Crescent City.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes it as a short, open timber tower with a partial catwalk, and says it appears to follow a Forest Service cabin plan.[^1] The register lists its construction and removal dates as unknown.[^1]

A 1940 forest plan, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said the view from the site largely duplicated the one from Buck Mountain, and that the site's chief value was fire suppression.[^2] The same plan said lightning was the only cause of fires.[^2] A 1941 Coast and Geodetic Survey description on that page calls the station a Forest Service lookout tower on the summit.[^2]

The register listed the site on January 28, 2020, as a former lookout.[^1] In June 2020 a hiker found a square of loose rocks, strewn with metal, marking the former site, with flattened chimney pieces nearby.[^4]

The trailhead is on the G-O Road (Forest Road 15), reached by South Fork Road from Highway 199, just east of Hiouchi.[^3] The Summit Valley Trail runs about 1.5 miles one way to the meadow in the valley, which sits below the summit.[^3][^4] The 2020 hiker found only splinters left of a sign at the meadow, a cairn-marked route climbing toward the summit, and dead trees near the top.[^4]

The trail lies within the Smith River National Recreation Area, where roughly 80 miles of trails are maintained for hiking, biking and horse riding.[^3] The most recent description we found shows rocks and metal scraps, not a standing tower.[^4] None of the sources we read say whether the summit route is maintained or open to visitors.[^4][^3]
