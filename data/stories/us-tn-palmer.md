Palmer stood on Palmer Mountain in Grundy County, Tennessee.[^1][^3] The Tennessee Landforms waypoint for the tower sits at 2,385 feet (727 m) above sea level.[^1] Its number, 3-22, is a state forest tower number from a 1976 list of towers, which suggests it was one of the Division of Forestry's lookouts.[^1][^2]

A National Register form on the division's towers credits the agency with constructing 208 fire lookout towers, put up around 1930 to 1970.[^4] Taken together, the towers embodied the division's fire detection system from 1933 to 1975.[^4]

A Palmer town history column by David Patton recalls a family living in a house at the Palmer Fire Tower, where the father, Isham Hobbs, was the lookout.[^3]

Tennessee Landforms marks the tower as removed.[^1] That National Register form also says many of the 208 towers are gone or slated for demolition, since they went unused or their leases with private owners lapsed.[^4]

The Landforms list adds that many towers are gone and most of their sites are now privately owned.[^2] No source describes what remains at the Palmer site today.
