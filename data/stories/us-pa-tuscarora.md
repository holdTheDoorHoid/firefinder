The Tuscarora lookout went up on Tuscarora Mountain in 1921, during a state program that ordered fifty steel fire towers.[^1][^2] A June 1921 newspaper item said two of them were likely to be sited in Buchanan State Forest, and that a fire observer on this summit could see fires across a wide area.[^2] The register describes the tower as a steel Aermotor type, built in 1921.[^1] It replaced an earlier tree tower on Big Knob, built in 1914, according to the register entry for that site.[^4]

An August 1921 notice listed it among the first eighteen towers to go up, and a September report said the state's steel tower was finished.[^2] That report put the lookout about eighty feet above the ground, on the mountaintop.[^2]

The 1921 items disagree on the summit's height, giving 2,440 feet in June and 2,540 feet in September.[^2] They also differ from the register on the county.[^1][^2] The register says Fulton, while the August notice says Franklin.[^1][^2]

Both the register and the lookout history site say the tower was removed in 1978.[^1][^2]

A 2016 state geology publication gives about 2,458 feet as the elevation of a Tower Road vista on Tuscarora Mountain, in Buchanan State Forest.[^3] The same publication places that vista in Franklin County, but it does not connect the spot to the old lookout.[^3] No source I read says what, if anything, remains of the tower today.[^1][^2]
