The Conservation Department erected an 80-foot Aermotor LS40 tower at New Boston in 1950.[^1] It stood north of State Route 177 in the hamlet of New Boston, in Lewis County.[^1][^2]

The state chapter's list says the tower closed at the end of the 1970 season, when aerial detection came in.[^1] DEC personnel took it down in 1982 and moved the pieces to its Harrisville facilities.[^1] We found no record of notable fires or named observers at this site.

The tower was later loaned to the Thompson Park Zoo in Watertown.[^1] The chapter's 2016 report says it was moved into the park in 1999, where it serves as a community educational exhibit.[^3] The 2003 report says the zoo restored it but kept it closed until it could secure liability insurance for people who climb.[^5] The 2004 report says a grand opening followed that summer, after which the tower was open to the public.[^6]

In 2016 the chapter awarded a $500 grant for repairs, and the work was finished that spring.[^4][^3] The chapter's list says the tower reopened to the public at the zoo that year.[^1] That does not match the 2004 opening, and the sources we read do not explain the gap.

Today the tower stands on the zoo grounds in Watertown, Jefferson County.[^2] A 2025 chapter newsletter calls it the former New Boston fire tower and mentions a garden at its base, which a local ADK chapter maintains.[^7] We could not confirm whether visitors may climb it now. Check with the zoo before you visit, and do not climb it unless staff say it is open.
