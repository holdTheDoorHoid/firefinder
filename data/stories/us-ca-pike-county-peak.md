Pike County Peak lies in Yuba County, California, in the Plumas National Forest.[^1] Its 1913 station had no tower at all.[^2] A 1913 newspaper report says that, with no money for a tower, Forest Service officials built a platform around a large pine on the crest, between 75 and 80 feet up, and reached it by a ladder against the trunk.[^2] The Register puts a permanent structure on the peak by 1923.[^1]

A watchman spent the 1913 summer in the tree and phoned fires in to the service's main office at Nevada City.[^2] In 1921 a lookout from the Pike County station was first to spot a fire in Camptonville.[^2] Joseph Shaffer began staffing the lookout on May 14, 1923, and that August a fire near Forbestown was reported from it and held to one acre.[^2] In 1928 Mrs. J. W. Vance was the only woman staffing a fire lookout in the Plumas National Forest, and her four-year-old son kept her company that summer.[^2] After it became clear in 1932 that fires were being set at night, the lookout got a night shift.[^2]

The cab and tower now on the peak came from Lexington Hill.[^1] A 1933 structure there gave way in 1961 to a CL-100 cab on a 20-foot steel tower.[^3] The Register says that cab and tower were taken down and set up again on Pike County Peak in 1983.[^1][^2][^3] It was registered on October 30, 2019, and the Register says the lookout was actively detecting fires that year.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4]

Today the sources read do not say whether the public can visit or climb it. Peakbagging.com says the lookout is usually staffed in fire season, but that note comes from a 2006 photo.[^5] Check with the Plumas National Forest before any trip.
