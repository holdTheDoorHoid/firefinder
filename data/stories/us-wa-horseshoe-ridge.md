A lookout went up on this Skamania County ridge in 1934.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register describes a six-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, and FireLookout.com gives the same design.[^1][^2] Willhite's site says the lookout was reported built that year, and that panoramic images were taken from 12 feet up on the roof on July 11, 1934.[^3]

The sources disagree about how it ended.[^1][^3] The register dates the abandonment to 1952 and reports the lookout gone, and one of its photos is captioned 1964.[^1] Willhite says it is unclear when the lookout was abandoned and burned, and points to a Forest Service image from 1964 that shows the lookout in good condition.[^3]

Today the site shows only scraps.[^3] Willhite's photo captions list hinges, nails, glass, a battery core and burned wood.[^3] Willhite writes that a visitor would never guess buildings once stood there.[^3] The Horseshoe Ridge Trail runs right past the site.[^3]

The Forest Service describes the trail as a 6.9-mile route along the ridge.[^4] It says a lookout formerly sat at the northwest end of the 3,495-foot summit.[^4] When its page was read, the trail was listed as temporarily closed because of the High Lava Fire.[^4] Gravel roads come within a mile of the site, and Willhite notes that the trailhead on the map is unsigned.[^3]

The register gives the site an elevation of 3,465 feet, while FireLookout.com gives 3,495 feet.[^1][^2]
