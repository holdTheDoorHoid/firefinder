Rocky Mountain sits in the Maine North Woods, in Aroostook County.[^1][^2] Landowners built the first lookout there in 1907, before the Fire District existed.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register calls that first building a wood tower, while a 1914 report describes it as log-built and too short for the territory.[^2][^3]

In 1917 Chief Warden Hosea Buck reported building a wooden tower 20 feet high on the south bluff.[^3] A 1919 report, though, says a new log tower went up in 1917 on another bluff, which does not match Buck's account.[^3]

The Maine Forest Service put up a steel tower with an 8-by-8-foot wooden cab in 1921.[^2] The register gives it as 54 feet tall. A 1920 inventory and a 1921 newspaper item give 48 feet instead.[^2][^3] A Maine Forest Service panorama map from 1922 is centered on the tower.[^6]

Chapter members visited in 2009 and found that the cab needed a new floor and probably some roof work. They could not put together a repair trip that year.[^5]

On October 30, 2017, a windstorm brought the cab down.[^3][^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list still shows the tower as standing, and its asterisk marks towers whose cab is no longer in place.[^7]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the site is no longer used for fire detection and that a radio repeater sits in the cab.[^1] The register gives no date for the repeater, and we could not reconcile it with the 2017 loss of the cab.

Reaching the site takes a permit for private timber roads from the Dickey checkpoint, and the summit can be reached only by trail.[^2]

None of the sources we read describes the site beyond these points.
