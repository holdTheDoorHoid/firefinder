Edson Butte is a lookout site about 12 miles northeast of Port Orford, at 2,786 feet.[^1] Sources disagree on its county. Firelookout.com says Curry County, and the Forest Lookouts list also places it under Curry. The FFLA's Oregon list says Coos.[^1][^2][^3]

A 14-foot tower was built there around 1941. The site was activated as an Aircraft Warning Service post on September 12, 1942.[^2][^1] In 1951 a 20-foot timber tower with a 14-by-14-foot cabin replaced the old building.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts also gives 1955 for that 20-foot tower, so the dates do not line up.[^2]

The sources do not agree on the rebuilds. Forest Lookouts quotes Coos district reports that the 20-foot tower was replaced in 1974 by a 52-foot tower, which a November 1975 windstorm blew down. The state restoration fund paid for a 1976 rebuild.[^2] Firelookout.com instead describes a 1974 tower that blew over and a 53-foot tower built in 1975.[^1] Both agree that the tower was lost to wind in 1996. Forest Lookouts gives December 31 as the date of the collapse.[^1][^2]

The present tower is also dated differently. Forest Lookouts says a new steel tower went up in 1999.[^2] Firelookout.com dates the present 40-foot, all-steel tower with a 6-by-6-foot cab to 2008, after a trailer lookout was used in the gap. Cheryl Hill's list also gives 2008.[^1][^4]

In September 1957 a fire six miles northeast of Langlois raced through slash and snags. Staff had to leave the station, but firefighters still saved it.[^2] In 1961 a summer lookout assignment was announced for the site.[^2] The Coos FPA installed a smoke detection camera system in 2010, and firelookout.com says the tower is no longer staffed.[^2][^1]

The present tower remains on site.[^1] Cheryl Hill's list marks the site as not accessible to the public. Her note says that column shows whether a site can be legally reached, not whether the lookout is open to visitors.[^4] We found no visiting information. Please do not climb the tower, and ask the Coos Forest Protective Association before any visit.
