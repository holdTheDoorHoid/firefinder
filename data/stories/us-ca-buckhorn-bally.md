Buckhorn Bally Lookout is a 13-foot enclosed timber tower in the Klamath National Forest, in Siskiyou County.[^1] The Register classes it as a Region 1 variant of the Region 5 BC-301 design and gives 1933 as its build year.[^1]

Work started on the ground in 1933. A diary entry dated October 21, 1933, quoted in a Klamath forest history, describes locating the site and picking the best trail for packing in materials.[^2] The lookout went up on ground leased from Southern Pacific Land Company.[^2] The tract was bought in 1935, and title to the 20-acre site was accepted that December.[^2] The fire finder went in during May 1934.[^2]

In the winter of 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service made use of the lookout as an observation post.[^1] A June 22, 1951, entry says the tower and cab were in good shape but needed a new coat of paint.[^2] A photo caption from July 2006 says it was usually staffed during fire season.[^3] NPR describes Kathy Shoopman, who started her watch in the early 1970s, as the Buckhorn-Bally fire lookout.[^4]

In the River Complex fires of late July 2021, crews declared the Bally Fire near the lookout out.[^5] NPR reported in August 2022 that the McKinney Fire killed Shoopman, 74, when it overran the community of Klamath River, where she had lived for almost 50 years.[^4] NPR also reported that her supervisor and sheriff's deputies had urged her to leave, but she kept her post.[^4]

The Register accepted the lookout in October 2019.[^1] A post from the ALERTCalifornia camera network describes the tower as the historic lookout, and says two of its cameras were placed there in partnership with the Klamath National Forest.[^6]

I did not find a public visitor program or rental listing for Buckhorn Bally. The Forest Service has staffed it in fire season, so please check with the Klamath National Forest before going up.
