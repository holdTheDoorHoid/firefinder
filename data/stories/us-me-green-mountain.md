Green Mountain is a fire tower site in Dolebrook Township, Somerset County, Maine.[^1][^2] A Maine Forestry Service panoramic map centered on the tower is labeled "n.d.," so it shows the tower was in use at some point without giving a date.[^1] I could not find a build date, height or builder in the sources I reached, so the early story below is mostly the state's, not this tower's record.

Maine's towers grew out of fire losses. After forest fires burned more than 200,000 acres in 1903, timberland owners began building lookouts on their own land, and by 1909 the state had taken over those privately funded towers.[^3] According to the Forest Society of Maine, the towers were a way for private landowners to guard their timber, and they were most numerous in Maine between 1930 and 1950.[^4]

Maine's more than 100 towers were linked by telephone, and lone observers staffed them, watching for smoke and fire.[^3] I found no record of who watched from Green Mountain, or for how long.

Maine's system outlasted most others. It was dropped after a budget crisis in 1991.[^3] Afterward, some towers came down on their own or were taken apart, and others have been put to new use.[^3]

The record disagrees about what stands today. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Green Mountain as standing with no asterisk, and its key says the asterisk marks towers whose cab is gone.[^2][^5] The Forest Society of Maine, though, reports tower remains on Green Mountain, a mountain where the society holds easements.[^4] I could not settle which account is current. Nothing I found says the public may climb the tower, so treat any visit as one that needs the landowner's permission.
