Signal Peak Lookout stands on a 7,781-foot mountain in the Tonto National Forest, in Gila County, Arizona.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers it, with the Globe Ranger District as a cooperator.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as US 444, AZ 40.[^1] Dave Lorenz, the AZ/NM registrar, nominated it, and the Register recorded it on July 20, 2002.[^1]

The Register says the present tower was built in 1934, succeeding a wooden tower from 1920.[^1] It also gives the tower as a 59-foot MC-39 Aermotor, with a metal cab 7 feet by 7 feet.[^1]

The sources read do not say who staffed the tower over the years or which fires it watched.[^1][^2]

The Register calls the site an active station.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Arizona index lists the tower as standing under the same register number.[^2][^3] Neither listing mentions a closure, rebuild, or rental.[^2][^3]

Peakbagging's Arizona lookout table, revised October 2, 2024, assigns the structure code A, meaning its condition is good and it could be staffed.[^4] That describes the building, not who staffs it now.[^4]

The sources do not say whether the public can drive up, visit, or climb the tower.[^1][^2] Anyone planning a trip should check with the Tonto National Forest first, and should stay away from any tower that is closed or off limits.
