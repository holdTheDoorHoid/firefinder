The Forest Lookouts page for Flat Top Mountain, in Pushmataha County, has little beyond a 1960 newspaper item.[^1]

On October 1, 1960, The Daily Oklahoman reported that Jack Cornelius Jr., who led the state board of agriculture, had said a fire watch tower would go up near Kosoma, in Pushmataha County.[^1] Lawmakers had funded four such towers in 1959, and this one was to draw on that money to guard southeastern Oklahoma's timber.[^1]

The page is titled for Flat Top Mountain, but the report places the planned tower near Kosoma.[^1] The sources do not say whether those names refer to one site.

The 1960 item reports a plan, not a finished tower.[^1]

Oklahoma's state forestry history gives the wider context. It says 23 state towers once stood in the agency's protection area.[^2] It also says aircraft took over from retiring observers, that only three towers remain staffed, and that the rest now house radio repeaters.[^2] That is the agency's general story, not a record of Flat Top Mountain.

No source describes the site today, so I cannot say what remains there or whether the public may visit it.
