Miller Mountain stands on the Spokane Indian Reservation in Stevens County, about 18 miles west-northwest of Wellpinit.[^1][^2] The register says the Spokane Indian Reservation administers the site, which makes it tribal land.[^1]

The register says the first lookout was built in 1932: a three-legged wooden tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, plus a tent and a cabin. An L-4 cab for living quarters followed in 1934.[^1][^2]

A Spokesman-Review item dated August 17, 1935, reprinted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the 78-foot lookout tower on Miller Mountain was completed and that Morris Ledoen put it up.[^3] The same page quotes a federal survey station description that gives the tower's height as 75 feet.[^3] The sources do not reconcile the 1932 date with the 1935 item.[^1][^3]

Sources differ on how the tower ended. The register says it was removed in 1962, firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year, and WillhiteWeb says removed or destroyed.[^1][^2][^4] The Forest Lookouts page marks it as removed.[^3]

The summit now has a parking area and a new communication structure.[^4] WillhiteWeb reports that the old lookout's foundation is on higher ground a short walk south of the parking area, and that other footings beside the parking area are likely a former communication structure.[^4] The road to the top is dirt, with ruts in places, and the page describes the site as drive-up.[^4]

The upper half of the road passes houses and signs of private property. WillhiteWeb thinks old easements probably allow access there.[^4] Because the site is on the reservation, ask before you go and do not assume open access.[^1]
