Cleman Mountain Lookout stood on a long ridge near the town of Naches, in Yakima County, Washington.[^1][^2][^4] The site was first developed in 1946 with a log cabin, set up by the Division of Forestry after the war.[^1][^4] An L-4 cab replaced the cabin in 1954.[^2]

A 40-foot wooden tower with a live-in DNR cab came later. The National Historic Lookout Register and firelookout.com date it to 1968, while Willhite Web says late 1968 or 1969.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The cab was 14 by 14 feet.[^1]

Willhite Web names Sharon Borgford and her husband as the lookouts in 1967, and Earl and Merline Blum in 1968.[^4] The Wenatchee National Forest last staffed the lookout in 1998.[^1][^3][^4]

A storm in the winter of 2006 and 2007 damaged the tower legs.[^1][^2] The sources differ on how many were hurt: the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register and firelookout.com say one leg broke, while Willhite Web says two were severely damaged.[^1][^2][^3][^4] Willhite Web says the DNR toppled the tower in April 2007, and the National Historic Lookout Register says it was destroyed that year.[^4][^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register registered the site on November 8, 2000.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register added it on April 15, 2020.[^2] The two registers also give different coordinates and elevations, 5,088 feet (1,551 m) in one and 5,115 feet (1,559 m) in the other.[^1][^2]

We found no source that says what remains of the lookout today. Willhite Web includes a photo captioned "Towers on the summit," but gives no date and does not say whether any part of the lookout is among them.[^4] The guide says both routes to the summit need high-clearance four-wheel drive. If those roads are no longer open, it suggests the Rock Creek Road to the ridge instead.[^4] We found no information about whether the site is open to the public.
