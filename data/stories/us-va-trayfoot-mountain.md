Trayfoot Mountain sits inside Shenandoah National Park in Rockingham County, Virginia, and it once had a fire lookout on its summit.[^1][^2]

A hobbyist's page about Appalachian Trail lookouts describes the last tower at the site as a metal, live-in cab tower with a catwalk, with a large tank on the ground beneath it.[^3] The page says that description was written from memory of a photo in the park archives.[^3]

A second WillhiteWeb page on the park's lookouts says Shenandoah National Park had six fire lookouts within its boundaries, and it lists Trayfoot Mountain among them.[^4] The same page says the park had stopped regularly staffing its five lookout towers by the late 1950s, and that by the early 1960s it had begun to dismantle, scrap, or sell them.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list shows the tower as gone, and the Eastern U.S. Lookouts site marks it as removed.[^5][^1]

A hobbyist site describes the summit area as overgrown, with some rocks at the summit.[^3] A hiking guide says a lookout once stood there, but the only trace left is a clearing that is slowly disappearing.[^2] No source we read describes footings or any other structure on the summit.[^2]

The guide describes a hike on foot from the Blackrock Summit parking area, about 1.5 miles one way with roughly 625 feet of climbing.[^2] It also notes that the summit has no open views.[^2]
