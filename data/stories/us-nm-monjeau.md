Monjeau Lookout sits above the Smokey Bear Ranger District of the Lincoln National Forest, at 9,582 feet, a few miles north of Ruidoso.[^1] Two sources, Living New Deal and the Lincoln National Forest's own visitor page, say the Civilian Conservation Corps put up a first lookout here in 1936 and then rebuilt it in 1940.[^5][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register and Wikipedia mention only the 1940 structure, so the 1936 original may have been a simpler or short-lived building that the later one replaced.[^1][^2]

What stands today is unusual for a fire lookout: a 14-by-14-foot base of native stone, built to double as living quarters, with a small 7-by-7-foot metal cab set on top for the actual watch.[^1] It was recognized as architecturally significant for that rustic CCC design, and in 1988 it was added to the National Register of Historic Places as part of a thematic listing covering fire lookouts across the Forest Service's Southwestern Region.[^4] It also went on New Mexico's State Register of Cultural Properties that same year.[^2] In 1996 it became the first lookout in New Mexico entered into the National Historic Lookout Register, nominated by the Forest's own archaeologist.[^1]

A 2007 volunteer visit report found the tower unstaffed, with signage poor and water damage rotting the wood inside; a restoration contract had gone out to bid but no work had yet been done.[^1] Staffing since then is not documented here. The lookout remains standing and is a popular stop for forest visitors, reached by about 5.5 miles of Forest Service gravel and dirt road; the final stretch is sometimes gated to vehicles, closed to cars for part of 2020 and reopened by mid-2021, and the road itself can close in winter snow.[^2][^3] There is no potable water on site.[^3]
