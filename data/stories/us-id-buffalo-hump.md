Buffalo Hump is a summit in Idaho County, Idaho.[^2][^3] One lookout listing gives the agency as the Nez Perce National Forest.[^2] A Forest Service history says the peak rises to 8,926 feet (2,721 m), and that early miners thought its outline looked like a buffalo's hump.[^3]

The earliest record of a lookout station there found so far is a July 1915 report in the Idaho County Free Press.[^1][^2] It says Supervisor George Ring had gone to Buffalo Hump to install the station, and that a cabin would be built there, and also at Pilot Knob.[^1][^2] The same report names J. B. Moss as the lookout man at the station.[^1][^2]

A 1917 paper named Will Schober as a lookout in the Buffalo Hump country.[^1] In September 1921 the Idaho Republican reported that the Buffalo Hump lookout had sent in word of a fire on the south fork of the Salmon.[^1]

No source describes the site today. What remains there, and whether visitors can reach the summit, is unknown.
