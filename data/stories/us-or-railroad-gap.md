Railroad Gap is in the Umpqua National Forest, in Jackson County, Oregon.[^1] It lies near the divide between the Rogue and Umpqua river watersheds.[^4]

In July 1932 the Roseburg News-Review reported that nine new lookout houses were planned, one of them on Railroad Gap.[^3] A late-July 1932 item already has a woman back from a week's visit with her husband "at the Railroad Gap lookout above Tiller," the year before the 1933 build date in the registers.[^1][^3]

The register describes a 15-foot pole L-4 tower built in 1933, and the Forest Lookouts page gives the same year and cab type.[^1][^3]

In August 1935 the News-Review reported that Ermil Chaney was working for the Forest Service at the Railroad Gap lookout.[^3] In July 1941 the paper said a Mr. Ulam had gone to the lookout for the fire season, and in August 1942 it said Mr. Redifer was stationed there by the Forest Service.[^3]

The lookout also served as an Air Warning Service post.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts page lists the post as active from August 24, 1942, until October 16, 1943, under the Roseburg Filter Center.[^3] The register says the tower was used all year as an AWS post in 1942.[^1] The two sources disagree on when that use began.[^1][^3]

In December 1949 the state forest patrol announced construction of a tower on Roundtop Mountain to replace the lookout at Railroad Gap.[^3] The register and the Forest Lookouts page both give 1951 as the year the tower was removed, while firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3]

A 2018 hiking report describes the Gap as a trail destination and says that a fire lookout once stood there.[^4] The same report describes a separate shelter cabin, built at the Gap in the 1930s and dedicated to the memory of a Forest Service packer.[^4] A large tree fell on it within roughly the previous decade, and only its concrete foundation remains.[^4] That foundation belongs to the shelter, not the lookout.[^4] The report reaches the Gap by the Cow Creek Trail from Forest Road 3232, and calls the summer round trip a 12-mile hike.[^4] No source we read says whether the trail is open to visitors now.
