Dutchman Butte is in Douglas County, about 21 miles southwest of Myrtle Creek.[^1][^2] In May 1925 a Roseburg newspaper reported that a lookout station had been built on the butte as a shared post for the Douglas and Coos county fire patrols.[^3]

The sources disagree about the first tower.[^1][^2][^3] An August 1934 note from the Umpqua forest supervisor to the regional forester describes a 10-foot tower built from native Douglas fir logs and resting on concrete blocks.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page says Civilian Conservation Corps men from Camp Bradford built a timber tower about 10 feet tall, with a 14-by-14 hip-roof cab, in 1936.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the site first had a 12-foot wooden live-in tower in 1936.[^1]

The Douglas Forest Protective Association ran the post.[^1] It paid to connect the lookout's telephone line to a Forest Service line in 1939, and by 1958 the lookout had a radio number.[^3] The register says the association staffed it when it was listed in 2000, and firelookout.com says it was staffed into the 2000s.[^1][^2]

In 1970 a three-legged steel tower replaced the old structure.[^1][^3] The register describes it as a 15-foot guyed tower that came from the U.S. Air Force.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page gives its height as 14 feet 8 inches, with a 14-by-14 flat-roofed cab.[^3] Its steel was recycled from a shut-down radar site on Fairview Peak above Cottage Grove.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon county list marks the site as gone.[^4] Firelookout.com says the structure stayed on site until the winter of 2026, but it does not say how it came down.[^2] No source we read describes the site today. A 1970 survey station description also notes a brass reference disk on the southeast foundation block of the old tower.[^3]
