Fredonyer Peak Lookout stands on Fredonyer Peak in Lassen County, California, just north of Eagle Lake.[^1][^2] The lookout sits at about 7,945 feet.[^1]

The register says Fredonyer was the first lookout to use this steel-pipe design. Five towers of the design have been erected, and a CDF engineer, Mike Plesha, designed the cab.[^1]

A local history says the Lassen County Board of Supervisors sought a lookout from the state forestry division in spring 1937.[^2] The division had no funds, so the plan was for the state and Lassen National Forest to supply materials and the Secret Valley CCC camp to supply labor, and everyone agreed.[^2] The CCC men had to build a road from Horse Lake to the summit, a telephone line was to be extended up to it, and the history says the job got done.[^2]

The sources disagree on the first build year. The register gives 1973 as the build year.[^1] A June 1946 newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says a lookout had been stationed on the peak by then.[^3] A 1952 photograph from the California Division of Forestry's records, now copied to the Internet Archive, shows a Fredonyer lookout.[^4]

In 1960 a time-lapse camera was installed at the lookout as part of a study by the State Forestry Division and the Weather Bureau, to photograph any cloud build-up staff could see.[^3] The item says radar was especially useful at night, when observers could not see, and that a radar system for early warning on lightning storms was set up around then.[^3]

CAL FIRE's 2011-2012 management plan, as quoted on the register's Dyer Mountain page, took Dyer off its list of historic buildings to preserve and put Fredonyer Peak on that list in its place.[^5] The plan notes that the state owns and controls Fredonyer.[^5]

The register says Bureau of Land Management observers staffed the lookout as of 2015, and that CAL FIRE's Lassen-Modoc Unit administers it.[^1] A 2023 local history says the lookout is still manned.[^2] Wikipedia describes an old fire lookout tower and a radio mast on the peak, reached by a dirt road.[^6] The sources do not say whether visitors may go up, so treat it as a working lookout and check with CAL FIRE before any visit.
