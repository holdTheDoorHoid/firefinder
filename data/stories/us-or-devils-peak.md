Devils Peak is in Clackamas County, on the Mount Hood National Forest, and the Forest Service administers the lookout.[^1] The first structure was a 12-by-12-foot log cabin with a cupola, built in 1924.[^1][^2] In 1933 an L-4 cab replaced it.[^1][^2]

The register dates the present cab, an L-4 with a catwalk on a 10-foot treated timber tower, to 1952.[^1] Firelookout.com instead calls the 1933 cab the present one, so the two sources disagree about when the current cab was built.[^2]

Firelookout.com reports that nobody has staffed the lookout in many years.[^2] The register's 2014 visit report also marks it as not staffed.[^1] Mountaineers says it is now decommissioned, and that volunteers maintain it.[^3] We found no record of a fire at the lookout in the sources we read.[^1][^2][^3]

The register recorded the tower on August 13, 2003.[^1] Its 2014 visit report said the tower needed paint, that some lower exterior boards needed replacing, and that the catwalk railing was a little loose in places.[^1]

Mountaineers says the lookout is open for public use. It can be used overnight on a first-come, first-served basis, with a wood stove, cots and shelving.[^3] Firelookout.com also says it is presently open to public use, though that page is undated.[^2]

The usual route is the Cool Creek Trail No. 794, about eight miles round trip with 3,200 feet of climbing, which starts on Still Creek Road.[^3] The Forest Service trail page, which is undated in our copy, says the trail is temporarily shut to the public, citing landslides and storm damage from December.[^4] A fire closure order for Clackamas River Ranger District lands was updated on September 16, 2026, and it runs until December 31, 2026, unless it is updated or rescinded first.[^5] Its boundary text does not name this trail, and we could not confirm whether it reaches this peak.[^5] Check with the Mount Hood National Forest before you go, and do not use a trail that is closed.
