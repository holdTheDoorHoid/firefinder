Leecher Mountain Lookout sits at roughly 5,000 feet in Okanogan County, on Okanogan National Forest land administered by the Forest Service's Methow Valley Ranger District.[^1][^2][^3] The summit has held several structures over the years.[^1][^2][^3] The oldest is a tree-top crow's nest, still standing about 1,000 feet south of the present tower. Sources date it to 1918 or 1920.[^1][^2][^3]

In 1921 a windmill tower with a six-by-six-foot cab went up, and a log house for living quarters followed in 1922.[^1][^2][^3] The log house was replaced by an L-4 cab in 1936, according to firelookout.com and the Methow Valley group.[^2][^3] NHLR tells it differently. It dates an L-4 on an 11-foot timber tower to 1941, and it says the windmill tower went to the Twisp Smokejumper Base that year.[^1] Firelookout.com and the Methow Valley group place the windmill tower's move to a smokejumper base in 1954, at the North Cascades base.[^2][^3]

The tower standing today went up in 1954, with an L-4 cab brought from Chiliwist Butte Lookout.[^1][^2][^3] Sources give its height as 40 or 41 feet.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Staffing has been uneven.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com says the lookout was used for emergency summer fire detection from the 1980s, and that restoration in 2009 was followed by full-time staffing in 2010.[^2] In 2014 Bill Austin, known as Lightning Bill, who had staffed Goat Peak for 19 seasons, was posted here.[^3] The accounts of that summer differ.[^3][^4] The Methow Valley group says the Carlton Complex fire surrounded the lookout and Austin was evacuated by helicopter. WTA says he was moved back to Goat Peak before the season ended.[^3][^4]

Both the lookout and the crow's nest came through the 2014 fire, which burned trees around them.[^3] The tower was restored in 2009.[^3] The NHLR registered the lookout on October 14, 2004, and says it has been recently renovated.[^1]

To reach the trailhead, head south on Highway 153 past Twisp and turn left onto Benson Creek Drive. After about 10 miles, turn right onto spur Road 100 and go 3 more miles to a parking area at a locked gate.[^4] The hike is listed at 2.4 miles round trip with 531 feet of climb.[^4] WTA lists either a Northwest Forest Pass or an America the Beautiful Pass for parking. It also notes that a ranger may or may not be on the summit.[^4]
