The Viola Fire Tower stood in Kent County, Delaware, along the Viola-Petersburg road, about one and a half miles west of Viola.[^1][^5] Civilian Conservation Corps men erected it in the summer of 1935, and it entered service that fall.[^3][^5]

The tower was first designed at 100 feet.[^1] The register gives its total height after modifications as 123 feet.[^1] The state's 1936 report describes adding a 13 1/2-foot section, which would bring the cabin floor to 113 feet and the top of the hurricane deck rail to 123 feet, but it describes that work as still planned.[^5] The association's page says an added section raised the tower to 113 feet in 1937.[^3] The state's 1937 report, however, says the section was inserted during the summer of 1936.[^5]

Like other Delaware towers, the cab roof doubled as an observation deck, called the hurricane deck, reached through a trap door in the ceiling.[^1]

The 1936 report says the first cabin, with a peaked top and steel sash, proved impractical for the local climate, and the state planned to convert it to a beacon-top design with sliding wood sash.[^5] A 1955 report says sliding aluminum-frame windows with blur glass were installed in the tower.[^5]

The register listed the tower on October 15, 1993, and the Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site on September 11, 2008.[^1][^2]

The register and the association both say the tower was removed between 2004 and 2006.[^1][^3] The association's Delaware page says all five of the state's towers went to the State Police for communications and were removed between 2002 and 2006.[^4] The tower-specific pages give the narrower span, so this story uses 2004 to 2006.

The sources we read do not describe what, if anything, remains of the tower on its site today, or whether the public can visit it.
