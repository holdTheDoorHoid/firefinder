Mount Elwell, a peak above the Lakes Basin in the Plumas National Forest, had a small fire lookout by the mid-1910s.[^1][^2][^3] The sources do not agree on the year it went up. The register's Built field says circa 1918, but its own description says the cabin was "constructed circa 1915."[^1] A 1916 newspaper mentions a summer worker at the "government lookout station" there,[^2] and a Lakes Basin guide counts Elwell among the first lookouts, dating it to 1910.[^3] The register describes an observation cabin about 8 by 8 feet, and says a 1914 article expected it to be built of galvanized iron.[^1]

The lookouts show up in the local papers. Daniel Morehead, of Belden, entered on his duties as lookout in June 1923, and a May 1924 notice said he would pass his third year on the job.[^2] Mollie Ingoldsby had registered about 650 visitors by August 1920, and a 1936 notice remembers her as one of the few women to hold the job.[^2] In 1925 a lookout named McCullough was struck by lightning while he was at the telephone.[^2]

In 1931 Victor R. Hake phoned in a fire report from the station.[^4] Hake later wrote that he would be the last to serve on Elwell and the first on Mills Peak.[^4]

The register says a new lookout at Mills Peak replaced Elwell as the main fire-detection site in 1933 or 1934, and that the Elwell structure was removed sometime after 1933.[^1] A Lakes Basin guide puts the switch in 1932.[^3] The Mills Peak register entry says its first lookout was built in 1933.[^5]

Today the summit is a hike. The Lakes Basin guide describes it as three miles from the campground, with a climb of about 1,500 feet.[^3] The guide also says some of the old tower can still be seen near the peak, and that bits of its metal roof still lie among the boulders at the summit.[^3] Mills Peak, the station that replaced it, still has a lookout posted for most of the summer.[^3]
