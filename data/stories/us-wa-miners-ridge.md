Miners Ridge Lookout sits on a grassy knoll inside the Glacier Peak Wilderness, in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1][^4] Lookout use at the site goes back at least to 1928, when Forest Service files log a fire report from the ridge.[^1]

The tower that stands there now is 20 feet tall and carries a 14-by-14-foot cabin built to the Forest Service's L-4 plan, with a catwalk.[^1] The nomination dates the tower to 1938, when it replaced an older lookout house, and says the cabin followed a plan revised in 1936. It names Claude Brazelton and Hugh Ritter as builders, possibly with a Civilian Conservation Corps crew.[^1]

Sources disagree on the date. The nomination says 1938, while the National Historic Lookout Register gives 1953 for the tower standing today.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com gives both years, 1938 for the replacement and 1953 for the present tower.[^3]

The nomination says the lookout has been staffed during high fire danger since it was first built, and that volunteers now do the job.[^1] Firelookout.com names Tom and Suzanne Brown as lookout personnel in 1973.[^3] One visitor comment on the register's page says two National Park Service rangers staff it, but that conflicts with the volunteer accounts and is unverified.[^2]

In 1951, district ranger Warren Pressentin fell through the lookout's floor hatch about 20 feet. A helicopter sent to help him crashed while landing on the ridge nearby. Everyone survived, and the stairway was moved in 1952 so people enter through the catwalk instead.[^1]

The register reports 65 years of activity, so the lookout is still in use.[^2] The nomination says steady use and relative isolation have kept it maintained and less exposed to vandalism, and the old fuselage from the crash remains near the lookout.[^1] The register listed it on January 20, 1994.[^2]

Access is by trail, and the sources disagree on how far it is. The Forest Service describes a round trip of about 26.5 miles from the Suiattle Trailhead, reached by a 23.5-mile drive on Suiattle River Road.[^4] The register describes a 12-mile hiking trail, and the nomination puts the nearest trailhead 16 miles away.[^2][^1] The Forest Service page lists the site as open and says no fee is required, but it also shows active alerts for a Suiattle River Road washout closure and a fire closure order. Check those before you go.[^4]
