Nose Mountain is a steel fire lookout tower in Alberta.[^2] The annual reports of the Department of Lands and Mines, as transcribed by the Canada Lookouts index, file the site under the Grande Prairie Division.[^1]

In the 1947–48 fiscal year, a steel tower bought for the site was at Grande Prairie, and a tentative location for the tower had been picked.[^1] In 1948–49 a new steel lookout tower was erected on Nose Mountain, with a cabin and an engine house at the site.[^1] A bulldozed road about 64 km (40 mi) long was cut to reach the tower.[^1] Steel had come into use for Alberta's lookout towers in the 1920s, and it gradually displaced wood.[^4]

Alberta says each of its lookout sites has an observer on duty all day, usually from April to October.[^3] The province's wildfire layer records the present tower as built in 2004, 18 m (59 ft) tall, and marks it active.[^2]

The layer lists the access mode as drive, which suggests a road reaches the site.[^2] No source read describes what, if anything, remains of the 1948–49 cabin, engine house or road today. No public visiting arrangement turned up, so treat the tower as a working fire-detection site rather than a place to visit.
