Cameron Hill in Harnett County is recorded as the first fire lookout tower the North Carolina Forest Service erected.[^1] The state's 1925–26 biennial report describes a wooden lookout tower, 60 feet tall, put up on the hill, and says a telephone line would link it to the surrounding country so that a fire spotted by the lookout could be reached quickly.[^1] By September 1926 The Forest Worker reported the tower complete. Its corner supports were 60-foot juniper poles, and an 8-by-8-foot cabin, glassed on all sides, sat on top.[^1] Federal cooperative money and local Cape Fear Forest Protective Association members' dues together covered the cost of the tower and about 50 miles of connecting telephone lines.[^1] On reasonably clear days the cabin commanded a view 15 to 17 miles out, and the average effective range was put at about 10 miles.[^1]

Sources disagree on the first tower's height. A 1926–28 state report gives 65 feet rather than 60.[^1] In August 1938 a 100-foot creosoted wood tower was erected on the hill. A 1940 state report credits the work to SCS Camp 18 as a cooperative project, while the lookout-history page credits the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^1] In 1951 a steel tower replaced the earlier wooden one.[^1]

In March 1965 an Air Force C-130 released a three-quarter-ton truck at 1,500 feet, and the truck came down about 200 feet from the fire tower. A Statesville newspaper report, as the lookout-history page quotes it, says William Holder was in the tower with his wife at the time.[^1]

A state list calls the older site "Cameron Hill (old)" and places it at 35.28027, -79.09888. A second entry, "Cameron Hill," sits at 35.27972, -79.10749. The sources do not say which entry matches the tower described above.[^2] The lookout-history page names the North Carolina Division of Forest Resources as the agency.[^1]

No source we found describes what stands on Cameron Hill today, or whether the public can reach the site.
