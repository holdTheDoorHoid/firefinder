The Moscow tower in Polk County, Texas, was a four-legged steel frame with a house on top.[^1] A National Geodetic Survey station description, made by the Coast and Geodetic Survey in 1949, places it about seven miles southwest of Corrigan and about five miles west of the village of Moscow, on the highest point nearby.[^1] The record gives a total height of 110 feet, with the survey sighting taken on the peak of the house's pyramidal roof.[^1]

The survey record is the earliest dated source we found for the tower, and it shows the tower already standing in 1949.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page that carries the description also names the Texas Forest Service.[^1]

A Texas lookout-tower list places a tower called Moscow at the same coordinates as the Loc 1 entry, in Polk County.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas index marks the Moscow (Loc 1) tower as gone.[^2] The same index lists a second Moscow entry, Loc 2, which this story does not cover.[^2]

No source we read describes what remains at the Moscow site today, or whether the site is open to the public.[^1][^2][^3]
