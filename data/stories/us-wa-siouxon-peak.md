Siouxon Peak is in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest in Washington, where a fire lookout once stood.[^1] The first lookout here was a cupola cabin, probably finished in the summer of 1921.[^2] An October 1921 newspaper item, quoted by WillhiteWeb, said the Columbia National Forest in Washington was the most thoroughly equipped with lookouts of any forest in its district. With the Siouxon Peak lookout finished, it had nine stations, and another house was being built that winter.[^2] Other pages only date the cabin to the 1920s.[^1][^3] A heading on firelookout.com gives the years as 1931 to the 1960s, which does not match the 1921 item.[^4]

Panorama photographs taken on July 31, 1937 show a phone line, a flagpole, a stovepipe, a weather station and a shed.[^2]

In August 1927, crews were pulled out of the Siouxon fire district ahead of the flames.[^2] A newspaper report that month expected the Siouxon, Gumboot and Lookout Mountain stations to burn. It also said the instruments had been removed and the houses abandoned.[^2][^5]

In 1935 an L-4 cab replaced the cupola.[^1][^4][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register cannot say when the L-4 was destroyed.[^1] WillhiteWeb's last image of the site is from 1964, and it does not know when the cab came down.[^2] Oregon Hikers thinks the tower was most likely torn down in the 1960s.[^3]

Today only remains are reported on the summit.[^2][^3] WillhiteWeb's visit notes, with photos dated November 20, 2021, mention a hook anchor, an old stovepipe tossed down the hillside, phone-line pegs still in the trees, a flat spot cut for the shed, and an old privy and garbage pit reached by a short spur trail.[^2]

Reaching the summit is a hike of about 2 miles with 1,500 feet of gain, and the short trail to the lookout has no signage.[^2] Some access roads need high clearance because of berms.[^2]
