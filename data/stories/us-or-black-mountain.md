Black Mountain is a peak in the Ochoco National Forest, about 37 miles east-northeast of Prineville, in Crook County.[^1][^2]

Firelookout.com describes a 70-foot crow's nest tree platform, built in 1937, and says it is still there.[^1] The same page says the frame cabin that went with the platform was moved to the Ochoco Ranger Station.[^1]

A photographer's page from about 2001 disputes parts of that account.[^4] It says 1931 photos show a Black Mountain lookout that was a tree tower, where the lookout climbed a ladder up a pine each day and sat on a small open platform.[^4] It also says a 1940 date in Ray Kresek's book, under the name "Black," may be the year the lookout was abandoned.[^4] The same page is not sure where the cabin came from, and says aerial views of the 5,326-foot peak it considered show no evidence of a cabin foundation.[^4]

A 2023 Central Oregonian article says a small cabin along the road near the office at the old Ochoco Ranger Station site is the Black Mountain Lookout Cabin.[^3] The article gives the site's distance from Prineville as about 25 miles south in one passage and about 25 miles east in another.[^3] The site now has information kiosks, picnic areas and day-use areas.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list calls the site Abandoned.[^2] We found no recent report on the summit itself, so the claim that the platform is still there rests on firelookout.com alone.[^1] The summit is on Ochoco National Forest land.[^1] We found no source saying whether it is open to visitors.
