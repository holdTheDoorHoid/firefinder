The Lakeland tower stood in Shelby County, on ground the Tennessee Landforms list puts at 397 feet, about 121 meters.[^1] The list gives it the code 9-19 and ties it to the survey mark FE2549.[^1] A National Register study says that from 1933 to 1975 the Division of Forestry's fire towers were the visible part of its fire detection system, and this tower's code places it in that system.[^1][^3]

The National Geodetic Survey datasheet for FE2549 calls the point "Brunswick Lot" and records its marker as a lookout tower.[^2] Both records carry the mark FE2549 and place it in Shelby County, so the two most likely describe the same place.[^1][^2]

The datasheet's 1958 description says the survey station sat at the apex of a roof on top of the tower. The steel support was four-legged, painted silver, and about 89 feet above the ground.[^2]

A 1958 recovery note adds that a new tower was going up 22.2 feet from the old one. It says the old tower would be destroyed once the new one was finished, with completion expected by January 1959, and that the mark should then be considered destroyed.[^2]

The Landforms list now marks the tower as removed.[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Lakeland site today, or whether the public can reach it. The 1958 datasheet also includes road directions, but they are more than six decades old and do not show whether the land is open.[^2] The list says most former tower locations are now private property, though it does not name this one.[^1] Visitors should ask the landowner before going onto any private land.
