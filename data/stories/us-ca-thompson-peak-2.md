Thompson Peak, in Tuolumne County on the Stanislaus National Forest, carried an enclosed tower.[^1][^2] A Peakbagging photo page says the tower was probably built in 1936, and adds that no other information is available.[^3]

In August 1939 a lookout-fireman named Carpenter was on duty at the tower.[^1] He noticed a reflection from a shining object close to a logging railroad about half a mile from the tower, then a tiny wisp of smoke. He reported it and was sent to investigate. He found a blaze about two feet across in the grass, and at its center a broken piece of window glass. The item, which the Forest Lookouts page reprints from the California Ranger, says sunlight focused through that glass started the fire. It adds that this is the first Stanislaus National Forest fire certainly traced to focused sunlight.[^1]

The California list marks the tower as gone, and the Forest Lookouts page carries the status "Removed."[^1][^2]

A Peakbagging photo taken on September 17, 2011 shows the foundation of the tower.[^3] We found no later source about the site, and none says whether it is open to visitors.[^1][^2][^3]

The California list also shows a separate Thompson Peak in Plumas County as a standing enclosed tower, so the two should not be confused.[^2]
