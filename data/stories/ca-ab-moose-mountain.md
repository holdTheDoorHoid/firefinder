Moose Mountain sits about 19 kilometres west of Bragg Creek, in Kananaskis Country.[^1] The first lookout structure on its summit went up in 1929, according to a history on the Canada Lookouts site, which draws on a 1998 guidebook.[^2] A fire road was built to reach it that same year, a local trail guide says.[^3]

A second lookout was completed in 1953, and a third structure went up in 1974.[^2] A 1937 Calgary Herald article, quoted on the same history page, says the cabin was built of lumber and anchored to the summit by cables that were secured to iron clasps set in eight cement blocks.[^2] A telephone line joined the forestry stations, so word of a fire could reach them at once.[^2]

In May 1930 the Calgary Herald reported that the supervisor of the Bow River forest reserve expected his men to staff this post and four others again.[^2] A Winnipeg Free Press item dated July 31, 1929, also quoted on that page, says a ranger from the lookout spotted a blaze and hurried to it, and an oil crew helped check it.[^2] By 1937, Dexter Champion had already put in three and a half seasons at the lookout.[^2] In dangerously dry weather, he had at times gone up to two months without leaving the lookout.[^2] Supplies reached the lookout from Canmore each month.[^2]

A 2026 Rocky Mountain Outlook feature describes an observer who spends the peak fire season on the summit, where the lookout is one of four active towers in Kananaskis.[^4] A Wikipedia photo caption shows the lookout and a helipad at the top.[^1]

Visitors can no longer reach the summit.[^3] A Bragg Creek guide says the Kananaskis Administration closed the single path up the scree slope, and that a forestry officer is stationed in the lookout from May through September.[^3] The provincial government says its lookout sites are generally staffed from April to October.[^5] The lookout is still an active fire lookout.[^4] Please do not try to reach the summit.
