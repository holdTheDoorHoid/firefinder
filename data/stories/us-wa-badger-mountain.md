The Badger Mountain lookout was set a little below the crest of a high mesa in Douglas County, Washington.[^1][^3] Sources place it seven or eight miles north-northeast of Wenatchee.[^2][^3]

According to the Register, an 18-foot wooden lookout with an L-4 cab went up in 1941. It served the Wenatchee National Forest from a spot on the other side of the Columbia River until the 1980s, even though the site was not on national forest land.[^1]

A 2022 visitor's account says the cab was first built in the 1930s by the Civilian Conservation Corps for a lookout called Lion Rock, and was later moved to Badger Mountain.[^4] A 1930 newspaper item already describes a lookout station on Badger Mountain, so the sources do not agree on when a station first stood there.[^3]

The 1930 item reports that Richard Basset was hired to staff the station that season.[^3] The Firelookout listing names Barbara Calder for 1946 to 1947, George Poling for 1948 to 1950, and Oscar Richardson for 1953 to 1974.[^2] In 1961 a newspaper item reported a low bid to build an unmanned radar gap-filler facility near the Badger Mountain Lookout Station.[^3]

When the lookout stood, access ran through private property.[^1] The cab went to the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center at Entiat in 1999.[^1] The sources do not say what became of the tower itself, and we found no source that describes the mountain site today.

The center lies north of Entiat along Highway 97A today.[^5] Its history says the center grew out of a wish to rescue an old Forest Service lookout and move it down from the mountains to a highway with more traffic.[^5] On a 2022 visit the building was closed, but the parking area and the half-mile interpretive trail were open, and the trail features three relocated lookouts.[^4] Visit Wenatchee describes the center as built around two lookout towers, so the count differs by source.[^6][^4] Check the center's hours before you visit.
