Red Mountain, also called Signal Peak, lies near Cisco Grove in Nevada County, California.[^2][^3] The register claims it is California's oldest fire-detection site.[^1] In 1876 the Central Pacific Railroad set up a fire lookout just below Signal Peak, to protect against fires in the railroad's snow sheds along the grade.[^1]

The first structure was wooden.[^1] In 1909 the Southern Pacific replaced it with a stone cabin, which the register says was staffed round the clock by two fire watchers.[^1]

Newspapers reported two incidents from the station's working years. In September 1920 the lookout saw a motorcycle rider fall from the grade into the Yuba River, flashed word to Cisco, and a rescue party hauled him out with ropes.[^2] A January 1931 report from a Reno newspaper said a lightning strike on his remote cabin had apparently killed the man stationed there.[^2]

The railroad stopped using the lookout in 1934.[^1][^3]

A 2023 sketching blog says the stone lookout was visible across the highway from a stop at the Cisco exit off Highway 80. The post says it was built in 1909 and used until 1934.[^4]

The register lists the site as administered by the Tahoe National Forest and dates its registration to April 2, 2020.[^1] I found no current access rules in the sources I read, so keep to public areas and check with the Tahoe National Forest before going near the building.
