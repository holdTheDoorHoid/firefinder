Mount Annie is in Okanogan County, Washington, within the Okanogan National Forest.[^1][^2] A road was under construction up the mountain in 1933, and a lookout tower went up that year with a log cabin at its base.[^1][^3][^4] A newspaper said in October 1933 that the tower was being framed to be raised.[^4]

In 1934 Mr. and Mrs. Vernon Wills of Tonasket staffed the tower for its first summer.[^3][^4]

Sources disagree on the height. The register and firelookout.com give 65 feet, while Willhite gives 60 feet, the same figure a 1933 newspaper planned.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

In 1956 rot was found in one tower leg, which was hollow.[^3] The tower came down in 1957.[^3] The register says it was removed and never replaced, while firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed.[^1][^2][^3]

The site has no views today, because the forest has grown up.[^3] Willhite's account of a 2020 visit includes photos of a building foundation, footings and woodpiles.[^3]

The road leaves Highway 20 and is signed.[^3] Willhite reports two gates about six miles from the highway. He says the left gate road is usually locked, though the road was open during a recent timber sale.[^3] Beyond the gate he describes good gravel to another gate two miles below the summit, then fallen trees across the road.[^3] The land is National Forest, administered by the U.S. Forest Service, and no source states visiting rules for today.[^1]
