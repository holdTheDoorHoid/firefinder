Westmoreland had a Tennessee state fire lookout tower in Sumner County. A list of Tennessee's towers gives it the number 6-1 and lists it at 991 feet.[^1] The numbers on that list are state forest tower numbers, written as district and tower, and they come from a 1976 list.[^1]

The towers were part of the Tennessee Division of Forestry's fire-detection network.[^2] A National Register study says the towers gave that network its physical form between 1933 and 1975.[^2] The study says surviving records are too sparse to establish how many towers went up in the 1930s, or where.[^2] It also cites a report that the Civilian Conservation Corps built 98 lookout houses and towers in Tennessee during that decade.[^2]

The list marks the Westmoreland tower as removed.[^1] The study reports that many of the 208 towers the network once had have since been removed or are awaiting demolition. It names two reasons: the towers fell out of use, and agreements to use privately owned land ran out.[^2]

No source found describes what, if anything, remains at the Westmoreland site today, or whether the public can reach it. Visitors should not assume a tower is still standing there.

[^1]: Tennessee Lookout Towers, tnlandforms.us (Tom Dunigan), https://tnlandforms.us/towers/ (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Tennessee Division of Forestry Fire Lookout Towers, 1933 to 1975, National Register of Historic Places multiple property documentation, National Park Service, https://npgallery.nps.gov/pdfhost/docs/NRHP/Text/64501237.pdf (accessed 2026-10-21).
