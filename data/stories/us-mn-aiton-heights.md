The Aiton Heights fire tower stands in Itasca State Park in Clearwater County, and the Minnesota Department of Natural Resources administers it.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the state Division of Forestry first erected the tower in the 1920s, then moved it to Itasca State Park in 1937, where it was given two jobs: teaching visitors and spotting fires.[^1][^2]

The sources disagree about its height. The Register gives 80 feet.[^1] A 2024 visitor account says the whole tower is 100 feet (30 m) high, including the cab, and a federal survey station description for the same park tower, listed as Alton Heights, says it is about 100 feet.[^2][^4] The same visitor account says the stairway inside has 135 steps.[^2]

The Register also mentions a CCC ground house that was occupied until 1957 and was later moved to serve a boat launch.[^1]

Spotters in the tower watched for smoke.[^3] The park's exhibit on its fire spotters says women made up a good number of them in the 1950s through the 1970s.[^3] Former spotters recall long days up in the tower and reporting fires by two-way radio.[^3]

The Register says the tower remains in service, with the park's interpreter attached to it.[^1] A 2024 visitor account describes a climb to the cab and a walk south from the tower on the Deer Lake Trail, which shows the tower could be climbed at that time.[^2] Check the park's current hours before you go.
