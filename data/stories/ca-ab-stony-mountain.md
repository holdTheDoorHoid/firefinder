Stony Mountain is a steel fire lookout in northern Alberta.[^1] The register places it at 56.39 N, 111.28 W, and gives 2014 as the year the present structure was built.[^1] Its height is recorded as 30 m (98 ft), and its ground is 762.35 m (2,501 ft) above sea level.[^1]

Alberta says its lookouts are located where visibility is favourable to detect and report wildfires.[^2] Each of the province's 127 lookout sites has a lookout observer, who watches all day, generally from April to October.[^3] Alberta's history of its lookouts says the towers on high boreal hills raised the observer above anything blocking the view.[^4] Seasonal staff hired for fire season also kept up the trails and phone lines and recorded the weather.[^4] The register lists a weather station at this site.[^1]

The register lists the tower as active.[^1]

The register codes the access mode as drive, which suggests a road reaches the site.[^1] Alberta describes lookout sites as isolated, and says staff working them need to plan for a wide range of contingencies.[^3]

No source describes the site today, including what stands on the ground or whether visitors may go up the tower.
