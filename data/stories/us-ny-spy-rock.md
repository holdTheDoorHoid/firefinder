Spy Rock sits in the Black Rock Forest in Orange County, New York.[^1] Its lookout was a 20-foot wooden tower of chestnut poles with iron straps, set in the rock face.[^1] The association's record says it was put up on a spot used for bonfire signals during the Revolutionary War period.[^1]

The record adds that the tower probably also watched for wildfires on the private estate.[^1] The owner later gave the estate to his alma mater, Harvard University, and the record says it then joined the Black Rock Forest consortium.[^1]

The record dates the tower's removal to the 1970s.[^1] The association's New York list now shows the Spy Rock site as a gone platform tower.[^4]

Black Rock Forest's geology page puts the Spy Rock summit at 1,461 feet above sea level, the tallest point in the Highlands west of the Hudson.[^2] The association's record gives the lookout site as 1,433 feet, or 437 meters.[^1] The two figures differ, so treat the elevation with care.[^1][^2]

A 2010 blog account describes a blue-blazed trail, about 0.15 miles long, that leaves the Scenic trail and ends at the rock.[^3] The writer notes a struggling pine on the rock and mentions no tower.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the rock today.

Black Rock Forest is a privately owned preserve, and the 2010 visitor reached the rock on its marked trails. The same account says driving the forest's roads, other than the entrance road, needed permission, so check the current rules before you go.[^3]
