A fire-watch station on Sugar Grove Knob was in service by 1916, when a state report described it with a watchman, O.O. Nutter.[^2] He lived half a mile away and stayed at the station at night only in extremely dry weather.[^2] By 1926 a tower stood on the knob and was linked to National Forest telephone lines.[^2]

The register says the West Virginia Division of Forestry built the tower, but it gives no construction date.[^1] Its photos include an earlier tower made entirely of wood, though the register does not say when the change came.[^1]

A 1947 newspaper item, quoted on a forest-lookout history site, said O.O. Nutter, a retired district forester, was in charge of the fire tower.[^2] The sources we read do not name any particular fire the tower helped watch for.

The register says the tower was sold to the landowner, and it now lists a private owner, formerly the Division of Forestry.[^1] It does not say when the sale happened.[^1] West Virginia's forest fire towers were closed in 1990, and the state encyclopedia says fewer than a dozen remain standing, none in service.[^4]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists this tower as standing under US 1678, WV 26.[^3] The register was filed on July 16, 2022, nominated by Robert Beanblossom, and includes a photo dated 2022.[^1] The register names only the private owner and says nothing about visits, so treat the tower as private property and do not climb it.[^1]

Sources disagree on the county. The register places the tower in Greenbrier County,[^1] while a West Virginia lookout list and a forest-lookout history site both say Nicholas County.[^2][^3]
