Flower Point is in Lincoln County, Montana, about two miles southwest of Libby.[^1] The page lists the Kootenai National Forest as its agency and gives an elevation of 4,260 feet.[^1]

A news item from September 11, 1930, quoted on Rex's Fire Lookout Page, reports a new station completed the week before by crews of the Libby district.[^2] The station sat on top of a tall pine, 88 feet from the ground, and the item says it was the highest station on the forest.[^2] The item names Harry Cooper as the observer on duty there, whose job was to spot fires across the district.[^2]

The firelookout.com page gives a more tangled history. It says the present platform, atop a 60-foot pine, could date from as early as 1921 or as late as 1931.[^1] It also says a frame cabin was built in 1931, and that a 50-foot pole L-4 tower about an eighth of a mile east went up in 1934 and was destroyed in the late 1940s.[^1]

The two accounts disagree on height: 88 feet in 1930 against a 60-foot pine on the firelookout.com page.[^1][^2] The sources do not say whether they describe the same structure.[^1][^2]

The Fire Lookout Association's Montana list marks the site as gone.[^3] A hiker's trip report dated October 3, 2020, describes the lookout tower as in shambles, with trees over the summit.[^4]

The same report describes a hike of about a mile from the Flower Creek trailhead, reached by the gravel Flower Creek Road (FR-128), with the trail fading near the top.[^4] It says to expect no view from the summit.[^4]

The sources we read do not say whether the site is officially open, so check with the Kootenai National Forest, the agency the page lists, before you go.[^4][^1]
