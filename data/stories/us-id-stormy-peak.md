According to the National Historic Lookout Register, a tent camp was activated on Stormy Peak in 1920.[^1] The register says a cab was built on the Salmon-Challis National Forest in 1928 to replace it.[^1] It describes a modified L-4 cab on a one-story log base.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page and the Idaho Fire Lookouts site describe a two-story log base with an R-4 cab instead, and firelookout.com calls it a two-story wooden cab.[^3][^5][^4]

Newspaper items quoted on the Forest Lookouts page name some of its staff.[^3] A 1935 item says Pete Burton left for the lookout, and a 1950 item says high frequency radio had been installed there.[^3] A 1951 item says Oscar Schmunk and his wife were stationed on the peak.[^3] A 1956 item says Al Clary manned the lookout, and that he and his wife, Wilma, planted a small vegetable garden.[^3] The Idaho Fire Lookouts site lists Susan Hunter for 1977 to 1978.[^5]

The register gives a registration date of September 24, 2011.[^1]

In 2022 the Moose Fire reached the lookout.[^1] The register says it was reported lost on July 25, 2022.[^1] A 2022 chapter report says the fire ran fast along the Salmon River, and that the logbook and fire finder had been pulled out a day before the lookout was destroyed.[^2] The report adds that it was not wrapped or protected by sprinklers, since other higher-priority historical structures were in danger.[^2] A local news report says the radio repeater on the site was also lost, but that the logbook was salvaged.[^6]

The Idaho Fire Lookouts site lists it as a hike-in, and the 2022 report says its trail access was poor and that it was rarely visited because of its remote location.[^5][^2] Because it burned, there is no lookout left to climb.[^5] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the site today.
