Firelookout.com says the site was first set up in 1908 as a lookout called Bald Mtn., with a wooden cab on the ground as its first structure.[^3] The cab that stands now dates to 1933, when an L-4 cab went up on a tower base.[^1][^2][^3][^4] The register describes a standard L-4 cab on an enclosed 10-foot tower.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts describes an L-4 cab on an 8-foot log crib for that year.[^2] The sources disagree on the base height: the register and firelookout.com say 10 feet, while Idaho Fire Lookouts says 8.[^1][^2][^3]

The 1960s brought a change. Idaho Fire Lookouts says the L-4 was altered and placed on a concrete basement in that decade.[^2] The same site says the lookout was moved to emergency service in the 2000s.[^2]

The staffing record is thin. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Linda Bellmer Bass as relief staff from 1974 to 1982, citing a former fire lookout registry book.[^2] Firelookout.com says the lookout is staffed on an as-needed basis.[^3] The register says it can be used in emergencies.[^1]

The register added Thorn Creek Butte on September 28, 2020, as US 1431, ID 123, and lists the Boise National Forest as its administrator.[^1]

Idaho Fire Lookouts describes the site as drive-up and staffed as needed.[^2] The sources do not describe public entry into the cab or give a route to it, so check with the Boise National Forest before you go.[^1][^2] I found no rental listing for this cab.
