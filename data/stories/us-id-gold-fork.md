The register and Idaho Fire Lookouts describe Gold Fork's first lookout as an open platform on a rock ledge, reached by a long staircase.[^3][^2] The register says it began operating around 1918 to 1920.[^3][^2] Firelookout.com instead describes a log platform atop a rock, so the sources differ on how it was built.[^1]

In 1933 an L-4 cabin on a concrete base went up about two miles from the first site.[^3][^2] Forest Lookouts quotes a 1933 note saying it was built at a more accessible spot.[^4]

Forest Lookouts quotes two old newspaper notes about the station. A 1935 item from the Payette Valley Sentinel says Mr. and Mrs. Wayne Barney spent that summer there.[^4] A 1945 Idaho Statesman item names Edna Cantwell of Boise as a staffer working from Cascade.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts shows photographs taken in August 1955 by Bluford W. Muir, with lookout Milton Greenlee and Ranger LeRoy Sprague at the tower.[^2]

The register says the lookout eventually went out of service, and in 1988 the cabin moved to the Crawford Forest Work Center northeast of Cascade.[^3] Firelookout.com instead says it went to a spot two miles east of Cascade for private use, and Forest Lookouts says it went to the Crawford Ranger Station.[^1][^4] The original platform site is listed as gone.[^5]

The latest placement in the sources is a 2012 register photo caption, which puts the cabin at the Crawford Forest Work Center.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts marks it drive-up and notes that it was added to the register in 2020.[^2] The register says the Boise National Forest administers it.[^3] No source we read says whether the public may visit the work center, so ask the forest before you go. The sources also give two elevations: 4,889 feet on the register, and 7,790 feet on firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts.[^3][^1][^2]
