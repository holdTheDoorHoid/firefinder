The Nordrum Mountain lookout stood in King County, Washington, about 11 miles northeast of North Bend, within the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service administered it.[^1]

The Register dates the first structure on the site, a cabin, to the early 1930s.[^1] WillhiteWeb cites the Kresek inventory for a cabin in the same period.[^3] By 1934 a 20-foot L-4 tower had replaced it.[^1][^2][^3] WillhiteWeb says logging on the Middle Fork reached the Taylor River by the end of the 1920s, and that the fire threat made a lookout worth building.[^3] The guide says the lookout's name honors Martin Nordrum, a pioneer whose home lay up Quartz Creek.[^3]

WillhiteWeb says CCC Camp F-85 built a garage at the site in 1940.[^3] The Forest Lookouts site quotes a 1940 letter about starting construction of that garage.[^4] During the Second World War, WillhiteWeb says, the lookout was kept in use all year as an aircraft spotting post.[^3] The Forest Lookouts site gives Aircraft Warning Service dates of September 4, 1942, to October 16, 1943, at the Seattle Filter Center.[^4] A photo caption on the guide refers to a May 1, 1944 document that shows an inactivation date.[^3] The two sources give different end dates, and we did not reconcile them.

Both the Register and the Fire Lookout site say the tower was removed in the 1950s.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says the whole lookout was taken away at some point in the 1950s.[^3]

WillhiteWeb describes a hike of about a mile from the Middle Fork Snoqualmie trailhead, with about 500 feet of gain.[^3] Its photo captions name footings and old tower timber and bolts at the site, and the captions for those photos carry no dates.[^3] The guide gives directions from North Bend along the Middle Fork Snoqualmie Valley Road to the trailhead, and it mentions no gate or permit.[^3]
