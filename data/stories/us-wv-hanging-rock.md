Hanging Rock Lookout stands on Peters Mountain in Monroe County, West Virginia.[^1][^2] The Gazette-Mail describes a weathered cabin on a rocky outcrop at the mountain's crest, while the register calls it a fire tower.[^2][^1]

The register says the West Virginia Division of Forestry built it in 1934. The Gazette-Mail says Civilian Conservation Corps volunteers built a hut for fire observers on the rock that year.[^1][^2] In 1956 the state built a larger lookout using U.S. Forest Service plans, and the register says it became one of only two state lookouts with a live-in cab.[^1][^3] The register's Built field lists 1997, which matches the rebuild year rather than the first build.[^1][^2]

The lookout was last used for fire detection in 1972. Afterward, bird watchers took on its upkeep.[^3][^1] A 2020 lookout report for West Virginia and Virginia still listed it among towers in partial service or ready stand-by, which does not fit that end date.[^4]

The Forest Service bought the tower and surrounding land in 1983.[^2] In February 1996 fire destroyed the structure.[^3] A Beckley newspaper reported little left but ashes, and authorities questioned four people; no charges were made public.[^3] Sources disagree on the rebuild. A lookout history site says a new 14-by-14 cedar cabin was finished in May 1996. The Gazette-Mail says it was rebuilt the following year, and the register gives 1999.[^3][^2][^1]

Forest Service staff found the decking and support arches unstable on November 7, 2024, and the building was closed.[^5][^2] Volunteers who maintained the building said the rotted floor joists were more than they could fix.[^6]

A September 19, 2026 report says the Forest Service will rebuild the observatory with federal funds.[^5] The existing building stays in place until a contract is awarded, and work could begin as soon as September 30, 2026.[^5]

As of that report, the trail up to the site is open, but the public may not enter the building, which is about a mile from the nearest parking lot.[^5] Please stay out of the closed building. Once construction starts, the whole area may be closed for safety.[^5]
