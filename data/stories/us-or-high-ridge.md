High Ridge Lookout stands in the Umatilla National Forest in Union County, Oregon, about 11 miles northwest of Elgin.[^1][^2] The site served as a daily horse-patrol lookout point from about 1928 to 1930.[^3] A 24-foot round timber tower with an L-4 cab went up there in 1941.[^3] Cheryl Hill's 2021 write-up gives the 1941 tower a height of 40 feet, so the earlier height is not settled.[^5] By 1957 the old tower needed two false legs to hold it up until a replacement could be built.[^3]

The present tower is a 67-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 flat cab.[^1][^2][^5] The Register and firelookout.com date it to 1959.[^1][^2] The Forest's own files, quoted on the history page, first planned it for the 1959 season and then said the work was delayed until 1960.[^3] A 1963 survey note on that page gives the tower's height as 100 feet.[^3]

Staffing changed over time. In 1949 the Pendleton District changed the site from a primary lookout to a lookout fireman position.[^3] A 1955 inspection report cautioned that advice to discontinue aging lookout towers needed more care, and it cited High Ridge as a case in point.[^3] In July 2005 a Register visit found the tower unstaffed that day, and the visitor said the staff were away at the time.[^1] Firelookout.com says it is staffed in the summer.[^2]

In July 2006 vandals broke in over the Fourth of July weekend and caused significant damage.[^3]

The Register says the tower was on standby for a time and is now in active service.[^1] Cheryl Hill's directory, as of July 2026, lists it as unmanned but accessible to the public.[^4] When Cheryl Hill visited in October 2021, the tower was no longer staffed and the catwalk was locked.[^5]

Cheryl Hill's directions run west on Road 31, then onto Roads 270 and 271.[^5] A gate on Road 271 may be closed and locked, in which case the tower is about a mile's walk up the road.[^5] The 2005 Register visit report says the visitor could not reach the very top.[^1] Visitors should treat the locked catwalk as closed.
