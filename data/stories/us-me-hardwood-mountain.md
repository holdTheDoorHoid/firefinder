The Hardwood Mountain tower site lies in a remote spot on the Canadian border, in Somerset County, Maine, in Township 9, Range 18.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts site files it under the Maine Forestry District.[^1]

The state built the tower in 1916.[^1] A November 1916 newspaper, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, called it substantial-looking and said it was built from local materials, which kept the cost down.[^1] A state forest commissioner's report from that year describes a new lookout station on the mountain.[^1] By 1920 the inventory listed a 60-foot steel tower.[^1]

The same newspaper said the tower was in the charge of Chief Warden W. F. Hinckley of Bangor. The 1916 report names a Hinckley as well, but with different initials, so the sources do not show whether they are the same man.[^1] We found no record of observers, named fires or day-to-day staffing for the tower.

The tower's current state is disputed. The Forest Lookouts list shows it as standing, and its asterisk means the cab is no longer in place.[^4] A 2016 chapter report says the tower had long been presumed gone, until one of the chapter's contacts went in and found it still standing.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register, which registered the site in 2008, says the tower is no longer standing.[^2] We found no date for when the cab came down.

Reaching it is the hard part. The 2016 report calls the tower very remote, and getting there meant a trip in.[^3] We found no public access statement. Do not climb the tower, and check with the state forest service before attempting the trip.
