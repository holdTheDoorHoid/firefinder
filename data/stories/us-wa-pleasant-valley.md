The Pleasant Valley lookout sat at the lip of a cliff about 70 feet high in Lewis County, at roughly 1,675 feet.[^1][^3] The register dates it to the 1930s and calls it a cabin.[^1] Eric Willhite, who visited in 2014, describes a tower and storage building that once stood there.[^3] Willhite's only information about the site comes from photographs taken in 1934.[^3] Panoramas dated July 14, 1934 are credited to the Pacific Northwest Region of the National Archives.[^1][^4] Willhite also holds panoramas shot from the Stahl Peak lookout, which overlook this site.[^3]

The lookout is gone. FireLookout.com records a 1930s cabin marked gone, and Forest Lookouts lists the site as removed.[^2][^4] On his 2014 visit, Willhite found only a fragment, because most of the structure had been taken away.[^3] Later visitors turned up more footings and anchor bolts.[^3]

Today the spot is a flattened clearing that resembles a logging landing, ringed by second-growth timber.[^3] Trees about 100 feet tall now screen the cliff edge from the valley below.[^3]

The register lists the Washington Division of Forestry and DNR as the administrator.[^1] Willhite describes a 1.5-mile hike on state and federal land as the legal way to reach the site.[^3] The route passes two locked gates on a minor forest road, with Tacoma Water land posted with No Trespassing signs to the right.[^3] If signs bar parking near the gates, Willhite advises parking on Pleasant Valley Road instead.[^3]

Willhite also notes future plans to log this area under the Flashback Timber Sale.[^3]
