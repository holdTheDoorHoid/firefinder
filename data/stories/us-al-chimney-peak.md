The register places the Chimney Peak Fire Lookout Tower on Choccolocco Mountain in Calhoun County, Alabama, near the Jacksonville State University planetarium.[^1] It is a 100-foot International Derrick tower with a metal 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1]

The register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it in the 1930s for the Alabama Forestry Commission, and it dates the tower to 1935.[^1] Newspaper items reprinted on the Forest Lookouts site tell a later story. In August 1947 a donor gave five acres on Chimney Peak, and the Anniston Star said a fire tower would be erected there.[^2] By August 1948 the steel had arrived, but erection had been held up by a steel shortage.[^2] On September 7, 1949, the tower was dedicated at Germanic Springs, north of Jacksonville. The paper described a steel tower 99 feet and 9 inches high, paid for by the State Department of Conservation, the Calhoun County Commission and private citizens of Jacksonville.[^2]

The two accounts cannot both be right about when the tower went up, and we could not settle the question from these sources. We have followed the newspapers for the construction dates and flagged the register's 1935 date for review.

We found no source naming anyone who staffed the lookout or any fire it watched. In October 1974 a reader asked why the tower looked abandoned, with its lines cut and its windows broken out. The Alabama Forest Service replied that the tower was still in use, but that repeated vandalism had forced it to remove equipment, including windows that had not been broken. The service said vandal repairs cost $300 to $1,000 a month, and that limited funds needed for fire control meant less money went to the tower's upkeep.[^2]

The register accepted the tower on January 15, 2009, on the nomination of Thomas Kaufmann, as US 759, AL 6.[^1] A February 2024 notice from the City of Jacksonville and Jacksonville State University says the two are working together to protect and preserve Chimney Peak.[^3] The same notice says a gate limits overnight access, the overlook is open to visitors daily from 5 a.m. to 9 p.m., and signs are planned on Mountain Street to tell drivers to turn back after hours.[^3] We found nothing saying whether the tower's cab is open to climbing, so visitors should treat the tower itself as off limits and enjoy the overlook during posted hours.
