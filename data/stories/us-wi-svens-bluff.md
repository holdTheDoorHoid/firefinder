Sven's Bluff, in Peninsula State Park, once carried a fire lookout tower.[^1] People called it Sven's Tower, after Sven Anondsen, a former sailor who once lived in the area.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's register lists the tower as gone.[^2]

A state report for 1915 and 1916 described two new lookout towers, one on Sven's Bluff and one on Eagle Bluff, meant to detect forest fires.[^3] Door County Pulse gives 1914 as the build year for both.[^3] The Department of Natural Resources says both were hand-built as fire lookouts.[^4] A historical marker gives the tower's years as 1915 to 1947, one year off from the 1914 build date, and the sources here do not explain the gap.[^1]

A ledger kept by A.E. Doolittle, who was the park's first manager, records payments to men who did fire watch work.[^3] The towers also became a draw for the county, and visitors climbed the stairs more often than forestry staff did.[^3]

The tower was dismantled in 1947.[^4] Door County Pulse says it came down that year and was not replaced.[^3] Park officials weighed building a replacement, but chose not to, since the bluff already offered a scenic view nearly 200 feet above Green Bay.[^3]

Today the spot is Sven's Bluff Overlook. The Door County Coastal Byway says the overlook has striking views of Green Bay and its shoreline.[^5] The Skyline Trail gives hikers and bicyclists access to the overlook, and drivers can park on Skyline Road nearby.[^5] A historical marker stands on the bluff within Peninsula State Park, which is a fee area.[^1] No source found describes any tower footings or other remains on the bluff.
