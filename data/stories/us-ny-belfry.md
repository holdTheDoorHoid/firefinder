Belfry Mountain rises in the Town of Moriah in Essex County, New York, near the old iron-mining villages of Mineville and Witherbee.[^5] The state Conservation Commission set up a lookout station on the summit in 1912, with a wooden tower.[^2][^3][^4] In 1917 the Commission replaced that tower with a 47-foot steel Aermotor LS40, the tower that stands there today.[^1][^2][^4]

In 1933 the state bought a half-acre tract on the peak from George and Julia Jacobs for $1,000, and the fire tower and its cabin sit on that land.[^3][^5]

Fire observers worked from the tower until 1988.[^4] Wikipedia and Stream & Steeple both say it went out of service that year and that the Department of Environmental Conservation officially closed it in early 1989.[^2][^6] The National Historic Lookout Register says it was closed in 1988, so the sources do not agree on the exact date.[^1]

The NHLR recorded the tower on January 23, 2017, on a nomination by Laurie Rankin.[^1] A visit report dated February 20, 2017, found the tower not staffed, its cab glass not intact, and its paint and graffiti in need of attention, though the structure itself was rated in good condition.[^1] The tower also carries radio equipment owned by the state environmental agency, the State Police and Essex County.[^1][^6]

Visiting is on foot.[^4][^5] The Adirondack Explorer describes a DEC-recommended trail that starts at Dalton Hill Road, puts the walk at 0.3 mile, and calls the route part of the Hammond Pond Wild Forest.[^4] A different Explorer piece gives the hike as 0.4 mile, so the distance is not settled.[^4][^5] Stream & Steeple says the tower is still standing and accessible to the public, but places it on private land, which does not match the state land described elsewhere.[^6] Stream & Steeple also lists Belfry in a challenge that requires hikers to climb fire tower summits, yet no source says the state has opened the tower to climbers.[^6] Because it was officially closed as a fire tower, treat it as closed unless the DEC says otherwise.[^2]
