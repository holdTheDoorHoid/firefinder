The Georgia Forestry Commission acquired this fire tower in January 1950.[^1] The sale sheet describes it as roughly 100 feet (30 m) tall, with a small storage container at its base.[^1]

A commission sale sheet, which carries no date, describes the tower as seen from the ground.[^1] The frame looked structurally sound with little rust, but the wooden stairs and platforms may be rotting, and the cab appeared to have been damaged by weather or vandalism.[^1] The lower flight of stairs had been removed for safety and to block unauthorized access, so close inspection of the cab, stairs and platforms was not possible.[^1] The electrical service appeared to have been disconnected.[^1]

The commission offered the tower for sale as-is, where-is, with no warranties.[^1] The buyer would have to dismantle and remove the tower and its container within 30 days of the bill of sale.[^1] Neither the commission nor the State of Georgia would provide labor, equipment or other assistance.[^1]

A photograph on Wikimedia Commons, titled Fire tower, Pretoria, is dated 1 January 2022 in its file data.[^2] No source describes the site today or says whether it is open to the public.
