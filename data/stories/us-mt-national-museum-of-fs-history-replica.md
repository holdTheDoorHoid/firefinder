The L-4 lookout at the National Museum of Forest Service History in Missoula is a replica, and it began its life in Washington, D.C.[^2] The design follows 1930s-era plans.[^1] The Forest Service's own preservation team built it in 2005 for the Smithsonian Folklife Festival on the Mall, to mark the agency's centennial, using materials reclaimed from Minton Peak Lookout.[^1][^2]

Once the festival closed, the lookout was dismantled and moved to the museum grounds west of Missoula. There it was re-erected on a short tower and partly refurbished. Visitors could not yet use the site, and permits and restoration work remained incomplete.[^2] A $500 grant from the Forest Fire Lookout Association in 2020, matched by its Montana-North Idaho chapter, went toward restoring it.[^2]

A 2020 association report says the lookout was later relocated to the central section of the museum's Forest Discovery Trail, where it sits at grade on concrete footings, a change made to satisfy Missoula County ADA rules.[^2]

Today the replica stands on the museum campus.[^3] The Destination Missoula listing for the museum puts the campus at 31 acres, a mile west of Missoula International Airport; the 2020 association report says 36 acres.[^3][^2] The listing gives seasonal hours of 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., seven days a week, running from Memorial Day weekend until the day following Labor Day. It was written while the new building was still under construction, so current hours may differ.[^3] The museum's new building, the National Conservation Legacy Center, opened to the public on July 17, 2026.[^4]
