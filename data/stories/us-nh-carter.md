Carter Tower is a round stone tower beside Route 2 in Jefferson, in Coos County, New Hampshire.[^1][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a standing stone observation tower.[^3]

The early history rests on secondhand accounts. Both sources that give a date place the tower at about 1897, when the Carter family privately operated it to keep an eye on the railroads.[^1][^2] A 2014 hiking blog adds that the family watched for fires along the rail lines, and it reports that the tower is in remarkable shape.[^2] The NH Family Hikes guide calls it one of the state's earliest fire towers.[^1] Because the dates come from a guide and a blog, they should be treated as tentative.[^1][^2]

The guide's description is short and says nothing about who staffed the tower or what fires it saw.[^1]

Today the tower still stands, but it is closed to the public.[^1] The door is locked and posted as private property.[^1] Drivers can stop beside the road, where there is limited parking and a view of the northern Presidentials down the road.[^1]
