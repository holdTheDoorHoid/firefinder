Mount Ascutney, in Windsor County, Vermont, had a fire lookout from the early 1900s until 1952.[^1][^2] A 1921 agricultural report, quoted by Forest Lookouts, says a cabin and a tower went up on the mountain that year.[^2] The steel tower came later.[^2] A side camp from the Plymouth CCC Camp put up a 54-foot steel lookout, and a 1940 newspaper credited the Civilian Conservation Corps with building it.[^2] A watchman was to be posted in the tower when fire danger was highest.[^2] A 1942 state report says the copper roofs were too light and blew off, and galvanized iron replaced them.[^2]

The later years are less tidy. A Vermont Forest Service report covering 1957 and 1958 says the station was kept on an inactive basis.[^2] The state park, however, gives 1952 as the end of fire lookout use, so the two accounts do not match.[^1]

The tower standing today is a shorter structure.[^1][^4] A 2016 Happy Vermont hiking post says the summit's old fire tower was cut down and made into an observation platform.[^4] The state park says today's tower near the summit is 25 feet tall and looks out over Vermont and New Hampshire.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a relocated Mount Ascutney tower as standing, and marks the original tower as gone.[^3]

Visitors who drive or ride can take the paved Mountain Road to the summit, and four trails, roughly 12 miles in all, climb from the base.[^1]
