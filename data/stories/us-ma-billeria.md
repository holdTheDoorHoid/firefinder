The Billeria tower began as a stone building rather than a lookout. The register describes a structure of stone already standing on Maryknoll and St. Vincent Seminary land, and a 2015 blog places it off Wyman Road in Billerica.[^1][^2] A report dated October 10, 1938 calls the grounds the former Dunlap estate, and says the state set up a fire lookout there that autumn.[^4]

It was a stopgap. The 1938 hurricane had destroyed the steel tower at Chelmsford, and the state wanted a watch kept over the Pinehurst and Nuttings Lake camping areas, where the storm had left extreme fire hazards.[^1][^3][^4] The same report names Arthur R. Hallenborg as observer and says he spotted six fires the day before.[^4]

The sources disagree on how long it stayed in use. The register and a 2015 blog say it served until a new Chelmsford tower was rebuilt in 1939.[^1][^2] A 2018 post from the Massachusetts chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association says it was used briefly, in 1938.[^3]

The register says the stone structure still stood in 2015 and was privately owned.[^1] A 2015 blog found the site had become a horse paddock.[^2] A 2018 chapter post says the structure is still there, held privately, and serves as a horse barn.[^3] I found no source newer than 2018, so its condition now is unconfirmed. It is private property,[^1][^3] so please keep off the grounds.
