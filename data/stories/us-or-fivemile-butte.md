Fivemile Butte has had a fire lookout since the 1920s, when the Forest Service put up a small cab on a pole tower. That first structure was replaced in 1932 by a more standard L-4 cab on a 30-foot pole tower, but heavy snow collapsed it just a decade later, in 1942.[^1][^2] The Forest Service rebuilt again in 1957, this time with a non-typical structure: a 30-foot treated-timber tower topped with an R-6 flat cab, which is the building still standing on the butte today.[^1][^2][^3]

The lookout sits at 4,627 feet in forest dominated by ponderosa pine, Douglas fir and western hemlock, part of the Mt. Hood National Forest's Barlow and Hood River Ranger Districts.[^3] It was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on September 4, 2011.[^1]

Like several of Mt. Hood's historic lookouts, Fivemile Butte has been kept alive by turning it over to overnight renters rather than letting it sit empty between fire seasons, and it's now available year-round through the Forest Service's recreation rental program.[^3] Access changes with the seasons: in summer, visitors can drive straight to the tower, but once winter conditions arrive — anywhere from early fall to late spring — the road closes and the only way in is up to 3 miles on skis, snowshoes or a snowmobile from the Billy Bob Sno-Park, which requires a Northwest Forest Pass.[^3] There's no drinking water on site, so guests need to bring their own.[^3] The surrounding area is popular with hikers, mountain bikers and winter sports enthusiasts, and wildlife in the vicinity ranges from wild turkeys and grouse to deer, elk, coyotes, cougars and the occasional bear.[^3]
