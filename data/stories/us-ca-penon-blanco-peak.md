Penon Blanco Peak Lookout went up in 1936, in the Civilian Conservation Corps era, as an L-601 tower with a matching BC-301 cab.[^1][^2] The register calls it a good example of its type and says it retains strong historic integrity.[^1] It also says CDF, the state forestry agency, has only four L-601 towers.[^1]

The register says tower alterations are few.[^1] Aluminum siding went over the original v-groove in 1946, the wood fire escape was replaced by a roll-up chain ladder, and the ground floor holds a 350-gallon water storage tank.[^1]

I found no record of the lookout's operators or of any notable fires in the sources I could read.[^1][^2]

The register says the exterior is in poor shape and that siding damage has occurred since a 1991 survey.[^1] I could not confirm from the sources I read whether the tower still stands.[^1][^2] The entry itself was registered on October 22, 2019.[^1]

The Cal Fire Madera-Mariposa-Merced Unit administers the site.[^1] The register says the station is hard for the public to reach, about two miles from a paved road, in a note dated October 1991.[^1] Radio repeaters sit inside the lookout, with antennas on the catwalk.[^1] I found no rental for the tower in the sources I read.[^1][^2]
