The Mount Pleasant lookout stood on a forested summit about four miles south-southeast of Port Angeles, in Clallam County, Washington.[^1][^2][^4] The register lists it under the Washington Division of Forestry and the Department of Natural Resources (DNR).[^1]

A 1952 state forestry report said a lookout was urgently needed on Mount Pleasant, and a new road to the site was already under way.[^3][^4] The register and Firelookout.com date the 40-foot wooden DNR live-in tower to 1953.[^1][^2] Willhite says it carried a 14-by-14 cab on top.[^4]

The Port Angeles Evening News reported in June 1957 that the lookout was manned.[^3] By May 1963 it was not on its regular manning schedule, but it was staffed on a temporary basis.[^3] One obituary cited by Willhite says a woman worked several summers there as a lookout.[^4]

High winds wrecked the tower in late December 1963.[^3][^4] Willhite and a photo caption on the Washington Lookouts page give December 28, but a January 1964 newspaper notice says the wind came on Christmas Eve, so the sources disagree on the day.[^3][^4] A replacement was built in 1964, and the register and Firelookout.com both describe the tower as rebuilt that year.[^1][^2][^4]

In January 1968 vandals got into the lookout by damaging a lock, two shutters and a window, and took small tools and the fire finder.[^3] The register says the lookout was abandoned that year and is now gone.[^1][^2]

Willhite's undated trip report describes the summit as forested and full of communication towers, and a photo caption on his page shows an eyebolt at the summit without saying what it was for.[^4]

Willhite's route starts at a trailhead on the edge of Port Angeles, follows Mt. Pleasant Road for roughly four miles, and passes a small gate that he calls likely open.[^4] He advises drivers without a Discover Pass to park on the main road.[^4] The hike is about 2.5 miles with a climb of 1,635 feet.[^4] No official access rule turned up in the sources, so check with DNR before going.
