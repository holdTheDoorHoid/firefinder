The Wisconsin Department of Conservation put up a steel fire lookout on South Bluff in 1933, about six and a half miles southwest of Babcock in Wood County.[^1][^2] It took the place of an earlier tower on lower, easier-to-reach ground a short distance away.[^1]

The older site drew criticism. An August 1931 column from The Daily Tribune, quoted on the Central US Lookouts page, complained that the tower had been set on the most convenient knoll rather than the highest point, so a taller bluff to the northwest blocked the view of the country around City Point, Pray and the Bissig Marsh, which burned often.[^2] The writer said the fire wardens had blasted rock and hauled material for a new site about half a mile away, but the steel braces and ladder still lay rusting in the open.[^2] The column gave the older tower a height of 75 feet.[^2]

The 1933 tower appears to sit on the higher ground the column wanted. A 1934 Coast and Geodetic Survey description places it at the summit of the hill called South Bluff, on Wood County property. It describes a conventional square steel framework tower with a closed cabin at the top, 64 feet tall.[^2] The two heights probably describe different towers: the 75-foot older one and its 1933 replacement.[^2]

I have not found any source naming who staffed the tower or describing notable fires there.

The register accepted the tower on March 24, 2020, on a nomination by Jacob Hufnagle.[^1] A Wisconsin lookout list still shows it as standing.[^3]

Today the register places the tower on Wood County Forest land, administered by Wood County.[^1] I could not find a source saying whether the public may climb it or whether it can be rented, so check with Wood County before any visit.
