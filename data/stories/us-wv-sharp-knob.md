Sharp Knob is in Pocahontas County, West Virginia, on the Monongahela National Forest.[^1][^2] Civilian Conservation Corps labor built its tower in 1936. It was a 40-foot steel tower with a 14-by-14-foot cab.[^1][^2]

A Forest Service history places the tower on the Gauley Ranger District and calls it the finest example of a traditional Forest Service fire tower.[^3] A National Archives photo from 1947 shows the tower on a windy day.[^3]

The register and the Forest Lookouts page both give 1970 as the last year the lookout was staffed.[^1][^2] The Forest Service history says the forest began phasing out staffed towers once aerial flights to spot fires came into use, and that the phase-out was done by the end of the 1970s.[^3]

The register lists Sharp Knob as a former lookout site and gives no removal date.[^1] The same history says most of the forest's towers were demolished, with only concrete piers and steps left behind.[^3] The site was registered on January 26, 2017, on the nomination of Brian M. Powell.[^1]

No source we read describes what, if anything, stands at Sharp Knob now, or whether the public can reach the site.
