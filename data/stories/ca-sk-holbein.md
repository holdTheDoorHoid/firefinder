Holbein is an organized hamlet in Saskatchewan's Rural Municipality of Shellbrook No. 493, and it is reached from Highway 3.[^1] The Website Service listing places the Holbein tower at 53-12-39.4 N, 106-08-30.4 W, and gives the structure as 24.4 m (80 ft) of light steel, with a new cupola in 1957.[^2]

The early history is tied to a ranger station. Elwood Koppang's account of his years in the towers, 1980 to 1989, says the first ranger, Ernest Jewitt, served from 1914 to 1918.[^3] It says a steel fire tower was built at the station in 1915, but it also gives 1947 for the "original square Holbein Tower", so its early dates do not line up neatly.[^3]

Koppang lists a long run of rangers at the station, from Jewitt through Harry Minifie, who is listed from 1966.[^3] A towermen list on the same website places Holbein among the towers worked by Sherry Reidt from 1986 to 2001 and by Richard Sevigny from 1992 to 2002.[^4]

Koppang writes that a 1937 wildfire nearly destroyed the growing hamlet, that a fire came within about six miles of Holbein in 1981, and that the North Cabin Fire came close again in 1989.[^3] Those accounts concern the hamlet rather than the tower.

Koppang says progress led to the ranger station being abandoned in 1984, and that the old tower was demolished and the buildings sold.[^3] He also describes serving in the Holbein tower over ten seasons, which does not sit easily with a 1984 teardown.[^3] A Sno Cruise page on the Nisbet-area towers says the Holbein tower "disappeared" after a fire around 2005, and the writer cannot tell whether it was taken down before that fire or because of it.[^5] A note beside Richard Sevigny's entry in the towermen list says his last tower was manned after mid August 2001, and that North Cabin was still active in the early 2002 season, which leaves Holbein's last season unclear.[^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the tower site today, or whether the public may go there.
