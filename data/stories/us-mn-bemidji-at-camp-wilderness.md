The National Historic Lookout Register says the Minnesota Department of Natural Resources built the Bemidji fire tower in 1935, and that it worked as a lookout for 60 years.[^1] The tower was 100 feet tall, an Aermotor design with a metal cab about seven feet square.[^1]

After it stopped serving as a lookout, the tower went to the Minnesota Department of Transportation for use as an antenna site.[^1] A 2007 report from the Minnesota chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association says the state transportation department was the owner and had used it that way for about fifteen years.[^3]

In 2007 the Boy Scouts moved the tower to Camp Wilderness in Hubbard County and rebuilt it as an 80-foot tower beside the mess hall.[^1][^2][^4] Sources differ on the kind of camp. One county report calls the new site a private camp, while the Register lists the Northern Lights Council of the Boy Scouts as the administrator.[^4][^1]

The council's own page thanks Steve Inkel of Diversified Builders and Design.[^5] The page lists an option to purchase a step on the tower as currently unavailable, and it offers a virtual tour of the plaques on the steps, landings and crow's nest.[^5]

I did not find public climbing hours, so I cannot say whether visitors may climb the tower. Ask Camp Wilderness before you go.
