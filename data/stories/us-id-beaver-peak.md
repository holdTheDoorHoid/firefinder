Beaver Peak is in Shoshone County, Idaho, on the Coeur d'Alene National Forest.[^1] It lies about 11 miles south of Clark Fork, at an elevation of 5,384 feet.[^1][^2]

The lookout was a 50-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6-foot cab and L-4 cabin living quarters, put up around 1928.[^1][^2][^3]

Several people are named as lookouts.[^1][^2] Bob Knox is pictured in the page's black-and-white photographs and is recorded as lookout in 1941.[^1] Leonard Dobler appears in a 1946 Kellogg Evening News item.[^1][^2] Paul Tusch is recorded for 1967.[^1][^2]

By 1967 the lookout was a pop-up cupola trailer, which the Forest Lookouts page calls a portable trailer lookout.[^1][^2][^3]

The site was gone by 1969.[^1][^2][^3] Firelookout.com's header gives the span as 1928 to 1967, but its text and Idaho Fire Lookouts both put the end at 1969.[^1][^2]

None of the three sources describes what remains of the site today.[^1][^2][^3] A survey description written by the U.S. Geological Survey in 1938 places a station mark, cemented in rock and set in earth, under the center of the lookout house, but no source says whether that mark survives.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts labels the site drive-up, but no source says whether it is open to visitors now.[^2]
