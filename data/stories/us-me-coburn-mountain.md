Coburn Mountain's first lookout was a wooden tower that the Maine Forestry District set up in 1910 in Somerset County.[^1][^2] That year, the forest commissioner's report describes fourteen miles of telephone line run from the mountain to West Forks.[^1]

A 24-foot steel tower with a cab went up in 1914, and a 1920 inventory still listed a 24-foot steel tower.[^1][^2] By 1919 the district had built a 10-by-12-foot camp at the foot of the mountain and fitted it with a telephone, since the watchman could struggle to hear from the tower on windy days.[^1] A photo record from about 1920 says the watchmen who manned the towers lived in cabins close by.[^2]

In 1938 ice crushed the cab, and the cab was rebuilt.[^1][^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list marks the tower as standing without its cab.[^3] Wikipedia describes an abandoned fire tower on the summit.[^5]

The summit can be reached on a two-mile trail that climbs 2,500 feet.[^5] The route is a groomed snowmobile trail, and a local club, the Coburn Summit Riders, maintains it.[^5] In 2025 the association returned and found that a weather event had wiped out a section of the access trail to the summit, and its members cleared it.[^4] I could not find who owns the summit or whether the old tower may be climbed, so check before you go.
