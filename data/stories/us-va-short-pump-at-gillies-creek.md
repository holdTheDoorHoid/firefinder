Henrico County says two fire towers were built in the county in the early 1930s.[^1] The Upper Henrico Tower in the West End appears to be the same tower that the Forest Fire Lookout Association lists as Short Pump (Upper Henrico).[^1][^3] We found no build year or design for the Short Pump tower itself.

The Henrico County Historical Society says Mason Wade was once the attendant of the Short Pump tower, and that he went up it twice daily to watch for forest fires.[^2]

The sources give different accounts of the tower's fate. Henrico County says the Upper Henrico Tower was demolished in the late 1980s.[^1] The Historical Society also says a tower like the one in its photograph was kept in Goochland County.[^2] The association lists the Short Pump site as gone, and lists a standing tower at Gillies Creek, in Henrico County, as relocated from Short Pump.[^3]

None of the pages we read describes a tower at Gillies Creek. The association's list gives no date or mover for that move, and files the entry under "Relocated / Replica Lookouts".[^3] The Historical Society describes its Goochland tower only as similar, so it may be a different tower. The sources do not settle whether the Short Pump tower survives, or where.

We found no access, visiting or rental information for Gillies Creek or for the Goochland exhibit. Check with Henrico County before going to look for the Gillies Creek tower, and do not climb any tower you find on private or closed ground.
