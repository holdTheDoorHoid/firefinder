Lorraine was a ground-level lookout in the Wenatchee National Forest in Chelan County, Washington, about four miles south of Stevens Pass.[^2][^4] Its L-4 cab sat below the ridgetop, not on the summit, at an elevation of 5,451 feet.[^1]

Sources disagree on when the lookout started. The register gives 1931 as the build year, and FireLookout.com gives the same year for the cab.[^1][^2] The register's own description, however, places the cab below the ridgetop and dates it to 1934.[^1] A July 1932 Wenatchee World item reported that new lookouts had been set up on Lorraine peak and Jack ridge, which gave the Icicle watershed complete lookout coverage.[^3]

A Leavenworth paper listed William Hansen as the lookout-fireman at Lorraine Point in June 1933, and FireLookout.com names him as the known lookout for that year.[^3][^2] Panorama photographs were taken on August 12, 1934.[^3] The register credits its 1934 panoramas to the National Archives Pacific Northwest Region.[^1]

The cab was abandoned in 1941.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a ground site that is gone.[^4]

We found no source that describes what remains at the site today, such as footings or a foundation, or whether the public can reach it.
