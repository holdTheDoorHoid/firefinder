Armstrong Mountain Lookout sits on a summit within the Colville Indian Reservation, about 22 miles east-southeast of Omak in Okanogan County, Washington.[^1][^2] The steel tower there is an Aermotor design, 100 feet tall, built in 1939 and the only lookout the summit has carried.[^2][^3] Its cab is roughly seven feet square.[^2]

An August 1939 Spokesman-Review item reported plans for about four miles of new telephone line to the tower.[^4] In July 1943 the Colville Examiner reported that numerous teachers had summer jobs as fire lookouts in the Colville National Forest, and it placed one of them on Armstrong Mountain.[^4] The sources I read give no further staffing record after that, and no notable fire at the site.

The site has stood empty since 1952, and its ground-level living quarters and the lookout roof have disappeared.[^1][^2] The NHLR and TrailChick both describe the steel tower itself as still in place, and TrailChick's photos date from a November 2017 visit.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register registered the lookout on April 11, 2022, as US 1521, with Brad Eells as nominator.[^1]

The summit is on Colville tribal land.[^1][^3] Non-tribal visitors must buy a Colville recreation permit, which TrailChick says costs $80 a year.[^3] The same blog cautions that the rules may change without warning.[^3] Anyone interested should check the current rules with the Colville Tribes first. TrailChick's 2017 visitor did not climb to the top, citing wind and a lack of nerve.[^3]
