Owl Mountain, in Jefferson County about 20 miles north of Lake Quinault, held a two-story modified cupola cabin.[^1][^2] The register lists it as administered by the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR, and Kemnow's index places it under the Washington Department of Natural Resources.[^1][^3] The register and firelookout.com both date the cabin to 1941.[^1][^2]

The date is complicated by a 1944 report. Kemnow's page quotes that May 1944 report on Air Warning Service stations. It says a station called "Baker 6-6" was activated on August 22, 1942, used existing state-owned facilities, and added a shake cabin built of native material.[^3] Willhite's list also gives 1941 for Owl Mountain, while noting that the author drops sites that were used only as Aircraft Warning Stations in WWII.[^4] The sources do not say whether the 1941 cabin and the 1942 station were the same structure, so the timeline is unsettled.[^1][^3]

Every source I read says the cabin is now gone.[^1][^2][^5] When the Air Warning Service finished with the site, the state kept it.[^3]

The only description of the setting I found is from the 1944 report, which put the station 14 miles from a road.[^3] No source describes what, if anything, remains on the site now, or whether the public can reach it.
