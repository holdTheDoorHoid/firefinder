South Mount Hawkins Lookout stood in the Angeles National Forest in Los Angeles County, California, at 7,791 feet, above Crystal Lake.[^1][^4] The Register says it was constructed in 1938 as a 14-by-14-foot wooden cab with shutter outriggers, on top of a 30-foot treated timber tower.[^1][^2]

An August 1937 item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page says truck-trail work was discontinued for lack of equipment, and that crews hoped to reach the site of the new tower before winter halted the work.[^4]

The staffing record we found is thin. A November 1966 item quoted on the same page says rangers nearly failed to bring a seasonal lookout, Sandy DeLappe, off the tower. Their truck stuck in fresh snow, and she had to hike out.[^4] A 1960 item on the page names a lookout called Sandy DeLapp, but it does not name this tower, so we have not counted it here.[^4]

The Register lists the tower as registered on October 1, 1998.[^1] It says volunteers from the Angeles National Forest Fire Lookout Association refurbished and reopened the lookout in September 1999.[^1]

The Register and the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register both say the tower was destroyed by fire during the Curve Fire, on September 1, 2002.[^1][^2] The association's inactive-towers page says the same.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page names a different fire, saying the Leona Fire burned it in September 2002.[^4] We follow the three sources that name the Curve Fire, but we could not check the fire name against a fire record.

An effort to rebuild the tower was underway, but the poor and worsening condition of the road to the site changed the plan.[^1] The association says it is searching for another peak in the Angeles National Forest on which to rebuild a fire lookout tower, after the Vetter Mountain Lookout rebuild is complete.[^3]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, is left at the site today, or whether the road is open to visitors. Check with the Angeles National Forest before planning a visit.
