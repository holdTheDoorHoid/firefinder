Meridian Mountain is in Cowlitz County, southwest Washington.[^1][^2] Will Hite's guide calls it a low-elevation mountain, while the register and Firelookout.com give its elevation as 5,153 feet.[^1][^2][^3] The name comes from the Willamette Meridian, which passes within feet of the summit.[^3]

The lookout was built in 1942.[^1][^2][^3] The tower was 40 feet tall, with a 7-by-7-foot cab on top and a ground house at its base.[^1][^2][^3]

The register lists the Washington Division of Forestry/DNR as the administrator.[^1] Firelookout.com lists Dennis Gross as known lookout personnel in 1953.[^2]

The lookout came to an end in 1963.[^1][^2][^3] The register says it was removed, while Firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2] Will Hite's guide ties the loss to the Columbus Day storm.[^3] Wikipedia names that storm the Columbus Day storm of 1962, so the guide's year and the storm's name do not agree, and we could not settle which year is right.[^4][^3]

Today tree cover blocks the view, according to the guide.[^3] Hunting camps left so much garbage at the top that the lookout's remains are hard to spot.[^3] A reference benchmark, Meridian RM 2, lies at the edge of the open area.[^3] A road runs to within feet of the site, but the guide says you need a St. Helens Tree Farm Permit to be legal there, so check the rules before you go.[^3]
