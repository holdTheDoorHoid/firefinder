McGuire Mountain's lookout started as a simple camp site, used from 1921, before a permanent cabin went up. Sources disagree on exactly when: the National Historic Lookout Register and firelookout.com date the cab to 1924, while the Forest Service's own Recreation.gov listing for the site says 1923.[^1][^2][^3] It is a D-6 cupola design, a style common for its era, built with a pyramidal roof topped by a small windowed cupola reached by a wooden ladder inside the cabin.[^1][^2][^3] The D-6 design was eventually superseded by the better-known L-4 cab, and only two or three D-6 lookouts are reported still in fire service anywhere in the country.[^1]

The lookout was staffed to watch for smoke for a couple of decades. The National Historic Lookout Register says it sat abandoned for 35 years before its 1982 restoration, which would put abandonment around 1947; Recreation.gov instead says the lookout was abandoned around 1944, after more than 20 years of active use.[^1][^2] Either way, by the mid-1940s the cabin had gone out of service and sat empty for decades, on land now administered as part of the Kootenai National Forest.

Restoration eventually returned the cabin to use. The National Historic Lookout Register and firelookout.com both point to a 1982-83 restoration, while Recreation.gov describes a longer renovation stretching from 1983 to 1998; after that work it was placed into the Forest Service's recreation rental program.[^1][^2][^3] It was added to the National Historic Lookout Register in 2000 as US 345.[^1]

Today the 12-by-12-foot wood-frame cabin, windowed on all sides, sits about 14 miles southwest of Eureka, Montana, near the Koocanusa Reservoir, and is reached by a 2.5-mile hike from a trailhead at Little Sutton Mountain.[^2] It sleeps up to four, has a wood stove but no electricity or running water, and is kept locked, with guests given the combination ahead of their stay; it is meant to be visited through a booked rental rather than climbed on a whim.[^2]
