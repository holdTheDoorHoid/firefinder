The Bonheur Fire Tower went up in 1941, about 25 km east of Ignace in Ontario's Kenora District.[^1] The land was acquired in an exchange with the Canadian Pacific Railway, and the tower cost $1,500 for materials and ranger labour.[^1]

A marker in Ignace says a towerman watched for possible fires and reported them without delay.[^3] The tower was used until 1969, and a run of towermen made it their home for that time.[^1]

In 1991 the tower was dismantled and rebuilt in Ignace.[^1] The original stood 24.4 m (80 ft) high, and the rebuilt tower is 20.1 m (66 ft).[^1]

The register spells the name Bonhuer, while the marker uses Bonheur.[^2][^1]

Today the rebuilt tower is a historical display on the south side of the Ignace Public Library, near 36 Main Street.[^1][^2] The marker says it can be reached from Main Street, which is also the Trans-Canada Highway.[^1]
