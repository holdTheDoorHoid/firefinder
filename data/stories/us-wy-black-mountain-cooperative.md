Black Mountain Cooperative Lookout stands on a rock knob on Wyoming state land inside the Medicine Bow National Forest, in Albany County.[^1][^2] The Forest Service built it in 1958 as a metal cab, about 13 feet by 13 feet, with a flat roof and a catwalk.[^1][^2] A metal stairway winds up between the boulders to the cab.[^1] The lookout is a cooperative one, staffed under a joint agreement among the Forest Service, the Wyoming State Forestry Division, the Bureau of Land Management, and Platte-Albany and Converse Counties.[^1]

The register says the lookout is staffed only sometimes, by seasonal Forest Service staff working roughly mid-May to September 30, with the dates shifting for snow and fire conditions.[^1] Two past lookout staff are on record: Marnia Wormus in 1982 and Kathy Fraser in 2005.[^2] I found no notable fire tied to the tower in these sources.

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on January 30, 1994, on the nomination of the Wyoming State Forester.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as a standing ground-level lookout.[^3] A 2018 visit report rates it in good condition, noting two coats of paint on the woodwork inside and out, a roof that was not leaking, and one slightly cracked pane.[^1] The same report notes that internet dishes were mounted on the railing, and that a solar array and battery bank were added.[^1]

Getting there is rough. The register describes about three miles of steep, rocky road that needs a 4x4, or a walk.[^1] I found no rental or visitor program in these sources, so it is best treated as a part-time working lookout.
