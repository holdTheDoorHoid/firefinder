Loon Creek Point was a fire lookout in the Challis National Forest, about 35 miles northwest of Challis in Custer County, Idaho.[^1][^2] The site lies in the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness.[^1]

In August 1937 Civilian Conservation Corps enrollees at a camp on Loon Creek were building the lookout, and The Post-Register noted that their spare time went to panning gold.[^3] Sources date the finished L-4 cab to 1938.[^1][^2][^3] Sources disagree on how it was set: Idaho Fire Lookouts calls it ground-mounted, while firelookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page say it sat on a log crib.[^2][^1][^3]

Firelookout.com says full-time staffing ended in the early 1970s.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts dates the last staffing to the 1970s, and says it has no documented staffing history.[^2]

The Red Bluff Fire overran the lookout on August 13, 2007.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2007 western report called it the only confirmed lookout lost to wildfire that year.[^4] The report names the forest as the Salmon-Challis National Forest, while the other sources say Challis.[^4][^1][^2]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today. Idaho Fire Lookouts marks it as a hike-in site that was destroyed.[^2] Anyone planning a trip should check current conditions with the national forest first.
