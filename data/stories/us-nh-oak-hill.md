The Oak Hill tower stands on a summit in Merrimack County, New Hampshire, a short distance from Concord.[^1][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register says the steel tower was constructed in 1928 at 55 feet, and that its design also included a platform for visitors just below the cab.[^1] Map data lists the height at 19.8 metres, which matches the original height plus the 1971 raise described below.[^5]

Sources disagree on the town. A January 1929 notice in The Forest Worker put the new tower in Loudon, close to the Concord border.[^2] A 1947 newspaper item, as quoted by a lookout history site, placed the hill in Concord.[^2] A 2025 hiking blog says the trail crosses the Concord and Loudon town line before it reaches the tower.[^4]

In June 1947 the governor and council opened proceedings to take about two acres of the hill by eminent domain, because the owners declined to sell.[^2] The state sought an easement for telephone and power lines, plus a route to a natural spring.[^2] At that time the State Forestry Department ran a lookout there. The 1947 item said the tower had started as a trial and had been used successfully over a wide area of the Merrimack valley.[^2]

The Register says the tower was raised 10 feet in 1971 and then ran irregularly for a time. The tower came back into service in 1991, with a volunteer lookout at first and later as part of the state network.[^1]

In 2019 the cab was replaced and further work was carried out on the tower, according to a Forest Fire Lookout Association report.[^3] The same report, citing one member, says New Hampshire towers were manned for about four days last spring, but it does not single out Oak Hill, so the sources give no current staffing picture for this tower.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Hampshire list shows the tower as standing.[^6] A 2025 hiking blog describes parking in the Oak Hill City Forest, on Shaker Road, then a trail and a steep, narrow final climb to the tower.[^4] No source found sets a rule on climbing, so anyone planning a visit should ask the land manager before going up.
