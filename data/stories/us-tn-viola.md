The Tennessee Landforms list includes a lookout named Viola in Warren County.[^1] It gives the elevation as 1,089 feet and marks the tower as standing.[^1] The town of Viola had 93 residents at the 2020 census.[^2]

The sources we checked do not say when the Viola tower was built, what design it uses, or how tall it stands.[^1][^3]

The 2014 National Register document about the state Division of Forestry's towers lists a Viola tower in Warren County among those still standing as of March 26, 2014.[^3] The same document says the Division was responsible for building 208 fire lookout towers between about 1930 and 1970.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register counts 76 registered lookouts in Tennessee, and its Tennessee page does not name a Viola tower.[^4]

The document describes the towers as the physical part of the Division's fire detection network.[^3] As of its 2014 date, it says aircraft and satellite imagery play a bigger part in spotting fires, and that only a handful of the towers remain in active service.[^3] We found no record that the Viola tower was staffed.[^1][^3]

Today the sources we checked say nothing about who owns the tower, whether it can be climbed, or whether anyone maintains it.[^1][^3] The document notes that many of the old towers have been removed or are awaiting demolition. It adds that surviving towers are often in disrepair, and that visitors are commonly cautioned about the risks of climbing them.[^3] Treat the tower as closed to climbing unless its owner or land manager says otherwise.
