Davis Memorial is a steel fire tower on Pine Mountain near Warm Springs, in Meriwether County, Georgia. Newspapers from 1931 call it the Charles Lamar Davis memorial tower, and a Georgia lookout list now places it with the Georgia Forestry Commission.[^1][^2]

Work began on 14 September 1930, when the Macon Telegraph reported that the tower was going up at a point on Pine Mountain known as "The View."[^1] The paper said it would stand 80 feet tall and be used for fire protection in Meriwether, Harris and Talbot counties.[^1] Two men from the state forestry department, H. M. Sebring and Jack Thurmond, supervised the job, and Meriwether County supplied the labor.[^1] The paper also said the courthouse at Greenville could be picked out from the ground with the naked eye.[^1]

Miss Georgia Wilkins, of Columbus, gave the tower in memory of her uncle, Charles L. Davis of Warm Springs.[^1] Governor Franklin D. Roosevelt of New York gave the dedication address on 8 December 1930, and a tablet to Davis was unveiled at the ceremony.[^1]

The sources disagree about who received the gift. The 1930 Macon report says the tower was donated to Meriwether County. An Atlanta Constitution caption from April 1931 says it was a gift to the Warm Springs Foundation.[^1] A January 1931 Forest Worker item calls Davis "for many years owner of the Warm Springs property."[^1] A National Park Service history says a Charles Davis built the Meriwether Inn at Warm Springs in 1893.[^3]

In April 1931 a powerful telescope was mounted on the tower. A Butler Herald report said it was for watching for forest fires and for visitors who came to Warm Springs.[^1] The Atlanta caption said watchmen in the tower could locate forest fires for many miles around.[^1]

The Georgia lookout list gives the tower an elevation of 1,245 feet (379.5 m).[^4] The map behind that list was last revised in March 2016.[^4] No source I found describes the tower or its site today, including whether it still stands.
