Mount Holyoke has had a summit house since 1821, when a local committee built an 18-by-24-foot guest cabin that was one of the first of its kind in New England.[^2] In 1851 the property was rebuilt as the Prospect House, a two-story hotel with eight rooms, under John W. French.[^2][^4] A four-story addition finished in 1894 raised its capacity to forty guests.[^4] From 1911 to 1918 a 60-foot fire tower operated from the top room of that hotel, according to the register.[^1]

The hotel stayed open until the Great Hurricane of 1938 seriously harmed its 1894 annex, which was later torn down.[^3] Joseph A. Skinner then offered the hotel and the surrounding land to Massachusetts, on condition that the park bear his name.[^3] Sources disagree on the donation year: one article gives 1939,[^2] while Estuary Magazine gives 1940 and the state park article gives June 1940 as the formal dedication.[^4][^3] Maintenance funding fell short for years, and by the mid-1970s some proposed condemning and tearing down the summit house, which drew a public outcry.[^3] In the mid-1980s the 1851 structure and the 1861 addition were restored by the state and local volunteers.[^2]

Today the summit house sits in J.A. Skinner State Park, which the Department of Conservation and Recreation manages.[^3] Tours resumed in 2014, after restoration work.[^3] Visitors can go in on weekends and holidays between Memorial Day and Columbus Day.[^2] Cars can use the summit road from April through November.[^2] The surviving building is the 1851 structure and the 1861 addition, and no source says which part of the hotel held the lookout room.[^2][^1]
