Dry Lake Mountain stands at 6,780 feet in the Klamath National Forest, in Siskiyou County [^1]. The station was in use well before the present cabin [^2]. A 1914 newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page reports that Everson T. Rider had been named lookout on Dry Lake mountain and had taken up the post [^2]. A 1923 inspection report recommended a standard lookout house there and noted that the station sat on Southern Pacific land that stockmen lease [^2].

The register dates the present cabin to 1925, when it replaced an earlier small shack [^1]. Its design is a California Region 5 Plan 4AR cabin on an enclosed timber tower [^1].

Newspaper items name several people who worked the post [^2]. Perry W. Fisher had spent 13 years as a lookout at the Dry Lake station, a 1924 item said [^2]. In 1929 Al Barnum replaced E. L. Spencer as lookout operator [^2]. A 1941 item said the lookout was manned for only three weeks that year [^2].

A Forest Service road to the station was completed for the Fourth of July in 1930 [^2]. In 1936 the Klamath forest accepted title to 20 acres for the site, which had been leased from the Southern Pacific Land Company [^2].

The lookout was removed in 1971 [^1][^2]. The register says only a foundation remains [^1].

The register lists the Klamath National Forest as administrator [^1]. No source found describes road access today or whether visitors are welcome at the site.
