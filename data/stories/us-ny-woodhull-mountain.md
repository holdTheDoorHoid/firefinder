Woodhull Mountain rises to 2,359 feet in the Adirondacks, in the Town of Webb, Herkimer County.[^1] The state Conservation Commission put a wooden fire lookout on its summit in September 1911.[^1][^2] Five years later the Commission built a steel Aermotor LL25 tower on the peak.[^1][^2] The sources disagree on its height. Wikipedia gives 50 feet and Forest Lookouts gives 53 feet. The record here uses the taller figure.[^1][^2]

Bill Starr's article on New York's steel tower models calls the LL-25 the earliest standard steel tower the state installed.[^3] Compared with the 1917 design, this tower was lighter and had no stairs; climbing it meant a galvanized steel rung ladder on the outside.[^1] In 1936 a self-supporting staircase, tied to the original tower, replaced the wood ladder on the outside.[^1]

Neither Wikipedia nor the Forest Lookouts page names a lookout who worked this tower or a fire it watched.[^1][^2] A December 1939 newspaper report, reprinted by Forest Lookouts, mentions the tower only in passing.[^2] It describes a length of telephone wire, cut in the woods, that linked a paper company's office with its camps and "the tower at Woodhull Mountain," and it follows the trooper's investigation of the cut.[^2]

The tower stopped working as a fire lookout at the end of the 1970 season, as aerial detection increased.[^1] It still stands, according to Wikipedia and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list.[^1][^4]

According to Wikipedia, members of the public can still visit the structure, but the cab is excluded.[^1] We found no closure notice, but we also found no current notice from a land manager about access or conditions. Check before you go, and stay out of the cab.
