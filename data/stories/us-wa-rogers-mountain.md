Rogers Mountain sits in Stevens County, Washington, inside the Colville National Forest.[^3] The Forest Service and the Civilian Conservation Corps built a lookout on its summit, a 45-foot pole tower with an L-6 cab.[^4][^1] The sources disagree on the year. Two of them give 1933, but a 1933 survey party found no structure during its visit, and a May 1934 Colville Examiner item says a lookout was only then being established, so 1934 looks more likely.[^1][^2][^4][^3]

Mr. and Mrs. Hazen Walston are listed as lookouts for 1945.[^2] WillhiteWeb names a couple for each year from 1950 through 1954, the last being Mr. and Mrs. Robert Dewey.[^4] A 1959 newspaper item, quoted by a local history page, says the lookout was discontinued about 1950, which does not match those later names.[^3]

The tower came down in 1959.[^2][^4] A Statesman Examiner item from October 1959 says it fell on a Sunday night.[^3] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register is the one source that says it was removed in the 1950s instead.[^1]

No source describes a standing structure today. A local history page says ruins remain on the ground, and WillhiteWeb's photo captions mention footings at the summit.[^3][^4] There is no tower left to climb. Today the road ends at the official Mt. Rogers trailhead, reached from Aladdin Road and Kolle Creek Road (FR 500), and a trail climbs from there to the highest point.[^4]
