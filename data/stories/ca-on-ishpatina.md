Ishpatina Ridge, about 90 km north of Sudbury, is the highest point of land in Ontario, at an estimated 693 m (2,274 ft) above sea level.[^2] Government of Canada mapping in the 1970s revealed that fact; until then, the better-known Maple Mountain was thought to be higher.[^2]

A fire tower went up on the ridge in 1930, according to Firelookout.ca, and was 24 m (80 ft) tall.[^1][^2] Its successor, about 30 m (100 ft) tall, is the tower that stands today.[^1]

Wikipedia says the tower was staffed by forest workers, who would have been ferried in by float plane.[^2] Firelookout.ca says the tower man would have lived in a cabin on the shore of Scarecrow Lake and hiked a 3.5 km trail to the summit, taking about two hours to climb.[^1]

The successor was abandoned in the 1960s.[^1] Across Ontario, towers saw less and less use through that decade, as aerial spotting and then firefighting crews took over the job.[^3]

The abandoned tower still stands on the highest part of the ridge.[^1] Firelookout.ca says it is often called the Ellis Tower.[^1][^2]

Lady Evelyn-Smoothwater Provincial Park contains the ridge.[^2] The most popular approach is by canoe and portage from Capreol, following a logging road to the south.[^2] A northern route from Gowganda has many portages, and Wikipedia notes that the remote setting makes the ridge hard to reach.[^2]

No source we read says whether the abandoned tower is open to visitors. Do not climb it.
