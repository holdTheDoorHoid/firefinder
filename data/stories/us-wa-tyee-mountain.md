Tyee Mountain Lookout stands at about 6,654 feet in what is now the Okanogan-Wenatchee National Forest, east of the Cascade crest in Chelan County, Washington.[^3][^2] An earlier L-4 cab stood on the same site starting in 1931; the Wenatchee National Forest replaced it in the 1950s with the hip-roofed, 14-by-14-foot L-4 groundhouse that stands there today, complete with a catwalk.[^2][^1] Sources differ slightly on the exact year — the National Historic Lookout Register gives 1952, while another lookout survey simply says "the 1950's" — likely because one is dating the replacement structure and an earlier source may be counting from the original 1931 cab.[^1][^2]

The lookout earned a moment of grim fame in the summer of 1994, when it reported what began as a small lightning strike that grew into the Tyee Fire, a blaze that burned roughly 180,000 acres over several weeks, destroyed 22 homes, and even burned down the lookout's own outhouse.[^1] Forest Service personnel staffed the tower into the 1990s.[^3] By 2013, a Forest Fire Lookout volunteer reported that the Entiat Ranger District had deemed the structure's lightning protection system out of code, with no funds budgeted to fix it, leaving no plans to use Tyee for fire detection going forward even though volunteers had put real work into its upkeep.[^1] Since then it has relied on volunteer maintenance and sometime staffing by volunteers coordinated through the district.[^1]

Tyee Mountain Lookout was added to the National Historic Lookout Register on July 1, 1995.[^1] It is now rented out as a Forest Service recreation cabin, a 16-by-16-foot room that sleeps four on foam mattresses, though Recreation.gov's own listing calls it "a rough place": the final 3.6 miles of access road are narrow, steep, sandy, and cross two rock ledges, requiring a four-wheel-drive, high-clearance vehicle (or a roughly four-mile walk), and the ground around the lookout itself is rocky and uneven with steep drop-offs on two sides.[^3] As of this writing, Recreation.gov lists Tyee Lookout as closed for the remainder of the 2026 season because of nearby wildfires.[^3]
