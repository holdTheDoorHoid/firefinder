The Forest Fire Service used a wooden lookout on Bearfort Mountain in Passaic County from 1916 until 1932, when it burned.[^1] The register describes it as fully enclosed, with stairs inside.[^1] Before it, Newark's watershed department had built an iron-frame tower on the mountain in 1909.[^2]

Sources disagree on its size. A 1923 newspaper item describes a forty-foot frame structure built in 1916 by the City of Newark, while the National Historic Lookout Register describes a 50-foot wooden tower.[^3][^2] A state annual report for 1915-16 says the city board's lookout tower had become unfit for use, and that the board erected a new structure to replace it.[^3]

Watchers were posted here from early on. A fire warden's report from 1913 says a watcher was on constant duty in a tower already standing on the mountain, and a 1917 report says a watchman was continuously on duty in the city's tower from April to December.[^3]

The wooden lookout did not last. A newspaper item dated April 15, 1932 reported that the old fire tower had burned to the ground, probably from stovepipe sparks that found a tear in the tar-paper roof.[^3] Fire warden Charles Stalter of Hewitt was on duty but heard of the fire before he got home, and by the time the section warden reached the summit the wooden structure had been completely razed.[^3] The NHLR says a tree platform served until the steel tower went up.[^2]

The 68-foot steel tower that followed still stands and is in use as an active lookout.[^1][^2] The two sources disagree on its build year: the NHLR says 1934, while the register says 1937.[^2][^1] None of the three sources describes what, if anything, remains of the 1916 wooden lookout itself.

The mountain is Newark watershed land.[^2] The NHLR describes a highly visited area with several hiking trails, and says a sign that was posted at one time has since vanished.[^2] None of the sources says whether visitors may climb the steel tower.

The register added the wooden lookout's record on July 24, 2016, nominated by Robert Spear.[^1]
