Georgia's Kathleen fire tower, now usually called the Oaky Woods tower, was built around 1950 to guard the forests of central Georgia, including the Oaky Woods and Ocmulgee wildlife management areas.[^1] It stands 110 feet tall, and its watch platform is a seven-foot-square room at the top.[^1][^2]

Forest rangers once kept lookout from that room.[^2] Around 1980 that duty began passing to aircraft watchers, and the register says the tower has been unmanned since the 1980s.[^2][^1]

The tower is still standing.[^1][^2] Vandals knocked out the platform's windows years ago. An old chair base and a fire extinguisher are still inside.[^2] The state's Department of Natural Resources bought the land the tower sits on in 2010, and the tower was renamed the Oaky Woods Fire Tower.[^1] It now houses radio equipment for local government.[^1]

John Trussell, who led the effort to save Oaky Woods from development, has been trying to raise money to repair the tower.[^2] The article estimates roughly $5,000 would cover fixing the windows, the platform and the stairs.[^2] The register recorded the tower on July 30, 2018.[^1]

Access is the open question. The tower sits in the Oaky Woods Wildlife Management Area.[^1] Trussell told the article the public would never be free to climb it whenever they like, but supervised visits might be possible.[^2] Please do not climb it without permission.
