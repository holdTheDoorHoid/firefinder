Hanlon Mountain is in the Colville National Forest, in Pend Oreille County, Washington.[^1][^2] The register puts the lookout site at 4,143 feet, or 1,263 meters, while WillhiteWeb gives 4,120 feet.[^1][^3]

In early June 1935 the Forest Service started work on a lookout station there.[^3] The Newport Miner reported on June 13, 1935 that its lookout had begun work at Hanlon Peak station the week before.[^4] The tower, a 30-foot pole structure with an L-4 cab, was completed in 1936.[^3][^1]

The lookout was staffed in the years that followed. In May 1940 the Newport Miner reported that a Mr. Waggoner of Chatteroy had been stationed on the lookout.[^3][^4] In 1946 the Sandpoint News-Bulletin reported that Don Jones had spent the summer on it.[^3][^4]

Three sources date the tower's end to the 1960s, though they differ on how it ended.[^1][^2][^3] The register says it was removed in that decade.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^2] WillhiteWeb says it was removed or destroyed.[^3] Forest Lookouts lists it as removed, with no date.[^4]

No source describes a standing tower today. A photo caption on WillhiteWeb reads "Footings looking south," so some footings appear to remain at the site.[^3] Another caption says somebody set up a display there, but the page does not say what it shows.[^3]

WillhiteWeb's notes, as of 2023, list the access as any vehicle, but they also mention a year-long wildlife closure on the road and show a gate.[^3] At that time only the last quarter mile of road was starting to grow over.[^3] The hike is listed at 2.6 miles one way.[^3] The sources do not say whether foot travel is allowed past the gate, so check with the Colville National Forest before you go.
