Williams Mountain rises in Somerset County, in the lands between Misery Ridge and the Moose River.[^1][^2][^3] A fire station went up on the mountain in the spring of 1911 and was in operation early that year.[^2] The building, furnishings and telephone line came to $183.34 in total.[^2] A 48-foot steel tower was erected in 1914, and a 1920 inventory still listed it at that height.[^2] In 1918 the Maine Forest Service also published a 360-degree panoramic map centered on the tower.[^4]

Within a year, the chief warden credited the station with limiting the damage.[^2] In July 1912 a watchman spotted a fire near Brassua Pond on July 5. On July 10 another fire was noted on the west part of Misery, near Cold Stream Pond.[^2] The chief warden said lightning started the first and someone dropping a match started the second.[^2] Putting out both cost the state $150, and little land was damaged.[^2] The account we read does not name the watchman.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^1] Its legend marks towers that have lost their cab with an asterisk, and Williams Mountain is not marked that way.[^1]

Today the summit is reached by a hiking trail.[^3] The Maine Trail Finder describes a former fire warden cabin and fire tower at the top, and says both are best viewed rather than entered.[^3] The trail crosses land owned by Weyerhaeuser, with a state trail easement, and the state plans to own the trailhead and summit through a donation from the company.[^3] The trail page, which we could not date, says the trailhead is not yet built, and that the company allowed parking in a temporary area.[^3] Do not climb the tower. Check the trail page or the Maine Bureau of Parks and Lands for current conditions before going.
