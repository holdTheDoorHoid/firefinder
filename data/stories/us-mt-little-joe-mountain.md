Little Joe Mountain, also called Little Joe Peak, sits in Mineral County, Montana, inside the Lolo National Forest.[^1][^2] Our two sources disagree on how far it lies from St. Regis. One puts it four miles southwest, and the other says five.[^1][^2]

The peak got a log cabin and a platform tower in the 1920s.[^1] In November 1921 the Great Falls Tribune reported, as quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, that new lookouts were going up at Tamarack Point and at Little Joe.[^2] A 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab followed in 1939.[^1]

An August 1927 Independent Record item, also quoted on that page, reported lightning near the tower and concern for the lookout's safety after he could not be reached by telephone for two hours.[^2] The firelookout.com page names eight people who worked the lookout between 1955 and 1967, from Daniel Craver in 1955 to Terrance Cox in 1967.[^1]

In 1967 the cab was on display at a scout jamboree in Idaho before it was placed on the tower.[^1][^3] In 1969 a 53-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 flat cab was built at Little Joe.[^1][^3]

In 1977 the tower and cab were moved to Williams Peak, where they replaced an older 30-foot tower from 1934.[^1][^3][^4] The Little Joe summit lost its lookout that year.[^1]

Today the structure stands at Williams Peak, according to a May 2026 radio activation report on pnwsota.org.[^5] The National Historic Lookout Register places that lookout in Fish Creek State Park in Mineral County, and says Montana Fish, Wildlife and Parks administers the site.[^3] The firelookout.com page adds that the lookout has gone unstaffed for many years and now stands on state land.[^4] The 2026 report says the Williams Peak summit is reached by a gated road, FS-7721.[^5] No source we read says whether the tower is open to visitors, so ask the managing agency before going up. Nothing we read describes what, if anything, remains at the Little Joe summit today.
