The Eagle Pass Fire Lookout was a stone cabin on the peak of Eagle Pass Mountain, in Columbia-Shuswap, British Columbia.[^1] The Dominion Forestry Branch, which managed forest land in BC's Railway Belt, put it up in 1921.[^1] The page says it was the first of four cabins, built of mortared stone under a pyramidal hip roof. Each measured 3.7 by 3.7 m (12 by 12 ft).[^1]

Senior Fire Ranger John "Jack" Mizon of Malakwa found the route for the trail and supervised the lookout's construction.[^1] The work involved 21 km (13 mi) of pack trail, a telephone line, and an overnight cabin halfway along the route.[^1] The lookout opened in 1922 on a prominent peak at 2311 m (7,700 ft).[^1]

J. H. "Jack" Aubrey is said to be the first lookout man. The page says he is known to have staffed the lookout from 1924 to 1928, at $120 a month.[^1]

The lookout was active until 1931. The page says the impacts of the Great Depression closed it, and it did not reopen.[^1]

Heavy snow later took the roof off the empty cabin, sometime before 1975, leaving lower walls 1.2 m (4 ft) high.[^1] A growing number of visitors reached the ruins on foot or by sled, along the old pack trail.[^1]

In 2016 two local outdoor enthusiasts built a new shelter on top of the stone walls. They believed they had verbal permission from the Ministry of Forests, Lands and Natural Resource Operations. The Ministry ordered work stopped on the nearly finished structure and fined one of the two men $20,000.[^1]

Rec Sites and Trails BC, which looks after the trail and the lookout site, set up a working group to weigh all options.[^1] The page says the agency decided to dismantle the new cabin. It calls that decision again controversial, and says the dismantling is scheduled for summer 2022.[^1]

No source I could read describes what remains at the site today.
