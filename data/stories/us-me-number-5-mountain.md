Number 5 Mountain stands in the heart of northwestern Somerset County, Maine, inside the Leuthold Forest Preserve. The Nature Conservancy owns and manages the preserve.[^1] A 2019 Press Herald column dates the fire tower to 1933 and places it on the open summit ledges.[^1]

The sources we read do not say who built the tower, who staffed it, or when its watch ended. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing and gives it no asterisk, the mark it uses for towers whose cab is gone.[^2]

By 2019 the Nature Conservancy had removed the tower's lower steps because of the public risk.[^1] It warns people not to climb the tower.[^3] The summit is reached on foot. Getting there involves a moderate section of trail, and TNC trail crews restored the old fire warden's trail that climbs the mountain from the south.[^1] The Press Herald calls the route a 6-mile round-trip hike.[^1]

We found no recent inspection, so the tower's current condition is unconfirmed.
