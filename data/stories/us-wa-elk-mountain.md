Elk Mountain rises to 4,538 feet in Cowlitz County, in the St. Helens Tree Farm.[^1][^4]

Willhite dates the earliest known lookout on the summit, a person or camp, to 1929.[^4] A crow's nest had gone up there around 1927 or 1928.[^3]

The Register dates the first built tower to 1930 and describes a 60-foot pole tower with a cab and living quarters.[^1] A 1930 newspaper quoted by Forest Lookouts described a 57-foot tower, and Willhite gives the same height.[^3][^4] The 1936 annual report, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, says an older white fir tower had been built "a decade ago" and had become dangerous. It was replaced by a 60-foot cedar tower.[^3] If that tower was built about a decade before 1936, it predates the Register's 1930 date.[^1][^3] Willhite's photo caption gives the 1936 tower a height of 66 feet.[^4]

Jake Nordvik was an early lookout. A December 1937 story, reprinted by Forest Lookouts, says he had held the post for 11 years and hewed the timbers for his tower himself.[^3] Firelookout.com lists him from 1927? to 1947.[^2] That story places the mountain in Lewis County, while the Register and Willhite place it in Cowlitz County.[^3][^1][^4]

According to the Register, the tower came down in the winter of 1939, and a 30-foot live-in tower took its place in 1940.[^1] Firelookout.com describes that replacement as a 30-foot treated-timber tower.[^2] Willhite quotes a state report that calls it a 29-foot ring-connected tower with a ground house.[^4]

Willhite says the Aircraft Warning Service used the site for a time in 1942.[^4] Firelookout.com lists other lookouts from 1948 through 1954 and Leland Peterson in 1963.[^2] Firelookout.com says the Columbus Day storm of 1962 damaged the tower.[^2]

Martin Burrows was the lookout in 1966. Willhite says he was 18 and reached the tower by a three-mile hike with no electricity, phone or outhouse.[^4]

The Register and firelookout.com both date the abandonment to 1968 and describe the site as gone.[^1][^2] Willhite says it was blown down and abandoned around 1968.[^4]

Willhite says roads run to within half a mile of the lookout, but legal use requires a St. Helens Tree Farm Permit.[^4] The final approach climbs steeply up a road that has been taken out of service, starting from a high parking lot.[^4] He expects the views to be excellent in good weather.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today.

The Register lists the site as registered on April 7, 2020, nominated by Tammy McLeod.[^1]
