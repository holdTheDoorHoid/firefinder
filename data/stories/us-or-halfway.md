The Halfway lookout stands in the yard of the Pine Ranger Station, a Forest Service office about two miles southeast of Halfway in Baker County.[^1][^2] A 2013 visitor noted that it sits in a valley instead of on a mountain.[^3]

The register describes a 52-foot open timber tower with a 7-by-7-foot observation cab and catwalk, built in 1938 at the Pine Field Office of the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest.[^1] Firelookout.com calls it a treated timber tower and gives the same height and build year.[^2] The sources say little about its early years. A 2023 visitor found little information and said it was unclear when it was last used for steady fire detection.[^4]

Its current role is emergency use.[^2][^3] The register says it can be used in case of emergencies, and visitors report that it is still used during emergencies.[^1][^3][^4] The register listed it on July 3, 2022, as US 1623, OR 144.[^1] The sources we read name no lookout staff and no fires connected with it.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The register puts the site at 2,566 feet, while firelookout.com and the 2013 visit give 2,600 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

A standing-lookout directory lists it as unmanned and as legally reachable by the public. That column does not mean the tower itself is open.[^5] The 2023 visitor noted that a fence now surrounds the tower to keep people from climbing it, and that a ranger onsite might know the story if you stop in during visiting hours.[^4] Please do not climb it.
