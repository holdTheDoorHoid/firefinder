The Los Alamos Lookout is also called the East Gate Tower. It is a security tower at the entrance to Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL).[^2][^3] The register places it at the former Main Gate of the lab, in Los Alamos County, at 7,020 feet.[^1] From 1943 to 1957 the town was closed off, and everyone going in, residents included, cleared security checkpoints.[^3]

When the Atomic Energy Commission (AEC) set up the lab in 1943, it took over most of the land there and much of the fire protection.[^2] Under a cooperative agreement with the Forest, the AEC pledged to watch the area from its security tower by the main entrance.[^2] The Los Alamos Fire Department carried out those duties.[^2] Fire plans listed a Los Alamos Lookout from 1944, but no photographs of that first tower have been found.[^2] The current tower was built in 1948.[^2] The register says the 1948 tower replaced an earlier structure that the AEC and the Forest Service ran jointly from 1944 to 1948.[^1] Zinn's wording suggests an AEC security tower that the fire department staffed, so the two accounts differ on who ran the earlier structure.[^1][^2]

The register says the lookout was mainly staffed by Los Alamos Fire Department personnel after 1951.[^1] Zinn's history describes firefighter Longino Vigil, who worked four-hour shifts in the lookout from 1951 through 1953 and occasionally spotted smokes with an Osborne firefinder.[^2] Jim Trehern joined the department in 1962 and also worked some shifts in the tower.[^2] I found no notable fires named in these sources.

The sources do not agree on when the tower stopped being used. Zinn's overview says that during the 1970s the Los Alamos tower closed.[^2] Zinn writes that in the 1970s the AEC gave part of its land back to the Forest, after which fire department personnel still staffed the tower, though fewer fires occurred. Zinn also says that by the time Jim Trehern was fire chief, from 1981 to 1987, the tower was no longer used for fire detection.[^2] The register says the lookout operated on and off for about 30 years.[^1]

The lab's own post says that after renovations the tower now greets visitors to town, as a National Historic Lookout rather than an active guard station.[^3] The lookout was added to the register on December 31, 2023.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^4] I found no public visiting or climbing rules. The tower sits at a laboratory gate, so treat it as a landmark to look at, and do not climb or enter it.
