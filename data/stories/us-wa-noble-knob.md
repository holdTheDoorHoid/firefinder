The Noble Knob lookout stood in Pierce County, Washington, in the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administered it.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register registered the site on June 18, 2012.[^1]

The Register says the L-4 cab went up in 1934.[^1] The Fire Lookout site gives the same year.[^2] WillhiteWeb disagrees. It says the cab was likely built in 1932 or 1933, and it dates an order for a storage shed to 1933.[^3] Forest Lookouts records panoramic photographs taken on August 15, 1934, by George Clisby.[^4] WillhiteWeb also says panoramic images were taken from the roof that year.[^3]

The Register says the cab was removed in 1954.[^1] The Fire Lookout site says it was destroyed in 1954.[^2] WillhiteWeb says the lookout was last used in 1954, that surveyors found it abandoned in 1955, and that it was destroyed in 1961.[^3] These sources do not agree on what happened or when, and we did not find one that settles it.

WillhiteWeb's photo captions include a footing and an anchor, both labeled 2026.[^3] We found no source that describes a standing structure today. The guide describes a hike of 2 to 10 miles from gravel roads, depending on the route.[^3] It directs drivers 30.5 miles along Highway 410 from Enumclaw, then onto road 7174 to Corral Pass.[^3] The guide mentions no gate or permit.[^3]
