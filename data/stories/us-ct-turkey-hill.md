The Turkey Hill Observation Tower stood in Cockaponset State Forest, in Haddam, Connecticut.[^1][^2] Company 1195 of the Civilian Conservation Corps put it up in July and August of 1938, drawing timber from the surrounding forest.[^1] The site had burned in a forest fire in 1932.[^1] The tower was an open wooden structure 23 feet high, with a 10-foot square platform. Two flights of railed stairs, with one landing between them, climbed to the platform.[^1]

The crew worked out of Camp Filley.[^1] The Haddam Historical Society says the camp was established in 1933 within the state forest.[^3] A 2009 Hartford Courant column says more than 200 men from the camp blazed the main trail and its smaller trails.[^4]

Forest Lookouts lists the tower under Haddam, Middlesex County.[^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register recorded it on September 9, 2008, on a nomination from Henry Isenberg.[^1]

Forest Lookouts marks the tower as removed.[^2] The register says the concrete footings remain.[^1] A 2009 Hartford Courant column described a concrete and iron foundation beside the trail.[^4] The Connecticut Forest and Park Association puts the footings east of the Cockaponset Trail, just north of Jericho Road.[^5]

Today, what remains is the concrete footings, not the tower.[^1][^2] They sit in the state forest near the Cockaponset Trail, which is marked with blue blazes.[^1][^5] We found no source that describes a sign or marker at the spot.
