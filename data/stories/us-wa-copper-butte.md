Copper Butte is the highest point in Ferry County and in the Kettle Range, within the Colville National Forest.[^1][^2] In July 1921 the forest supervisor reported that construction had started on a lookout on the summit, and the plan called for pack horses to carry the material up the mountain.[^2] A cupola cabin was built there that year.[^2] A telephone line was also under construction to the summit, so that fires could be reported quickly to the district rangers.[^2]

A 1925 U.S. Geological Survey note described a 12-by-12 house on the summit, topped by a cupola about six feet square.[^2]

The Fire Lookout page names three lookouts: Ray Ward in 1923, Harold White in 1929 and Lee Roy Kinnan in 1945.[^3] A 1994 fire consumed close to 11,000 acres of woodland around the butte.[^2]

The sources disagree about how the cabin ended. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and WillhiteWeb say the cupola was removed in the 1950s, while the Fire Lookout page says the cabin was destroyed in that decade.[^1][^2][^3]

No structure is left: a 2024 hike write-up describes only a rusted bed frame and scattered debris.[^4] The Washington Trails Association describes the summit as a former fire lookout site on the Kettle Crest trail, reached from the Old Stagecoach Trail.[^5] The hike write-up puts the summit three miles along the route, for a six-mile round trip.[^4] Today the summit is a hike to a viewpoint, not a tower to climb.[^5]
