Washington Butte's lookout was a 40-foot pole tower topped by an L-4 cab, built in 1936 in Okanogan County, Washington.[^1][^2][^3] The site sits about eight miles southwest of Pateros, in the Wenatchee National Forest.[^1][^3]

Willhite's 1938 photographs were shot from a roof 52 feet up. That figure does not match the 40-foot height in the register, and the sources do not explain the difference.[^1][^2]

The tower was removed in 1952.[^1][^2]

A June 2020 visit found that the old summit road, reached from the saddle, has become a footpath.[^2] Willhite calls the walk easy and open, about a quarter mile with roughly 200 feet of climb.[^2] One photo from that visit is captioned "Remaining relic," and a second caption line guesses "Maybe a metal jug."[^2]

Forest roads reach the area from the Chelan-Manson Highway.[^2] Willhite says any vehicle can use them, and they typically open in spring as the snow melts.[^2] With no tower standing, there is nothing to climb.[^1]
