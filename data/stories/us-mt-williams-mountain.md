The lookout stood on Williams Mountain in Meagher County, Montana, about 12 miles west of Neihart, in the Lewis and Clark National Forest.[^1]

The Forest Service put it up in 1937.[^1][^2] The firelookout.com record gives it as a 50-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^1] A 1937 Big Timber Pioneer item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says four such towers were finished or nearly finished in the Little Belt Mountains that year, and that one stood on Williams Mountain.[^2] The item describes the towers' main supports as four 50-foot logs, with a 14-foot square cabin and 2-foot catwalks.[^2]

Lookouts worked here for the summer season.[^2] A 1938 Independent Record item names Arnold H. Chloupeck as the summer lookout, and a 1941 Billings Gazette item names Bill Jones.[^2] Jones had to bring in creosote to coat the tower's legs. Flies crept into the cracks, and woodpeckers after the flies made his perch unusually unsteady.[^2]

In August 1946 lookouts on Williams Mountain and on Porphyry Peak reported a fire near Black Butte, short of Ringling, and a county crew was sent out to fight it.[^2]

A National Geodetic Survey station record says the Forest Service described the station in 1964 as a lookout house on a tower.[^2]

The firelookout.com record says the tower was destroyed in 1971.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Montana list also marks it as gone, without a date.[^3] Neither source says how it was destroyed.[^1][^3]

We found no source that describes what remains at the site, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
