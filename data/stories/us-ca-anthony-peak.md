Anthony Peak Lookout stands in the Mendocino National Forest, in Mendocino County, California, at about 2,100 meters.[^1] Its build year is disputed.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register gives 1932,[^1] but a Forest Lookouts history says the CCC built it in 1934 and quotes a 1994 Ukiah newspaper that names the California Conservation Corps.[^2]

The Register describes an L-4 pyramid roof design, with a wooden live-in cab and catwalk on a wood tower.[^1] The tower was enclosed in 1941 so that it could be staffed all year as a station that warned of enemy aircraft during World War II.[^1]

The sources read give only scattered staffing notes. A 1940 item names Ralph McCombs as in charge of the station, and a 1946 item says a lookout was established with Bob Pinkerton, of Covelo, in charge.[^2] A 1956 item says equipment had to be carried the last mile on people's backs because a snowdrift blocked the truck.[^2] In 2012 the North Pass Fire burned about 42,000 acres, much of it on the Covelo Ranger District, and precautions were taken to protect the lookout by wrapping the building in foil.[^2]

The lookout was found unsafe and condemned in 1990, after it had been used continuously until then.[^2] The Forest Service and volunteers rehabilitated it in 1993.[^2] The Register credits the restoration to the Forest Service and the Twin Rocks Landowners Association, and it accepted the lookout on September 16, 1994, as US 88, CA 2.[^1] In July 1994 the Covelo Ranger District announced a dedication ceremony to reopen it.[^2]

Today, a 2026 article about the rebuilt High Glade lookout says Anthony Peak is one of three lookouts left on the Mendocino National Forest and that it is staffed each year from May to October.[^3] The sources read do not say whether visitors may climb the tower, and none lists it as a rental. Anyone planning a trip should check with the Mendocino National Forest first.
