Mt. Peter is a fire lookout in McNairy County, Tennessee, and the Tennessee Division of Forestry is named as its administrator [^1]. The registry describes the tower as an 80-foot Aermotor LS-40 [^1]. The agency favored this model above all others, and it stayed in service there until the mid-1970s [^3].

I found no build date for this tower in the sources I checked [^1][^2]. The agency raised many of its lookouts in the 1930s, working with the Civilian Conservation Corps [^3]. The LS-40's cab is steel and about 7 feet on each side [^3]. The sources I read say nothing about who staffed the tower or about any fires it watched [^1][^2][^3].

A National Register study of the state forestry agency's towers lists Mt Peter as extant as of March 26, 2014 [^3]. The Tennessee Landforms list also marks it as standing [^2]. The registry entry dates from August 6, 2023 [^1], and its visit notes say the stair treads were recently swapped out [^1].

The sources disagree about who owns the land [^1]. The registry names the forestry agency as administrator, but its details also read "Privately Owned" [^1]. The entry's writer believes the site is privately owned, though it carries no marking to say so [^1]. The record gives no public access route, and ownership is still unresolved [^1]. Until ownership is confirmed, treat the site as private and do not climb it.
