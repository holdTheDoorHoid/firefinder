The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register dates the Paradise Peak lookout to about 1931. It describes a ground cabin of National Park Service design and cites the Thornton Survey for the date.[^1] A May 1931 item from the Visalia Times-Delta, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, said the lookout was 9,370 feet high, that supplies reached the keeper from Atwell Mill, and that a glass and wooden house sat on the rock.[^2] The same item says W.P. Sutherland would return to the station on May 15, after working there the season before.[^2]

The sources disagree on who managed the land. The register places the lookout in Sequoia National Park, administered by the National Park Service.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page files it under Sequoia National Forest.[^2] The register's construction photo is credited to the National Park Service, but that does not settle the question, and I could not resolve it from the pages I read.[^1]

The register gives no removal date.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the Paradise Peak site as gone.[^3] I found no account of how the structure came down, and no source describing what, if anything, remains at the site. I found no source describing access either, so check with the managing agency before going to the summit.[^1]
