The Big Quilcene lookout sat about five miles northwest of Quilcene, in the Olympic National Forest.[^2][^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates it to the 1930s and says it no longer stands.[^1] Willhite says the Kresek Lookout Inventory is almost the only source, and that it suggests a Forest Service cabin from the 1930s.[^3]

I found no source that names anyone who staffed the lookout. In 1981 local rangers asked a former lookout man, Cliff Schafer, to write a history of his experiences, and he claimed he helped build the lookout.[^3] Willhite says Schafer may have helped build the 1930s cabin, but he does not confirm it.[^3]

The sources do not settle whether a lookout stood here. Willhite could not find any footings or guy anchors, and he has found no map showing a trail or fire lookout there.[^3] The summit road first appears on a 1968 Olympic National Forest map.[^3] Willhite suggests the lookout may have been removed the year before his visit, but his page does not date the visit.[^3]

The summit is reached by a good gravel road, which Willhite describes as a drive-up.[^3] Willhite found a concrete slab on the summit, near what he calls the likely ham antenna tower. Ray Kresek, quoted on the same page, thinks what is left of an antenna tower was probably erected in the 1970s.[^3] The sources do not say whether the road is gated.[^3]
