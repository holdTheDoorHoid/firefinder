East Butte, which the Forest Lookouts page also calls Little Butte, sits in Bingham County, Idaho, where the Bureau of Land Management ran a fire lookout.[^1] Firelookout.com lists a cabin on the site from the 1950s, though it marks that date with a question mark.[^2]

Firelookout.com names Mr. and Mrs. Ralph King as the known lookout personnel for 1961.[^2] A Twin Falls Times News item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, dated April 1, 1962, describes the Kings living in a trailer about 75 feet from the ridge top.[^1] The article says residents killed twenty-three rattlers on the butte that summer, and that the worst fire the Kings worked that season for the BLM was named after Dead Horse cave.[^1]

The article says the duties were moved to Big Southern Butte.[^1] Firelookout.com says the East Butte lookout was discontinued once a lookout was established at Big Southern Butte, and it marks the East Butte site as gone.[^2] That Big Southern Butte tower, a two-story slanted-window cab, was built in 1963 and was gone by the summer of 2013.[^3]

Wikipedia says the mountain is on Idaho National Laboratory property, which restricts access, though a road goes up to several radio towers at the top.[^4] The 1962 article already mentions television towers, a transmitter and a power company unit on the butte.[^1] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout itself.

The sources disagree on height. Wikipedia gives 6,572 feet for the summit, firelookout.com gives 6,549 feet, and the 1962 article puts the butte at 6,800 feet, with the lookout another 1,010 feet above it.[^4][^2][^1]
