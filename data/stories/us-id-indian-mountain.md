Indian Mountain Lookout sits on a basalt ridge in Adams County, Idaho, inside the Payette National Forest, with views over the West Mountains and the northern Weiser River Valley.[^1][^4] The National Register nomination puts the ridge at 7,255 feet; the National Historic Lookout Register gives 7,136 feet.[^1][^4]

Fire detection began on the ridge in 1910, when a tent camp was set up there.[^1] A log cupola cabin followed in 1922.[^1][^2]

The present two-story lookout went up in 1933.[^2][^4] Civilian Conservation Corps enrollees built it from a modified Forest Service plan that let pre-cut and prefabricated parts be carried to remote sites, often by mule train.[^4] The cab sits on a storage room and is reached by wood stairs and an exterior catwalk.[^4] The National Historic Lookout Register notes that no other lookout of its type was built on the Payette National Forest.[^1]

The sources disagree about how long it stayed staffed. Andy Clelland appears as a lookout in 1942, the earliest name in the staff lists I could read.[^2][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register describes the tower as still in active service.[^1] A post dated August 11, 2021 on Idaho Fire Lookouts says the tower is staffed for fire detection.[^2] The firelookout.com listing says it was staffed in the summer "until recently," while the National Register nomination says staffed activity ended in 2015 and the tower is now automated.[^3][^4]

Restoration work in 1993 and 1994 returned the tower to its original specifications, and new shingles went on the roof in 1993.[^1][^4] In 2017 the Forest Service found the stairs, catwalk and railings unsafe and replaced them, raising the railings to meet current building codes.[^4] Wikidata gives 11 August 2022 as the start date of the tower's National Register listing.[^5]

Today the lookout is reached by driving to the end of National Forest Road 243, and Idaho Fire Lookouts tags it as drive-up.[^4][^2] The sources I read describe no rental or public tours. The Forest Service still uses the site for fire detection.[^4] I found no public visiting arrangement, so ask the Payette National Forest before planning a visit to the cab.
