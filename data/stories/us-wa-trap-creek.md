Trap Creek Lookout stood on a summit in Pacific County, Washington.[^3] Sources give its distance from Raymond as nine or eleven miles to the south.[^2][^4] The Washington Division of Forestry built a wooden tower with a slant-walled cab in 1942.[^1][^2][^3] The sources disagree on its height. The register and FireLookout.com say 30 feet, while WillhiteWeb and a 1953 Coast and Geodetic Survey description say 42 feet.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

In 1961 the state Department of Natural Resources replaced it with a new wooden tower.[^1][^3] The register and FireLookout.com put that tower at 50 feet, and WillhiteWeb puts it at 40 feet.[^1][^2][^3] A DNR report covering 1960 to 1962 says its crew finished nine lookouts in two years, one of them at Trap Creek.[^4]

WillhiteWeb names Maude Thompson as the lookout in 1964 and 1965, although its list repeats the 1965 year.[^3]

The register says the DNR tower was suddenly abandoned in 1967 and probably removed soon after.[^1] FireLookout.com says it was probably destroyed, and WillhiteWeb says it was abandoned and destroyed.[^2][^3] A 1967 survey note says the tower had been taken down and rebuilt south of the survey mark, but the note does not say when.[^4]

WillhiteWeb says the summit is on private timber company land, which was owned by Rayonier Timber in 2015.[^3] It says visitors need a permit to go past the gates.[^3] Its photos show old concrete footings and a survey benchmark, with the footings shoved aside among small trees.[^3] Anyone thinking of visiting should ask the landowner first.
