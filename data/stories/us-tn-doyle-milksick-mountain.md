Milksick Mountain sits in White County, Tennessee, and the register puts the tower site at 1,671 feet (509 meters).[^1] Local history and legend tie the name to early settlers who saw sickness and deaths follow drinking milk from the mountain's grazing cattle.[^1] The mountain was eventually fenced to keep cattle off, after the harm mounted.[^1]

The lookout that stood here was a 50-foot tower with a metal cab, an Aermotor tower administered by the Tennessee Division of Forestry.[^1]

The register records the tower as still standing at least until the early 1990s.[^1] A Tennessee lookout-tower list marks the Milksick Mountain tower as removed.[^2] That list gives the elevation as 1,640 feet, which does not match the register's 1,671 feet.[^2][^1]

By April 2018, the register describes only the concrete footings.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site in February 2019.[^1] No source describes the site since then, or says whether the public can reach it, so access is unknown. At that last description, no tower stood there to climb.[^1]
