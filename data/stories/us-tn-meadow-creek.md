Meadow Creek Lookout stands on a hilltop in the Cherokee National Forest, in Cocke County, Tennessee, near Del Rio.[^1][^5] The register says an earlier tower stood on the site in 1926; it was a platform mounted between three chestnut trees. It calls the present block tower the fifth to stand there.[^1] The register does not give a build year for the block tower.[^1] A user-submitted travel listing says the tower dates to the early 1920s, and that claim cannot be checked against the register.[^5][^1]

The block cab was two stories tall and built of cinderblock.[^1] Jeff Flanary nominated the tower and visited it on September 20, 2008.[^1] He found it in decent shape but in need of paint, a new door and glass, and interior restoration.[^1] The visit sheet records that it was not staffed.[^1]

Vandalism to the original structure led to the conversion.[^3] By 2013 the tower had been converted into an all-steel viewing platform open to the public, and about $23,000 was spent to keep one of the county's last two lookouts.[^3] A 2017 blog post says Cocke County funded the work, and that the live-in cabin was removed and replaced with a plain metal grated deck, with the aim of making the tower resistant to vandalism.[^4]

The two registers do not agree on its status. The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on January 3, 2015.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register listed the same location on March 16, 2015, as a former site.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register text still describes a block tower, and its visit note dates from 2008, so the entry may predate the 2013 conversion.[^1][^3]

A travel listing says the Forest Service opened the tower to visitors, that the drive ends at a gravel parking lot, and that a short walk leads to the top of the hill.[^5] No official Forest Service notice on the tower's current condition was found. The 2008 visit sheet also noted that no signs directed visitors to the tower.[^1] Check with the Cherokee National Forest before planning a visit.
