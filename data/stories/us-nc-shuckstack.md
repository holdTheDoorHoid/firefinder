Shuckstack's fire tower went up in 1934. The National Historic Lookout Register says the Public Works Administration and the National Park Service built it that year, as a steel Aermotor tower about 60 feet tall with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1][^3] The tower was used to spot wildfires, and later it became a radio repeater site.[^3] The summit also has remains of an old fire watchman's cabin: a chimney, a foundation and a cistern.[^2]

The register added the tower in 2007, with Peter Barr as its nominator.[^1] Barr, director of the North Carolina chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association, took on its cause.[^3] In 2008 the chapter was raising money and seeking a cost-share partnership with the Park Service for the restoration.[^4] In 2009 a group hike went up to the tower to inspect it and judge its state, and the chapter's report that year says it had raised over $700 for the restoration.[^5]

By January 2013 the tower was in poor shape. An article from that month described rusting bolts, rotting floor planks and shattered windows, and it said some guardrails had gone missing. It also described serious vandalism during the previous year.[^3] About $4,000 was on deposit for the tower's restoration and repair.[^3]

The chapter's 2015 report lists the tower as rehabilitated, but its goals for 2016 still included planning an announcement for the restoration.[^6] I could not find a park statement that settles how much work was done.

The park once had 11 towers, and seven were demolished in the 1980s. One of the four that remained had since been shut to visitors, according to that 2013 article.[^3] Wikipedia says the tower has been retired from service but people may still visit it.[^2] The summit is reached by the Appalachian Trail and a short spur trail about 3.5 miles from the parking area on the north side of Fontana Dam, and Wikipedia calls the hike strenuous.[^2] Because of the damage reported in 2013, check the tower's condition before you climb.
