Tidbits Mountain lookout stood in the Willamette National Forest, in Linn County, Oregon, about 10 miles northwest of McKenzie Bridge.[^2] The site sits at 5,185 feet, or 1,580 meters.[^1]

The sources differ on when the lookout began. Forest Lookouts says the site was established as a lookout point in 1912, and that a lookout structure was built there around 1914 by John Short.[^3] The register's account begins in 1915, when the site had an 8-foot platform and a log cabin roughly an eighth of a mile west of the summit.[^1]

The cab is dated to 1925 or 1926, depending on the source. Forest Lookouts describes a 14-by-14-foot Supervisor Hall design house built in 1925.[^3] The register and Rex's Fire Lookout Page both describe it as a Supervisor Hall Special, a ground cab built in 1926.[^1][^2] A July 1925 newspaper item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, called Tidbits the seventh and last of the lookout stations being started that week.[^3]

Several newspaper items that Forest Lookouts quotes name people tied to the mountain. A 1922 item said J.A. Moe had been lookout man there since 1912.[^3] A 1939 item said John W. Turbyne was stationed on the mountain.[^3]

The register says the cab was removed in 1967, and Rex's page says it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2] Forest Lookouts records that on October 27, 1967, the lookout was burned at its location.[^3] A November 1967 memo quoted on the same page discusses burning three lookouts before the AD-112s reached them, and it lists Tidbits with two others.[^3]

The register recorded the site on May 14, 2011, on the nomination of Ray Kresek.[^1]

A 2023 hiking feature in The Bulletin describes the Tidbits Mountain South Trail, which the article puts about 20 minutes up a rough logging road from Blue River. An editor's note on that article says the trail had temporarily closed because of the Lookout Fire.[^4] No source we read describes what remains of the lookout on the summit today.
