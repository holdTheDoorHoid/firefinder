The First Thought lookout stood on First Thought Mountain, near the town of Orient, in Stevens County and the Colville National Forest.[^1][^3]

The register dates its first structure, a 35-foot pole platform with a log shelter at its base, to 1916.[^1][^2] A 1914 newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page describes a station recently built on First Thought Hill, so a lookout may have stood there earlier than the register's date.[^3]

A 60-foot pole tower with a 6-by-6 cab replaced the platform in 1925, according to the register and firelookout.com.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb says that tower stood 76 feet, and a June 1925 item quoted on the Forest Lookouts page describes a 70-foot wooden tower.[^5][^3]

Footings for a steel tower with an L-4 cab were poured in 1946.[^3][^5] In 1947 a 40-foot steel Aermotor tower with an L-4 cab went up, and the Forest Lookouts page says its steel was reused from a parachute-training tower at Farragut, a recruit base on Idaho's Lake Pend Orielle.[^1][^3][^5]

Keith Miller was the lookout in 1928.[^5] The firelookout.com list of known lookout personnel ends with Ernie Keyes, for 1958 and 1959, and gives no end date for staffing.[^2]

In 1985 the Orient Improvement Clubs bought the lookout, intending to move it to Orient Community Park as a historic attraction.[^3][^5] In 1986, after attempts to airlift it failed, a crane lowered the cab onto a trailer and moved it to the park.[^5][^3][^1]

The register's photo caption, however, says the cab moved in 2001, which conflicts with the register's own text.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Washington list shows a standing tower at Orient and marks the original First Thought Mountain site as gone.[^4]

Today the cab is in Orient Community Park.[^1][^4] None of the pages checked gives visiting hours or says whether the cab can be entered, so check locally before visiting.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5]
