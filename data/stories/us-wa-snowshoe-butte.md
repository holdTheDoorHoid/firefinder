Snowshoe Butte rises on the Cascade crest in King County, Washington, between Stampede and Tacoma Passes.[^1][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register places it in the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1]

The first structure went up in 1935.[^1][^2] Willhite says it was "said to be" an L-5 cab.[^4] The register and Firelookout.com both describe an L-5 cab from that year.[^1][^2] A year earlier, in August 1934, the Osborne photo team made panoramas showing felled trees and a line that may have been a phone line or a clothesline.[^4]

The register records the cab as removed in 1965.[^1] Firelookout.com and Willhite say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^4] Washington Lookouts lists the site as removed.[^3] The sources agree on the year but not on how the cab went.

Today a 2022 hike write-up describes rusted metal remains on the summit, which were once part of a lookout standing from 1935 to 1965.[^5] Willhite's photo captions mention a phone line, nails and glass, and remnants at the site.[^4]

Willhite calls the route from Stampede Pass the simplest. You reach it from I-90 at exit 62, and he says high clearance is recommended.[^4] He notes the access road comes close to 5,000 feet, and he describes a short bushwhack with brushy game trails.[^4] Washington Lookouts files the site under the Snoqualmie National Forest, not the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie name the register uses.[^3] No source we read says whether the road or the summit is open to the public.
