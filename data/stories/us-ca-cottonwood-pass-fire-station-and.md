The Cottonwood Pass station combines a fire station and a lookout under one roof.[^1] It sits in Kings County, overlooking the Kettleman Plains.[^1][^2]

By May 1935 the state's forestry division had a lookout man at the pass.[^2] That year E.R. Wiles was put in charge of a fire truck there.[^2] In 1936 the station was set to open on Rattlesnake Hill in the pass, and in 1937 it was moved to a better-placed site, with a new road under construction.[^2]

In October 1941 winds reaching 60 miles per hour tore the roof off the lookout station.[^2] The lookout's family relocated to Kettleman City during the repairs.[^2]

The sources do not agree on when the present building went up. The Register gives 1953 and describes a combined CDF Plan 804 station and lookout, which it calls the only building of its kind.[^1] A Fresno-Kings unit history dates completion to the early 1940s and says Cottonwood was the county's only CDF Schedule B station.[^3] An archive caption for a photograph taken in December 1952 already names the lookout and fire control station.[^4]

Later years are thinly recorded. A former firefighter quoted in the Register said that in 1976 the lookout went unmanned and was mostly kept shut.[^1] Another former firefighter recalled the station reopening in 1985 after a couple of years closed.[^1] The Register says the lookout was operating in the early 1980s and is now abandoned.[^1] The unit history gives 1986 as the year the station closed.[^3]

Today the Register describes the lookout as abandoned and partly exposed to the weather and to roosting birds.[^1] The site still has a 10,000-gallon concrete tank for water.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list still shows the building as standing.[^5] No public use is documented, and the building should not be entered.
