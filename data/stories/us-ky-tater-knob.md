Tater Knob is the highest point in Bath County, Kentucky.[^3] The Forest Service built its lookout there in 1934, and the Civilian Conservation Corps erected a 35-foot steel tower with a wooden cab on top.[^1][^2] For decades the tower spotted wildfires in the Daniel Boone National Forest, and it stopped being used in the 1970s.[^3][^4]

One history says that in 1959 the cab was rebuilt on a shorter structure, half as tall as the original.[^2]

On December 3, 2008, fire destroyed the cab.[^1][^2] A local news report says vandals set fire to the wooden cab, while the Forest Service and the Eastern lookouts history call it arson.[^1][^2][^4]

In a statement, the Forest Service said officials were, at that time, uncertain whether the structure would be rebuilt.[^1] The cab has since been taken off the tower, and the platform has a hole in it.[^3]

Brent Frizzell, a former Salt Lick fire chief who responded to the 2008 fire, has been involved since 2017 and now leads the restoration effort.[^3] The project has a $75,000 grant.[^3][^4] LEX18 says it came in 2023, while the local news report says the county was awarded it in 2024.[^3][^4]

Before rebuilding can start, required historical and archaeological surveys must be finished. They are expected to cost about $11,000, which the grant does not cover.[^3][^4] Frizzell doubts the roughly $77,000 on hand will be enough as construction costs climb.[^3]

A September 17, 2026 report describes a meeting at the tower with local, state and federal officials and the Tater Knob Restoration Group.[^4]

The tower remains closed to the public. Its stairs have worn down, plants are taking over the site, and the Forest Service has said it is no longer safe for visitors.[^1][^3][^4] If the cab is completed, the area around the tower will be cleared for a better view of the national forest, the county judge says.[^4] Donations can be made in person at the Bath County History Museum or through Venmo.[^3]
