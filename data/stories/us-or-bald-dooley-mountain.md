Sources give two different build dates for the lookout cab on Bald (Dooley) Mountain, and they do not agree on how long it stood. A July 1933 item reported that a couple had moved out to the Bald Mountain lookout station, and Rex's page says a 14 x 14 lookout cabin was constructed in 1934.[^5] The register's Bald (Dooley) entry gives 1934 for an L-4 cab, and firelookout.com's Bald Mountain page agrees on 1934 and also lists a camp in the 1920s.[^1][^3] A second register entry, for Dooley Mountain in the same forest and county, dates its L-4 cab to 1936, and firelookout.com's separate Dooley page gives the same year.[^2][^4]

firelookout.com names lookouts across several decades, including W.J. Sargent in 1921, John Bacon in 1932 and Jack Poe in 1965.[^3] Rex's page gives the 1933 lookout as John Badon, which may be a spelling variant of the 1932 name.[^3][^5] In July 1959 the Record-Courier reported that Jim Brooks was manning Bald Mountain, the first season lookout on the Baker district.[^5] In 1970 the Baker Democrat-Herald reported that the Bald Mt. and Mt. Ireland lookouts were staffed around the clock until fire danger eased, and in 1975 the Record-Courier said Patty Piper was in charge of the Bald Mt. state forestry lookout post that summer.[^5]

The Dooley Mountain entry says its cab was abandoned in 1947 and is now gone, and firelookout.com's Dooley page gives the same 1947 date.[^2][^4] The Bald (Dooley) entry says only that its cab is now gone, with no date.[^1] The 1947 date does not sit easily with the 1959, 1970 and 1975 newspaper items about Bald Mountain, and no source we read explains the gap.[^2][^5] firelookout.com's Dooley page also says the site may not have actually been used.[^4]

A 1946 Coast and Geodetic Survey description says the survey station sits in the middle of a Forest Service lookout house, so a house stood on the summit by then.[^5]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the summit now. The register has a 2017 photo of the site, but its caption does not say what the photo shows.[^1] The site is about 14 miles south of Baker City in the Wallowa-Whitman National Forest.[^4][^2] We found no source that says whether it is open to visitors, so check with the forest before you go.
