Passadumkeag Mountain, in Grand Falls Plantation in Penobscot County, got a steel fire tower in 1919.[^1][^2] Materials for it were bought the year before.[^1] A photograph from about 1920 shows the Maine Forest Service tower, and a 360-degree panoramic map of that year is centered on it.[^2][^3]

Sources disagree on its height. A 1919 newspaper item and a Maine Memory description give 36 feet, a 1919 state report gives 39 feet, and a 2023 hiking article gives 30 feet.[^1][^2][^4] The same article says Jesse Harriman served as the fire warden there from 1923 to 1953.[^4]

The Maine Forest Service kept up a Jeep road to the summit tower until 1970.[^4] The article says the lookout was given up in 1970 and felled a few years after that.[^4] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Maine list marks it as gone.[^5]

The 2023 article places the rusted remains of the fallen tower directly behind the summit's lightning shack.[^4] A 2025 hiking guide describes the rusted tower as toppled on its side, behind a little cabin.[^6] The ridge now carries two communications towers and 13 wind turbines.[^4]

Hiking guides describe a walk of about two miles one way from a yellow gate on Greenfield Road, and the 2023 article asks visitors not to block the gate.[^4][^6] A 2025 guide calls the gate locked.[^6] No source says whether the summit is open to the public.[^4][^6]
