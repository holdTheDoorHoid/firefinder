The provincial register lists Carbondale as a cabin-type fire lookout, 5 m (16 ft) tall, on a site 1,807 m (5,930 ft) above sea level.[^1] The lookout stands on Carbondale Hill, which lies between the Carbondale and Castle rivers in southwestern Alberta.[^2][^3]

The register gives 1982 as the year the present structure was built.[^1] A 2020 visitor account traces the names of the hill and the nearby river to a coal mine that had worked close by.[^2] A Mountain Legacy Project repeat-photograph pair of the hill, from 1914 and 2015, shows the forest filling in and the treeline rising, with signs of fire in both images.[^3]

Today the register marks the site as active, with an access mode of "drive" and a weather station on site.[^1] The 2020 account describes a gate on the fire lookout road that prevents motor vehicles from reaching the lookout, and says the party hiked up the road beyond it.[^2] Lookouts are not open to public viewing, the province says, but staff are glad to see hikers who come across a site.[^4] Alberta also bars the public from climbing the lookout tower, and keeps the observer's residence on site for private use.[^5] The 2020 account is the most recent description found, so the building's present condition is not documented here.[^2]
