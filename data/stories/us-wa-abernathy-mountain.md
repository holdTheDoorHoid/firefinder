Abernathy Mountain sits about ten miles northwest of Castle Rock in Cowlitz County, Washington.[^1][^2] A timber company manages the land, though my sources name two different companies.[^1][^3]

The first lookout there was a cabin on the ground, set up in 1929 by the Long-Bell Lumber Company with the state Division of Forestry.[^2][^3] The state built a tower on the summit in 1932.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com gives it as 83 feet tall, while Willhite gives 92 feet.[^2][^3] A tower built by CCC enrollees followed around 1940.[^3] Its height appears as 40, 83 and 85 feet in different places, so I have not settled it.[^2][^3]

In October 1950, high winds knocked the summit tower over.[^2][^3] The state Department of Natural Resources put up a new 40-foot wooden tower with a 14-foot cab in 1951.[^1][^2][^3]

Lookouts kept watch for years.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com names Cliff Helm for 1942 to 1952 and Mrs. George Baxter for 1965.[^2] Willhite names Leland Peterson as the lookout in 1952.[^3] In July 1938, during a fire that had flames on three sides of the mountain, a chief warden telephoned from the lookout.[^3] A newspaper quoted through Willhite said the fire endangered 300 firefighters.[^3]

Aircraft took over the fire watch, and vandals did real damage.[^3] A department official, quoted in a newspaper item that Willhite reprints, blamed vandalism for the tower's fate and said it had not been manned since 1966.[^3]

Sources disagree on when it ended. Firelookout.com says it was abandoned in 1966 and destroyed in 1969.[^2] FFLOS says the 1951 tower was deactivated in 1968, and Willhite says abandoned in 1968 and gone by 1973.[^1][^3]

The tower is gone.[^4] FFLOS describes foundations that date to the 1951 tower, and Willhite's photos show footings at the lookout site.[^1][^3] No source I read describes anything else that remains, or what the view is like now.

The land is company timber ground, and the company sets the access rules.[^1][^3] Willhite says non-motorized access was allowed as of 2018, and that the company closes access when fire danger runs high in hot, dry summers.[^3] Willhite's photo captions show a gate on the route.[^3] A mountain bike trail network covers much of the mountain.[^3] Willhite gives about 7 miles each way and about 2,000 feet of climbing.[^3] Ask the landowner before you go.
