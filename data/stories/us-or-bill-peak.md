Bill Peak, also called Bill's Butte in the old newspapers, rises about 1,541 feet in Coos County, Oregon.[^1][^2]

In 1938 the Coos Fire Patrol added a lookout there, because the area had been hard to watch from its existing stations and the land had just been logged.[^2] The lookout was a ground cabin, and a road and a telephone line were being built to it that year.[^2] The Camp McKinley CCC put up a standard lookout cabin on Bill's Butte that year.[^2][^3] The register describes it as an L-4 cab built by the CCC.[^1] Newspaper reports put the site eight miles southeast of Bandon in 1938 and ten miles in 1939.[^2]

The patrol manned it in its early years. In July 1939 Ned Baker of Marshfield was on duty at the station, and in 1940 an Oregon State College student was assigned there.[^2] In 1948 full-time staffing of the lookout cost $561.00.[^2]

In 1942 the Aircraft Warning Service activated the site on March 9, and the record lists the Roseburg Filter Center alongside it.[^2] By September a sealed, double-walled 10-by-12-foot sleeping quarters was under construction.[^2] When the service was dropped, the state took the site back.[^2] A 1942 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description calls the summit building a white wooden lookout house, 12 feet square and 15 feet high.[^2]

The old lookout was torn down after the 1961 fire season.[^2] A 1961 newspaper item mentioned a new tower being erected there.[^2] The 1962 district report instead describes a new 14-by-14-foot ground house, built at a cost of $3,469.[^2][^3] The register, by contrast, names an Amort cab from 1962.[^1] The lookout was reactivated in 1965, after two years out of use, to watch an area where incendiary fires had been reported.[^2]

The register lists the lookout as removed in 1967.[^1] The state inventory still listed it as a secondary observation point in 1974, and as a secondary lookout in 1977.[^2] The sources disagree on when it came down.[^1][^2] Fire Lookout lists the site simply as gone.[^3]

None of the sources we read describes what remains on the summit today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
