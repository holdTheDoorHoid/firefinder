Bear Mountain carries a fire lookout about 7,200 feet up, northwest of Custer in the southern Black Hills of South Dakota.[^1] The register gives the elevation as 7,166 feet, so the two sources differ slightly.[^1][^2]

The first tower went up in 1910, and an enclosed cab was added in 1914. The cab was enlarged in 1924.[^1] A 1915 newspaper item, quoted by a lookout history site, describes a forest guard posted at the Bear Mountain station throughout the dangerous fire season.[^3]

In 1939 the old wooden tower was taken down and replaced with a 30-foot all-steel tower topped by an L-4 cab.[^3][^2][^4] The Forest Service says the Custer Civilian Conservation Corps built the 1939 tower and its garage.[^1] The cab was modernized in 1967, and in 2010 the old radio shelter was swapped for a prefabricated building.[^1]

A lookout history site lists named personnel starting in 1926.[^4] According to the Forest Service, the lookout is still in use, with staff from late spring to early fall.[^1] The garage, once used for vehicle storage, is now a winter warming station for recreationists, and the Forest Service plans to remove it to make way for a public toilet and two interpretive panels.[^1]

The register added the tower in June 2017. The Forest Service describes that as a first step toward National Register listing.[^2][^1] According to the register, a gravel road runs all the way to the summit.[^2] Because it is still a staffed lookout, check current Forest Service guidance before going up. The sources do not say whether visitors may climb the cab.
