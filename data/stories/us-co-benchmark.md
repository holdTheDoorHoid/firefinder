Benchmark Lookout stands on a high ridge in the San Juan National Forest, north of Dolores in Dolores County, Colorado.[^1][^4] The U.S. Forest Service administers it, and the Dolores Ranger District cooperates.[^1]

The lookout was built in 1970 to replace the Glade Mountain Lookout.[^1][^2] Its cab is a 14-by-14-foot flat-roofed R2 design with a catwalk, set on a 42-foot tower.[^1][^2][^4] The register describes the tower as wood, and a third-party guide calls it steel, so the sources disagree on the material.[^1][^5]

In August 2009 the Narraguinnep Fire grew from 286 acres on a Friday to 4,707 acres by Sunday evening. A cabin and the lookout were evacuated, and an account of the fire said no damage to any structure had been reported yet.[^2]

The register gives a registration date of March 17, 2009, and says a current lookout was keeping up the tower as of 2019.[^1] A 2020 article reports that Rick and Linda Freimuth had spent the past three fire seasons living in the tower, and that Rick had called in 74 smoke reports since 2018.[^4] The same article counts six staffed towers among the 14 Colorado lookouts still standing.[^4]

In 2018 the cab was vandalized before fire season, and the district added a locked gate and fencing. That year the catwalk railing and most of the east deck were replaced.[^1] In 2019 the solar batteries were being replaced with sealed gel batteries for the 2019 season.[^1] By 2021 three new 190-watt panels and a remote camera for district fire staff were in place.[^3] The 2022 report also says 55 stair treads were replaced with Douglas fir and that a new cab door replaced the one vandalized in 2018, though it does not give the year of that work.[^3] It adds that a federal renovation line item for the district lists this tower.[^3]

Today the unpaved Road 26 climbs about 3.9 miles from County Road 27 to the summit, and snow blocks it from October to June, according to a third-party guide.[^5] That guide places a locked metal gate just below the tower, while the register puts the gate 0.3 mile below the tower.[^1][^5] The guide also says the tower sits on an exposed ridge, so anyone there must come off the mountain at once when a thunderstorm nears.[^5] A 2020 article says the San Juan National Forest and nearby agencies rely on the lookout to spot and monitor new fires.[^4] The sources read do not say whether visitors may pass the gate, so check with the Dolores Ranger District first.
