The survey station Wolf Mountain Lot lies in Ripley County, about 15 miles west of Doniphan, and the settlement of Gatewood is about 4 miles south of it.[^1][^2] A steel lookout tower stood there, listed under the Mark Twain National Forest.[^1]

The tower's height is disputed. The Central US Lookouts listing puts the height at 50 feet (15.2 m) for a steel tower with nothing else built on the site.[^1] A 1956 Coast and Geodetic Survey description says the tower was 90 feet (27.4 m) high.[^2] The same description says a reference disk was set at the center of the tower's base and gives the distance found for it as 27.87 feet (8.5 m).[^2] The survey mark's elevation is listed at 922 feet (281 m), a figure scaled from a topographic map.[^2]

Across the National Forests, lookout tower use peaked in the late 1930s and very early 1940s.[^3] By the early 1940s Missouri's national forests had 87 fire lookout towers, and an early protection plan had called for a mix of 100-foot primary towers and 50-foot secondary ones.[^3]

Dwellings, outbuildings and a number of the towers themselves were declared surplus, and their removal from Forest lands began. By 1991 only 22 towers still stood in the Mark Twain National Forest.[^3] The Central US Lookouts site lists the Wolf Mountain tower as removed.[^1]

No source I found describes what remains at the site today.
