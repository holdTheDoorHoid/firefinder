Jay Point Lookout began as a log cabin with a crow's nest, which two lookout-history sites date to 1929.[^1][^2] A 41-foot treated timber tower with an L-4 cab was built in 1955.[^1][^3]

The sources disagree on the start date. Forest Lookouts quotes a newspaper report dated September 15, 1928, which lists "Jay peak" among new structures on the Lolo forest.[^3] We could not confirm that the report means this site, so we follow the 1929 date from the other two pages.[^1][^2]

The Idaho Fire Lookouts page lists known staff for 1972 and 1976, and asks readers to add what they know.[^2]

The Jay Point Fire was a lightning-caused ignition on August 17, 2015, in the upper reaches of the Lochsa subbasin, south of the Lochsa River near Powell, Idaho.[^5] It burned 3,946 acres of national forest land and 502 acres of private timber land.[^5]

The lookout burned in that fire.[^1][^2][^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's 2015 chapter report says retardant drops could not save it, and that it had been used for several decades.[^4]

Today, no source we read describes what, if anything, is left at the site. The Association's Idaho list still marks the tower as gone.[^6] Before the fire, the Idaho Fire Lookouts page listed it as a hike-in site.[^2] We found nothing on whether the ground is open to visitors now, so ask the Clearwater National Forest before going.
