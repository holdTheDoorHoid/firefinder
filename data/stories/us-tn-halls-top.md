The Halls Top Fire Tower is in Cocke County, Tennessee, inside the Cherokee National Forest.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the U.S. Forest Service built it in 1936 and lists the agency as its administrator.[^1] The Register gives the site elevation as 3,610 feet (1,100 meters).[^1] It entered the tower on May 22, 2022, as US 1577 and TN 39, on the nomination of Michael Jardine and Roger Ford.[^1]

Map data lists a Hall Top Lookout at the site and tags it as an observation tower with lattice construction.[^2] The sources I read say nothing about who staffed the tower in its working years, which fires it watched, or whether it was ever moved or rebuilt.

A news report, undated in the copy I read, describes a wildfire in the Hall Top area that grew to 225 acres. Authorities thought the fire was probably arson, and a district ranger said an investigator would likely come in to find where it started. The report does not say the tower was damaged.[^4]

A Trails Offroad guide says the tower is still standing, but it warns that the stairs are dilapidated and that climbing them would put a visitor at risk.[^3] The guide describes a road leading up to the tower, while the Register says it is reached by the 9.9-mile Stone Mountain Trail, so the sources describe the approach differently.[^1][^3] I found no closure notice or restoration record in the sources read. Anyone planning a visit should check with the Unaka Ranger District of the Cherokee National Forest first, and should not climb the stairs as the guide describes them.
