Mount Watatic lies in Ashburnham and Ashby, Massachusetts, just south of the New Hampshire border.[^3] From about 1916 until 1979 the state operated a fire lookout on the mountain.[^2][^1] Brush fires were the concern behind it.[^2] In November 1913 a company said brush fires had troubled it and offered to contribute to the plan.[^2]

In March 1916 an article to appropriate $100 toward a fire lookout on the mountain was adopted.[^2] By June 1916 work on a fire tower there was underway.[^2] The state report for 1917 later described a 40-foot tower with fire-escape stairs and said two miles of telephone line had gone in.[^2] Firetower.org gives a different picture: a 48-foot tower built in 1917, with one mile of phone line.[^1] The sources disagree on height, line length and year, so this story follows the state report.

G.E. Walker ran the lookout in 1918, and by October he had wrapped up his work and gone home for the winter.[^2]

In April 1941 the lookout said the smoke had eased and the fire seemed under control, but Associated Press reports said another 2,000 acres had burned.[^2] That September the Ashburnham forest fire flared up again, according to word from the tower.[^2]

The Great New England Hurricane destroyed the original lookout in September 1938.[^1][^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps built a new steel tower in 1939.[^1] Hurricane winds brought that one down in 1950. A replacement in the Aermotor MC-39 style went up in 1951, and a new cab was built in 1960.[^1]

The station shut down when the 1979 fire season ended.[^1] A Connecticut resident bought the tower when it was taken down in 1996.[^1][^3]

In 2000 the summit was sold to a company that planned a 150-foot cellular tower and a road up the mountain.[^3] In 2002 two towns, two land trusts and two state agencies bought the mountain for $2,500,000.[^3] The purchase protected roughly 281 acres, the summit among them.[^3]

Today the summit is protected land, and two long-distance trails, the Wapack and the Midstate, run across the mountain.[^3] The tower itself has been gone since 1996.[^3] No source we found describes what, if anything, remains of its footings on the summit.
