Hickory Flats Fire Tower stands in Rowan County, Kentucky, and the Route 377 Volunteer Fire Department administers the site.[^1] It stands off Cranston Road (KY 377), along Elk Lick Road.[^2]

Sources disagree on who built it. The register says the U.S. Forest Service built it on the Cumberland National Forest, later renamed Daniel Boone.[^1] The county says the Civilian Conservation Corps built a wooden tower in 1934 and replaced it with steel in 1935.[^2][^3]

The register describes an 80-foot steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7 metal cab.[^1] The county calls the cab wooden and gives the height as 80 feet, but one county news post says 35 feet.[^2][^3] Inside the 200-square-foot cab were a wood stove, two cots and an Osborne fire finder.[^2] Towers used that instrument together: a second tower's bearing was used to fix where a fire was.[^2] The tower sits on about six-tenths of an acre that Jim Gitz of Muncie, Indiana, donated.[^2]

The register's visit reports list the tower as not staffed in 2008 and 2016, and as sometimes staffed in 2022.[^1] A 2016 visit noted that the glass needed replacing, though the overall condition was rated good.[^1]

Sources differ on when fire lookout use ended. The county's tower page says the Forest Service stopped using the tower in the 1960s.[^2] The news post dates the end of lookout work to the mid-1970s, when planes had taken over, and says the tower was then abandoned.[^3] When the tower left service, the National Forest land was exchanged, and the register says the tower was nearly forgotten.[^1] Arson damaged the tower on December 3, 2008.[^3] It was later restored to serve as an overlook.[^3]

The register lists a November 12, 2002 registration as US 447, KY 4, nominated by Danny Blevins.[^1] The register also says the site was being transferred to the Hwy 377 Rural Fire District.[^1] A visit report dated October 18, 2022 says the tower was now available for rental.[^1] The county says overnight stays are booked online and that guests hike in to reach the tower.[^3] The Outbound describes a 1.2-mile hike past a vehicle gate, where foot traffic is welcome on Daniel Boone National Forest land.[^4] It also says visitors are welcome to climb the steps to the top.[^4]
