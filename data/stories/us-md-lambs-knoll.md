Lamb's Knoll is a summit on South Mountain, on the line between Washington and Frederick counties, and the state erected a fire lookout there in 1934.[^1] A Civilian Conservation Corps crew built the tower, and its parts included a 60-foot steel tower moved from Thayersville, Maryland, with 20 feet added.[^3][^2] A 1934 plan also called for an air beacon on top so that night flyers would not strike it.[^2]

Sources disagree about its height. The 1934 plan said 80 feet,[^2] a 1939 Coast and Geodetic Survey description gave 110 feet to the apex,[^3] and the state's 2007 inventory lists the original tower at 91 feet.[^1]

In March 1942 a newspaper reported that the state tower had just opened, with Henry P. Turner named as towerman.[^2][^3] A 1956 article said lookouts were employed at the towers five to six months a year, and a new road was then being built from Reno Monument Road up to the tower.[^2]

The original lattice tower came down in 2006.[^3][^1] The Maryland Historical Trust found it was not historically significant. During review of a proposed new state tower, citizen and agency opposition was raised against the original because of its perceived adverse visual impact on the national battlefields in the valley below.[^1]

Today the summit holds a communications complex, with a modern monopole, equipment buildings, generators and a propane tank inside a chain-link fence.[^1] The fence is painted black to reduce its visibility from the Appalachian Trail, which runs within meters of the site.[^1] Willhite gives the access from the Appalachian Trail as a 0.1 side trail that climbs 150 feet.[^3] The old lookout is gone, and the compound is fenced for security.[^1] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the 1934 tower's base.
