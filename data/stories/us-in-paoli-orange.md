Paoli, sometimes called the Valeene fire tower, was a steel fire tower in Orange County, about five miles south of Paoli.[^1][^2] Sources give it several names. The Indiana Fire Towers site lists Paoli, Valeene and, with a question mark, Orange,[^4] and the Forest Service list has an "Orange Fire Tower" at Paoli and a "Paoli Tower" south of Paoli.[^5]

In August 1932 The Indianapolis News reported plans for a steel tower of 80 to 100 feet at the junction of the Marengo and English highways. A representative of the state forestry department had obtained the site that week. The plan called for a watchman on duty three months in the spring and three in the fall.[^1]

By January 1933, The Forest Worker described a 100-foot tower about five miles south of Paoli, on 55 acres reserved for experimental and demonstration tree planting.[^1]

A Dubois County Herald article, drawn from a local heritage project, says the tower was photographed shortly after it was built in 1934.[^2] The 1933 and 1934 dates do not agree, so the sources disagree on when it went up.[^1][^2]

According to the Herald, the tower stood for more than 40 years, and several local men watched it. One of them, Doyel, may have been the first person hired, paid a quarter an hour.[^2]

A 2025 Forest Fire Lookout Association chapter report says the Paoli Tower is no longer standing and was taken down.[^3] The Indiana Fire Towers site lists it as not standing.[^4] A nearby survey-marker datasheet says the tower still existed in 1965.[^4]

No source describes what remains at the site today, and none says whether the land is public.
