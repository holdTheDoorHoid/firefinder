The Forest Lookouts page dates the lookout site on Bosque Peak, in the Manzano Mountains, to 1911.[^1] An October 1911 newspaper account, quoted on that page, describes the site as a Forest Service station in the high Manzanos, and says the peak was linked to the ranger stations by phone.[^1] A July 1912 newspaper item, also quoted there, says the lookout station on the Bosque was established for the first time the year before, with a telephone line to Tajique ranger station.[^1]

The item calls it the Rea ranch lookout station, on top of the peak.[^1] Supervisor Calkins credited the station with catching fires while they were still small, so they could be put out before much damage was done.[^1]

The July 1912 item places the Manzanos in the Manzano national forest.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page now lists the peak in the Cibola National Forest.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks the lookout as gone.[^2]

Hikers reach the crest on the Bosque Peak Trail (174), which passes a Manzano Mountains Wilderness sign.[^3][^4] Reaching the high point means leaving the trail.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit.[^1][^4] A 2017 blog says the hiking group's report mentions a log cabin ruin, which apparently belonged to the Rea family.[^4] The hiking group's page we read does not mention a cabin.[^3] The July 1912 item links the lookout to the Rea ranch, and the blog's Rea family shares that name, but no source ties the cabin to the lookout.[^1][^3][^4]
