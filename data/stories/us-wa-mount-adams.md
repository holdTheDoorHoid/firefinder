The Forest Service set out in 1917 to put a fire lookout on the summit of Mount Adams, in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^1][^3] Supplies went up by mule as far as the glacier, then crews hauled them the rest of the way with ropes and sleds.[^3] The D-6 cupola cabin was built in 1918, but accounts differ on when it was finished, with 1921 and 1922 both given.[^1][^3][^4]

The cabin was in service as a lookout from about 1921 to 1924.[^1] Crews hauled their own supplies, cleared snow from the windows and doors, and kept the single phone line intact.[^4] In 1923 lightning blew the door off.[^4] The Forest Service gave up the lookout in 1924, though one account puts the retirement in 1925.[^2][^3][^4]

Sulfur miners moved into the cabin in the early 1930s and added two lean-to rooms.[^1][^3] Since the mid-20th century the building has mostly been buried in snow and ice, and it is exposed for only a few weeks in the driest summers.[^1][^3] A 2019 visit report said the glass was no longer intact and the site was not staffed.[^1]

Reaching the cabin means climbing the mountain.[^3] TrailChick says even the least technical route may call for an ice axe and crampons, and that conditions on the mountain can turn fast.[^3] The cabin itself is often hidden under snow and ice, so anyone who goes up should expect to see little of it.[^3]
