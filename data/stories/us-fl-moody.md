The Moody fire tower, just north of Ebro on Highway 79 in Washington County, Florida, had stood there since the 1960s.[^1][^2]

By the 1990s, use of fire-watch towers had sharply declined, and towers across the state were slowly dismantled over the following years.[^1] In 2005, then-Governor Jeb Bush decided to decommission the 35 towers still in service across the state.[^1]

The Moody tower and its caretaker's cottage later fell into disrepair.[^1] The tower itself has weathered numerous tropical storms and hurricanes, Hurricane Michael among them.[^1]

Excelsior Fire Towers owner Mike Vilegi bought the tower to reuse it elsewhere.[^1] The top 60 feet (18 m), including the cab, was bound for Dade City, Florida, to serve as a stargazing tower.[^1] The lower 40 feet (12 m) was slated for Tennessee or New Hampshire, to become a wraparound deck for a house being built into a mountain.[^1] Vilegi and two helpers needed about six hours and a crane to take it down.[^1]

A report posted on 4 November 2021 says the tower was dismantled that Thursday, so no standing tower is left to climb.[^1] The report does not say whether the cottage or any footings remain, so no source describes what, if anything, is on the site today.
