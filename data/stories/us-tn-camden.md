The Camden Fire Tower stands at an active Tennessee Division of Forestry site in Benton County, Tennessee.[^1] It is an 80-foot Aermotor LS-40.[^1] The LS-40 ranked among Aermotor's most popular steel designs, and the state forestry division used it heavily until the mid-1970s.[^3]

The register lists the build date as unknown, and we found no other source that gives one.[^1][^2] We also found no record of who staffed the tower or of any fire it watched.

The register added the tower on February 1, 2023, on the nomination of Jason Johns.[^1] The register lists a site visit dated January 28, 2023.[^1] Its description says the original Aermotor badge is gone, but its outline is still visible.[^1] A 2014 list of surviving state towers, published in a National Register document, includes it.[^3]

The register gives the elevation as 579 feet (176 meters), while the Tennessee Landforms list gives 588 feet. The two sources differ by nine feet.[^1][^2]

Neither source describes public access or climbing rules for Camden. Because the tower sits at an active state forestry site, check with the Tennessee Division of Forestry before going near it.[^1][^2]
