The earliest source found for Trout Mountain describes a plan.[^1] A December 1959 item from the Peace River Record Gazette, as quoted by the Canada Lookouts website, reported that lumber, steel and building supplies were being hauled through the district toward Trout Mountain, where a fire lookout tower was to go up without delay.[^1]

The provincial wildfire data gives 2003 as the construction year of the steel tower standing there now. The tower is 30 m (98 ft) high, at an elevation of about 796 m (2,611 ft).[^2]

According to the province, each wildfire lookout site has an observer on duty, mainly between April and October.[^3] The province also says the sites are remote and that staff have to plan for many possibilities, including an inquisitive bear.[^3]

The data lists Trout Mountain as active and gives its access mode as "drive".[^2] It does not say whether the public may visit, so treat the tower as a working fire-watch structure and do not climb it.
