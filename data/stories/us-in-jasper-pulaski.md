The Jasper-Pulaski lookout was a steel fire tower on the Jasper-Pulaski Game Preserve in Pulaski County, Indiana.[^1][^2] Land acquisition for the preserve began in 1929.[^4]

In October 1933 the state announced that a steel fire tower, 80 feet tall, was to go up on the preserve.[^2] The Culver Citizen reported that it was one of six towers the federal forestry division assigned to Indiana.[^2] Civilian Conservation Corps workers were to erect the towers and string the telephone lines. Each tower was to be connected to the nearest exchange, so that fire warnings could reach the proper authorities quickly.[^2] By May 1934, Outdoor Indiana magazine ran an article on the preserve with a photo of a fire tower.[^1]

The tower is gone. The Indiana Fire Towers site says it is not standing and that no evidence remains.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list marks it gone.[^3] A Forest Service list of the state's fire towers places it at the Jasper-Pulaski Game Reserve, without the asterisk it uses for towers still standing in 2003.[^5]

The author of the Indiana Fire Towers site visited in December 2023 and found that the spot is now a parking lot.[^1] In 2022, a state conservation employee at Tippecanoe River State Park confirmed the location and said no remnant of the tower is left.[^1]

The preserve is now the Jasper-Pulaski Fish & Wildlife Area, which the Indiana Department of Natural Resources describes as offering outdoor recreation.[^4] Visitors other than hunters, range users and dog runners are encouraged to get a one-day access permit before entering the field.[^4] On the author's account, a visit to that spot would show no remnant of the tower.[^1]
