The Cassatt Tower went up in 1936 as a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40 steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, erected by Civilian Conservation Corps enlistees stationed at the Cassatt State Forest in Kershaw County.[^2] The Register gives the same 1936 date and the same Aermotor model.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts site quotes a 1947 study of South Carolina's natural resources, which says the state forest was deeded to the Commission in 1935 and that a fire lookout tower was located there.[^2] We found no names of anyone who staffed this tower, and no fires it is recorded to have watched.

The Commission ended its fire tower network on October 1, 1993.[^4] The Register says Billy McIntosh bought the tower from the Commission in the late 1990s, moved it to Sumter County, and re-erected it on his property.[^1] It also says the tower was formerly 100 feet tall and was reconstructed at 40 feet on its original concrete footings.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site gives the sale only as a year in the 1990s, marked with question marks, and says it was moved to a private location.[^2] Columbia Metropolitan Magazine dates McIntosh's purchase to the early 2000s, so the sources disagree on the year.[^3]

Today the Register describes the tower as a hunting and wildlife observation spot.[^1] The magazine says McIntosh uses his towers to manage and hunt wild game, and that two of his four towers stand at 40 feet.[^3] It does not say which two, but the Register's 40-foot figure matches this tower. No public access is described, so please do not climb it without the owner's permission.
