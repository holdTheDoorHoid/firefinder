In September 1940 the Portsmouth Times reported that a secondary fire tower was to be set up on Telegraph Hill, four miles south of Blackfork, with Homer Lloyd as its local watchman.[^1]

The two secondary towers covered in a 1942 item, this one among them, were open platforms of the "Crow's Nest" type, giving the towermen no shelter from the weather.[^1] That April the Forest Service had received two cab enclosures, one for the Telegraph Hill tower a mile east of State Route 75, and Carl Malone was then manning the tower.[^1] The cabs were meant to shelter radio gear and keep people from climbing, and a Civilian Conservation Corps crew from Camp Vesuvius was to build them.[^1]

The same item said a recent government ruling barred the general public from climbing the towers, since many were in use as airplane spotter stations and for other war work.[^1] It also said these secondary towers were manned only when the fire hazard was high.[^1]

The Ohio fire tower list marks the Telegraph Hill tower as gone, and the Forest Lookouts page ends its entry with the single word "Removed," with no date or method.[^3][^1] The register places the site in the Wayne National Forest, lists the U.S. Forest Service as the agency, and records the site as registered on September 3, 2014.[^2]

No source I read describes what, if anything, is left at the site today or whether visitors can reach it.
