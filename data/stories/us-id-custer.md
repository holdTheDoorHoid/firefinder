The Custer Lookout is a 14-by-14-foot groundhouse with a catwalk, built in 1933.[^1] Its cab is a Region 5 design that dates to 1917 and came before the L-4 cab, which was developed in the early 1930s.[^1][^2] The register says it is one of very few of its kind in Idaho.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts adds that the design was unpopular in the Northwest.[^3]

Rex's Fire Lookout page says the lookout was last used for fire detection in 1972, though that line is garbled.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts reports that Teresa Fleck staffed it in 1981 and had to leave when a forest fire came within 100 yards, citing the Kellogg Evening News.[^3] The two accounts do not agree, and I could not settle which is right.

The National Historic Lookout Register added the lookout on November 20, 2008.[^1] A Forest Fire Lookout Association list marks it as standing.[^5]

Today the lookout is reached on a 0.7-mile Forest Service trail, number 4164, which starts at a junction with Ramey Cr #4163 and ends at the lookout.[^6] The trail page lists horseback riding and motorcycle trail riding as its uses.[^6] Idaho Fire Lookouts labels the site "Hike-In Emergency Use."[^3] I found no recent condition report, so check with the Salmon-Challis National Forest before you visit.
