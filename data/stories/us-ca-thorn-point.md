Thorn Point Lookout stands on Thorn Point in the Sespe Wilderness of the Los Padres National Forest, in Ventura County, California.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register dates it to 1933.[^1] In August 1934 a newspaper reported that a trail was being built up to the new lookout.[^2]

The register describes a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab with a catwalk, set on a 20-foot H-braced tower. It remains close to its original state and still has its early telephone.[^1] During World War II the lookout was staffed around the clock, and a ground cabin was added for the off-duty observer, who worked 12-hour shifts.[^1]

The lookout stayed in use after the war. A 1956 item says a Ground Observer Corps post was run from the lookout by the Forest Service.[^2] Austin Mack staffed the post from May to November; a July 1958 item calls it his fourth year there.[^2] That item also says he had seen condors on 12 different days by that point.[^2]

The register notes that the lookout is near the Condor Sanctuary and has been used to monitor condors.[^1] Some sources spell the name Thorne.[^2]

Today the trail to the lookout starts at Thorn Meadows Campground and runs about 3.5 miles into the wilderness, where motorized use is not allowed.[^3] The Forest Service says the tower is one of a small number still standing in the Los Padres.[^3] The sources read do not say whether the cab is open to visitors or whether it may be climbed, so check with the Los Padres National Forest before going up.
