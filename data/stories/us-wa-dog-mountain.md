Dog Mountain rises from the north bank of the Columbia River Gorge, and a fire lookout went up on its south shoulder in 1931.[^5][^6] It was put up to watch for blazes on the Oregon shore.[^5][^6] The first cab was an L-4 with a gable roof and windows on three walls, sited a quarter mile from the top, on the south side.[^1][^4]

Mount Hood National Forest personnel ran the station, although the peak lies within Gifford Pinchot National Forest.[^6] The FFLOS register says that in later years the lookout was staffed for its view across the Columbia.[^1]

In 1938 a 21-year-old Columbia National Forest employee was stationed alone at the lookout when a rattlesnake bit him.[^3][^4] He gave himself first aid while alone, waiting for a pack horse, and was taken down by horse and car.[^3] WillhiteWeb says he recovered.[^4]

A second L-4 cab replaced the first, and sources disagree on the year.[^1][^4] The FFLOS register, WillhiteWeb and the hiking blog give 1953.[^1][^4][^6] Wikipedia says the lookout was reconstructed in 1952.[^5] A historical report quoted on the Washington Lookouts page has an entry dated February 8, 1954, that reads "Dog Mtn L.O. Erected."[^3]

The lookout came down in 1967.[^5] Wikipedia says it was taken apart after roads and planes made it obsolete.[^5] The FFLOS register calls it removed,[^1] while firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say it was destroyed.[^2][^4]

Today the site lies beside the Dog Mountain Trail, a popular six-mile hike that starts from Route 14 at milepost 53.[^5] Hikers can walk to the old lookout spot on the summit's south shoulder.[^6] The blog's author describes one remnant, a concrete block that once held the lookout in place.[^6]
