Bosley Butte, in Curry County, had a camp on the site by 1935.[^1][^3] The register gives 1940 as the year the first ground cabin went up, but a June 1941 newspaper item describes a new lookout station being built on the butte that year, so the build year is uncertain.[^1][^2]

The register says the L-4 cab was replaced in 1954 with another L-4.[^1] A 1954 district report lists a 14 x 14 ground house at the site, with no unit given.[^2] The register dates its Amort cab to 1984, while the other two sources date a new cab to 1987.[^1][^2][^3] That 1987 cab was staffed every summer and was still standing in 2010.[^3] In 1958 the station reported to Grizzly Mountain by radio, and no telephone line reached it.[^2]

A lightning strike in February 2005 destroyed the lookout's antenna, but its fuses held and the damage stayed with the antenna.[^2] The Coos Forest Protective Association installed a smoke-detecting camera system on the lookout in 2009.[^2]

The lookout burned down in 2012.[^1][^3] The cause was still under investigation in mid-May, and vandalism was suspected.[^2] A 2012 blog post reported that the association did not plan to replace the lookout, and firelookout.com says it had already put a detection camera in its place.[^4][^3]

I could not find a source that describes the site today or says whether the public can visit the summit. The most recent dated source read is from September 2012.[^4] The register lists the site at 3,432 feet.[^1]
