Naneum Ridge lookout, also called Naneum Point, stood on a summit in the Wenatchee National Forest, in Chelan/Kittitas County, Washington.[^1][^2][^3] Willhite notes that the spot is not the ridge's highest point, though it is where the former lookout stood.[^3]

A lookout went up in 1945 as an 8-foot log crib with a 12-by-12-foot frame live-in cab.[^1][^2][^3] A 1945 forestry report, as the Forest Lookouts site quotes it, describes a new lookout at the head of Naneum Creek.[^4]

In 1958 a 40-foot wooden tower with a DNR live-in cab replaced it.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts site gives a 1956-58 entry saying a lookout was completed on Naneum during that biennium.[^4]

Sources disagree on the agency. The register names the U.S. Forest Service, while the Forest Lookouts page lists the State Forestry Division.[^1][^4]

The lookout was abandoned in the late 1960s.[^1][^3] The register says it is unclear when it was removed, and Willhite says the removal date is unknown.[^1][^3]

According to Willhite, the old footings now sit in a pile south of the main communication tower, and his photo captions show communication towers on the site today.[^3] The Naneum Ridge Road leads visitors to the site, and Willhite warns drivers to expect slow going, rocks and possible ruts.[^3] Most visitors he saw were on ATV-type vehicles.[^3] The sources read do not mention a closure or permit for the road.
