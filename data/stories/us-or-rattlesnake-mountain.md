Rattlesnake Mountain lies in the Umpqua National Forest in Douglas County, Oregon. A fire lookout stood on its summit from 1933 until 1962.[^1][^2]

A Roseburg newspaper item from July 1932 announced nine new lookouts, one of them to be built on Rattlesnake Mountain in the Diamond Lake region.[^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower in 1933. It was 30 feet tall, round and made of hemlock timber, with a 14-by-14 L-4 cab under a hip roof.[^1][^2]

In September 1937, after a lightning storm, a ranger and a writer on a trail below the summit noticed a smell that might have been smoke. The lookout on duty had seen no smoke, but later that day he reported a fire in an old stump in dense timber below the lookout.[^2]

On October 12, 1962, the Columbus Day storm destroyed the lookout.[^2][^4] The register and firelookout.com call the storm a hurricane.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts history page marks the lookout as removed but gives no date for that.[^2]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today. A 2015 hike report started at Happy Camp, near the end of Forest Road 870, and reached the summit by a set of trails that included the #1497. The road runs into the Rogue-Umpqua Divide Wilderness. The report does not mention any remains of the lookout.[^4] Conditions may have changed since then, so check before you go.
