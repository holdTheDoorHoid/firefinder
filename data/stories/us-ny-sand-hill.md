Sand Hill was a state fire tower near Massena, in St. Lawrence County.[^1][^4] An April 1950 newspaper said a station started the year before by the Conservation Department would be operated by a man from Brasher Iron Works.[^1] Starr's list dates the tower to 1950 and says it was one of ten towers bought that year with a state fund.[^2] Read literally, the newspaper puts the start in 1949, so the two sources differ by a year.[^1][^2]

The tower was never run full time.[^2] Fire wardens ran it as a secondary facility on a need-by-need basis.[^2] Its last entry in the state's annual report to the Legislature was in 1957.[^2] It was officially closed in 1959, and Forest Rangers removed it around 1961.[^2]

Starr's list gives the tower a height of 80 feet, as an Aermotor LS-40.[^2] A state unit plan for the St. Lawrence Flatlands also gives 80 feet and dates the tower from about 1950 to 1961.[^3]

The plan says that when the site was seen in 2012, only the footings of the tower and an observer's cabin remained.[^3] The site is on State Highway 420, across from the Norfolk fire station.[^3] No source I found describes the site since 2012.
