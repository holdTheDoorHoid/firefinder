Grizzly Tower is a wooden fire lookout in Alberta.[^1] The federal Canadian Forest Service built it in 1922.[^1] It is a guyed tamarack pole tower with a 2.1 by 2.1 m (7 by 7 ft) wooden cab and a log ground house.[^1] Provincial Forest Rangers staffed the tower.[^1]

The tower was abandoned in 1929, when a replacement lookout was set up at a better location.[^1]

The site was inspected in May 1996 and judged suitable for restoration.[^1] The register said the work would be carried out with the Alberta Chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on June 5, 1996, as CAN 1, AB 1.[^1] It gives the elevation as 1,073 m (3,520 ft).[^1]

The 1996 entry says it is probably the final surviving wooden lookout of its period, both in Alberta and across the country.[^1] That 1996 description is the latest word we found.[^1] We did not find a later source on whether the restoration went ahead, what condition the tower is in now, or whether the site is open to visitors. No source we read describes the site today, so ask the land manager before you go.

[^1]: Grizzly Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/can/ab/grizzly-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-20).
