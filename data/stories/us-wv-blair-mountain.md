The Blair Mountain tower stood in Logan County, West Virginia.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association registered the former site in 2008.[^1] The register names the State of West Virginia as administrator, while the Forest Lookouts page names the West Virginia Division of Forestry. The sources do not say whether those are the same body.[^1][^2]

The earliest record we found is from October 1926.[^2] A Charleston Gazette item, reprinted by the Forest Lookouts site, said the tower was linked to the Madison central office through the Boone County Coal Corporation's private telephone exchange at Sharplea.[^2] In 1927 a Raleigh Register item reported that a district ranger was completing the phone link between the tower and Sharples, a job of 73 poles and five miles of wire.[^2]

A photograph in the e-WV collection is labeled as showing the Blair Mountain fire tower in January 1987.[^3]

The register says the tower has been removed, and the association's West Virginia list marks it as gone.[^1][^5]

The mountain is better known for the 1921 Battle of Blair Mountain, which Wikipedia calls the biggest labor uprising in US history.[^4] The battlefield was first listed on the National Register in March 2009.[^4] The listing was removed because of clerical errors in how the state preservation office handled objections, and a June 2018 decision made it a register site again.[^4]

No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the tower, or whether the public can reach the spot.[^1][^2][^3][^5]
