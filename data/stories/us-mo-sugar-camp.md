In December 1935 a newspaper said forestry service workers planned a 100-foot tower a short way east of Seligman.[^1] That notice does not name the site, so linking it to the steel tower that follows is an inference. The Cassville Republican called the finished steel tower the Sugar Camp Tower, and reported that a crew of 15 under Foreman Hopkins completed it on May 27, 1936, eight days after work began.[^1] The paper gave its height as 99 feet 9 inches to the steel cabin.[^1]

A Forest Service page says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower in the 1930s.[^2] The 1935 and 1936 newspaper items do not mention the CCC, so this story reports both accounts without choosing between them.[^1][^2]

In June 1936 the Cassville Republican said Varner Ball and Orville Hodges were in charge of "the look-out tower between Eagle Rock and Seligman." The item does not name the tower, so it may or may not refer to Sugar Camp.[^1]

A Forest Service page lists the tower as a picnic stop on the Sugar Camp National Forest Scenic Byway.[^2] The 28-mile byway includes about 8 miles of gravel road, which turns off Highway 112 shortly after Roaring River State Park.[^2] Forest Road 1004 leads to the tower from Forest Road 197.[^2] The page says the site has a picnic table and that climbing the tower is not allowed.[^2]
