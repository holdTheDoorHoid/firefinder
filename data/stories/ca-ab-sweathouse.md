A tower at Sweathouse Creek was going up in January 1952, according to a Peace River Record Gazette item that Canada Lookouts has transcribed.[^1] By 1955 the lookout was an 18 m (60 ft) steel tower with a three-room cabin nearby, and its view reached as far as High Prairie.[^1]

Alberta's wildfire register gives 2003 as the construction year for the steel tower it lists today. That tower is 30 m (98 ft) tall and stands about 892 m (2,927 ft) above sea level.[^2] The heights do not match, so the 2003 tower is probably a replacement for the 1955 one rather than the same structure.[^1][^2]

Lookouts were at the tower in the mid to late 1950s.[^1] A 1955 report described a towerman in his cabin at the site, and noted that the season had brought just a handful of small fires, all put out quickly.[^1] In 1958 a farming family returned home from the Sweathouse fire tower.[^1] In 1959 a newspaper reported that a man had left for a tower staff course in Grande Prairie and planned to return to the tower as its lookout for the season.[^1]

The register lists the tower as active and reached by road.[^2] Alberta reports that each of its 127 wildfire lookout sites has a lookout observer on watch, usually from April to October.[^3] No source describes the site itself today or any public visits, so treat it as a working fire tower, not a place to visit.
