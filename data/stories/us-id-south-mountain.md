South Mountain sits in Owyhee County, Idaho, about 70 miles southwest of Boise, at 7,801 feet.[^1][^2] The first lookout here was a cabin, dated to 1933 by most sources.[^1][^2][^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 1932 instead.[^3]

The current structure replaced that cabin in 1964.[^2] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts describe a two-story building with a concrete lower level and a frame cabin on top.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts Idaho page describes a concrete building with an observation room on top, so the sources do not agree on the design.[^4] The Bureau of Land Management administers the site.[^2]

Burt and Maxine Search are listed as known lookout staff for 1969.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the site is usually staffed in the fire season, and a climbing guide says the BLM mans the lookout in summer.[^2][^5]

In August 1971, a weekend cloudburst damaged a six-mile stretch of the road used to reach the BLM lookout.[^4] The Idaho Free Press reported an estimate of a week to reopen it.[^4] The lookout joined the National Historic Lookout Register on September 27, 2020, as US 1421, ID 113.[^2][^3]

The road is the main question for visitors today. The climbing guide says almost all of it crosses private land, and the BLM has no recorded right-of-way through that land, so owners could close it at any time.[^5] As of July 2017, the road was open to the public and signed by the largest landowner, and that is the most recent access note I found.[^5] The final stretch needs a four-wheel-drive vehicle.[^5] The guide suggests parking about three-quarters of a mile short of the summit and walking the rest, and it asks visitors to stay on the road and obey posted signs.[^5]
