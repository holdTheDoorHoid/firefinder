Two steel observation towers went up in Maryland in 1928, and one of them, 93 feet high, stood at Brandywine in Prince George's County.[^1] In February 1938 the State Forestry Department announced plans to rebuild that tower to 120 feet, so that a new two-way radio set would reach farther.[^1]

We found no source that names who staffed the Brandywine tower or any fire it watched. A draft state fire tower report lists it among towers moved from their original location, with the property passing to the University of Maryland.[^2] The tower now stands at the Fairview Outdoor Education Center in Clear Spring, Washington County.[^2] The report gives no date for the move.[^2]

The Fairview site is the Claud E. Kitchens Outdoor School, a Washington County Public Schools facility renamed in 2008 for a former superintendent who oversaw its construction.[^3] The school's address is 12808 Draper Road.[^4]

In 2015 the county school system sought bids for restoration work on the tower, including replacing part of the steel structure and the wooden treads and landings. The notice does not say whether that work was done.[^4]

An outdoor-school teacher at the Fairview site wrote in December 2022 that for some 40 years thousands of local fifth graders have made the climb, and that the view from the top takes in four states.[^5]

That same article says the tower once stood on Maryland's Eastern Shore, and it gives the height as 104 feet.[^5] The Eastern Shore claim conflicts with the state's Brandywine record, and the 104-foot height differs from the 120 feet announced in 1938.[^2][^1] The origin and present height remain unsettled.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, with its cab no longer in place.[^6] We found no account of public visits. Because the tower sits on a school site, check with the school before going, and do not climb it without permission.[^3]
