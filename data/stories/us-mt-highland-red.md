Red Mountain, also called Devil's Peak, rises south of Butte inside the Beaverhead-Deerlodge National Forest.[^3] The lookout there was first developed around 1933, and an L-4 cab stood on the summit.[^2] The cab standing today went up in 1954 on a concrete base, about a quarter mile north of the peak, according to firelookout.com.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree about what came before and where the newer cab sits. The National Historic Lookout Register says the 1954 cab succeeded an L-4 groundhouse put up in 1933.[^1] A travel guide places the 1954 tower at the summit and says it replaced that same groundhouse.[^3] The firelookout.com listing puts the newer cab a quarter mile north and says the earlier cab stood on the summit.[^2]

According to the register, the present building is an L-4 cab with a catwalk, sitting on a single-story concrete base.[^1]

One event stands out in the sources. On August 22, 1922, a lightning bolt struck a group of Boy Scouts from Butte during a hike. One scout died and others were hurt.[^3] A monument beside the tower, installed in 2022, marks that day.[^3]

The firelookout.com listing says the cab is still there and available for emergencies.[^2] Getting there means driving Fish Creek Road, which is totally unpaved and needs a 4x4 with high clearance.[^3] The road is not plowed, and vehicles usually cannot get through from October to June.[^3] When there is no snow and the car has enough clearance, a four-wheel-drive trail runs up to a gate just over 9,000 feet.[^4] Snow can close two gates on the road to the campground in winter.[^4] The tower stands below the peak, and a road leads to it.[^4]

Because the listing says it is available for emergencies, and the Forest Service administers it, treat it as a Forest Service structure rather than a place to climb.[^1][^2]
