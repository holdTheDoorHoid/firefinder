Alberta's wildfire register lists the present Whitecourt lookout as a steel tower, 30 m (98 ft) tall and about 1,172 m (3,845 ft) above sea level. It gives 2017 as the year that structure was built.[^1]

The Whitecourt lookout had an older tower long before that. A 1946 entry in the Department of Lands and Mines annual reports, as quoted by a lookout-history site, says material was taken to the Whitecourt Lookout Tower site to replace an old tower that was becoming unsafe.[^2] The same entry describes a road cleared and graded, nine culverts built along it, and a well dug and cribbed at the tower site.[^2]

The annual report for the year ended 31 March 1948 records a new tower taking the place of the old one at the same location.[^2] The next report, for the year ended 31 March 1949, says the road to the tower was upgraded and kept in repair.[^2]

The register lists the tower as active and reached by road, and it shows no weather station.[^1] Alberta calls its lookouts integral to wildfire detection and sites them where visibility is good.[^3]

No source I found describes the site on the ground today, or says whether the public may visit. Check with the managing agency before planning a trip.
