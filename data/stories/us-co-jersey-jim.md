Jersey Jim Lookout takes its name from an early cattleman who grazed Jersey cattle in this corner of the San Juan National Forest, in the La Plata Mountains of southwest Colorado — land still used for grazing today, under permit.[^2] A lookout has stood here since 1942, originally built of local spruce.[^1] That first tower was replaced in 1964 with the structure that stands now: a 55-foot treated-timber tower topped by a 15-by-15-foot live-in cab, at an elevation near 10,000 feet.[^1] From the deck, visitors can see Hesperus Peak, sacred to the Navajo Nation, and Sleeping Ute Mountain to the southwest.[^2]

The tower served as a U.S. Forest Service fire lookout from the 1940s through the 1970s, a working life that ended as aerial detection and other methods made many ground lookouts redundant across the West.[^2] By 1991 the tower was slated for demolition, but the Jersey Jim Foundation, a local volunteer nonprofit, stepped in to renovate and save it, and has operated and maintained it ever since under a permit with the San Juan National Forest.[^2] A 2019 condition report found the tower structurally good and the cab's exterior, roof, and interior all in excellent shape.[^1]

Rental income goes toward keeping the tower maintained, and it's rented today through the Forest Service and the Jersey Jim Foundation.[^1][^2] The cab still holds its original furniture, along with propane heat, lighting, a propane refrigerator, and a stove — there's no electricity or running water. Windows wrap the cab on all sides for 360-degree views, and the historic fire finder still sits in the middle of the room, available for guests to use to identify the surrounding peaks.[^2] Overnight stays are capped at four people, and renters pick up their key — and sign a waiver — at the P&D Grocery nearby.[^2] Because the deck sits 55 feet up with a trap door and steep stairs, and lightning is a real hazard in these mountains, the rental rules are strict about staying inside and off the metal furnishings during storms.[^2]
