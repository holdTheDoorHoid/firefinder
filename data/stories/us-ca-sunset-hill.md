Sunset Hill is a lookout site in Butte County, near Challenge, in the Plumas National Forest area.[^1] A Forest Service lookout man was posted there by November 1927.[^1] In May 1934 the Feather River Bulletin reported on a new lookout station on the hill, and gave its tower as 50 feet high.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register describes a 30-foot battered steel tower under a 196-square-foot wood-frame cab, and says the cab was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s to a Forest Service plan.[^2] The heights do not agree, and the sources here do not settle it, so this story follows the Register on height.[^1][^2]

The papers name a few of the people who worked it.[^1] In 1927 Charles Leyval held the Forest Service lookout post on the hill.[^1] Robert Owens took over in September 1935, after the lookout's wife, Mrs. Halloran, was hurt.[^1] Alice M. Marvin began at Lexington Hill in 1942 and was moved to Sunset Hill in spring 1943.[^1]

In April 1948 roughly 250 people turned out for a dawn Easter service close to the lookout.[^1]

The Register says the 1930s cab was replaced with a CDF design in 1972.[^2] The site was registered on June 1, 2004, as US 579, CA 59.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] CAL FIRE's June 2008 list named Sunset Hill among lookouts that might be staffed that season.[^4]

The sources I read say nothing about public access. CAL FIRE's list says the agency does not rent its lookouts.[^4] Check with CAL FIRE before planning any visit, and do not climb the tower without permission.
