The Cuyama Peak lookout was built in 1934 on a peak in the Los Padres National Forest.[^1][^3] The Register describes a modified L-4 cab on a 20-foot steel tower with an H-braced frame.[^1]

A telephone line to the peak was finished in January 1935, linking the Cuyama ranger station to the summit.[^2] In May 1939 lightning storms led the forest supervisor to order the lookout opened, the first lookout of that season to open.[^2]

During World War II the site was also used by the Aircraft Warning Service, which kept a cabin there.[^1] The Register says that cabin is one of just four left in California, and it has since collapsed.[^1][^4]

The sources disagree on when the lookout stopped being used. A blog post from 2011 says the lookout went out of service in the 1960s.[^3] A reader's comment on the same page says the reader was hired and lived there for part of the summer of 1970, when the lookout was manned.[^3] A California Lookouts history page dates the cab's structural failure and collapse to December 2012.[^2] Updated photographs in the Register show the cab down on the tower platform.[^1] ForestWatch says the lookout cabin and the Aircraft Warning Service residence below it have fallen into ruin in recent years.[^4]

The Register records the lookout's registration on October 10, 1998.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks the tower as standing, with an asterisk.[^5] A maintained dirt road reaches the top, though a blog warns against taking ordinary passenger cars up it.[^4][^3] With the cab down, the site is best seen from the road rather than climbed.
