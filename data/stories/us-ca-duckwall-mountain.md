The Civilian Conservation Corps built the Duckwall Mountain lookout in 1935, according to the National Historic Lookout Register, which describes a 100-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab.[^1] Peakbagging.com gives the same 1935 date and calls the tower an Aeromotor design.[^2] The site sits in the Stanislaus National Forest in Tuolumne County, at 5,835 feet.[^1]

Records describe a residence beside the tower.[^1][^2] Peakbagging.com dates the cinder block building there to 1951, when it replaced a wooden residence lost in a wildland fire, and says the interior is damaged and needs major repair.[^2] The Register's description calls a BC-2 residence "still in use," but its January 2019 visit report says the residence "has been severely vandalized and burned."[^1]

The sources disagree about staffing. The 2019 visit report says the lookout "has not been staffed for over a decade," and that its historic status rules out a rebuild.[^1] Peakbagging.com, which gives no date, calls it "in good condition" and says it "may be staffed during periods of fire hazard."[^2]

The 2019 report calls the lookout "abandoned and unsafe" and says it is no longer used for fire detection.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3]

Today the Register says access is gated, and only Forest Service staff and repeater-site representatives may enter.[^1] The lookout was registered on April 24, 2017 as US 1190, CA 121.[^1] The newest dated record is the 2019 visit, so the tower's condition now is unknown. Visitors should stay away from it, since the 2019 report calls it unsafe.[^1]
