Allen Field Tower stood on Brushy Mountain in Montgomery County, Virginia, inside the Jefferson National Forest.[^1] The site sits at about 2,848 feet, or 868 meters.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administered the tower, and the register gives 1957 as the year it was built.[^1] It was roughly 50 feet tall, built on four wooden legs, with a cabin at the top.[^1]

A Forest Lookouts page for Virginia marks the Brushy Mountain (Allen Field) entry as removed.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list also shows the tower as gone, under the same register number, US 1728, VA 30.[^3] That list spells the mountain "Brush Mountain," but it is the same entry.[^3]

The register added the site on July 30, 2018, after a nomination by Kristin Reynolds.[^1]

Do not mix this one up with a different Brushy Mountain tower in Alleghany County.[^3] That tower has its own number, US 923, VA 9.[^3]

No source read describes what, if anything, remains on the mountain today, or whether the site is open to visitors. Anyone planning to look for it should first ask the Jefferson National Forest about access.
