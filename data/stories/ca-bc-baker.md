Mount Baker, also called Baker Mountain, stands near Cranbrook in the East Kootenay region of British Columbia.[^1][^2]

The provincial Forest Branch built a lookout station and cabin on the summit in 1913. The work came with about 10 km (6 miles) of trail and a telephone line of the same length, which linked the station to the fire-patrol service.[^1] The station was meant to aid fire protection in the Cranbrook Forest District.[^1]

The 1913 annual report expected a man to spend each dry-season day on top of the mountain, equipped with field glasses and a map.[^1] By 1921 Tom Dalton was in charge of the station.[^1] In 1932 the City of Cranbrook kept a lookout man on the summit to report fires near its watershed.[^1]

In August 1964 the B.C. Forest Service announced plans for a new fire tower at 2,133 m (7,000 ft) above sea level, to serve as a primary lookout.[^1] The plan called for a prefabricated frame building, and the site was also to become the key radio repeater for East Kootenay ranger stations.[^1]

A 2026 guide to BC's historic fire lookouts places the Baker lookout in a list of lookouts that are confirmed or likely still standing.[^3] No source I found describes the site today, confirms the 1964 tower was finished, or says whether the public can visit it.
