The Bald Eagle lookout stood in the Plumas National Forest, where it gave a view of Bucks Lake.[^1] The Forest Lookouts site calls the same place Bald Eagle Butte.[^1]

A May 1974 Feather River Bulletin notice told readers they could drive or hike a primitive dirt road to the lookout, seven miles north of a dam.[^1] It presented the trip as a chance to learn about the work of a fire lookout.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists Bald Eagle Mountain, a platform, as gone.[^2] The Peakbagging table of California lookouts lists a Bald Eagle Mtn as an LO site.[^3] That table defines an LO site as one whose building has been removed, possibly leaving no evidence behind, or whose status is unknown.[^3] Both lists place this entry in Plumas County, and the Peakbagging row sits on the Bucks Lake quadrangle.[^2][^3] Neither names the Forest Lookouts page, so we have not confirmed that all three describe the same site.

We found no source describing what remains at the site now, or whether it is open to visitors.
