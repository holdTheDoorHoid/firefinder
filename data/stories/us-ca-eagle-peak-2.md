Records place a lookout on Eagle Peak in Tehama County as early as 1933, when the first structure was an open platform.[^1] A BC-301 cab later sat atop an enclosed timber tower.[^1] The current tower has "1963" on its northwest pier, and its steel cab, made by California Blow Pipe of Escalon, California, follows a Forest Service design known as CL-30.[^1]

The California Department of Forestry took the site over in 1972, and a lower room was added about a decade later.[^1] The Tehama-Glenn Unit of Cal Fire administers it today.[^1]

A July 1971 item from the Red Bluff Daily News, reprinted on a Forest Lookouts page, describes a grass fire that burned 100 acres west of Henleyville on Paskenta Road.[^2] It notes that "Smoke was spotted from the Eagle Peak Lookout, located northwest of Paskenta."[^2] The same page recounts an October 1938 item saying the station was known locally as Pigeon Roost, because wandering homing pigeons favored it.[^2]

A CAL FIRE list from June 2008 names Eagle Peak among the lookouts that may be staffed during that fire season.[^4] The same list says most lookouts are "staffed only during peak fire season, or during high fire danger times."[^4] The sources read give no later staffing record.

The Register rates access as restricted, since the nearest paved road is 2.7 miles away.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^3] The Register listed the lookout on October 28, 2019 as US 1360, CA 183.[^1] The 2008 CAL FIRE document says the agency does not rent lookouts.[^4] The sources read do not say whether the public can visit, so check with CAL FIRE before any trip.
