The Ross Mountain Fire Tower in Somerset County, Maine, was first erected in 1914 at another location, not on the mountain where it stands today.[^1] The Register says it has been moved twice, and that it came to its present site in the 1940s. It gives only the decade for that move.[^1]

The Register describes a guyed tower about 60 feet (18 meters) tall with an 8-by-8-foot wooden cab. It calls the metal towers with wooden cabs unique to Maine.[^1]

Maine's network of more than 100 towers was linked by telephone, and observers working alone watched the woods for smoke.[^2] The state ended that network after a budget crisis in 1991.[^2] The sources read do not name anyone who worked this tower or any fire it watched.

The Register says the tower is no longer in active use, but the Maine Forest Service still maintains it for emergency detection.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists it as standing.[^3]

The Register lists it as US 170, ME 8, nominated by Bob Spear of the Forest Fire Lookout Association, and registered it on May 30, 1996.[^1] The Maine Forest Service administers it.[^1] The sources do not say whether the public can visit or climb it, so check with the Maine Forest Service before planning a trip.
