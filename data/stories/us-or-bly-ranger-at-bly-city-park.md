The first lookout in Bly, Oregon, was a platform on the roof of a warehouse. The Klamath Forest Protective Association set it up at its ranger station, a short distance east of the Forest Service station, and the platform dates to 1934.[^1][^4]

When the Klamath station closed in the mid-1950s, the Forest Service put a 20-foot wooden tower on its own compound, topped with a 7x7 flat-roof cab.[^4][^2]

Sources differ on the year. The register gives 1950 in its build field, but its own description and the Forest Lookouts page both say 1958.[^1][^2]

The register listed the tower on March 31, 2003.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page says the structure was decommissioned in 2006 and slated for removal to a local park, where it would serve as an interpretive center. The project then got a one-year extension, since the building had no way to be moved.[^2]

In 2007 the tower and cab were dismantled, and the cab was moved to a city park in Bly.[^2][^3][^5]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the cab as a standing, ground-level structure at Bly City Park, and it lists the original site as a tower that is gone.[^6] We could not check the cab's condition today, so its current state rests on that list alone.

We found no source describing visiting hours or whether anyone may go inside the cab, so ask park or Forest Service staff before going in.

The sources we read do not name anyone who staffed the lookout or any fire it watched.
