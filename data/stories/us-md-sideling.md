In September 1930 the State Forestry Department announced a program to build a dozen new fire towers across Maryland, and one was planned for Sideling Hill in Washington County.[^1] Each tower was expected to cost between $1,500 and $2,000, with local residents and timber owners raising the money, because the state had no appropriation for the work.[^1] The building was to run for about six years, starting with a tower at Springfield in Prince George's County.[^1]

We did not find a build date, a height, a staffing record or a fire history for the Sideling tower in the sources we read. The 1930 article describes a plan, not a finished tower.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a standing tower at Sideling in Washington County.[^2] Its legend marks towers whose cab is gone with an asterisk, and the Sideling entry carries no asterisk.[^2]

We found no information about who can visit the site, so anyone curious should ask the landowner before going near it. Please do not climb the tower.
