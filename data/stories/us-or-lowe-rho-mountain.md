Lowe (Rho) Mountain is a former lookout site in the Mt. Hood National Forest, in Clackamas County, Oregon.[^1][^2] The first lookout here was a log cabin with an attached roof platform, which the register and firelookout.com date to 1915.[^1][^2][^5] An Estacada Progress item from July 1914 reported that a new fire station was being built on Mt. Lowe, and Oregon Hikers dates the first cabin to 1916, so the start date is not settled.[^3][^4] In 1932 an L-4 cab replaced the cabin.[^1][^2][^4][^5]

Forest Lookouts quotes local papers that name some of the people who worked the station.[^3] Chas. Frazier was to be stationed there in 1915.[^3] Ralph Reed was in charge in 1916.[^3] In 1918 Mrs. W. Wash and her daughter Eva were in charge.[^3]

An inspection report dated August 22, 1963 judged the building old and recommended replacing it.[^3] The lookout was deactivated in 1964.[^3] Sources put its removal between 1964 and 1966.[^1][^2][^5]

No source we read describes a standing structure at the site. Oregon Hikers says the summit still has stone steps, corroded sheet metal, glass gathered and stored in cans, and a fragment of the stay cable, and it mentions a couple of wind shelters.[^4]

A July 2025 trip report says the writer parked at the junction of NF-220 and NF-4670 and walked mostly along the NF-220 road. The summit is 1.42 miles round trip with 339 feet of climb.[^5] Oregon Hikers tags the three routes to the summit "Closed" without saying why, so check the Forest's current notices before you go.[^4]
