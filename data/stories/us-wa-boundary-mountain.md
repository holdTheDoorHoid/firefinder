Boundary Mountain got a lookout in 1938: a 40-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^1][^2][^3] It sat at the south end of the summit ridge, about 0.3 miles south of the border.[^3] According to WillhiteWeb, that spot was enough to see into the United States.[^3] The elevation is recorded two ways. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 5,140 feet, and WillhiteWeb gives 5,120.[^1][^3]

The tower is gone. The register and firelookout.com both describe it as gone, and a Washington lookout list marks it removed.[^1][^2][^4] The register added the site on November 23, 2012.[^1]

The site lies in the Colville National Forest.[^2][^4] Maps from the 1930s show a trail reaching almost to the summit, and later maps show an old Jeep trail up to the lookout area.[^3] A WillhiteWeb description from 2024 says a footing can still be seen at the site, though it is gradually being covered over. The trees there are getting bigger.[^3]

Visitors reach the area from Highway 31 north of Metaline Falls, then take forest roads.[^3] A gate on the access road is shut from December 1 to July 1 to protect wildlife habitat.[^3] The hike is about 1.3 miles one way.[^3] The last few hundred feet are off-trail bushwhacking, reaching old trees near the summit.[^3]
