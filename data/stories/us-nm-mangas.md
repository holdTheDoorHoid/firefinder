The Mangas Mountain Lookout Complex sits on Mangas Mountain, near Mangas in Catron County, New Mexico.[^1][^2] Besides the tower, the complex has a building described as a cabin or cottage, and it was added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 28, 1988.[^1]

A lookout was on the mountain before the present tower went up.[^3] A July 1924 Albuquerque Journal item, as the Forest Lookouts site quotes it, reports that a fire lookout named Willis Bitmore, spelled that way there, was killed when lightning struck the cabin where he and a companion had taken shelter.[^3] The item does not say whether that cabin stood where the current tower does.[^3]

The tower went up in 1934.[^2][^3] The Forest Service's history of southwestern lookouts, as the Forest Lookouts site quotes it, describes a 30-foot Aermotor MC-24 tower with an observation cab, along with a ground cabin.[^3] The register gives the tower as 31.6 feet, with a 12-by-12-foot wooden cab and no catwalk.[^2] The sources do not explain the difference in height.

A 2020 chapter report of New Mexico lookouts marks Mangas with an R, which means restoration or rehabilitation is needed.[^4] The same report does not mark it as proposed for the cabin rental program.[^4]

The sources also disagree about the forest. The register places the lookout on the Apache National Forest.[^2] The 2020 report uses both the Apache and Gila names, and Wikipedia files it under the Gila National Forest.[^4][^1]

The sources do not agree on its condition.[^4][^5] A hiking guide calls the complex little altered and says a long forest-road drive reaches it.[^5] The same guide cautions that the old buildings may be frail and hazardous, and says to stay out of closed ones.[^5] None of the sources I read say whether the public may climb the tower, and I found no current staffing or rental details for it.
