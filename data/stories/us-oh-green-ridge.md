Green Ridge Fire Tower stands in Pike State Forest in Pike County, Ohio, at about 386 meters (1,265 feet) above sea level.[^1][^4] The state began buying land for the forest in 1924, aiming to repair woodland that fire and human activity had damaged. During the 1930s the Civilian Conservation Corps and the Division of Forestry carried out much of that repair work.[^3]

The tower was going up by early 1934.[^2] A January 1934 item in the Chillicothe Gazette, relayed by a lookout history site, said work on Green Ridge, just north of Latham, was far along, and that the tower would probably help spot fires across southwestern Ross County.[^2] I could not find the tower's design, its height, or the date it was finished.

An October 1934 notice from the same paper said Ralph Newman of Latham was set to staff the new tower, one mile north of Latham.[^2] That notice describes a plan. The sources do not confirm when he took up the post or how long the tower stayed staffed, and the lookout history site has no other notes on fires or incidents here.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list of Ohio towers marks Green Ridge as standing.[^4] The National Historic Lookout Register added it on September 3, 2014, as US 1023, OH 12, nominated by someone named Mark.[^1] The register page has no description or visit notes, and the sources record no later move, restoration, or closure.[^1][^4]

The sources do not say whether the tower can be climbed or how visitors reach it, so check with the forest's managers before planning a trip.
