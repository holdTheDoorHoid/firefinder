Priscilla Peak lookout is a cabin rather than a tower.[^1] It stands about 11 miles northeast of Thompson Falls, in Sanders County, Montana.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register describes it as an L-2 cupola cabin with shake walls and a gable roof, built in 1929 in the Lolo National Forest, and counts it as one of three cabins of that style left in the North Region.[^1]

Newspaper clippings reprinted by the Forest Lookouts site show someone posted on the peak in 1930, 1940 and 1954.[^3] A 1954 storm report says hail broke every window in the station.[^3] A lookout history page lists a lookout there in 1955 and says the cabin was used for emergencies into the 1970s.[^2] The sources do not name any fire it watched.

The Register says the cabin was refurbished and added to the rental program in 1994, then dropped from it later because of little use.[^1] A 2006 visit report found that it appeared to be out of the program, with packrats chewing holes through the floor and the door left open after the last visitors.[^1] The Register records its registration on April 24, 2009.[^1]

In 2020 the Forest Service proposed taking the cabin apart, restoring it and moving it to Driveway Peak, where it would be opened as a rental.[^4][^5] The Forest Service project page lists the plan as cancelled and was last updated on June 17, 2020.[^4] A May 2020 Sanders County Ledger article described the cabin as having fallen into disrepair where it stood.[^5]

The sources do not say whether the cabin can be visited or rented today. The Register notes that the trailhead is not clearly signed.[^1] Anyone planning a visit should check with the Lolo National Forest before setting out.
