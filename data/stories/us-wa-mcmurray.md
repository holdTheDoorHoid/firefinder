The McMurray lookout stood on a 1,400-foot summit in Skagit County, about eight miles southeast of Mount Vernon.[^1][^2][^3] Willhite places it near Stanwood, relatively close to Interstate 5.[^3] It was a live-in tower, reported to have been built in 1930.[^1][^2][^3]

The name is a puzzle. A 1930 entry quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, taken from a Washington Forest Fire Association annual report, describes an English Lookout set on land given by the English Lumber Company.[^4] A mile and a half of road was built to link it with the county road.[^4] Willhite suggests the tower may have been named for Edward English, a timber owner whose land was donated for it, and notes that English died in 1930.[^3] Willhite adds that no known lookout in the area carries the name English Lookout.[^3] The sources do not show that the two names belong to the same tower.

A July 26, 1951 item from The Arlington Times, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, credits "the lady at the McMurray lookout station" with getting the fire department to a blaze quickly.[^4] That is the only staffing detail we found.

Sources say the tower was gone by the 1950s.[^1][^2][^3] A 1951 item shows the station was still in use that year, which sits awkwardly with that date.[^4] We found no source that describes how the tower came down.

Today the site lies on the Pilchuck Tree Farm, which has hiking, biking and horse trails.[^3] Getting in costs $2 per person and a signed waiver, which Willhite says can be done at the trailhead.[^3] The Pilchuck Glass School complex does not allow entry, but visitors can hike around it.[^3] The page describes a loop of about five miles, but says nothing about remains of the lookout on the summit.[^3]
