Big Knob is a fire lookout in Pulaski County, Kentucky, which the National Historic Lookout Register dates to the 1930s.[^1] The Register believes the Kentucky Division of Forestry ran it, but lists its administrator as unknown.[^1] The sources I read do not say exactly when it was built or who put it up.

A reminiscence article by Billy Joe Fudge, who wrote about his days with the Fire Patrol, gives the tower's height as 80 feet and says it was the tallest of roughly three dozen lookouts in the South Central District.[^2] The same article describes an observer who had manned the tower for about a dozen years, and says fire tower observers generally worked about two and a half months each in spring and fall.[^2] The sources I read name no specific fires the tower watched.

The Register says that as of 2018 the tower was abandoned but largely sound, with a little broken glass and light rust.[^1] Newer communications towers that American Tower Corporation owns stand next to it.[^1]

I found no source newer than 2018 on its condition, so anything since then is unknown. The Register does not say who may enter the site. Treat it as off limits unless the owner or administrator gives permission.

[^2]: Great Wooded South: Adventure at Big Knob, Billy Joe Fudge, Columbia Magazine (columbiamagazine.com), https://columbiamagazine.com/index.php?sid=126393 (accessed 2026-10-09).
