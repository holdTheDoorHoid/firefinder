A fire lookout went up on Smarts Mountain in 1915, when the New Hampshire Forestry Commission established the site.[^4][^1] The register describes it as the mountain's earliest lookout.[^1]

The steel tower that stands now dates from 1939.[^1][^5] Sources disagree about who built it. The register credits the Forestry Commission and says federal emergency money paid for it after the 1938 hurricane.[^1] Willhite's history says the Civilian Conservation Corps built it with Forest Service funds, in response to fire danger after the Great New England Hurricane of 1938.[^4] The tower stands 41 feet high, with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and Willhite says it came as a kit from the International Derrick Company.[^4]

In 1922 the lookout was scaled back to emergency use only, and by 1942 the Dartmouth Outing Club was renting the tower from the state for a dollar a year and staffing it when fire danger ran high.[^4] The station reopened in 1957.[^4] Willhite gives 1973 as the final year of active duty, but the same page also says the tower was staffed to 1980.[^4] In 1980 the site passed to the state Department of Public Works and Highways, according to Willhite.[^4] In 1994 the Dartmouth Outing Club refurbished the tower with Forest Service money.[^4] The register lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator, and a 2014 chapter report says the Forest acquired the tower as part of an Appalachian Trail land purchase.[^1][^2]

In August 2014 the Forest announced the tower would stay shut to visitors until urgent repairs were done.[^2] A chapter visitor who went up on August 30 found no reason for the closure other than an ongoing problem: three of the four guy cables had been out of service since the Forest took over the tower.[^2]

In 2016 HistoriCorps workers replaced the roof and added lightning protection, and the Forest Service brought the tower back open to visitors.[^3] The register's visit report from August 2016 praised the tower after its June restoration, and noted that it was no longer staffed.[^1] That report also said the kiosk signs in the parking lot were wrong: they still listed the tower as closed.[^1]

A 2020 Valley News photo caption says anyone willing to climb the steps can go up.[^6] The ascent is all Appalachian Trail, about four miles from the Lyme-Dorchester Road trailhead, and it begins on the Lambert Ridge Trail.[^6] I found no notice from 2025 or 2026, so check with the White Mountain National Forest before you go.
