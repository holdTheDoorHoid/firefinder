In 1922 a stone cabin went up on Mount Hood's south slope, chiefly as a storehouse for the summit lookout.[^1] In 1933 the Forest Service put up an L-4 cab on a 20-foot pole tower, about half a mile above Timberline Lodge at the midway offramp of a ski lift.[^1][^2][^3] An October 1933 item quoted by Forest Lookouts describes a lookout cabin and a shelter cabin under construction at the time.[^3]

Afterward Lone Fir was the favored lookout on the mountain, and the summit station fell out of use.[^4] A 1936 item said the summit had gone unused for a few years because clouds often hid the valleys.[^3] George Harrington started his duties at Lone Fir that week.[^3] In 1948 Ole Lien was the lookout there, and he was among three Forest Service men cited for rescue work on Mount Hood.[^3]

In May 1963 the Forest Service burned the lookout down, according to a report in The Oregonian that Forest Lookouts quotes.[^3][^4] The paper said the station had gone unused lately and was in poor repair, and that a new Magic Mile chairlift ran directly over the building.[^3] The register gives 1964 as the removal year, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year. Those 1964 dates differ from the 1963 newspaper report.[^1][^2]

A hiking guide's photos show the footprint, and captions note nails, glass, metal parts and anchors still on the site.[^4] The guide places the site directly beneath the fifth pole of the Magic Mile lift, counted from the midway offramp.[^4] It also gives walking access: ride the Magic Mile up and hike down the road, or walk up the access road from Timberline Lodge.[^4] No source I found says whether the public may visit the site.
