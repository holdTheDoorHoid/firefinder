Work on a fire tower at Siloam Springs, in Howell County, began in January 1935 at the intersection of Highway 14 and Road T, for the Gardiner forestry unit.[^1] The Willow Springs News item that reported it, as quoted on a lookout history site, gave the tower a height of 99 feet 9 inches, about 30 metres, and said the completed towers would have a cabin on top, where a lookout would stay and keep an eye out for fires across the unit.[^1]

A June 1936 West Plains Journal item described planned grounds for the site: a circular drive, parking, tables and outdoor ovens for picnics, and a cabin for the tower guard.[^1] A June 1951 item in the Albany Ledger still mentioned picnic grounds close to the tower.[^1] A January 1937 Gasconade County Republican item said a fifth tower was being built near Siloam Springs. It does not say whether that was the same structure as the 1935 tower.[^1]

A 1963 survey note recorded that a forestry lookout had been constructed over a survey mark set in 1928.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page lists the tower as removed, without a date or method.[^1] A 2006 recovery note found the survey mark in good condition.[^2] None of the sources read for this story describes what remains of the tower, or whether the site is open to visitors.

[^1]: Siloam Springs - FOREST LOOKOUTS, Forest Lookouts website, https://centraluslookouts.weebly.com/siloam-springs.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Datasheet GF0638, SILOAM, National Geodetic Survey, https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/cgi-bin/ds_mark.prl?PidBox=GF0638 (accessed 2026-10-21).
