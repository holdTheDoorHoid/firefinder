The Knobs tower stands in the northern unit of Moshannon State Forest in Clearfield County, north of Odessa along Knobs Road.[^1][^2] The Pennsylvania Bureau of Forestry built it in 1921 to oversee that part of the forest.[^1][^2] It is a 60-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and the site also has a residence cabin built in 1933.[^1]

A retired forest fire warden told WPSU that he had climbed the wooden steps hundreds of times while staffing this tower and others nearby.[^3] A cabin beside the tower is where spotters once slept between shifts.[^3] I found no record of notable fires at this tower in the sources I read.

The Bureau refurbished the tower in 2004, adding new steps and an interior.[^1] The Register listed it on December 15, 1995, with a nomination from Stephen J. Cummings of the Bureau of Forestry and FFLA.[^1]

By 2019 the tower was no longer in use, though it still stood beside its cabin.[^3][^4] The newer lookout on Chestnut Ridge is 80 feet tall and was used for the first time that spring, and the two towers are about two miles apart.[^3][^4] The Register says the tower is active during fire season, but that page is undated, so the sources disagree.[^1]

I found no current source on public access or on whether visitors may go up the tower. The Register says the Bureau still maintains it, so check with the Bureau before you go, and do not climb the structure.[^1]
