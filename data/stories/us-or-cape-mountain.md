Cape Mountain rises about 11 miles north of Florence, in Lane County, inside the Siuslaw National Forest.[^1][^3] In 1932 Oregon newspapers reported a new forest road under construction toward the peak, with a lookout to follow once the road was finished.[^2] The lookout went up in 1933 as an L-4 cab.[^2][^1]

The first staffing we found dates to spring 1935. Floyd Fisk was moved from Prairie Mountain to Cape Mountain as a precaution in a hazardous district.[^2] Donald Johnson went up to the station that year and again in 1936.[^2] In 1941 the Mapleton district ranger was appointed to organize airplane observation posts, and Cape Mountain was one of them.[^2] In July 1943 Mr. and Mrs. Ernest Ready resigned, and Mr. and Mrs. Walter Dennis of Clatskanie took over, the last staffing change we found.[^2]

The sources disagree about the cab's fate. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says a private owner bought the lookout in 1951 and moved it to Heceta Beach, where it became a weekend cabin.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page also dates the removal to 1951, and says that, according to a Florence resident interviewed in the early 1980s, it went to a private site on Heceta Beach Road.[^2] The firelookout.com page says it was destroyed in 1950.[^3]

A federal survey station description on the Forest Lookouts page places the lookout at the highest point of the mountain.[^2] We found no source that describes what remains on the peak today, or whether visitors can reach it.[^1]
