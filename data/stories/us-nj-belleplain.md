Belleplain Fire Tower stands in Belleplain State Forest, in Cape May County, New Jersey.[^1] Its first date is disputed.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register says it was built in 1921 as a 60-foot tower.[^1] A state annual report for 1922-23, as reproduced on a lookout history site, says a 60-foot steel tower was erected about a mile north of Belle Plain in 1922 and opened for service on April 1.[^2] The sources we checked do not settle the year.[^1][^2]

Surrounding trees soon overtook the lookout, and in the 1930s a 40-foot section was added.[^1] The register gives the tower's height as 100 feet, an Aermotor steel structure with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab, and says it is still in use.[^1] A Wikipedia list of the state's towers gives the site an elevation of 56 feet (17 m).[^3]

The early record suggests a seasonal post.[^2] A December 1926 press note said the tower was shut for the winter.[^2]

In 1966 the state sought bids to relocate the existing tower and put up a new 40-foot extension.[^2] That notice shows a plan rather than a finished job, and the register does not mention any move.[^1][^2] It is unclear whether that extension was the same work as the 1930s section.[^1][^2]

Today the register lists the tower as administered by the New Jersey Forest Fire Service.[^1] We found no source saying whether the public may visit.[^1][^2][^3] Since it remains an active fire tower on state land, the safe course is to stay off it unless the agency invites you up.[^1]
