Flagstaff Mountain is in Somerset County, Maine, reached from Route 27 in Eustis by a long drive on dirt roads.[^1][^2] A steel tower replaced a wooden lookout on the mountain in 1917.[^3] A 1917 report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, gives its height as 50 feet, while the 1920 inventory lists 48 feet.[^3] Hikers today describe it as a 50-foot tower.[^2]

The former town of Flagstaff, near Eustis, was abandoned and taken apart in 1950 so a hydroelectric dam on the Dead River could be built. The dam enlarged Flagstaff Lake, and the water covered the settlement's site.[^4] The retired tower now overlooks Flagstaff Lake.[^5]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing, and its key says that asterisk means the cab is no longer in place.[^1] We did not find when the cab came down.

The Lewiston Sun Journal described the tower in August 2026 as retired and in rough shape, with rusted support cables that have come loose and about 15 feet of ladder gone from its base.[^5] Most visitors photograph it from below and leave, though some hikers still try to climb up to the ladder.[^5] The hike is about 4 miles round trip, starting from an unmarked trailhead on an overgrown path.[^5][^2] With the lowest rungs gone, reaching the first usable one means shimmying up the support bars.[^2] A June 2026 hiking blog says someone has kept up the trail, and most fallen trees across it had been cut.[^6]

We would not climb it. The tower is in poor shape, and its lower ladder is missing.[^5]
