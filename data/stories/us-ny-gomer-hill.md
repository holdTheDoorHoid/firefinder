Gomer Hill's lookout is a 67-foot-6-inch International Derrick steel tower on a hill about four miles west of Turin, in Lewis County, New York.[^1][^2] The Civilian Conservation Corps put it up in 1940, when its Boonville camp, S-122, built it.[^2][^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register and Wikipedia both give the height as 67 feet 6 inches.[^1][^3] A 1942 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description, though, calls the tower roughly 80 feet high overall.[^5] This story uses the 67-foot figure because three sources repeat it.

The lookout was first manned in 1941; that year it logged two fires and 161 visitors.[^2][^3] Operations ended with the 1988 season.[^2][^3]

The Department of Environmental Conservation officially closed it in early 1989, after concluding that towers had lost their effectiveness and were being phased out.[^2] The Register gives 1989 as the decommissioning year.[^1] The tower was left standing, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's New York list still marks it as standing.[^4] Wikipedia says the tower now supports antennas.[^3]

The Register places the site on public State Lands, yet the tower is closed because it carries a lot of radio gear.[^1] A New York lookout association page says people may visit the site, but the view is gone without the tower.[^2] Nothing in these sources suggests the public may climb the tower, so treat it as closed.
