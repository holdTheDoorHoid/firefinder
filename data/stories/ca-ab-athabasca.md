The Athabasca fire lookout sits about 25 km from Hinton, Alberta.[^1][^2] News reports disagree on the direction: Global News puts it northwest of the town, while the Edmonton Journal says north.[^1][^2]

Alberta's wildfire register gives 2011 as the construction year for the tower now listed on the site. It is a steel tower, 18 m (59 ft) tall, at about 1,582 m (5,190 ft) above sea level.[^3]

A tower was already in use there before that. For the last 13 years of a long career, a seasonal fire spotter worked at the Athabasca watch tower every April through September, and the tower had a cabin on site.[^1] If the register date is correct, the tower she used was an earlier structure.[^1][^3]

On 26 August 2006 the spotter was reported missing from the lookout after failing to make a routine radio check-in.[^1]

In 2018, twelve years on, the RCMP's historical homicide unit led a ground-and-water search around the tower.[^4] Roughly 100 searchers, RCMP and search-and-rescue crews among them, covered the ground near the tower.[^2] The missing spotter is presumed dead.[^2]

After the disappearance the province tightened security at its 128 fire watch towers, a newspaper report at the time said. Fire watchers received self-defence training, and they were issued two-way radios with panic buttons.[^2] Lookouts with road access were fitted with locked gates, and signs pointing visitors to popular lookouts were taken down.[^2]

The register still lists the Athabasca tower as active, reached by road, with a weather station.[^3] No source describes public visits to the site, so treat it as a working fire-watch tower rather than a place to visit.
