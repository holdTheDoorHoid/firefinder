The Conservation Commission acquired ten acres for a fire tower in 1939.[^1] Between 1939 and 1941, an order was placed for materials for a 68-foot (21 m) steel tower.[^1]

The site sat on the tallest hill nearby, called High Pointe, roughly ten miles south of Richwoods in Washington County.[^1] In July 1940, crews from Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) Company 2728 were readying the site for the tower and a towerman's cottage.[^1] District Forester William Towell, whose office was in Sullivan, was supervising the construction.[^1] The plan was for a tower identical to those at Meramec Park and Leasburg, which would make it the third fire lookout in the district.[^1] The plan also called for 22 miles of telephone wire between the tower and the CCC camp, so the towerman could alert the forestry office to fires.[^1]

The 1942 annual report lists that year's construction as one pole tower, a garage and a woodshed.[^1] The records disagree on the tower's type: the 1939 to 1941 order was for steel, and the 1942 report says pole. I have not chosen between them.[^1]

In September 1943, District Forester R.O. Danson said he had been occupied building the tower's cabin, roughly ten miles southwest of Richwoods.[^1] The 1940 items put the site ten miles south, so the two accounts differ slightly on direction.[^1]

The Official Manual of the State of Missouri for 1945-46 describes the towersite as 10 acres, owned by the Missouri Conservation Commission.[^1] In the summer of 1946, the tower's roads were being built.[^1] In August 1950, the Sullivan News reported that the Stoddard family had moved to the tower, with Tracy Stoddard to serve as towerman.[^1]

The annual report for fiscal 1953-54 records a replacement of the tower at High Point.[^1] Annual reports from 1967-68 to 1981-82 mostly record upkeep. Major repairs went to the residence in 1967-68, the towersite got a new well in 1968-69, a pump house and a new pump went in at the towersite during 1969-70, and the tower was rewired and fitted with a new heater in 1971-72.[^1] The last entry, for 1981-82, says the porch and a bathroom floor in the residence were replaced.[^1]

The page's latest entry is from 1981-82, and no source I could read describes the site today, so I cannot say whether the tower still stands or whether visitors can reach it.[^1] Do not climb any tower without permission.
