Gilson Mountain, in Fletcher, Franklin County, once carried a steel fire tower.[^1] A 1936 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, gave its height as 72 1/2 feet and called it the highest in the state.[^1]

Through the 1940s and early 1950s the station was kept up in small, steady ways. A logging operator destroyed the telephone line, and it was rebuilt.[^1] Lightning protection went onto the tower and cabin, and the cab was painted and a grounding system added.[^1] In the 1949–50 report period a new telephone was connected, the stairs and landing were painted, and a new cook stove went in.[^1]

In July 1955 a Rutland Daily Herald item, also quoted there, reported that dismantling had started in Fletcher and that the steel tower was headed for Bear Hill at Allis State Park in Brookfield, where it was to take the place of a wooden tower.[^1] The Vermont Forest Service's biennial report for 1955 to 1956 records the end of the lookout: the state stopped using it for fire watch, the steel tower came down and went to Bear Hill, and the telephone line was recovered. Airplane observers now cover the ground it once watched.[^1]

Allis State Park, established in 1928, has a fire or lookout tower on Bear Hill; the summit of Bear Hill is the park's main feature.[^2] The work at Bear Hill was to begin with a closure of the tower from May 1 to June 15.[^3] A WCAX report from May 2025 said the state was starting a project to maintain and improve its fire towers.[^4]

None of the sources we read says whether the tower on Bear Hill today is the steel one moved from Gilson in 1955.

The Vermont lookout list marks the Gilson tower as gone.[^5] No source we found describes what remains at the Gilson Mountain site now, or whether it is open to visitors.
