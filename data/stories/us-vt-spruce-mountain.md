Spruce Mountain sits within Plainfield, Vermont, and its summit has hosted three generations of fire lookouts.[^1][^3] The first was an open platform, built in 1919.[^1][^3]

The Register says that platform gave way in 1931 to a taller spruce log tower with a wooden cab.[^1] A state biennial report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, instead describes a glass-walled tower, 50 feet tall, put up that autumn.[^2] The steel tower standing there now, with its 7-by-7-foot cab, came from Bellevue Hill, and the Register and Forest Lookouts date that move to 1943.[^1][^2] The state's Jones State Forest page gives 1944.[^3] These sources do not settle the year or the 1931 design.

A ranger was on the tower at least once in its working life. A September 1959 Burlington Free Press item says a hiking group was met at the top by Neil Rogers and his wife, and identifies Rogers as the fire tower ranger.[^2] The tower no longer serves as a fire-watch post.[^1] The Register's visit page, based on an October 2016 visit, answers no to whether the tower is staffed.[^1]

The Register reports that the Vermont State Parks Division now looks after the tower.[^1] Interior cab railings were installed on it in 2025 as part of updates to Vermont fire towers.[^4] The 2016 visit report rates the tower in great condition, though one wall of the cab had come loose from the cab floor.[^1]

Hikers can still climb the tower, the state says.[^3] Access is from Spruce Mountain Road to the trailhead, and the trail to the summit is 2.2 miles in each direction.[^3] The visit notes say the trailhead is well marked.[^1] The state puts the summit at 3,037 feet, while the Register lists the site at 2,947 feet.[^3][^1] Check current conditions with the state before going up.
