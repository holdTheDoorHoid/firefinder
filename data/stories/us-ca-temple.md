Los Angeles County's forestry department built the Temple lookout in 1937, on a leased site in the San Gabriel wash, about four miles east of Temple.[^1][^2] The lease was from Anna M. Spence of Monrovia, at $5 a year.[^2] Forest Lookouts quotes county reports and a newspaper saying the tower was erected by a Civilian Conservation Corps camp at Tuna Canyon.[^2] The register says the lookout was dedicated on June 2, 1937, and that William D. Reed was its first operator.[^1]

The register describes an 8-by-8-foot observation cabin on a 75-foot open steel tower, with a residence cabin and a garage.[^1] Forest Lookouts quotes a January 1937 newspaper item that gave 70 feet, and a county annual report that gave sixty feet, so the height is not settled.[^2] The county said the tower would help spot brush fires in the Puente Hills and along the Santa Ana foothills.[^2]

The register places the site on California Street, north of Arrow Highway, and says it gave a wide view across the San Gabriel, Sawpit and Dalton canyons.[^1]

According to the register, smog had ruined the view of the mountain front, and the lookout closed in 1947.[^1] The county's own annual report for fiscal 1947 gave other reasons. Rural development had changed conditions, public telephone service meant more fires were reported by citizens, and a detection study led it to discontinue the tower.[^2] The register adds that the structure came down shortly afterward and the land reverted to the City of Monrovia.[^1]

I found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether it is open to visitors.
