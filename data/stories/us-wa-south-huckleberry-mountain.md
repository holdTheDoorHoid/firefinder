South Huckleberry Mountain is a former lookout site in the Colville National Forest, Ferry County, Washington, at 5,273 feet.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register describes a shingled cabin with a notably tall cupola and dates it to 1924.[^1]

Sources disagree about the build date. WillhiteWeb found photographs labeled 1915, others labeled 1924, and one that says the cabin or cupola went up in 1925 under Ranger Duffield.[^3] The same author suggests the cupola may postdate the original building.[^3]

WillhiteWeb names Roland Kelley as the lookout in 1923, when he was a high school student.[^3] A 1923 newspaper item says he had been working with the forest reserves on Huckleberry Mountain before he started his senior year.[^4] Rex's Fire Lookout Page spells the name Kelly.[^2] WillhiteWeb also names Stanly Morris for 1930 and Herman Keyser for 1932, who may have been a handyman rather than a lookout. In 1944 the lookouts were Mr. and Mrs. Hazen Walston of Colville.[^3][^4]

In August 1929 a Colville Examiner item reported that the Growden ranger station, eight miles west of Kettle Falls on Huckleberry Mountain, was destroyed by fire.[^3][^4] The lookout, Merritt Charlton, narrowly escaped.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb is not sure whether he was at his post or at the ranger station.[^3] Firelookout.com lists Jack Charlton as the 1929 lookout.[^2]

The register says the cabin lasted into the 1940s.[^1] Firelookout.com gives its span as 1924 to the 1940s.[^2] A Washington lookout index marks the site as removed.[^4]

No source describes the site today.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
