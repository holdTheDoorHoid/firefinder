White Rock Mountain, in Franklin County, Arkansas, is part of the Ozark National Forest.[^1] An August 1936 newspaper account, quoted on a lookout site, describes a government-built recreation area on the summit and an 85-foot (25.9 m) ranger lookout tower standing on top.[^1] The writer also notes that lookout pavilions sit right at the lip of the steep bluffs.[^1]

A federal survey mark on the summit was described in 1971. It sits 13 feet northwest of the southeast leg of the lookout tower.[^2] The same description places a frame house and a small concrete block building nearby.[^2] The mark is stamped "WHITE ROCK TOWER 1935 NO 1 1971," and the survey gives its height as 2301 feet.[^2]

The sources do not agree on the summit's height. The 1936 account gives 2281 feet, the survey 2301 feet, and a recreation-area listing 2,260 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

Today the mountaintop is a recreation area. Day use is open year-round from sunrise to sunset, and the campground is available year-round.[^4] Three natural stone cabins and a lodge, built in the 1930s by the Civilian Conservation Corps, are on the mountain, and guests can rent the cabins at any time of the year.[^3] A 1.5-mile loop trail circles the rim of the mountaintop.[^3]

No source we read says whether the lookout tower still stands.
