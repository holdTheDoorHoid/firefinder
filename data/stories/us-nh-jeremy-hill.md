Jeremy Hill is a summit in the town of Pelham, in Hillsborough County.[^1]

A hiking guide gives the tower's years as 1917 to 1981.[^2] A Lowell Sun writer who visited in June 1934 described a boxlike cab with windows on all four sides.[^1] The cab stood 60 feet (18 m) high on steel stilts, and guy wires braced it.[^1]

The tower also housed a weather station that the U.S. Weather Bureau and New Hampshire's forest fire service ran together.[^1] The article ranks it among four key weather stations in New England.[^1] Ernest J. Teer kept the tower and its meteorological station in 1934.[^1] That August, Teer watched from the tower as a very large turkey buzzard circled over the hill, a species that the newspaper called very rare in the area.[^1]

By 1967 a watchman, Malcolm Popp, was on duty.[^1] In September 1967 vandals broke into the state lookout, and several valuable items, including some of Popp's tools, went missing.[^1]

The Forest Lookouts list marks the tower as gone.[^5] A hiking guide says the remains include four cement footings, metal posts and barrels, set in the trees near the summit.[^2] The summit lies within Jeremy Hill State Forest.[^2] A visitor in November 2024 found the cement pad still in place, with metal poking out of it, and a scatter of rusty barrels and other odd objects nearby.[^3]

Hikers reach the summit from the end of Jeremy Hill Road.[^4] One account says to park in the cul-de-sac there, and it notes a private pond beside the route.[^4] Another warns of no-trespassing signs along the path, so check access before going.[^3]
