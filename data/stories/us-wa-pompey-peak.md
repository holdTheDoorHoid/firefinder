Pompey Peak is in the Gifford Pinchot National Forest in Lewis County, Washington, at 5,180 feet.[^1]

The Forest Service dates the summit lookout to 1934.[^4] Willhite thinks it was probably put up in the early 1930s, and describes a cab that rests partly on the summit rocks and partly on a lower level.[^3] The register describes the lookout as a 10-foot wooden L-4 tower.[^1]

The peak takes its name from a pack mule that the Forest Service says died in a fall in the 1890s.[^4] Willhite calls that story questionable, because the name was already on a 1915 map before any structure was built there.[^3]

During World War II the lookout was used by the Aircraft Warning Service. The post ran from November 8, 1942 to October 15, 1943.[^3][^5] A 1956 newspaper item says a woman worked with the Aircraft Warning Service during the war.[^5]

The lookout was abandoned in the 1950s.[^1][^2] It burned in 1958, and a 1961 USGS party found the lookout house and other buildings burned.[^2][^3] The register added the site on July 16, 2012.[^1]

Today the register describes the lookout as gone, and the Forest Service says it was given up and later destroyed.[^1][^4] Trail 128 allows hikers, horses and bicycles, but not motorized vehicles.[^4] A Forest Service note dated October 4, 2021 says the trailhead has been unreachable for a decade because of road washouts, so call the Cowlitz Valley Ranger District at (360) 497-1100 before you go.[^4] Willhite describes a different approach: drive Forest Road 20 for roughly 13 miles, then hike in from the east, using the Klickitat Trail and the Pompey Peak Trail, a 9.5-mile round trip.[^3]
