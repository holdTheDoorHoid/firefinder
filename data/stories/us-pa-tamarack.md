The Tamarack lookout stands in Clinton County, in the Sproul State Forest, and takes its name from the nearby village of Tamarack.[^3][^4] A 1926 description says that village lies a few miles to the north of the tower.[^4]

The register dates the lookout's service start to 1919.[^1] A compilation of Eastern lookouts is less sure. It quotes the Lock Haven Express from May 1920, which described a new steel fire tower at Tamarack as nearly finished, and it quotes a 1926 account dating construction to 1920.[^4] The story map also gives 1920.[^3] The sources differ by a year, and I could not settle which is right.

According to the register, the original was 65 feet tall, built by Aermotor, with a cab measuring 7 by 7 feet.[^1] The story map describes a 60-foot tower that was replaced in 2018, and its entry gives a height of 80 feet.[^3]

The register puts the replacement with a modern steel structure at around 2020.[^1] The story map dates the replacement to 2018.[^3] A separate entry on the Former Fire Lookout Sites register covers a Tamarack tower site where a bald tower was in use around 1917 and has since gone. It is not clear whether that entry describes an earlier tower on this spot.[^2]

The Bureau of Forestry still keeps staff at the lookout during fire season.[^1][^3] A 2021 visit describes the current structure as galvanized steel and kept in pristine shape.[^1] I did not find accounts of notable fires from this tower in the sources I read. The sources do not give access rules, so check with the Bureau of Forestry before visiting.
