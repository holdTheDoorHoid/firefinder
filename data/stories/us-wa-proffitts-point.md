Proffitts Point is a knoll three miles east of Skykomish, in King County, Washington.[^2][^3] Willhite places it among the earliest lookouts along the Skykomish Valley and its railroad corridor.[^3] The sources disagree on its elevation. The register gives 3,300 feet, while firelookout.com and Willhite give 1,360 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

The register and firelookout.com both date the cabin to 1920, the year it was built.[^1][^2] Willhite, citing lookout data, calls it a cabin from the 1920s.[^3]

An August 5, 1934 Osborne photo survey shows the cleared knoll, a cabin tucked behind a stump, what may be one or two outhouses or sheds, and two poles, one with the American flag.[^3]

The cabin was gone by 1938, according to the register, firelookout.com and Willhite.[^1][^2][^3]

Willhite reports that power lines now use the flat perch.[^3] He could find no evidence of the lookout left on the site.[^3] Firelookout.com and Willhite both say the site is on private property.[^2][^3] Willhite's road route reaches a gate with many No Trespassing signs, and he says the landowner or the power company must give permission to use the easement.[^3]
