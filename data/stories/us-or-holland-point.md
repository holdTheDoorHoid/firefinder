Holland Point, in the Umpqua National Forest about 8 miles southwest of Oakridge in Lane County, got its lookout in 1933.[^1][^3] A September 1933 Roseburg Chieftain item, quoted on Rex's page, said the CCC would build a 14-by-14 lookout before the snow flew.[^2] The same page describes the finished tower as a round fir pole structure with an L-4 model 1931 cab. Building it took 125 man-days, and getting the materials in required a 12-mile pack haul.[^2]

The sources disagree on the height. The register and firelookout.com both give 30 feet,[^1][^3] a July 1938 inspection letter says 40 feet,[^2] and Rex's 1933 description says 43 feet.[^2]

That July, District Ranger Brown visited the tower and house and wrote that the sway made the fire finder impossible to keep level.[^2] The letter said the lookout could do very little of the needed repair work alone.[^2] Ring-connected pole braces were added in 1940.[^2]

The register and firelookout.com both describe the tower as gone.[^1][^3]

The register has a 2007 photo captioned as the site, but no source we read describes what is there now.[^1]
