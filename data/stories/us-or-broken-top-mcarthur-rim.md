The Deschutes National Forest built a new lookout in 1931, half a mile south of Three Creek Lake.[^1] It was a 14-by-14-foot L-4 cabin with a gable roof and a wood frame, and its construction cost was $688.74.[^1][^3] A Bend Bulletin item from August 1931, reprinted on a lookout history site, reported that work had started on a new standard lookout house on a ridge near Three Creeks Lake.[^3]

The same item named Veldon Parker as the lookout on duty at the new post.[^3] In July 1935 a newspaper report described a lightning storm in which bolts struck rocks and trees near the cabin of Theron Beougher, the Deschutes lookout there.[^3] In July 1938, Charles Combs was assigned to the Broken Top lookout as its fireman.[^3]

The register says the cabin was abandoned in 1952 and is now gone.[^1] Firelookout.com, a second site, gives the same 1952 date.[^2] Only the concrete foundation blocks are left.[^1] The register also describes a structure in a nearby whitebark pine that may be part of an old crow's nest.[^1] An aluminum marker on a treated post is dated July 31, 1966.[^1]

According to the register, the access road is barely visible and hard to follow, and the newer TWA Trail is the easier route.[^1] It does not say whether the public may visit the site.[^1]
