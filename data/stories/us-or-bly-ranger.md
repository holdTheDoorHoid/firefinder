Bly's lookout started in 1934 on a platform atop a warehouse at the Klamath Forest Protective Association station.[^1][^2][^3] The sources do not agree on when the tower came. The register gives 1950 as the build year, but its own description dates the 20-foot tower to 1958, and the Weebly page agrees with 1958.[^1][^4] The Former Fire Lookout Sites record says the Forest Service put it up in the mid-1950s, after the warehouse station closed.[^2]

The tower stood in the Forest Service ranger station compound.[^2][^4] The register describes it as a 20-foot wooden tower with a 7x7 flat-roofed cab and a catwalk.[^1] Nobody I found names a lookout who worked it. The Weebly page lists it as under emergency status, and a 2022 travel blog says it saw use only in emergencies.[^4][^5]

In 2006 the lookout was decommissioned and scheduled to go to a local park as an interpretive center.[^4] The move was delayed a year because there was no way to move the building.[^4] By 2007 the tower had been dismantled. The superstructure was destroyed, and the cab went to the community park.[^3][^4]

The cab now sits in Bly's community park, under a mile from the ranger station.[^5] The blog's June 2022 visit found it in a small park it calls The Perch, next to the Bly Rural Fire Protection District building, with fire-related items inside.[^5] The blog lists the lookout as maintained for educational purposes.[^5] The sources call it a community park, but I found nothing that says whether visitors may go inside the cab.

None of the sources I read describes what is left of the 1934 platform or the 1958 tower at the ranger station today.
