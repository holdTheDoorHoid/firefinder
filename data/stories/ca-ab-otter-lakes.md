Otter Lakes is a steel fire lookout tower, 30 m (98 ft) tall, standing at 784 m (2,572 ft) above sea level, according to Alberta Wildfire's lookout layer.[^1] The layer gives 2014 as the year the present structure was built.[^1] Steel came into use for lookout towers in the 1920s, and wood gradually gave way to it.[^2] For over a century, Alberta's seasonal lookout observers have been responsible for detecting fires, identifying them and passing on their locations.[^2]

The layer names the site Otter Lakes. A Lakeside Leader report places lakes of that name north of Little Buffalo, about 30 km east-northeast of Red Earth Creek.[^3] Alberta reports that each of its 127 wildfire lookout sites has a staff member on watch daily, usually from April through October.[^4]

The layer lists the site as active, with access mode fly, which suggests staff reach it by air.[^1] No source I found says the public may visit. Alberta describes the sites as remote, and staff there have to plan for many contingencies, including a curious bear.[^4] Treat Otter Lakes as an active, staffed lookout, not a place to visit.
