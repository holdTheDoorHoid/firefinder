The earliest record I found for the Piute Mountain station is from June 1931, when a Bakersfield paper reported that Theo Leibel had moved up the mountain for the summer and taken a government lookout job there.[^1] A second notice that June described a government road camp on the mountain, where a forestry worker was employed on a new road being built from Bodfish to the station. The paper said the road made the station reachable from the Kern River side and cut the drive by about 50 miles.[^1]

Road building went on into 1933, when an assistant foreman, John Potter, was reported to be building a road from Bodfish grade to the station.[^1] In 1935 a telephone line was being strung up from Tehachapi to the station.[^1]

In 1936 high winds made it necessary to re-anchor the lookout. Ted Leibel, who was stationed there, had several tense moments before the job was finished.[^1] The notices spell his first name Theo in 1931 and Ted in 1936, and they do not say whether these are the same man.[^1]

In November 1942 a Fresno paper reported that Floyd J. Walker, 35, a forest fire lookout at Piute Mountain, had died when his car crashed into a shallow ravine seven miles below the lookout. The paper said he was believed to have dozed at the wheel on his way back to his post after visiting family at Rosedale.[^1]

The Forest Lookout Association's California list marks the Piute site as gone, and classes it as a ground lookout rather than a tower.[^2]

I found no source that describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today. A Forest Service page describes the Lookout Trail, 4.9 miles long, which runs from Piute Mountain Road near French Meadow to Piute Lookout Road. It is open to hiking, horseback riding, motorcycles and mountain biking.[^3] The same page warns that fires and floods may leave washouts and downed trees.[^3] A peak list calls the summit a lookout site, a label it uses for sites whose building is gone or whose status is unknown.[^4]
