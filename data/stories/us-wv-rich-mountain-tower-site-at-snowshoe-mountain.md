The Rich Mountain fire tower stood in Randolph County, West Virginia, by 1925, and the state's Division of Forestry administered it.[^1] In 1999 the tower was sold to Snowshoe Mountain, Inc.[^1] It was taken down and moved to the Snowshoe ski resort in Pocahontas County.[^1][^2] One history page says the state's Department of Natural Resources sold it for one dollar.[^3]

In 2000 the tower was reconditioned to its original specifications, then painted and reassembled.[^2][^3] It is now an 80-foot tower used as an attraction.[^3]

The NHLR entry gives 1999 as the build year, but that is the year the tower was moved.[^2] The older site record places its construction by 1925, so the 1999 date describes the relocation, not the first build.[^1]

A June 2021 visit recorded the tower in good condition, with sound stairs and wood, and signed directions to it.[^2] The register describes it as standing at the privately owned Snowshoe Mountain ski resort, so check with the resort before you go.[^2] Do not climb it without the resort's permission.

At the original site in Randolph County, a July 2022 photograph shows footings.[^1]
