Mud Spring was a lookout site on a ridge in the Santa Fe National Forest, in Rio Arriba County, New Mexico.[^2][^1] By 1922 the ridge lay on the Chupadera patrol route, which extended north into Jicarilla land.[^1] A 1924 composite seen-area map did not show the lookout, but it demonstrated a blind spot to the northwest, and the forest history says that analysis probably prompted the lookout here.[^1]

The build date is uncertain. The Forest Fire Lookout Association gives 1929, but Pablo Casados said it dated to the mid-1920s, about when Deadman's first cabin and tower went up.[^1] Forest maps show the lookout from 1933 through 1950.[^1]

Two pole platforms stood on the mesa top, one against a boulder facing south and one facing north, each with a map board.[^1] Pablo Casados recalled a tent rather than any building, while Sergio Morfin remembered a lean-to or throw-down cabin.[^1]

In the 1940s and 1950s, head fire guards Solomon Lovato and Sergio Morfin brought lookouts up to their posts and trained them for the job.[^1] Morfin posted Ben Archuleta here soon after World War II, and the site was also called Deer Run.[^1] The Forest classed it as a secondary or emergency lookout, staffed mainly by patrolmen when fire danger was high.[^1]

A 1947 recommendation for a tower, latrine, accessory building, cistern and corral never led to construction.[^1] By 1956 the telephone line had been taken out, and that year a new lookout at Cedar Springs, on nearby Jicarilla land, began reporting smokes, so the Forest cut staffing here.[^1] The site was listed as an emergency lookout in 1960 and 1961, when it reported a few fires; by 1963 it had dropped out of the Fire Plan.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list still marks the platforms as gone.[^2] The 2017 forest history, written by Barbara Zinn, tells a fuller story: in 2014 remains of the telephone line and trail were still visible, the telephone tree still stood, and map board pedestals lay on the platforms.[^1]

Crews used a Mud Spring patrol point in the 1990s, but now visit an electronics site at Wolf Draw to scan Deadman's blind spot.[^1] The latest dated observation of the site in the report is from 2014, and the report does not say whether visitors can reach the mesa.[^1]
