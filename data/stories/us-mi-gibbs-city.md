The Gibbs City Fire Tower stands in Iron County, Michigan, in the Ottawa National Forest, and the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^1] The register credits Company 3611 (James Lake) of the Civilian Conservation Corps with erecting it in 1939 and 1940.[^1] It is a 100-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1]

The forest also holds the site of Gibbs City, a sawmill town.[^2] A fire destroyed the mill in 1921, and most of the town was left empty.[^2] A controlled burn destroyed the last standing buildings in 1966.[^2]

The register says the tower remained active until 1970, when air detection replaced it.[^1] We found no named lookouts or notable fires for this tower in the sources we read.

A Michigan fire-tower history site says that in 1962 aerial surveillance and population growth led to the decline of the state's towers, and that the state began selling the towers in 1970.[^3] That is a statewide account, not a record of this tower.

According to the register, this is the only fire tower left on the Iron River district.[^1] It gives no recent condition report, and we found no current access information. The Iron River Ranger District is listed as a cooperator, so ask there before visiting.[^1] Do not climb the tower unless the Forest Service says it is open.
