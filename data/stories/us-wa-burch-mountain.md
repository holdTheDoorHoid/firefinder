Burch Mountain rises to 7,782 feet in Okanogan County, Washington, and sits along the edge of the Pasayten Wilderness.[^1][^2][^4]

The first lookout here dates from the 1920s. It was a log and frame cabin with a cupola.[^1][^2] A December 1930 item described it as log construction, 14 feet by 14, with a 6-by-6 dog house on its roof.[^3]

In July 1931 the Wenatchee Daily World reported that a couple from Hollywood, California, had gone to "Birch Mountain" to stay as long as the fire danger lasted.[^3] The Forest Lookouts page files that item under Burch, but the paper spells the name Birch, so we have not confirmed the match. The sources we read name no later staff.

The second lookout was built in 1934.[^1][^2][^4] The register and Firelookout.com give it an L-4 cab on a 10-foot tower, while WillhiteWeb says the cab sat on a 15-foot tower.[^1][^2][^4] A 1950 Coast and Geodetic Survey description says the station was a 14-by-14-foot lookout house of the usual Forest Service design.[^3]

The lookout burned in 1957. The register, Firelookout.com, the Forest Lookouts page and WillhiteWeb all call the fire accidental, and none describes it further.[^1][^2][^3][^4] It was never replaced.[^1][^4]

WillhiteWeb's undated photo captions describe footings, an anchor and a good deal of rock work at the summit.[^4] We found no source that describes a standing structure there. At its end the Forest Service trail climbs a steep talus slope, and a side route to the summit is unmarked.[^5] The trail has gone without official upkeep for many years, and the Forest Service advises against stock use.[^5] Most trees along the trail's northern 1.5 miles died in the 2017 Diamond Creek Fire.[^5]
