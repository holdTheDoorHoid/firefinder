The Forest Service built Seven Point Lookout in 1930 on Seven Point Peak in Sanders County, Montana.[^1][^2][^3] That September a local paper reported that four ready-cut lookout houses had been installed, one of them on Seven Point Peak in the Trout Creek district.[^3] The register calls it the last gable-roofed L-4 cab still on a tower in the Northern Rockies Region. It says the lookout has never been moved, though it has stood on three national forests.[^1]

The records we found are thin. A 1959 item from the Sanders County Ledger says a 15-foot snowdrift on the trail meant the post could not yet be manned.[^3] Jay Jopling is the one named lookout we found, listed for 1967.[^2] In 1971 heavy snow and rain had brought the cabin's roof down.[^3] Workers from the Trout Creek district reopened the lookout that spring, and a chartered government helicopter ferried crews and tools each day.[^3]

The last staffed season was 1973, after which the lookout was abandoned.[^2][^3] The register dates the abandonment to 1973, the year much of the Trout Creek Ranger District was transferred to the Cabinet Ranger District.[^1] The register lists the lookout as registered on August 20, 1996, and says a Passport in Time project restored it that year.[^1]

FireLookout.com and the Forest Lookouts page both say the tower burned in 2006.[^2][^3] We found no source describing what remains on the peak today, or whether the site is open to visitors.
