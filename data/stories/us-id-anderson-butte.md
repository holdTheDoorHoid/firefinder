Fire lookout use on Anderson Butte, about six miles northeast of Elk City in the Nez Perce National Forest, dates to 1914.[^1][^4] Its earliest setup was a tent camp.[^1] In the early 1930s an L-4 lookout went up on a short tower, and a photo caption in the climbing guide dates a log cabin with a cupola to 1932.[^1][^4]

The tower standing now, an L-4 cab on a 53-foot timber tower, was built in 1953.[^1] Idaho Fire Lookouts gives the same year and describes a treated tower with an L-4 cab.[^3] The same site lists Jim Reid as known staff in 1964 and a Mr. and Mrs. Kirkpatrick in 1978.[^3]

The tower moved to emergency status in the 1980s.[^3] The register says it is staffed on an emergency basis, firelookout.com says it has been used that way since the early 1980s, and the climbing guide says the current lookout is used on an emergency basis.[^1][^2][^4]

The register lists August 25, 1997 as the date it was registered, and calls the lookout the final one of its type on the Nez Perce National Forest.[^1] Firelookout.com says no other example of its type remains on the forest.[^2] The sources give the elevation as 6,827 feet on the register and 6,847 feet on the other two sites.[^1][^2][^3]

Access is on foot.[^3] The climbing guide names Elk City as the main starting point, with the Anderson Butte National Recreation Trail and other trails climbing to the summit, and advises checking conditions with the Nez Perce National Forest–Elk City Ranger Station.[^4] Idaho Fire Lookouts tags the tower as hike-in and on emergency status.[^3]
