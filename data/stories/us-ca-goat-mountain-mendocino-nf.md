The Goat Mountain lookout site is in Lake County on the Mendocino National Forest, at 6,129 feet.[^1] The cabin that once sat on its steel tower was built in 1934 to a California Region 5 Plan BC-301 design, on a 30-foot K-brace tower.[^1] It took the place of an earlier Plan 4A cabin that sat on a shorter tower built before 1920.[^1]

The earliest staffing record we found is from 1919, when a newspaper named Ernest Phelps as the fire lookout.[^2] That August a motorist became the first to drive the trail to the top, and he talked with the lookout from his car.[^2] In March 1920, Phelps reported that a Thanksgiving windstorm had torn the shutters off the lookout house and carried away the steps up the tower.[^2] The same report noted that the lookout had been given the number 13 so airplane patrol observers could identify it.[^2]

In June 1932, Arlie C. Killebrew was picked as the new lookout after James Lucas resigned.[^2] In June 1943, Mrs. Virginia L. Shriver, 86, of Lakeport, went up to the lookout with her grandson George, who was to take the post for the fire season.[^2]

By 2005 the tower was derelict, and parts of the cabin still sat on top.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the removal status as standing ruin, and it added the site on February 1, 2020.[^1]

The most recent description we found is the register entry, which calls the site a standing ruin.[^1] No source describes what remains there today, or whether visitors can reach it. Check with the Mendocino National Forest before planning a trip.
