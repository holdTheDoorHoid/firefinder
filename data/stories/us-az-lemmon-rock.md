Lemmon Rock Lookout sits just south of the summit of Mount Lemmon in Pima County, Arizona, at 8,720 feet.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the first lookout on the mountain within the Coronado National Forest, which overlooked Tucson, went up in 1913, and that a replacement was built in 1928 on Lemmon Rock for a better view.[^1] The National Register record is titled Lemmon Rock Lookout House, lists 1928 as its significant year, and gives the architect as unknown.[^2]

The Forest Lookouts site describes a May 1970 radio exchange from the lookout, in which Larry Davis, just starting his first season there, asked about bears on the rock. The story is credited to the Tucson Daily Citizen.[^3] The Forest Lookouts site also says the lookout was added to the National Register in 1988, and the register record places it in the multiple listing for national forest fire lookouts in the Southwestern Region.[^2][^3]

On June 12, 2015, Coronado Forest officials announced that the lookout was closed to visitors. They said visitors nearby were distracting staff, whose priority was watching for fires. The trail remains open, but the lookout structure is off limits.[^3]

The most recent notice in the sources read is the 2015 closure. None of them report a reopening.[^3]
