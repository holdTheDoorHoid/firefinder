Mitchell Peak rises to 10,369 feet in Kings Canyon National Park, where a fire lookout was constructed in 1962.[^1] The National Park Service administered it.[^1] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the lookout appears to be an R-6 flat-top plan on a 10-foot steel open tower, but it does not know the site's earlier history.[^1]

The register gives no removal date.[^1] A September 2006 Los Angeles Times column says the tower was pulled down in the 1970s, around the time airplanes and helicopters began replacing lonely fire lookouts.[^2]

The only thing left of the tower is a concrete slab, set among boulders on the summit.[^2] The register also records a concrete slab dated 1962 at the site.[^1]

The column describes a stiff 3.2-mile hike to the top, with a wide view of the central Sierra Nevada.[^2] It is the most recent account we found, so the route may not match conditions today.
