The Beebe Hill tower has stood on three sites, and it spent its first decades on other mountains.[^1] The Taconic State Park Commission put up a 60-foot Aermotor LS-40 on Alander Mountain in 1928, just inside Massachusetts, to watch for fires on park land.[^1][^2] In 1930 the Alander land burned, and its owner asked that the tower be taken away. Park staff dismantled it and stored it, then re-erected it on Washburn Mountain, inside park boundaries, in 1933.[^1][^2]

Forest fire control took over operation around 1935 and ran the tower on Washburn until 1963.[^1] In November 1963, Forest Rangers took it down and flew it by helicopter to Beebe Hill, where it was re-erected.[^1] Wikipedia dates that final move to 1964, so the two sources disagree on the year.[^2]

The Beebe Hill lookout ran until it closed in 1987.[^1][^2] It was entered on the National Historic Lookout Register on December 15, 1997.[^1]

A visit report from December 2016 found the tower in good condition, but the watchman's cabin had been vandalized. After the damage was reported to DEC, the cabin was to be closed up again.[^1] The NHLR reports a recent restoration as of 2025, in which wooden stairs were replaced with metal.[^1] Wikipedia reported in late 2024 that the state forest's website had deemed the tower unsafe.[^2] By contrast, the state's current forest page presents the tower as open to visitors.[^2][^3]

Together the state forests feature about 30 miles of multiple-use trails, reached from several parking areas, and hikers can use a public lean-to near the summit, beside the main trail.[^3] Because the sources disagree on the tower's current condition, check with NYSDEC before you plan a climb.
