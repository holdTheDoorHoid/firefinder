The Ashby Fire Tower stands in Volusia County, Florida, in the wet cypress and pine country around Lake Ashby.[^2] The sources reviewed for this story do not say when it was built, how tall it is, or who designed it, and no construction record turned up.

The earliest source found that mentions the tower is a map. A 1966 topographic survey, built from 1957 photogrammetry and published in 1968, lists the Ashby Lookout Tower among the area's significant landmarks.[^2] Nothing reviewed says whether the tower was staffed or whether it ever saw a fire call.

The National Historic Lookout Register listed the tower on July 24, 2016, as US 1070, FL 17, with Robert Spear as nominator.[^1] Its entry says the tower "received extensive repairs to its footers in the mid-2010s," and names the Florida Department of Agriculture & Consumer Services as the agency that administers it.[^1] Those repairs point to a tower still standing at registration, though no later report on its condition was found.[^1]

The register entry does not describe public access or climbing rules.[^1] Anyone planning a visit should check with the state agency before assuming the tower is open, and should not climb it without that confirmation.

[^2]: 1966 Map of Lake Ashby, Alamana, FL (USGS topo, published 1968), Pastmaps, https://pastmaps.com/map/lake-ashby-volusia-county-fl-usgs-topo-1966-p1968 (accessed 2026-10-08).
