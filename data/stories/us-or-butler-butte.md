Butler Butte is a 5,513-foot summit on the Rogue-Umpqua Divide, in the Umpqua National Forest, Douglas County.[^1][^3]

The register says a crow's nest and cab went up in 1920.[^1] In 1932 a 20-foot round fir pole tower with an L-4 cab replaced that setup.[^1][^3]

A Glendale Log item from November 1929 says a lookout fireman on Butler Butte was held in service for some weeks after the fire season closed, to survey the extent of a summit ridge fire.[^3]

The lookout was staffed for 85 days in 1941 and reported by forest line to the Tiller Ranger Station.[^3]

Sources differ on when the Aircraft Warning Service post opened. The Forest Service says early 1942, while Forest Lookouts gives August 24, 1942.[^4][^3] The Forest Service says the post watched for Japanese planes and incendiary balloons.[^4] A cabin was built for the observers in 1942, and the post ran until October 16, 1943.[^3][^4]

The tower and cab were removed in 1958, but the AWS cabin and outbuildings were kept.[^3] Firelookout.com says the tower was destroyed that year.[^2]

The AWS cabin is now the Butler Butte Cabin, a Forest Service rental at $40 per night per group, for up to eight occupants, with reservations through Recreation.gov.[^4] The Forest Service page, which carries no date in our copy, says the cabin is closed due to structural issues and will be closed for maintenance until further notice.[^4] Firelookout.com says the cabin is still available for rental, which conflicts with that closure notice.[^2] No source we read describes the tower site today. Winter roads to the cabin go unplowed, so the Forest Service recommends a high-clearance or four-wheel-drive vehicle.[^4]
