Apple Springs sits fifteen miles northeast of Groveton, in the northeastern part of Trinity County, Texas.[^1] Apple Springs dates from the period after the Civil War, when it went by May Apple Springs.[^1] In 1884, when a post office opened, the "May" was dropped from the name.[^1] The town relocated to State Highway 94 in 1931, when the railroad was abandoned.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas list includes an Apple Springs tower at coordinates 31.18267, -95.04218, and marks it gone.[^2]

Fifty men reached Trinity on May 26, 1933, and formed the Civilian Conservation Corps camp there.[^3] According to a historical marker, the camp's members helped build and maintain firebreaks, lanes and lookout towers across Trinity, Houston and Walker counties.[^3] The marker does not mention the Apple Springs tower.[^3]

No source found describes what, if anything, remains at the Apple Springs tower site today, or whether the public can reach it.
