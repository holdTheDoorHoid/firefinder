A lookout tower went up on Haeckel Hill, north of Whitehorse in the Yukon, in 1954, according to a 1956 report in the Whitehorse Daily Star.[^2] The ExploreNorth guide has found no photo or description of that first tower.[^1] The current structure was built in 1961.[^1][^2]

In 1956 the tower stood 1,524 m (5,000 ft) above sea level, about 23 km (14 mi) from town by road.[^2] The last stretch, about 10 km (6 mi), was rough bulldozed road that only a four-wheel-drive power wagon or a good Jeep could handle.[^2] Provisions came up from town every two weeks.[^2]

An August 1961 report describes Territorial Construction crews putting up a tower and accommodation for forestry staff.[^2] The tower's first listed towerman, Hugh Birch, served from 1954 to June 9, 1956.[^1] Harry Burnett took over the post on June 16 that year.[^2] The forestry division ran the lookout at that time.[^2]

In the 2014 season the tower was one of six Yukon towers staffed regardless of fire risk, on a five-days-on, two-days-off basis.[^1]

In September 1991 vandals hit the tower once more.[^2] The damage was extensive, reaching the tower and the ham radio club's repeater, and the tower's electrical gear and electronics were all destroyed.[^2] Keith Kepke, head of fire management for federal Northern Affairs, said officials would need to weigh security measures, which could mean closing the road or the tower, because repairs kept costing money.[^2]

As of 2026, Wildland Fire still uses the tower for a local VHF repeater, plus a UHF link to a repeater near Teslin.[^1] Today the hill is mainly known for its four wind turbines, and the current ones went up in 2023.[^1]

According to a 1991 clipping, a 4x4 or, in good weather, a two-wheel-drive truck could get to the tower.[^2] The newest source, a 2026 guide, does not describe public access.[^1]
