A fire lookout cabin went up on the slopes of Castle Mountain, in Banff National Park, Alberta, around 1942.[^1][^2] On October 31, 1941, the Crag & Canyon of Banff reported that another lookout site had been chosen, the top of Castle Mountain.[^1] The sources differ on where the cabin ended up: a hiking guide places it less than halfway up the mountain, not at the top.[^2]

A hiking guide says the park's lookouts were built quickly and manned for several decades, and that by 1978 all of them had been torn down or abandoned.[^2] The Kemnow page says the mountain itself was renamed in 1946 for General Dwight D. Eisenhower, though hiking guides still call it Castle Mountain.[^1][^2][^3]

The same guide reports that just the foundation survives today.[^2]

The site can be reached on foot. The trail runs 3.7 km one way from the Bow Valley Parkway, with a steady climb through dense forest.[^3] The cabin overlooked a large section of the Bow Valley, and the trail guide calls the views from the top outstanding.[^2][^3] Bears are active throughout the park, and the trail guide advises carrying bear spray.[^3]
