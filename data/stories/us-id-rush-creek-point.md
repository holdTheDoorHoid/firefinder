Rush Creek Point overlooks the Rush, Cabin and Big Creek drainages in the Payette National Forest.[^4] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the cabin in 1937, according to the register, Idaho Fire Lookouts and Intermountain Histories.[^1][^2][^4] The register describes an R-4 design, plan 80, with a 15-by-15-foot cab and a pyramid roof, and calls it similar to an L-4.[^1][^2]

Two other pages disagree. The firelookout.com page and a Forest Lookouts page both give a 1935 L-4 cab.[^3][^5] I could not settle that from these sources, so the story follows the register's date and design.

Intermountain Histories ties the lookouts to the Forest Service's 10:00 AM policy, which aimed to have fires out by ten the next morning. The Forest Service kept that policy until 1977.[^4]

A 1939 newspaper item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, reported a fire that endangered the Rush Creek lookout station, though the station was saved.[^5]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists a known staffer for 1953 and two for 1975.[^2] The register says the cabin was rehabilitated once and has been used only by wilderness patrols since 1986.[^1] The 1980 Central Idaho Wilderness Act placed this part of the Payette forest inside the Frank Church-River of No Return Wilderness. That limited the fire suppression the Forest Service could legally carry out, and Intermountain Histories says it made the lookout less necessary.[^4]

The register added the lookout on March 1, 2000.[^1][^2] Intermountain Histories calls it abandoned and says it has long been inactive.[^4] The register describes it as in fairly good shape but in need of maintenance, and its photo is from 2009.[^1] I found no later visit report, so today's condition is unknown. The only way in is by trail, so plan on hiking in, and check with the Krassel Ranger District before you go.[^1]
