The Ventana Double Cone lookout stood in the Santa Lucia range, behind the Big Sur region, in Monterey County.[^2] In May 1936 a newspaper reported that the new station would be the fifth radio- or telephone-equipped lookout protecting the Monterey division of the Santa Barbara national forest.[^2] Civilian Conservation Corps crews built a 14-by-14 ground cabin atop a rock base over 1935 and 1936.[^2] The Firetower record agrees on the build years and places the cabin under California Region 5 plan BC-301.[^1]

In 1942 mules packed in materials for an Aircraft Warning Service cabin, according to the Forest Lookouts page.[^2] The Firetower record dates that cabin to 1943, when a pair of operators staffed the lookout around the clock.[^1] It says the last staffing came in the 1960s.[^1]

The lookout burned in 1976 or 1977.[^1][^2] The Firetower record says it burned "under mysterious circumstances," while the Forest Lookouts page says vandals burned it. The two accounts agree on the years but not the cause.[^1][^2]

No source I could read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit today. None says whether visitors may go there.[^1][^2]
