Bald Peter is a 6,574-foot peak on the Warm Springs Indian Reservation in Jefferson County, Oregon, about five miles east of Mount Jefferson.[^1][^2] Foresters had long used it as an observation point.[^3] In an April 1925 letter, the writer weighed it against Mutton Mountain and called Bald Peter the better observation point, though not an ideal one.[^3] On May 13, 1925, a newspaper reported that a lookout tower was to be erected there that season, the first in the reservation's forests.[^3]

The sources give different dates for when the first tower went up.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com dates a 40-foot pole live-in tower to 1933.[^2] The register describes the same kind of first tower without a date.[^1] A 1935 forestry report says two lookouts were finished that fall, one of them a 45-foot wooden tower on Bald Peter.[^3]

The lookout was in use in 1934.[^3] A July 30, 1934 newspaper item says the Bald Peter lookout reported that the east line was apparently not holding during a large fire near Fly Creek.[^3]

In 1938 the pole tower was replaced by a 6-by-6-foot ground cab and a tent cabin.[^1][^2] In 1943 a forest fire ended the lookout.[^1][^2] The register says it was removed in that fire, and firelookout.com says it was destroyed.[^1][^2] Oregon lookout lists now mark the tower as gone.[^3][^4]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today. The register names the Warm Springs Indian Reservation as the body that administers the site, and none of the sources says whether visitors may go there.[^1]
