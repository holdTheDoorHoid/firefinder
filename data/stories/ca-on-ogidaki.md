Ogidaki Mountain is a high hill in the Algoma Highlands, north of Sault Ste. Marie.[^2] Its fire tower came from somewhere else.[^1] A February 2005 Ministry of Natural Resources management statement says a steel tower of 24.4 m (80 ft) first stood north of the reserve, close to Mekatlna Lake in Desbiens Township.[^1] In 1968 it was moved up to the summit and raised to 30.5 m (100 ft).[^1] The same statement says ministry data rank it as the newest fire tower the province built.[^1] The statement also mentions a staff cabin at the mountain's base.[^1]

The statement says the cabin and some of the ladder had been taken down by the late 1970s.[^1] It also says the tower stayed in use for radio relays run by the ministry and by Quattra Communications.[^1]

A hiker who reached the summit on 22 July 2008 found the tower standing, with a ministry shack close by.[^2] Its bottom ladder was missing, which kept people from climbing.[^2] The 2005 statement and the 2008 account do not clearly match on the cabin and the shack, so it is unclear whether they describe the same building.[^1][^2]

A later edit to the 2008 account claims the ministry took the fire tower down in May 2016.[^2] No ministry record in these sources backs that edit. A still-standing list on Ontario's Fire Tower Lookouts site includes a Mount Ogidaki Tower, and says the ministry decided in 2013 to remove many standing towers.[^3] The same list says the ministry no longer looks after these towers, and asks visitors not to climb them.[^3]

No source describes the summit today. The 2005 statement said the tower might appeal to curious sightseers, and the 2008 hiker reached the summit over logging roads that were active but also overgrown.[^1][^2] Whether the tower still stands, and whether visitors may go up it, is not settled by these sources.
