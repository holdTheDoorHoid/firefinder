According to a blog post, forest fire lookouts were scarce in national parks until the late 1930s, when the Dominion Forest service adopted standard-plan towers for the western parks.[^2] The blog counts around ten towers built in Riding Mountain National Park in 1938 and 1939, though it says the exact number is hard to pin down because some were built outside the park boundary.[^2] The usual design, it says, was an octagonal cabin 2.4 metres (8 feet) across on a steel tower 12.2 or 15.2 metres (40 or 50 feet) tall.[^2] That is the general pattern, not a description of the Makinak tower.

A 1972 item in The Brandon Sun, quoted on the Canada Lookouts page for Makinak, describes the tower as the place where a fire warden for Riding Mountain National Park worked.[^1]

The same blog, written after an early August 2018 visit, says only five towers remained in the park: Baldy, Clear Lake, Sugarloaf, Heron Creek and Rolling River.[^2] Makinak is not on that list.[^2] The blog also says lookouts were mostly phased out in the 1970s, and that many were taken down.[^2]

The Parks Canada Agency administers Riding Mountain National Park.[^3] No source we found describes what, if anything, remains of the Makinak tower or whether the site is open to the public.
