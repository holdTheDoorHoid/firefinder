The Roberts lookout started as a crow's nest about 1920. In 1924 it was built as a 40-foot steel tower with a 6-by-6-foot wooden cab.[^1] The site did not get permanent cabin quarters until the 1940s.[^1] The firelookout.com entry names the Kootenai National Forest as the agency.[^1]

Firelookout.com reports that the tower was moved to a city park in Eureka in 1979.[^1] The Northwest Montana Lookout Association's 2024 newsletter describes a special project that highlighted the lookout's history and helped complete an interpretive sign about it.[^2] The same newsletter places the tower at the Tobacco Valley Historical Village in Eureka.[^2]

Volunteers, fundraisers and donations support the village's ten structures and their contents.[^3] Its list of buildings includes a fire tower.[^3]

We did not find whether visitors can climb the tower today, what hours apply to it, or what condition it is in. Ask the village before you go.
