The tower now called the O'Bannon Woods Fire Tower stands in O'Bannon Woods State Park in Harrison County, Indiana, on land that was once part of the Harrison-Crawford State Forest.[^1][^3] A hobbyist's survey says the first tower on the site, intended as temporary, was a 55-foot wooden pole tower.[^3] The register, by contrast, dates the first fire tower on the site to 1937.[^1] A 1928 state annual report, as quoted by one lookout site, describes a short-term wooden tower near Wyandotte Cave in Harrison County, but none of the sources read ties that tower to this exact spot.[^2]

In February 1932 the state acquired 2,600 acres of woodland in Harrison County for its reforestation program.[^2] A newspaper report from that month said a temporary wooden tower, 60 feet high, would soon go up and later be replaced with steel.[^2]

Sources agree the tower was rebuilt in 1941 after fire damage, but they disagree about the sequence before that.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the first fire tower on the site in 1937, that fire damaged it, and that it was replaced in 1941 by an 88-foot steel tower with a 7-by-7-foot metal cab.[^1] A sign at the tower reads "Erected: 1932; Burned; Rebuilt 1941," and the hobbyist survey places a 100-foot CCC steel tower in place of the wooden one by 1937.[^3]

None of the sources read names who staffed the lookout or when anyone watched from it, so that part of its history is unrecorded here.

When the land was transferred to O'Bannon Woods State Park, the tower took the park's name.[^1] The register lists it as US 686, IN 4, and records its registration on January 10, 2007.[^1]

A hobbyist's page records the tower as standing and says the cab can be climbed into, calling the view great.[^3] No source read says whether the cab is open to visitors, so check with the state park before planning a visit, and stay out of the cab if it is closed.
