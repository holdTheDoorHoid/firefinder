The James City Fire Tower, also known as the Toano tower and the Barhamsville lookout, was built in 1933.[^1][^2] A newspaper report of October 17, 1933, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page for the site, said a look-out had been erected on H. P. Hazelwood's land by the Chesapeake Pulp and Paper Company for observing forest fires. The same report says the company held extensive timber holdings in James City and New Kent counties.[^2]

The Register calls it a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40 tower.[^1] A 1942 federal survey description calls it a steel tower on four legs, about 100 feet high, with a glass-enclosed cab, and places it about two miles southeast of Barhamsville.[^2] The 1933 report gave a height of 120 feet, so the sources disagree on height.[^2] The two later descriptions agree on about 100 feet.[^1][^2]

The 1942 survey names the Chesapeake Corporation as the owner and says the tower was run in cooperation with the state Forest Service.[^2] The Register now lists a private owner, David Nice.[^1] None of the sources read explain when or how the ownership or the company name changed. The Register added the tower to its list on December 25, 2022.[^1]

No source read names anyone who staffed the tower or any fire it watched.

The Register's description says most of the boards are missing. The cab still seems to keep most of its glass panels. The Register also describes a stand at the spot meant for the Osborne fire finder, but the finder itself is no longer there.[^1] The sources do not say whether visitors can go up. The tower stands on privately owned land, so anyone interested should ask the owner first and should not climb it.

[^2]: Toano, Forest Lookouts, https://easternuslookouts.weebly.com/toano.html (accessed 2026-10-08).
