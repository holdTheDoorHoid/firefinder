The Rehoboth Fire Tower stands on Long Hill in Bristol County, Massachusetts.[^1] The register gives its elevation as 200 feet, or 61 meters, and says the Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control administers it.[^1]

The register dates the present tower, a 68-foot steel tower with a 10-by-10-foot wooden cab, to 1931.[^1] The former-site register, whose entry is for Great Meadow Hill, says a new tower went up there that year to replace the older one.[^2]

Sources disagree about where the older tower came from.[^1][^2] The register says a 28-foot steel tower stood on Richmond Hill in 1912, and that in 1916 it was moved to Great Meadow Hill, where a 12-foot extension and a spiral staircase were added.[^1] The former-site register says a 50-foot steel tower, first operated in 1916, was moved to Great Meadow Hill from Dighton.[^2] We could not settle which account is right.

In 1946 the tower was fitted with a new cab and two-way radios.[^2] The sources we read do not name any observers or notable fires at this site.

In 1955 the U.S. Army asked for the tower to be moved.[^1] The former-site register says the Army ordered the tower removed on national security grounds, and that it came down in June 1955 and was moved three miles south to Long Hill.[^2] A state forest history records that in 1955 a U.S. Government Nike Battery PR-19 fire-control area was built on top of Great Meadow Hill. It says this forced a fire tower to be relocated to Reservoir Avenue, Rehoboth.[^3] That conflicts with the Long Hill account, and we could not resolve it.

The register gives the Long Hill site and coordinates but no recent visit or condition report, so we cannot say how the tower looks today or whether visitors can reach it.[^1] Ask the Massachusetts Bureau of Forest Fire Control before going to the site, and do not climb a tower that is closed or private.
