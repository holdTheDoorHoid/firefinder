Page Pond Hill is a mountain in the Southern Tier of New York, north-northwest of Sanford in Broome County.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built a steel lookout tower on it in 1935.[^1][^2] It is an International Derrick design.[^2] The state list gives its height as 79 feet 6 inches, but the 2008 chapter report gives 76 feet 9 inches for the same model, so the two figures disagree.[^2][^3]

The tower went into service in 1936 and reported 12 fires and 29 visitors that first year.[^1][^2] It stopped operating as a lookout at the end of the 1988 season.[^2] In early 1989 the Department of Environmental Conservation closed it, having judged that towers were no longer effective.[^2] The 2007 chapter report names two former Page Pond observers, Fred Murphy and Tom Vroman, among the people who took part in the October work weekend.[^4]

In 2007 the Forest Fire Lookout Association gave a $500 restoration grant for the tower and its observer's cabin.[^5] Cabin work began that fall, and members held a work party on October 13 and 14.[^3][^4][^5] Work continued in 2008, and the 2008 report planned to finish the project in 2009.[^3] The state list, however, says the chapter completed the restoration in 2007.[^2]

In June 1992 the tower was transferred to the landowner, which both the state list and Wikipedia name as the Girl Scouts Indian Hills Council.[^1][^2] The FFLA's 2007 grant report says instead that the Twin Rivers Girl Scout Council owns the tower and cabin, so the sources disagree about who owns the site.[^5]

Today the site is on private land that the public may not enter.[^1] The state list says a visit can be set up ahead of time.[^2] The FFLA's New York list still shows the tower as standing.[^6] We found no later report on the cabin's condition, so anyone interested should ask the landowner first.
