Fly Hill is a peak in Clearwater County, Idaho, in the Clearwater National Forest.[^1][^2][^4] Its lookout was an L-4 cab set on top of a log crib.[^1][^2][^3] Three sites date the cab to 1934, and the peak stands at 6,342 feet.[^1][^2][^3]

The sources do not say why the cab was built. A Clearwater National Forest study of its lookouts says building expanded fast in the 1930s, and it ties that growth to the CCC and other government work programs.[^5] The online copy of that study stops after its introduction and omits the lookout list, so it does not mention Fly Hill by name.[^5]

Staffing is thin. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists Mark E Lawrence as known staff for 1931, three years before the cab's 1934 date.[^3] The sources do not explain that gap. None of the sources describes a notable fire at the site. An October 1920 newspaper item appears under the Fly Hill heading on the Forest Lookouts Idaho page. It describes a "Fly camp" about seven miles south of Chamberlain Basin, but it does not name Fly Hill, so the sources do not tie it to this site.[^4]

The register says the cab was abandoned in the 1950s and no longer stands.[^1] Firelookout.com and Idaho Fire Lookouts give the same decade.[^2][^3] The study's introduction says that from the late 1950s into the early 1960s, only lookouts with a proven high-detection record were replaced, and only once they became unusable.[^5] That is a general pattern, and the study does not say what happened at Fly Hill. The register recorded the site on January 20, 2011.[^1]

No source describes remains at the site today. Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as drive-up, with no notes on access or visitors.[^3] With no cab standing, there is no lookout to climb, and the sources give no route or visitor information.
