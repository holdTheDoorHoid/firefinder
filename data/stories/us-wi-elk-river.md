The Elk River lookout tower stood in Price County, Wisconsin, near 45.75067 north and 90.11616 west.[^2][^3] A Price County Review article describes its design: a ladder ran straight up inside the support beams. Sentinel Point, another tower in the same article, had a staircase instead.[^1]

Leonard Stein, who had a background in forestry, spent many years as a fire lookout and kept a regular post in the Elk River tower.[^1] In the fall of 1942 he fell ill, and his 19-year-old daughter Betty took his place.[^1] She climbed the ladder to her first lookout post and, from the cab, phoned the Park Falls office and settled in to watch for smoke.[^1] The article reports that she was the first woman to work as a lookout for the Forest Service in its eastern region.[^1] Her lookout work was cut short when veterans came home from the war, and the jobs women had held during the war went back to men.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the Elk River tower as gone.[^2] The article describes the Fifield tower as the sole survivor of the three towers it covers, which implies the Elk River tower is no longer standing.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the public can visit it.
