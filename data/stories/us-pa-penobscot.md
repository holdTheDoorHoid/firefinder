Penobscot is a fire lookout on Penobscot Knob, a summit just east of Mountain Top in Luzerne County, Pennsylvania.[^1] In 1925 the Department of Forests and Waters put up a 60-foot steel tower there, with a glass-enclosed cab where a towerman was on duty day and night.[^1]

The summit was first developed in 1923 to take over from the Glen Summit station.[^1] It sits at about 2,100 feet, and a 1925 newspaper welcomed the public to visit the site.[^1] A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1929 says the summit was occupied by the Penobscot lookout tower.[^1]

In 1926 Margery Weyhenmeyer, of Nuangola, was named a fire warden responsible for the tower.[^1] A dirt road built by forest fire wardens in 1932 ran three-quarters of a mile, linking the concrete road with the tower.[^1]

In 1941 the Forest Intercepting Command at Mitchell Field made the tower one of its air defense lookouts.[^1] Observers were assigned to watch for plane movements over 32 square miles, and Harold Shafer, a state fire warden, was on duty there too.[^1] About a week before a report dated October 11, 1941, the tower burned.[^1] The article said it burned mysteriously, and the structure was replaced in haste during that week.[^1]

Later reports name John Hasselberger in charge in 1947 and Austin Hauser operating the tower in 1955.[^1] In July 1962, towers including Penobscot were staffed around the clock because of a dry spell.[^1] Normally, fire towers were staffed only in April, May, October and November.[^1]

Today the summit is perhaps best known for its many local TV and radio transmitters.[^2] Its earliest broadcaster was WIZZ FM, with WILK FM coming after.[^2] WNEP-TV's analog transmitter tower there collapsed in December 2007 amid severe ice, wind and snow.[^2] No source found describes what remains of the fire tower, or whether the site is open to the public.
