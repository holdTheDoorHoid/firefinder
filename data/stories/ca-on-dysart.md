The Dysart fire tower was built on a hill on the east side of the village of Haliburton, just off Ontario Highway 118.[^1][^4] Its registry entry at firelookout.ca places it at Haliburton, Ontario, but gives no year built or height.[^2] Ontario's former Department of Lands and Forests erected it in 1956 as an early detection post to protect local forests from fire.[^1] It was one of many towers in the area that belonged to the former Lindsay Forest Fire District.[^1]

Its frame is 30 m (100 ft) tall.[^1] The tower was retired in the late 1960s, when aerial detection came in.[^1]

According to Wikipedia and a fire tower history site, the frame still stands and the cupola is gone. Neither source dates that description.[^1][^4]

The Ontario list of still-standing towers marks the Dysart tower with an asterisk. Its key says such a tower has had its cupola removed and is now used as a communications tower.[^3] Wikipedia says nothing about the tower being used for radio, so that claim rests on the list alone.[^1][^3] The list also says the Ministry of Natural Resources no longer maintains these towers, has removed the bottom 6.1 m (20 ft) of ladder, and asks visitors not to climb.[^3]

The tower is clearly visible from Highway 118.[^4] No source says whether the hill is open to the public.
