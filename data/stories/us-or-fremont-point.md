Fremont Point sits on the rim above Summer Lake in Lake County, Oregon, at about 7,134 feet.[^1][^2][^4] The register names the Fremont National Forest, and the Forest Service now writes it as Fremont-Winema.[^1][^4] A camp was started there in 1930.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree on when the tower went up. Forest Lookouts quotes a 1931 entry describing a new 72-foot all-steel Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7 steel cab, finished that September for $1,575.40.[^3] The register, firelookout.com and Every Lookout in Oregon date the steel tower to 1936 instead.[^1][^2][^6] Most sources give its height as 72 feet. A 1942 ranger memo says 75, and a 1931 newspaper account of a visit says 80.[^1][^2][^3][^6]

A 16-by-18 garage and a 12-by-16 living-quarters cabin went up in 1933.[^3] The Forest Service, by contrast, calls the cabin lost in 2002 the 1931 original.[^4]

The station became a fire-weather station in 1940, with readings phoned to the Paisley Ranger Station daily at 4:30.[^3] Helen (Johnson) Farmer was the lookout in 1944. She was 17 that year, the youngest of the Fremont's lookouts.[^3] Frank Ott was on the lookout in 1960.[^3] The register says the tower was used in emergencies from the 1960s.[^1]

The Winter Rim fire swept across Fremont Point in 2002.[^6] It destroyed the ground cabin, which had been on the recreation rental program, and severely weakened the tower.[^1][^3] The steel tower was removed in 2003 because the fire had weakened its structure.[^3][^6]

The Forest Service rebuilt the ground cabin on the same footprint, and it reopened as a rental in 2015.[^4][^5] It sleeps three or four people and has a wood stove and a wrap-around porch.[^4][^5] It sits about a quarter mile past the day-use area, behind a privacy gate that opens with a reservation code.[^4] Reservations are made through Recreation.gov. In winter the roads are not maintained, so visitors may have to walk or use a snowmobile, depending on the weather.[^5][^6]
