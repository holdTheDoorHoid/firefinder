Hodag Lookout stands just outside Rhinelander, in Oneida County, Wisconsin.[^1][^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says its design is a standard that the Wisconsin Conservation Commission, now the DNR, drew up in the 1930s. The tower has an external, cage-protected ladder and stands 120 feet tall.[^1]

The register gives 1949 as the build year.[^1] A Rhinelander Daily News report from May 24, 1949, reprinted on the Central US Lookouts site, said the new tower, three miles east of the city, was planned for completion by the end of June.[^2] The same report said the cab would hold the controls for a radio network linking the ranger station with the conservation department's pick-up trucks, and the register says the tower served as a base station in the state's early radio network.[^1][^2]

The register says the tower takes its name from the hodag, a mythical creature said to have terrorized Northwoods logging camps. The creature has since become a namesake of the Rhinelander community.[^1] Until 2015 the lookout was manned only part of the time, during the spring fire season, before Wisconsin retired its lookout network.[^1] A WJFW report says the state's 72 towers still in use were decommissioned that year.[^3] The sources read do not name any particular fire spotted from Hodag.

The register lists a private owner, with the Wisconsin DNR shown as the former owner.[^1] The sources read do not say when or how it was sold.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4] A WJFW report describes its author and a friend, both former fire-tower staff, climbing it, with no date or permission given.[^3] The register lists it as privately owned, so do not climb it without the owner's permission.[^1]
