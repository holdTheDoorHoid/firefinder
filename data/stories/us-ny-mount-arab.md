Mount Arab's steel fire tower went up in 1918, replacing a wooden lookout that had opened in 1911.[^1][^4] New York began building a statewide network of permanent steel towers in 1916, to replace wooden ones that cost too much to keep up.[^1] This one is a prefabricated Aermotor design.[^3]

Sources disagree on its height. The National Historic Lookout Register and Adirondack Explorer both give 35 feet, while Wikipedia says 40 feet.[^1][^4][^3] Across from the tower stands the old observer's cabin, which Wikipedia dates to about 1948.[^4][^3]

The state's towers stayed in service until 1990, when air patrol replaced them.[^1] The sources I read name no particular fire this lookout watched, so I cannot tell you about any notable blaze here.

Mount Arab is the only one of seven St. Lawrence County towers still standing, and the National Historic Lookout Register recorded it in July 1995.[^1] It joined the National Register of Historic Places on September 23, 2001.[^3][^5]

The Friends of Mount Arab, a volunteer nonprofit, works in close partnership with the state Department of Environmental Conservation. Its work centers on the cabin, tower and trails.[^2][^1]

The register says the Friends have fully restored the tower and cabin, but it does not say when that work was done.[^1] A visit report dated December 2017 found the cabin in good condition, with the steps and landings also in good shape. It noted that the wooden window frames needed repair and that the steel could use paint.[^1]

The register describes the tower as open to the public and a popular hiking destination.[^1] The summit is a relatively easy two-mile round-trip hike, and one can climb the steel tower.[^4]

The observer's cabin is now a museum. It is open whenever a summit steward is on duty, from late spring to early fall.[^4] The Friends group brings in those stewards every summer.[^1]
