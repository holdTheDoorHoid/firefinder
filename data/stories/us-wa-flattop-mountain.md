The Flattop Mountain lookout stood in Washington's Gifford Pinchot National Forest, with a view of Mount Adams.[^1] The cab that stood on the summit was built in 1946.[^3][^5] The Forest Service had it built that year by a company in Portland, Oregon.[^5] It was a two-story prototype with slanted windows and a curved hip roof.[^1][^3] It was intended as the model for the next generation of lookouts in the region, but the plainer R-6 flat cab became the standard instead.[^1][^3]

The sources disagree on the cab's size. The register gives 12 by 12 feet, while firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb give 14 by 14 feet.[^1][^3][^4]

The summit had an earlier frame house, put up on the east end about 1921 or 1922 and moved to the west end in 1930. The two histories give different first years.[^3][^4] In 1938 Myron Halse was stationed at the lookout.[^4] The site was abandoned in 1960.[^3][^4]

A May 2005 visit report in the register says the building "has been completely neglected for several years."[^1] The register also says the lookout nearly came down to clear room for a microwave tower.[^1] The landowner, the state DNR, made repeated threats to tear it down until 2005.[^3][^4] In 2005 the Forest Fire Lookout Association dismantled the east-side cab and moved it to the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center in Entiat.[^3][^4]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives a later date. It says the tower was disassembled by 2025.[^2] The two histories above put the dismantling in 2005, so the sources disagree.[^3][^4]

The cab was moved to the Columbia Breaks Fire Interpretive Center, about a mile north of Entiat on the west side of US Highway 97.[^3][^4][^5] The center says its structure is a replica of the top story, built partly from the lookout's old wood, especially for the roof and window frame supports.[^5]

The summit itself no longer holds the lookout cab.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb's "Location Today" note describes communications towers on the east side and trees on the west.[^4] The summit is reached by forest roads.[^4] The same site's access notes say to use FR 8821, give a rocky road where good tires are best, and warn that the last mile is rocky.[^4]
