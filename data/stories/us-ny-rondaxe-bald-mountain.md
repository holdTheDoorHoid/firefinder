Rondaxe Mountain, also called Bald Mountain, rises in the Adirondacks above the Old Forge tourist area.[^1][^4] A wooden fire tower went up on the summit in 1912.[^2] The steel tower that stands there today replaced it in 1917.[^2][^1] The register describes that steel tower as 35 feet tall, with a 7-by-7-foot steel cab.[^1]

The name has its own history, and the sources do not agree on it. Wikipedia says the mountain was officially renamed Rondaxe in 1912, around the time a tower went up on a mountain in Croghan with the same name.[^4] A note on the register page describes a different Bald Mountain tower near Old Forge that was removed after its ownership changed, around 1975.[^1]

Rangers staffed the tower seasonally until 1990, and then it sat closed.[^2] A Road Trip Warriors post says more than 30 observers staffed it during 73 years of active service.[^5] Those observers reported smoke to forest rangers and logged planes crossing the Adirondacks during World War II.[^5]

The state closed the tower in 1990, and the register calls Rondaxe the last fire tower the state closed.[^1] Volunteers working with the state's Department of Environmental Conservation restored the tower between 2002 and 2005.[^2] It reopened on June 18, 2005.[^5] The register page also says the tower is on the National Register of Historic Places, though I could not find a National Register record that confirms it.[^1]

Today the Adirondack Explorer reports that the tower is restored and climbable.[^3] The trail gains roughly 500 feet over just under a mile, starting from a trailhead on Rondaxe Road, which leaves Route 28 at Fourth Lake.[^3] Wikipedia says it is the Adirondacks' most popular fire tower, drawing more than 15,000 visitors a year.[^4] A 2017 visit report suggests a volunteer steward on duty would help hikers, given the crowds.[^1] Volunteers also paint over graffiti often.[^1] In December the trail can be icy.[^5]
