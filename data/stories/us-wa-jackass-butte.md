Jackass Butte is a former fire lookout site in the Okanogan National Forest, in Okanogan County, Washington.[^1] The Forest Service administered it.[^1] Fire Lookout places it one mile southeast of Okanogan.[^2] A 1956 U.S. Geological Survey description puts it about one mile south of Okanogan and about four miles southwest of Omak.[^3]

The lookout cab came from another butte. The Register says an L-5 cab was moved here from Chiliwist Butte in 1939.[^1] The Chiliwist Butte record says a 30-foot treated-timber L-4 tower went up there that year, and the L-5 cab went to Jackass Butte.[^5]

Panorama photos were taken at the site on July 9, 1936.[^3] WillhiteWeb notes two cars, a phone line and plenty of wood scraps in the photos.[^4]

In 1942 the site went to wartime use. WillhiteWeb says the AWS used it to spot enemy aircraft.[^4] The Forest Lookouts page gives the service dates as March 16, 1942 to July 1, 1943.[^3] A photo caption on WillhiteWeb says Jackass was manned all year during that service.[^4]

In 1956 the U.S. Geological Survey placed a benchmark on the lookout house and called it abandoned. The survey described a 14-by-14-foot white building with its roof peak 13.6 feet above the ground.[^3][^4]

The sources disagree on what happened to the cab in 1963. The Register says it was removed that year.[^1] Fire Lookout says it was destroyed.[^2] WillhiteWeb says it was removed or destroyed.[^4]

The Register's photo list includes a foundation from the tower, credited to Bob Pfeifer.[^1] No source I read describes the site today beyond that caption. None says whether the land is open to visitors or whether anything else stands on the hill now.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
