The Stony Point tower stood in Dickson County, Tennessee, at about 817 feet, or 249 meters.[^1] Tennessee Landforms spells the name Stony Point, while the National Historic Lookout Register spells it Stoney Point.[^1][^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower in 1935-36 for the Tennessee Division of Forestry.[^2] The tower was an Aermotor design, 70 feet tall, with a metal cab measuring 7 by 7 feet. A 14-by-24-foot chestnut log cabin was also built to house the towerman.[^2]

Tennessee Landforms lists the Stony Point entry as moved.[^1] In 1997 Joel and Jason Clark bought the tower and trucked it about 12 miles to Towerview Farm, cabin included, without taking it apart.[^2]

The register now calls it the Towerview Fire Tower and places it on the farm in Houston County, Tennessee.[^2] The owners nominated it, and it was registered on March 1, 1998.[^2] The Clarks also picked up a forestry fire truck, which the register says remains in active use as part of the Towerview Farm Independent Volunteer Fire Service.[^2]

The register names the Clarks as the farm's owners and describes no public visits, so the tower should be treated as private.[^2] We found no source describing what, if anything, remains at the original Stony Point site today, or whether that site is open to visitors.[^1]

[^2]: Towerview Fire Tower, National Historic Lookout Register, http://nhlr.org/lookouts/us/tn/towerview-fire-tower/ (accessed 2026-10-21).
