The Manning Knob tower stood south of Richwood, in Greenbrier County, on Monongahela National Forest land.[^1] The U.S. Forest Service administers the site.[^1] The register puts the tower next to the grave of Nancy Hart, a Confederate spy, and gives its elevation as 3,968 feet (1,209 m).[^1]

Ivan Hunter, a Richwood resident who died in April 1965, spent years staffing the Manning Knob lookout.[^2] A Gazette-Mail feature says the tower overlooked Hart's grave, and a photo caption describes Hunter at the tower as a fire spotter.[^2] The feature adds that Hunter had his relatives pledge to bury him beside Hart on Manning Knob.[^2]

The tower has disappeared, but the feature notes that both graves survive.[^2] The register says the tower's footings are now swallowed by overgrown rhododendron.[^1]

A split-rail fence encloses Hart's grave, and Hunter's lies just beyond it.[^2] The Mon Forest Towns guide, which covers former fire tower sites in the Monongahela, says a visitor can find what is left of the tower at the gravesite, above Richwood.[^3] The register and the guide describe the tower's condition in different terms.[^1][^3]
