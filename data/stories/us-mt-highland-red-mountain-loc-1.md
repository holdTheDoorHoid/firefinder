A lookout once stood on Highland (Red) Mountain in Silver Bow County, about 15 miles south of Butte.[^2] The national register describes a ground-level L-4 groundhouse built there in 1933, and a lookout history page gives about the same early date.[^1][^2]

Montana Standard reports from 1939 tell a different start. They describe Civilian Conservation Corps crews completing a new Forest Service lookout station on Highland peak, with a trail and telephone line built to reach it.[^3] One of those reports puts the peak 20 miles south of Butte rather than 15.[^2][^3] The sources do not say whether the 1933 building was replaced in 1939 or whether the dates simply conflict.[^2][^3]

Don McPherson, 20, served as a lookout atop Red Mountain in the summer of 1953.[^3] During that season a lightning bolt hit the building and broke more than 70 panes of glass.[^3] The newspaper said it was the first strike on a lookout tower in that area in a long time.[^3]

A 1956 survey description of the Red Mountain tower calls it a small white cabin on pole legs.[^3] That does not fit a ground-level groundhouse, and the sources do not say which building the survey saw.[^3]

The original site is listed as gone.[^4] The 1954 L-4 cab that replaced the groundhouse sits on a concrete base about a quarter mile north of the Red Mountain summit.[^1][^2] The Montana list shows it as standing, and the register says the U.S. Forest Service administers it.[^4][^1] A lookout history page says it is kept for emergency use and does not describe public visits.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains on the original ground site today.[^4]
