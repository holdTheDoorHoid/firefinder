High above Bergen Park, west of Denver, a squat stone-and-timber cabin has watched the Front Range since 1940. It is the second lookout on this 11,000-foot summit: the first, a 16-by-20-foot cabin, was built in 1925 by the City and County of Denver to protect the mountain parklands spreading into the foothills.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps replaced it in 1940 with the structure still standing today, set higher on the peak to widen the visible area by about a quarter.[^1] The CCC crews built a 14-by-14-foot wood cab, ringed by windows and a catwalk, on top of a one-story stone base of native granite.[^1][^2]

The mountain and lookout carried the name Squaw Mountain for decades before being renamed Mestaa'ėhehe Mountain in 2021.[^1] The lookout earned a milestone of its own in 2003, when it became the 500th structure entered on the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1]

The tower has not been left to decay. On September 22, 2007, the Colorado/Utah Chapter of the Forest Fire Lookout Association joined forces with the Colorado Forestry Association and the U.S. Forest Service for a one-day repair project, giving the walls, overhang, guardrail and catwalk a fresh coat of stain and putting back the west-side shutters a storm had torn loose, which had cracked a window.[^1] Fifteen volunteers took part, including Evergreen Rotary Club members, a Denver Mountain Parks Fund representative, a retired Jefferson County teacher and his children, and FFLA and Forest Service staff.[^1]

Today the Forest Service rents the lookout out through Recreation.gov, accommodating up to four people a night with a stove, refrigerator, heat and beds, though guests pack in their own water and pack out their trash.[^2] The summit itself is also an active communication-tower site, and winters there are extreme — the two wall heaters and one space heater do not always keep up with the wind at 11,000 feet.[^2] From the catwalk, renters are promised views stretching to the Great Plains, Denver, Mount Blue Sky, Pikes Peak and Longs Peak.[^2]
