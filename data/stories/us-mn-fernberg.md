Fernberg was a fire lookout on a hill east of Ely, in Lake County.[^2][^1] The county's tower report says two forest guards, Ole Fernland and John Handberg, built a wooden lookout there in the late 1910s, and that the lookout was named Fernberg after them.[^1] The report's table gives 1911 for that first lookout, so its dates disagree with its own text.[^1]

In March 1923 the Star Tribune reported steel for an 80-foot tower being hauled to the hill, with Ranger Frank Carney, a Superior National Forest employee, overseeing the work.[^2] The Minneapolis Star reported the next day that the tower would go up there.[^2] The report calls the steel tower an 80-foot ladder tower, and says it was named for the same two guards.[^1]

The sources also disagree on distance. The 1923 newspapers put the hill 18 miles east of Ely, while the report places the first lookout several miles east of the town.[^2][^1]

Newspapers of that year spelled the name Fernburg. The report and the Forest Fire Lookout Association use Fernberg.[^2][^1][^3]

The county's survey notes from 1978 say the tower had been removed by then, and the association lists the site as gone.[^1][^3]

No source I found describes what, if anything, remains on the hill today, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
