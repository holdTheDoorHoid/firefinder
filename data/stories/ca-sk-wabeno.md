Prince Albert National Park once had six fire towers, and the Waskesiu Heritage Museum says each took its name from its site.[^1] Wabeno is one of them.[^1] The museum says the first five were built in 1938 and Boundary in 1940.[^1] Wabeno is third on the museum's list, within the first five, so if the list runs in build order, 1938 is the likely build year.[^1]

Each lookout site had a one-room cabin at its base and a pit privy, but no electricity or running water.[^1] Staff stayed at one tower for stints of up to two weeks.[^1] Lookouts kept in touch with fire dispatch by two-way radio and checked in with it every day.[^1]

The park has stopped using fire towers. According to the museum, the towers were retired in 2001, with helicopter smoke patrols taking over.[^1] A cupola now at the museum came off the Sanctuary Lake tower, itself one of the six.[^1]

A Saskatchewan tower list gives a Wabeno tower at 54-17.0000 X 106-14.0667. A separate Wabeno entry under its heading for national park towers gives no location, so linking the two rests on the shared name.[^2] A Canada Lookouts index also lists Wabeno.[^3]

No source we found describes what remains at the Wabeno site today, or whether it is open to visitors.
