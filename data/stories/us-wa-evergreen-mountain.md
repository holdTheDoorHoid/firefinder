# Evergreen Mountain Lookout

Evergreen Mountain sits on a ridge of the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest in Snohomish County, about ten miles northwest of Stevens Pass.[^1][^2] By 1934 the U.S. Forest Service had already run a telephone line to the site and cleared trees to open the view, with cab lumber piled on the summit waiting to go up; the 14-by-14-foot L-4 ground cab itself was built in 1935.[^3][^1] At the time it was one of only two intact lookouts left standing on what was then the old Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1]

During fire season a lookout watched the Skykomish drainage for lightning strikes and smoke, then radioed landmarks back to the Skykomish Ranger Station so crews could find a fire quickly.[^4] In the 1940s, Ellis and Faye Ogilvie staffed the tower together; the two had first met over the radio while posted at separate lookouts, married, and later worked Evergreen Mountain as a pair.[^3] During World War II the cab doubled as an Aircraft Warning Service post, watching the sky as well as the ridgelines.[^1][^3]

In 1967, loggers working the mountain's south side accidentally started what became known as the Evergreen Fire. It burned across the rocky south face of the ridge and stopped only a few hundred feet short of the lookout; the scar is still visible on that slope today.[^3][^5] Regular fire-season staffing continued after that and ran until the early 1980s, when the lookout was retired from active duty.[^2][^3]

Evergreen Mountain Lookout was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on July 14, 1987, as part of a multiple-property group of Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie fire lookouts.[^6] Recreation.gov's current listing for the site instead describes the lookout as being "placed on the National Register of Historic Places" during volunteer restoration work in the 1990s[^7] — the two accounts disagree by about a decade, and this story cannot resolve which is right. What is clear is that a road washout eventually cut off vehicle access, leaving only a long trail approach, and that the Seattle Area Explorer Scout Search and Rescue Group adopted the lookout in 1990, with the Seattle Mountaineers and Quest School pitching in; the 141st Army Reserve Aviation Battalion airlifted restoration materials to the site, and the work was finished by 2000.[^1][^3] Washington's National Historic Lookout Register separately records the lookout as registered on February 15, 1997, nominated by Ray Kresek — a different listing from the NRHP one above.[^1]

Sources also differ on the mountain's elevation: the National Historic Lookout Register gives 5,483 feet (1,671 m), while Recreation.gov's description of the site gives 5,587 feet.[^1][^7]

Evergreen Mountain Lookout still stands and is open to the public as an overnight rental through Recreation.gov, reached by a trail that climbs through the old 1967 burn scar before easing at a saddle below the cab.[^7] The cabin sleeps four on one bed and three extra mattresses, has a propane stove but no water or heat, and renters are asked to pack out their trash and clean the cabin before leaving.[^7] As of this writing the listing shows the access road washed out by winter flooding, with the Skykomish Ranger District hoping for repairs in time for the 2027 rental season, so anyone planning a visit should check Recreation.gov directly first.[^7]
