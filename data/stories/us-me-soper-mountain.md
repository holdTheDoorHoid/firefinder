The state built a log station on Soper Mountain in Piscataquis County in 1909.[^1] Maine Memory describes the lookout as a Maine Forest Service fire lookout.[^2] It was rebuilt of logs in 1916, and the lookout was improved with a log crib tower with a cab on top.[^1] A 1914 Forest Commissioner's report had recommended a steel tower of thirty-six feet for the site.[^1]

In June 1916 W.H. Edminster was named lookout watchman No. 19 on the mountain.[^1] A photo of about 1920 names the watchman as Tolman.[^2]

In 1924 a steel tower replaced the wood structure.[^2] Maine Memory gives its height as 24 feet.[^2] Other sources differ.[^1][^2] A 1924 newspaper item had planned a 27-foot tower, and a 1920 inventory had listed a 20-foot wood tower.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list of Maine towers marks the Soper Mountain tower as gone.[^3]

No source I read says what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether the mountain is open to visitors.
