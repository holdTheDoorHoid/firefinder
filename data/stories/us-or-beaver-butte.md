Beaver Butte stands at 5,066 feet on the Warm Springs Indian Reservation in Wasco County, Oregon.[^1] A tent camp went up on the site in 1928.[^1] On January 10, 1928, a letter from the forest ranger at Wapinita to the Warm Springs forest supervisor said a telephone line had been built to North Pinhead the year before, that the material was on hand for a line to Beaver Butte's summit that year, and that both points would be occupied only during periods of high fire hazard.[^4]

In 1931 the tent camp gave way to a 16-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab.[^1][^2]

On July 17, 1928, the emergency lookout on Beaver Butte flagged a fire in the same place as a May lightning fire on Beaver Creek that had been presumed out.[^3] The quoted report says the new fire appears to have been a holdover that lodged in a treetop, and that it stayed inside the original fire line, which enclosed about one-eighth of an acre.[^3]

In the summer of 1950, James Beima, an assistant coach at Madras Union High School, worked as the fire lookout, according to a Bend Bulletin item quoted on the page.[^3][^2]

The tower was gone by 1967.[^1] Firelookout.com gives its span as 1931 to 1967.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the site, registered on August 17, 2010, on the nomination of Ray Kresek.[^1] It says the site is administered by the Warm Springs Indian Reservation.[^1] The register does not describe what remains at the site today, and it says nothing about whether visitors may go there.[^1] Anyone thinking of a visit should ask the reservation first.
