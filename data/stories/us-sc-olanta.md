The Lee-Matthews fire tower first stood near Olanta, in Florence County, South Carolina.[^1] A Florence Morning News item from April 30, 1934, quoted by the lookout-history site, says the tower was then under construction, and that DeLeon Lee and Alex Matthews gave ten acres for it.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register's description also gives 1934 as the year it was built at Olanta, about six miles northwest of Lake City.[^2] Neither the register nor the garden mentions the Lee-Matthews name, so linking that tower to the one now at the garden rests on the shared 1934 date and Olanta site.

According to the garden, the tower was in service at Olanta for many years before it was taken out of use.[^3] According to the register, the state Forestry Commission sold the tower, and it was moved to the garden in 2004.[^2] The garden describes the purchase differently. It says the tower was bought at a silent auction in Olanta, where the winning bid looked like a bargain until the bill for moving the 100-foot steel tower arrived.[^3]

The tower now stands at Moore Farms Botanical Garden, at 100 New Zion Road in Lake City, where the garden calls it one of its most recognizable landmarks.[^3][^4] The structure rises more than 100 feet, with a bright red top, and can be seen from almost anywhere in the garden.[^3] The register added it on April 16, 2014.[^2]

The garden is not open to the public on a daily basis. Visitors can see the property through tours, events, or camps and classes, and the garden asks that tours be booked at least two weeks ahead.[^4] We found nothing saying visitors may climb the tower.

At the original Olanta site, OpenStreetMap has a point called Olanta Lookout Tower, with a note saying it does not show on recent imagery.[^5]
