Azure Mountain's first lookout was a wooden observation station built in 1914.[^1][^2] The state's present tower followed in 1918, a 35-foot Aermotor LS-40 with a metal cab.[^1][^2] The register says New York's Department of Environmental Conservation built it for $300, but the volunteer group that helped restore the tower gives the cost as $530.[^1][^2] The Wikipedia article on the site gives the height as 40 feet, against 35 feet in the other two accounts, so the sources disagree on both figures.[^1][^2][^4]

The lookout stayed staffed until 1978, when the DEC closed the tower and removed the lower two sets of stairs to block access.[^1][^2] The 1936 observers' cabin came down in 1995.[^1][^2]

In 2001 the tower joined six other fire towers on the National Register of Historic Places. Of the seven, it was the one still awaiting restoration.[^2] Azure Mountain Friends formed later that same year.[^2] The tower was restored in 2002 by state staff, AmeriCorps workers and volunteers, and it reopened at a September 27, 2003 observance.[^2] The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the tower on September 30, 2003.[^1] The tower marked its centennial in July 2018.[^2]

As of the register's 2018 visit, the Azure Mountain Friends group was keeping up the trail and tower and ran a volunteer steward program.[^1] Azure Mountain sits on Blue Mountain Road, and the summit is about a mile from the parking lot, with a climb of 944 feet. The group advises that hiking boots or shoes are highly recommended.[^3] The group's site describes the view from the tower cab, but no source confirms that visitors can climb to it now.[^3] At a March 11, 2018 visit, the register's inspector found the tower in good condition and recently painted. The glass was not intact, and the lookout had no staff.[^1]
