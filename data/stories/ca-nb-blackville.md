The Blackville Fire Tower is a steel watchtower, 31.7 m (104 ft) tall, standing in a private backyard in Blackville, New Brunswick.[^1][^2] The register describes its cabin as unusually oval-shaped.[^1]

Sources do not agree on when it was built.[^1][^2] The register's built field and a CBC News account both give 1929, and the CBC account says the tower was one of a network of watchtowers across Canada.[^1][^2] The register's own description says it was built in 1950 in Bettsburg.[^1]

Forest rangers used the towers until the early 1970s to watch for smoke.[^2] The register says this tower was last staffed in the early 1970s.[^1][^2] Patrol aircraft made the towers obsolete.[^2]

The tower's second life began in 1980.[^1][^2] Alvin Stewart put in a bid of $110 to buy an old watchtower slated to be torn down in Bettsburg, about 50 km southwest of his home. No one else bid.[^2] The register says he rescued it and moved it to his yard in 1980.[^1] CBC says the move happened in spring 1981, piece by piece, and that the rebuild was not finished until summer 1984.[^2]

The tower still stands in Stewart's backyard.[^2] The CBC account says it is still the tallest structure in Blackville.[^2] From the top there is an unobscured view of the Miramichi River and the whole village.[^2] Stewart has used the tower for a Christmas light display.[^2] He had not been up it for two years when the article was written, and he said he ought to go back up soon to replace the rotting plywood floor.[^2]

The tower stands on private land.[^1] The register lists the owner, Alvin Stewart, as its administrator.[^1]
