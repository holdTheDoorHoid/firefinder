The Simpson lookout stood in Fairfield County, South Carolina. The register lists the South Carolina Forestry Commission as its agency.[^1]

The register quotes the State Commission of Forestry's report for 7/1/39 to 6/30/40 as saying the Civilian Conservation Corps did the state's tower construction at no cost to the state, and that a new tower was built at the Simpson site.[^1] The Corps also completed the tower unit buildings there.[^1] The report, as the register quotes it, lists a four-room dwelling, barn, chicken house, smokehouse and latrine for each tower unit.[^1] Across the state, most tower sites covered about ten acres and included a year-round house for the operator.[^2]

The register quotes the report for 7/1/57 to 6/30/58 as saying the old wooden tower at Simpson was swapped for a new steel one.[^1] Over time, all of the state's wooden towers were replaced with steel.[^2] Starting in the 1960s, most of the state's tower operators were women, and their husbands often held firefighting jobs.[^2]

The register lists the Simpson tower as removed.[^1] South Carolina's forestry agency ended its fire tower program on October 1, 1993.[^2]

No source describes what remains at the Simpson site, or whether the public can visit it.
