Black Butte is a lookout site in Glenn County, California, in the Mendocino National Forest.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the site served as a lookout as early as 1912, when it communicated by heliograph, and it dates the structure to about 1914.[^1] The register calls the structure one of a kind, with a very narrow window set. It adds that the first structure may have been replaced in 1936.[^1]

According to the register, Winchester Riffe was lookout by 1914 and served through 1928.[^1] Newspaper items name a Riffe at the post too, under different names. A December 1919 California District News Letter item says "Chess Riffe" had spent five years as lookout on Black Butte, and a June 1927 Ukiah Dispatch-Democrat item reports that "Chas. Riffe" had begun his work as forest lookout there.[^2] The sources do not say whether these are the same man as Winchester Riffe.

A 1930 Ukiah Republican Press item describes a tall stone slab on a bald rocky knoll west of the station, bearing Winchester Riffe's name. A letter reply quoted in the item says he put up the slab when he took over the post, carved his name and the date he started the job there, and added a record on it each year while on duty.[^2]

The register gives no removal date.[^1] A Peakbagging table lists the Glenn County site as "(LO site)", a code it uses for sites whose building has been removed or whose status is unknown.[^3]

No source we read describes what, if anything, stands on the summit today. We also found no information on whether the site is open to visitors.
