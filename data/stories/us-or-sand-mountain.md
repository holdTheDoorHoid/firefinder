Sand Mountain is a double summit in Linn County on the Willamette National Forest.[^1][^2]

The mountain was used as a fire outpost in the early 1920s. The watch stood on a wooden pole with a firefinder on top, and neither the firefinder nor the staffer had any cover from the weather.[^4]

Sources differ on when the first cab went up. Firelookout.com gives 1932 for a gable-roofed L-4 cab.[^2] Forest Lookouts lists a 14 x 14 gable-roof lookout house around 1931.[^3] The Sand Mountain Society gives 1930 as the style date and says a Grange-hall-style L-4 was built in 1933.[^4]

In July 1926 a newspaper reported that an additional lookout would be posted on Sand Mountain if the fire hazard stayed high.[^3]

In August 1967 the Big Lake fire swept over the summit, and for hours the station was considered lost. It survived that fire.[^3] A Eugene Register-Guard report dated July 1, 1968 says it then burned to the ground on a Saturday. The Forest Service said the cause was unknown, but firelookout.com says the cab accidentally burned that year.[^3][^2] A pop-up cupola trailer served as a temporary lookout from 1968.[^2]

The site sat abandoned for many years.[^2] The relocation project was first conceived in 1988 to deal with off-road vehicle damage in the Sand Mountain Geologic Special Interest Area.[^4] In 1989 the Society moved the cab from Whisky Peak and rebuilt it on a 10-foot treated timber tower.[^2][^4] A dedication ceremony marked the new lookout in 1990, and the National Historic Lookout Register recorded it on August 18, 1990.[^4][^1]

Today the lookout doubles as a fire lookout in high fire season and as an interpretive center for the regrowth from the 1967 fire.[^1] It is staffed during the summer field season, from late June to mid October.[^4] The spur road is gated 1.25 miles from the lookout, so plan for a walk, and high-clearance vehicles are recommended on the approach road.[^4] The register lists the lookout as available for rental.[^1]
