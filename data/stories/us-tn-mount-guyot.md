Mount Guyot ranks second in elevation among the peaks of Great Smoky Mountains National Park.[^1] It rises to 6,621 feet (2,018 m) on the line between Tennessee and North Carolina, in Sevier County and Haywood County.[^1] The top is made up of two peaks roughly half a mile apart, and the one farther southwest, on the Tennessee side, is the true summit.[^1]

Tennessee Landforms lists a Mount Guyot tower in its table of lookout sites and marks it historical.[^2] Its entry for the park's Tennessee side names Guyot among the current or former lookout towers there. It marks Mount Cammerer as standing, but does not mark Guyot that way.[^2]

Today, a hiking guide says the peak has no road, no observation tower, no maintained summit trail, and no panoramic platform that draws crowds.[^3] No source we found describes the tower's remains, so we cannot say what, if anything, is left of it.

The Appalachian Trail crosses the south slope and comes to within 1,000 feet (300 m) of the summit.[^1] Reaching the high point means leaving the trail and pushing through dense spruce and fir forest.[^3] The guide suggests most visitors hike the Appalachian Trail across the slopes instead of trying for the top.[^3]
