The Burt lookout stood in Pacific County, Washington, about 15 miles east of Raymond.[^1][^2] Firetower.org lists DNR as the administrator.[^1] Its name most likely came from a logging camp in the Willapa Hills during the 1920s, which was called after logging engineer Burt Bunker.[^3] WillhiteWeb says the lookout probably just adopted the camp's name.[^3]

Sources date the lookout to 1937, when a 12-foot wooden live-in tower was built there.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb is more careful and says the tower was only said to have stood in 1937.[^3]

We found no source that names the people who staffed the lookout or for how long. WillhiteWeb suspects the Aircraft Warning Service used the site during World War II, though its wartime records place the station in an adjacent section.[^3]

Sources agree the lookout was abandoned in 1953.[^1][^2][^3] Firetower.org says it is no longer standing.[^1] A Weebly list of Washington lookouts marks the site as removed.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site. The site's author visited in 2019 and could not find any sign of the exact spot, though the highest point there had flat ground that suited a tower.[^3] USGS maps from the 1950s and 1960s show an unnamed summit at the location.[^3]

The land is part of Weyerhaeuser's McDonald Tree Farm, and WillhiteWeb lists a Weyerhaeuser permit as the way in.[^3] There is no tower left to climb.[^1] Anyone who wants to visit should get the landowner's permission first.
