Barfield was a Merced County fire lookout, set about four miles northeast of Ballico, where Lee Road meets East Avenue.[^1]

In February 1948 the fire committee got approval to study a lookout station at the Barfield schoolhouse.[^1] The current tower and station opened for the 1962 fire season, replacing the Dickinson tower, which sat across the Stanislaus-Merced county line at Keyes Road, and the old Barfield station, a small garage with a former schoolhouse used as living quarters.[^1] A 1975 newspaper report said no other county-owned station had a lookout tower.[^1]

A 1975 report describes a glass-walled room of roughly 10 square feet, about 50 feet above the rolling countryside.[^1] A 1983 note gives the tower as 65 feet tall.[^1]

In 1975 the crews at the station and tower were kept on alert and staffed "24 hours a day," according to Engineer Bill Parsley, who, with Engineer Eldron Thorton, kept the site part of the county fire network.[^1] In 1963 Fire Chief James Laginha's request for a radio base at the lookout was approved.[^1] In 1983 the county fire department supplied Castle Air Force Base with weather readings taken four times a day from the tower, which was also used to spot fires.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's California list marks the tower as gone, with no date.[^2]

No source we read describes the site today. We cannot say whether the station, the tower or any footings remain, or whether the grounds are open to visitors.
