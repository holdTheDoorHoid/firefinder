Work on a steel fire lookout on Peters Mountain, in McCreary County, began in December 1937. A newspaper report of the time called it the county's 17th steel observatory, and said twenty-two towers were needed to spot forest fires.[^1]

By April 1938 the tower was ready to be manned. The report called it the first 73-foot (22 m) observatory built since the old limit for such structures, 40 feet (12 m).[^1] The cab at the top, 12 by 12 feet (about 3.7 m), housed the lookout's equipment and his living quarters.[^1] The 1938 report places the tower in the Cumberland National Forest.[^1] A register entry for another McCreary County tower, the Stearns tower, says that forest has since been renamed the Daniel Boone National Forest.[^2]

A 1950 Coast and Geodetic Survey description gave the height as 90 feet (27 m) and called it a four-legged steel structure with an observation room on top.[^1] That disagrees with the 73 feet of 1938, and the sources do not explain the difference. The survey point sat at the center of the tower's base.[^1]

In 1970 six residents of Stearns, spelled Sterns in the report, were convicted of damaging the tower. Jail time and fines were both suspended, they were put on six months' probation, and they had to reimburse the Forest Service for the harm.[^1]

Today no source I found says whether the tower still stands or what remains on the summit. A federal survey listing for that 1950 point (PID GB2629) includes a code meaning its surface mark was reported destroyed, but the listing does not say what became of the tower.[^1][^3] No source describes whether the site is open to visitors.
