Old Rag Mountain rises to 3,284 feet (1,001 m) near Sperryville in Madison County, Virginia, and it sits inside Shenandoah National Park.[^1] Conscientious objectors working for the CPS program built the lookout in 1943.[^2] Their work was civilian service instead of military duty. Crews carried building materials on foot up a climb of more than a mile at a 45 percent grade.[^2]

The sources differ on what kind of structure stood there. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list records a "Ground" type.[^3] A 1957 photo caption on the Willhite page names it the Old Rag Fire Tower and places Ranger Dick Rayner beside it, using an Osborne Fire Finder.[^2]

The Willhite history dates the lookout's removal to 1959.[^2] A second Willhite page, covering the park's lookouts as a group, reports that regular staffing of its five towers ended by the late 1950s.[^4] Dismantling, scrapping and selling the towers had begun by the early 1960s.[^4] The two dates do not match exactly, so this story uses the 1959 date from the Old Rag page.[^2][^4]

According to Wikipedia, Old Rag ranks as the park's most popular hiking spot.[^1] A network of trails leads to the summit, and the most common approach starts from a lot in Nethers.[^1] One loop hike spans 9 miles (14.5 km).[^1] According to Willhite, hikers need a ticket for Old Rag during most of the year.[^2] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the mountain today.
