The St. Johns lookout was a firefinder mounted on the outside wall of a city building and viewed from a second-floor window.[^1][^2] The Portland Parks Bureau set it up in 1952 with help from Engine 32 of the Portland Fire Bureau, to watch the timber of Forest Park across the Willamette River.[^2][^4]

The push came from a fire.[^3] In August 1951 a blaze burned 2,400 acres, about a quarter of the park.[^3][^4]

The building itself was older, built in 1907 as the St. Johns City Hall.[^3][^4] Sources disagree on when it became a fire station.[^3][^4][^5] Two give 1915.[^3][^4] Portland's Station 22 history says Engine Company 32 was stationed there from 1913, until a new station was built in 1954.[^5]

A survey crew in 1959 found the cupola and flagpole already removed.[^3][^4] Firelookout.com says the lookout was abandoned but still there, and the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Oregon list uses the same word.[^1][^6] The building was renovated in 1964 and again in 1996.[^3][^4] A cupola was on the roof in 2021.[^4] One visitor could not date when it was added, and another thinks a renovation rebuilt it.[^3][^4]

The building was still standing in 2021.[^4] Two visitors that year found it in use by city staff, but they name different occupants: one says the Park Rangers, the other the Portland Police.[^3][^4] The first visitor says you cannot get into the building without an appointment.[^3] Neither says whether the firefinder is still mounted on the wall.[^3][^4]
