The Chuck Swan Fire Lookout Tower stands in Chuck Swan State Forest in Union County, on one of the tallest hills in the region.[^1] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it for the Tennessee Valley Authority around 1935.[^1] The sources disagree on the year. A National Register study of the state's towers and Wikidata both give 1934.[^2][^3]

The tower was first called the Central Peninsula Fire Lookout Tower.[^1] In 1952 the TVA sold the tower and the surrounding forest land to the state, which renamed the site for Conservation Commissioner Charles "Chuck" Swan, who had overseen the purchase.[^1] It is a 100-foot steel tower of the Aermotor LS-40 design.[^1][^2]

Around 1971 the state stopped using its fire towers continuously, as newer ways of identifying fires became available.[^1] The lookout operator's cabin nearby once gave shelter to spotters.[^1] We found no named fire in the sources we read.

The nomination says every resource remains at its original location.[^1] The operator's cabin is now used for storage, and the crew house from about 1950 is office space.[^1] A radio tower and equipment building from about 2013 stand nearby, and the equipment building sits inside a chain-link fence.[^1]

The National Register listed the tower on June 28, 2021.[^1] Gravel forest roads reach the site.[^1] The sources we read do not say whether the public may visit or climb it. Tennessee Landforms advises against climbing old towers, because steps are often missing or decayed.[^4] Please do not climb it, and ask the Division of Forestry before going to the site. We found no rental or lodging use.
