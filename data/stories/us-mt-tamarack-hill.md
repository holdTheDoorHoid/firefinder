Tamarack Hill lies about four miles northeast of St. Regis, in Mineral County, Montana, at an elevation of 4,202 feet.[^1] The site is on the Lolo National Forest.[^1] Early accounts also call the place Tamarack point, Tamarac lookout and Tamarack peak.[^2]

In November 1921 a Lolo forest fire assistant headed to St. Regis, where a newspaper item said lookout towers were being built at Tamarack point.[^2] By June 1927 a cabin was going up on the lookout, and that fall Gene Emerick and Walt Hahn built a log cabin measuring 12 x 14 for living quarters, replacing a tent camp.[^2]

In August 1927 lightning struck near the lookout.[^2] Gene Emerick, who was stationed there, was knocked down twice, and the telephone fuses blew out.[^2] Walter Lake of Noxon spent the summer of 1932 as the Forest Service lookout on Tamarack peak.[^2]

Firelookout.com gives 1936 for an L-4 tower here and marks it gone, with no date for its removal.[^1] The 1927 accounts already describe a tower in use, so the 1936 date does not line up with them.[^1][^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's list also marks the tower as gone.[^3]

I found no source describing what remains at the site today or whether the public can reach it.
