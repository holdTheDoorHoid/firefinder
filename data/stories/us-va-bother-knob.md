Bother Knob is a summit on the crest of Shenandoah Mountain in Rockingham County, Virginia, inside the George Washington National Forest.[^1][^2] It stands at 4,344 feet (1,324 m).[^1]

Several fire towers stood on Shenandoah Mountain, and Bother Knob was one of them.[^3] The Friends of Shenandoah Mountain describe its lookout as a steel tower.[^2]

Gerald Fawley, a recent graduate of Broadway High School in Rockingham County, went to work for the Civilian Conservation Corps (CCC) soon after finishing school.[^2] When the CCC camp shut down in 1937, he moved on to the Forest Service.[^2]

When the fire season came around, the Forest Service posted him at the steel tower on Bother Knob.[^2] From there he kept an eye out for fires and phoned a dispatch station in Bridgewater whenever smoke or flames appeared.[^2] When tree growth blocked the view, the Forest Service moved him to the High Knob lookout, five miles to the north.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register added the site on June 9, 2015, with Renick Proffitt as nominator.[^1] According to that register, all that survives is a cable anchor and an open clearing.[^1]

A hiker's account on SummitPost describes a fire road running along the mountain, with the summit several hundred feet above it, and a poorly marked trail up from the road.[^4] There is no tower there to climb.[^1]
