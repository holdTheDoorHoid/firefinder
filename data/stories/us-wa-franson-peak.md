Franson Peak sits on Washington state forest land in Ferry County, a short drive from Curlew.[^1][^4] The first lookout went up in 1934 with an L-5 cab.[^1][^3] In the 1950s a 15-foot treated timber tower with an L-4 cab replaced it.[^1][^3]

From March 27 to October 5, 1942, the site was an Aircraft Warning Service post.[^1][^2] On October 7, 1944, The Colville Examiner reported that women were manning lookouts during a manpower shortage. It named Alevida Webb of Ellensburg as one of them, on Franson Peak.[^2] Staffing ended in 1995.[^1][^3]

The current structure, a 15-foot tower with a DNR live-in cab, was built in 1986.[^1][^3] When it went up, the older L-4 cab was moved to the Molson Museum, in the ghost town of Molson east of Oroville.[^1][^5] The cab sits at ground level there, beside the Schoolhouse Museum.[^5]

The National Historic Lookout Register listed Franson Peak on April 11, 2022, nominated by Christine Estrada.[^1]

The summit is reached by a gravel road from Curlew, and recent accounts say a high-clearance vehicle is needed.[^4][^3] In wet or snowy weather four-wheel drive is required, and the road gets no winter upkeep for wheeled vehicles.[^4] Some stretches are narrow and badly rutted, and the route passes a gate near the top.[^3] A few communications towers also stand at the summit.[^1][^3] None of these sources mention renting the lookout, and no current access notice turned up.[^1][^3][^4] Check with the DNR before you go, and respect any closed gate.
