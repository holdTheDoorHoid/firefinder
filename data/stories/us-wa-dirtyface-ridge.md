Dirtyface Ridge is the site of a former fire lookout in the Wenatchee National Forest, in Chelan County, Washington, about two miles north of Lake Wenatchee.[^1][^2] A camp was set up on the summit in 1914 as a heliograph lookout, and in the summer of 1915 crews were building a telephone line and a trail up the mountain.[^3][^4] A D-6 cupola cabin and a platform went up in 1920.[^1][^4]

Sources do not agree on the cab that came next. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register and the Willhite guide both say an L-4 cab replaced the cupola in 1933.[^1][^4] FireLookout.com says photo evidence suggests that cab was never built.[^2] All three put an R-6 flat cab in place in 1957.[^1][^2][^4]

Several seasonal lookouts are named. Harry T. Gisborne, a student from an eastern forestry school, held the post in 1916.[^4] Ben S. Maxwell is listed for 1921, and Gladys West acted as relief lookout in 1923.[^2][^3] Lawrence Cunningham was on the peak in 1924, and Pat Douglas in 1933.[^3][^2] Bud Ault worked the station in 1934 and again in 1935.[^3]

Lightning hit the station in 1929. Ranger Kellicut's account says the flag pole was shattered and some connections on a signal set in the tower were badly burned.[^3][^4] The sources differ on the timing. The Willhite guide says early 1929, while the Weebly page dates the report to November 1929 and describes a storm early in that season.[^4][^3]

Plans called for the R-6 cab to be flown by helicopter to nearby Alpine Lookout in June 1975. Because it could not be lifted off the summit, it was burned to the ground.[^4][^1] The guide adds that a sixty-two-year lookout journal burned too.[^4]

No lookout stands on the summit now. A hiker's account describes cement foundation blocks where the lookout stood, and says the abandoned site is used for weather monitoring.[^1][^5] The route is about four miles with 4,000 feet of gain, according to WTA, and starts at a trailhead next to the Lake Wenatchee Ranger Station. The Willhite guide puts the old site about 4.5 miles up the trail.[^6][^4] WTA's trail note, dated July 30, 2026, says the trail is closed until further notice because of the Little Giant Fire. That is the latest notice we found, so visitors should not expect access for now.[^6]
