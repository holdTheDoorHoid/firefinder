Tenas Peak is an old cinder cone just west of the Cascade Crest, on the northwest corner of the Mount Thielsen Wilderness in Douglas County.[^5][^4][^1] The Umpqua National Forest manages the trail to the summit, which was once a Forest Service fire lookout.[^4]

The sources do not agree on when the lookout began. The Oregon Lookouts page says the site was used as a lookout in the 1920s but gives no details, and that a 20-foot round timber tower with an L-4 cab went up at an undetermined date after 1936.[^3] The register, firelookout.com and a 2016 hiking blog date a 20-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab to 1939, which fits that later estimate.[^1][^2][^5]

The tower was removed in 1966.[^1][^5] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2] A 2016 hiking blog says four concrete footings are all that remain on the summit.[^5]

The Forest Service describes the hike from the Kelsay Valley Trailhead: 2.5 miles on the North Umpqua Trail, then 4.5 miles up the Tolo Creek Trail to the Tenas Peak trail junction.[^4] Wilderness rules apply.[^4] The Forest Service says the summit has excellent views, including of Cinnamon Butte Lookout, which still provides fire surveillance in summer.[^4] A 2016 blog says that view means walking off the summit to find a gap in the trees, which have grown up since the old lookout's day.[^5]
