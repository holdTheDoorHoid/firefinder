In April 1942, work began on a standard fire lookout on the summit of Peavine Mountain, northwest of Reno.[^1] The project was a joint effort of the Mono National Forest and the Grazing Service. The Forest Service supplied the building, a standard 14-by-14-foot lookout with sleeping quarters. The Grazing Service supplied the workers, the supervision and the engineering plans.[^1] The building itself came from a Susanville site where it had sat unused.[^1] The Grazing Service also completed a truck trail to the station, planned to run five miles from U.S. Highway 395.[^1]

The station opened on July 21, 1942, with two-way radio.[^1] J.P. Ryan of the Mono forest service was assigned to the post for the summer season.[^1]

Sources disagree about who built it. The 1942 newspaper items name the Mono forest and the Grazing Service, but a 2012 Forest Fire Lookout Association report says the Tahoe National Forest built it originally.[^1][^2] That report also says the Nevada Division of Forestry staffed the lookout for a while, and that it was abandoned in 1964 and still standing in 1972.[^2] The report cannot say when the lookout was removed.[^2]

Today the summit carries radio towers and transmitters, reached by a road that winds up the north and east faces.[^3] Under the Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest's stewardship, the mountain now has a large network of hiking and mountain-bike routes. Over the past fifteen years, access from northwest Reno has tightened.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the lookout as gone.[^4] No source we read describes what, if anything, is left of the lookout itself.
