Watch Mountain rises a few miles north of Randle, in Lewis County, Washington.[^3] Its first lookout was an L-4 ground cab on the eastern end of the ridge, and the lookout that stands today is on the western side.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

Sources disagree on how far apart the two sites are. The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the newer lookout is about a mile to the west, while TrailChick says about two miles and Washington Lookouts says a little under two miles.[^1][^4][^5]

The state built the first lookout in 1933 to protect private land, and a cabin was added in 1940.[^1][^2][^3]

TrailChick reports that Charles Boyer and Irma Huntington staffed the first lookout in 1938, in the first year after their wedding.[^4] A July 29, 1955 item in The Daily Chronicle, printed before the 1963 tower existed, says the U.S. Forestry Service activated two lookouts as ground observer posts and that Watch Mountain was manned by James E. Grafton. An August 9, 1955 item in the same paper spells the name Crafton.[^5]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register says the first lookout was gone in the 1960s.[^1] TrailChick says the site was replaced in 1963 by the current tower.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains of the first site today. The National Historic Lookout Register, which covers the 1963 tower, says only one of the lookouts once above the north side of the Cowlitz valley still stands.[^2]

The 1963 tower is now mainly an electronics site.[^2] A visitor on July 24, 2003 reported walking about six miles past three locked gates to reach it.[^2] TrailChick warns that the shorter routes from Highway 12 cross private land, with gates and no-trespassing signs.[^4] We found no source that describes public access to the first site, so check with the Forest Service before heading out, and do not climb any structure you find there.
