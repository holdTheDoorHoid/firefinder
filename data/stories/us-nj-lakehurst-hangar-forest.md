The state's forest fire service opened a lookout on the roof of a large Navy hangar at Lakehurst Naval Air Station in 1922.[^1][^2] In March that year the state said a watchman would soon be posted there, with the Navy's approval.[^2] Newspapers in June reported that the station had just opened, while a state report says it went into operation in the fall of 1922, so the start date is unclear.[^2] The registry gives 1922 as the build year and mentions a May 31, 1922 memo about a contract to install a phone at the station.[^1]

The registry describes a 7-by-7-foot metal cab on a 6-foot tower, set on a hangar it gives as 95 feet tall.[^1] That height looks too low. A 1922 press account put the roof 212 feet above the surface,[^2] and the Navy's historical society gives the doors of Hangar No. One as 177 feet tall.[^4] The sources do not name the hangar the lookout sat on, so we cannot say whether those figures apply to it.

The sources name no observers.[^1][^2] The registry says the observer would have seen the Hindenburg explode in 1937, but it does not say anyone did.[^1] The Hindenburg was destroyed by fire at NAS Lakehurst on May 6, 1937.[^3]

The registry says the lookout ran until after World War II. Military security rules then led to other lookouts being built in the region.[^1] It also says the cab still sits on the roof, but we found no recent photo or inspection to confirm that.[^1]

The lookout is at Lakehurst Naval Air Station, a military site.[^1] The registry calls it a classified military area that the public is unlikely to visit, so we rate access as restricted.[^1] We found no public tour that includes the lookout, so do not plan a trip there.
