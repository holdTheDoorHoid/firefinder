The Uncanoonuc Mountains are two small peaks in Goffstown, New Hampshire.[^1] NH Family Hikes lists South Uncanoonuc Mountain among the known fire tower sites in the state.[^2]

In the early 1900s the area became a resort, with a hotel and an incline railway.[^1] A Wikimedia Commons caption, based on a 1910 postcard, describes the Uncanoonuc Hotel on the South Peak as built in 1907 and burned in 1923.[^3]

The earliest lookout entry we found dates to January 1925, when George E. Flanders, the forest fire warden at Uncanoonuc, died after a long illness.[^4] A Portsmouth Herald obituary described his lookout station as perched on the mountain top, and said he was well known to thousands of summer visitors to it.[^4]

In an October 1937 item, the State Department of Forestry and Recreation said the Mt. Uncanoonuc lookout would close once repair work then in progress was completed.[^4]

The ski operation served by the incline railway ran through the 1930s and 1940s, until a fire damaged the railway in 1941.[^1] The railway has since been turned into a hiking trail, and the mountains are still a good place to hike and snowshoe.[^1]

The FireLookout index lists the tower as gone, and the Forest Lookouts page marks it as removed.[^5][^4] No source we read gives a removal date, and none describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout on the summit today.
