Pine Mountain sits about 20 miles west of Yakima, in Yakima County.[^1][^2] The Washington Department of Natural Resources put up a 40-foot wooden tower there in 1961.[^1][^2] The DNR built its lookouts with its own carpentry crew, and a state biennial report quoted on the Forest Lookouts page says the crew finished nine lookouts at about $8,000 each, one of them on Pine Mountain.[^4]

The register and firelookout.com both say this tower was the only lookout structure ever on the site.[^1][^2] Willhite's page raises a doubt.[^3] Metsker maps from 1934 and 1959 label the high point of Cowiche Mountain "Cowiche L.O.," which Willhite takes as a sign of an earlier lookout.[^3] He calls this a gut feeling, and he notes that nobody knows anything about that earlier lookout.[^3]

The tower still stood in 2006 but was gone by 2012.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page marks it as removed.[^4]

Willhite's page says the property was owned by Pine Mountain LLC in 2015, and that nobody knows where the lookout went.[^3] An LLC owning the land suggests it is privately held.[^3] A National Geodetic Survey station description quoted on the Forest Lookouts page says to obtain a key to a locked gate, then follow the main-traveled road 3.4 miles to the top. The page's recovery notes date from 1966 and 1971, so those directions may be out of date.[^4]

No source here describes what, if anything, is left on the site today.
