The Helena Fire Tower, nicknamed the Guardian of the Gulch, stands in a city park on Helena's west side.[^1][^2] The National Historic Lookout Register lists it as administered by the City of Helena and registered it on January 8, 2021.[^3]

The Visit Helena listing says the 25-foot tower went up in 1876, following a major fire that destroyed downtown, and the register gives the same year, calling it the second tower on the site.[^1][^3] Two other sources disagree. A firelookout.com guide dates the wooden tower to 1869, and the Montana Free Press says it was built in 1874.[^4][^2] This account uses 1876 because the register and the Visit Helena listing agree, but the build year is not settled.

An octagonal cab was added in 1886.[^1][^4] The Visit Helena listing says the tower was manned around the clock, and that a sighting of fire set off an alarm to warn the town.[^1] It was last used in 1931.[^3][^4]

A 1936 earthquake damaged the tower, and it was repaired. A 1950 fire, thought to have been started by lightning, damaged it too, and it was repaired again.[^4]

In August 2016 an arson fire damaged the southwest strut.[^4][^6] The city then floated plans to tear the tower down and build a replica, and in February 2017 it announced demolition.[^6][^4] Public outcry saved the tower from demolition, and a nonprofit, Friends of the Fire Tower, formed in 2018 to restore it.[^4]

The city chose to keep the tower and restore it.[^2] It awarded a $424,383 contract to Dick Anderson Construction in November. The Free Press said that figure ran roughly $150,000 above the original budget.[^2] Local TV reported a bid award of nearly $386,000, so the two figures disagree.[^5] The Free Press dates the start of renovation to January, while KTVH says December.[^2][^5] Nearly 70 percent of the old lumber had rotted, so crews salvaged what they could and set the original cab on a new frame.[^2]

KTVH reported that the work was finished in May and the tower rededicated with a ribbon cutting, nearly ten years after the arson.[^5]

Today the tower stands in the city park, with a hiking trail up from Cruse Avenue, just across Broadway.[^1] A donation has also come in for new lights.[^5] I found no source saying whether visitors may go inside or climb the tower, so ask the city before you try.
