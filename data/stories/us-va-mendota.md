The Mendota Fire Tower stands on Clinch Mountain, on the line between Washington and Russell counties.[^1][^2] The Register describes it as a 100-foot steel tower.[^1] The sources we read do not say when it was built, who built it, or why it was put there, so the early part of its history is missing from our record.

Its best-documented role since then is as a hawk-watching site. Fall counts have been made from the site since 1958, and HawkCount calls the tower an abandoned fire tower.[^2] The Register says it is no longer manned.[^1] It was registered on September 3, 2014, on a nomination by Kristin Scholetzky.[^1] The Register gives its elevation as 2,976 feet, while HawkCount gives 3,018 feet.[^1][^2]

Access is on foot. HawkCount says visitors park along Route 612 near the crest at Fugate Gap and walk up an old gated dirt road.[^2] HawkCount puts the walk at about a third of a mile.[^2] East Coast Hiker says the trail is 0.5 miles and steep, and that the bottom section of steps has been removed for safety.[^3] We found no official statement that the tower is open to climbers, so we do not encourage climbing it.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as standing.[^4] We found no rental listing.
