In late 1939 the Wisconsin Conservation Department finished erecting a 100-foot steel fire tower about two miles south of Plover, in Portage County.[^1] It was the county's second fire tower, and the other stood several miles further south.[^1] The Stevens Point Journal reported that the Plover tower was one of 31 erected statewide through a WPA project the conservation department sponsored.[^1] WPA crews laid the footings, landscaped the sites and built the access roads, while state conservation crews did the erecting.[^1]

The tower was a secondary type, so it would be manned only when fire hazard was extreme.[^1] Primary towers, by contrast, were occupied through the summer.[^1] Its main purpose was to work out whether smoke to the north came from timber fires or from the mills at or near Stevens Point.[^1] A private telephone line linked it to the Friendship district ranger station.[^1]

The statewide lookout list marks the tower as gone.[^2] No source describes the site today, so what remains there and whether visitors can reach it are unknown.
