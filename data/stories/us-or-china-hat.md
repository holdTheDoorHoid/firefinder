China Hat is first a volcanic butte on the southeastern flank of Newberry Volcano, in Deschutes County.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com also lists a China Hat Fire Lookout about 22 miles east of LaPine, in the Deschutes National Forest, at an elevation of 6,573 feet.[^2]

Firelookout.com dates the lookout only to the 1930s and says the date is unknown.[^2] The oldest dated use of the name we found is in a letter dated November 27, 1936, quoted in a 1938 Coast and Geodetic Survey station description.[^3] That description places the Forest Service's East Butte lookout tower on the summit of East Butte, and says the tower was called China Hat Lookout in the directions for the Pilot Butte station.[^3]

Firelookout.com describes the lookout as abandoned.[^2] The Oregon lookout list, from the Forest Fire Lookout Association, marks China Hat as gone.[^4] The two sources do not agree on whether anything is left.[^2][^4]

No source we read describes what remains at the China Hat site, or whether visitors can reach it.[^2][^4] The National Historic Lookout Register says the East Butte Lookout is still in active service.[^5] Whether the two names refer to one tower is not settled by the sources we read, so do not assume the East Butte tower is China Hat.[^3][^5]
