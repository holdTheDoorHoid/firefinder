Miners Ridge is in Yakima County, above Bumping Lake, about seven miles southeast of Chinook Pass.[^1][^2][^3] The register describes the lookout as a two-story L-4 cab and dates it to 1934.[^1] Willhite cites an inventory that gives 1933 to 1934.[^3] George Clisby took panoramic photographs from its roof on July 19, 1934.[^3][^4]

Willhite's page names several lookouts: Allen New in the late 1930s, Dave Wright in 1938, Douglas Jones in 1942 and Betty Gallant in 1943.[^3] In the summer of 1944 a team of three women staffed the tower: a grandmother, her daughter, and the daughter's 14-year-old, Pamela, who later remembered the work.[^3] An article by Joan Burton, reproduced on Willhite's page, says the trio were the only three-woman team in Region Six, which covered Washington and Oregon.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1972. Firelookout.com and Willhite both say it was destroyed that year.[^1][^2][^3]

Today the summit road is kept up to the top, but it is rough and rocky throughout.[^3] The site is reached by a drive-up or walk road, and Willhite advises high clearance, with 4WD best.[^3] A Root Lake trailhead is at the top, and Granite Lake Campground is near the summit.[^3] The site sits empty in the off season and fills up in midsummer. Snow can linger on the summit road until then.[^3]

Garage foundations lie in the parking area a short distance below the summit.[^3] A photo caption on Willhite's page says a few foundation parts were still on the summit in 2018, but the caption does not say which structure they belonged to.[^3] Apart from those, the sources we read describe no standing part of the lookout.[^3] A separate Miners Ridge tower in Snohomish County, 8 miles northeast of Glacier Peak, is a different lookout. Its present tower was built in 1953 and is staffed by volunteers.[^5]
