Mount Mitchell sits in the Siouxon Block of state timberland, due south of Mount St. Helens, in Skamania County.[^1][^2] Older documents from the 1930s and 1940s frequently used the name Mitchell Peak.[^1]

Sources disagree on when the first cab went up.[^1][^2] A former DNR lookout recalls the Forest Service building it around 1933, due to the Yacolt Burn.[^1] Willhite found an L-4 cab on the summit by 1942, with no known build date, and a 1948 Forest Service document called the lookout recently constructed.[^1] The register says a two-story L-4 cab from 1957 replaced an earlier emergency lookout.[^2] Willhite also questions the 1957 date.[^1]

From March 16, 1942 to October 16, 1943, the Aircraft Warning Service used the site, and it added sleeping quarters and a woodshed.[^3] The register says the Forest Service ran the lookout at first, and the DNR after that until 1966.[^2] The former DNR lookout remembers serving there in the summer of 1965, when a significant lightning bust centered on the lookout.[^1]

Accounts of the end do not match. The same former lookout's note says the lookout was burned as a fire hazard, because the public visited it often.[^1] In August 1969 The Oregonian reported that the lookout would be discontinued in favor of air patrols and kept as a supply cache.[^3] The register gives removal as 1970, while the inventory and firelookout.com say it was destroyed that year.[^2][^1][^4]

Today, the Willhite page shows a photo captioned "Footing at the summit," along with outhouse debris photos from the 1990s and about 2000.[^1] Private property issues have kept the main road to the official trail closed for years, so the page describes an unofficial trail instead.[^1] The page warns that the roads in the block are confusing and poorly marked.[^1] Check with the Washington DNR, which manages the Siouxon Block, before you go.[^1]
