A new wooden fire tower joined the detection system on White Oak Knob in Boone County in the second half of 1937, according to a district ranger's report that a February 1938 newspaper quoted.[^2] The newspaper gives the knob's elevation as 2,000 feet, while the register gives 1,856 feet (566 m).[^2][^1]

The register says the tower watched over the Wharton area of Boone County.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association registered the site on November 27, 2016, as US 1630, WV 26, and names the West Virginia Division of Forestry as its administrator.[^1]

The state list calls it a wooden tower and marks it gone.[^3] The register page includes a photo dated 1937, credited to Jim Foster.[^1]

None of the sources we read describes what, if anything, remains on the knob, or whether the site is open to the public.[^1][^2][^3]
