The Smyrna lookout, which the National Historic Lookout Register lists as Smyrna (Evansville), is a 65-foot Aermotor tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab, built in the late 1950s.[^1] It stands in Rhea County[^3] and is administered by the Tennessee Division of Forestry.[^1]

The state's fire towers have a longer history. Between 1933 and 1941, the Civilian Conservation Corps put up 98 lookout towers in Tennessee, and construction went on through the 1950s.[^2] Smyrna belongs to that later period.[^1][^2]

The state's inventory numbers towers by district and tower, and lists this one as 3-2, standing, in District 3.[^2][^3] A National Register study of the state's towers says the Aermotor LS-40 design saw broad use by the forestry division until the mid-1970s.[^4] The sources I read do not say which Aermotor model Smyrna is, who chose the site, who staffed it, or whether it saw any notable fires.

The register says the cab is locked, though signage permits climbing at the visitor's own risk.[^1] The register also reports active use as of 2019.[^1] I could not confirm its condition or any use after 2019. The state's list says many towers are gone and most of their former sites are privately owned.[^2] The sources do not say who owns this site. The same list advises against climbing these towers.[^2] Visitors should not count on being able to climb Smyrna.
