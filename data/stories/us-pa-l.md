Long Mountain, in Cumberland County, got a steel fire tower in 1922, after a new road was finished up the mountain to the site.[^1][^2] Before the tower, the spot was a bare observation point, which the lookout history page dates to before 1918.[^1] The register describes the tower as a steel Blaw-Knox design.[^2]

In 1931 Henry C. Sheaffer was the observer on the tower, in the Michaux Forest district. He had been a forest fire warden for about forty years.[^1] By 1933 a newspaper called him the towerman, and the tower was listed as Long Mountain Fire Tower No. 2, south of Boiling Springs. He had 69 visitors on Sunday, April 23, and the paper reported that three mountain fires had kept him busy over a recent weekend.[^1]

In 1952 the tower was moved and given the name Bandana.[^2]

The register lists the original site as a former lookout site, with the road junction given as US 359 and PA 188, and it was registered on September 16, 2008.[^2] The register does not say where the moved tower stands now, and no source reviewed describes the site today.
