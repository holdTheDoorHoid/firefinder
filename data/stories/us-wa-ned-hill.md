Ned Hill was a pole platform lookout on Olympic National Forest land in Clallam County, Washington.[^1][^2] The U.S. Forest Service administered it.[^1] The register says it was constructed in 1933, and a visitor page describes a crude tower of standing snags and poles.[^1][^4]

When fire danger ran high, a fire guard based at Slab Camp Creek climbed up to staff the lookout, according to an undated visitor page.[^4] The same page dates the Maynard Peak fire only to "the early part of the century."[^4] TrailChick, however, dates the fire to 1935 and says it swept the whole area, including both main posts of the lookout.[^3] The visitor page gives no year, so the two sources do not agree on the date.

Panorama photos were taken on August 21, 1935,[^5] and TrailChick credits the 1935 panoramas to the Forest Service.[^3]

The National Historic Lookout Register added the site on May 22, 2022.[^1]

A firelookout.com page says the tower had been abandoned for many years before it collapsed during the 2024/2025 winter.[^2] TrailChick gives April 2025 as the date it was reported collapsed, and says the tower was already in poor shape when its writer visited in 2018.[^3]

We found no source describing what is left at the site now. TrailChick's photos date from that 2018 visit.[^3] A visitor page warns that "Climbing will likely bring down some pieces, please stay off!"[^4]

The site sits on national forest land, and sources describe a public hike to it.[^3][^4] Directions start on Highway 101 at the western edge of Sequim.[^4] A visitor page gives the hike as 1.1 miles, while TrailChick gives a usual distance of 2.2 without making clear whether that is one way.[^4][^3]
