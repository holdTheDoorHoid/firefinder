Hamma Hamma GS appears on the Forest Fire Lookout Association's Washington list as a standing ground site in Mason County.[^1] It was planned in 1936 as a combined guard station and fire lookout, on a bench that overlooks the valley.[^2] A May 1936 letter called the area blind, which is why the proposed design pushed the living room toward the edge of the bench and added window area for a better view.[^2] Civilian Conservation Corps crews built it between 1936 and 1937.[^3] Revisiting Washington gives 1937 as the year it was completed.[^4]

In that period, a gate below the station was to restrict the watershed during fire season.[^2] If someone held a permit to enter the watershed, the guard had to stop patrolling and travel down to the gate and unlock it, which the document says cost valuable detection time.[^2] I found no named guards or fires tied to the lookout in the pages I read.

The building still stands, and the Forest Service rents it out as a vacation cabin.[^2] Campflare says a nomination for the National Register of Historic Places cites its craftsmanship, but I did not find a confirmed listing.[^3]

Today the cabin is booked through Recreation.gov.[^3] It is open year-round, closed on Thursdays for cleaning, and limited to seven nights per stay.[^3] Guests are given gate access to the short gated road that runs up to the cabin, but the gate is not guaranteed to be open.[^2] No drinking water is provided on site, so guests need to bring their own.[^3] Head north on Highway 101 from Hoodsport for 14 miles, turn onto Hamma Hamma River Road, and follow it 5.7 miles to the turnoff for the cabin.[^3] The Forest Service describes the Hamma Hamma drainage as having one historic cabin among its recreation options.[^5]
