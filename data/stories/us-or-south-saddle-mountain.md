The lookout on South Saddle Mountain stood near the line between Washington and Tillamook counties, in Oregon.[^1][^3] The FFLOS register says a 90-foot crow's nest with an enclosed platform and a shake cabin went up in 1932.[^1] A 1932 district report also records a log lookout house built on Saddle Mountain that year.[^3]

A July 1930 daily report from the lookout says a cougar killed a dog 40 feet from the lookout tower.[^3] An August 1931 item recalls that the year before, while Tom Stevenson was up in his lookout 90 feet above the ground, a cougar came to the foot of the tree and killed his dog.[^3] Both items place a lookout on the mountain before the 1932 date, so the 1932 date may not be the first lookout on the site.[^3]

In August 1933 the Wilson River fire swept up the mountain and drove Stevenson from his post.[^3] An August 25 item said it was still unknown whether the lookout or the cabin had burned.[^3] A report dated August 26 says Stevenson reported the lookout destroyed by fire.[^3]

The FFLOS register says a 40-foot pole L-4 tower replaced the crow's nest in 1947 and was removed in 1974.[^1] The weebly history dates the replacement differently, to a tower in 1946 and a 40-foot timber tower completed in 1948, and it also lists a smaller 18-foot tower built in 1944.[^3] Firelookout.com gives the 1947 replacement and the 1974 removal.[^2]

A 1941 survey recovery note, quoted on the same weebly page, describes a tree topped with a platform about 90 feet up and an old brick chimney about 35 feet from the tree's base.[^3] That is a description from 1941. I found no source that describes what remains at the site since the 1974 removal, or whether it is open to visitors.
