Elk Mountain stands on the southern side of Glacier National Park, west of Marias Pass.[^1] Its summit once held a two-story lookout cab, described as an NPS cab.[^2]

A 1930 Park Service report, quoted by one lookout history, describes a trail up to the summit and a stopgap lookout there.[^3] It also reports two new lookouts that year, one of them on Elk Mountain, close to Fielding Ranger Station.[^3] Dates for the permanent two-story cab vary. One site gives 1930,[^2] another says it was under construction in 1932,[^4] and a Glacier National Park Archives record says it was built that year.[^5]

Most sources give 1965 as the year it ended.[^1][^3][^4][^5] One history says the Park Service burned it on May 27 of that year.[^4] The archive record says it was destroyed in 1965.[^5] A 2019 hike description says it was last used in 1959 and razed by the Park Service in 1965.[^1] One lookout site gives 1963 instead.[^2]

Today only an old concrete base with corroded bolts and loose cable marks the spot.[^1] The same 2019 description gives a round trip of about 6.8 miles on a footpath that starts at a parking area. It says the route runs through private land before it meets the rail line south of the park boundary.[^1]
