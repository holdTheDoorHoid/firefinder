A log cabin went up at Kalispell Rock in 1927, in the Kaniksu National Forest of Pend Oreille County.[^1][^2][^3] Above it, a small firefinder cab was placed on top of the summit boulder.[^3] WillhiteWeb says a post lifted the cab several feet above the rock, and that the log cabin below served as living quarters.[^3]

The Register says the lookout was last manned in 1935.[^1] WillhiteWeb, citing a Kresek inventory, says it was no longer used around 1934.[^3] A brief 1936 item in the Colville Examiner names Wyatt B. Morehead as the lookout on Kalispell and says he put out three small lightning fires in his district that August.[^4][^3] The sources do not agree on when the lookout ended.[^1][^3]

Firelookout describes the site as abandoned, with ruins.[^2] WillhiteWeb says the cabin has somewhat survived, and its log walls bear the marks of many visitors.[^3]

The Register reports cabin ruins on the site, and no source we read describes the firefinder cab still standing.[^1][^3] WillhiteWeb says the post hole is still in the summit rock, and a 2023 photo caption shows the cabin with the rock behind it.[^3] The Register lists the U.S. Forest Service as administrator.[^1]

WillhiteWeb gives the hike as about 3 miles one way, with 1,200 feet of climb.[^3] Its route follows a closed road for much of the way, then a ridge trail with some off-trail walking, and the page warns that the trail has had little maintenance.[^3] The top needs a short scramble with some exposure.[^3] Its access field says most any vehicle could reach the site, which does not match the closed road on the route. We found no source that says whether the public may use that road.[^3]
