Blue Hill is a hill west of Sundre, Alberta, inside the Rocky Mountains Forest Reserve.[^1][^2] A fire lookout has stood there since at least 1927.[^1]

The first cab was 3.7 m (12 ft) square and sat on a round pole tower about 6 m (20 ft) high.[^1] A 1930 Calgary Herald item, quoted by the same volunteer index, named Blue Hill among five forest reserve lookout posts.[^1] A replacement structure of a similar kind was built in 1952.[^1]

The index dates a steel tower with a cupola cab, about 6 m (20 ft) high, only to the 1960s. It cites a 1998 guidebook and adds a three-room house for living quarters.[^1] A 2008 trip report calls the tower 20 feet high.[^5] The index also records that the living quarters cabin was rebuilt in 2011.[^1]

A lookout observer was on duty when a hiker visited in May 2008. That report says she had spent seven of her last thirty years as an observer on Blue Hill.[^2]

A December 2019 trip report says the small tower is set aside for the lookout observer while the station is manned in spring and summer.[^3] A route guide calls the lookout still active.[^4] The same report says trees surround the lookout building and auxiliary installations at the top.[^3]

To reach the hill, park near a gate at the start of an unmarked side road off the Forestry Trunk Road (940), 2.3 km east of the Red Deer Ranger station.[^4] A logging road climbs about halfway, then a narrower maintenance track leads to the top.[^3] A route guide describes the trail to the lookout as an old fire road that is closed.[^4]

We found no source that says the public may enter the tower, and none says it is closed. The tower is reserved for the observer, so do not climb it.
