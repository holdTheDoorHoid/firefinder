Castle Peak is a rocky summit north of Eagle, Colorado, and a Forest Service fire lookout once stood on it.[^1][^2] A 1919 guidebook says the peak is named for an unusual formation that resembles the notched walltops of an ancient castle.[^2]

The register describes the lookout as a square "ground cabin" of unusual design, and places the site in the White River National Forest.[^1] A 1962 Eagle Valley Enterprise item says the Forest Service erected the small structure in 1916 as a post for watching for fires.[^2] The same article called it a picturesque "glass house."[^2] A 1913 item from the same paper already mentions a "Castle Peak Lookout station" and a telephone on the summit, so the sources do not agree on when the station began.[^2]

Frank Alvord was the lookout in the summers of 1919 and 1920, and Herman Brown was named for 1921, according to newspaper items.[^2] The 1962 item says the building had sat unused since 1930.[^2]

In November 1962 the Forest Service said the building had to go, because it threatened people climbing the peak.[^2] The register dates its removal to the late 1960s, several years after that notice.[^1]

The register gives the site's coordinates and says the Bureau of Land Management administers it.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Colorado list marks the structure as removed.[^3] Nothing in the sources I read describes what, if anything, remains on the summit today, or whether visitors can reach it.
