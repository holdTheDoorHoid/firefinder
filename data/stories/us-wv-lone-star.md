A photograph posted by the WV fire lookouts Facebook page is captioned "Lone Star Fire Tower, Nicholas Co. 1929."[^1] That caption gives 1929 for a picture of the tower, not a construction date.[^1] No source read for this story gives a build date.

On March 8, 1946, a local towerman named Ted Hughes was told to start work at Lone Star about two weeks early, because dry, windy weather had raised the fire danger in Nicholas and Clay Counties.[^2] The usual start was March 20.[^2] Fred Hanna, the county forest protector, was stationed at Clay and was in charge of fire protection in the western part of Nicholas County and in Clay County.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register lists the site in Nicholas County at 2,227 feet (679 m), administered by the West Virginia Division of Forestry, and it was registered on January 26, 2017.[^3] It does not describe the tower or its removal.

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's county list marks the tower as gone.[^4]

No source describes the site today, or whether it is open to the public.
