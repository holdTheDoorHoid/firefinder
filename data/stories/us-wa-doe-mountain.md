Doe Mountain is a 7,154-foot peak about 18 miles north of Winthrop, in Okanogan County, Washington.[^1][^2] The register places the site in the Okanogan National Forest.[^1]

A wooden L-4 tower with a cab was built on the mountain in 1935.[^1][^3] The register and FireLookout.com both call the tower a 6-foot structure.[^1][^2] A Willhite note on the 1936 panoramic photos says they were taken on the roof, 15 feet above the ground, so the sources do not agree on the height.[^3] Panorama photos are dated July 25, 1936.[^4][^1]

The cab was gone by 1952, according to the register, FireLookout.com and the Willhite guide.[^1][^2][^3]

A hiker's trip report describes the approach. The author drove up the West Chewuch to Forest Road 100, then on FR-170, where a downed tree marks the end of the road a little over a mile up.[^5] From there the hike went cross-country up the southeast ridge.[^5] On the summit the author found the summit benchmarks, along with debris that looked like melted lead glass, a few ceramic insulators, and small pieces of hardware such as hinges and nails.[^5] No source we read describes a foundation or other structure on the summit today.[^1][^5]
