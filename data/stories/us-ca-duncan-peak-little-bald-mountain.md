The lookout on Little Bald Mountain in Placer County is the third fire-detection station on that spot, according to the National Historic Lookout Register.[^1] The original station was a 10-foot tower, put up sometime between 1910 and 1915.[^1] The register also places a ground house just north of the present structure, built in 1917 and taken down in 1941.[^1]

The lookout that stands today is a BC-3 ground house measuring 14 by 14 feet, with a catwalk. The register dates its construction to 1943.[^1] The register recorded it on September 21, 2004.[^1]

A 2020 hiking write-up says the lookout had stood on the real Duncan Peak until 1941. That account places the earlier station differently from the register, and it comes from a hiking blog, so I treat it as unconfirmed.[^4]

Staffing has been seasonal, at least in 2023.[^2] A Forest Service feature on fire lookouts describes Maxwell Psaledakis spending his first season at the Duncan Peak lookout in June 2023.[^2] The caption of an interior photo mentions small amenities such as a stove, a refrigerator and running water.[^2]

In 2025 the Tahoe National Forest signed off on a project to mount two fire-detection cameras, with solar panels and batteries, on structures already at the lookout.[^3] The decision is dated May 16, 2025, but I could not confirm whether the cameras are installed.[^3]

I found no rental listing or reservation page for Duncan Peak, and no staffing record after 2023. A 2020 trip report describes a hike that starts at the Robinson Flat trailhead, east of Foresthill.[^4] The register names the Foresthill Ranger District as a cooperator, so that office is the place to ask about current access.[^1]
