In 1954 the lookout was called Deer Creek Point.[^2] It stood about 26 miles east-northeast of Jackson Hole, at 9,613 feet, in what is now the Bridger-Teton National Forest.[^1] Firelookout.com describes it as a log and frame cabin.[^1]

In July 1954, with hot, dry weather, the Teton Forest moved to staff several of its lookouts.[^2] A Jackson Hole Courier item from that month listed Deer Creek Point among those to be manned within the next week.[^2] In 1956 two Courier items, dated August 2 and August 30, reported that a worker had gone from the Goosewing station up to Deer Creek Lookout on a Monday, and later moved back to the ranger station on a Friday.[^2]

A Forest Service history says that in the mid-1970s the agency judged some lookouts to be possible liability issues and fire hazards, and torched the Munger Mountain and Deer Creek lookouts.[^3] The history says the aim was to discourage squatters.[^3] Its photo caption places Deer Creek Lookout near the Goosewing Guard Station.[^3] The 1956 items and that caption both tie the lookout to Goosewing, so the sources seem to describe the same site as Deer Creek Point, though none says so outright.[^2][^3] Firelookout.com says it was abandoned in the 1960s and gone by the 1970s.[^1] The history adds that by 1970 every lookout in the forest's southern part had been abandoned.[^3]

No source describes what remains at the site today. It lies on Bridger-Teton National Forest land, but no source says whether the spot can be reached.[^1]

Do not mix this up with Deer Creek Cabin. That is a separate Forest Service guard station about 13 miles from Alpine, which visitors can rent for overnight stays.[^4]
