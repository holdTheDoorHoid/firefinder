The McCune Ridge tower site is in Jackson County, Ohio, at an elevation of 980 feet (299 m).[^1] It was registered as US 1567, OH 13 on September 3, 2014.[^1]

In August 1934 the Chillicothe Gazette reported that a forestry tower had gone up on McCune Ridge.[^2] Associate State Forester B. F. Leete announced it as part of a forestry department plan to place fire towers about 15 miles apart across southern Ohio's forests.[^2] The new tower stood 20 feet taller than the one on the Scioto trail and had an aluminum finish.[^2] Federal money paid for the work, and labor came from CCC camps under Henry B. Sherman's supervision.[^2]

A fire warden was to staff the tower in spring and fall.[^2] In October 1934 the Gazette named Allen Weese as the observer for the new tower near Limerick.[^2]

A May 1935 Portsmouth Times report describes an enrollee from Camp Stoney Creek who was hurt while cutting brush near the Limerick fire tower in Liberty Township.[^3] Wikipedia places the community of Limerick in Jackson Township, which does not match that report.[^4]

The Forest Lookouts page lists the agency as Wayne National Forest, yet the 1934 report credits the state forestry department and federal funds.[^2] Wikipedia says the national forest was originally established in 1935, after the tower went up.[^5]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Ohio list marks the tower as gone.[^6]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the ridge today, or whether it is open to visitors.
