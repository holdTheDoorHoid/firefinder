Cliff Top is one of the towers that Tennessee Landforms lists in Great Smoky Mountains National Park.[^1] The entry gives an elevation of 6,555 feet (1,998 m) in Sevier County and marks the site as historical.[^1]

The park's lookouts date to the 1930s.[^2] Between 1934 and 1939 the park gained ten towers and nine lookout cabins, and lookouts staffed the sites through two fire seasons a year for decades.[^2] One guide says all but one of the ten towers were built by the Civilian Conservation Corps.[^3] A standard park tower stood about sixty feet on a steel frame, with a small cab at its peak and a one-room log cabin close by for the caretaker.[^4]

Fire management changed in the 1970s, and the towers were abandoned.[^2] Within the next ten years, all but four towers and one cabin were taken out of the park.[^2] The Mountain Press says four towers and one cabin still stand in the park, at Mount Cammerer, Mount Sterling, Cove Mountain, Shuckstack and High Rocks. Cliff Top is not on that list.[^4]

Today the name survives on a hiking trail. A steep route from the LeConte Lodge area climbs to a Cliff Top viewpoint with views toward Newfound Gap and Chimney Tops, and the guide does not mention a tower.[^5] No source describes what, if anything, remains at the old tower site, and none I found ties the viewpoint to it.
