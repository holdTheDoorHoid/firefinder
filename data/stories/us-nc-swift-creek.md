The Civilian Conservation Corps erected a steel fire tower at Swift Creek in Nash County, North Carolina, in March 1935.[^1] The tower was 79 feet 6 inches tall and came with a two-room ground cabin.[^1] The work cost $1,221.33 and was funded through the Emergency Conservation Works program.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history gives these figures from the state's 1934–36 biennial report, and lists the tower under the North Carolina Division of Forest Resources.[^1]

In November 1964 the county's forest ranger announced a new telephone number for the Swift Creek Fire Tower.[^1] The notice, reprinted by the Forest Lookouts history from the Rocky Mount Evening Telegram, said fires in the area could be reported by calling the tower.[^1]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the site is open to the public.
