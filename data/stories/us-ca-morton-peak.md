Morton Peak rises above State Highway 38 in the San Bernardino National Forest, about 16 miles east of Redlands.[^4][^3]

The Forest Service began a new lookout on Morton Peak in 1934, close to where Mill Creek Canyon opens out.[^3][^1] Newspapers that year planned steel towers for the site, and the cab on top was wooden until 1959.[^3]

On July 24, 1959, flames climbed the slope from Mill Creek and destroyed the lookout house.[^3] Aleta Johnson, the lookout on duty, was airlifted out only minutes before the flames reached the tower.[^3][^2] One 1959 report spells her name Aletha.[^3]

The old steel frame survived the fire.[^3] A 1960 notice said the new cab would go on the surviving steel tower, and by August 1960 the lookout had moved into a new steel cab.[^3] NHLR dates the rebuild to 1960, but the Mountains Foundation gives 1961.[^1][^2]

The lookout was staffed by the Forest Service in the 1970s.[^3] In 1992 a Forest Service fire-prevention officer said it had gone unmanned since the late 1970s, citing budget cuts.[^3] That same year vandals got through a gate, and a bomb went off in the garage at the tower.[^3] A 1992 newspaper report said the tower was headed for the Toiyabe National Forest, but later sources put it still on Morton Peak.[^3][^1][^4]

NHLR says volunteers brought the lookout back into active use in 2001, after it fell out of use in the 1970s.[^1] The volunteer host program's 2014 report says the lookout stayed open all year because of high fire danger, and it planned to put the site back on rental in 2015.[^5]

When a volunteer is on duty, the road to the summit may be open and the volunteer may invite visitors into the cab.[^4] Overnight stays for the public are no longer available.[^4] The road is unpaved and narrow, and the Forest Service recommends a high-clearance vehicle.[^4] The gate sits about 1.5 miles up, with the summit about 1.5 miles past it.[^4] With no one on duty, or during a fire emergency, you may have to leave your vehicle at the gate and walk in, and the cab may be off limits.[^4] A 2025 Mountains Foundation page said the area was closed under a Line Fire closure order.[^2] The Forest Service page does not mention that closure, so check before you go.[^2][^4]
