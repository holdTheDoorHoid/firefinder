Green Knob is a hilltop in Clark County, about 22 miles north of Washougal.[^2][^4]

Former Fire Lookout Sites (FFLOS) describes a two-story, 10-by-10-foot cab nicknamed a "smokehouse," built there in 1942.[^1][^2] WillhiteWeb reads the 1942 paperwork differently.[^3] It says the Army approved the summit as an observation post that year, and that the DNR did not follow through on a lookout. A 14-by-32-foot portable cabin was built in its place.[^3]

The post was active from January 5 to October 16, 1943.[^4] The only lookout firelookout.com names is Albert Miller, for the years 1947 to 1952.[^2]

In 1960 a DNR crew built a wooden tower there, which FFLOS and firelookout.com give as 20 feet tall. WillhiteWeb is unsure whether it was 20 feet or 40.[^1][^2][^3]

In August 1969 The Oregonian reported that Green Knob was one of five Battle Ground-district lookouts to be discontinued and disposed of after air patrol took over.[^4] One plan was to move the tower to the DNR district headquarters, cut it from 40 feet to 10, and rebuild the cab as an interpretive center.[^4]

FFLOS says the tower was removed in 1974. firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year, and WillhiteWeb says its Lookout Inventory suggests the same.[^1][^2][^3] WillhiteWeb has no word on whether the interpretive-center plan went ahead.[^3]

Undated WillhiteWeb visit notes say trees have filled in around the summit, that roughly half the view is still open, and that the site has been somewhat preserved and has kept some of its character.[^3] Its photo captions show lookout parts near the summit and anchor bolts.[^3] A gated road off Forest Road 54 enters a Weyerhaeuser permit area, and roads reach to within about 200 feet of the summit.[^3]
