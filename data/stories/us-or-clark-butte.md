Clark Butte is a former lookout site in Lane County, Oregon, on the Willamette National Forest, about 24 miles east-southeast of Springfield.[^1][^2] In 1912 the site got two crow's nest tree platforms and a camp.[^1][^3]

In July 1920 H.E. Vincent, a deputy supervisor, went to Fall Creek to look at Clark's Butte and Little Cowhorn Mountain. He was there to help make a "seen" map of the ground each lookout could see. Supervisors would use it to judge coverage and to pin down where fires were.[^3] By 1922 C.A. Logan of Fall Creek had worked the lookout on Clark's Butte and Little Cowhorn Mountain for several years.[^3]

The site changed over time. A frame cabin was added in 1944, which the Forest Lookouts page describes as a non-standard, gable-roofed lookout house.[^1][^3] A new lookout house went up in 1955.[^3]

Its final use, for emergencies, came in the 1960s. The Forest Lookouts page says the lookout structure was removed in 1968.[^1][^2][^3] The register and firelookout.com both say that as of 1996 one of the crow's nests still stood on the site.[^1][^2] The Oregon list of lookouts marks the site as abandoned rather than gone.[^6]

The Forest Service trailhead page, last updated March 28, 2025, lists the trailhead as open. It says the trailhead provides access to the Clark Butte Trail (#3456.1).[^4] The directions start in Lowell and run 2 miles north on the Jasper-Lowell Road, turn right at the Unity Bridge, then go 12.8 miles on Big Fall Creek Road (Forest Road 18).[^4] An undated hiker's trip report says the writer stopped at Clark Butte to hide a geocache and could find no trace of the two crow's nests.[^5]

I did not find a source that describes whether the remaining crow's nest still stands. Do not try to climb any platform that remains.
