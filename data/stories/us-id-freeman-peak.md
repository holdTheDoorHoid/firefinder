Freeman Peak stands at 7,298 feet in Idaho County, about six miles east-northeast of the Moose Creek Ranger Station.[^1] A lookout was posted there years before the cabin went up.[^3] A 1916 item from the *Idaho County Free Press*, quoted on one lookout-history site, reports that Billie Hartman, the lookout on Freeman's Peak, had come down to the station a few days earlier.[^3] Idaho Fire Lookouts gives 1919 as a staffing year and names Frank Freeman.[^2]

A log cabin went up near water in 1921, and a sighting instrument called an alidade was placed on the summit.[^3] In 1923 that cabin was moved to the peak and given a cupola.[^3] A University of Idaho document gives 1923 as the build year for a log cabin with a cupola.[^5] Three lookout sites put a cabin with a cupola on the south point in 1926, and one of them credits that date to a source it calls Kresek.[^1][^2][^3]

Sources disagree on when the cabin was destroyed in the mid-1950s. The Forest Service list and one lookout-history site say 1954, while Idaho Fire Lookouts and firelookout.com say 1956.[^4][^3][^2][^1] In 1957 administration of the site moved from the Bitterroot National Forest to the Nez Perce National Forest.[^3]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as a hike-in.[^2] An undated University of Idaho document says that only one of the forest's 20 log-cabin lookouts with cupolas remains, Sourdough.[^5] No source I found describes what, if anything, is left on the summit today, or whether the ground is open to visitors.
