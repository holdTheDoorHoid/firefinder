Whatcom Peak was a lookout on the north summit of Lookout Mountain, about seven miles southeast of Bellingham, Washington.[^1][^2]

The tower went up in 1930, according to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register, which describes an 80-foot wooden tower with a 7-by-7-foot cab and a house.[^3] WillhiteWeb dates construction to 1931. It argues that a June 1931 date stamp on a photo was written over with 1930.[^4] A Washington Forest Fire Association annual report, quoted on a lookout history site, gives the height as 87 feet. The same report puts the tower at 80 feet from ground level to the floor and 89 feet overall.[^5]

Whatcom and Skagit County fire patrol crews did all the framing and erecting.[^5] The bents were built from single trees felled less than half a mile away and dragged into position.[^5] They were braced on the ground and then raised, and donated wire rope guyed the structure.[^5] A single ladder served as the way up.[^5]

The Aircraft Warning Service also used the site.[^5] Its record gives a May 21, 1942 activation and names the Bellingham Filter Center.[^5] The station, called Yoke 0-8, was inactivated on October 16, 1943.[^5] The state kept the site for fire detection, and a 12-by-20-foot garage was built there with Aircraft Warning Service funds.[^5]

The tower and house are gone.[^3] FireLookout.com also lists the site as gone.[^1] SummitPost says the Department of Natural Resources later destroyed the tower.[^2]

Both summit areas of Lookout Mountain remain open to the public. Whatcom County designated the Lookout Mountain Preserve, which has more than four miles of trails.[^2] To reach the former site, hike or bike the main road about four miles to a Y intersection, then turn right and go 1.5 miles.[^4] WillhiteWeb reports that both former lookout summits now carry communication towers and provide little in the way of views.[^4] No source describes what, if anything, remains of the tower at the summit.
