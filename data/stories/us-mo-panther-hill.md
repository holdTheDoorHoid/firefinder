In 1938, 1,187.9 acres were obtained for a towersite on Panther Hill, close to Ellington in Reynolds County.[^1] In 1940 the Conservation Commission acquired 109.93 acres to erect a 66-foot (20.1 m) wooden fire tower.[^1] By August 1941 the Civilian Conservation Corps had finished a fire tower of wood on the hill.[^1] A Franklin County newspaper, quoted on the same page, gave its height as 68 feet (20.7 m) to the lookout floor and said it overlooked Deer Run State Forest.[^1]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1963 portrays it as a four-legged tower of wood, with a cabin for observers near its summit, and gives an overall height of about 60 feet (18 m).[^1] The 1941 figure is measured to the lookout floor and the 1963 figure is overall, so the two are not directly comparable.[^1]

A man who grew up roughly a mile from the tower, and later spent 33 years working for the department, recalls it as a 65-foot (20 m) wooden tower with a walkway encircling the cab.[^2] The same article notes that most Missouri towers were steel, and that early towers were built of wood.[^2]

The site also had a residence, and annual reports mention an extra bedroom added in 1956-57 and repairs after lightning damage in 1957-58.[^1] The same man recalls that his father spent many years on fire duty with the department, and that by about age 14 he was manning the tower whenever a crew went out on a fire run.[^2]

Annual reports say a new fire tower went up at Panther Hill in 1973.[^1]

The department's 2017 management plan for Logan Creek Conservation Area lists a Panther Hill tower and a fire tower parking lot among the area's existing infrastructure.[^3] The area sits north and west of Ellington, and the department lists Routes F, Y and 106 as ways in.[^4] The 1963 survey description also gives road directions from Ellington to the tower.[^1]

No source found says what condition the tower is in today, and none says whether its cab is open to climbers. Treat it as a look-from-the-ground site unless posted signs say otherwise.
