The Bowser lookout was built in 1946.[^1] Work on the site did not start until June, but the cabin was occupied in early July.[^1] A bulldozed tractor road led to the site.[^1] Lumber came from what was left of an old A.S.W. camp, the windows came from the property clerk in Victoria, and the district furnished the hardware and shingles.[^1]

A November 1946 Forest Branch News Letter item, reproduced on the Canada Lookouts page, places the lookout in the Vancouver Forest District.[^1] The page's heading names Nanaimo as the area.[^1] Wikipedia places the community of Bowser in the Regional District of Nanaimo.[^2]

The cabin was about 4.3 m (14 ft) square.[^1] Bunks, tables, cupboards and a stove ran along the four walls beneath the windows.[^1] The fire finder sat in the middle of the room, which kept a clear view in every direction.[^1] The lookoutman did almost all of the building himself, in about twenty man-days of labour.[^1] The newsletter put the cost at about $100 and called it a temporary lookout.[^1]

The lookoutman was Charles Egg, a recently discharged veteran who was still affected by illness he had contracted in service.[^1] He and his family were comfortably housed in the building.[^1] In June 1947 the Daily Colonist reported Mr. and Mrs. Neil Magee on duty at the lookout, as the Canada Lookouts page quotes it.[^1]

No source I could read describes what remains at the site today, or whether the public can reach it.
