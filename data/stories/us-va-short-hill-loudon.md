The Short Hill fire tower stood on Short Hill Mountain, a ridge of the Blue Ridge in Loudoun County, Virginia, south of Hillsboro.[^1][^3]

The first record we found of the tower is a newspaper item dated April 4, 1937, quoted on the Forest Lookouts site.[^1] It says a slight fire had burned two acres on the Loudoun side of the Blue Ridge, and that the forest warden moved quickly to put a watchman in the fire tower on Short Hill Mountain.[^1] The new watchman was Hirst H. Potts.[^1] The item said that from a height of 120 feet, Potts would be able to survey a broad area of mountain land.[^1]

Forest Lookouts lists the Virginia Department of Forestry as the agency.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list shows the tower as gone.[^2]

Wikipedia says the National Park Service owns only a small part of the mountain, and that the rest is privately owned and for the most part undeveloped.[^3] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains at the tower site, or whether the public can reach it.[^1][^2][^3]
