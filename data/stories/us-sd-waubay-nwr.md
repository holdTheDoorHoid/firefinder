Waubay National Wildlife Refuge was created in 1935 as a place for migratory birds to breed, during the Depression and a severe drought.[^3][^4] The tower stands about seven miles north-northwest of Waubay, in Day County.[^1]

The refuge brochure says the Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower in 1935.[^4] A marker on the refuge entrance road says a local CCC crew finished its work on the tower.[^3] The same crew also put up a stone picnic shelter and cleared away hundreds of drought-killed trees for use as fence posts.[^3] According to the brochure, the tower was at first a lookout where refuge staff kept watch for wildfires.[^4]

One listing calls it a steel lookout tower and gives the year as 1935.[^2] The brochure puts the height at 110 feet above ground and says the climb is 133 steps.[^4]

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service governs the refuge.[^6]

Today the refuge welcomes visitors during daylight hours, seven days a week, and the brochure invites them to climb the tower.[^5][^4] I found no recent report on the tower's condition.
