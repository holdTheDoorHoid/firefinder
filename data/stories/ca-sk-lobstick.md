A note in The Blue Jay, the journal of the Saskatchewan Natural History Society, recounts a visit to Lobstick Fire Tower, in the northeastern section of the Pasquia Hills.[^1] The same printed page carries the society's notice of its October 1971 annual meeting, so the note dates from about that year.[^1]

The Firetowers of Saskatchewan list places the tower at about 53.66 N, 102.01 W.[^2] The Canada Lookouts index names it too.[^3]

The towermen list gives Clara Norum at Lobstick from 1999 to 2002, and notes that Lobstick switched to aerial detection.[^4] It also lists Harold Barillo as relief at Mile 13, Lobstick and Porcupine.[^4]

The Department of Natural Resources began putting up towers through the province's north roughly fifty miles apart, a gap chosen so that towermen could talk to one another by radio. That is the general pattern of the network, not a record of this site.[^5]

No source reached describes the Lobstick site today, so it is not known whether a tower or other structure remains, or whether the public can visit.[^2][^3]
