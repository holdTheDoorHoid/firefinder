Smith Ferry is a fire lookout tower in the piney woods of Tyler County, in East Texas, built and run as part of the Texas Forest Service's county-level fire-detection network.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Texas register places it near 30.968, -94.327 and lists the tower as still standing.[^2]

Beyond that, little specific to Smith Ferry has turned up. No construction date, design, height, or staffing record for this particular tower appears in the sources checked for this entry, and it is not listed in the National Historic Lookout Register or the Forest Fire Lookout Society's register of Texas towers. More broadly, lookout towers went up across East Texas's timber counties in the early-to-mid twentieth century so that fire spotters could watch for smoke from a fixed, elevated point instead of climbing trees; many were later retired as the Texas Forest Service shifted to airplane patrols, and were "used from the thirties until they were phased out in the l970s."[^3] Whether Smith Ferry's own history followed that same arc, and what became of it in the decades since, is not documented in what is available here.

For now the tower is recorded as standing, with no trail, rental program, or public access noted. It is not a destination to visit on the strength of what is currently known; the record is a placeholder more than a history.
