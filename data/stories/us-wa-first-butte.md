First Butte Lookout stands on a summit in the Okanogan National Forest, about 11 miles north-northeast of Winthrop, Washington.[^1][^2] The Forest Service built the lookout in 1938. Its 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab, with a catwalk, sits on a 28-foot timber tower.[^1][^2]

The record is not entirely settled on the earliest date. A 1931 newspaper item, quoted on a lookout-history site, describes a house and tower built for a lookout station on the same peak.[^4] The register and the Methow Valley association both date the current structure to 1938, so an earlier station may have stood there first.[^1][^3]

For most of its working life the lookout was staffed each fire season. In 1942 the Forest Service staffed it year-round as a World War II aircraft warning station, with two people assigned to each station on 12-hour shifts.[^1][^3] That year the ground house, originally a garage or woodshed, was turned into sleeping quarters for spotters watching for enemy aircraft.[^3] Staffing ran every season through 1997.[^2][^3] In 1998 the lookout was used only for emergencies, and in 1999 it was staffed five days a week again.[^2][^3]

In July 2021 the Cub Creek 2 fire left heavy burn damage across much of the summit.[^3] The ground house was lost, but the lookout came through.[^3] Earlier in 2021, before the fire, volunteers from the National Forest Foundation and Filson gave the tower a fresh coat of paint.[^3] The lookout joined the National Historic Lookout Register in 2000, and the register still describes it as an active detection site.[^1][^3]

The sources also disagree on elevation. The register gives 5,417 feet, while the firelookout page gives 5,491 feet.[^1][^2]

Today the lookout is still used for emergencies.[^2][^3] None of these sources describes public visits or overnight rental, so treat it as a working Forest Service site rather than a place to climb.
