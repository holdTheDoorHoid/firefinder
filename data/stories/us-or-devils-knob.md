Devils Knob, a former lookout site in the Umpqua National Forest, lies about 24 miles east-southeast of Canyonville, in Douglas County, Oregon.[^1][^2] A lookout house went up there in 1912, and the Oregon Lookouts history page calls it the first lookout building on the Umpqua National Forest.[^3]

The sources agree on 1912 but not on the next change. The register says a cupola cabin replaced the first house in 1922. The history page says a cupola was added to the house in 1927.[^1][^3]

In 1914 a newspaper reported that the forest planned to use Devils Knob as one of three principal lookout stations.[^3] By 1919 F.A. McMillan was working there as the forestry lookout, and in 1920 his wife also served at the station.[^3] In 1929 Jake Smith was the lookout.[^3]

A 1933 letter from a CCC camp in Douglas County describes the man in charge, who located fires by calling a second lookout for his bearing and marking where the two lines crossed.[^3]

In 1936 the old house was torn down to make way for a new 14-by-14-foot L-4 cab, set on six-foot concrete pillars.[^1][^3] The first house had been built of hewn logs.[^3]

The register says the L-4 cab was removed in 1962, and the history page says the lookout structure was removed that year by burning it on site.[^1][^3] FireLookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2]

No source we read describes the site today or says whether it is open to visitors, so ask the Umpqua National Forest before going.
