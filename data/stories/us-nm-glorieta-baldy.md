Glorieta Baldy Lookout stands in the Santa Fe National Forest near La Cueva, about 10 miles east of Santa Fe.[^2][^4] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1940.[^4] Its design is an Aermotor MC-24 tower with a live-in wooden cab, 14 feet by 14 feet and without a catwalk.[^1]

The National Historic Lookout Register ranks it among the best surviving examples of the Aermotor MC-24 design.[^1] The tower was added to the National Register of Historic Places on January 27, 1988.[^2][^3] Dave Lorenz nominated it to the lookout register, which entered it as US 709 on December 7, 2007.[^1]

The register does not say who staffed the lookout or when it last watched for fires.[^1]

In September 2017 the Associated Press reported that the tower had been out of service for a long time, and that a support group for the forest was collecting up to $100,000 to fix it up as a rental.[^4] As of September 2017, Wikipedia said the tower was in poor repair.[^2] Work was to be paid for by Friends of the Santa Fe National Forest, a nonprofit, and the tower was to join the forest's cabin rental program.[^2] Forest archaeologist Mike Bremer said a restored tower would let visitors see how it looked when the Civilian Conservation Corps built it.[^4] Bill Zunkel of the nonprofit Friends of the Santa Fe National Forest said the tower must be shielded from lightning and bad weather.[^4]

A Campflare listing, which carries no date, says the lookout is shut until further notice and that the site is for day use only, with no overnight camping.[^5] The listing also says Forest Road 375 usually closes from early September until spring, and that the rough road is a poor bet for low-clearance vehicles or trailers.[^5] I found no source confirming that a cabin rental has opened, so treat the tower as off limits until the forest says otherwise. The Pecos/Las Vegas Ranger District is named as a cooperator on the register and is the place to ask about its current status.[^1]
