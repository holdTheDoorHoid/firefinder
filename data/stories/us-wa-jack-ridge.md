Jack Ridge is a former fire lookout site in the Wenatchee National Forest, in Chelan County, Washington.[^1] It sits about 12 miles west of Leavenworth.[^2] The Forest Service administered the lookout.[^1]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites Register dates the site's L-4 cab to 1931.[^1] WillhiteWeb says the cab stood on a 15-foot tower.[^3]

A July 1932 Leavenworth Echo item, reprinted on Forest Lookouts, said E.S. Gaines was leaving to serve as lookout and patrolman at Jack Ridge.[^4] On July 19 that year a Wenatchee World item reported that stations on Lorraine Peak and Jack Ridge had been set up, filling out lookout coverage of the Icicle watershed.[^4] Those 1932 notices describe the station as newly set up, which sits uneasily with the 1931 date.[^1][^3][^4] In June 1933 the Echo named Richard Siders as the Jack Ridge lookout-fireman.[^4] Panorama photos were taken from the site on August 14, 1934.[^1][^4]

The sources disagree about how the cab ended. The Register says it was removed in 1967.[^1] Fire Lookout says it was destroyed that year.[^2] WillhiteWeb says it was removed or burned, and notes evidence of a burn on the ground.[^3]

The most recent account I found is a Washington Trails Association trip report from July 2026. Its writers found old footings, melted glass and rusty nails at the top of the peak.[^5] The report says the trees have grown up, but the views still open out in most directions.[^5] WillhiteWeb's photo captions also list two footings and burned tower posts.[^3]

The route is a hike. WillhiteWeb follows Icicle River Road out of Leavenworth to a signed Jack Creek trailhead, then describes a loop of about 13 miles that climbs the Jack Ridge trail to the knob where the lookout stood.[^3] The trip report calls the Jack Ridge trail the rougher of its two routes, with many blowdowns across the path.[^5]

I found no source that says the site is closed. None describes a standing tower or cab, so there is nothing to climb.[^1][^2][^3][^5]
