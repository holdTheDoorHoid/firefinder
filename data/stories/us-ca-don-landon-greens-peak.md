Don Landon Lookout sits on Greens Peak, on a ridge south of Eagle Lake in Lassen County, at about 7,145 feet.[^1]

Its early history is hard to pin down because the sources do not agree. A June 1948 newspaper item said a new 30-foot steel tower would probably go up on Green's Peak between Susanville and Eagle Lake.[^3] The National Historic Lookout Register says a structure was erected on the peak in 1948, while a Lassen County history site says the lookout opened on September 4, 1949, and that it was in reality a remodeled naval tower.[^1][^2] The site now covers ground that Shaffer Mountain's lookout once guarded; the register says that lookout closed in the early 1950s.[^1]

A 1952 newspaper item says the lookout was dedicated to Donald A. Landon, a former member of the forestry service killed in action in Europe in 1944, and that work on the new tower had begun two years earlier.[^3] The current tower dates to 1974, when it replaced the older structure, according to the register.[^1] A June 1979 Lassen County Times item instead says a new tower replaced a World War II airport control tower that had been used there since the war.[^3] The tower plan, 880-1A, matches Fredonyer's, and the cab is essentially a 732-6A design.[^1]

Peakbagging, citing a photo taken in August 2002, says the lookout is usually staffed during fire season.[^4] The register notes large fire scars near the peak.[^1]

The register listed the lookout on November 27, 2019.[^1] An undated copy of a CAL FIRE lookout list also places it among the Lassen-Modoc lookouts.[^5]

The register says the site is not easy for the public to reach, about four miles from paved road.[^1] A photo caption on Peakbagging shows a sign near the lookout listing visiting hours.[^4] The sources do not give current staffing or access rules, so check with CAL FIRE's Lassen-Modoc Unit before planning a visit.[^1]
