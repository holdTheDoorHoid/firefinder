The tower stands on the summit of Culp's Hill, on the Gettysburg battlefield.[^3] Confederate troops attacked the hill on July 2 and 3, 1863, and Union defenders held it at various times.[^3] The park service says most of the battlefield is visible from the top.[^1]

The date is disputed. The park service dates completion to 1896 and credits the Gettysburg Battlefield Commission.[^1] A 2008 post from Gettysburg Daily gives 1895 and says it was first built so the military could study the battlefield.[^2] Stone Sentinels and a nearby marker credit the War Department, and Stone Sentinels says five towers went up in the 1890s, three of which still exist.[^3][^4]

The ground around the hill was protected early. In 1864 the Gettysburg Battlefield Memorial Association bought land here. A marker calls it among the earliest battlefield lands ever set aside in the United States.[^4]

The park service describes a metal tower 60 feet high, with a covered platform on top.[^1] The only way up to the platform is seven flights of stairs.[^1]

Today the park service calls it a key spot for visitors to study the lay of the land.[^1] Visitors can park in a small lot that circles the top of the hill.[^3] I found no recent report on the tower's condition or its opening hours, so check with the park before you go.
