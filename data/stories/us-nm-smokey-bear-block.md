The Block lookout stood on the north side of the Capitan Mountains in Lincoln County, New Mexico, inside the Lincoln National Forest, at about 6,056 feet.[^1][^2] The Former Fire Lookout Sites register gives 1931 as its build year and calls it a ground house-type lookout.[^1] A 1957 newspaper item quoted on the Forest Lookouts site says Bob lived at an old station "long before the present tower was built." That item gives no build date, so the 1931 date rests on the register alone.[^2]

The same item says Mr. Latham, whom it also calls Bob, had served as the summertime fire lookout at Block for 25 years and was retiring in August 1957.[^2]

According to the register, the 1950 fire that Smokey Bear was rescued from was first seen from this lookout.[^1] A December 1959 item from The Sun in Flagstaff, Arizona, describes that fire as about 14,000 acres on the Capitan Ranger District, and says the smoke was first detected from the Block Lookout.[^2] The newspaper says the rescued cub is now recognized internationally as a symbol of forest fire prevention.[^2]

In December 1959 the Forest Service announced that Block Lookout would become Smokey Bear Lookout.[^2]

The register says the lookout stood on the site of a prehistoric pueblo and was removed so that the pueblo could be preserved.[^1] No source we read gives a removal date. The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the site as a gone ground lookout.[^3]

We found no source that describes what remains at the site today, or whether visitors may go there.
