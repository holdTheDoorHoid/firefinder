Sanger Peak is in Del Norte County, California.[^1] In July 1916 the Forest Service took on Worth Hamilton to work as lookout there.[^1] A 1919 item quoting a woman lookout's diary says the peak had no lookout tower, and the diary describes a tent at the station.[^1]

A cabin was packed in from Page Creek and built on the peak in 1926, and by September the lookout had moved into the finished house.[^1] A 1952 survey note calls the house a standard Forest Service design, roughly 14 feet square and 25 feet high.[^1]

In 1918 two women, Juanita Parker and Edna Cornell, were installed at the station near Takilma.[^1] In October 1943 an Aircraft Warning Service station on the peak was placed on inactive status but kept on the inventory, and the service had added sleeping quarters to the site.[^1] Dennis Bottel, an Oregon State University student, was employed as lookout in 1961 and was due to spend the 1962 summer there.[^1]

In 1957 a new flat-top tower measuring 15 by 15 feet was set to replace the old building.[^1] A 1959 California Division of Highways note says the original house was torn down.[^1] A photo page says the lookout was rebuilt in 1957.[^2]

Photos dated August 23, 2005, show what the page describes as the only surviving part of the lookout house.[^2] The Forest Service says the remains of the former lookout are at the top of the 5,862-foot peak.[^3] The approach is Forest Road 4803, reached from Highway 199 by Waldo Road or Knopki Creek Road.[^3] The Forest Service describes the 0.6-mile hike as moderate to strenuous, with steep, rocky stretches.[^3]
