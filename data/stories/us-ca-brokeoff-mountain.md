Brokeoff Mountain is the Tehama County high point. A lookout station was already on it in 1915, years before the cabin the register dates to 1924.[^1][^2]

A July 30, 1915 newspaper report says fire totally destroyed that station on a Wednesday evening.[^2] The station was a framed platform topped with a heavy canvas tent. Ranger Frank Hardin was away when the fire started, and the paper said the probable cause was live coals from his cook stove.[^2] The paper expected the station to be rebuilt, with a tent for shelter in the meantime.[^2]

The register dates a later cabin to 1924 and calls it a California Region 5 Plan 4AR ground cabin.[^1]

John Gransbury staffed the lookout for years after that.[^2] A 1934 newspaper said he had held the lookout job since 1924. A 1935 article said he had held the post for 11 years, going up about June 1 and coming down when the fall rains had removed the fire hazard.[^2] That article describes the station as a small building about 12 by 12 feet, fixed to the bare rock with heavy cables.[^2]

The height figures disagree. A 1933 newspaper gave 9,232 feet, a 1935 article said 9,400 feet, the register gives 9,127 feet for the site, and the Park Service trail listing runs up to 9,235 feet.[^2][^1][^3]

In August 1929 Forest Ranger Hudson, in charge of the station, reported an eruption from the main peak of Mount Lassen, with smoke and steam spouting 6,000 feet into the air.[^2] In June 1935 John Gransbury reported that his tower rocked violently from earth shocks near Lassen Peak.[^2]

The register says the tower was demolished in 1956 and that nothing remains at the site.[^1] The Forest Lookouts page gives a different account, saying a trail maintenance crew burned the lookout that year.[^2]

Lassen Volcanic National Park now manages the mountain.[^1][^3] Its trail starts at the Brokeoff Mountain parking lot near the southwest entrance and runs 7.4 miles round trip, about six hours, over rocky, forested and alpine ground.[^3] The trail is considered one of the toughest in the park, and the view from the summit takes in the old Brokeoff Volcano caldera.[^3] With no lookout left to see, a visit is a hike for the mountain and its views.[^1][^3]
