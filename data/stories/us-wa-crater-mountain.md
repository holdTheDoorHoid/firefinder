Crater Mountain sits in Whatcom County, on the Mt. Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest.[^1] HistoryLink places it about 30 miles east-northeast of Marblemount, in the North Cascades.[^2]

The summit began as a camp in 1929, and George Hadley is named as its first watcher.[^3][^4] An L-4 cab went up on the primary summit in 1932.[^1][^3] Before the cab, a watcher who spotted smoke sighted it on the firefinder, then walked 7.5 miles down to a cabin with a phone line to Marblemount.[^4] Late in summer, getting water meant descending the steep north side to Jerry Glacier for chunks of ice, which were hauled back up to melt.[^4] Supplies came up by cable, and the last stretch was on foot.[^4]

Records list Earle and Bonnie St. Aubin in 1951 and Gary Snyder in 1952.[^3][^4] HistoryLink says Snyder spent six weeks on the mountain that year.[^2] In 1955 Tommy Buller nailed the old cab shut and moved the firefinder to a newer L-4 cab on a lower summit a mile east.[^3][^4]

The register gives 1968 as the year the site was abandoned and 1973 as the year the cabin burned.[^1][^3] It dates the newer east cab to about 1950, while the other two pages say 1955.[^1][^3][^4]

The register lists the site at 7,761 feet, while two other pages give 8,128 feet.[^1][^3][^4]

WillhiteWeb's photo captions document what is left: one phone pole still standing near treeline, and tramway remnants near the cliff section.[^4] The register places the site on Forest Service land, but none of the sources read says whether the summit is open to visitors.[^1] WillhiteWeb describes the climb as seven miles one way, with about 6,400 feet of gain and mostly easy but exposed class 3 scrambling, with perhaps one low class 4 move.[^4]
