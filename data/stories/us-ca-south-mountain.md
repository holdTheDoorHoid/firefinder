South Mountain Lookout stands near Santa Paula in Ventura County, California, at about 2,334 feet.[^1][^2] The register says the Ventura County Fire Department built no other fire tower.[^1]

A newspaper reported on May 31, 1933 that the county's newest lookout station would go on the ridge of South Mountain.[^2] The first set-up was to be a tent with an operator and a telephone, with a more modern station planned for later.[^2] By July 21, Civilian Conservation Corps crews were working on a new trail to the summit, and a tower was being put up.[^2] A later history from the county fire district says the district built its own concrete-block tower that year.[^3] The newspaper credits the corps with the trail work, and the history credits the district with the tower.[^2][^3] Both may be partly right, and the sources do not settle it.

The register gives the cab as 14 feet on each side, set on a 10-foot brick base that it calls unusual.[^1] The district history describes the tower as concrete block, so the sources disagree on the material.[^3]

The tower spotted fires for years.[^1][^2] On August 28, 1957, the lookout reported smoke, and Fillmore firemen were sent to a grass and sagebrush blaze.[^2] A brush fire at Sence Ranch took the roof off the tower in 1967.[^3] The district ran the tower in every fire season through 1974.[^3] The register dates fire detection use to at least 1962, which is consistent with the district's 1974 end date.[^1]

The register says detection work has ended and the tower is kept to hold radio equipment.[^1] A Forest Fire Lookout Association list describes it as converted to a communications facility.[^4] The register added it on June 19, 2010, as US 885, CA 95.[^1]

I found no visitor or access notes for this site. Since the tower holds radio equipment, do not assume the public can go up it. Check with the Ventura County Fire Department before you plan a visit.
