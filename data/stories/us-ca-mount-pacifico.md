Mount Pacifico, which modern maps call Pacifico Mountain, is a peak in the San Gabriel Mountains of the Angeles National Forest.[^1][^2] The register dates a fire lookout here to 1932, and says the listing rests on one photo and a 1932 Los Angeles Times story.[^2] Angeles Adventures says that newspaper story is all it knows about the lookout.[^3]

Unlike the other lookouts built in the San Gabriels around that time, this one had no cab.[^3] The 1932 story describes steel rungs leading up to a small platform at the top.[^3]

Elevations differ. The register gives the site 7,114 feet, and Wikipedia gives the peak 7,129 feet.[^1][^2]

The register says its use for fire detection is unconfirmed, and that the structure is not listed in a 1986 inventory of fire lookouts.[^2] It also lists the removal as unknown.[^2]

Today, Angeles Adventures describes two metal posts cemented onto boulders at the summit, with drill holes and bolted hardware nearby.[^3] The writer had not yet worked out how these remains fit together, so their link to the lookout is unconfirmed.[^3] Wikipedia says the Pacific Crest Trail crosses the mountain and is its main route to the summit, a strenuous hike of about 11.9 miles.[^1] We found no source that says whether the lookout site itself is open to visitors.
