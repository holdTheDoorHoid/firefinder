In May 1930 the state's Department of Conservation and Development announced a new fire lookout for the western part of Columbus County, near Chadbourn.[^1] The plan called for a steel tower 100 feet (30.5 m) high, with an inside stairway and an enclosed cab at the top. From there the lookout was expected to see about 15 miles in every direction.[^1] The first notice said the tower would probably be placed about two miles east of town.[^1] The completion notices of 1931 put it about a mile east, beside State Highway 20.[^1]

Completion was reported in August 1931.[^1] A 1931 notice numbered it 22nd among the 125 towers planned for a statewide system.[^1] C. R. Bailey of Chadbourn gave the state the land for the site.[^1]

The North Carolina page of Forest Lookouts lists the Columbus County tower under the North Carolina Division of Forest Resources.[^2] The tnlandforms.us list of North Carolina fire towers also includes a Chadbourn entry in Columbus County.[^3]

Today no source I found says whether the tower still stands, what remains at the site, or whether the spot is open to visitors. Ask the state forest service before you go looking for it.
