The Civilian Conservation Corps built the lookout on Flagg Mountain in 1935, on land that is now part of the Weogufka State Forest in Coosa County, Alabama.[^1] The register describes a 50-foot stone tower with walls two to three feet thick and a cab 12 feet by 12 feet on top.[^1] Timbers that were once set in a crosshatch pattern into the rock have since given way to inlaid stone, according to the register.[^1]

The Alabama Forestry Commission staffed the cab until 1989.[^1] A 2017 visit report says the lookout was not staffed then, though a full-time caretaker lived on site, and that the stairs were unsafe.[^1]

The commission owns the mountain and leased trail work on it to the Alabama Hiking Trail Society.[^2] The Coosa County News reports that the tower had been closed to the public for more than 20 years, and that it reopened in June 2022 with a gathering of more than 200 people.[^2] Earlier leaseholders, the Coosa County Cooperators, had replaced the roofs on the tower and five of the cabins.[^2] A well-known hiker also became the mountain's full-time caretaker.[^2]

The Alabama Trails Foundation now describes the stairs as newly renovated, so the 2017 finding is out of date.[^3] It says the tower area is open to the public for picnics and invites visitors to climb the stairs to the cab.[^3] Two CCC-built cabins can be rented overnight, and all rental income goes to the Alabama Forestry Commission.[^3] The 170-mile Alabama Pinhoti Trail begins on the mountain.[^3]
