The Striped Peak lookout stood on a lesser summit southeast of the main peak, about 11 miles west-northwest of Port Angeles.[^1][^2] Willhite says it may first have gone by Crescent Bay, but by 1942 people were calling it Striped Peak.[^1]

Sources disagree on the build year: Willhite and the Washington Lookouts page both quote a 1937 Division of Forestry annual report saying a lookout was erected at Crescent Bay,[^1][^4] while the former lookout sites register and firelookout.com both give 1940.[^3][^2]

The lookout's reported height is 53 feet, with a 7-by-7-foot cab on top; a cabin below may have housed the lookout.[^1] A survey party in 1957 suggested a height of 70 feet, though Willhite notes that the figure may have been influenced by an earlier party's report.[^1]

During World War II the site became an Air Warning Service station named "William 9-1." A record gives the activation as March 9, 1942, with the Port Angeles Filter Center.[^4] The service used structures the state already owned, and a 12-by-20-foot garage was built with Air Warning Service funds.[^4][^1] The state kept the site after the service ended.[^1]

Newspaper items quoted on the Washington Lookouts page show that observers were posted here in the 1950s and 1960.[^4] In August 1956 the state reported no fires, and the observer said the Lake Crescent lightning looked like the kind that flashes inside clouds, with few strikes to the ground.[^4] Maxine Wanzer was manning the lookout in 1957, and Myra Vanderhoof was stationed there in June 1960.[^4]

Sources disagree on how the tower ended: Willhite and firelookout.com say it was destroyed in 1967, while the former lookout sites register says it was removed that year.[^1][^2][^3] In February 1968 a Port Angeles newspaper reported vandalism at the transmission station at Striped Peak, where the transmitter house and lookout station had locks broken off their doors.[^4]

A 1974 survey party confirmed the lookout was gone, though its footings were still in place.[^1] By 1981 a later party reported the footings gone and a chain-link fence around an FAA radio facility.[^1]

Willhite says the FAA radio facility was still there in 2016.[^1] His page also places some footings and timbers over the embankment on the south side, which conflicts with the 1981 note.[^1]

The mountain lies in the Salt Creek Recreational Area, which encompasses Striped Peak and has hiking routes to it.[^5] Willhite's photo captions name a Striped Peak trail from Salt Creek County Park, and his driving directions from Freshwater Bay Road end at a gate.[^1]
