The Perkins Memorial Lookout is a stone tower on the summit of Bear Mountain, inside Bear Mountain State Park in the New York Highlands.[^1][^3] The Palisades Interstate Park Commission owns the site, which lies west of the Hudson River near West Point.[^1]

The register says the tower was dedicated to honor a chairman of the commission, and that it was built between 1931 and 1934.[^1] A 1934 newspaper names that man as George W. Perkins and calls him the commission's first president.[^3] The same newspaper says the stone was hand-quarried on the mountain.[^3] The register's attached report says the tower was completed and opened in 1933 under Civilian Conservation Corps administration.[^2]

A steel fire tower stood on the summit before it. The attached report dates that tower to 1923, gives its height as 60 feet, and says park rangers staffed it. The report says it stood until 1930, when it was removed, possibly to make way for the stone tower, which was built about 300 feet to the west.[^2] A 2016 visit report puts the old tower's footings 500 feet from the stone tower.[^1] Wikipedia says a 60-foot steel tower was built in 1928 and taken down seven years later to make room for the stone tower, so the sources disagree on when it went up.[^4]

The sources also disagree on the stone tower's height. The register gives 40 feet, the attached report 50 feet, and the 1934 newspaper says it rose "some sixty feet" above the summit.[^1][^2][^3] Its cab, which the register gives as 20 feet square, is still used by the park during periods of high fire danger, and the register says the tower is staffed as a fire lookout at those times.[^1] Wikipedia says the tower served as a weather station and fire lookout until 1953.[^4]

Visit reports on the register describe the tower as in good condition, and the observation deck was open to the public in 2016.[^1] The park maintains the site, signs the way to it, and radio repeater antennas are mounted on top.[^1] The Appalachian Trail passes by its base.[^2] Wikipedia says the Perkins family agreed in 1990 to fund its upkeep, and that the tower was later renovated and reopened to the public.[^4]

Check with the park about current hours before you visit, and do not climb the tower when it is closed.
