The Boulder Hill Fire Tower is a 100-foot steel lookout that now stands at the Forest History Center in Grand Rapids, Minnesota.[^1][^2] It began its working life on the Chippewa National Forest, near Bena.[^2][^3]

The Civilian Conservation Corps put it up in 1934, together with a towerman's cabin and a garage.[^1][^2] Its cab is 7 by 7 feet and made of metal.[^1]

The tower was still in use until the late 1960s.[^1] In 1976 it was dismantled and moved to Grand Rapids.[^3] The Register says it was closed to the public after the move, and that it was restored to how it first stood in 1997.[^1] The Register registered it on April 15, 1998, as US 270, MN 7.[^1]

The sources I read do not name any fires the tower watched, or the people who staffed it in its working years. Interpreters staff the tower today.[^1]

Visiting hours are where the sources differ. The Register says the tower is open every day from Memorial Day through October 15, and ranks it among the most climbed towers in the country.[^1] The Forest History Center's page says the tower keeps regular business hours and is shut from November through May for safety.[^2] It limits the climb to four people at a time, and climbers must be at least six years old and wear proper footwear.[^2] The climb covers 100 feet and 126 steps, and the tower shuts during thunderstorms, strong rains and high winds.[^2] Check current hours before you go.
