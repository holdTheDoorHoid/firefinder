Mount Cammerer is a stone fire lookout in the northeast part of Great Smoky Mountains National Park in Tennessee, near the Appalachian Trail.[^1] Tennessee Landforms also calls it White Rock, a rock tower along the trail.[^2]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built it between 1937 and 1939 from materials on the site.[^1][^3] The register describes a two-story octagonal stone building, while the 2014 state form describes stone-and-timber construction, so the two descriptions do not match exactly.[^1][^3]

The register says the lookout was used for fire detection only briefly and was abandoned in the 1960s.[^1] It joined the National Historic Lookout Register on April 25, 1994.[^1]

The Friends of the Smokies was founded in 1993, and restoring this tower in 1995 was its first project.[^5] In 2012 the Forest Fire Lookout Association gave the group a grant to repair weather and vandalism damage, including a first-story door and frame, hinges and lock, graffiti paint, and a rotten spruce log roof beam.[^4]

The register credits the Friends of Great Smoky Mountains National Park, the Panther Press and the National Park Service with restoring it.[^1] It also describes the lookout as a destination hikers like to reach, though the trip is tough.[^1]

In September 2016 twenty hikers made an 11.4-mile round trip to it on a Friends of the Smokies classic hike, and the group described the tower as giving a 360-degree view of the Smokies.[^5] The 2014 state form adds that visitors are often cautioned about the risks of climbing surviving fire towers.[^3]

I did not find a closure notice, and I could not confirm the tower's condition after 2016. Check the park's trail and tower notices before you go, and stay off the tower if it is closed or looks unsafe.
