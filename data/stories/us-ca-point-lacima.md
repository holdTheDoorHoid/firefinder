The Point Lacima lookout stood on the North Dome of the Kettleman Hills, in Kings County.[^1] In 1935 the newly organized West Side Fire Protection District planned two lookout stations, and Point Lacima was one of them.[^1]

The station was set to go into service in May 1935. It was to be hooked up to the Avenal and Coalinga phone lines under a deal with several oil companies, and Mr. and Mrs. O.H. Upton of Hanford were to staff it around the clock.[^1] A telephone was installed at the station by June 1935 and could be reached through the Coalinga exchange.[^1]

In 1936 the station was due to open on May 5. The county fire warden, J.C. Griswold, announced it, and Guy Knapp was to be in charge during fire hazard season.[^1] The 1937 notice again named Knapp in charge and gave the same May 5 opening. The notice said there would be no truck at the site that year, and that the lookout would phone fire locations to the Cottonwood Pass and Kettleman City stations instead.[^1]

The Forest Lookout Association's California list marks Point Lacima as gone and gives its type as unknown.[^2]

I found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it. The 1936 notice places the lookout on the North Dome of the hills.[^1] Wikipedia says the hills are the location of the Kettleman North Dome oil field, which occupies their northernmost part.[^3][^4]
