The National Historic Lookout Register says the Hayden Hill lookout was built in 1940 as a Region 5 Plan BC-301 cabin on a 10-foot enclosed timber tower.[^1] The site was approved for administrative use in 1912, and the register says a structure stood there by 1915.[^1][^2] A July 1923 California District News Letter item, quoted by Forest Lookouts, mentions a visit to a new lookout house on Hayden Hill.[^2] The sources do not explain how that house relates to the 1940 cab.

The lookout kept working into the 1940s.[^2] A 1943 item quoted by Forest Lookouts mentions a man's wife caring for him at the Lookout Station at Hayden Hill.[^2] The same collection credits the lookout with 13 "first discoveries" between 1942 and 1946, the fire term for spotting a blaze before anyone else.[^2]

In March 1991 the Lassen County Times reported that the county supervisors had approved a request from a museum board member to move the Hayden Hill tower to the Bieber Museum grounds.[^2] The tower was still in operation then, and the board member said it had been declared a historical site.[^2] A new tower had to be built first so that coverage would not lapse.[^2] Amax Gold was to handle the dismantling, relocating and reassembly.[^2]

The register says only the cab was reassembled at the museum, and that it stands there today.[^1] The tower was not reassembled there.[^1] The cab sits on the Big Valley Museum grounds in Bieber, and the register listed it on October 30, 2019.[^1] As of 1991, the register says, it was one of the best surviving examples in California of an L-101 tower with a BC-301 cab.[^1]
