Boulder Hill sits in the Black Hills National Forest in Pennington County, South Dakota, according to the Former Fire Lookout Sites Register.[^1][^2] A 30-foot steel tower was built there in the spring of 1939, with a 14-by-14-foot cab that gave the lookout living quarters.[^1][^2]

The sources give three different elevations for the hill: 5,175 feet in the register, 5,335 feet on firelookout.com, and 5,400 feet in a 1939 newspaper item.[^1][^2][^3]

A July 1939 newspaper item said the lookout was established to spot fires in the extensively used areas south of Rapid, Spring and Battle creeks.[^3] The register gives the same purpose.[^1]

The register says the tower was originally staffed by William Grover, who was to man it for the entire fire season.[^1] Firelookout.com lists Galen and Effie McCain for 1949, 1953 and 1955, and Ruth McCrorey for 1960 to 1963.[^2]

Two fires appear in the newspaper items. In September 1947 a lookout phoned the sheriff's office about white smoke north of the cement plant. It was a fire set to burn off grass near the railroad tracks, and a group of men kept it under control.[^3] In 1958 the lookout spotted a man-caused fire near Pactola Reservoir that burned only two acres.[^3]

The register says appropriations for the tower ended in 1961 and dates its removal to about that year.[^1] A March 1961 land order listed the Boulder Hill lookout among the sites it covered.[^3] Firelookout.com's staffing list runs to 1963, two years past the register's removal date.[^2][^1]

According to the register, only the concrete foundations of the tower and its stairs were left by 2022.[^1] Its photo, taken looking south, shows those foundations.[^1] No source we read says whether visitors can reach the site.[^1]
