The Oswald Dome fire tower went up in 1935 on Bean Mountain, the Polk County summit also called Oswald Dome.[^1][^2] The historical marker, erected by Cherokee National Forest, gives its height as 79 feet 6 inches.[^1] Tennessee Landforms describes it as an 80-foot tower.[^3]

In the 1960s, the heyday for fire towers in the forest, Oswald Dome was one of 17 towers used to spot fires.[^1] Lookouts worked with binoculars and radios, and neighboring lookouts could pinpoint a smoke's location by triangulation.[^1] Regular staffing of the forest's towers was discontinued by the late 1980s.[^1] The marker places that change alongside the growing use of spotter planes.[^1]

The marker dates the tower's dismantling and its removal from its original site to 2003.[^1] A segment of the structure and its cab were restored and set up at the Ocoee Whitewater Center in 2004.[^1][^5] Wikipedia says a fire tower was moved from the mountain there in 2004. That wording suggests the whole tower moved, but the marker describes only a segment with its cab, and Tennessee Landforms says only the top or cab section was relocated.[^2][^1][^3]

The lookout no longer stands on the summit.[^4] Southeast Tennessee's trail guide counts more than six communication towers on the summit now, and Wikipedia mentions radio and television transmitters.[^4][^2] The summit is reached by Oswald Dome Road, a Forest Service road that meets U.S. 64, and by two trails.[^2] The Oswald Dome Trail starts at Quinn Springs Recreation Area and covers nearly four miles with about 2,200 feet of climb. The guide recommends a pickup or SUV for the dirt road.[^4]

To see what remains, go to the Ocoee Whitewater Center, where the guide says the tower's top part is a draw.[^4]
