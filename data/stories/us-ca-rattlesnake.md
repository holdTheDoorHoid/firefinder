The Rattlesnake lookout stood on the northeast slope of Little Rattlesnake Mountain in Del Norte County, about 13 miles east-southeast of Crescent City.[^1]

A Siskiyou National Forest history from 1939, as quoted by Forest Lookouts, records a lookout cabin built in 1935.[^1]

A Coast and Geodetic Survey description from 1941 says the tower was wood and painted white. It was about 14 feet square at the top and about 25 feet high. A small garage and store shed stood about 150 feet east of it.[^1]

A May 1940 plan for guard placement described the area the post could see, which was mostly lightning country. It also gave suggested dates, described as a fair average of the position's past use.[^1]

Forest Lookouts also lists an Aircraft Warning Service station, "Mike 6-1," that was placed on inactive status in October 1943. The entry says no improvements were involved.[^1]

A USGS recovery note from 1977 reports that the tower is no longer there.[^1] The firelookout.org list also marks the site as gone.[^2]

Forest Lookouts lists the site under the Siskiyou National Forest and then the Six Rivers National Forest.[^1] Peakbagging lists it under Six Rivers.[^3] Its table calls the entry a lookout site, a term it uses where the building has been removed or its status is unknown.[^3]

No source we read describes the site today, so we cannot say what, if anything, remains there, or whether the public can reach it.
