The Okeechobee Fire Tower stands in Okeechobee County, Florida.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register's entry gives it as a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40 with 15 landings, and records a 2009 rehabilitation of the viewing cab.[^1] The Register does not give a build date, and I found none in the other sources I read.

A WPTV news report, undated in the copy I read, says that for half a century the county's fire watch tower has helped firefighters spot brush fires.[^2] It describes a climb of 81 steps and nine flights of stairs, and a view that reaches 30 miles.[^2] It also describes a Florida Forest Service ranger, Robert Smith, who spends up to eight hours in the tower during severe drought.[^2] The report does not say how the tower is staffed outside severe drought.[^2] At the top, a fire finder lets firefighters pin down a fire's exact location.[^2]

The Register names the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services as administrator, while the report refers to the Florida Forest Service.[^1][^2] The Register recorded the tower on July 24, 2016, as US 1066, FL 13, with Robert Spear as nominator.[^1]

Neither source reports the tower being closed, moved or torn down. Neither describes public access or visits. This is a working fire watch tower, so check with the Florida Forest Service before any visit, and do not climb it without permission.
