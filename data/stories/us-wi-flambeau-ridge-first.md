A fire lookout tower on Flambeau Ridge, which the 1931 to 1936 reports call Flambeau Mountain, was built in the Town of Birch Creek in Chippewa County.[^2] A newspaper report from October 1931 says the foundation had been laid for a 100-foot steel observation tower, and that the work formed part of the Wisconsin conservation commission's reforestation program.[^2] The plan called for a six-foot steel house at the top, with the station kept on duty day and night in dry weather.[^2]

The tower was in use by May 1932.[^2] A newspaper that month reported that the tower man installed there was on the job, and that he had signaled for help for fires starting near Foster Lake.[^2] The same report credits the lookout man's promptness with stopping another blaze before much damage was done.[^2]

In September 1935 a newspaper reported that the tower was being removed. The Conservation Commission was moving it to a higher spot farther north on the mountain, where it would give a better view of part of the Mud Brook Valley that lies west of the mountain.[^2] Conservation Warden Ringstead of Ladysmith oversaw the relocation.[^2] By April 1936 the tower was in place at its new spot, ready for the start of the fire permit season.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Wisconsin list gives two Flambeau Ridge entries.[^1] The one labelled "Flambeau Ridge – first" is marked gone.[^1] The other, a short distance west by its listed coordinates, is marked standing.[^1] The list does not say which structure each entry describes, so the older reports cannot be matched to either entry with certainty.[^1]

No source found describes what remains at the site today, so it is not known whether footings or steel are still there, or whether the public can reach the spot.[^1] Visitors should not assume that the older tower can be seen or visited.
