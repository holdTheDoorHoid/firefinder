Mount Cammerer sits at the northeastern end of Great Smoky Mountains National Park in Tennessee, near the Appalachian Trail.[^1]

The Civilian Conservation Corps built the tower between 1937 and 1939.[^1][^2] Crews used materials from the site, and the result is a two-story octagonal structure of stone.[^1][^2]

The tower saw little use for detection, and the register says it was abandoned in the 1960s.[^1]

The Friends of the Smokies formed in 1993, and restoring this tower in 1995 was the group's first project.[^3] In 2012 the Forest Fire Lookout Association gave the group a restoration grant for repairs after extreme weather and vandal damage.[^2] The work covered a replacement first-floor door and frame, hardware, paint over graffiti, and a rotten spruce roof beam.[^2] The register credits the restoration to the Friends of Great Smoky Mountains National Park, the Panther Press and the National Park Service.[^1]

The tower joined the National Historic Lookout Register on April 25, 1994.[^1]

In September 2016 twenty hikers covered an 11.4-mile round trip to the tower on a Friends of the Smokies classic hike, and the group's account describes the 360-degree view from it.[^3] The register says many hikers go there, though the trip is demanding.[^1]

The federal National Register record for the tower carries the title Mount Cammerer Fire Lookout.[^4] I could not confirm the tower's condition after 2016.

Before you go, check the park's current trail and tower notices. Stay off the tower if it is closed or looks unsafe.
