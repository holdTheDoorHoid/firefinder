Whitmore Mountain has two lookout sites on the Colville Reservation.[^1] The older one is a ground-level cab about a quarter mile northeast of the current steel tower.[^1][^2] The Indian department was working on the mountain in June 1934.[^5] Its crews had a camp there and had hauled up some lumber. They were also cutting poles and putting in a trail.[^5] The Civilian Conservation Corps built the cab and a three-legged wooden tower for a reported $1,046.[^1][^2] The register dates the cab to 1934 and the tower to 1935.[^1] Fire Lookout dates both structures to 1938,[^3] and SummitPost dates the tower to 1938.[^4] The 1960 inspection report gives 1935 for the structures.[^5] The sources disagree, so we keep the register's dates here and flag the rest.

The wooden tower stood 60 feet high on three legs, with a 7-by-7-foot cab on top.[^3][^5] The cab on the ground served as living quarters.[^3][^5] In 1943 the Colville National Forest hired teachers as summer fire lookouts.[^5] A newspaper that summer named one of them on Whitmore Mountain.[^5]

By 1960 an inspection report called the wooden tower unsafe. It proposed a 50-foot steel tower with quarters on top, about 1,200 feet south of the wooden tower's site.[^5] A 1962 letter suggested moving the new tower a quarter mile southwest, onto private land.[^5] The steel lookout that stands today went up in the 1960s on land a private owner allowed.[^1][^3] Most sources give its height as 53 feet, but SummitPost says 67 feet.[^2][^3][^6][^4]

Lightning ignited the Whitmore fire on August 3, 2021.[^1] Crews cut a hand line around the historic cab and wrapped it to protect it. The fire spared both lookouts.[^1]

The register says the cab remains on site, but its condition is very poor.[^1] The steel lookout is staffed every summer.[^1][^6] It is locked.[^1]

The mountain is on tribal land.[^1][^2] The tribes require a reservation use pass for visitors.[^7] Their purchase sheet, which appears to date from April 2024, lists an annual pass at $80.[^7] TrailChick advises a high-clearance 4x4, because road conditions on the Colville can vary dramatically from year to year.[^2] SummitPost warns that access for non-tribal visitors can change at any time, so ask the tribes before you go.[^4] Keep off the historic cab, given its very poor condition.[^1]
