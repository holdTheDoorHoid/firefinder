The Almond Lookout Tower stands in Independence County, Arkansas, about half a mile south of State Highway 25 and of the settlement of Almond.[^1][^2] The Arkansas Forestry Commission administers it.[^1]

The sources do not give a build date. The Register lists the build year as unknown.[^1] A federal survey station description from 1954 describes a steel tower on four legs that carries an observation cabin, about 120 feet high.[^2]

The Register identifies it as an Aermotor MC-39 and gives its height as 100 feet. It also says the first flight of stairs has been removed.[^1] The 100-foot Register figure and the 120-foot survey figure disagree, and the sources do not say whether the tower was changed in between.[^1][^2]

The Register added the tower on April 10, 2023, on a nomination by Jason Johns. Its page also records a site visit dated 1/22/22.[^1]

The encyclopedia calls the general store and post office the lifeblood of early Almond, and it says both opened soon after the Civil War.[^3] The same entry says the community sat in Independence County until February 20, 1883, and it now places it in northeastern Cleburne County.[^3] The Register and the survey both put the tower in Independence County, so the county question is not fully reconciled.[^1][^2]

The sources do not say whether the public may visit or climb the tower. Check with the Arkansas Forestry Commission before going, and do not climb a tower that is closed or private.
