The state put up the Ellendale tower in 1931 as one of a group of towers erected that year.[^3] Erection started on July 29, and the towers went into service on October 15 of that year.[^3] Each tower was built of steel, with a wooden staircase inside and a seven-foot square cabin glazed across its upper half.[^3] The Forest Fire Lookout Association describes this one as a 100-foot Aermotor LS-40, placed at a DOT compound.[^1] The state's own figures give 100 feet from ground level to the bottom of the cabin floor, or 110 feet overall.[^3]

The tower stood on a 50-foot rise roughly two miles east of Ellendale, just south of the Ellendale-Milton state highway.[^3] Reaching the site by telephone meant building a 1.8-mile line out from Ellendale.[^3]

The towers were staffed.[^4] In 1933 each tower was issued a small map so that towermen could compare smoke locations with one another.[^4] Reports through the 1930s and 1940s refer to towermen at work.[^4] A 1934 report says glare in the cabins was hard on the towermen's eyes, so dark green curtains went up in three towers, Ellendale among them.[^4] The window sash was replaced in 1941, and by 1946 the Ellendale cabin had insulated ceilings, the last of three towers to get them.[^4] In 1951 the tower was wired for electric power, one of four towers wired after a radio installation contract was awarded.[^4]

The association says the State Police took the tower over for communications, and that it was removed at some point between 2002 and 2006.[^1][^2][^5] The association's page carries a photograph of the tower captioned 2002.[^1]

No source describes what remains at the site now.
