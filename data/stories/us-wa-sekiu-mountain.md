Sekiu Mountain lies in Clallam County, Washington, and the state's Department of Natural Resources (DNR) put a fire lookout on it.[^1] The register gives 1964 as the year the tower was built, and it describes a 40-foot wooden live-in tower.[^1] WillhiteWeb says the same.[^3] A DNR report quoted by Forest Lookouts says a new tower was erected there in 1962 to 1964.[^4] An April 1964 newspaper item, also quoted there, said the lookout was still to be built.[^4] A 1965 newspaper item quoted by WillhiteWeb says the DNR put the new tower into operation on June 15.[^3] That item gives the mountain as 1,960 feet, while the register gives 1,943 feet (592 m).[^1][^3]

The first person to live in the tower was Mrs. Della Turner, wife of the DNR fire warden, who moved into the new cabin-on-stilts that summer.[^3] A 1969 item listed Sekiu Mountain as one of three lookouts to be manned.[^4] Mike Drovdahl worked a summer in the lookout in 1975.[^3] He described watching fog come in off the ocean until everything but the tower was covered.[^3]

The register says the tower was removed in 1988.[^1] Rex's Fire Lookout Page says it was destroyed that year.[^2] WillhiteWeb has the fullest account.[^3] It says the original plan was to move the lookout into the Forks Timber Museum.[^3] The tower was blasted down by dynamite in a way meant to save the cab, but a crew then burned the wrong pile and the lookout was destroyed.[^3] The same page notes that a similar story is told about Mount Octopus, and that the writer is not sure which account is correct.[^3]

The Forks Timber Museum has a lookout tower that came from near Bellingham in 1985.[^3] Its furnishings and equipment came from Sekiu, according to WillhiteWeb.[^3] Willhite's photos label an eyebolt in the woods below the lookout site and some wood nearby.[^3] None of the pages we read says what is on the summit now. The route lies on Olympic Tree Farm land that has a gate and is open for recreation. Willhite describes good gravel roads that reach the summit.[^3]
