Mount Olga Fire Tower stands on the summit of Mount Olga in Windham County, Vermont, inside Molly Stark State Park, at about 2,316 feet.[^1][^4]

The sources do not agree on the first tower. The National Historic Lookout Register says the Division of Forestry built an octagonal wooden tower on the mountain in 1930.[^1] The Forest Lookouts history says the tower opened in 1933.[^2] A 1935 newspaper item quoted there says a fire warden and a helper put up a 40-foot structure.[^2]

By 1949 the wooden tower had rotted badly.[^2] A newspaper that year reported that it was being taken down and that a 50-foot steel tower from Bald Mountain in Townshend would take its place.[^2] The Register says the steel tower was moved to the site in 1949 and was in use until the 1970s.[^1] A state park page, though, says the tower was rebuilt on Olga in 1958, which does not match the 1949 accounts.[^4]

Fire wardens watched from the summit through the 1960s.[^2] A 1965 newspaper item names Philip Pratt as the lookout that season, and says that on weekend days as many as 50 people came up, mostly city folks who hiked up from Molly Stark State Park.[^2]

Today the state uses the tower for public viewing inside Molly Stark State Park.[^4] The Register says the tower is maintained as a popular hiking destination and viewpoint.[^1] In 2016 a visitor found the tower in good condition, but said most of the glass in the cab was gone. The same report says directions to the tower were signed.[^1] The state set aside $60,000 for safety railings on Mt. Olga and four other towers, with work running into spring 2026.[^3] A state chapter report says interior cab railings were installed at Mt. Olga.[^5]

A state park page says the tower is temporarily closed while repairs are scheduled for mid-December, but the notice gives no date, so check current closures before going.[^4]
