A Coast and Geodetic Survey station description from 1954 places Hilda's fire lookout in the Mark Twain National Forest. It calls the structure a steel tower with four legs, roughly 50 feet (15 m) tall, topped by an open crow's nest.[^1] The lookout stood on Lime Kiln Mountain, south of the unincorporated community of Hilda in Taney County, Missouri.[^2]

The two sources give different distances. The 1954 description puts the station 1.5 miles south of the Hilda post office, while Wikipedia says the former site is approximately one mile south on Lime Kiln Mountain. The two may use different starting points.[^1][^2]

Wikipedia refers to the former Hilda Lookout Tower, and it gives no date for its removal.[^2]

No source I could read describes what remains at the site today, so I cannot say whether footings, a tower or only a clearing is left. I also found no source that says whether the public can reach the site now. Check with the Mark Twain National Forest before you go.

[^1]: Hilda - Forest Lookouts (National Geodetic Survey station description GF0739 as reproduced), Central US Lookouts, https://centraluslookouts.weebly.com/hilda.html (accessed 2026-10-21).
[^2]: Hilda, Missouri, Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilda,_Missouri (accessed 2026-10-21).
