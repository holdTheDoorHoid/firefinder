Sourdough Peak Lookout is a log cabin with a cupola on a summit of roughly 6,800 feet in Idaho County, within the Nez Perce National Forest.[^3][^2] It was constructed in 1927 using the Nez Perce log lookout house plan, but the builder changed the planned 16-by-14-foot structure to 15 by 15 feet and added windows all around.[^1]

From 1919 through 1931, 20 log cabin lookouts with cupolas were built on the Forest. Sourdough Peak is the only one left on the Forest today.[^4]

The lookout was last manned in 1969. Idaho Fire Lookouts says that was the last year it was staffed, after which it went on emergency status.[^2] Firelookout.com and Dangerous Roads both say it was abandoned in 1969, so the sources differ on how to describe that change.[^3][^5] Idaho Fire Lookouts says its staffing record for the lookout is not yet documented.[^2]

The Nez Perce National Forest restored the cabin in 1994 and 1995 as a Passport in Time project, with volunteers working alongside Forest Service staff.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register recorded the site on November 25, 1996.[^1]

Idaho Fire Lookouts lists the site as drive-up, with emergency status.[^2] A visit it reported in September 2023 found the cabin and grounds holding up reasonably well, though two windows were broken and mouse droppings covered the building.[^2] The writer lingered at least an hour over the graffiti carved inside.[^2]

The road in is NF-492 (Santiam-Sourdough Road). It is unpaved, runs 19.5 miles from Santiam Road, is not plowed in winter, and a 4x4 is recommended.[^5]
