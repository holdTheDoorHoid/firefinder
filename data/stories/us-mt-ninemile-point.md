The Nine Mile Point lookout stood in Flathead County, about eight miles north of Columbia Falls, on the Flathead National Forest.[^1][^2]

Firelookout.com dates the tower to 1934. It describes a 30-foot pole L-4 tower about 1.25 miles north-northwest of Spoon Lake.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page quotes a 1971 newspaper that calls the tower 35 feet tall, so the two sources disagree on its height.[^3]

Sources name several people who staffed the lookout.[^2][^3] In August 1940, a carrier pigeon with a metal leg band landed on a stump at the lookout while Orville Moen was stationed there.[^3] In 1955 a Hungry Horse News item said a newly married couple, Phillip Hodapp Jr. and his wife, would spend the summer at Nine Mile lookout.[^3] Firelookout.com lists Mr. and Mrs. Floyd Domoth as lookout personnel in 1957.[^2] In 1971, a Daily Inter Lake item said Karen Hey lived and worked in the tower that summer while holding a Forest Service lookout post.[^3]

Firelookout.com says the tower was destroyed in 1974.[^2] The Forest Lookouts page closes with the single word "Removed" and gives no date, so the two sources describe its end differently.[^3]

No source we read describes what remains at the site today.
