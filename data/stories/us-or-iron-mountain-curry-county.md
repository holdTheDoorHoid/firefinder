Iron Mountain stands in the Siskiyou National Forest in Curry County, about 18 miles east-southeast of Port Orford.[^1][^2] The first lookout was a Region 5 cabin, 20 feet by 20, and the register and firelookout.com both date it to 1924.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts history, which quotes old newspapers, adds detail.[^3] Lumber was whipsawed near the site in 1924 instead of being packed up ready-cut, and the house was finished in the summer of 1925.[^3] The sources disagree by a year on when the building was done.[^1][^3]

Newspaper items quoted by Forest Lookouts name a few of the people who worked the station.[^3] The lookout went on duty in May 1929 and reported one fire.[^3] George Valentine was the lookout man in 1930.[^3] James Cook, who had served as lookout, was attending a service school at the Oregon Caves in June 1933.[^3] The station was staffed for 84 days in 1941.[^3]

One episode from 1943 stands out. A Roseburg News-Review item, quoted on the same page, says Orris Wood, a 17-year-old, had been commended for facing a fire in her station to call headquarters.[^3] Soon after she left, the building fell, burning, over a 150-foot cliff.[^3]

The sources disagree about what came next.[^1][^2][^3] The register and firelookout.com both give an L-4 cab in 1940.[^1][^2] The Forest Lookouts page instead records a new lookout building in 1944, after the fire.[^3] The register says the lookout was removed in 1963.[^1] Firelookout.com says it was destroyed that year.[^2]

The Forest Service's Iron Mountain Trail page describes three high points on the ridge.[^4] The southernmost used to be a Forest Service lookout, and a short side trail leads to it.[^4] The trail is dry, has no water, and is open year round, though access is limited from late fall through spring.[^4] No source we read describes what remains on the summit today.[^1][^4]
