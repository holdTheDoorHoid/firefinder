Mount Scott is the highest point in Crater Lake National Park.[^5] The first summit lookout was a cupola cabin from the 1920s, and the sources do not agree on the exact year.[^3][^2][^1] Firelookout.com says about 1923, and the register says 1924.[^2][^1] A history page that draws on park files says a three-way agreement to build a lookout was made in the winter of 1924/25, and that materials for the lookout house were being delivered in 1929.[^4]

The lookout was still a shared post in the 1930s. In 1935 it was a cooperative lookout between the park and the Klamath district of the Rogue River National Forest.[^4] The same history page records that in August 1928 the lookout reported seven fires on the reservation, one on the Crater National Forest, and two on Association territory.[^4]

In its final 15 years the cupola was so loose that a fire's location could not be plotted on a windy day, the register says.[^1] The cupola gave way in 1952 to the present two-story structure, a modified L-4 with a fold-up catwalk.[^1][^3]

That 1952 lookout is no longer staffed, and it now holds USGS seismic monitoring gear.[^3] Firelookout.com describes the present NPS cab as available for emergencies.[^2] The register says it is now kept up for emergency use.[^1]

The summit trail starts on Rim Drive and runs about 2.5 miles, with many switchbacks, to the tower.[^5] The sources I read do not say whether visitors may go inside the cab, so check with Crater Lake National Park before you plan a trip.
