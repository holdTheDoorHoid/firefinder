Lake Peak is a summit in the Pecos Wilderness of the Santa Fe National Forest, about 20 miles from Santa Fe.[^1] In 1916 the Forest Service set out to build a lookout there.[^2]

A newspaper report dated September 16, 1916, and quoted on the Forest Lookouts site, said the Forest Service had begun work on a new dwelling for a fire lookout at the head of Santa Fe Canyon.[^2] The plan was for a substantial log cabin on a stone and masonry base, with a steep roof to resist heavy snow.[^2] A shelter for the lookout was also being built farther up, at the head of the canyon, and the service telephone was to go inside it.[^2]

The same report said no lookout had yet been assigned, because recent rains had made one unnecessary.[^2]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's New Mexico list records Lake Peak as a rock shelter, now gone.[^3] That does not match the 1916 plan, which describes a log cabin, so the list may describe only the shelter, or a different structure altogether.[^2][^3]

Today the peak is a summit climb. SummitPost describes a route that follows a rocky ridge to the top, and it gives the trailhead at the parking lot of the Santa Fe Ski Area.[^1] No source we read describes what, if anything, remains of the lookout at the site today.
