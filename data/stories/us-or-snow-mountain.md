Snow Mountain's summit has carried a lookout since at least 1915, when a lookout house was established there within the Ochoco National Forest.[^3] In August 1920 a lightning storm set eleven fires across one ranger's district and burned out some of the station's telephone parts.[^3] The ranger reportedly fitted a spare ignition coil from an old Ford and got the instrument working.[^3]

The early cabins are described differently. The register says a cupola lookout from 1915 and an L-4 ground house from 1930 were both replaced by the present cab.[^1] The firelookout page also gives an L-4 cab for 1930.[^2] A September 1930 newspaper report calls the new house an R-1 Aladdin Jr. type, and says the old cabin, built in 1914, was torn down to make room for it.[^3]

The present cab went up in 1961.[^1][^2] The register and firelookout both describe a flat-topped R-6 cab on a 10-foot treated timber tower.[^1][^2][^3] Contract records show a start work order for August 1, 1961, and a final inspection on August 24.[^3] An October 1961 memo reported that the concrete footings were already deteriorating.[^3]

Lookouts have manned the station since at least 1927.[^3] A June 1928 notice said a Pacific University student would occupy the lookout, and in 1938 Roy Nolen was lookout forest guard on the mountain.[^3] The station reported twelve fires in 1944.[^3] The register describes the lookout as manned and in a busy fire zone,[^1] and the firelookout page says it is manned each summer.[^2] The 2026 directory still lists it as staffed.[^4]

The register listed Snow Mountain on March 31, 2003.[^1] Today the cab still stands.[^4] The directory lists the site as legally accessible to the public, but it notes that this does not mean access to the lookout itself.[^4] Because the cab is a working station, treat it that way: check with the district office before going up.
