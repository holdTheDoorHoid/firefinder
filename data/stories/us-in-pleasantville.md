In June 1940 the Division of Forestry of the Indiana State Department of Conservation announced a fire tower for the Greene-Sullivan forest, with federal WPA money helping to pay for it.[^1] The papers disagreed about where it would stand: a June item put it west of Pleasantville, while a July item said north of town.[^1] That July report described a 100-foot steel tower meant to protect about 125,000 acres around the forest.[^1] By December, WPA workers had started the foundation, and the planners expected the tower to have a visibility radius of about ten miles.[^1] The state's 1941 annual report says a 100-foot steel tower had been built on the forest.[^2]

The tower stayed on state lists for some years.[^4] An Outdoor Indiana article from January 1960 counted 13 state towers in District 3 and included Pleasantville among them.[^4] A 1978 table of parks and forests with towers listed the Greene-Sullivan forest, though that article said few if any towers were still used for fire detection by then.[^4] A Forest Service page that reproduces the state's list places the tower on Greene-Sullivan State Forest.[^3]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association's database marks the Pleasantville tower as gone.[^5] The Indiana fire tower guide also says it is not standing, although its author has not yet visited the site.[^2] We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the spot today. The state forest has trails used by hikers and horseback riders.[^6]
