The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists a gone tower called Hortense and a separate one called Camden, both in Polk County.[^1] Our record links the Central U.S. lookouts page for Camden to the Hortense listing, but no source we read confirms that link.[^1][^2]

In 1935 the Coast and Geodetic Survey described a station on a thickly wooded hill about 2.5 miles south of the village of Camden.[^2] The survey mark sat under a bolt in the peak of the roof of a lookout house, and a lookout tower stood over the station that year.[^2]

Camden began as a company town that the lumber firm W. T. Carter & Brother started in the early 1900s.[^3] In 1891 a post office came to Hortense, a community in east central Polk County. Community leader John Handley obtained it and named it for his youngest daughter.[^4]

By the time of a 1949 station recovery note, the tower had been torn down, and only its concrete footings remained.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association marks the Hortense listing as gone.[^1]

No source describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether the hilltop is open to visitors.
