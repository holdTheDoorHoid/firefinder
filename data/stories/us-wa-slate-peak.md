Slate Peak sits at the end of the highest road in Washington, near Harts Pass in the Okanogan National Forest.[^1][^5] A gable-roofed cupola cabin is reported for 1924, but WillhiteWeb says it may never have existed.[^4][^6]

The first cab the sources agree on was a gable-roofed L-4, built in 1931, the year a telephone line reached the summit.[^4][^6] A 1931 item quoted on one lookout site says the peak was occupied for the first time that year.[^5] Ed Allen and Raymond Johnson were still working on the lookout house in early September, after which it was staffed for the rest of the fire season.[^6]

In 1954 the Forest Service set a hip-roofed L-4 ground cab on the summit.[^4][^6] The register and WillhiteWeb say it came from Leecher Mountain, but firelookout.com says that cab went to Twisp instead.[^1][^4][^6] In 1956 the Air Force took over the summit for a radar station, removed the cab and cut the top 40 feet off the peak.[^1][^6] Sources disagree on whether the radar was finished: the register says it was never built, firelookout.com says it was partly built, and WillhiteWeb says its tower was sold for salvage in 1961.[^1][^4][^6] A cab was back on a 40-foot tower by 1960, and MVFFLA dates the current structure to 1956.[^3][^6]

Staff named in the sources include Roy Paul in 1932, Al Anderson in 1934, Bill Horn in 1954 and Carl Schmidt in 1957.[^4][^6] Mr. and Mrs. Jon Shepard staffed the lookout in 1963, and Al Anderson of Winthrop finished that season.[^6]

A lightning bolt damaged the cabin's roof in 1932 and set Roy Paul's bed on fire.[^6] In 1994 the lookout was called into emergency duty when Goat Peak Lookout had to be evacuated.[^3][^6] In 1996 it kept watch on the Elbow Basin fire and relayed radio messages.[^3][^6]

Firelookout.com says the lookout was abandoned for some years, with its bottom stairs removed, before the tower collapsed in the winter of 2026.[^4] WillhiteWeb says a windstorm blew it over in early 2026.[^6] MVFFLA says only that it collapsed over the winter and gives no cause.[^2]

The Forest Service will close Harts Pass Road until repairs are finished, after significant damage to the road.[^2] MVFFLA and the district are documenting and salvaging what remains.[^2] The structure belongs to the Forest Service, and the group asks people to respect closures and not disturb the site.[^2] The register, which listed the lookout on November 4, 2000, describes it as kept up for emergency use.[^1] No source I read describes what remains on the summit today, so I cannot say what can be seen there.
