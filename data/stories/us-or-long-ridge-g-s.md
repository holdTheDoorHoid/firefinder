Long Ridge sits in Curry County, in the Siskiyou National Forest, about 26 miles northeast of Brookings.[^1]

Firelookout.com lists two cabins for 1930.[^1] The Forest's inventory describes the first lookout there as a 14-by-14-foot R-1 style ground cabin, built that year.[^2] A 1930 Siskiyou Bulletin notice, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, says the district drew two new cabins, one for Long Ridge and one for Packsaddle.[^3]

In July 1930 the Forest also finished four miles of road up the ridge to the lookout house, so a car could unload at its door.[^3]

In 1940 a Forest plan called the station "principally necessary for lightning" and noted that it probably could see an area where many fires had been started by people.[^3]

The Forest Lookouts page also names N.B. Moore as a fire watcher posted at Long Ridge in 1916, years before the cabin. The Forest's inventory calls the 1930 cabin the first lookout for the area, so the two accounts do not agree.[^3][^2]

In 1941 the station was staffed for 94 days and reported by telephone to the Gold Beach station.[^3] The same page records an activation on March 21, 1942, and names the Roseburg Filter Center beside it.[^3]

Firelookout.com says a 1962 Columbus Day storm destroyed the cabin.[^1] A newspaper item the Forest Lookouts page dates to October 23, 1962, says the lookout was demolished.[^3] Its duties then moved to the Quail Prairie Mountain lookout, which the inventory says was constructed in 1963.[^3][^2]

The FFLA list shows the Long Ridge site as ground-level and gone, and the Quail Prairie tower as standing.[^4]

We found no source that describes what, if anything, remains at the site today, or whether visitors can reach it.
