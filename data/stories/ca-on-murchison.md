Firelookout.ca places the Murchison Fire Tower on Highway 60 in South Algonquin Township, Nipissing District, at an elevation of 514 m (1686 ft).[^1] A Department of Lands and Forests tower detection map from 1963 shows the tower.[^1]

Ontario's Department of Lands and Forests, now the MNR, founded the Pembroke Forest Fire District in 1922; it was one of 17 districts.[^2] The district's headquarters were in Pembroke, and the district was the central location for 15 fire tower lookouts, Murchison among them.[^2] Once a fire was seen, the towerman took degree bearings from his own tower and radioed them to headquarters.[^2] When bearings also came in from one or more other towers, the rangers at headquarters could plot the fire's exact location on their map.[^2]

According to the Firelookout.ca entry, a researcher's cousin worked in the tower during the 1960s.[^1]

Wikipedia says the district's towers were expected to be phased out after aerial firefighting methods were adopted in the 1970s.[^2]

No source describes the site today. None says whether a tower, footings or other remains stand there, or whether the site is open to visitors.
