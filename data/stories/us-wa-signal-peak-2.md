Signal Peak sits on the Yakama Reservation in Yakima County, in the middle of Yakama Nation forest land, about 17 miles east of Mount Adams.[^1][^3][^5]

The first lookout here was a pole tower with a tent camp, dated to 1920.[^1][^2][^4][^5] In 1935 it was replaced by a wooden tower on three legs, 55 feet tall, with an 8-by-8-foot cab and a ground house with living quarters.[^1][^3][^5]

In December 1940 a Goldendale paper reported that two lookouts stationed here radioed in twice a day to the Satus Pass station.[^4] The current tower is a 68-foot Aermotor steel tower that was moved here from Panther Creek in 1964.[^1][^2][^3][^5] The register and Willhite both note that it has no guy wires.[^1][^5] Firelookout.com says the lookout is staffed in summer, though that listing is undated.[^3]

Most of the reservation is off-limits to non-tribal visitors.[^2] The register lists the Yakima Indian Agency as administrator.[^1] Willhite gives the access as restricted, by Yakama Nation tour only.[^5] TrailChick reports that in 2019 the Yakama Nation gave a small group a spring and a fall guided tour, in return for volunteers who help open and close a lookout for the season. That page names Satus in the sentence, which may be a typo for Signal.[^2]

The register listed the lookout on April 13, 2022.[^1]

The sources describe tribal tours, not public visits, so treat the tower as a working site and do not climb it.[^2][^5]
