Sierra Peak sits in Cleveland National Forest in Riverside County, California, at 928 meters (3,045 feet).[^1] The record says no primary lookout was ever built on the summit.[^1] What stood here was a Region 5 design from 1934, the BC-201 "Forest Fireman Lookout Station," a 19-by-30-foot wood frame building with a glassed room that gave a 270-degree view, plus a kitchen, a bedroom and an enclosed porch.[^1]

BC-201 stations were built at secondary sites, or used as guard stations and residence cabins for towers that only observed.[^1] The record says this one was likely put up in the mid-1930s, along with many other lookouts in the area that the Civilian Conservation Corps built for the Forest Service in 1935 to 1937.[^1]

The record lists the station as now gone.[^1] The site was added to the Former Fire Lookout Sites register on February 22, 2011, as US 853, CA 12.[^1]

Today the peak serves as a major communications site, alongside nearby Santiago Peak, which the record calls "Talking Mountain."[^1] We found no source describing what remains at the lookout spot, and nothing we read says whether the summit is open to visitors.

[^1]: Sierra Peak Lookout Site, Former Fire Lookout Sites, http://firetower.org/lookouts/us/ca/sierra-peak-lookout-site/ (accessed 2026-10-10).
