Lookout Mountain's tower is a 30-foot Aermotor MC-24 steel tower in Sierra County, on the Gila National Forest.[^1][^2] The sources agree on the tower's design but not on its age. A fire-lookout history page that draws on a Forest Service account says a 30-foot MC-24 tower with a 12-by-12 wooden cab was constructed in 1933.[^2] A 2020 New Mexico review gives the same 1933 date for the tower.[^3] The register, by contrast, says the lookout was constructed in 1965 on the existing tower, and its photo caption gives 1934 for the MC-24 tower and cab.[^1]

The cab changed in 1965. Its original observation cab was swapped for a steel cab in the Forest Service's CL-100 series, and the top flight of stairs had to be changed.[^2][^3] The 2020 review says the lookout is actively staffed.[^3]

A 1988 review for the National Register found the lookout not eligible, because its original design had lost integrity.[^3] The 2020 review suggests re-evaluating it in its current form, since that configuration is now 55 years old.[^3] The register added the lookout on July 7, 2022, as US 1651 (NM 50), with Brad Eells as nominator.[^1]

The Forest Service's trailhead page describes a limited parking area on Forest Road 226A, Lookout Mountain Road, that serves the Continental Divide National Scenic Trail (Trail #74).[^4] Its directions run north from Winston on New Mexico Highway 52 for 9 miles, then west on Highway 59 for about 14.5 miles, then south on Forest Road 226 for about 5.5 miles to Forest Road 226A.[^4] The sources I read do not say whether the cab itself is open to visitors. Because the 2020 review lists it as actively staffed, treat it as a working tower and do not climb it.
