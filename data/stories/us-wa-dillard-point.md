Dillard Point stood at about 2,400 feet in the Mount Baker-Snoqualmie National Forest, roughly seven miles southeast of Mount Baker in Whatcom County.[^1][^2] A June 1961 Marysville Globe item, quoted by Washington Lookouts, announced a planned lookout house and tower for the Baker River drainage.[^3] The register and FireLookout.com date the tower to 1962 and describe a 20-foot treated timber tower with an R-6 flat cab.[^1][^2]

The register says the tower was removed in 1981, and Washington Lookouts gives the same year for its removal.[^1][^3] FireLookout.com and WillhiteWeb call it destroyed that year.[^2][^4] The register listed the site in March 2012 as US 1238.[^1]

According to WillhiteWeb, the site has grown over entirely and offers no views.[^4] Its remains are spread over a wide area: an outhouse at the north end, footings and picnic tables at the south, and communications wiring and posts in between.[^4]

Drive east from Sedro-Woolley on the North Cascades Highway (Highway 20) for 14.5 miles, then turn onto Baker Lake Road. Follow it about 12.5 miles, then take forest roads 12 and 13.[^4] Park where an old road peels off to the right. The site lies 0.7 miles along that road, which the guide found manageable on foot as of 2014.[^4] The guide also reports one major washout, which may explain why the road was given up for good.[^4] No source records a closure.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
