Whitestone Ridge Lookout sits on the Colville Indian Reservation in Ferry County, Washington.[^1][^2] The summit was first used as a fire lookout camp in 1922.[^1][^2]

Sources disagree about the first cabin. The register and a Forest Lookouts page say the Civilian Conservation Corps built an L-4 ground house there in 1935 for about $900.[^1][^4] Firelookout.com instead says the first structure was a frame cabin in 1932.[^3] By 1960 an inspection report found the cabin in very poor condition, and parts of the rock cliff under it had slipped.[^4] A 20-foot tower with an R-6 cab replaced the old cabin in 1964.[^1][^3]

The present structure went up in 1985: a 53-foot steel tower with a live-in cab.[^1][^2] The register and firelookout.com both say it is staffed every summer.[^1][^3]

The 2019 Williams Flats fire burned the summit and the land around it. The lookout survived, although its outhouse was lost.[^2]

The land is Colville tribal land, and TrailChick reports that non-tribal visitors must buy an annual recreation permit, which it lists at $80.[^2] Its 2019 route used a BIA road up the north ridge, and it warns that conditions may have changed after the fires.[^2]

The register recorded the lookout on April 13, 2022, as US 1564.[^1]
