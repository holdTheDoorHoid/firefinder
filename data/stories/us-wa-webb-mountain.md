Webb Mountain, above Hood Canal in Jefferson County, had two lookouts, one on the summit and one lower on the slope.[^1][^4][^5] The sources disagree about which building was which and when each one went up.

The lower site sat on a sidehill about three-quarters of a mile east of the summit.[^2] FFLOS and Firelookout.com date the sidehill building to about 1934.[^1][^2] Firelookout.com describes a 10-foot pole L-4 tower there at first.[^2] WashingtonLookouts, whose page covers the upper site, gives 1934 for a 16-foot round timber tower with a 14-by-14 cab on the Hoodsport district.[^3]

A document dated July 1, 1936 judged the lower site a poor place and recommended re-establishing the lookout further back on the same ridge.[^4] WillhiteWeb says the summit building was built, or moved there, around 1937 or 1938. It offers the idea that the lower cab was moved up the hill as a working theory.[^4]

During the war the site was an Aircraft Warning Service station, which WashingtonLookouts dates from March 16, 1942.[^3][^4] WillhiteWeb says a final post-war AWS document records that the site was winterized and that no funds went to new structures.[^4] Its photo captions name Mr. and Mrs. Lloyd Wolcott as AWS observers.[^4]

Firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say the summit building was destroyed in 1966.[^2][^4] FFLOS dates the removal of its sidehill cabin to the same year.[^1]

Today little remains at the summit. WillhiteWeb mentions an old outhouse structure near the road and a survey benchmark disk, stamped Webb Mtn 1955, set in a concrete post.[^4] Little of the view is left, and the last mile up the road has turned into a rough bushwhack.[^4] At the lower site, WillhiteWeb describes one of the foundations, still holding a post, and two timbers still standing vertically, a few feet from the National Forest boundary. A recent timber harvest opened the view again.[^5] The summit road is gravel and described as mostly good.[^4] We found no source saying whether either site is open to the public.
