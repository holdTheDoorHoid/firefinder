Coffin Mountain stands in the Willamette National Forest in Linn County, Oregon.[^1] The Forest Service says the name comes from the long, coffin-shaped ridge the lookout sits on.[^2]

Lookouts have stood on the summit since the early 1900s, though the sources disagree on the first year. The National Historic Lookout Register says a platform went up in 1906, while a local history site dates a crude pole structure to about 1905.[^1][^3] The Forest Service also points to at least three other lookout structures from as far back as 1905.[^2] A D-6 cupola cabin replaced that first lookout in 1921.[^1][^5]

Newspaper items that the local history site reprints give a glimpse of the early staff.[^3] In 1921 Tom Humphreys, a former city mail carrier, took a lookout job there, and he later wrote that snow still lay 25 feet deep less than thirty yards from the station.[^3] A 1925 item said the lookout had reported a fire on private land near the Calapooia River.[^3]

An L-4 ground cabin followed in 1936, about fifteen years after the cupola.[^1][^3][^5] A July 1984 Register-Guard item, reprinted on that site, said the cabin had suffered extensive deterioration from weather at 5,771 feet.[^3] At one stage the lookout was left unused and its windows were taken out.[^3] The item says the Civilian Conservation Corps built that cabin in the 1930s.[^3]

The replacement, a flat-roofed R-6 cabin with a catwalk, was finished in 1984 and measures 15 by 15 feet.[^1][^3] The sources also disagree on the elevation. The Register gives 5,595 feet, while the Forest Service gives 5,771.[^1][^2]

Today the Forest Service lists the lookout as currently staffed by the Detroit Ranger District.[^2] A field guide adds that summer staffing continues.[^6] A local lookout site notes that a 2018 Statesman Journal article profiled a woman who had by then staffed it for 29 years.[^4]

The Forest Service describes the hike as somewhat long but easy, passing through an old burn, with views toward Mount Jefferson and Detroit Lake.[^2] To drive there, head east from Detroit on Highway 22 for about 19.5 miles, then take Forest Road 11 and Forest Road 1168 to the trailhead.[^2] The Forest Service says its roads are not maintained for snow and ice, and it asks visitors to contact the local ranger station for current conditions.[^2]
