The lookout on Bearhead Mountain was an L-4 cab, built in 1931.[^1][^2][^3] A hiking guide says the Forest Service built it at the summit that year.[^5]

The summit was in use before the cab was built. WillhiteWeb says there was a lookout camp there in 1929, and that Jess Rose was the lookout in 1929 and 1930.[^3] The hiking guide says the cabin was staffed through 1957.[^5]

The summit was captured in panoramic photos in 1934.[^1][^3][^4] The register's photo captions credit the National Archives Pacific Northwest Region for the 1934 panoramas.[^1] The Washington lookouts list dates them to August 1, 1934.[^4]

Sources disagree on how the cab ended, but all of them give 1957.[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5] The register, the hiking guide and the Washington lookouts list say it was removed.[^1][^4][^5] firelookout.com and WillhiteWeb say it was destroyed.[^2][^3]

The hiking guide is the only source we read that describes the site today. It says the cabin has left little trace, and that only a rusted pipe stub among the rocks remains of a shaft that once mounted an Osborne Firefinder, the instrument lookouts used to locate fires.[^5]

A 1955 U.S. Geological Survey station description, quoted on the Washington lookouts page, places the Bearhead station about 15 miles southeast of Enumclaw.[^4]

The hike is six miles on Bearhead Mountain Trail #1179.1, and the guide says a Northwest Forest Pass is required.[^5] We list access as public.
