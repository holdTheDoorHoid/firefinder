Mount Jefferson in Ashe County got a 35-foot fire tower in 1947, erected by the State Forestry Service.[^1] The lookout history dates it from a 1948 state biennial report.[^1]

Two private citizens, J. B. Hash and H.C. Tucker, gave 26 acres toward a public park on the mountain, and the state made the needed road improvements, according to a park history quoted by the Watauga Democrat.[^2] The park also included 10 acres that the State Forestry Service used as a fire station.[^2] Mount Jefferson officially became a state park in 1956.[^2]

A 2001 state bill about the natural area would let certain public entities use the fire tower for public communications purposes.[^3] Its draft text would let the state provide space at the communications tower site to public safety and emergency management agencies, for antennas and other communications devices.[^3] No source here describes the tower's condition after 2001, or whether visitors may climb it.[^3]
