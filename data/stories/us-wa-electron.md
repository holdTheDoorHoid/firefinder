The Electron lookout stood in Pierce County, Washington, in the Mount Rainier region.[^1] Firelookout.com places it 20 miles northwest of the mountain.[^3]

According to the Register, the site's first building was a house, put up in 1942.[^1] Willhite describes a different 1942 building, a 14-by-32-foot cabin that the Aircraft Warning Service put up there.[^4] The Forest Lookouts site adds that an Aircraft Warning Service station called Jig 2-7 used existing state facilities at the site in 1944.[^2]

The tower's date is disputed. The Register and firelookout.com both give 1948.[^1][^3] The Forest Lookouts site quotes the 40th annual report of the Washington Forest Fire Association, filed under 1947, which describes building a lookout tower near Electron. Willhite also gives 1947.[^2][^4]

It was a 40-foot pole tower with an L-6 cab.[^1][^4] Its legs were western red cedar and its bracing was fir. The cab was eight feet by eight feet with a pyramid roof, and a two-foot catwalk ran around it. People entered through a trap door in the floor.[^2] The living quarters were a former Aircraft Warning Service house beside the tower.[^2]

The tower was removed in 1963.[^1][^2][^4] Firelookout.com uses the word destroyed for the same year.[^3]

The Register lists the site as registered on May 26, 2012, nominated by Ray Kresek.[^1] Willhite visited in 2016 and found one tower leg still on the ground, along with three nearly buried footings, at least one of which had been moved.[^4] He describes the site as a drive-up, but the land is private timberland. In 2016 the company was Hancock Forest Management, and he says access needed an expensive permit, though the rules may have changed since then.[^4] He found the view poor right at the footings, though he says views open up within about 40 feet of them.[^4] We found no source that describes the site after that visit.
