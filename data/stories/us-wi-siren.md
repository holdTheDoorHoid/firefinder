The Siren lookout tower stands in Burnett County, in northwestern Wisconsin. The Forest Fire Lookout Association's statewide list places it at about 45.78 north and 92.43 west and marks it as standing. That list gives it no national register number.[^1]

OpenStreetMap, the open map, names it the Siren Lookout Tower, lists its elevation as 345 meters, and tags it as an observation tower.[^2] Beyond those details, the sources we read give no build date, builder or staffing history for it, so those parts of its story remain unknown here.

Wisconsin's wider record is clearer. In 2015 the Associated Press reported that dozens of Wisconsin fire lookouts, most of them raised in the 1930s and 1940s to watch for forest fires, were being removed from service.[^3] The Department of Natural Resources said the 72 towers still in use were not worth the expense of upkeep, repairs or replacement.[^3] The report does not name Siren, so this account cannot say whether it was among them.

Access today is not documented in these sources. A Wisconsin travel article names the Mountain Fire Lookout Tower in the Nicolet forest as the state's sole remaining fire tower open to climbers.[^4] That suggests, without stating it, that Siren is not open to visitors. Before going near the site, check who owns the land and whether the tower is open. Do not climb a tower that is closed or private.
