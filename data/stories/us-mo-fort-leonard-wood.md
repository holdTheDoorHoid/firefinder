The Missouri Conservation Commission built the Fort Wood fire tower in Pulaski County, near Fort Leonard Wood and Waynesville. A 1942 report lists 27 acres acquired for the towersite, while a 1943 report says seventy acres were acquired and that an 85-foot steel tower was erected.[^1] Accounts also differ on its position.[^1] A 1948 newspaper item places it near Fort Leonard Wood on the edge of the Mark Twain National Forest, and a 1949 newspaper item places it along U.S. Highway 66 near Waynesville.[^1]

The earliest keeper the page names is James Krausland, reported in March 1944 as the fire tower keeper just north of Fort Leonard Wood.[^1] By 1947 the district was in the care of James F. Crossland, a forest fire tower man from Waynesville.[^1] The page spells the name Crossland in 1947 and 1950 but Krausland in 1944, and does not say whether these are the same man.[^1] In April 1950 a church near Waynesville caught fire, and Forest Towerman James Crossland put it out with his tank and pump-mounted pickup before much damage was done.[^1]

The tower also drew sightseers.[^1] In October 1949, visitors from 12 states and one foreign country stopped by to look out over the Ozark hills.[^1]

In fiscal 1954-55 the old tower was replaced with a new 100-foot steel tower.[^1] Later entries are mostly upkeep: new garage doors in 1968-69, new bathroom tile and a kitchen countertop for the residence in 1969-70, and a repaired residence in 1971-72.[^1] A new heating system went into the residence in 1972-73, and a new water line ran to the tower residence in 1980-81.[^1]

The tower's last recorded event came in February 2023, when it was sold and removed from the site.[^1] The page does not say where the tower went, or whether anything from it stays on the ground.[^1] I found no other source describing the site today, so I cannot say whether footings or other remains are still there, or whether the site is open to visitors.[^1]
