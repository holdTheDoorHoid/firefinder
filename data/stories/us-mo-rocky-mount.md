A tower was erected at Rocky Mount in 1949.[^2] The Missouri Conservation Commission had acquired a 40-acre towersite there in the 1947-48 fiscal year, and a state manual of the period lists the site as owned by the commission.[^2] The Department of Conservation now lists the site as the Rocky Mount Towersite in Miller County, with directions from Eldon.[^4] According to the register, the tower went up in the summer of 1949 and carried an Osborne Fire Finder.[^1]

Towerman P.A. Hess staffed the tower from 1956 to 1987, according to the register.[^1] Staff were there before that as well.[^2] In March 1950 towerman Harold Gregg Hibdon was struck by a car while removing firefighting equipment from his jeep at a brush fire, and he died in hospital in early April.[^2]

A Department of Conservation annual report for fiscal 1968-69 records that a new steel tower was erected at Rocky Mount.[^2] In 2000 a tower was dismantled and moved to the Conservation Education Center in Jefferson City, where the upper portion was to be re-erected.[^2] Forest Lookouts does not say whether that was the 1949 tower or the steel tower erected in fiscal 1968-69. A Forest Lookouts entry for the Runge site calls it the tower formerly at Rocky Mount, and mentions a cab lying on the ground from an unknown tower.[^3]

Today the register places the lookout at the Runge Conservation Nature Center in Cole County.[^1] The original site keeps concrete bases: a Department of Conservation photo caption describes one of four concrete bases left from the tower that once stood there.[^4] The nature center's grounds have area hours of 6 a.m. to 9 p.m. daily.[^5] We found nothing saying the tower is open to climbers, so please do not climb it, and ask the center before visiting.[^5]
