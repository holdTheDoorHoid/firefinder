The Sanders Hill tower is in Pike County, Alabama, and the Alabama Forestry Commission administers it.[^1] The register gives 1958 as the build year, but its own description says a private contractor constructed the tower in 1957 for the commission.[^1] The description also spells the hill "Saunders Hill," while the heading uses "Sanders."[^1]

The tower is a 100-foot MC-39 Aermotor with a 7-by-7-foot cab, and the register says it is among the most recent lookouts built in Alabama.[^1] A 1819 News opinion column says the MC-39 was the steel design most often seen in Alabama's forests.[^2]

We found no record of who staffed the tower or of any fires it helped with.

The register shows a registration date of January 18, 2010, and a nomination by Thomas Kaufmann.[^1] It does not describe the tower's condition.[^1] We could not find a newer account of whether it still stands.

Today we could not confirm whether the tower can be visited. The same 1819 News column estimates there are probably under 75 historic lookouts still standing in Alabama, and says many are unsafe and that climbing them is illegal.[^2] Please do not climb this tower unless the Forestry Commission confirms that the site is open.
