Watson Butte rises to 5,687 feet in the Umpqua National Forest, in Douglas County, Oregon.[^1] The register places a rag camp on the butte's western summit in 1934, and a telephone line reached the summit in 1920.[^1][^2] That camp gave way to an L-4 ground cab in 1937.[^1]

Sources disagree on when the lookout began. A Forest Lookouts listing gives 1933 as the year a lookout was established, and the Roaming Civic says the Forest Service dates the building to 1934, while other sources say 1937.[^3][^2]

A 1962 News-Review item, quoted on the Forest Lookouts page, names a local team member.[^3] According to the Roaming Civic, no one has actively staffed the lookout since the 1960s.[^2] The same post notes that salvaging the cab for the rental program was discussed once but never carried out.[^2]

The Former Fire Lookout Sites register dates the collapse to the winter of 2021-22.[^1] The Roaming Civic cites Facebook for the last record of the cab standing, on May 24, 2021, and thinks snow most likely brought it down that winter.[^2] A Forest Lookouts page gives only weather as the cause.[^3]

On its visit on July 2, 2022, the Roaming Civic found only a heap of boards on the summit.[^2] The trip report puts the hike from the trailhead at about 1.1 miles and describes the drive in as hard to follow, with poorly marked roads.[^2]

A notice at the top of that 2022 report says the area was closed as of July 20, 2024, because of the active Diamond Complex.[^2] The 2022 report is the most recent description of the site we found, and no later status turned up after that notice.[^2]
