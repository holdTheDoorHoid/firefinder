Bear Ranch Hill is a former lookout site perched on a sheer cliff above the Feather River canyon, near Belden, on the Butte and Plumas county line.[^1] The lookout went up in 1936 to watch the new Feather River highway, which was due to open the following year, and it stood close to the road itself.[^1]

By late July 1936 the foundations had already been laid. The planned base measured 16 feet by 16 feet, and the room at the top was to be 14 feet square.[^1] A paper in early September 1936 expected the station to be finished the following week.[^1]

A December 1938 report said the station was linked to the telephone system, and that Civilian Conservation Corps enrollees from Camp Concow would build the new line.[^1]

By August 1944 the lookout was Freda Danielson, a Plumas National Forest lookout who had been on duty about two months. A Forest Service training officer reported that in that time she had killed three wildcats and seven rattlesnakes.[^1]

The Forest Fire Lookout Association lists the tower as gone.[^2]

No source describes what, if anything, remains on the hilltop today, or whether the spot is open to visitors.
