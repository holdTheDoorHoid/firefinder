Austin Ridge Lookout stands in the Clearwater National Forest in Idaho County, Idaho, at about 4,731 feet (1,442 meters).[^1][^2] The first lookout on the ridge was a 30-foot pole tower with an L-4 cab, built in 1932.[^3] A windstorm destroyed that original in 1951, according to a Forest Service list.[^4] In 1960 it was replaced by the tower that stands today: a 53-foot treated timber tower topped by a 15-by-15-foot R-6 cab with a catwalk.[^1][^2]

The register says staffing ended in the early 1990s.[^1] A history site names a few of the people who worked it, including John Opresik in the 1930s, Nancy Beseman in the late 1970s and Carol Petty in the early 1980s.[^2] In the 1990s the tower went on emergency staffing and then into the rental program.[^2] The July 2023 visit report also shares a memory of competitive training from the tower: teams of firefighters spotted a smoke that management had ignited, located it, and raced to reach it.[^2]

Sources disagree on how the tower is used now.[^1][^2][^4] A Forest Service list from June 2016 says it was on standby.[^4] The register says it is now inactive.[^1] The 2023 visit report says it has left the rental program and now runs first-come, first-served, and it files the site under drive-up.[^2] The 2023 account is the most recent in the sources, but check with the Clearwater National Forest before going.
