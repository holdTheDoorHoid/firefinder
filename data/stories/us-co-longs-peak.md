In 1907 a newspaper described a plan for a fire lookout on Long’s Peak. On December 25, 1907, the Fort Collins Courier reported that 21 miles of telephone line had been built on the Medicine Bow (Colorado) National Forest, and that a branch line was planned to run to the summit, which was intended for use as a fire lookout.[^1]

Wikipedia’s Shadow Mountain Lookout article says three other lookouts, all gone now, stood at Twin Sisters Peak, on the north fork of the Thompson River, and near Long’s Peak.[^2] The Forest Fire Lookout Association’s Colorado list places Long’s Peak among its unknown or undocumented sites. It says a site will be moved onto its lookout lists if evidence shows regular use as a wildland fire lookout.[^4]

In the summer of 1925, after a January winter climb ended with a climber dying of exposure, Rocky Mountain National Park set up an emergency telephone at the Boulder Field, near the summit.[^3]

No source describes what, if anything, remains of a lookout on the summit today, or whether the spot is open to visitors. The Forest Lookouts page lists the peak’s agency as Rocky Mountain National Park.[^1]
