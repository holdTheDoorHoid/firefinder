The village of Miskimon sits on the county line between Northumberland and Lancaster in Virginia, and the Miskimon Fire Tower stands there.[^4][^1] In spring 1938 the chairman of the Virginia Conservation Commission announced a forest fire lookout for both counties, to be erected by the Forest Service, a division of the commission.[^2] The completion was announced in June that year.[^2][^1]

The two counties each paid $25.[^1][^2] A 1938 newspaper says that money covered the land, bought from Samuel Haynie, and gives the tower an investment of about $1,800.[^2] The Register says the $25 went toward a total cost of $1,800, so the two sources disagree on what it paid for.[^1] The tower arrived unassembled from Aermotor of Chicago, and local workers put it up.[^1]

The tower stands 100 feet tall, with a 10-by-10-foot cab at the top that has two windows on each side.[^1] Wooden platforms sit at each ten-foot level, and a trap door gave the spotter access to the cab.[^1]

Spotters worked the tower from about March or April until fall each year, generally on a shift from 10 to 4.[^1] The National Historic Lookout Register says the tower was vital to the farming and lumbering counties for spotting and fighting fires, from completion through the 1980s, or a little beyond.[^1] The sources read name no particular fire it watched.[^1]

Today a cell tower stands beside it, and the Register notes that some of the landings are in disrepair.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Virginia list shows the tower as standing.[^5] The Rappahannock Record calls it almost 90 years old and the smaller, older of the two towers at the site.[^3]

The Register accepted the tower on January 2, 2026, as US 1803, VA 49, on a nomination by Susan Anthony-Tolbert.[^1] She also wrote Almost in the Clouds, a book about the tower.[^3] The Record describes the book as the tower's story, while Wikipedia says the tower was the subject of a novel by her.[^3][^4]

None of the sources read says whether the public may visit or climb the tower.[^1] The Register names the Virginia Department of Forestry as administrator, so ask that office before any trip.[^1]
