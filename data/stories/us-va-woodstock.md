Woodstock Tower stands on Massanutten Mountain between Fort Valley and the town of Woodstock, in the George Washington National Forest.[^1][^4] The Civilian Conservation Corps built it in 1935, and the register describes it as a steel observation tower.[^1][^3][^4]

Sources differ on who paid for it. The register says the Woodstock Chamber of Commerce funded it, while the Forest Service and a county library exhibit say the chamber supplied the materials.[^1][^3][^4] A May 1947 note quoted by Forest Lookouts says the tower was a joint effort of Woodstock citizens and the Forest Service.[^2]

The register says the tower served both fire spotting and recreation.[^1] The county library exhibit says it was meant to lure tourists to the area.[^4] The register also says the Forest Service has repaired the tower over the years, and a Virginia lookout list shows it as still standing.[^1][^5]

Today the Forest Service calls it a day-use observation site, open year round from sunrise to sunset, with no fee.[^3] To get there, follow Woodstock Tower Road (SR 758) roughly four miles up to the mountaintop, and look for the tower's footpath on your left.[^3] Access can be cut off by Virginia Department of Transportation closures, and there is no drinking water on site.[^3]
