The Bay Leaf lookout began on Six Forks Road, roughly eight miles north of Raleigh, where the lease was first recorded in 1936.[^1] The next year an Aermotor steel tower went up on the west side of that road.[^2] A 1938 state report, quoted by the Forest Lookouts site, describes it as a steel tower 99 feet 9 inches high, built by Civilian Conservation Corps crews from Camp P-53.[^3] In March 1938, CCC labor from Camp P-68 finished a five-room frame house for the lookout's living quarters.[^3]

Jenny Tilley ran the lookout for decades.[^2][^1] WRAL names her as the operator from the 1950s onward and says she was 22 when she began.[^1] The Legeros history calls her the long-time operator.[^2] Her son Jerry says the Six Forks Road site was the highest ground nearby back then.[^1] The tower was last manned in 1985.[^2][^1]

In 1967 officials bought a new site on Howard Road (then SR 1828), and the tower was re-erected there in 1968.[^1][^2] Jerry Tilley recalls that the move added 20 feet to the tower's height.[^1]

The Legeros history, last updated in January 2017, lists the Howard Road tower as no longer used.[^2] When WRAL wrote about it in May 2023, the tower was vacant and rusting, and it still stood near Creedmoor Road.[^1] WRAL's opening points out that regular drivers can pass it without spotting it.[^1]

Tilley stayed on at the site until the 1990s, WRAL reports.[^1] On the house beside the tower, the sources disagree. WRAL says a little house below the tower is now the Forest Service office.[^1] The Legeros history, by contrast, says the ranger house on the same ground is now a private residence.[^2] WRAL also reports that the Forest Service removed the bottom steps to keep people from climbing, and says the tower is one to look at, not touch.[^1] No source reviewed says the public may enter the grounds or climb the tower, so treat it as something to see from the road.
