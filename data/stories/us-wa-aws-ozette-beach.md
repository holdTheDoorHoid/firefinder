Bald, an Aircraft Warning Service post run by the Army, sat on the beach near Lake Ozette in Clallam County, Washington.[^1][^2] It was not a regular fire lookout, and the state did not want the site.[^2]

In 1942 a cabin of native materials was built there, about six miles south of Ozette.[^1][^2] Getting to it was hard.[^2] It took a six-mile boat ride or a six-mile hike along the trail.[^2]

A March 1943 record says the station was complete but not yet occupied, because a workable radio hookup had not been arranged.[^2]

The Coast Guard took over the site on October 1, 1943.[^2] The firelookout.com page gives 1944 as the year it was abandoned, so the two dates disagree.[^1] The Forest Fire Lookout Association's Washington list marks the site as gone.[^3]

The WillhiteWeb account recalls that in the early 1980s a cable handrail and rock steps still led to where the cabin had been.[^2] The same undated account says erosion has collapsed much of the cabin site.[^2] We found no source describing the site today, or whether it can be visited.
