About Firefinder

Every fire lookout, on one map

Firefinder gathers what is known about the fire lookouts of the United States, standing or long gone, and puts it on one map. Every lookout site gets its own page: practical information first (can you visit, can you stay, what are the rules), then its history, with sources.

Lookouts have been documented for decades by volunteers, registers and agencies, across many separate websites. Firefinder links back to all of them and credits them on every page. It is named for the Osborne Firefinder, the round sighting table in the middle of every lookout cab, used to take a bearing on a smoke.

Reading the map

Each marker's shape says what kind of structure it is, and its fill says whether it still stands. Colour only repeats what the shape and fill already say, so the map reads the same in any colour vision and in print.

Access warnings (private land, tribal land, closed sites) are shown prominently on each lookout's page. Firefinder shows the exact location of every site, so please respect those warnings.

How sure are we?

Every entry carries one of four labels, shown on its page:

Unverified
From a single source and not yet checked. Shown with a visible Unverified badge. Details may be wrong.
Facts agree
Two or more independent sources agree on where it is and whether it stands.
Researched
Its history has been researched and written up, with footnoted sources.
Verified
Researched, then checked independently.

When sources disagree, for example on where a lookout stood or when it was built, the page says so plainly instead of quietly picking one.

Your checklist

You can mark lookouts as Visited, Stayed overnight or Want to go, on the map or on any lookout's page, and filter the map to show only those. The checklist is saved only in this browser. There are no accounts and nothing is sent anywhere.

Because it lives in one browser, clearing your browsing data deletes it. Download a copy now and then; you can import it on another device.

Map and site

  • Base map: OpenFreeMap, using OpenMapTiles and data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
  • Topographic map: USGS The National Map (public domain): National Boundaries Dataset, 3DEP Elevation Program, Geographic Names Information System, National Hydrography Dataset, National Land Cover Database, National Structures Dataset and National Transportation Dataset; USGS Global Ecosystems; U.S. Census Bureau TIGER/Line data; USFS road data; Natural Earth Data; U.S. Department of State HIU; NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information.
  • Old topo maps: the USGS Historical Topographic Map Collection (public domain), scanned maps from the 1880s to 2006. The list of sheets comes from USGS TopoView; the scans themselves are read from The National Map's public storage. Firefinder reprojects them in your browser and softens their colours a little so the markers stay readable.
  • Tower designs: facts from National Register of Historic Places documents, Historic American Buildings and Engineering Record surveys, Forest Service publications and the lookout registers and associations credited on the designs page, which links each design's sources and original plan drawings. The schematic drawings are Firefinder's own.
  • Shaded relief at country scale: Natural Earth (public domain), served by OpenFreeMap.
  • Terrain for the 3D views (view from the cab, what it could see, the smoke lesson): Terrain Tiles made by Mapzen (Tilezen Joerd) and hosted on the AWS Open Data Registry. United States 3DEP (formerly NED) and global GMTED2010 and SRTM terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey; ETOPO1 sea-floor data courtesy of NOAA's National Centers for Environmental Information. See the full attribution.
  • Peak names: USGS Geographic Names Information System (GNIS), public domain. Peak heights shown with the view are read from the terrain data and are approximate.
  • Terrain engine: Firefinder's own, written in Rust and compiled to WebAssembly, using the png and wasm-bindgen libraries (MIT or Apache-2.0). It runs in your browser; nothing is computed on a server.
  • Map software: MapLibre GL JS (BSD-3-Clause).
  • Typefaces: Public Sans and Besley (SIL Open Font License), served from this site.

Takedown requests

Firefinder shows photos and facts gathered from many people's work, always with credit. If you own a photo or text shown here and want it credited differently, or removed, just ask. We will do it promptly, with no questions asked and nothing to prove beyond telling us which item and what you would like.

  1. Open a takedown request on GitHub. Its title starts with “Takedown:” followed by the lookout's name. On a lookout's page, the link under its photos fills this in for you.
  2. Say which photo or text it is (a link to the page is enough) and whether you want a different credit line or removal.

Open a takedown request

GitHub issues are public, so please do not include private details such as your address or phone number.

Corrections and additions

Every lookout page has a Suggest an edit button. It opens a short form on GitHub with the lookout already filled in: tell us what is wrong, what is right, and how you know (a link, a book, or your own visit with its date). If you have photos you took yourself and are happy to share, say so in the form.

Privacy

No accounts, no cookies, no analytics. Your checklist and colour-theme choice stay in your browser. Like any web map, the map images are downloaded from OpenFreeMap's and the USGS's servers, which see your IP address as part of normal web traffic. “Show my location” on the map uses your device's location only in your browser, and only after you allow it. The 3D views download terrain tiles from Amazon's servers (AWS) only when you ask for one. The old topo maps, when you switch them on, come from USGS servers and The National Map's storage on Amazon's servers.
