# Possible duplicates: review of 2026-10-08

The merge report (`data/merge_report.json`) lists pairs of separate towers that might be one lookout:
71 pairs under `near_misses` (64 with the same name 1.5 to 10 km apart, 7 with different names within
350 m), and 2 pairs of approximate pins within 3 km under `approximate.close_pairs`. Every one of the 73
is below, with the evidence and the decision. Nothing was deleted: where two towers became one, the tower
with the stronger sources (a register entry, or FFLA's row) kept its id and the other id was retired (its
file is kept, hidden, with `merged_into`, and its address redirects; DESIGN.md 3.5, "Retired towers").
The joins are `RECORD_JOINS` entries in `pipeline/record_joins.py`, each with its evidence as a comment.

## How the evidence was weighed

- **The sources' own words.** NHLR and FFLOS give the area and the cooperator, the weebly pages give
  1900s-1950s newspaper lines, NGS station descriptions and often a township-range-section (PLSS); Wikidata
  and OSM give names and GNIS ids. Where two records describe one structure (the same design, year, area or
  organisation), that settles it.
- **PLSS.** Ron Kemnow's western pages give a section for most lookouts. I worked each one out to a
  latitude and longitude (about 1 km off in the checks against known pins, worse far from the meridian; the
  pairs 20 or more ranges out were not leaned on) and compared it with both towers' pins. In 13 pairs the
  section lands within 3 km of the better-sourced tower and at least 1 km nearer to it than to the other pin
  (the weebly page's own map pin, or the idahofirelookouts.com pin it had been joined to).
- **GNIS.** Where the USGS has exactly one summit, ridge or gap of that name in the county and one pin sits
  on it, the other source's pin is a rough one on the same feature. Where the two pins sit on two same-named
  features, or on none, that is no help.
- **Lineage.** firelookout.com is FFLA's older map (3,173 of its 3,266 pins are FFLA's). A firelookout.com
  record with no FFLA row at its pin, and one FFLA row of the same name a few kilometres away, is FFLA
  having moved its own pin. The merge then shows FFLA's current pin, unless GNIS says otherwise.
- **Moved structures.** NHLR and FFLOS say when a tower was sold and moved; the original site and the new
  one are two records and stay two (DESIGN.md 3.5).
- **Not enough on their own:** the same name in the same county a few kilometres apart. That was enough to
  join only when GNIS, PLSS or the text also pointed one way (the "likely" joins). Nine pairs with nothing
  more stay apart.

How far off the weebly and idahofirelookouts.com pins usually are, for the tail these pairs sit in: of the
records already in a tower with a register, FFLA or tnlandforms position, the median distance to it is
30 m for the western weebly sites (95% within 0.6 km; 38 of 2,161 are over 1.5 km, none over 5 km) and
30 m for idahofirelookouts.com (95% within 1.1 km; 37 of 944 over 1.5 km, none over 5 km). The pairs here
are the same tail, beyond the matching radius.

## The name-variant fix

`name_variants` in `pipeline/merge.py` used to take any parenthetical as an alternate name, so FFLOS's
"Pine Hill Tower Site (Potter County)" and "Round Top Tower Site (Potter County)" both answered to
"Potter County" and scored 1.0 against each other. A parenthetical that ends in a county or forest word
("(Potter County)", "(Centre County II)", "(Lewis Co.)", "(Sierra NF)", "(Fremont-Winema National Forest,
OR)") is now never a name variant; one that merely contains the word ("(Los Angeles County Fairplex)",
where NHLR's Johnstone Peak lookout now stands) still is. The approximate-position code still has its own
guard (`named_forms`) for a main name or slash part with the word in it.

Effect, from two from-scratch merges of the same inputs (8,482 towers each): **0 tower memberships
change and no id appears or disappears.** The only difference is that the name score of 4 pairs of
records already in two towers drops (NHLR's Ouabache (Wells County) against OSM's "Well's County Fire
Tower"; Lookout Mountain, Custer County, against the OSM "Chase Lookout"); both still join by position
(2-5 m). So the bug was real but, in the current data, never decided a join. 183 source records (FFLOS,
NHLR, RIDB, FFLA's chapter reports, WillhiteWeb) carry a county or forest note in their name.

## Things for the owner to look at

1. **Nine probable duplicates left apart** (Blue Joe, Capitol Hill, Grouse Point, Smith Triangle, Wounded Doe and
   Experimental Spur in Idaho; Chadbourn NC; Nebo Peak OR; Aniwa WI). Say the word and they become joins.
2. **Delaware Water Gap and Mount Minsi (PA)** are probably one 1921 steel tower, but one hobby site lists
   them as two pages, and one source's two records are never merged. A pinned exception would join them.
3. **Three NHLR pins look wrong** (Horsethief AZ, Sandwich MA, Hornsby TN); the joined towers show the
   other source's pin (`LOCATION_PICKS`). NHLR's Fall Creek Falls TN pin is 4 km from the tower, too, but
   the three sources that agree outvote it. FFLOS's Burnt Hill WA pin is 8 km from the GNIS summit that FFLA
   and the weebly page agree on; the merge still shows FFLOS's.
4. **Moved towers are not linked.** Appleton SC, Eastover SC and Crawford AL are each one tower in two
   places (the original site and where it was moved); neither name says "Relocated", so the pair is not
   linked as relocated_from / relocated_to and the original site's status is not "relocated".
5. **A county bug in the western weebly extract.** The sidebar of `idaholookouts.weebly.com` has two headers
   for the biggest county, "IDAHO COUNTY (A-L)" and "IDAHO COUNTY (M-W)"; `COUNTY_HEADER_RE` in
   `pipeline/regional/weebly_west.py` does not match the bracket, so the lookouts under them inherit the
   header above ("Gem County"): 127 of the 681 Idaho records have the county Gem and stand in Idaho County.
   Counties matter for the name-only step and for approximate pins; the extract needs re-running.
6. **Checklist marks on a retired id** do not move to the new tower: the address redirects, the checklist
   (a list of ids kept in each visitor's browser) does not. If any of the 47 retired ids was already ticked or
   shared, a migration is a few lines in `web/src/lib/checklist.ts` reading `redirects.json`.
